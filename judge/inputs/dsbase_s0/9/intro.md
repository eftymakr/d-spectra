## Related Works

The field of multiple sclerosis (MS) lesion segmentation has evolved significantly, driven by the need for accurate and robust methods to distinguish between different lesion types, particularly rare paramagnetic rim lesions (PRLs). Traditional methods, such as k-means clustering and level-set techniques, have been widely used for image segmentation but often struggle with the high variability and heterogeneity of MS lesions \[[Hang Zhang' 2020-09-29](http://arxiv.org/abs/2009.13755v1)\]. These methods typically rely on manual feature extraction and are limited in their ability to generalize across different imaging protocols and scanners \[[Liad Doniza' 2024-04-30](http://arxiv.org/abs/2404.19309v1)\].

Recent advances have shifted towards deep learning models, particularly convolutional neural networks (CNNs), which have demonstrated superior performance in handling the complexity of MS lesions. A multi-branch CNN was proposed to process multi-modal brain MRI data, leveraging both spatial and contextual information to improve segmentation accuracy \[[Tanya Nair' 2018-08-03](http://arxiv.org/abs/1808.01200v2)\]. This approach showed promising results in capturing the intricate morphology of MS lesions. However, these models often require large amounts of training data and face challenges in generalizing across different imaging protocols and scanners \[[Shradha Verma' 2022-11-09](http://arxiv.org/abs/2211.04764v1)\].

To address the issue of class imbalance, which is a critical challenge in MS lesion segmentation due to the rarity of certain lesion types, synthetic data generation has emerged as a promising solution. A self-adaptive network was introduced to handle various MRI sequences and adapt to different imaging protocols, significantly improving segmentation performance \[[Shradha Verma' 2022-11-09](http://arxiv.org/abs/2211.04764v1)\]. Another study proposed a method for generating synthetic quantitative susceptibility maps (QSMs) of PRLs, demonstrating that synthetic data could enhance classifier performance and improve the detection of rare lesion types . This approach showed that synthetic data could be used to train models on ambiguous rim cases and increase the minority class representation.

Attention mechanisms have also been integrated into deep learning models to improve the segmentation of MS lesions. An augmented U-Net architecture with a convolutional long short-term memory (LSTM) layer and attention mechanism was proposed, showing significant improvements in lesion segmentation \[[Liad Doniza' 2024-04-30](http://arxiv.org/abs/2404.19309v1)\]. This approach enhanced the model's ability to capture long-range dependencies and improved the robustness of the segmentation process.

Generative adversarial networks (GANs) have been explored for synthetic data generation in MS lesion segmentation. A study proposed a QGANs approach that utilized quaternion-valued networks to reduce the number of parameters and improve training efficiency \[[Snehashis Roy' 2018-03-24](http://arxiv.org/abs/1803.09172v1)\]. This method outperformed real-valued GANs in terms of FID scores and parameter efficiency, making it a promising direction for future research.

In summary, the related works highlight the ongoing efforts to improve the accuracy and robustness of MS lesion segmentation through the integration of advanced deep learning techniques, synthetic data generation, and attention mechanisms. These approaches have shown promising results, particularly in addressing the challenges of class imbalance and improving the generalizability of segmentation models across different clinical settings.
