## Related Works

The study of economic inequality and its mitigation through taxation has been extensively explored in both theoretical and applied contexts. Traditional approaches to tax policy, such as the U.S. federal income tax, aim to reduce inequality but often lack the adaptability required to address the complexity of taxpayer behavior \[[Iago Nascimento Barros' 2025-01-15](http://arxiv.org/abs/2501.08573v1)\]. The Saez Optimal Taxation model \[[Affan Shoukat' 2020-07-08](http://arxiv.org/abs/2007.04192v1)\] represents a significant advancement by incorporating dynamic adjustments to tax rates based on economic conditions. However, it has limitations in addressing taxpayer heterogeneity and irrational behavior, which are critical factors in real-world tax policy \[[Osonde A. Osoba' 2020-06-09](http://arxiv.org/abs/2006.05048v3)\].

Recent advancements in artificial intelligence and machine learning have introduced new methodologies for economic modeling and policy design. For instance, the integration of large language models (LLMs) with agent-based modeling (ABM) has shown promise in simulating complex economic systems \[[Xudong Lu' 2024-02-22](http://arxiv.org/abs/2402.14800v2)\]. This approach allows for the creation of heterogeneous agents that can mimic real-world taxpayer behaviors, enabling more accurate and adaptive policy simulations \[[Fu-Chieh Chang' 2024-11-25](http://arxiv.org/abs/2411.16260v3)\]. The TaxAgent framework described in this paper builds upon these advancements by leveraging LLMs to iteratively optimize tax rates, balancing equity and productivity in a dynamic economic environment \[[Simone Righi' 2019-01-13](http://arxiv.org/abs/1901.03951v1)\].

Several studies have explored the use of ABM in economic policy analysis. For example, \[[Kaichen Zhang' 2025-04-28](http://arxiv.org/abs/2504.19599v4)\] presents an agent-based model to examine the impact of aporophobia on poverty levels, highlighting the importance of social and institutional factors in economic outcomes. Similarly, \[[Renxi Wang' 2024-02-18](http://arxiv.org/abs/2402.11651v2)\] develops an agent-based simulator for economic systems with learning agents, demonstrating how reinforcement learning can be used to optimize economic policies. These models emphasize the need for adaptive and data-driven approaches to economic policy, which aligns with the goals of the TaxAgent framework \[[Isabelle Augenstein' 2023-10-08](http://arxiv.org/abs/2310.05189v2)\].

The application of LLMs in economic modeling is also gaining traction. \[[Ahmet Ak' 2019-09-23](http://arxiv.org/abs/1910.03141v1)\] investigates the use of LLMs in simulating economic systems, emphasizing the potential of these models to provide more accurate and flexible policy recommendations. The integration of LLMs with ABM, as proposed in this paper, represents a novel approach that combines the strengths of both methodologies to address the limitations of traditional tax policy models \[[Daniel Hübner' 2023-03-15](http://arxiv.org/abs/2303.08710v1)\].

In addition to economic modeling, the use of ABM in policy evaluation has been explored in various domains. \[[Oluwatosin Ogundare' 2023-05-05](http://arxiv.org/abs/2305.03715v1)\] presents an agent-based model for poverty and discrimination policy-making, demonstrating how such models can inform the design of more effective and equitable policies. These studies underscore the importance of adaptive and data-driven approaches in economic policy design, which is a core principle of the TaxAgent framework \[[Nicolas Zucchet' 2025-03-27](http://arxiv.org/abs/2503.21676v2)\].
