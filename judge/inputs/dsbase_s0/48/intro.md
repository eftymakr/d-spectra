## Related Works

Neural retrieval methods have seen significant advancements with the advent of transformer-based pre-trained language models (PLMs), which enable more effective multilingual and cross-lingual retrieval \[[Maharaj Brahma' 2025-04-14](http://arxiv.org/abs/2504.10335v2)\]. However, the effectiveness of these models for low-resource, morphologically rich languages such as Amharic remains underexplored due to data scarcity and suboptimal tokenization \[[Craig Macdonald' 2021-08-13](http://arxiv.org/abs/2108.06279v2)\]. Prior work has addressed these challenges by leveraging multilingual pre-trained models and fine-tuning them on domain-specific tasks \[[Georgios Sidiropoulos' 2024-03-16](http://arxiv.org/abs/2403.10939v1)\]. For instance, the use of multilingual BERT (mBERT) and XLM-RoBERTa has shown promise in cross-lingual retrieval tasks, but they often struggle with low-resource languages due to limited training data and linguistic diversity \[[Wayne Xin Zhao' 2022-11-27](http://arxiv.org/abs/2211.14876v1)\].

Several studies have focused on improving retrieval performance for low-resource languages by developing language-specific models. For example, the work by \[[Tharindu Ranasinghe' 2021-05-12](http://arxiv.org/abs/2105.05996v3)\] introduces a dense retrieval model for Amharic based on pre-trained BERT and RoBERTa backbones, demonstrating improved performance over multilingual baselines. Similarly, \[[Bonaventure F. P. Dossou' 2021-03-14](http://arxiv.org/abs/2103.08052v2)\] presents a study on the use of RoBERTa for Amharic named entity recognition, highlighting the importance of language-specific adaptations. These models are trained on limited data, which can lead to suboptimal performance \[[Gokul Karthik Kumar' 2022-04-12](http://arxiv.org/abs/2204.05814v1)\].

In addition to language-specific models, there has been growing interest in using dense retrieval approaches for low-resource languages. The work by \[[Shiguang Wu' 2024-03-31](http://arxiv.org/abs/2404.00684v1)\] explores the use of ColBERT for cross-lingual retrieval, showing that dense retrieval can outperform traditional lexical methods. However, the performance of these models is often limited by the availability of high-quality training data \[[Dan John Velasco' 2020-10-13](http://arxiv.org/abs/2010.06447v2)\]. The use of synthetic data generation techniques has also been explored to address data scarcity \[[Atharva Mutsaddi' 2025-01-09](http://arxiv.org/abs/2501.05260v1)\].

Another line of research focuses on the challenges of cross-lingual retrieval for low-resource languages. The study by \[[Kunal Sawarkar' 2024-03-22](http://arxiv.org/abs/2404.07220v2)\] investigates the performance of multilingual models on Amharic, highlighting the need for language-specific adaptations to improve retrieval effectiveness. Similarly, \[[Melusi Malinga' 2024-11-06](http://arxiv.org/abs/2411.04316v1)\] discusses the limitations of multilingual models in handling morphologically rich languages like Amharic, emphasizing the importance of domain-specific training data.

Recent efforts have also focused on the development of benchmark datasets and evaluation metrics for low-resource languages. The work by \[[Sean MacAvaney' 2018-11-21](http://arxiv.org/abs/1811.08772v1)\] introduces a new benchmark dataset for Amharic, which is used to evaluate the performance of various retrieval models. This dataset is crucial for assessing the effectiveness of different approaches in low-resource settings \[[Marc Sloan' 2016-01-18](http://arxiv.org/abs/1601.04605v1)\].

In summary, while there has been significant progress in neural retrieval methods for multilingual and cross-lingual tasks, the effectiveness of these models for low-resource languages such as Amharic remains a challenge. The work presented in this paper addresses this gap by introducing Amharic-specific dense retrieval models based on pre-trained BERT and RoBERTa backbones, achieving significant improvements over existing multilingual baselines \[[Tilahun Yeshambel' 2025-03-24](http://arxiv.org/abs/2503.18570v1)\].
