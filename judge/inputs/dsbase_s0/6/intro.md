## Related Works

Narrative summarization, the task of condensing long-form texts such as books, movies, and TV scripts, has been a challenging area for large language models (LLMs) due to the complexity of capturing intricate plotlines, character interactions, and thematic coherence \[[Yijia Xiao' 2024-12-28](http://arxiv.org/abs/2412.20138v7)\]. Prior work has explored various methods to improve the quality of narrative summaries, often focusing on fine-tuning models on specific datasets or leveraging domain-specific knowledge \[[Zehan Li' 2025-04-10](http://arxiv.org/abs/2504.07459v2)\]. However, these approaches typically require extensive training data and are not easily scalable to diverse storytelling domains \[[Bowen Jiang' 2024-03-21](http://arxiv.org/abs/2403.14783v1)\].

Several studies have investigated the use of multi-agent systems to enhance the summarization process. For example, \[[Matt Grenander' 2025-04-12](http://arxiv.org/abs/2504.09071v2)\] introduces a multi-agent prompting strategy for text classification, where multiple LLMs collaborate to generate principles that guide the classification task. This approach demonstrates the potential of multi-agent frameworks in improving the accuracy of LLMs by leveraging diverse perspectives \[[Shoya Matsumori' 2021-06-29](http://arxiv.org/abs/2106.15550v1)\]. Similarly, \[[Kevin R. McKee' 2021-02-16](http://arxiv.org/abs/2102.08370v2)\] proposes a multi-agent system for financial trading, where specialized agents handle different aspects of the trading process, such as fundamental analysis and sentiment analysis. This framework highlights the benefits of modular, specialized agents in complex decision-making tasks \[[Cinjon Resnick' 2018-09-19](http://arxiv.org/abs/1809.07124v2)\].

In the context of narrative summarization, \[[Chairi Kiourt' 2018-07-07](http://arxiv.org/abs/1807.02648v1)\] explores the use of a multi-agent system to generate summaries of long-form texts. The approach involves a structured pipeline that includes preprocessing, chunk processing, and output control to ensure the summaries are both accurate and coherent. This work is closely related to the proposed NexusSum framework, which also employs a multi-agent architecture to handle the complexities of narrative summarization \[[Salvador Tamarit' 2016-03-09](http://arxiv.org/abs/1603.03011v2)\].

The preprocessing stage in narrative summarization is critical for improving coherence and consistency. \[[Haochen Zhang' 2023-12-04](http://arxiv.org/abs/2312.01678v6)\] introduces a preprocessing method that standardizes character dialogue and descriptive text into a unified format, which is essential for generating coherent summaries. This approach is similar to the Dialogue-to-Description Transformation introduced in NexusSum, which aims to standardize input data for better summarization \[[Leo Ardon' 2023-03-24](http://arxiv.org/abs/2303.14061v4)\].

Additionally, \[[Brice Ozenne' 2024-04-23](http://arxiv.org/abs/2404.14882v2)\] proposes a method for generating causal graphs from narrative texts, which can help in understanding the relationships between events and characters. This work is relevant to the thematic coherence aspect of narrative summarization, as it provides a structured way to represent the narrative's underlying structure \[[Qiaolin Qin' 2024-12-06](http://arxiv.org/abs/2412.05254v1)\].

In summary, the related works highlight the challenges and opportunities in narrative summarization, particularly in the context of multi-agent systems. The proposed NexusSum framework builds on these prior efforts, introducing novel innovations in preprocessing and summarization pipelines to achieve state-of-the-art results \[[Xiaohan Huang' 2025-04-24](http://arxiv.org/abs/2504.17355v1)\].
