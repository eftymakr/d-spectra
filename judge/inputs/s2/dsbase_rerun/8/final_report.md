## Related Works

The rapid adoption of large language models (LLMs) has underscored the critical need for robust fairness benchmarks \[[13' 2024-09-22](http://arxiv.org/abs/2409.14583v4)\]. Traditional fairness metrics, such as Equalized Odds, focus on discrete accuracy-based evaluations, often overlooking the implicit impact of model uncertainty \[[13' 2024-09-22](http://arxiv.org/abs/2409.14583v4)\]. This limitation has prompted researchers to explore alternative fairness metrics that account for model confidence and decision bias \[[13' 2024-09-22](http://arxiv.org/abs/2409.14583v4)\]. One such effort is the development of the uncertainty-aware fairness metric, UCerF, which enables a more nuanced evaluation of model fairness by capturing internal bias in decision-making \[[13' 2024-09-22](http://arxiv.org/abs/2409.14583v4)\]. This approach aligns with earlier studies that highlighted the insufficiency of conventional fairness measures in capturing bias in LLMs \[[13' 2024-09-22](http://arxiv.org/abs/2409.14583v4)\].

Several studies have investigated gender bias in LLMs, particularly in occupational recommendations and translation systems \[[1' 2025-05-05](http://arxiv.org/abs/2505.02456v2)\]\[[6' 2025-02-26](http://arxiv.org/abs/2502.19104v2)\]. For example, research has shown that LLMs encode significant gender and country biases, with intersectional occupational biases persisting even when models show parity for gender or country individually \[[1' 2025-05-05](http://arxiv.org/abs/2505.02456v2)\]. Similarly, gender bias in machine translation has been evaluated using datasets like WinoMTDE, which assesses occupational stereotyping in German MT systems \[[6' 2025-02-26](http://arxiv.org/abs/2502.19104v2)\]. These studies emphasize the need for multilingual and intersectional approaches to fairness evaluation \[[1' 2025-05-05](http://arxiv.org/abs/2505.02456v2)\].

The evaluation of gender bias in natural language inference (NLI) has also been a focus of recent research \[[3' 2021-05-12](http://arxiv.org/abs/2105.05541v1)\]. Studies have demonstrated that state-of-the-art NLI models, such as BERT and RoBERTa, are significantly prone to gender-induced prediction errors, highlighting the challenges of detecting and mitigating gender bias in language understanding tasks \[[3' 2021-05-12](http://arxiv.org/abs/2105.05541v1)\]. De-biasing techniques, such as augmenting training data with gender-balanced examples, have shown some success in reducing such biases \[[3' 2021-05-12](http://arxiv.org/abs/2105.05541v1)\].

In addition to gender bias, research has also explored racial and age disparities in LLMs, particularly in occupational and crime scenarios \[[13' 2024-09-22](http://arxiv.org/abs/2409.14583v4)\]. These studies reveal that debiasing efforts often create new fairness trade-offs, highlighting the complexity of addressing bias in LLMs \[[13' 2024-09-22](http://arxiv.org/abs/2409.14583v4)\]. For instance, efforts to reduce gender and racial bias may inadvertently over-index one sub-class, potentially exacerbating disparities \[[13' 2024-09-22](http://arxiv.org/abs/2409.14583v4)\].

To address these challenges, researchers have proposed new datasets and benchmarks for evaluating fairness in LLMs. For example, the OccuGender benchmark was introduced to investigate occupational gender bias in LLMs, with results showing substantial gender bias in models like Llama and Mistral \[[5' 2022-12-20](http://arxiv.org/abs/2212.10678v4)\]. ### <a id='category-debiasing-techniques-for-language-models'></a>Debiasing Techniques for Language Models: 3 papers found.
The related works in debiasing techniques for language models focus on addressing inherent biases in large language models (LLMs) through various strategies. One key area is the systematic investigation of bias inheritance, where LLMs trained on synthetic data may propagate and amplify existing biases, negatively impacting fairness and robustness in downstream tasks \[[1' 2025-05-05](http://arxiv.org/abs/2505.02456v2)\]. This issue is tackled through three mitigation strategies: token-based, mask-based, and loss-based approaches, which aim to reduce bias by aligning values, group data, and data distributions \[[1' 2025-05-05](http://arxiv.org/abs/2505.02456v2)\]. Additionally, efforts to improve model efficiency in multi-modal tasks, such as PruneVid, highlight the importance of reducing redundancy in video data while maintaining performance, leveraging LLMs' reasoning capabilities for selective pruning \[[2' 2024-11-20](http://arxiv.org/abs/2411.13738v1)\]. Another relevant line of work involves understanding the internal mechanisms of LLMs, such as identifying important neurons for predictions and distinguishing between "value neurons" and "query neurons" that activate them, offering insights into knowledge storage and retrieval \[[3' 2021-05-12](http://arxiv.org/abs/2105.05541v1)\]. These studies collectively emphasize the need for comprehensive approaches to debiasing, including both data-level and model-level interventions, to ensure fairness and reliability in LLMs.
