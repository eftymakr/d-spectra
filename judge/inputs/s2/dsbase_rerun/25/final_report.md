## Related Works

The integration of knowledge graphs (KGs) into large language models (LLMs) for claim verification has gained significant attention in recent years. While LLMs have demonstrated strong reasoning capabilities, their performance on complex, multi-step tasks involving structured knowledge remains limited \[[18' 2024-12-17](http://arxiv.org/abs/2412.12881v1)\]. This is partly due to the fact that most existing claim verification methods rely on unstructured text corpora rather than leveraging the structured, semantically rich representations provided by KGs \[[1' 2025-02-28](http://arxiv.org/abs/2502.20988v2)\]. This limitation highlights the need for frameworks that can effectively bridge the gap between LLMs and KGs.

Several approaches have explored the use of KGs to enhance LLM reasoning. For instance, the Observation-Driven Agent (ODA) introduces a cyclical paradigm of observation, action, and reflection to integrate KG reasoning into LLMs, achieving notable performance improvements \[[3' 2024-04-11](http://arxiv.org/abs/2404.07677v2)\]. Similarly, the StructGPT framework enables LLMs to reason over structured data through an iterative reading-then-reasoning approach, significantly boosting performance on structured tasks \[[9' 2023-05-16](http://arxiv.org/abs/2305.09645v2)\]. These methods demonstrate that structured reasoning can be enhanced when LLMs are equipped with appropriate mechanisms to interact with KGs.

The use of retrieval-augmented generation (RAG) has also been explored to improve claim verification. The Reasoning-CV method, for example, fine-tunes powerful LLMs using a supervised fine-tuning (SFT) stage and a self-improvement direct preference optimization (DPO) stage, leading to superior performance in knowledge-assisted claim verification \[[2' 2025-05-18](http://arxiv.org/abs/2505.12348v1)\]. Similarly, RAG-Star integrates retrieved information into a tree-based deliberative reasoning process, leveraging Monte Carlo Tree Search to plan intermediate sub-queries and answers \[[18' 2024-12-17](http://arxiv.org/abs/2412.12881v1)\]. These approaches highlight the potential of combining external knowledge with LLMs to improve reasoning accuracy.

The challenge of effectively integrating LLMs with KGs has also been addressed through the development of specialized frameworks. The FOLK method employs first-order logic-guided reasoning to verify complex claims and generate explanations without relying on human-annotated data, demonstrating the potential for explainable claim verification \[[6' 2023-10-08](http://arxiv.org/abs/2310.05253v2)\]. The Dual Graph Embedding (DGE) method further advances this by modeling both first-order and high-order proximities in KGs, leading to improved link prediction performance \[[10' 2020-07-16](http://arxiv.org/abs/2007.08304v1)\]. These methods illustrate the growing interest in leveraging KGs for structured reasoning tasks.
