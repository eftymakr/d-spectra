## Related Works

Deep sequential recommendation models have seen significant advancements, driven by the need to effectively model user behavior patterns and handle variable-length sequences. Early approaches relied on recurrent neural networks (RNNs) and their variants, such as Gated Recurrent Units (GRUs) and Long Short-Term Memory (LSTM) networks, to capture sequential dependencies \[[22' 2023-10-03](http://arxiv.org/abs/2310.02367v2)\]. However, these models often struggle with capturing long-range dependencies and suffer from computational inefficiencies due to their sequential nature \[[22' 2023-10-03](http://arxiv.org/abs/2310.02367v2)\]. 

Attention-based models, particularly those based on the Transformer architecture, have emerged as a promising alternative. These models leverage self-attention mechanisms to capture complex interactions between items in a sequence \[[1' 2025-01-02](http://arxiv.org/abs/2501.01242v1)\]. However, the quadratic computational complexity of self-attention poses a challenge, especially for long sequences \[[1' 2025-01-02](http://arxiv.org/abs/2501.01242v1)\]. To address this, linear attention mechanisms, such as those proposed in HydraRec, have been introduced to reduce the computational complexity while preserving temporal context \[[1' 2025-01-02](http://arxiv.org/abs/2501.01242v1)\]. Similarly, Mamba4Rec leverages state space models (SSMs) for efficient sequential modeling, offering linear complexity and hardware-aware designs \[[3' 2024-03-06](http://arxiv.org/abs/2403.03900v2)\]. 

Recent works have also explored the integration of state space models with attention mechanisms. For instance, EchoMamba4Rec combines bidirectional state space models with spectral filtering to capture complex patterns in user interactions \[[2' 2024-06-04](http://arxiv.org/abs/2406.02638v2)\]. This approach benefits from the ability of SSMs to perform parallel computations, enhancing both computational efficiency and scalability \[[2' 2024-06-04](http://arxiv.org/abs/2406.02638v2)\]. Additionally, SSD4Rec introduces a structured state space duality (SSD) model that enables efficient modeling of variable-length sequences while maintaining high performance \[[5' 2024-09-02](http://arxiv.org/abs/2409.01192v2)\]. 

The use of mixture-of-experts (MoE) architectures has also gained traction in sequential recommendation. MoE allows for the dynamic routing of different behavioral patterns to specialized experts, improving both efficiency and effectiveness \[[21' 2024-02-22](http://arxiv.org/abs/2402.14800v2)\]. This concept is further extended in STAR-Rec, which combines preference-aware attention with state-space modeling through a sequence-level MoE framework \[[23' 2024-09-08](http://arxiv.org/abs/2409.05022v1)\]. 

In addition to attention and SSMs, recent studies have explored the integration of temporal and contextual information. For example, MEANTIME introduces multi-temporal embeddings to capture diverse patterns in user behavior \[[15' 2020-08-19](http://arxiv.org/abs/2008.08273v2)\]. Similarly, Déjà vu proposes a contextualized temporal attention mechanism to dynamically weigh the influence of historical events based on time and context \[[9' 2020-01-29](http://arxiv.org/abs/2002.00741v1)\]. 

Finally, the incorporation of negative feedback and contrastive learning has been shown to improve recommendation accuracy. For instance, FEARec enhances the frequency domain representation of sequential data to capture both low- and high-frequency information
