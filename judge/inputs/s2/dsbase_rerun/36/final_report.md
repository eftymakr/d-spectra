## Related Works

Deploying large language models (LLMs) on edge devices has been a focal point of recent research due to the increasing demand for on-device intelligence and privacy-preserving inference \[[1' 2025-04-04](http://arxiv.org/abs/2504.03360v1)\]. However, the computational and memory constraints of edge hardware pose significant challenges for deploying LLMs \[[2' 2025-04-07](http://arxiv.org/abs/2505.03745v1)\]. Several studies have explored techniques to reduce the resource footprint of LLMs while maintaining acceptable performance.

Model quantization has emerged as a key technique to enable efficient LLM inference by reducing model size and computational overhead \[[1' 2025-04-04](http://arxiv.org/abs/2504.03360v1)\]. Research has focused on various quantization schemes, including weight-only quantization and mixed-precision matrix multiplication (mpGEMM) \[[3' 2025-02-19](http://arxiv.org/abs/2502.15816v1)\]. Similarly, \[[3' 2025-02-19](http://arxiv.org/abs/2502.15816v1)\] introduces MixPE, a specialized mixed-precision processing element designed for efficient low-bit quantization in LLM inference, achieving significant improvements in speed and energy efficiency.

Algorithm-hardware co-design has also been proposed to address the challenges of deploying LLMs on edge devices \[[4' 2024-11-25](http://arxiv.org/abs/2411.16158v1)\]. Mixed-precision and low-bit quantization approaches have emerged as critical techniques for efficiently deploying large language models (LLMs) on edge devices, addressing the limitations of traditional quantization methods \[[1' 2025-04-04](http://arxiv.org/abs/2504.03360v1)\]. For instance, MixPE introduces a specialized processing element that minimizes dequantization overhead by performing it after per-group mpGEMM, alongside using shift-and-add operations to enhance efficiency \[[3' 2025-02-19](http://arxiv.org/abs/2502.15816v1)\]. T-MAC further advances this field by introducing a lookup table (LUT)-based method that directly supports mpGEMM without dequantization, significantly improving throughput and energy efficiency on CPUs \[[5' 2024-07-16](http://arxiv.org/abs/2407.12070v1)\]. Additionally, Automatic Mixed-Precision Quantization (AMED) challenges traditional assumptions by treating quantization as a random process, optimizing bitwidth allocation based on direct hardware signals \[[6' 2025-04-11](http://arxiv.org/abs/2504.08242v1)\]. These innovations collectively highlight the growing focus on algorithm-hardware co-design to achieve efficient and accurate low-bit LLM inference on resource-constrained devices.
