## Related Works

Deploying large language models (LLMs) on edge devices has been a focal point of recent research due to the increasing demand for on-device intelligence and privacy-preserving inference \[[1' 2025-04-04](http://arxiv.org/abs/2504.03360v1)\]. However, the computational and memory constraints of edge hardware pose significant challenges for deploying LLMs \[[2' 2025-04-07](http://arxiv.org/abs/2505.03745v1)\]. Several studies have explored techniques to reduce the resource footprint of LLMs while maintaining acceptable performance.

Model quantization has emerged as a key technique to enable efficient LLM inference by reducing model size and computational overhead \[[1' 2025-04-04](http://arxiv.org/abs/2504.03360v1)\]. Research has focused on various quantization schemes, including weight-only quantization and mixed-precision matrix multiplication (mpGEMM) \[[3' 2025-02-19](http://arxiv.org/abs/2502.15816v1)\]. For instance, the study by \[[1' 2025-04-04](http://arxiv.org/abs/2504.03360v1)\] evaluates 28 quantized LLMs from the Ollama library, deployed on an edge device (Raspberry Pi 4), highlighting trade-offs between energy efficiency, inference speed, and accuracy. Similarly, \[[3' 2025-02-19](http://arxiv.org/abs/2502.15816v1)\] introduces MixPE, a specialized mixed-precision processing element designed for efficient low-bit quantization in LLM inference, achieving significant improvements in speed and energy efficiency.

Algorithm-hardware co-design has also been proposed to address the challenges of deploying LLMs on edge devices \[[4' 2024-11-25](http://arxiv.org/abs/2411.16158v1)\]. The AccLLM framework \[[4' 2024-11-25](http://arxiv.org/abs/2411.16158v1)\] integrates pruning, Λ-shaped attention, and a W2A8KV4 quantization scheme to reduce memory and bandwidth requirements, while a dedicated FPGA-based accelerator enhances inference performance. Another study, \[[5' 2024-07-16](http://arxiv.org/abs/2407.12070v1)\], proposes a co-design method for efficient end-to-end edge deployment of Transformers, achieving up to 49.37x throughput gains and 88.53x better energy efficiency over state-of-the-art accelerators.

Collaborative inference approaches have also been explored to mitigate the resource limitations of individual edge devices \[[6' 2025-04-11](http://arxiv.org/abs/2504.08242v1)\]. Jupiter \[[6' 2025-04-11](http://arxiv.org/abs/2504.08242v1)\] introduces a pipelined architecture for generative LLM inference, differentiating between the prefill and decoding phases to achieve significant latency reduction. The study demonstrates up to 26.1x end-to-end latency reduction while maintaining generation quality. Similarly, \[[7' 2025-03-10](http://arxiv.org/abs/2504.00002v1)\] proposes a collaborative edge computing framework that reduces communication overhead and improves resource utilization.
