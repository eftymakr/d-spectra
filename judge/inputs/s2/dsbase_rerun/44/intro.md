## Related Works

Representation learning has become a cornerstone in modern recommender systems, particularly for user-item matching \[[1' 2019-06-05](http://arxiv.org/abs/1906.01829v1)\]. The use of vectorized embeddings allows for capturing complex interactions between users and items, enabling more accurate and personalized recommendations. However, the computational and memory costs associated with dense embeddings have motivated research into binarization techniques, which convert latent features into compact binary representations \[[2' 2021-02-05](http://arxiv.org/abs/2102.03135v1)\]. This approach offers significant advantages in terms of efficiency, particularly for online inference \[[3' 2022-03-29](http://arxiv.org/abs/2203.15789v1)\]. 

Several studies have explored the binarization of deep neural networks, focusing on minimizing information loss during quantization. For instance, \[[4' 2023-02-04](http://arxiv.org/abs/2302.02113v1)\] proposed a loss-aware binarization method that directly minimizes the loss function with respect to binarized weights, leading to improved performance compared to traditional quantization approaches. This work emphasized the importance of preserving the integrity of the model's output during binarization, a concern also addressed in \[[5' 2016-11-05](http://arxiv.org/abs/1611.01600v3)\], where a proximal Newton algorithm with diagonal Hessian approximation was introduced to enhance the optimization process.

In the context of collaborative filtering, binarization has been explored in conjunction with graph neural networks (GNNs). For example, \[[6' 2019-06-26](http://arxiv.org/abs/1906.11171v1)\] introduced a framework that distills ranking information from GNNs into binarized collaborative filtering models, leveraging binary representations to improve the efficiency of online recommendations. However, binary codes are inherently challenging to optimize and may lead to information loss during training \[[7' 2017-08-10](http://arxiv.org/abs/1708.03058v2)\]. To address these challenges, \[[8' 2020-10-04](http://arxiv.org/abs/2010.07027v5)\] proposed a novel framework that converts the binary constrained optimization problem into a continuous optimization problem, enabling the use of popular solvers like SGD and Adam.

Another line of research has focused on incorporating auxiliary information to enhance the performance of collaborative filtering models. For example, \[[9' 2024-08-05](http://arxiv.org/abs/2408.02404v1)\] introduced a framework that combines explicit and implicit graph collaborative filtering information through an attention mechanism and auxiliary loss functions, leading to improved representation learning. Similarly, \[[10' 2018-09-17](http://arxiv.org/abs/1809.06120v1)\] proposed a method that leverages textual information and pre-trained NLP models to initialize embeddings, effectively alleviating data sparsity issues in graph-based recommendation systems.

In addition, recent work has emphasized the importance of modeling embedding dimension correlations to improve the effectiveness of collaborative filtering. \[[11' 2023-03-15](http://arxiv.org/abs/2303.08537v3)\] introduced a framework that explicitly models pairwise and high-order correlations between embedding dimensions using outer products and convolutional neural networks, resulting in more accurate user-item interactions. This approach has been further extended in \[[12' 2021-02-04](http://arxiv.org/abs/2102.02549v2)\], where a dual-embedding framework was proposed to enhance user and item representations by incorporating historical interactions from both the user and their interacted items.
