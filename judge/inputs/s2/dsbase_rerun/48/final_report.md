Recent advancements in neural retrieval methods, particularly those leveraging transformer-based pre-trained language models, have significantly improved multilingual and cross-lingual retrieval capabilities \[[1' 2023-05-17](http://arxiv.org/abs/2305.15425v2)\]. However, the effectiveness of these models for low-resource, morphologically rich languages such as Amharic remains underexplored due to data scarcity and suboptimal tokenization strategies \[[1' 2023-05-17](http://arxiv.org/abs/2305.15425v2)\]. This challenge is not unique to Amharic; similar issues have been observed in other morphologically rich languages, where tokenization disparities can lead to unfair treatment in language services \[[2' 2024-02-22](http://arxiv.org/abs/2402.15010v2)\]. For instance, disparities in tokenization length can be as high as 15 times between languages, affecting cost, processing time, and content context \[[2' 2024-02-22](http://arxiv.org/abs/2402.15010v2)\].

The importance of tokenization in language models has been extensively studied. Subword tokenization, including methods like BPE and WordPiece, has become the standard in NLP, outperforming character and word-level tokenization \[[3' 2022-03-16](http://arxiv.org/abs/2203.08459v2)\]. However, the optimal segmentation granularity and the influence of morphological information in languages like French and Indo-European languages remain underexplored \[[3' 2022-03-16](http://arxiv.org/abs/2203.08459v2)\]. This is particularly relevant for biomedical terminology, where agglutinative structures and specific morpheme combinations are common \[[3' 2022-03-16](http://arxiv.org/abs/2203.08459v2)\]. In the case of morphologically rich languages, such as Hebrew, incorporating explicit morphological knowledge during pre-training has shown significant improvements in both semantic and morphological tasks \[[4' 2023-11-01](http://arxiv.org/abs/2311.00658v1)\].

For low-resource languages, the lack of sufficient training data and prior knowledge poses additional challenges. Techniques such as back translation and data augmentation have been used to generate synthetic data and improve translation performance \[[23' 2025-05-05](http://arxiv.org/abs/2505.02463v1)\].
