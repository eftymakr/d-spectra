## Related Works

The field of large language model (LLM) alignment and steering has seen significant advancements, particularly in the development of automated methods for identifying and modifying model behavior. Traditional approaches often rely on predefined templates or manual analysis to detect behaviors such as refusal, which limits their applicability and robustness \[[14' 2023-09-05](http://arxiv.org/abs/2309.02144v1)\]. Recent work has explored the use of cosine similarity for alignment tasks, highlighting its potential in measuring semantic similarity between embeddings \[[1' 2021-07-08](http://arxiv.org/abs/2107.04071v1)\]. However, cosine similarity is not a metric and does not satisfy the triangle inequality, which can limit its use in certain search and alignment scenarios \[[1' 2021-07-08](http://arxiv.org/abs/2107.04071v1)\]. To address these limitations, researchers have proposed modified cosine similarity metrics that account for data variance and covariance, leading to improved performance in tasks such as K-Nearest Neighbors \[[11' 2025-02-04](http://arxiv.org/abs/2502.02233v1)\]. These modifications are crucial for ensuring that cosine similarity remains a reliable measure in diverse data settings.

In the context of model steering, prior methods have focused on identifying refusal behaviors through output tokens or manual inspection, which can be both labor-intensive and limited in scope \[[14' 2023-09-05](http://arxiv.org/abs/2309.02144v1)\]. The introduction of COSMIC (Cosine Similarity Metrics for Inversion of Concepts) represents a significant advancement by leveraging cosine similarity to identify steering directions and target layers without relying on model outputs \[[14' 2023-09-05](http://arxiv.org/abs/2309.02144v1)\]. This approach is particularly valuable in adversarial settings and for weakly aligned models, where traditional methods may fail to detect or effectively modify behavior \[[14' 2023-09-05](http://arxiv.org/abs/2309.02144v1)\]. COSMIC's ability to steer models toward safer behavior with minimal increase in false refusals demonstrates its robustness across a wide range of alignment conditions \[[14' 2023-09-05](http://arxiv.org/abs/2309.02144v1)\].

The use of cosine similarity in alignment tasks has also been explored in the context of multi-modal semantic alignment, where traditional pairwise similarity measures are insufficient for handling an arbitrary number of vectors \[[3' 2025-05-06](http://arxiv.org/abs/2505.03532v1)\]. The Joint Generalized Cosine Similarity (JGCS) method, which is based on the Gram determinant, provides a more comprehensive approach to measuring similarity across multiple modalities \[[3' 2025-05-06](http://arxiv.org/abs/2505.03532v1)\]. This method has been applied in contrastive learning, showing improved performance in tasks such as image-text alignment \[[3' 2025-05-06](http://arxiv.org/abs/2505.03532v1)\]. Similarly, the Variance-Adjusted Cosine Distance (VACD) has been proposed to address the limitations of traditional cosine similarity by incorporating variance and covariance adjustments, leading to better performance in classification tasks \[[11' 2025-02-04](http://arxiv.org/abs/2502.02233v1)\].

In addition to cosine similarity, other alignment techniques have been explored, such as genetic algorithms for manipulating LLMs in black-box settings \[[2' 2023-09-04](http://arxiv.org/abs/2309.01446v4)\]. These methods aim to uncover model vulnerabilities and improve alignment with human intent, highlighting the importance of robust and automated alignment strategies
