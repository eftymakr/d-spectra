## Related Works

Nonverbal communication plays a crucial role in human interaction, encompassing gestures, facial expressions, and body language that convey intent and emotion \[[12' 2024-10-29](http://arxiv.org/abs/2410.21652v1)\]. However, existing large language models (LLMs) have limited capacity to incorporate these nonverbal elements, hindering the development of fully immersive conversational experiences \[[1' 2024-07-17](http://arxiv.org/abs/2407.12580v1)\]. This limitation has spurred research into multimodal models that can understand and generate nonverbal cues alongside text \[[12' 2024-10-29](http://arxiv.org/abs/2410.21652v1)\]. 

Several studies have explored the integration of nonverbal cues into language models. For instance, \[[12' 2024-10-29](http://arxiv.org/abs/2410.21652v1)\] discusses the importance of nonverbal cues in human-robot interaction and suggests that integrating these cues can foster more natural and accessible experiences. Similarly, \[[8' 2018-11-23](http://arxiv.org/abs/1811.09362v2)\] introduces the RAVEN model, which dynamically shifts word representations based on nonverbal behaviors, achieving competitive performance on sentiment analysis and emotion recognition tasks. These works highlight the necessity of incorporating nonverbal signals to enhance the understanding of human language \[[8' 2018-11-23](http://arxiv.org/abs/1811.09362v2)\].

The challenge of aligning linguistic and nonverbal information has also been addressed in vision-language pretraining. \[[2' 2023-08-24](http://arxiv.org/abs/2308.12898v2)\] investigates how linguistic knowledge, including semantics and syntax, impacts multimodal alignment. Their work introduces the SNARE benchmark to evaluate the comprehension of complex syntactic structures and semantic relationships in vision-language models. They find that models often rely on content words for sentence comprehension and struggle with negation logic and spatial relationships \[[2' 2023-08-24](http://arxiv.org/abs/2308.12898v2)\]. These findings underscore the importance of developing models that can effectively integrate both linguistic and nonverbal cues \[[2' 2023-08-24](http://arxiv.org/abs/2308.12898v2)\].

In addition to nonverbal cues, multimodal models have been developed to handle various tasks, such as video classification and visual question answering. \[[7' 2023-09-19](http://arxiv.org/abs/2309.10783v1)\] proposes a method that leverages large language models to generate detailed textual descriptions of visual and auditory modalities, enabling zero-shot video classification without additional fine-tuning. This approach demonstrates the potential of using textual descriptions as proxies for visual and auditory inputs \[[7' 2023-09-19](http://arxiv.org/abs/2309.10783v1)\]. Similarly, \[[15' 2018-05-08](http://arxiv.org/abs/1805.02997v1)\] introduces a deep learning model for fine-grained venue discovery from multimodal data, emphasizing the importance of cross-modal correlation for accurate search \[[15' 2018-05-08](http://arxiv.org/abs/1805.02997v1)\].

The development of large-scale datasets has also been critical in advancing research on multimodal models. \[[10' 2024-03-21](http://arxiv.org/abs/2403.14168v3)\] presents the M$^3$AV dataset, which includes audio-visual academic lectures with high-quality human annotations, supporting multiple recognition and understanding tasks. This dataset exemplifies the importance of annotated multimodal data for training and evaluating models \[[10' 2024-03-21](http://arxiv.org/abs/2403.14168v3)\].
