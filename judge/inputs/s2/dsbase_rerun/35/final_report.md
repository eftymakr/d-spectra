## Related Works

Protein conformation generation has seen significant advancements with the integration of deep learning and generative modeling techniques. Traditional methods, such as molecular dynamics (MD) simulations, are computationally intensive and often struggle with sampling rare events and equilibration times \[[1' 2024-03-21](http://arxiv.org/abs/2403.14088v2)\]. Recent approaches have shifted toward data-driven models, particularly denoising diffusion models, which offer more efficient and accurate generation of protein structures \[[2' 2022-09-30](http://arxiv.org/abs/2209.15611v2)\]. However, integrating physical constraints into these models remains a challenge, as standard energy-based objectives often lead to intractable optimization \[[3' 2025-05-23](http://arxiv.org/abs/2505.17478v2)\].

Several studies have explored the use of diffusion models for protein structure prediction. ConfDiff \[[1' 2024-03-21](http://arxiv.org/abs/2403.14088v2)\] introduces a force-guided SE(3) diffusion model that incorporates physical priors to generate high-fidelity protein conformations. Similarly, Protein structure generation via folding diffusion \[[2' 2022-09-30](http://arxiv.org/abs/2209.15611v2)\] presents a model that mirrors the native folding process by denoising from a random state to a stable folded structure. These models demonstrate the potential of diffusion-based approaches in capturing the complex conformational landscape of proteins.

The importance of energy-based models in protein conformation prediction has also been emphasized. Energy-based models (EBMs) operate at the atomic scale and are trained on crystallized protein data, achieving performance comparable to state-of-the-art methods like Rosetta \[[7' 2020-04-27](http://arxiv.org/abs/2004.13167v1)\]. These models capture physicochemical properties relevant to protein energy, providing a more interpretable and physically grounded approach \[[7' 2020-04-27](http://arxiv.org/abs/2004.13167v1)\]. The integration of energy functions into generative models is critical for ensuring the physical plausibility of generated structures.

Recent work on inverse protein folding has also addressed the challenge of generating valid amino acid sequences that fold into desired structures. MapDiff \[[8' 2024-12-10](http://arxiv.org/abs/2412.07815v2)\] introduces a mask-prior-guided denoising diffusion framework that incorporates structural information and residue interactions, improving the accuracy of sequence design. This approach demonstrates the potential of combining diffusion models with structural constraints to enhance the quality of generated sequences.

In addition, the role of conformational selection in protein binding and function has been explored. Conformational selection refers to the process by which ligands select specific protein conformations for binding or unbinding \[[12' 2014-09-09](http://arxiv.org/abs/1409.2584v1)\]. This concept is crucial for understanding the dynamic nature of protein interactions and has implications for drug design and structural biology.

### <a id='category-diffusion-models-in-protein-structure-prediction'></a>Diffusion Models in Protein Structure Prediction: 3 papers found.
Recent advances in diffusion models have significantly impacted protein structure prediction and conformation sampling, addressing limitations of traditional molecular dynamics (MD) simulations \[[1' 2024-03-21](http://arxiv.org/abs/2403.14088v2)\]. These models, such as ConfDiff \[[1' 2024-03-21](http://arxiv.org/abs/2403.14088v2)\] and the diffusion-based generative model presented in \[[2' 2022-09-30](http://arxiv.org/abs/2209.15611v2)\], leverage deep learning to generate diverse and physically plausible protein conformations. ConfDiff introduces a force-guided network that integrates physical prior knowledge, ensuring generated structures align with equilibrium distributions \[[1' 2024-03-21](http://arxiv.org/abs/2403.14088v2)\]. Similarly, the model described in \[[2' 2022-09-30](http://arxiv.org/abs/2209.15611v2)\] mirrors the native folding process by generating protein backbone structures through denoising, capturing the energetically favorable conformations proteins naturally adopt. This approach not only enhances realism but also simplifies the modeling of complex structural transformations by utilizing a transformer backbone \[[2' 2022-09-30](http://arxiv.org/abs/2209.15611v2)\]. Additionally, ConfRover \[[3' 2025-05-23](http://arxiv.org/abs/2505.17478v2)\] extends this paradigm by learning both conformational dynamics and static structures from MD trajectories, offering a unified framework for time-dependent and time-independent sampling. By incorporating an SE(3) diffusion model as a structure decoder, ConfRover enables the generation of conformations in continuous space, further improving the accuracy and flexibility of protein structure prediction \[[3' 2025-05-23](http://arxiv.org/abs/2505.17478v2)\]. These developments highlight the growing role of diffusion models in bridging the gap between computational methods and biological reality, offering new tools for structural biology and drug discovery.

### <a id='category-efficient-conformational-sampling-techniques'></a>Efficient Conformational Sampling Techniques: 3 papers found.
Efficient conformational sampling techniques have gained significant attention due to their critical role in understanding protein dynamics and improving drug discovery. Traditional methods, such as molecular dynamics simulations, are computationally expensive and often struggle with sampling equilibrium conformations \[[1' 2024-03-21](http://arxiv.org/abs/2403.14088v2)\]. In contrast, recent advances in deep generative models, particularly diffusion-based approaches, have shown promise in generating protein conformations more efficiently \[[4' 2024-10-24](http://arxiv.org/abs/2410.18403v2)\]. However, these methods often rely on diffusion processes within a 3D geometric space, which can be inefficient in terms of runtime and limited in exploring diverse ensemble modes \[[4' 2024-10-24](http://arxiv.org/abs/2410.18403v2)\]. To address these limitations, Structure Language Modeling (SLM) has been introduced as a novel framework that encodes protein structures into a compact latent space using a discrete variational auto-encoder, followed by conditional language modeling to capture sequence-specific conformation distributions \[[4' 2024-10-24](http://arxiv.org/abs/2410.18403v2)\]. This approach enables more efficient and interpretable exploration of diverse conformations, achieving a 20-100x speedup over existing methods \[[4' 2024-10-24](http://arxiv.org/abs/2410.18403v2)\]. Additionally, the stepwise assembly (SWA) protocol, based on a 'stepwise ansatz' strategy, has been developed to predict biopolymer structures at atomic resolution by enumerating residue-by-residue conformational possibilities, achieving sub-Angstrom accuracy for loops up to 24 residues in length \[[10' 2012-08-02](http://arxiv.org/abs/1208.2680v2)\]. These methods highlight the ongoing efforts to integrate physical supervision into data-driven models, improving the physical plausibility of generated structures and enhancing their utility in structural biology and drug discovery \[[1' 2024-03-21](http://arxiv.org/abs/2403.14088v2)\].

### <a id='category-challenges-in-physical-supervision-of-generative-models'></a>Challenges in Physical Supervision of Generative Models: 4 papers found.
The category "Challenges in Physical Supervision of Generative Models" highlights the difficulties in integrating physical constraints into generative models, particularly in scenarios where paired training data is scarce or where the forward models are unknown. For instance, in image reconstruction tasks, the lack of paired data hinders the training of neural networks, and denoising algorithms, including diffusion models, are proposed as a means to supervise training without such data \[[1' 2024-03-21](http://arxiv.org/abs/2403.14088v2)\]. Similarly, in semi-supervised 3D object detection, the challenge lies in generating reliable pseudo-labels for unlabeled point clouds, and diffusion models are leveraged to enhance the quality of these labels by simulating a denoising process \[[2' 2022-09-30](http://arxiv.org/abs/2209.15611v2)\]. Furthermore, the study of Denoising Diffusion Models (DDMs) reveals that while they are designed for image generation, their components are not all essential for self-supervised representation learning, suggesting that simpler models like Denoising Autoencoders (DAEs) could be more effective in certain contexts \[[3' 2025-05-23](http://arxiv.org/abs/2505.17478v2)\]. These findings underscore the broader challenge of aligning generative models with physical principles, as seen in the case of large language models, where alignment with human values remains a critical issue despite advances in adaptive training and inference-time methods \[[4' 2024-10-24](http://arxiv.org/abs/2410.18403v2)\]. Overall, these works emphasize the need for innovative approaches that bridge the gap between data-driven methods and physical supervision, ensuring that generative models can produce results that are both accurate and physically plausible.

In the field of structural biology and drug discovery, recent advancements have focused on improving the accuracy and efficiency of protein structure prediction and conformation sampling. Traditional methods rely on time-consuming molecular dynamics (MD) simulations, while newer approaches leverage generative modeling, particularly denoising diffusion models, to learn distributions over crystallographic structures \[[1' 2024-03-21](http://arxiv.org/abs/2403.14088v2)\]. However, integrating physical supervision into these data-driven models remains challenging, as standard energy-based objectives often lead to intractable optimization \[[1' 2024-03-21](http://arxiv.org/abs/2403.14088v2)\]. To address this, Energy-based Alignment (EBA) has been introduced, aligning generative models with feedback from physical models to balance conformational states based on energy differences \[[1' 2024-03-21](http://arxiv.org/abs/2403.14088v2)\]. This approach enhances the physical plausibility of generated structures, improving model predictions and holding promise for applications in structural biology and drug discovery \[[1' 2024-03-21](http://arxiv.org/abs/2403.14088v2)\]. Additionally, research has explored the role of local energetic frustration in protein molecules, where high local frustration often indicates functionally relevant regions such as binding or active sites \[[2' 2022-09-30](http://arxiv.org/abs/2209.15611v2)\]. Network approaches have also been employed to describe protein structure and dynamics, with classifications into cumulus-type and stratus-type networks influencing conformational changes and energy transmission processes \[[3' 2025-05-23](http://arxiv.org/abs/2505.17478v2)\]. Furthermore, methods such as DeepRank-GNN-esm have been developed to rank modeled protein-protein interaction (PPI) structures, aiding in the identification of near-native conformations \[[4' 2024-10-24](http://arxiv.org/abs/2410.18403v2)\]. These contributions collectively highlight the growing integration of physical principles and advanced modeling techniques to advance our understanding of protein behavior and function.

Advanced NMR experiments indicate that these conformational changes can occur in the absence of ligand molecules (or with bound ligands), and that the ligands may 'select' protein conformations for binding (or unbinding). In this review, we argue that this conformational selection requires transition times for ligand binding and unbinding that are small compared to the dwell times of proteins in different conformations, which is plausible for small ligand molecules. Such a separation of timescales leads to a decoupling and temporal ordering of binding/unbinding events and conformational changes. We propose that conformational-selection and induced-change processes (such as induced fit) are two sides of the same coin, because the temporal ordering is reversed in binding and unbinding direction. Conformational-selection processes can be characterized by a conformational excitation that occurs prior to a binding or unbinding event, while induced-change processes exhibit a characteristic conformational relaxation that occurs after a binding or unbinding event. We discuss how the ordering of events can be determined from relaxation rates and effective on- and off-rates determined in mixing experiments, and from the conformational exchange rates measured in advanced NMR or single-molecule FRET experiments. For larger ligand molecules such as peptides, conformational changes and binding events can be intricately coupled and exhibit aspects of conformational-selection and induced-change processes in both binding and unbinding direction.»
