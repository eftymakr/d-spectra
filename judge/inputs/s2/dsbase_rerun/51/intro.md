## Related Works

Federated unlearning has emerged as a critical research area within federated learning (FL), driven by the need to comply with privacy regulations such as the General Data Protection Regulation (GDPR) and the "right to be forgotten" \[[1' 2023-10-30](http://arxiv.org/abs/2310.19218v5)\]. This concept enables the selective removal of data contributions from FL models, ensuring that users can request the deletion of their data without compromising the global model's performance \[[2' 2024-03-04](http://arxiv.org/abs/2403.02437v3)\]. However, the development of effective and efficient federated unlearning methods remains challenging due to the inherent differences between centralized and distributed learning paradigms \[[3' 2024-01-19](http://arxiv.org/abs/2401.11018v1)\].

Several studies have explored the theoretical foundations and practical implementations of federated unlearning. For instance, \[[4' 2023-10-31](http://arxiv.org/abs/2310.20448v4)\] introduces a framework for exact federated unlearning that ensures the unlearned model is statistically indistinguishable from one trained without the deleted data. This work emphasizes communication efficiency and provable unlearning, making it a significant contribution to the field. Similarly, \[[5' 2024-01-10](http://arxiv.org/abs/2401.05146v3)\] proposes a method that allows for the removal of specific clients' contributions without requiring full model retraining, which is crucial for maintaining performance and reducing computational overhead.

The evaluation of federated unlearning methods has also been a focus of recent research. \[[6' 2022-11-21](http://arxiv.org/abs/2211.11656v5)\] highlights the limitations of existing evaluation practices, which often rely on single metrics, and advocates for a more comprehensive approach that includes multiple evaluation criteria to assess both under-unlearning and over-unlearning scenarios. This aligns with the findings of \[[7' 2020-03-09](http://arxiv.org/abs/2003.04247v2)\], which introduces a novel evaluation metric based on epistemic uncertainty to provide a more accurate assessment of unlearning success.

Verification mechanisms for federated unlearning have also been explored. \[[8' 2022-05-25](http://arxiv.org/abs/2205.12709v1)\] presents a framework called VeriFi that integrates federated unlearning with verification, allowing participants to independently verify the unlearning effect. This is particularly important in ensuring transparency and trust in FL systems. Additionally, \[[9' 2022-07-12](http://arxiv.org/abs/2207.05521v3)\] introduces a method that enables the verification of unlearning through the injection of carefully designed markers, which can be used to assess the model's performance on these markers.

The challenges of federated unlearning are further compounded by security concerns, such as poisoning attacks. \[[10' 2024-05-24](http://arxiv.org/abs/2405.15474v2)\] introduces a framework called UnlearnGuard that is designed to be robust against such attacks, ensuring the security of the unlearning process. This highlights the need for a holistic approach that considers both the effectiveness and security of federated unlearning methods.
