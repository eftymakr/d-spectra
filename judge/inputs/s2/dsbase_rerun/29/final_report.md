## Related Works

Maximum Inner Product Search (MIPS) has been extensively studied in recent years due to its critical role in recommendation systems, information retrieval, and high-dimensional data applications \[[1' 2025-03-10](http://arxiv.org/abs/2503.06882v2)\]. Early approaches to MIPS either relied solely on inner product similarity, which can lead to local optima and redundant computations \[[1' 2025-03-10](http://arxiv.org/abs/2503.06882v2)\], or reduced the problem to nearest neighbor search under the Euclidean metric through space projection, which often results in topology distortion and information loss \[[1' 2025-03-10](http://arxiv.org/abs/2503.06882v2)\]. These two paradigms have been considered as opposing strategies, but recent work suggests that they are not inherently mutually exclusive \[[1' 2025-03-10](http://arxiv.org/abs/2503.06882v2)\].

Several studies have explored approximate methods for MIPS. For instance, the BanditMIPS algorithm \[[6' 2022-12-14](http://arxiv.org/abs/2212.07551v3)\] introduces a randomized approach that estimates inner products by subsampling coordinates, achieving a complexity independent of the dimensionality $d$. This method is particularly effective in high-dimensional settings, as it avoids the $O(\sqrt(d))$ complexity of existing algorithms. Similarly, the Quantization-based Fast Inner Product Search \[[4' 2015-09-04](http://arxiv.org/abs/1509.01469v1)\] proposes a method that approximates inner products using quantized subspaces, eliminating the need for higher-dimensional feature space augmentation. This approach has shown significant improvements over existing state-of-the-art techniques on various datasets.

The work in \[[10' 2019-09-30](http://arxiv.org/abs/1909.13459v2)\] investigates the norm bias in the MIPS problem, highlighting that large-norm items are more likely to be the result of MIPS. This insight is leveraged in the ip-NSW+ algorithm, which introduces an angular proximity graph to improve search performance. Experimental results show that ip-NSW+ outperforms ip-NSW and provides more robust performance under different data distributions.

### <a id='category-approximate-mips-methods'></a>Approximate MIPS Methods: 3 papers found.
There has been substantial research on sub-linear time approximate algorithms for Maximum Inner Product Search (MIPS), with existing methods often relying on preprocessing or having limited control over suboptimality \[[1' 2025-03-10](http://arxiv.org/abs/2503.06882v2)\]. Techniques based on locality-sensitive hashing (LSH) and tree-based solutions have been widely investigated for approximate MIPS, but they may not always provide the desired balance between speed and accuracy \[[2' 2018-12-15](http://arxiv.org/abs/1812.06360v1)\]. More recently, approaches based on k-means clustering, particularly spherical k-means, have been proposed to reduce MIPS to a Maximum Cosine Similarity Search (MCSS), offering significant speedups while maintaining retrieval precision \[[3' 2017-06-05](http://arxiv.org/abs/1706.01449v3)\]. Additionally, IVF-based retrieval has shown promise for sparse MIPS, leveraging dimensionality reduction and clustering strategies to improve efficiency \[[4' 2015-09-04](http://arxiv.org/abs/1509.01469v1)\]. These methods highlight the ongoing efforts to develop faster and more robust approximate MIPS algorithms, with varying trade-offs between computational cost, accuracy, and adaptability to different data distributions \[[5' 2024-02-21](http://arxiv.org/abs/2402.13858v1)\].

### <a id='category-algorithmic-innovations-in-mips'></a>Algorithmic Innovations in MIPS: 17 papers found.
Recent advancements in Maximum Inner Product Search (MIPS) have focused on improving efficiency and accuracy, particularly in high-dimensional settings. Several approaches have been proposed to address the computational challenges of MIPS, such as the use of approximate methods, indexing structures, and hybrid techniques combining different similarity metrics. For example, BanditMIPS \[[4' 2015-09-04](http://arxiv.org/abs/1509.01469v1)\] introduces a randomized algorithm that reduces the complexity from $O(\sqrt(d))$ to $O(1)$, making it suitable for high-dimensional data by adaptively sampling coordinates. Similarly, the quantization-based approach in \[[2' 2018-12-15](http://arxiv.org/abs/1812.06360v1)\] enables fast approximate MIPS by decomposing database vectors into subspaces, avoiding the need for higher-dimensional feature space augmentation. Additionally, the introduction of the Ball-Cone Tree (BC-Tree) index in \[[3' 2017-06-05](http://arxiv.org/abs/1706.01449v3)\] enhances search efficiency for diversity-aware $k$MIPS by enabling data-dependent approximations. Furthermore, the work in \[[6' 2022-12-14](http://arxiv.org/abs/2212.07551v3)\] proposes a dimensionality reduction method, CEOs, which leverages extreme order statistics to estimate inner products with sublinear complexity. These methods highlight the ongoing efforts to balance search quality and computational efficiency, often through innovative indexing strategies or statistical modeling of data topology. The integration of multiple metrics, such as the Euclidean and inner product, has also been explored to enhance performance, as seen in the Metric-Amphibious Graph (MAG) and Adaptive Navigation with Metric Switch (ANMS) algorithms \[[1' 2025-03-10](http://arxiv.org/abs/2503.06882v2)\].
