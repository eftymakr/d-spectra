## Related Works

Generative retrieval has emerged as a promising paradigm in information retrieval, shifting from traditional index-based methods to end-to-end neural networks that directly generate document identifiers \[[1' 2023-06-22](http://arxiv.org/abs/2306.12756v1)\]. This approach leverages the capabilities of large language models (LLMs) to perform retrieval by generating relevant document identifiers through constrained decoding \[[1' 2023-06-22](http://arxiv.org/abs/2306.12756v1)\]. The generalization of generative retrieval models to out-of-distribution (OOD) data has been a focal point of recent research, highlighting the need for robustness in real-world applications \[[1' 2023-06-22](http://arxiv.org/abs/2306.12756v1)\]. 

Several studies have explored the limitations of generative retrieval, particularly in terms of constraint handling and decoding strategies. For instance, the work by \[[1' 2023-06-22](http://arxiv.org/abs/2306.12756v1)\] investigates the impact of constraints on the performance of generative retrieval models, deriving a lower bound for the error in terms of KL divergence between the ground-truth and model-predicted distributions. This aligns with broader efforts to improve the robustness of generative retrieval models against OOD scenarios \[[1' 2023-06-22](http://arxiv.org/abs/2306.12756v1)\]. 

Beam search, a common decoding strategy, has also been scrutinized for its effectiveness in generative retrieval. Research has shown that relying on marginal distributions during beam search may not be optimal, suggesting that alternative decoding strategies could enhance retrieval performance \[[1' 2023-06-22](http://arxiv.org/abs/2306.12756v1)\]. This finding is consistent with earlier studies that emphasized the importance of aligning token-level generation with document-level relevance \[[4' 2025-04-07](http://arxiv.org/abs/2504.05181v2)\]. 

The use of reinforcement learning for improving generative retrieval has also been explored. However, methods such as reinforcement learning from relevance feedback (RLRF) introduce significant computational complexity and instability \[[4' 2025-04-07](http://arxiv.org/abs/2504.05181v2)\]. In contrast, the proposed direct document relevance optimization (DDRO) simplifies the optimization pipeline by aligning token-level generation with document-level relevance through pairwise ranking \[[4' 2025-04-07](http://arxiv.org/abs/2504.05181v2)\]. This approach has demonstrated superior performance compared to reinforcement learning-based methods \[[4' 2025-04-07](http://arxiv.org/abs/2504.05181v2)\].

Additionally, the integration of nonparametric contextualized vocab embeddings has been proposed to enhance generative retrieval models. Nonparametric decoding (Np Decoding) allows models to utilize both parametric and nonparametric spaces, improving performance across multiple datasets \[[3' 2022-10-05](http://arxiv.org/abs/2210.02068v3)\]. This aligns with the broader goal of enhancing the generalization capabilities of generative retrieval models \[[1' 2023-06-22](http://arxiv.org/abs/2306.12756v1)\].

The challenges of hallucination in LLM-based generative retrieval have also been addressed. For example, frameworks that incorporate knowledge distillation and decision agents have been proposed to improve retrieval precision and reduce irrelevant outputs \[[7' 2025-03-27](http://arxiv.org/abs/2503.21098v3)\]. These approaches aim to ensure the credibility of generative retrieval in practical applications \[[7' 2025-03-27](http://arxiv.org/abs/2503.21098v3)\].
