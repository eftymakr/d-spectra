## Related Works

The growing adoption of cloud-native applications has led to increased interest in optimizing both resource and financial aspects of containerized systems. Prior research has primarily focused on system-level optimization strategies for cost and resource efficiency in containerized environments \[[1' 2023-07-12](http://arxiv.org/abs/2307.06318v1)\]. However, the analysis of network costs in Kubernetes clusters remains underexplored, despite the critical role of network usage in determining overall cloud expenditures \[[2' 2022-07-04](http://arxiv.org/abs/2207.01222v1)\]. This paper contributes to the emerging FinOps discipline by addressing the financial and operational aspects of managing network costs in cloud-native environments \[[3' 2018-05-30](http://arxiv.org/abs/1805.11750v2)\].

Several studies have investigated the scheduling and placement of containers in Kubernetes clusters to improve resource utilization and reduce costs. For instance, SAGE is a tool designed to compute optimal deployment plans by analyzing application demands and cluster resources, surpassing other schedulers in efficiency and consistency \[[4' 2012-07-11](http://arxiv.org/abs/1207.2799v1)\]. Similarly, KubeAdaptor introduces a docking framework to ensure consistency in task scheduling order between workflow systems and Kubernetes, improving execution efficiency \[[5' 2021-06-07](http://arxiv.org/abs/2106.03998v1)\]. These works highlight the importance of scheduling and resource allocation in optimizing containerized environments.

In the realm of distributed optimization, asynchronous methods have been proposed to improve communication efficiency in multi-agent systems. For example, an asynchronous distributed gradient-descent algorithm has been developed to tolerate failures and reduce waiting times, leading to improved performance in distributed machine learning tasks \[[6' 2020-01-06](http://arxiv.org/abs/2001.01357v3)\]. These approaches are relevant to the broader challenge of optimizing cloud-based applications, where communication overhead and latency can significantly impact cost and performance.

Network cost analysis has also been explored in the context of infrastructure recovery and anomaly detection. For example, optimal recovery of damaged infrastructure networks has been studied, focusing on minimizing the cost of installing or recovering a given network based on its structure and the sequence of node installation \[[7' 2019-05-08](http://arxiv.org/abs/1905.03193v1)\]. Similarly, network volume anomaly detection has been addressed using time-structured traffic tensors and low-rank subspace tracking, enabling efficient identification of abnormal flows \[[8' 2016-08-19](http://arxiv.org/abs/1608.05493v1)\]. These techniques are valuable for understanding and managing network costs in large-scale cloud environments.

In addition, the analysis of consumer expenditure and budget shares has been studied to improve cost-effectiveness in survey methodologies. A recent work proposes an approach that assumes uniform prices across sampling units to reduce survey costs while maintaining inferential power \[[9' 2015-07-20](http://arxiv.org/abs/1507.05492v1)\]. This is relevant to the broader challenge of optimizing financial aspects in cloud environments, where accurate cost modeling is essential for effective resource management.
