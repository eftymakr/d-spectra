## Related Works

Open-vocabulary object detection (OVD) has gained significant attention in recent years due to its ability to detect objects from a large or even unbounded vocabulary, beyond the categories seen during training \[[1' 2024-04-14](http://arxiv.org/abs/2404.09216v1)\]. This capability is particularly useful in real-world scenarios where the set of object categories is not fixed or fully known \[[2' 2024-05-30](http://arxiv.org/abs/2405.19854v1)\]. However, existing methods often face challenges in accurately detecting objects when the vocabulary is either too broad or mis-specified, which can negatively impact performance \[[3' 2022-11-27](http://arxiv.org/abs/2211.14843v1)\]. This section reviews related works that have contributed to the development of open-vocabulary object detection and related tasks.

Early approaches to open-vocabulary detection relied on zero-shot learning, where models were trained on a predefined set of categories and then generalized to unseen objects using semantic embeddings \[[4' 2024-11-27](http://arxiv.org/abs/2411.18207v4)\]. These methods typically used pre-trained vision-language models like CLIP to bridge the gap between visual and textual representations \[[5' 2021-11-18](http://arxiv.org/abs/2111.09452v3)\]. However, they often struggled with accurate localization and classification of novel objects \[[6' 2025-04-13](http://arxiv.org/abs/2504.09480v1)\].

Recent works have focused on improving the alignment between visual and linguistic representations to enhance detection accuracy \[[7' 2023-12-19](http://arxiv.org/abs/2312.12437v1)\]. For example, DetCLIPv3 introduces a robust open-set detection framework that integrates a caption head to generate hierarchical labels for detected objects \[[8' 2023-11-20](http://arxiv.org/abs/2311.11646v4)\]. This approach is complemented by high-information-density data, generated through an auto-annotation pipeline that leverages visual large language models \[[9' 2022-07-07](http://arxiv.org/abs/2207.03482v3)\]. Similarly, RTGen proposes a method to generate scalable region-text pairs, which are crucial for training open-vocabulary detectors \[[10' 2024-06-17](http://arxiv.org/abs/2406.11739v1)\].

Another line of research has explored the use of pseudo labels to improve detection performance without requiring extensive manual annotations \[[11' 2024-10-11](http://arxiv.org/abs/2410.08645v1)\]. For instance, the Pseudo Bounding-Box Labels method generates pseudo labels from large-scale image-caption pairs, enabling models to generalize to novel object categories \[[12' 2022-06-22](http://arxiv.org/abs/2206.11134v4)\]. This approach has shown promising results on benchmark datasets like COCO and LVIS \[[13' 2024-05-16](http://arxiv.org/abs/2405.10053v1)\].

Furthermore, there has been growing interest in addressing the limitations of open-vocabulary detection in real-world scenarios, such as handling out-of-distribution objects and improving robustness to background noise \[[14' 2024-06-26](http://arxiv.org/abs/2406.18115v1)\]. The BIRDet method, for example, addresses the limitations of CLIP in handling background samples by introducing a dynamic background embedding that enhances the model's ability to classify oversized regions as background \[[15' 2023-06-28](http://arxiv.org/abs/2306.15880v4)\].

In addition to detection, there has been research on open-vocabulary segmentation and attribute detection, which extend the capabilities of vision-language models to more complex tasks \[[16' 2022-05-11](http://arxiv.org/abs/2205.05543v1)\]. For instance, the Open-Vocabulary Attribute Detection (OVAD) task focuses on detecting object-level attributes
