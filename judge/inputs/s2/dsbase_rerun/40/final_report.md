## Related Works

Zero-Knowledge Proofs (ZKPs) have emerged as a critical tool in privacy-preserving and verifiable computing, enabling a prover to demonstrate the truth of a statement without revealing additional information \[[1' 2024-08-12](http://arxiv.org/abs/2408.05890v1)\]. Despite their potential, ZKPs have not yet achieved widespread adoption due to the computational complexity of the proving process \[[1' 2024-08-12](http://arxiv.org/abs/2408.05890v1)\]. Recent efforts have focused on accelerating key primitives of ZKP protocols using hardware, particularly GPUs and ASICs \[[1' 2024-08-12](http://arxiv.org/abs/2408.05890v1)\]. However, existing approaches face significant limitations, such as the requirement for a trusted setup for each application or the generation of larger proof sizes with higher verification costs, which restricts their use in scenarios with numerous verifiers or strict verification time constraints \[[1' 2024-08-12](http://arxiv.org/abs/2408.05890v1)\].

Several works have explored hardware acceleration for ZKP protocols. For instance, SZKP presents a scalable ASIC accelerator that achieves speedups of over 400×, 3×, and 12× over CPU, ASIC, and GPU implementations by optimizing the Number Theoretic Transform (NTT) and Multi-scalar Multiplication (MSM) primitives \[[2' 2024-11-10](http://arxiv.org/abs/2411.06350v1)\]. Similarly, if-ZKP introduces an FPGA-based architecture for accelerating zk-SNARK prover computations, focusing on MSM and achieving speedups of 110×–150× over reference software libraries \[[3' 2024-12-17](http://arxiv.org/abs/2412.12481v1)\]. These works highlight the importance of optimizing key primitives such as MSM and NTT for performance improvements.

In addition to hardware acceleration, there has been research into ZK-friendly hash functions, such as MiMC, which are well-suited for ZKP applications due to their low complexity and security \[[4' 2024-03-23](http://arxiv.org/abs/2403.15676v5)\]. AMAZE, a hardware-optimized framework for MiMC, outperforms standard CPU implementations by more than 13×, enabling efficient ZK-friendly hashing on resource-constrained devices \[[4' 2024-03-23](http://arxiv.org/abs/2403.15676v5)\]. These efforts demonstrate the need for specialized hardware to support the cryptographic operations required by ZKP systems.

The challenge of verifying ZKP circuits has also been addressed in prior work. AC4 introduces a novel approach for pinpointing bugs in ZKP circuits by encoding constraints into polynomial equations and solving them over finite fields \[[5' 2021-04-06](http://arxiv.org/abs/2104.02251v1)\]. This method improves the accuracy and efficiency of circuit verification, addressing issues such as under-constrained or over-constrained circuits \[[5' 2021-04-06](http://arxiv.org/abs/2104.02251v1)\]. These tools are essential for ensuring the correctness of ZKP systems.

### <a id='category-hardware-acceleration-for-zkp-primitives'></a>Hardware Acceleration for ZKP Primitives: 3 papers found.
Hardware acceleration for zero-knowledge proof (ZKP) primitives has emerged as a critical area of research to address the computational inefficiencies inherent in ZKP generation. Key primitives such as the Number Theoretic Transform (NTT) and Multi-scalar Multiplication (MSM) are particularly resource-intensive, with NTTs involving irregular memory accesses and MSMs requiring data-dependent memory operations \[[1' 2024-08-12](http://arxiv.org/abs/2408.05890v1)\]. Prior efforts have explored GPU and custom RTL implementations to accelerate these operations, but these approaches often struggle with the complex dataflow patterns required for efficient execution \[[1' 2024-08-12](http://arxiv.org/abs/2408.05890v1)\]. To overcome these limitations, SZKP was introduced as the first ASIC to accelerate the entire proof generation process on-chip, leveraging structured dataflows for both NTTs and MSMs \[[1' 2024-08-12](http://arxiv.org/abs/2408.05890v1)\]. This approach achieved significant speedups, with conservative full-proof improvements of over 400×, 3×, and 12× compared to CPU, ASIC, and GPU implementations, respectively \[[1' 2024-08-12](http://arxiv.org/abs/2408.05890v1)\]. Similarly, the need for efficient cryptographic hashing in ZKP protocols has led to the development of ZK-friendly hash functions like MiMC, which are optimized for both software and hardware \[[2' 2024-11-10](http://arxiv.org/abs/2411.06350v1)\]. The AMAZE framework, designed for resource-constrained edge devices, demonstrates that hardware-optimized MiMC implementations can outperform standard CPU implementations by more than 13× \[[2' 2024-11-10](http://arxiv.org/abs/2411.06350v1)\]. These advancements highlight the growing importance of hardware acceleration in improving the performance and practicality of ZKP systems, particularly in scenarios where computational efficiency is paramount \[[1' 2024-08-12](http://arxiv.org/abs/2408.05890v1)\]\[[2' 2024-11-10](http://arxiv.org/abs/2411.06350v1)\].

[Snippet]: «By how much must the communication complexity of a function increase if we demand that the parties not only correctly compute the function but also return all registers (other than the one containing the answer) to their initial states at the end of the communication protocol? Protocols that achieve this are referred to as clean and the associated cost as the clean communication complexity. Here we present clean protocols for calculating the Inner Product of two $n$-bit strings, showing that (in the absence of pre-shared entanglement) at most $n+3$ qubits or $n+O(\sqrt(n))$ bits of communication are required. The quantum protocol provides inspiration for obtaining the optimal method to implement distributed CNOT gates in parallel whilst minimizing the amount of quantum communication. For more general functions, we show that nearly all Boolean functions require close to $2n$ bits of classical communication to compute and close to $n$ qubits if the parties have access to pre-shared entanglement.
