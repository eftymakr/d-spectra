## Related Works

Zero-Knowledge Proofs (ZKPs) have emerged as a critical tool in privacy-preserving and verifiable computing, enabling a prover to demonstrate the truth of a statement without revealing additional information \[[1' 2024-08-12](http://arxiv.org/abs/2408.05890v1)\]. Despite their potential, ZKPs have not yet achieved widespread adoption due to the computational complexity of the proving process \[[1' 2024-08-12](http://arxiv.org/abs/2408.05890v1)\]. Recent efforts have focused on accelerating key primitives of ZKP protocols using hardware, particularly GPUs and ASICs \[[1' 2024-08-12](http://arxiv.org/abs/2408.05890v1)\]. However, existing approaches face significant limitations, such as the requirement for a trusted setup for each application or the generation of larger proof sizes with higher verification costs, which restricts their use in scenarios with numerous verifiers or strict verification time constraints \[[1' 2024-08-12](http://arxiv.org/abs/2408.05890v1)\].

Several works have explored hardware acceleration for ZKP protocols. For instance, SZKP presents a scalable ASIC accelerator that achieves speedups of over 400×, 3×, and 12× over CPU, ASIC, and GPU implementations by optimizing the Number Theoretic Transform (NTT) and Multi-scalar Multiplication (MSM) primitives \[[2' 2024-11-10](http://arxiv.org/abs/2411.06350v1)\]. Similarly, if-ZKP introduces an FPGA-based architecture for accelerating zk-SNARK prover computations, focusing on MSM and achieving speedups of 110×–150× over reference software libraries \[[3' 2024-12-17](http://arxiv.org/abs/2412.12481v1)\]. These works highlight the importance of optimizing key primitives such as MSM and NTT for performance improvements.

In addition to hardware acceleration, there has been research into ZK-friendly hash functions, such as MiMC, which are well-suited for ZKP applications due to their low complexity and security \[[4' 2024-03-23](http://arxiv.org/abs/2403.15676v5)\]. AMAZE, a hardware-optimized framework for MiMC, outperforms standard CPU implementations by more than 13×, enabling efficient ZK-friendly hashing on resource-constrained devices \[[4' 2024-03-23](http://arxiv.org/abs/2403.15676v5)\]. These efforts demonstrate the need for specialized hardware to support the cryptographic operations required by ZKP systems.

The challenge of verifying ZKP circuits has also been addressed in prior work. AC4 introduces a novel approach for pinpointing bugs in ZKP circuits by encoding constraints into polynomial equations and solving them over finite fields \[[5' 2021-04-06](http://arxiv.org/abs/2104.02251v1)\]. This method improves the accuracy and efficiency of circuit verification, addressing issues such as under-constrained or over-constrained circuits \[[5' 2021-04-06](http://arxiv.org/abs/2104.02251v1)\]. These tools are essential for ensuring the correctness of ZKP systems.
