## Related Works

Narrative summarization, particularly for long-form texts such as books, movies, and TV scripts, remains a challenging task due to the complexity of plotlines, character interactions, and thematic coherence \[[1' 2020-04-27](http://arxiv.org/abs/2004.12727v1)\]. Existing large language models (LLMs) often struggle with these tasks, as they may fail to capture the full depth and nuance of the narrative \[[10' 2024-11-24](http://arxiv.org/abs/2411.15993v2)\]. This section reviews related works that address these challenges, focusing on narrative structure detection, multi-agent collaboration, and long-form text generation.

Several studies have explored the detection of narrative structures in texts. For instance, the CompRes dataset was introduced to capture narrative elements in news articles, using a modified version of Labov and Waletzky’s narrative theory \[[4' 2020-07-09](http://arxiv.org/abs/2007.04874v2)\]. This work emphasized the importance of identifying key narrative components such as complication and resolution, which are critical for accurate summarization. Similarly, the paper on screenplays proposed the use of latent narrative events (turning points) to improve summarization performance \[[1' 2020-04-27](http://arxiv.org/abs/2004.12727v1)\]. These latent structures help in capturing the essence of a narrative, which is essential for generating coherent summaries.

Multi-agent systems have also been explored for complex problem-solving tasks. The paper on Hanabi highlights the role of conventions in improving multi-agent cooperation, showing that human-like strategies can significantly enhance performance \[[6' 2024-12-09](http://arxiv.org/abs/2412.06333v3)\]. Similarly, the TradingAgents framework demonstrates how multi-agent LLMs can be used for financial trading, leveraging specialized roles and collaborative dynamics \[[17' 2024-12-28](http://arxiv.org/abs/2412.20138v7)\]. These studies highlight the potential of multi-agent systems in tackling complex, structured tasks, which is a key innovation in the proposed NexusSum framework.

In the context of long-form text generation, the paper on factuality in LLMs reveals that factuality tends to decline in later sentences, accompanied by an increase in unsupported claims \[[10' 2024-11-24](http://arxiv.org/abs/2411.15993v2)\]. This underscores the need for structured approaches to ensure coherence and accuracy in long-form outputs. The Dial2Desc system, which generates concise descriptions from dialogues, offers a different approach to dialogue summarization by focusing on the object or action being discussed rather than maintaining the natural flow of the conversation \[[7' 2018-11-01](http://arxiv.org/abs/1811.00185v1)\]. This method aligns with the preprocessing step in NexusSum, which standardizes dialogue and descriptive text for improved coherence.

Additionally, the paper on temporal search for long-form video understanding highlights the challenges of efficiently extracting relevant information from long videos \[[5' 2025-04-03](http://arxiv.org/abs/2504.02259v3)\]. While not directly related to text summarization, the emphasis on structured search and efficient information retrieval is relevant to the structured pipeline in NexusSum.
