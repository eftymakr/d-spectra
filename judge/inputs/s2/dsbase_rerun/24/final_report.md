Diffusion transformers (DiT) have emerged as a powerful architecture for generating high-quality images and videos from textual descriptions \[[1' 2024-06-04](http://arxiv.org/abs/2406.02540v3)\]. However, their large parameter counts and computational demands hinder their deployment on resource-constrained devices \[[1' 2024-06-04](http://arxiv.org/abs/2406.02540v3)\]. Quantization, which reduces the bit-width of model parameters, offers a promising solution to alleviate these challenges by decreasing memory usage and improving inference speed \[[4' 2025-05-08](http://arxiv.org/abs/2505.05530v1)\]. Despite its success in image generation, quantization methods for video generation remain underexplored \[[1' 2024-06-04](http://arxiv.org/abs/2406.02540v3)\]. This section reviews relevant works that address quantization in diffusion models and video generation tasks.

Several studies have investigated quantization techniques for diffusion models. The method identifies key difficulties, including the changing output distributions of noise estimation networks over time steps and the bimodal activation distribution of shortcut layers. It introduces timestep-aware calibration and split shortcut quantization to mitigate these issues, achieving 4-bit quantization with minimal performance degradation \[[3' 2023-02-08](http://arxiv.org/abs/2302.04304v3)\].
