## Related Works

Diffusion transformers (DiT) have emerged as a powerful architecture for generating high-quality images and videos from textual descriptions \[[1' 2024-06-04](http://arxiv.org/abs/2406.02540v3)\]. However, their large parameter counts and computational demands hinder their deployment on resource-constrained devices \[[1' 2024-06-04](http://arxiv.org/abs/2406.02540v3)\]. Quantization, which reduces the bit-width of model parameters, offers a promising solution to alleviate these challenges by decreasing memory usage and improving inference speed \[[4' 2025-05-08](http://arxiv.org/abs/2505.05530v1)\]. Despite its success in image generation, quantization methods for video generation remain underexplored \[[1' 2024-06-04](http://arxiv.org/abs/2406.02540v3)\]. This section reviews relevant works that address quantization in diffusion models and video generation tasks.

Several studies have investigated quantization techniques for diffusion models. Q-Diffusion \[[3' 2023-02-08](http://arxiv.org/abs/2302.04304v3)\] proposes a novel post-training quantization (PTQ) method tailored for diffusion models, addressing the unique challenges posed by their multi-timestep pipeline and model architecture. The method identifies key difficulties, including the changing output distributions of noise estimation networks over time steps and the bimodal activation distribution of shortcut layers. It introduces timestep-aware calibration and split shortcut quantization to mitigate these issues, achieving 4-bit quantization with minimal performance degradation \[[3' 2023-02-08](http://arxiv.org/abs/2302.04304v3)\]. Similarly, ViDiT-Q \[[1' 2024-06-04](http://arxiv.org/abs/2406.02540v3)\] focuses on quantizing diffusion transformers for image and video generation, addressing the challenges of text-to-image and video tasks by introducing an improved quantization scheme. It validates the effectiveness of its approach across various models, achieving W8A8 and W4A8 with negligible visual quality degradation \[[1' 2024-06-04](http://arxiv.org/abs/2406.02540v3)\].

For video diffusion models, QVD \[[2' 2024-07-16](http://arxiv.org/abs/2407.11585v2)\] introduces the first PTQ strategy specifically designed for video tasks. It addresses the unique challenges of temporal features and inter-channel disparities by proposing High Temporal Discriminability Quantization (HTDQ) and Scattered Channel Range Integration (SCRI). These methods aim to preserve high discriminability of quantized features and improve coverage of quantization levels, respectively. QVD achieves near-lossless performance degradation on W8A8, outperforming existing methods by 205.12 in FVD \[[2' 2024-07-16](http://arxiv.org/abs/2407.11585v2)\].

In addition to quantization, several works have explored temporal consistency in video generation. Temporally Consistent Semantic Video Editing \[[9' 2022-06-21](http://arxiv.org/abs/2206.10590v1)\] presents a method to ensure temporal coherence during video editing by minimizing photometric inconsistency through joint optimization of latent codes and pre-trained generators. Similarly, Upscale-A-Video \[[24' 2023-12-11](http://arxiv.org/abs/2312.06640v1)\] introduces a text-guided latent diffusion framework for video upscaling, ensuring temporal coherence through local and global mechanisms.
