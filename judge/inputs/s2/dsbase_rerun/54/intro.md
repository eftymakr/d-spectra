## Related Works

Graph Convolutional Networks (GCNs) have become a cornerstone in collaborative filtering (CF) for recommendation systems, due to their ability to model complex interactions between users and items \[[1' 2021-07-10](http://arxiv.org/abs/2107.04755v3)\]. However, existing GCN-based methods face several limitations. One major issue is the embedding layer, which often results in a latent space with limited capacity, failing to capture the rich, locally observed preference patterns \[[1' 2021-07-10](http://arxiv.org/abs/2107.04755v3)\]. This limitation is closely tied to the use of linear graph filters that restrict the model's ability to learn non-linear relationships \[[1' 2021-07-10](http://arxiv.org/abs/2107.04755v3)\]. Additionally, neighborhood aggregation in GCNs is typically limited in its capacity to fine-grained preference patterns, as it often aggregates information in a uniform manner without considering the diversity of interactions \[[1' 2021-07-10](http://arxiv.org/abs/2107.04755v3)\].

These challenges are not unique to the current work but have been identified in prior research. For instance, \[[2' 2020-03-08](http://arxiv.org/abs/2003.03777v5)\] highlights that many GCN-based models suffer from the "over-smoothing" problem, where node representations become indistinguishable after multiple layers of aggregation. This issue is exacerbated by the lack of non-linear activation functions, which can hinder the model's ability to capture complex patterns \[[2' 2020-03-08](http://arxiv.org/abs/2003.03777v5)\]. Similarly, \[[3' 2020-01-01](http://arxiv.org/abs/2001.00267v1)\] points out that existing graph convolutional filters are essentially low-pass filters, which ignore the potentially useful middle and high frequency bands of graph signals. This limitation restricts the ability of models to capture a full spectrum of information, leading to suboptimal performance \[[3' 2020-01-01](http://arxiv.org/abs/2001.00267v1)\].

To address these issues, several works have proposed alternative approaches. For example, \[[4' 2022-09-06](http://arxiv.org/abs/2209.02544v4)\] introduces a multi-graph convolution collaborative filtering framework that explicitly incorporates multiple graphs in the embedding learning process, allowing for more expressive modeling of high-order interactions \[[4' 2022-09-06](http://arxiv.org/abs/2209.02544v4)\]. The work also integrates proximal information through user-user and item-item graphs, enhancing the model's ability to capture nuanced relationships \[[4' 2022-09-06](http://arxiv.org/abs/2209.02544v4)\]. Another approach, \[[5' 2019-01-05](http://arxiv.org/abs/1901.01343v7)\], proposes a graph neural network with convolutional ARMA filters, which provides a more flexible frequency response and better captures the global graph structure compared to polynomial-based filters \[[5' 2019-01-05](http://arxiv.org/abs/1901.01343v7)\].

Moreover, \[[6' 2020-01-28](http://arxiv.org/abs/2001.10167v1)\] explores the use of linear residual graph convolutional networks to alleviate the over-smoothing problem, demonstrating that removing non-linearities can enhance recommendation performance \[[6' 2020-01-28](http://arxiv.org/abs/2001.10167v1)\]. This finding aligns with the current work's approach of using a linear model to avoid the limitations of non-linear activation functions \[[1' 2021-07-10](http://arxiv.org/abs/2107.04755v3)\]. Additionally, \[[7' 2020-04-30](http://arxiv.org/abs/2004.14734v1)\] introduces a robust hierarchical GCN model that mitigates training difficulties and improves robustness against adversarial attacks by separately aggregating messages from different order neighborhoods \[[7' 2020-04-30](http://arxiv.org/abs/2004.14734v1)\].
