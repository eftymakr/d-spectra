## Related Works

Video super-resolution (VSR) has been extensively studied, with a growing focus on real-world scenarios where videos are often degraded due to compression, noise, and other factors. Early methods primarily relied on 2D CNNs with early fusion or 3D CNNs with slow fusion, but these struggled to maintain temporal consistency across frames \[[5' 2020-08-13](http://arxiv.org/abs/2008.05765v2)\]. Recurrent Neural Networks (RNNs) were introduced to model temporal dependencies, but they often suffered from instability during training and introduced artifacts \[[5' 2020-08-13](http://arxiv.org/abs/2008.05765v2)\]. To address these limitations, the Recurrent Residual Network (RRN) was proposed, which uses residual learning to stabilize training and improve performance, achieving state-of-the-art results on several benchmarks \[[5' 2020-08-13](http://arxiv.org/abs/2008.05765v2)\].

Recent advances in diffusion models have shown promise for VSR due to their ability to generate high-quality, realistic images \[[19' 2023-12-04](http://arxiv.org/abs/2312.02246v4)\]. However, diffusion-based VSR methods are prone to introducing artifacts and inconsistencies due to the inherent randomness in the denoising process \[[1' 2025-02-11](http://arxiv.org/abs/2502.07381v3)\]. To mitigate these issues, several works have focused on improving temporal consistency and reducing artifacts. For example, the Spatial Degradation-Aware and Temporal Consistent (SDATC) diffusion model incorporates a distortion control module and a spatio-temporal attention module to refine the generation process \[[1' 2025-02-11](http://arxiv.org/abs/2502.07381v3)\]. Similarly, the Upscale-A-Video framework introduces a flow-guided recurrent latent propagation module to enhance overall video stability \[[3' 2023-12-11](http://arxiv.org/abs/2312.06640v1)\].

Another challenge in real-world VSR is the presence of unknown and complex degradations, which existing models struggle to simulate effectively \[[7' 2023-05-24](http://arxiv.org/abs/2305.14669v3)\]. To address this, the NegVSR method introduces a negatives augmentation strategy that expands the degeneration domain and improves robustness against out-of-distribution noise \[[7' 2023-05-24](http://arxiv.org/abs/2305.14669v3)\]. Additionally, the STAR framework leverages text-to-video (T2V) models to enhance spatio-temporal quality by introducing a Local Information Enhancement Module (LIEM) and a Dynamic Frequency (DF) Loss to reinforce fidelity \[[4' 2025-01-06](http://arxiv.org/abs/2501.02976v1)\].

To further improve the efficiency and quality of VSR, the Zooming SlowMo framework proposes a one-stage approach that directly reconstructs high-resolution slow-motion videos from low-resolution and low-frame-rate inputs, avoiding the limitations of two-stage methods \[[12' 2021-04-15](http://arxiv.org/abs/2104.07473v1)\]. The BSRT method, which integrates Swin Transformer and flow-guided deformable alignment, has also shown significant improvements in burst super-resolution tasks \[[18' 2022-04-18](http://arxiv.org/abs/2204.08332v2)\].
