## Related Works

Federated Learning (FL) has emerged as a promising paradigm for training machine learning models on distributed data while preserving privacy \[[1' 2020-04-24](http://arxiv.org/abs/2004.11794v2)\]. Early works focused on the challenges of communication efficiency and client heterogeneity \[[4' 2022-06-20](http://arxiv.org/abs/2206.10032v4)\]. For instance, \[[4' 2022-06-20](http://arxiv.org/abs/2206.10032v4)\] introduced a variant of the classic federated averaging (FedAvg) algorithm that supports data and client heterogeneity, partial client asynchrony, and communication compression, achieving fast convergence on large-scale setups. This work laid the foundation for addressing the practical challenges of FL at scale.

The problem of data heterogeneity, where client data distributions differ significantly, has been extensively studied. \[[5' 2025-04-15](http://arxiv.org/abs/2504.11216v2)\] proposed FEDDIVERSE, a novel client selection algorithm that manages and leverages data heterogeneity across clients by promoting collaboration between clients with complementary data distributions. This approach enhances performance while maintaining low communication and computational overhead. Similarly, \[[12' 2022-05-25](http://arxiv.org/abs/2205.12493v3)\] introduced a unified framework for self-supervised learning in heterogeneous settings, enabling collaborative representation learning without requiring labeled data. This work demonstrated that local training and alignment procedures can be used to achieve task-independent models that outperform state-of-the-art methods.

In the context of semi-supervised learning, \[[19' 2020-06-22](http://arxiv.org/abs/2006.12097v3)\] proposed FedMatch, a method that improves upon naive combinations of federated learning and semi-supervised learning by introducing an inter-client consistency loss and decomposing parameters for disjoint learning on labeled and unlabeled data. This approach outperformed both local semi-supervised learning and baselines that naively combined federated learning with semi-supervised learning. Additionally, \[[13' 2022-06-01](http://arxiv.org/abs/2206.00686v2)\] proposed FedDPMS, an FL algorithm that uses differentially private synthetic data to mitigate the effects of data heterogeneity without compromising privacy. This method demonstrated superior performance in deep image classification tasks compared to existing FL methods.

### <a id='category-semi-supervised-learning-in-fl'></a>Semi-Supervised Learning in FL: 2 papers found.
The category of Semi-Supervised Learning in Federated Learning (FL) addresses the limitations of traditional supervised FL approaches, which often assume fully labeled data on clients \[[19' 2020-06-22](http://arxiv.org/abs/2006.12097v3)\]. In reality, data obtained at the client-side is frequently unlabeled due to high annotation costs or the lack of expert knowledge \[[19' 2020-06-22](http://arxiv.org/abs/2006.12097v3)\]. This has led to the emergence of Federated Semi-Supervised Learning (FSSL), a framework that leverages both labeled and unlabeled data across clients to improve model performance while maintaining data privacy \[[19' 2020-06-22](http://arxiv.org/abs/2006.12097v3)\]. Existing studies have primarily focused on homogeneous datasets, but real-world scenarios often involve heterogeneous data distributions, making it challenging to apply standard FL techniques \[[1' 2020-04-24](http://arxiv.org/abs/2004.11794v2)\]. This has prompted research into more robust methods that can handle the complexities of semi-supervised learning in FL, such as the introduction of inter-client consistency losses and parameter decomposition strategies to enable disjoint learning on labeled and unlabeled data \[[19' 2020-06-22](http://arxiv.org/abs/2006.12097v3)\]. These approaches aim to better exploit the vast amounts of unlabelled data available on mobile devices, which remain underutilized in most FL frameworks \[[1' 2020-04-24](http://arxiv.org/abs/2004.11794v2)\]. The development of such methods is critical for advancing FL towards more practical and scalable applications in domains like human activity recognition, where labeled data is scarce and heterogeneous \[[1' 2020-04-24](http://arxiv.org/abs/2004.11794v2)\].

### <a id='category-data-heterogeneity-management'></a>Data Heterogeneity Management: 3 papers found.
Federated Learning (FL) has emerged as a promising approach for training machine learning models on decentralized data while preserving privacy, yet it faces significant challenges due to data heterogeneity \[[1' 2020-04-24](http://arxiv.org/abs/2004.11794v2)\]. In real-world FL settings, client data is often non-identically distributed and imbalanced, leading to statistical data heterogeneity that negatively impacts model generalization, slows convergence, and reduces performance \[[1' 2020-04-24](http://arxiv.org/abs/2004.11794v2)\]. This issue is further compounded by the fact that as data heterogeneity increases, feature representation in models like FedAVG deteriorates more significantly compared to classifier weights, and the gap between feature norms of observed and unobserved classes widens \[[2' 2022-07-17](http://arxiv.org/abs/2207.08187v1)\]. Additionally, the performance of FL methods can be adversely affected when the local data distributions vary significantly across clients, making it difficult to achieve a globally accurate model \[[3' 2024-11-02](http://arxiv.org/abs/2411.01240v3)\]. To address these challenges, researchers have proposed various approaches, such as FEDDIVERSE, which introduces a client selection algorithm designed to manage and leverage data heterogeneity by promoting collaboration between clients with complementary data distributions \[[1' 2020-04-24](http://arxiv.org/abs/2004.11794v2)\]. Another approach, FedFN, introduces feature normalization updates to improve feature representation and reduce the negative impact of data heterogeneity \[[2' 2022-07-17](http://arxiv.org/abs/2207.08187v1)\]. Furthermore, FedDPMS employs differentially private means sharing through variational auto-encoders to augment local datasets, thereby ameliorating the effects of data heterogeneity without compromising privacy \[[3' 2024-11-02](http://arxiv.org/abs/2411.01240v3)\]. These methods highlight the ongoing efforts to enhance the robustness and performance of FL systems in the presence of heterogeneous data.

### <a id='category-client-heterogeneity-in-fl'></a>Client Heterogeneity in FL: 6 papers found.
Client Heterogeneity in Federated Learning has been a focal point of recent research, addressing the challenges posed by diverse data distributions and varying model behaviors across clients. To tackle these issues, several approaches have been proposed. Additionally, frameworks like Flower have been developed to support large-scale FL experiments on heterogeneous edge devices, enabling more realistic and scalable FL scenarios \[[11' 2020-07-28](http://arxiv.org/abs/2007.14390v5)\]. Another approach, Hetero-SSFL, enables self-supervised learning on heterogeneous clients without requiring labeled data, aligning lower-dimensional representations across clients for collaborative learning \[[12' 2022-05-25](http://arxiv.org/abs/2205.12493v3)\]. In vertical federated learning, secure aggregation techniques have been proposed to address privacy concerns while improving training efficiency \[[16' 2023-05-18](http://arxiv.org/abs/2305.11236v1)\]. These studies collectively highlight the ongoing efforts to address client heterogeneity in FL, emphasizing the need for robust, scalable, and privacy-preserving solutions.
