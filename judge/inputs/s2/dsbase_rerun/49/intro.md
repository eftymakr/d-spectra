## Related Works

Neural retrieval models have shown significant promise in improving the effectiveness of Web search \[[1' 2023-11-27](http://arxiv.org/abs/2311.15563v1)\]. However, their training typically requires a large amount of labeled query-document pairs, which are expensive to obtain \[[1' 2023-11-27](http://arxiv.org/abs/2311.15563v1)\]. To address this challenge, researchers have explored the use of synthetic queries generated by large language models as a scalable alternative \[[1' 2023-11-27](http://arxiv.org/abs/2311.15563v1)\]. These synthetic queries, however, often vary in quality, leading to suboptimal performance in downstream retrieval tasks \[[1' 2023-11-27](http://arxiv.org/abs/2311.15563v1)\]. Existing methods typically filter out noisy query-document pairs using signals from an external re-ranker \[[1' 2023-11-27](http://arxiv.org/abs/2311.15563v1)\]. In contrast, this work proposes a framework that integrates ranking signals directly into the query generation process using Direct Preference Optimization (DPO) \[[1' 2023-11-27](http://arxiv.org/abs/2311.15563v1)\]. This approach aims to generate high-quality queries that maximize downstream retrieval effectiveness \[[1' 2023-11-27](http://arxiv.org/abs/2311.15563v1)\].

The use of DPO for preference learning has been explored in several studies. For instance, \[[3' 2024-04-22](http://arxiv.org/abs/2404.13846v4)\] introduced fDPO, an extension of DPO that uses a trained reward model to monitor the quality of texts within the preference dataset during training. This method discards lower-quality samples, resulting in a more accurate dataset \[[3' 2024-04-22](http://arxiv.org/abs/2404.13846v4)\]. Similarly, \[[9' 2024-07-19](http://arxiv.org/abs/2407.14477v4)\] proposed augmenting standard preference pairs with rationales that explain the reasoning behind human preferences, which improves learning efficiency and model alignment \[[9' 2024-07-19](http://arxiv.org/abs/2407.14477v4)\]. These works highlight the importance of incorporating quality signals during the preference learning process \[[3' 2024-04-22](http://arxiv.org/abs/2404.13846v4)\]\[[9' 2024-07-19](http://arxiv.org/abs/2407.14477v4)\].

In the context of query generation, \[[12' 2024-06-10](http://arxiv.org/abs/2406.06729v1)\] investigated the use of Large Language Models (LLMs) to generate synthetic queries that are similar to existing VA usage. The study found that LLMs generate more verbose queries compared to template-based methods, and the generated queries are specific enough to retrieve the relevant entity \[[12' 2024-06-10](http://arxiv.org/abs/2406.06729v1)\]. This aligns with the idea of generating high-quality queries directly, rather than relying on external re-rankers \[[1' 2023-11-27](http://arxiv.org/abs/2311.15563v1)\].

Furthermore, \[[13' 2023-01-13](http://arxiv.org/abs/2301.05508v1)\] analyzed whether findings from document and passage retrieval generalize to the retrieval of responses for dialogues. The study found that several key findings, such as the effectiveness of query expansion and dense retrieval, generalize to dialogue response retrieval \[[13' 2023-01-13](http://arxiv.org/abs/2301.05508v1)\]. This suggests that the principles underlying retrieval effectiveness can be applied across different retrieval tasks \[[13' 2023-01-13](http://arxiv.org/abs/2301.05508v1)\].

In addition, \[[18' 2021-02-23](http://arxiv.org/abs/2102.11903v2)\] reviewed neural ranking models for document retrieval and compared various deep learning approaches. The paper highlighted the benefits of end-to-end training for ranking tasks, which aligns with the proposed DPO-based framework \[[18' 2021-02-23](http://arxiv.org/abs/2102.11903v2)\].
