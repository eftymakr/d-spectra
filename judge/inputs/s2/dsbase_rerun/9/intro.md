## Related Works

Quantitative susceptibility mapping (QSM) has emerged as a powerful tool for characterizing tissue susceptibility in neurological disorders, including multiple sclerosis (MS). QSM provides critical insights into lesion composition, particularly distinguishing between "rim" and "non-rim" lesions, which are associated with different pathological processes \[[2' 2022-11-09](http://arxiv.org/abs/2211.04764v1)\]. Rim lesions, characterized by paramagnetic iron deposition, are increasingly recognized as biomarkers for disease progression and disability \[[1' 2023-12-28](http://arxiv.org/abs/2312.16835v2)\]. Traditional QSM methods, such as COSMOS and MEDI, rely on regularization strategies to solve the ill-posed dipole inversion problem but often suffer from artifacts or require multiple orientations \[[10' 2018-03-15](http://arxiv.org/abs/1803.05627v2)\]. Recent advances have leveraged deep learning to improve QSM accuracy and efficiency. For example, QSMnet was introduced to generate high-quality susceptibility maps from single orientation data, outperforming conventional methods in image quality and consistency \[[10' 2018-03-15](http://arxiv.org/abs/1803.05627v2)\]. Similarly, DCRNet was developed to recover both magnitude and phase images from undersampled data, significantly accelerating QSM acquisition while maintaining high fidelity \[[11' 2021-03-17](http://arxiv.org/abs/2103.09375v1)\]. These methods highlight the growing role of deep learning in overcoming the limitations of traditional QSM approaches.

The detection and segmentation of paramagnetic rim lesions (PRLs) have been the focus of several studies. RimSeg, an unsupervised segmentation method based on level-set methodology, achieved a Dice score of 78.7% against ground truth, though it struggled with partial rim lesions \[[1' 2023-12-28](http://arxiv.org/abs/2312.16835v2)\]. QSM-RimNet was proposed for automated PRL detection, but it lacked rim segmentation capabilities necessary for microglial density quantification \[[5' 2024-12-13](http://arxiv.org/abs/2412.10492v2)\]. In contrast, QSM-RimDS was developed to jointly detect and segment PRLs using T2-weighted FLAIR lesion masks, achieving a mean Dice similarity coefficient (DSC) of 0.57 and significantly better detection performance compared to QSM-RimNet \[[5' 2024-12-13](http://arxiv.org/abs/2412.10492v2)\]. These studies underscore the importance of accurate segmentation in understanding PRL morphology and its clinical relevance.

To address class imbalance in PRL detection, synthetic data generation has been explored. The work by Agr78 introduced a GAN-based approach to generate synthetic QSM maps of PRLs, improving classifier performance by increasing the minority class representation \[[1' 2023-12-28](http://arxiv.org/abs/2312.16835v2)\]. This approach was extended to include multi-channel outputs for generating accompanying contrasts and probabilistic segmentation maps \[[1' 2023-12-28](http://arxiv.org/abs/2312.16835v2)\]. The use of synthetic data in medical imaging has also been explored in other contexts, such as skin lesion segmentation, where GANs have been used to generate realistic images to address class imbalance \[[7' 2018-09-05](http://arxiv.org/abs/1809.01410v2)\].
