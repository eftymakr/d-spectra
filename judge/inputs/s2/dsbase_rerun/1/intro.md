## Related Works

The management of Key-Value (KV) caches in Large Language Models (LLMs) has garnered significant attention due to its critical role in improving inference efficiency and reducing memory overhead. Prior work has explored various strategies for optimizing KV cache usage, including eviction policies, quantization techniques, and attention-aware methods. These approaches aim to balance memory constraints with the need for maintaining model performance during long-context inference.

Several studies have focused on KV cache eviction policies. For instance, \[[6' 2024-07-16](http://arxiv.org/abs/2407.11550v5)\] introduces **Ada-KV**, which proposes an adaptive budget allocation strategy that considers the unique attention patterns of each head. This method enables a more efficient use of cache space by dynamically adjusting the eviction budget based on the model's requirements. Similarly, \[[7' 2024-08-07](http://arxiv.org/abs/2408.03675v2)\] presents **NACL**, a general and effective framework for KV cache eviction that combines accurate attention score statistics with diversified random eviction strategies to reduce bias and improve robustness. These approaches demonstrate the importance of tailoring eviction policies to the specific characteristics of the model and the workload.

Quantization techniques have also been explored to reduce the memory footprint of LLMs. \[[4' 2024-02-19](http://arxiv.org/abs/2402.12065v2)\] proposes **WKVQuant**, a PTQ framework that quantizes both model weights and the KV cache. This method incorporates past-only quantization and a two-dimensional quantization strategy to handle the distribution of KV cache, achieving significant memory savings while maintaining high performance. Such quantization approaches are crucial for deploying LLMs in resource-constrained environments.

Attention-aware methods have emerged as a promising direction for optimizing KV cache management. \[[5' 2025-03-11](http://arxiv.org/abs/2503.08879v1)\] introduces **SAGE-KV**, which leverages self-attention to guide the eviction of less critical tokens during long-context inference. This method performs a one-time top-k selection at both the token and head levels, enabling efficient inference with a reduced cache. \[[10' 2024-10-15](http://arxiv.org/abs/2410.12876v3)\] proposes an **Attention-Gate** module that dynamically decides which tokens to evict based on the global context, improving both efficiency and performance by reducing redundant computations.

Furthermore, \[[3' 2025-02-06](http://arxiv.org/abs/2502.03805v2)\] presents **CriticalKV**, which analyzes attention output perturbation to identify critical KV cache entries. This method provides a formal grounding for cache eviction, ensuring that the most important tokens are retained while minimizing the loss of model accuracy. These studies highlight the importance of understanding the impact of cache eviction on model performance and developing methods that balance efficiency with accuracy.
