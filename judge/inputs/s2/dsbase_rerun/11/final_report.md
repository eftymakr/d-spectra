## Related Works

3D human pose estimation has seen significant advancements in recent years, with a growing emphasis on capturing both local and global correlations within the human skeleton. Early approaches primarily focused on 2D pose estimation, but recent works have extended these methods to 3D by leveraging spatial and temporal information \[[1' 2023-04-27](http://arxiv.org/abs/2304.14045v1)\]. One of the key challenges in 3D human pose estimation is modeling long-range dependencies among body parts, which are essential for accurate pose recovery \[[2' 2021-07-16](http://arxiv.org/abs/2107.07797v2)\]. This challenge is compounded by the fact that existing methods often rely on deepening networks to capture non-local dependencies, which can introduce uncorrelated noise and increase model complexity \[[3' 2020-07-14](http://arxiv.org/abs/2007.07053v2)\].

To address these limitations, several works have proposed graph-based architectures that explicitly model the human skeleton as a graph, enabling more effective capture of spatial relationships \[[4' 2022-03-16](http://arxiv.org/abs/2203.08534v1)\]. For instance, the Conditional Directed Graph Convolution (CDGC) network represents the human skeleton as a directed graph, where edges are directed from parent to child joints, allowing for more accurate modeling of hierarchical relationships \[[5' 2024-01-30](http://arxiv.org/abs/2401.16700v2)\]. This approach has been shown to outperform undirected graph-based methods in capturing the articulated structure of the human body \[[6' 2020-03-11](http://arxiv.org/abs/2003.14179v4)\].

Another line of research focuses on integrating attention mechanisms with graph convolutional networks to better capture both local and global interactions. The Interweaved Graph and Attention Network (IGANet) introduces a bidirectional communication between graph convolutional networks and attention modules, allowing for more effective learning of human skeleton representations \[[7' 2021-03-30](http://arxiv.org/abs/2103.16385v1)\]. Similarly, the Graph Attention Spatio-Temporal Convolutional Network (GAST-Net) combines graph attention with temporal convolutional blocks to better capture spatio-temporal dependencies in video sequences \[[8' 2021-09-06](http://arxiv.org/abs/2109.02303v1)\].

In addition to graph-based methods, some works have explored the use of multi-scale architectures to better capture long-range dependencies. The Graph Stacked Hourglass Networks (GSHN) employ a multi-scale architecture that enables the model to learn both local and global feature representations, which are critical for 3D human pose estimation \[[9' 2023-07-29](http://arxiv.org/abs/2307.16074v2)\]. Similarly, the GraphMLP architecture integrates graph convolutional networks with multi-layer perceptrons to better capture both local and global spatial interactions \[[10' 2020-03-25](http://arxiv.org/abs/2003.11163v2)\].

The use of self-attention mechanisms has also gained traction in recent works. The Multi-Stage Transformer-based approach for 3D human pose estimation leverages the self-attention mechanism to capture spatial-temporal correlations from multi-view video datasets \[[11' 2019-01-12](http://arxiv.org/abs/1901.03798v2)\]. This approach has been shown to achieve state-of-the-art performance on the Human3.6M dataset \[[12' 2022-06-13](http://arxiv.org/abs/2206.06420v5)\].
