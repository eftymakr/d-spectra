## Related Works

The rapid growth of open-source machine learning (ML) resources has significantly impacted information retrieval (IR) research, particularly in the areas of model sharing and dataset management \[[1' 2023-11-22](http://arxiv.org/abs/2311.13380v2)\]. While platforms like Hugging Face (HF) have become central hubs for ML model development and sharing, they lack structured representations that enable advanced querying and analysis \[[1' 2023-11-22](http://arxiv.org/abs/2311.13380v2)\]. This limitation hinders tasks such as tracing model evolution and recommending relevant datasets \[[1' 2023-11-22](http://arxiv.org/abs/2311.13380v2)\]. 

Several studies have explored the evolution and maintenance of ML models on HF, highlighting the need for structured data to support deeper analysis \[[2' 2023-02-28](http://arxiv.org/abs/2303.06031v1)\]. For instance, a repository mining study of over 380,000 models on HF revealed trends in framework usage, author grouping, and tag evolution, emphasizing the importance of metadata for model maintenance \[[2' 2023-02-28](http://arxiv.org/abs/2303.06031v1)\]. Additionally, the study introduced a new classification system for evaluating maintenance status based on commit messages and other attributes \[[2' 2023-02-28](http://arxiv.org/abs/2303.06031v1)\]. These findings underscore the gap in structured representation that HuggingKG aims to address.

In the domain of knowledge graphs (KGs), several works have focused on improving representation learning and addressing biases in embeddings \[[3' 2020-04-05](http://arxiv.org/abs/2004.02195v1)\]\[[11' 2020-06-29](http://arxiv.org/abs/2006.16309v2)\]. For example, clustering-based contrastive learning (CCL) has been proposed to enhance face representations by leveraging clustering as a form of weak supervision \[[3' 2020-04-05](http://arxiv.org/abs/2004.02195v1)\]. This approach has shown promising results in video face clustering tasks, achieving state-of-the-art performance on multiple datasets \[[3' 2020-04-05](http://arxiv.org/abs/2004.02195v1)\]. Similarly, adversarial learning techniques have been introduced to debias knowledge graph embeddings, addressing issues such as popularity bias and gender bias \[[11' 2020-06-29](http://arxiv.org/abs/2006.16309v2)\]. These methods aim to filter out sensitive attribute information from embeddings, ensuring fairer and more accurate representations \[[11' 2020-06-29](http://arxiv.org/abs/2006.16309v2)\].

In the context of tax compliance and business logic representation, the Tax Knowledge Graph (TKG) has been developed to automate complex calculations and rules \[[8' 2020-09-13](http://arxiv.org/abs/2009.06103v1)\]. This work demonstrates how KGs can be used to represent intricate business logic, enabling more efficient and personalized user experiences in applications like TurboTax \[[8' 2020-09-13](http://arxiv.org/abs/2009.06103v1)\]. The TKG integrates large-scale tax rules and provides automated reasoning capabilities, enhancing both accuracy and user satisfaction \[[8' 2020-09-13](http://arxiv.org/abs/2009.06103v1)\].

Moreover, the application of knowledge graphs in the fashion domain has been explored, where techniques such as named entity recognition (NER) and entity linking (EL) are used to enrich product metadata \[[6' 2022-06-02](http://arxiv.org/abs/2206.01087v1)\]. This work highlights the challenges of domain-specific data representation and the need for transfer learning approaches to overcome limited labeled datasets \[[6' 2022-06-02](http://arxiv.org/abs/2206.01087v1)\]. The success of these methods in the fashion domain suggests that similar strategies could be applied to other specialized domains, including ML model management.

[Snippet]: «Knowledge Graphs (KG) are gaining increasing attention in both academia and industry. Despite their diverse benefits, recent research have identified social and cultural biases embedded in the representations learned from KGs. Such biases can have detrimental consequences on different population and minority groups as applications of KG begin to intersect and interact with social spheres. This paper aims at identifying and mitigating such biases in Knowledge Graph (KG) embeddings. As a first step, we explore popularity bias -- the relationship between node popularity and link prediction accuracy. In case of node2vec graph embeddings, we find that prediction accuracy of the embedding is negatively correlated with the degree of the node. However, in case of knowledge-graph embeddings (KGE), we observe an opposite trend. As a second step, we explore gender bias in KGE, and a careful examination of popular KGE algorithms suggest that sensitive attribute like the gender of a person can be predicted from the embedding. This implies that such biases in popular KGs is captured by the structural properties of the embedding. As a preliminary solution to debiasing KGs, we introduce a novel framework to filter out the sensitive attribute information from the KG embeddings, which we call FAN (Filtering Adversarial Network). We also suggest the applicability of FAN for debiasing other network embeddings which could be explored in future work.»
