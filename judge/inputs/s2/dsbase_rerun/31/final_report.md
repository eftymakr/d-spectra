## Related Works

However, these approaches often yield suboptimal results because pure DNA sequences lack sufficient information to capture the complex regulatory mechanisms that govern genomic functions \[[1' 2023-07-11](http://arxiv.org/abs/2307.05628v3)\]. Specifically, DNA functions are influenced by genomic profiles such as chromatin accessibility, which are not directly encoded in the DNA sequence itself \[[1' 2023-07-11](http://arxiv.org/abs/2307.05628v3)\]. As a result, models trained solely on DNA sequences may fail to generalize effectively across diverse biological contexts \[[1' 2023-07-11](http://arxiv.org/abs/2307.05628v3)\].

For instance, DNAGPT is a generalized pre-trained model designed to handle versatile DNA analysis tasks by incorporating tasks such as DNA sequence order classification and guanine-cytosine content prediction \[[1' 2023-07-11](http://arxiv.org/abs/2307.05628v3)\]. This model leverages a comprehensive token language to process both sequence and numerical data, demonstrating superior performance on genomic signal and region recognition tasks \[[1' 2023-07-11](http://arxiv.org/abs/2307.05628v3)\]. However, DNAGPT is primarily focused on DNA sequence analysis and does not explicitly address the integration of genomic profiles such as chromatin accessibility \[[1' 2023-07-11](http://arxiv.org/abs/2307.05628v3)\].

The Mixture of Experts (MoE) frameworks, as explored in various studies, have been increasingly applied to address complex prediction tasks across different domains. These frameworks leverage a modular and flexible architecture where multiple specialized experts are trained to handle specific aspects of the data, and a router network selects the most appropriate expert for a given input \[[22' 2018-06-21](http://arxiv.org/abs/1806.08200v1)\]. This approach has been particularly effective in scenarios where data distributions are imbalanced, such as in pedestrian trajectory prediction, where simpler samples dominate and safety-critical scenarios are underrepresented \[[6' 2024-02-13](http://arxiv.org/abs/2402.08698v2)\]. Similarly, in multilayer link prediction, MoE frameworks have been designed to integrate information from multiple network layers, enhancing predictive accuracy by synthesizing decisions from diverse experts \[[16' 2025-01-29](http://arxiv.org/abs/2501.17557v1)\]. In the context of human mobility prediction, MoE has been combined with transformer-based models like BERT to capture spatial-temporal dynamics, leading to improved performance in cross-city prediction tasks \[[26' 2024-10-18](http://arxiv.org/abs/2410.14099v1)\]. These studies highlight the versatility of MoE in handling complex, heterogeneous data by allowing modular integration of new experts without retraining the entire system, thus promoting scalability and adaptability \[[16' 2025-01-29](http://arxiv.org/abs/2501.17557v1)\]. The core idea of MoE—leveraging specialized models to enhance overall performance—aligns with the proposed SPACE framework, which uses MoE to capture relationships between DNA sequences and genomic profiles across species \[[1' 2023-07-11](http://arxiv.org/abs/2307.05628v3)\].

[Snippet]: «We introduce $\textit(scadnano)$ (https://scadnano.org) (short for "scriptable cadnano"), a computational tool for designing synthetic DNA structures. Its design is based heavily on cadnano, the most widely-used software for designing DNA origami, with three main differences:
  1. scadnano runs entirely in the browser, with $\textit(no software installation)$ required.
  2. scadnano designs, while they can be edited manually, can also be created and edited by a $\textit(well-documented Python scripting library)$, to help automate tedious tasks.
  3. The scadnano file format is $\textit(easily human-readable)$. This goal is closely aligned with the scripting library, intended to be helpful when debugging scripts or interfacing with other software. The format is also somewhat more expressive than that of cadnano, able to describe a broader range of DNA structures than just DNA origami.»
