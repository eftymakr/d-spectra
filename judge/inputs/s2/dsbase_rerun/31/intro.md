## Related Works

Recent advances in pre-training models have inspired researchers to apply similar paradigms to DNA sequence analysis. However, these approaches often yield suboptimal results because pure DNA sequences lack sufficient information to capture the complex regulatory mechanisms that govern genomic functions \[[1' 2023-07-11](http://arxiv.org/abs/2307.05628v3)\]. Specifically, DNA functions are influenced by genomic profiles such as chromatin accessibility, which are not directly encoded in the DNA sequence itself \[[1' 2023-07-11](http://arxiv.org/abs/2307.05628v3)\]. As a result, models trained solely on DNA sequences may fail to generalize effectively across diverse biological contexts \[[1' 2023-07-11](http://arxiv.org/abs/2307.05628v3)\].

Several studies have explored the use of pre-trained models for DNA analysis. For instance, DNAGPT is a generalized pre-trained model designed to handle versatile DNA analysis tasks by incorporating tasks such as DNA sequence order classification and guanine-cytosine content prediction \[[1' 2023-07-11](http://arxiv.org/abs/2307.05628v3)\]. This model leverages a comprehensive token language to process both sequence and numerical data, demonstrating superior performance on genomic signal and region recognition tasks \[[1' 2023-07-11](http://arxiv.org/abs/2307.05628v3)\]. However, DNAGPT is primarily focused on DNA sequence analysis and does not explicitly address the integration of genomic profiles such as chromatin accessibility \[[1' 2023-07-11](http://arxiv.org/abs/2307.05628v3)\].

In contrast, SemanticCAP introduces a gene language model to capture the contextual information of gene sequences, which is essential for predicting chromatin accessibility \[[2' 2022-04-05](http://arxiv.org/abs/2204.02130v2)\]. This model integrates features from the gene language model into a chromatin accessibility prediction framework, achieving better performance compared to existing computational models \[[2' 2022-04-05](http://arxiv.org/abs/2204.02130v2)\]. The ability to model contextual information is crucial for understanding how DNA sequences interact with regulatory elements, such as transcription factors and chromatin modifiers \[[2' 2022-04-05](http://arxiv.org/abs/2204.02130v2)\].

Another relevant work is the development of Mixture of Experts (MoE) frameworks for various tasks. For example, AMEND is a modular framework designed for long-tailed trajectory prediction, leveraging a specialized mixture of experts to improve performance on challenging scenarios \[[6' 2024-02-13](http://arxiv.org/abs/2402.08698v2)\]. Similarly, MoE-ML-LP is a framework specifically designed for multilayer link prediction, synthesizing decisions from diverse experts to enhance predictive capabilities \[[16' 2025-01-29](http://arxiv.org/abs/2501.17557v1)\]. These frameworks demonstrate the potential of MoE in handling complex and heterogeneous data, which is also relevant for genomic profile prediction \[[6' 2024-02-13](http://arxiv.org/abs/2402.08698v2)\]\[[16' 2025-01-29](http://arxiv.org/abs/2501.17557v1)\].

In the context of genomic data science, there is a growing need to diversify the research community and improve access to tools and resources \[[18' 2022-01-20](http://arxiv.org/abs/2201.08443v2)\]. The Genomic Data Science Community Network has been established to support students and researchers at underserved institutions in leveraging cloud-enabled genomic data science tools \[[18' 2022-01-20](http://arxiv.org/abs/2201.08443v2)\]. This initiative highlights the importance of inclusivity and accessibility in advancing genomic research \[[18' 2022-01-20](http://arxiv.org/abs/2201.08443v2)\].
