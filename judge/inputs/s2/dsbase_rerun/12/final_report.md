## Related Works

Event cameras, which capture scene changes asynchronously on a per-pixel basis, have gained significant attention for their ability to provide high temporal resolution and low latency \[[1' 2024-08-24](http://arxiv.org/abs/2408.13627v2)\]. However, this advantage comes with challenges such as high bandwidth, memory, and computational demands \[[1' 2024-08-24](http://arxiv.org/abs/2408.13627v2)\]. Prior work has explored event downsampling as a means to mitigate these issues, but most approaches rely on fixed heuristics or threshold-based strategies, which limit their adaptability to varying application scenarios \[[1' 2024-08-24](http://arxiv.org/abs/2408.13627v2)\]. For instance, some studies have used simple thresholds to filter out less significant events \[[2' 2024-02-23](http://arxiv.org/abs/2402.15584v3)\], while others have applied fixed sampling rates regardless of the scene dynamics \[[3' 2025-04-16](http://arxiv.org/abs/2504.12515v2)\]. These methods often fail to adapt to scene-specific requirements and can lead to performance degradation in complex environments \[[4' 2025-01-02](http://arxiv.org/abs/2501.01040v1)\].

To address these limitations, recent research has introduced more sophisticated methods for event downsampling. One notable approach is the use of state-space models (SSMs) with learnable timescale parameters, which enable models to adapt to varying frequencies without requiring retraining \[[2' 2024-02-23](http://arxiv.org/abs/2402.15584v3)\]. This approach has shown improved performance compared to traditional RNN and Transformer architectures, with minimal performance degradation when tested at higher frequencies \[[2' 2024-02-23](http://arxiv.org/abs/2402.15584v3)\]. Another study introduced a probabilistic framework for event importance modeling, which allows for adaptive sampling based on scene-specific characteristics \[[5' 2018-01-30](http://arxiv.org/abs/1801.10202v2)\]. This framework uses a probability density function (PDF) to estimate the importance of individual events, enabling more intelligent sampling strategies \[[5' 2018-01-30](http://arxiv.org/abs/1801.10202v2)\].

Additionally, there has been growing interest in simulators that generate synthetic event data for training and evaluation. The Event Quality Score (EQS) metric has been proposed to assess the realism of simulated event camera streams by comparing them to real-world data \[[3' 2025-04-16](http://arxiv.org/abs/2504.12515v2)\]. This metric leverages deep features from the RVT architecture to evaluate the quality of simulated events, helping to reduce the simulation gap \[[3' 2025-04-16](http://arxiv.org/abs/2504.12515v2)\]. These simulators can be trained end-to-end and applied directly to incoming event streams, offering a promising direction for future research \[[8' 2021-04-30](http://arxiv.org/abs/2104.15139v1)\].

In the context of event-based vision, there has also been work on improving the performance of models trained on simulated data by aligning them with real-world data \[[3' 2025-04-16](http://arxiv.org/abs/2504.12515v2)\].
