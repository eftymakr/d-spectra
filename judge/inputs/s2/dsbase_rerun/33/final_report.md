## Related Works

Rehearsal-based methods have been widely studied in continual learning (CL) to mitigate catastrophic forgetting, where models lose knowledge from previous tasks when learning new ones \[[1' 2022-03-28](http://arxiv.org/abs/2203.14544v1)\]. These methods typically store a subset of past data in a memory buffer and train the model on both new and old data simultaneously \[[2' 2020-10-07](http://arxiv.org/abs/2010.04003v2)\]. While concurrent rehearsal has been shown to be effective, recent work has questioned whether this approach is always optimal \[[3' 2017-08-07](http://arxiv.org/abs/1708.02072v4)\]. Inspired by human learning, where revisiting tasks sequentially helps retain knowledge, researchers have explored whether sequential rehearsal could offer greater benefits compared to standard concurrent rehearsal \[[4' 2020-07-14](http://arxiv.org/abs/2007.07400v1)\].

Several studies have investigated the effectiveness of rehearsal strategies in CL. For instance, \[[5' 2023-06-16](http://arxiv.org/abs/2306.10181v1)\] proposed a coreset selection method for rehearsal-based CL, aiming to maintain a small, informative subset of data that closely matches the gradients induced by the full dataset. This approach reduces memory usage while preserving model performance \[[6' 2022-03-31](http://arxiv.org/abs/2203.17269v2)\]. Similarly, \[[7' 2021-05-16](http://arxiv.org/abs/2105.07385v1)\] introduced a method to measure catastrophic forgetting by analyzing how performance on earlier tasks degrades after learning new ones. This work highlighted the importance of evaluating forgetting in addition to accuracy \[[8' 2025-05-28](http://arxiv.org/abs/2505.21942v1)\].

The idea of sequential rehearsal has also been explored in the context of deep reinforcement learning. \[[9' 2024-02-12](http://arxiv.org/abs/2402.08096v3)\] introduced a dual memory system that separates continual learning from reinforcement learning and uses a pseudo-rehearsal mechanism to "recall" past tasks via a deep generative network. This approach enables models to learn sequentially without catastrophic forgetting, avoiding the need to store raw data or revisit past tasks \[[10' 2020-04-29](http://arxiv.org/abs/2004.14046v1)\]. Similarly, \[[11' 2018-01-04](http://arxiv.org/abs/1801.01423v3)\] proposed pseudo-rehearsal using non-photorealistic images, demonstrating that synthetic data can be used for rehearsal without requiring high-fidelity representations \[[12' 2023-03-06](http://arxiv.org/abs/2303.02954v1)\].

In addition to rehearsal-based approaches, other methods have been developed to address catastrophic forgetting. \[[13' 2024-10-14](http://arxiv.org/abs/2410.10521v1)\] introduced a theoretical analysis of catastrophic forgetting using the NTK overlap matrix, showing that task similarity plays a key role in forgetting. This work also analyzed common gradient-based algorithms and proposed a variant of orthogonal gradient descent to mitigate forgetting \[[14' 2019-04-15](http://arxiv.org/abs/1904.07734v1)\]. Another study \[[15' 2023-08-20](http://arxiv.org/abs/2308.10328v3)\] explored the use of regularization techniques, such as elastic weight consolidation (EWC), to preserve knowledge from previous tasks while learning new ones \[[16' 2022-06-16](http://arxiv.org/abs/2206.07932v1)\].

### <a id='category-theoretical-models-of-continual-learning'></a>Theoretical Models of Continual Learning: 4 papers found.
Theoretical models of continual learning focus on understanding and mitigating catastrophic forgetting, a phenomenon where models rapidly lose previously learned knowledge when exposed to new data. Research in this area explores both empirical and theoretical approaches to address this challenge. For instance, one study introduces a coreset selection method for rehearsal-based continual learning, leveraging gradient matching and neural tangent kernel theory to maintain a representative subset of data without prior training \[[1' 2022-03-28](http://arxiv.org/abs/2203.14544v1)\]. Another paper highlights the role of task similarity in catastrophic forgetting, proposing a measure called the NTK overlap matrix to quantify task alignment and analyze how common gradient descent algorithms can mitigate forgetting \[[2' 2020-10-07](http://arxiv.org/abs/2010.04003v2)\]. Additionally, a theoretical framework using teacher-student learning provides insights into how input distribution similarity and input-output relationships affect task forgetting, suggesting that lower input distribution similarity and higher input-output relationship similarity can help avoid catastrophic forgetting \[[3' 2017-08-07](http://arxiv.org/abs/1708.02072v4)\]. Furthermore, the concept of artificial neural variability is introduced as a mechanism inspired by biological neural systems, offering implicit regularization that improves generalizability and robustness against catastrophic forgetting \[[4' 2020-07-14](http://arxiv.org/abs/2007.07400v1)\]. These studies collectively contribute to a deeper understanding of the mechanisms underlying continual learning and provide theoretical foundations for developing more effective memory management strategies.

### <a id='category-catastrophic-forgetting-analysis'></a>Catastrophic Forgetting Analysis: 11 papers found.
Catastrophic forgetting, the phenomenon where neural networks lose previously learned information when trained on new tasks, has been extensively studied in the context of continual learning \[[3' 2017-08-07](http://arxiv.org/abs/1708.02072v4)\]. This issue is particularly pronounced when tasks are dissimilar, as deeper layers of the network are disproportionately affected \[[2' 2020-10-07](http://arxiv.org/abs/2010.04003v2)\]. Various approaches have been proposed to mitigate this problem, including regularization, ensembling, rehearsal, dual-memory, and sparse-coding \[[3' 2017-08-07](http://arxiv.org/abs/1708.02072v4)\]. Data rehearsal, which involves reusing a portion of past data during fine-tuning, has been shown to be effective, especially when combined with techniques like Elastic Weight Consolidation (EWC) \[[3' 2017-08-07](http://arxiv.org/abs/1708.02072v4)\]. Additionally, task-based hard attention mechanisms have been introduced to preserve previous task information without interfering with current learning \[[4' 2020-07-14](http://arxiv.org/abs/2007.07400v1)\]. The impact of catastrophic forgetting in deep reinforcement learning (DRL) has also been explored, where it affects the ability of models to adapt to changing jammer patterns in dynamic environments \[[5' 2023-06-16](http://arxiv.org/abs/2306.10181v1)\]. Furthermore, the width of neural network architectures has been found to significantly influence catastrophic forgetting, with wider networks exhibiting different forgetting behaviors \[[7' 2021-05-16](http://arxiv.org/abs/2105.07385v1)\]. Recent efforts have also focused on interpretability, introducing tools like Auto DeepVis and Critical Freezing to dissect and mitigate catastrophic forgetting while maintaining explainability \[[8' 2025-05-28](http://arxiv.org/abs/2505.21942v1)\]. These methods aim to improve the robustness of models in sequential learning scenarios, offering promising directions for future research.

### <a id='category-regularization-for-continual-learning'></a>Regularization for Continual Learning: 6 papers found.
The category "Regularization for Continual Learning" explores various techniques to mitigate catastrophic forgetting in machine learning models that learn from continuously evolving data. These methods aim to preserve knowledge of previously seen tasks while adapting to new ones without requiring explicit rehearsal of past data, which can be memory-intensive and privacy-violating. Regularization-based approaches, such as L2 parameter regularization and Elastic Weight Consolidation (EWC), have been extensively studied for their ability to constrain parameter updates and maintain performance on prior tasks \[[14' 2019-04-15](http://arxiv.org/abs/1904.07734v1)\]. However, recent work has shown that vanilla L2 regularization can outperform EWC and feature distillation in rehearsal-free settings, particularly when applied to self-attention blocks in Vision Transformers (ViT) for tasks like ImageNet-R \[[1' 2022-03-28](http://arxiv.org/abs/2203.14544v1)\]. Additionally, methods like OML propose learning representations that are robust to forgetting and accelerate future learning, demonstrating that regularization can be combined with other strategies to improve continual learning performance \[[3' 2017-08-07](http://arxiv.org/abs/1708.02072v4)\]. Furthermore, the shift toward parameter-efficient fine-tuning techniques in foundation model adaptation has led to the development of prompt-based and regularization-based methods for rehearsal-free continual learning (RFCL), though some studies question whether these methods truly outperform simpler baselines \[[4' 2020-07-14](http://arxiv.org/abs/2007.07400v1)\]. Overall, the field emphasizes the need for regularization strategies that balance adaptation to new tasks with retention of prior knowledge, often through novel formulations or hybrid approaches \[[6' 2022-03-31](http://arxiv.org/abs/2203.17269v2)\].

### <a id='category-efficiency-and-scalability-of-rehearsal'></a>Efficiency and Scalability of Rehearsal: 2 papers found.
Efficiency and scalability in rehearsal-based continual learning (CL) have been critical areas of research, particularly due to the computational and memory overhead associated with traditional methods. Existing approaches often rely on experience rehearsal or full-model surrogates to mitigate catastrophic forgetting, but these methods are resource-intensive and limit scalability \[[1' 2022-03-28](http://arxiv.org/abs/2203.14544v1)\]. To address these limitations, SPARC was introduced as a lightweight CL approach that eliminates the need for full-model surrogates and experience rehearsal, achieving parameter efficiency by leveraging task-specific working memories and task-agnostic semantic memory \[[2' 2020-10-07](http://arxiv.org/abs/2010.04003v2)\]. Similarly, recent work has focused on optimizing rehearsal strategies under computational constraints, such as the mix-cd method, which prioritizes rehearsal of "collateral damage" samples—those predicted correctly by the prior model but forgotten by the incrementally tuned one—without requiring additional model inferences \[[3' 2017-08-07](http://arxiv.org/abs/1708.02072v4)\]. This approach is computationally efficient and outperforms existing methods in compute-constrained settings \[[3' 2017-08-07](http://arxiv.org/abs/1708.02072v4)\]. These advancements highlight the growing emphasis on developing scalable and efficient rehearsal strategies that balance performance with resource constraints.
