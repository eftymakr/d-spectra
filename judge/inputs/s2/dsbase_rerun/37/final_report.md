## Related Works

Vision Transformers (ViTs) have gained significant traction in computer vision due to their ability to capture global context effectively \[[1' 2021-03-16](http://arxiv.org/abs/2103.09301v1)\]. However, their deployment on hardware remains challenging due to the computational intensity of operations such as softmax and layer normalization, which are sensitive to model accuracy and difficult to map onto low-precision hardware \[[2' 2024-03-21](http://arxiv.org/abs/2403.14302v2)\]. Existing approaches often offload these operations to the CPU, leading to suboptimal performance due to communication overhead between the CPU and accelerator \[[3' 2023-02-17](http://arxiv.org/abs/2302.09108v1)\].

Several works have attempted to address these challenges. Softermax \[[4' 2022-06-21](http://arxiv.org/abs/2206.10552v2)\] introduces a hardware-friendly softmax design that reduces energy consumption while maintaining accuracy. This approach includes base replacement, low-precision computations, and online normalization, achieving 2.35x energy efficiency improvement. Similarly, ViTA \[[5' 2025-03-05](http://arxiv.org/abs/2503.03213v1)\] proposes a configurable hardware accelerator for vision transformer inference, targeting edge devices by reducing off-chip memory accesses and improving hardware utilization efficiency. These methods focus on optimizing specific operations but do not address the broader challenge of mapping the entire model onto hardware.

Efforts to accelerate ViTs have also explored sparsity and low-rank approximations. Vicinity Vision Transformer \[[6' 2018-05-08](http://arxiv.org/abs/1805.02867v2)\] introduces a locality-aware attention mechanism to reduce computational complexity, achieving state-of-the-art performance with fewer parameters. Evo-ViT \[[7' 2021-08-03](http://arxiv.org/abs/2108.01390v5)\] further reduces computational cost through dynamic token pruning, maintaining spatial structure and information flow. ViTALiTy \[[8' 2024-02-07](http://arxiv.org/abs/2402.04563v1)\] unifies low-rank and sparse approximations for ViT acceleration, improving both algorithmic and hardware efficiency. These methods highlight the importance of efficient attention mechanisms but often focus on specific aspects of model design rather than full hardware mapping.

Hardware-aware design methodologies have also emerged. HAT \[[9' 2019-02-28](http://arxiv.org/abs/1902.11269v1)\] proposes neural architecture search to optimize transformers for specific hardware platforms, achieving significant speedups and size reductions. HeatViT \[[10' 2020-05-28](http://arxiv.org/abs/2005.14187v1)\] introduces an adaptive token pruning framework for efficient ViT acceleration on FPGAs, achieving higher accuracy and computation reduction compared to existing methods. ViTCoD \[[11' 2023-10-09](http://arxiv.org/abs/2310.05572v1)\] presents a co-design framework for ViT acceleration, integrating algorithmic and hardware optimizations to reduce data movement costs and improve performance.

In language modeling, redesigning the softmax layer has also led to significant improvements in training efficiency, reducing the computational burden associated with large vocabularies \[[5' 2025-03-05](http://arxiv.org/abs/2503.03213v1)\]. These advancements collectively highlight the importance of optimizing softmax operations to enhance both the performance and scalability of transformer models across various applications.
