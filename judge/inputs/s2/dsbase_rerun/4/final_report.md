## Related Works

In-context learning (ICL) has gained significant attention as a method for leveraging demonstrations without requiring explicit fine-tuning \[[1' 2024-07-27](http://arxiv.org/abs/2407.19346v1)\]. Existing approaches often rely on perplexity metrics to identify noisy annotations, assuming that noisy samples exhibit higher perplexity than clean ones \[[2' 2023-08-10](http://arxiv.org/abs/2308.05286v2)\]. However, this assumption is challenged when noise ratios are high, as the presence of many flawed demonstrations can distort the reliability of perplexity-based methods \[[3' 2023-02-05](http://arxiv.org/abs/2302.02453v1)\]. This issue has been explored in various contexts, including synthetic data generation and model debiasing, where the integrity of training data is critical \[[4' 2025-04-15](http://arxiv.org/abs/2504.10987v1)\].

Several studies have investigated the limitations of perplexity as a metric for evaluating model performance. For instance, in the context of synthetic data generation, researchers have highlighted the need for robustness against biases introduced by training data \[[5' 2024-09-20](http://arxiv.org/abs/2409.13884v1)\]. Similarly, in the field of debiasing, it has been shown that traditional methods may fail to account for biases inherent in both the data and the model's knowledge \[[6' 2024-10-10](http://arxiv.org/abs/2410.08355v3)\]. These findings align with the challenges faced in ICL, where the quality of demonstrations directly impacts the effectiveness of the learning process \[[7' 2022-12-02](http://arxiv.org/abs/2212.00966v1)\].

To address these limitations, recent work has proposed alternative frameworks for improving the reliability of ICL. One such approach involves the use of synthetic data to enhance the quality of training samples, particularly in scenarios where real-world data is scarce or biased \[[8' 2025-02-13](http://arxiv.org/abs/2502.09564v5)\]. Another line of research has focused on multi-LLM frameworks, which leverage the strengths of multiple models to reduce bias and improve performance \[[9' 2024-08-06](http://arxiv.org/abs/2408.03202v1)\]. These methods share a common goal: to ensure that the learning process is not unduly influenced by noisy or biased data.

In the context of model debiasing, researchers have explored techniques such as fine-tuning and prompt engineering to mitigate biases in large language models \[[10' 2023-12-10](http://arxiv.org/abs/2312.05933v1)\]. These methods have been applied in various domains, including natural language processing and computer vision, where the presence of bias can significantly affect model performance \[[11' 2023-11-29](http://arxiv.org/abs/2311.17794v1)\]. The proposed dual debiasing framework in this paper builds upon these ideas, introducing a novel approach to correct perplexity estimates using synthesized neighbors \[[12' 2023-05-22](http://arxiv.org/abs/2305.12658v1)\].

The effectiveness of such debiasing techniques has been validated in multiple studies, demonstrating their ability to improve model performance across different tasks and datasets \[[13' 2023-12-19](http://arxiv.org/abs/2312.12275v3)\]. ### <a id='category-in-context-learning-(icl)-methodology'></a>In-context Learning (ICL) Methodology: 3 papers found.
In-context Learning (ICL) has been explored in various domains, with recent efforts focusing on improving robustness under noisy annotations and limited data scenarios. Researchers have proposed alternative approaches to traditional methods that rely on perplexity-based metrics, which can be biased by both the annotation quality and the domain knowledge embedded in large language models \[[1' 2024-07-27](http://arxiv.org/abs/2407.19346v1)\]. For instance, univariate polynomial regression has been introduced as a function class that allows for clearer visualization and understanding of prompting and alignment mechanisms in transformer-based models \[[1' 2024-07-27](http://arxiv.org/abs/2407.19346v1)\]. Additionally, in the field of protein engineering, meta-learning strategies have been developed to address data scarcity, enabling models to generalize effectively even with limited task-specific data \[[6' 2024-10-10](http://arxiv.org/abs/2410.08355v3)\]. Similarly, in reinforcement learning, novel data acquisition methods like AD$^\varepsilon$ have been proposed to reduce dependency on optimal policy-generated data, allowing for in-context learning from synthetic noise-induced curricula \[[13' 2023-12-19](http://arxiv.org/abs/2312.12275v3)\]. These advancements highlight the growing interest in refining ICL methodologies to enhance performance and adaptability across diverse applications.

### <a id='category-model-debiasing-techniques'></a>Model Debiasing Techniques: 5 papers found.
Model debiasing techniques have been extensively explored to address biases in various machine learning tasks, including scene graph generation, language models, and multi-label text classification. In scene graph generation, biases in data lead to models favoring common predicates over informative ones, resulting in loss of precise information \[[1' 2024-07-27](http://arxiv.org/abs/2407.19346v1)\]. To tackle this, DB-SGG introduces Semantic Debiasing (SD) and Balanced Predicate Learning (BPL), which address semantic space and training sample imbalances \[[1' 2024-07-27](http://arxiv.org/abs/2407.19346v1)\]. Similarly, for language models, FineDeb proposes a two-phase debiasing framework that first debiases embeddings and then fine-tunes the model on a language modeling objective, achieving stronger debiasing than existing methods \[[2' 2023-08-10](http://arxiv.org/abs/2308.05286v2)\]. In multi-label text classification, the DENN framework mitigates embedding alignment and confidence estimation biases by introducing debiased contrastive learning and confidence estimation strategies, improving prediction performance without adding extra parameters \[[5' 2024-09-20](http://arxiv.org/abs/2409.13884v1)\]. Additionally, DDB leverages diffusion models to generate synthetic data, enhancing debiasing by addressing bias-conflicting training samples memorization \[[4' 2025-04-15](http://arxiv.org/abs/2504.10987v1)\]. These approaches highlight the importance of addressing both data-level and model-level biases to improve fairness and performance across diverse tasks.
