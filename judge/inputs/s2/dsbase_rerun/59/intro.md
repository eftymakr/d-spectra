## Related Works

Fairness in re-ranking has garnered significant attention in recent years, with a growing body of research exploring the trade-offs between ranking accuracy and item fairness \[[1' 2024-04-27](http://arxiv.org/abs/2404.17826v1)\]. Early work on fairness-aware re-ranking focused on balancing accuracy and provider-side fairness, introducing methods such as the Fairness-Aware Re-ranking (FAR) algorithm, which iteratively generates rankings by trading off accuracy and provider coverage \[[2' 2018-09-09](http://arxiv.org/abs/1809.02921v2)\]. This approach incorporates personalized user tolerance towards provider diversification, demonstrating that fairness can be improved with a slight sacrifice in accuracy \[[2' 2018-09-09](http://arxiv.org/abs/1809.02921v2)\].

The concept of fairness in recommendation systems has also been extended to include both consumer and producer fairness, as seen in the CPFair approach, which integrates fairness constraints from both sides in a joint optimization framework \[[7' 2022-04-17](http://arxiv.org/abs/2204.08085v1)\]. This work highlights the importance of considering the two-sided marketplace in recommender systems, where both users and providers are stakeholders \[[7' 2022-04-17](http://arxiv.org/abs/2204.08085v1)\]. Similarly, the P-MMF method addresses provider fairness by formulating the problem as a resource allocation task, aiming to improve the utility of the worst-performing providers \[[21' 2023-03-12](http://arxiv.org/abs/2303.06660v1)\].

The accuracy-fairness trade-off has been a central theme in fairness-aware machine learning, with several studies exploring the theoretical and practical implications of this trade-off. The work by \[[15' 2024-05-24](http://arxiv.org/abs/2405.15443v2)\] introduces a causal perspective to analyze the fairness-accuracy trade-off, proposing the path-specific excess loss (PSEL) to quantify how much the predictor's loss increases when a causal fairness constraint is enforced. This approach provides a more nuanced understanding of the trade-off between fairness and accuracy \[[15' 2024-05-24](http://arxiv.org/abs/2405.15443v2)\].

The fairness-accuracy trade-off has also been examined from an economic perspective, drawing parallels between re-ranking and the dynamics of economic transactions. The Tax-rank method, for instance, reformulates fair re-ranking as a taxation process, leveraging the Sinkhorn algorithm in optimal transport to optimize the objective \[[1' 2024-04-27](http://arxiv.org/abs/2404.17826v1)\]. This approach theoretically demonstrates continuity and controllability over accuracy loss, offering a superior tax policy for fair re-ranking \[[1' 2024-04-27](http://arxiv.org/abs/2404.17826v1)\].

In addition to these methods, there has been a growing interest in understanding the fairness-accuracy trade-off through the lens of diversity and inclusion. The work by \[[12' 2023-05-16](http://arxiv.org/abs/2305.09319v1)\] explores the relationship between diversity and fairness in information access systems, arguing that fostering diversity can lead to fair outcomes without necessarily implying fairness itself \[[12' 2023-05-16](http://arxiv.org/abs/2305.09319v1)\]. This insight is supported by the study on diversity-based re-ranking, which uses image fairness representations to incorporate fairness with respect to demographic groups \[[4' 2018-09-10](http://arxiv.org/abs/1809.03577v1)\].
