## Related Works

Instruction-following in natural language processing has attracted significant attention in recent years, with a growing body of research focused on improving the ability of models to align with user instructions. Prior work has primarily explored instruction tuning, where models are fine-tuned on instruction-response pairs to enhance their ability to follow instructions \[[7' 2023-04-06](http://arxiv.org/abs/2304.03277v1)\]. This approach, however, often requires extensive computational resources due to the need to re-encode the entire corpus for each new instruction \[[1' 2025-05-27](http://arxiv.org/abs/2505.21439v1)\]. 

To address this limitation, several studies have investigated alternative methods for instruction following. For instance, Lin et al. \[[3' 2024-05-30](http://arxiv.org/abs/2405.19874v3)\] proposed URIAL, an in-context learning method that aligns base LLMs using only three examples, achieving non-trivial instruction following performance. However, this approach still underperforms compared to instruction fine-tuning on established benchmarks like MT-Bench, particularly with more capable base models \[[3' 2024-05-30](http://arxiv.org/abs/2405.19874v3)\]. This highlights the trade-off between computational efficiency and performance in instruction-following tasks.

Another line of research explores implicit instruction tuning, where models are adapted without explicit instruction-response pairs. For example, \[[5' 2024-09-21](http://arxiv.org/abs/2409.14254v1)\] found that training solely on responses, without corresponding instructions, can still yield instruction following, suggesting that pretrained models may already possess some instruction-response mapping. However, this approach is not always effective, especially when instructions are significantly different from those in the training domain \[[5' 2024-09-21](http://arxiv.org/abs/2409.14254v1)\]. 

Efforts to reduce the amount of data required for instruction tuning have also been explored. LIMIT \[[6' 2023-11-22](http://arxiv.org/abs/2311.13133v1)\] demonstrated that small, high-quality datasets can suffice for general-purpose instruction following, achieving good performance on both traditional NLP benchmarks and model-based evaluations. This suggests that instruction tuning can be effective even with limited data, provided the data is carefully selected \[[6' 2023-11-22](http://arxiv.org/abs/2311.13133v1)\].

Additionally, the role of instruction-following in speech recognition has been explored, with models trained to interpret and execute free-form text instructions \[[8' 2023-09-18](http://arxiv.org/abs/2309.09843v1)\]. This work highlights the potential of instruction-following training to advance speech foundation models, enabling a wide range of tasks such as transcript manipulation and summarization without relying on predefined command sets \[[8' 2023-09-18](http://arxiv.org/abs/2309.09843v1)\].

Finally, the importance of instruction-following in ethical tuning of large language models has been emphasized, with efforts to create datasets containing toxic or unethical instructions to improve model safety and ethical awareness \[[9' 2023-11-30](http://arxiv.org/abs/2311.18215v1)\]. This work underscores the broader implications of instruction-following beyond mere task performance, extending to ethical and safety considerations \[[9' 2023-11-30](http://arxiv.org/abs/2311.18215v1)\].

Instruction-Aware Embedding Frameworks have been actively explored to enhance the ability of models to follow user instructions in various tasks. Recent works such as InF-IR \[[1' 2025-05-27](http://arxiv.org/abs/2505.21439v1)\] introduce a large-scale training corpus with <instruction, query, passage> triplets to improve retrieval models' instruction-following capabilities, demonstrating significant improvements in p-MRR scores. Similarly, \[[2' 2024-10-18](http://arxiv.org/abs/2410.14516v5)\] investigates how LLMs encode information related to instruction-following success, identifying an instruction-following dimension in the input embedding space that predicts compliance with user instructions. Additionally, \[[3' 2024-05-30](http://arxiv.org/abs/2405.19874v3)\] proposes Multi-Task Label Embedding to convert labels into semantic vectors, enabling better performance in multi-task text classification by leveraging semantic correlations. The role of embeddings in delayed generalization, or grokking, is also examined in \[[4' 2023-03-18](http://arxiv.org/abs/2303.10475v8)\], highlighting their importance in modular arithmetic tasks and the mechanisms behind embedding update dynamics and bilinear coupling. These studies collectively emphasize the importance of embedding-based approaches in improving instruction-following, multi-task learning, and generalization in neural networks.
