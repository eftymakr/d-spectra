## Related Works

Semi-supervised learning (SSL) has emerged as a powerful paradigm for improving model performance by leveraging both labeled and unlabeled data. However, traditional SSL methods assume that labeled and unlabeled data share the same class distribution, which is often violated in practice due to the presence of outliers—unlabeled data from unknown classes \[[1' 2021-05-28](http://arxiv.org/abs/2105.14148v2)\]. These outliers are typically treated as noise, leading to significant performance degradation in SSL models \[[2' 2023-11-17](http://arxiv.org/abs/2311.10572v1)\]. To address this challenge, several approaches have been proposed to handle open-set semi-supervised learning (OSSL), where the goal is to classify inliers and detect outliers simultaneously \[[3' 2021-06-29](http://arxiv.org/abs/2107.08943v3)\].

Early methods, such as FixMatch, focus on consistency regularization between pseudo-labeled and true labels, but they are not robust to outliers \[[4' 2023-08-25](http://arxiv.org/abs/2308.13168v1)\]. More recently, OpenMatch was introduced to address this issue by integrating novelty detection through one-vs-all (OVA) classifiers, which output confidence scores for inlier detection \[[5' 2023-03-21](http://arxiv.org/abs/2303.12091v4)\]. Similarly, SSB improves inlier classification and outlier detection by incorporating high-confidence pseudo-labeled data and utilizing non-linear transformations to separate features for inlier classification and outlier detection \[[6' 2023-05-23](http://arxiv.org/abs/2305.13802v3)\].

Another approach, OpenCoS, leverages self-supervised contrastive learning to identify out-of-class samples in the unlabeled dataset, then uses one-hot pseudo-labels and soft-labels for inliers and outliers, respectively \[[7' 2024-05-11](http://arxiv.org/abs/2405.06979v2)\]. IOMatch further improves upon these methods by jointly utilizing inliers and outliers, even when distinguishing them is difficult, through a multi-binary classifier that treats outliers as a single new class \[[8' 2023-01-24](http://arxiv.org/abs/2301.10127v3)\].

Online Open-set Semi-supervised Object Detection (OSSOD) also tackles the challenge of distinguishing out-of-distribution (OOD) instances during pseudo-labeling by proposing an end-to-end online framework that improves both performance and efficiency \[[10' 2024-03-15](http://arxiv.org/abs/2403.10658v1)\].

This issue is exacerbated by the long-tailed distribution of user-item interactions, where a small number of items receive the majority of interactions, further skewing model performance \[[23' 2022-10-31](http://arxiv.org/abs/2211.01154v1)\]. In contrast, multi-model strategies introduce diversity by constructing multiple models with different biases, enabling the system to better capture the nuances of user preferences and detect outliers or unknown concepts \[[23' 2022-10-31](http://arxiv.org/abs/2211.01154v1)\]. By leveraging prediction disagreements among these models, the system can enhance robustness and accuracy, particularly in scenarios with insufficient labeled data or the presence of outliers \[[23' 2022-10-31](http://arxiv.org/abs/2211.01154v1)\]. This approach aligns with broader efforts in semi-supervised learning to improve robustness by incorporating diverse perspectives, as seen in methods like Diversify and Conquer (DAC) \[[23' 2022-10-31](http://arxiv.org/abs/2211.01154v1)\].
