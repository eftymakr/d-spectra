## Related Works

Multi-token prediction (MTP) has emerged as a promising pre-training objective for language models, offering potential improvements in downstream performance, inference speed, and training efficiency \[[1' 2024-04-30](http://arxiv.org/abs/2404.19737v1)\]. Unlike traditional next-token prediction (NTP), which predicts only the immediate next token, MTP extends the prediction horizon by forecasting the next $k$ tokens at each step using multiple prediction heads \[[1' 2024-04-30](http://arxiv.org/abs/2404.19737v1)\]. This approach has shown significant gains in generative tasks, particularly for large models, with studies reporting up to 17% improvement on benchmarks like MBPP and 12% on HumanEval \[[1' 2024-04-30](http://arxiv.org/abs/2404.19737v1)\]. Furthermore, MTP has been found to enhance inference speed, with models trained using 4-token prediction achieving up to 3 times faster inference times \[[1' 2024-04-30](http://arxiv.org/abs/2404.19737v1)\].

However, prior research has highlighted challenges in applying MTP to smaller language models (SLMs), where performance is often suboptimal \[[5' 2025-02-13](http://arxiv.org/abs/2502.09419v1)\]. This limitation has motivated the exploration of curriculum learning strategies to gradually introduce MTP during pre-training. Curriculum learning has been widely studied in reinforcement learning and other domains, where it is used to shape learning by presenting tasks in increasing order of difficulty \[[3' 2020-03-10](http://arxiv.org/abs/2003.04960v2)\]. In the context of MTP, two curriculum variants have been proposed: a forward curriculum, which transitions from NTP to MTP, and a reverse curriculum, which starts with MTP and moves toward NTP \[[1' 2024-04-30](http://arxiv.org/abs/2404.19737v1)\]. The forward curriculum enables SLMs to better leverage MTP during pre-training, leading to improved downstream NTP performance and generative output quality, while retaining the benefits of self-speculative decoding \[[1' 2024-04-30](http://arxiv.org/abs/2404.19737v1)\]. In contrast, the reverse curriculum achieves stronger NTP performance and output quality but fails to provide any self-speculative decoding benefits \[[1' 2024-04-30](http://arxiv.org/abs/2404.19737v1)\].

Curriculum learning has also been applied to code language models, where it has been shown to significantly improve performance on code execution tasks, though its impact on code completion is less pronounced \[[7' 2024-07-14](http://arxiv.org/abs/2407.10194v1)\]. Similarly, curriculum learning has been explored in small-scale language models, where fine-grained curricula based on linguistic acquisition theories have been found to outperform non-curriculum baselines \[[10' 2024-10-30](http://arxiv.org/abs/2410.22886v2)\]. These findings suggest that curriculum learning can be a powerful tool for adapting MTP to smaller models, though the effectiveness may vary depending on the task and model architecture.

### <a id='category-multi-token-prediction-(mtp)-methodology'></a>Multi-token Prediction (MTP) Methodology: 4 papers found.
Multi-token prediction (MTP) has emerged as a promising alternative to next-token prediction (NTP) in training large language models, with several studies exploring its benefits and challenges. Unlike NTP, which predicts one token at a time, MTP involves predicting multiple future tokens simultaneously, often using independent output heads while sharing a common model trunk \[[1' 2024-04-30](http://arxiv.org/abs/2404.19737v1)\]. This approach has been shown to improve downstream performance, particularly in generative tasks like coding, where models trained with MTP outperform NTP baselines by significant margins \[[1' 2024-04-30](http://arxiv.org/abs/2404.19737v1)\]. Additionally, MTP can enhance inference efficiency, with some models demonstrating up to a 3x speedup in inference time \[[1' 2024-04-30](http://arxiv.org/abs/2404.19737v1)\]. However, integrating MTP into pre-trained models poses challenges, as hidden layers in NTP-trained models are highly specialized for sequential prediction, making adaptation difficult \[[3' 2020-03-10](http://arxiv.org/abs/2003.04960v2)\]. To address these limitations, researchers have proposed variants such as leap multi-token prediction (L-MTP), which introduces a leap-based mechanism to skip intermediate tokens and improve long-range dependency capture \[[2' 2025-05-23](http://arxiv.org/abs/2505.17505v2)\]. Furthermore, the effectiveness of MTP is data-dependent and scales with model size, suggesting that larger models benefit more from this approach \[[3' 2020-03-10](http://arxiv.org/abs/2003.04960v2)\]. These findings highlight the potential of MTP while underscoring the need for further research into its integration and optimization.

### <a id='category-curriculum-learning-strategies'></a>Curriculum Learning Strategies: 3 papers found.
Curriculum learning in reinforcement learning (RL) has emerged as a promising approach to improve learning efficiency by structuring the sequence of tasks or data samples to guide the agent's exploration \[[3' 2020-03-10](http://arxiv.org/abs/2003.04960v2)\]. This strategy is inspired by pedagogical principles, where learners progress from simpler to more complex tasks, enabling them to build foundational knowledge before tackling harder problems \[[8' 2019-01-31](http://arxiv.org/abs/1901.11478v3)\]. In RL, curriculum learning has been applied to shape exploration by gradually increasing task complexity, thereby reducing the time required to reach a desired performance level \[[2' 2025-05-23](http://arxiv.org/abs/2505.17505v2)\]. Recent research has explored various objectives for curriculum design, such as improving initial performance, minimizing suboptimal actions during exploration, and discovering better policies \[[8' 2019-01-31](http://arxiv.org/abs/1901.11478v3)\]. One notable approach, ProCuRL, is based on the Zone of Proximal Development (ZPD) concept, which suggests that optimal learning occurs when tasks are neither too easy nor too difficult \[[11' 2023-04-25](http://arxiv.org/abs/2304.12877v1)\]. This strategy has been shown to accelerate the training of deep RL agents across multiple domains, with minimal hyperparameter tuning required \[[11' 2023-04-25](http://arxiv.org/abs/2304.12877v1)\]. Additionally, curriculum learning has been adapted to address challenges in multi-task settings, where the goal is to design curricula that maximize learning progress while adapting to varying task complexities \[[3' 2020-03-10](http://arxiv.org/abs/2003.04960v2)\]. These methods highlight the versatility of curriculum learning in enhancing RL performance, particularly in scenarios where direct interaction with the environment is costly or impractical \[[3' 2020-03-10](http://arxiv.org/abs/2003.04960v2)\].
