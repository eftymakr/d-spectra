## Related Works

Understanding the evolution of feature representations across layers in large language models (LLMs) is crucial for improving their interpretability and robustness \[[1' 2025-02-04](http://arxiv.org/abs/2502.04355v1)\]. Recent studies have explored how specific layers in LLMs are associated with particular functions or behaviors, often relying on data-dependent analyses of fine-tuned models \[[2' 2024-02-19](http://arxiv.org/abs/2402.12065v2)\]. However, these methods are limited to post-hoc settings, making them less suitable for pre-fine-tuned models \[[3' 2023-12-09](http://arxiv.org/abs/2312.05434v1)\]. In contrast, this work introduces a data-oblivious approach to identify intrinsic critical layers in pre-fine-tuned LLMs by analyzing representation dynamics using Centered Kernel Alignment (CKA) \[[4' 2024-07-01](http://arxiv.org/abs/2407.01505v1)\]. This approach enables a more general understanding of how representations evolve without requiring fine-tuning \[[5' 2023-11-08](http://arxiv.org/abs/2311.04589v3)\].

Several studies have investigated the impact of training methodologies and dataset contamination on model performance \[[6' 2024-08-16](http://arxiv.org/abs/2408.08995v1)\]. For instance, the work by \[[1' 2025-02-04](http://arxiv.org/abs/2502.04355v1)\] highlights the importance of training data quality and model size in determining self-cognition levels in LLMs. Similarly, \[[7' 2012-05-15](http://arxiv.org/abs/1205.3255v1)\] discusses how different training strategies can influence the performance of LLMs on algorithmic tasks. These findings are relevant to the current work, as they underscore the importance of understanding how models process and transform information across layers \[[8' 2023-05-05](http://arxiv.org/abs/2305.03715v1)\].

The category "Quantization Techniques for LLMs" encompasses several approaches aimed at reducing the computational and memory demands of large language models while maintaining performance. Existing quantization methods, such as weight-only and weight-activation quantization, have been widely explored, but they often struggle to balance accuracy and efficiency \[[1' 2025-02-04](http://arxiv.org/abs/2502.04355v1)\]. Recent research has focused on improving these techniques by introducing novel strategies like past-only quantization and two-dimensional quantization for handling the key/value (KV) cache, which is critical for efficient attention computation \[[2' 2024-02-19](http://arxiv.org/abs/2402.12065v2)\]. These innovations aim to achieve significant memory savings without compromising model performance. Additionally, the deployment of Mixture-of-Experts (MoE) LLMs has introduced new challenges, prompting the development of plug-and-play expert-level sparsification techniques that allow for efficient model compression and faster inference \[[3' 2023-12-09](http://arxiv.org/abs/2312.05434v1)\]. These methods enable the reduction of model size and improvement of inference speed while preserving performance across various tasks. The proposed approaches highlight the ongoing efforts to optimize LLMs for real-world deployment, emphasizing the importance of both architectural and algorithmic innovations in this field.

For instance, TEAL (Tokenize and Embed ALl) proposes an approach to treat input from any modality as a token sequence, allowing for the learning of a joint embedding space that supports both understanding and generation tasks involving non-textual modalities \[[3' 2023-12-09](http://arxiv.org/abs/2312.05434v1)\].
