## Related Works

Understanding the evolution of feature representations across layers in large language models (LLMs) is crucial for improving their interpretability and robustness \[[1' 2025-02-04](http://arxiv.org/abs/2502.04355v1)\]. Recent studies have explored how specific layers in LLMs are associated with particular functions or behaviors, often relying on data-dependent analyses of fine-tuned models \[[2' 2024-02-19](http://arxiv.org/abs/2402.12065v2)\]. However, these methods are limited to post-hoc settings, making them less suitable for pre-fine-tuned models \[[3' 2023-12-09](http://arxiv.org/abs/2312.05434v1)\]. In contrast, this work introduces a data-oblivious approach to identify intrinsic critical layers in pre-fine-tuned LLMs by analyzing representation dynamics using Centered Kernel Alignment (CKA) \[[4' 2024-07-01](http://arxiv.org/abs/2407.01505v1)\]. This approach enables a more general understanding of how representations evolve without requiring fine-tuning \[[5' 2023-11-08](http://arxiv.org/abs/2311.04589v3)\].

Several studies have investigated the impact of training methodologies and dataset contamination on model performance \[[6' 2024-08-16](http://arxiv.org/abs/2408.08995v1)\]. For instance, the work by \[[1' 2025-02-04](http://arxiv.org/abs/2502.04355v1)\] highlights the importance of training data quality and model size in determining self-cognition levels in LLMs. Similarly, \[[7' 2012-05-15](http://arxiv.org/abs/1205.3255v1)\] discusses how different training strategies can influence the performance of LLMs on algorithmic tasks. These findings are relevant to the current work, as they underscore the importance of understanding how models process and transform information across layers \[[8' 2023-05-05](http://arxiv.org/abs/2305.03715v1)\].

In the context of multi-modal LLMs, \[[9' 2012-03-02](http://arxiv.org/abs/1203.0550v3)\] presents TEAL, an approach that enables frozen LLMs to perform both understanding and generation tasks involving non-textual modalities by treating all inputs as token sequences and learning a joint embedding space \[[10' 2024-02-22](http://arxiv.org/abs/2402.14800v2)\]. This aligns with the current work's focus on analyzing representation dynamics across layers, as both approaches aim to improve the interpretability and performance of LLMs \[[11' 2023-10-02](http://arxiv.org/abs/2310.00905v2)\].

Additionally, \[[12' 2024-11-16](http://arxiv.org/abs/2411.10654v1)\] introduces WKVQuant, a PTQ framework designed for quantizing weights and the key/value (KV) cache of LLMs, which can significantly reduce memory consumption while maintaining performance \[[13' 2021-11-18](http://arxiv.org/abs/2111.09854v1)\]. This is relevant to the current work, as quantization techniques can influence the representation dynamics of LLMs during inference \[[14' 2018-12-19](http://arxiv.org/abs/1812.08238v3)\].

The work by \[[15' 2024-02-22](http://arxiv.org/abs/2402.14679v2)\] investigates the multilingual safety of LLMs, highlighting the importance of developing safety alignment for non-English languages \[[16' 2011-01-10](http://arxiv.org/abs/1101.1792v3)\]. This is a critical consideration when evaluating the robustness of LLMs across different languages and tasks \[[17' 2023-06-23](http://arxiv.org/abs/2306.13549v4)\].
