## Related Works

Conversational Recommender Systems (CRSs) have gained significant attention for their ability to engage users in multi-turn interactions to infer preferences and deliver personalized recommendations \[[1' 2023-10-31](http://arxiv.org/abs/2310.20109v1)\]. However, accurately capturing user preferences remains a fundamental challenge, especially when preferences are complex and multifaceted \[[1' 2023-10-31](http://arxiv.org/abs/2310.20109v1)\]. Traditional approaches often rely on handcrafted reward functions, which may not align well with user intent \[[1' 2023-10-31](http://arxiv.org/abs/2310.20109v1)\]. This has led to the exploration of intrinsic reward learning, where rewards are learned from user interactions rather than being manually defined \[[1' 2023-10-31](http://arxiv.org/abs/2310.20109v1)\]. 

Several works have focused on improving the quality of recommendations through user feedback. For example, \[[5' 2019-10-21](http://arxiv.org/abs/1910.12735v2)\] introduced a synthetic feedback loop to simulate user behavior and enhance recommendation accuracy by augmenting observed user preferences with generated feedback. Similarly, \[[6' 2024-10-26](http://arxiv.org/abs/2410.20027v2)\] proposed an agentic feedback loop that enables collaboration between a recommendation agent and a user agent, leading to improved performance by up to 21.12% over a single user agent. This approach emphasizes the iterative refinement of both agents based on each other's feedback \[[6' 2024-10-26](http://arxiv.org/abs/2410.20027v2)\].

In the context of recommendation systems, the use of large language models (LLMs) has also been explored to provide human-interpretable explanations alongside recommendations. \[[2' 2024-10-30](http://arxiv.org/abs/2410.23180v1)\] introduced ReasoningRec, a framework that leverages LLMs to model user and item preferences and generate explanations. This approach enhances recommendation accuracy while providing interpretable feedback, achieving up to 12.5% improvement over state-of-the-art methods \[[2' 2024-10-30](http://arxiv.org/abs/2410.23180v1)\]. 

Another line of work focuses on the role of dialogue history in conversational agents. \[[9' 2018-12-02](http://arxiv.org/abs/1812.00350v1)\] investigated the impact of varying amounts of dialogue history on dialogue reward prediction, finding that longer histories (up to 25 sentences) lead to better reward prediction performance \[[9' 2018-12-02](http://arxiv.org/abs/1812.00350v1)\]. This suggests that incorporating sufficient context is crucial for accurate reward modeling in conversational settings.

Additionally, \[[12' 2024-06-18](http://arxiv.org/abs/2406.12845v1)\] proposed a method for building interpretable reward models by training on multi-dimensional absolute-rating data, allowing for human-understandable objectives such as honesty or safety. This approach enhances the interpretability of reward models, which is essential for aligning large language models with human preferences \[[12' 2024-06-18](http://arxiv.org/abs/2406.12845v1)\].

To address the challenge of sparse data in recommendation systems, \[[5' 2019-10-21](http://arxiv.org/abs/1910.12735v2)\] proposed a synthetic feedback loop that simulates user behavior to enrich the learning of user preferences. This method improves recommendation performance by incorporating unobserved feedback into the training process \[[5' 2019-10-21](http://arxiv.org/abs/1910.12735v2)\].
