## Related Works

Sensor fusion in autonomous driving and robotics has been extensively studied, with radar and camera systems being particularly popular due to their complementary sensing capabilities \[[1' 2023-09-02](http://arxiv.org/abs/2309.00787v2)\]. However, accurate calibration between these sensors remains a critical challenge. Traditional calibration methods often rely on target-based approaches, which require complex setups and are not suitable for real-time or online calibration \[[4' 2023-07-28](http://arxiv.org/abs/2307.15264v1)\]. To address these limitations, several researchers have proposed targetless calibration techniques. For instance, a method leveraging deep learning to extract common features from radar and camera data was introduced to enable online targetless calibration \[[1' 2023-09-02](http://arxiv.org/abs/2309.00787v2)\]. This approach implicitly uses common features to match objects across sensor modalities, leading to improved calibration accuracy.

Another approach, the Dual Perspective Fusion Transformer (DPFT), was designed to overcome the sparsity of radar point clouds and improve robustness against adverse weather conditions \[[2' 2024-04-03](http://arxiv.org/abs/2404.03015v2)\]. DPFT utilizes lower-level radar data and projections in both the camera and ground planes to enhance fusion performance. Similarly, the work on 3D radar and camera co-calibration proposed a flexible and accurate method that does not require a dedicated calibration environment \[[4' 2023-07-28](http://arxiv.org/abs/2307.15264v1)\]. This method uses a single corner reflector and ROS to collect radar and camera data, then solves the perspective-n-point (PnP) problem to obtain the extrinsic calibration matrix.

The need for robust and flexible calibration methods has also driven research into spatiotemporal calibration. A targetless spatiotemporal calibration algorithm was introduced that leverages the radar unit's ability to measure its own ego-velocity, enabling calibration in arbitrary environments without specialized infrastructure \[[5' 2022-11-03](http://arxiv.org/abs/2211.01871v4)\]. This method addresses the dynamic nature of sensor calibration during vehicle operation.

In addition to calibration, several works have focused on improving the fusion of radar and camera data for object detection and tracking. For example, the CramNet approach introduces a ray-constrained cross-attention mechanism to resolve geometric ambiguities between camera and radar features \[[16' 2022-10-17](http://arxiv.org/abs/2210.09267v2)\]. This method supports robust 3D object detection even when one sensor malfunctions. Similarly, the RCM-Fusion method proposes a multi-level fusion strategy that combines both feature and instance-level information to achieve state-of-the-art performance in 3D object detection \[[12' 2023-07-17](http://arxiv.org/abs/2307.10249v5)\].

Recent advances in neural architectures have also contributed to improved fusion techniques.
