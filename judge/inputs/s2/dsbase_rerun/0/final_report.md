Traditional approaches to addressing economic inequality often rely on static tax systems, such as the U.S. federal income tax, which lack the adaptability needed to respond to dynamic economic conditions \[[6' 2023-09-15](http://arxiv.org/abs/2309.09992v2)\]. These systems are typically based on fixed tax brackets and do not account for taxpayer heterogeneity or irrational behavior \[[6' 2023-09-15](http://arxiv.org/abs/2309.09992v2)\]. In contrast, models like the Saez Optimal Taxation framework attempt to dynamically adjust tax rates to optimize equity and productivity \[[6' 2023-09-15](http://arxiv.org/abs/2309.09992v2)\]. However, these models often fail to incorporate the complexity of real-world taxpayer behavior and the need for adaptive policy-making \[[6' 2023-09-15](http://arxiv.org/abs/2309.09992v2)\].

Recent advances in artificial intelligence have introduced new methodologies for policy design and evaluation. For instance, the use of large language models (LLMs) in conjunction with agent-based modeling (ABM) has been explored to simulate complex economic systems and design adaptive tax policies \[[6' 2023-09-15](http://arxiv.org/abs/2309.09992v2)\]. The integration of LLMs into policy-making processes has also been discussed in the context of improving reasoning capabilities and aligning model outputs with human expectations \[[1' 2023-09-05](http://arxiv.org/abs/2309.02144v1)\]. The concept of alignment fine-tuning (AFT) has been proposed to enhance the reliability of LLMs in reasoning tasks by calibrating their responses to ensure consistency with correct outcomes \[[1' 2023-09-05](http://arxiv.org/abs/2309.02144v1)\].

### <a id='category-alignment-and-calibration-of-llms-for-policy-tasks'></a>Alignment and Calibration of LLMs for Policy Tasks: 3 papers found.
The category "Alignment and Calibration of LLMs for Policy Tasks" encompasses efforts to improve the reliability and effectiveness of large language models (LLMs) in policy-related applications. Recent research highlights the importance of fine-tuning LLMs using chain-of-thought (COT) reasoning to enhance their reasoning capabilities, although this approach can lead to an "Assessment Misalignment" issue where models assign higher scores to subpar COTs \[[1' 2023-09-05](http://arxiv.org/abs/2309.02144v1)\]. To address this, the Alignment Fine-Tuning (AFT) paradigm was introduced, which includes three key steps: COT training, generating and categorizing COT responses, and calibrating scores with a constraint alignment loss to ensure positive responses outperform negative ones while keeping negative scores within a reasonable range \[[1' 2023-09-05](http://arxiv.org/abs/2309.02144v1)\]. This work also examines existing ranking-based alignment methods like DPO, RRHF, and PRO, identifying that the constraint component, often overlooked, is critical for their performance \[[1' 2023-09-05](http://arxiv.org/abs/2309.02144v1)\]. Additionally, there are studies that explore the validity of conventional personality questionnaires in capturing human-like traits in LLMs, aiming to understand the discrepancy between self-reported traits and actual behavior \[[3' 2025-01-15](http://arxiv.org/abs/2501.08573v1)\]. These efforts underscore the broader need for aligning LLMs with real-world policy tasks, ensuring they provide accurate, reliable, and equitable outcomes.

In multi-agent systems, ABM has also been applied to cooperative decision-making, where models like ATT-MADDPG \[[3' 2025-01-15](http://arxiv.org/abs/2501.08573v1)\] incorporate attention mechanisms to dynamically adapt to changing teammate policies, enhancing the efficiency of policy modeling in complex environments. Furthermore, ABM has been instrumental in public health policy, as seen in the development of ESOP \[[4' 2022-09-12](http://arxiv.org/abs/2209.05109v1)\], which uses machine learning to optimize lockdown schedules by balancing health and economic outcomes. These studies collectively underscore the versatility of ABM in simulating real-world dynamics and evaluating policy effectiveness across diverse domains.
