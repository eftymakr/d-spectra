## Related Works

Fund allocation in financial markets has attracted significant research interest, with a growing emphasis on integrating forecasting accuracy with risk-aware decision-making. Early approaches often relied on simple prediction-only strategies, which suffer from a mismatch between forecasting and allocation objectives \[[8' 2025-03-05](http://arxiv.org/abs/2503.03165v1)\]. These methods typically assume that accurate forecasts alone are sufficient for optimal allocation, ignoring the inherent uncertainty in time series predictions \[[16' 2024-10-24](http://arxiv.org/abs/2410.19025v1)\]. This limitation has led to the development of more sophisticated frameworks that explicitly address the interplay between forecasting and allocation \[[1' 2021-10-21](http://arxiv.org/abs/2110.11156v3)\].

Recent work on time series forecasting has introduced advanced models that improve prediction accuracy, such as the Time-Series Mixer (TSMixer) \[[3' 2025-02-25](http://arxiv.org/abs/2502.18410v2)\], which leverages multi-layer perceptrons (MLPs) to capture both spatial and temporal dependencies. Similarly, the TSKANMixer model, which integrates Kolmogorov-Arnold Networks (KANs) with TSMixer, has demonstrated improved performance in forecasting tasks \[[3' 2025-02-25](http://arxiv.org/abs/2502.18410v2)\]. These models are critical for providing reliable forecasts, which are then used in allocation strategies. However, they often do not account for the uncertainty in predictions, which can lead to suboptimal or even risky allocation decisions \[[16' 2024-10-24](http://arxiv.org/abs/2410.19025v1)\].

To address this, risk-aware frameworks have emerged, such as the Risk-Aware Trading Portfolio Optimization (RATPO) approach, which incorporates market risk (e.g., VaR) and profit&loss measures into the optimization process \[[11' 2025-03-06](http://arxiv.org/abs/2503.04662v1)\]. This aligns with the core idea of the Risk-aware Time-Series Predict-and-Allocate (RTS-PnO) framework, which integrates forecasting and allocation in a unified manner without prior assumptions about the forecasting model \[[1' 2021-10-21](http://arxiv.org/abs/2110.11156v3)\]. The RTS-PnO framework also introduces adaptive forecasting uncertainty calibration, which is essential for improving the reliability of allocation decisions in the presence of forecast uncertainty \[[16' 2024-10-24](http://arxiv.org/abs/2410.19025v1)\].

Another line of research focuses on the evaluation of forecasting models in financial contexts. The FinTSBridge evaluation suite was developed to bridge the gap between advanced forecasting models and financial asset pricing, introducing new metrics such as msIC and msIR to better capture the time series correlation \[[4' 2025-03-10](http://arxiv.org/abs/2503.06928v2)\]. This is particularly relevant for the RTS-PnO framework, which requires robust evaluation metrics to assess the performance of forecasting models in financial applications \[[1' 2021-10-21](http://arxiv.org/abs/2110.11156v3)\].

Additionally, the use of large language models (LLMs) for financial time series forecasting has gained traction, with models like GPT-2 demonstrating the potential of pre-trained models to outperform traditional approaches, even with minimal fine-tuning \[[16' 2024-10-24](http://arxiv.org/abs/2410.19025v1)\].
