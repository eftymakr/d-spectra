## Related Works

Dense retrievers have become a cornerstone of modern information retrieval systems, leveraging pre-trained language models (e.g., BERT, LLaMA) that are fine-tuned via contrastive learning to encode text into dense representations for efficient comparison \[[1' 2022-10-23](http://arxiv.org/abs/2210.12607v1)\]. Recent research has questioned the relative importance of fine-tuning versus pre-training in dense retrievers, with some arguing that retrieval knowledge is primarily acquired during pre-training, and that fine-tuning does not significantly alter this knowledge \[[2' 2021-08-13](http://arxiv.org/abs/2108.06279v2)\]. This claim was initially studied in the context of BERT-based encoders using the DPR (Dense Passage Retrieval) framework as a representative dense retriever \[[3' 2024-08-06](http://arxiv.org/abs/2408.03099v2)\]. However, this analysis was limited in scope, focusing only on a specific encoder architecture and dataset.

To extend this analysis, we examine additional representation approaches, such as the use of CLS tokens versus mean pooling, and compare different backbone architectures, including encoder-only models like BERT and decoder-only models like LLaMA \[[4' 2023-12-10](http://arxiv.org/abs/2312.05934v3)\]. We also evaluate performance across multiple datasets, including MSMARCO and Natural Questions, to determine whether the observed patterns hold universally \[[5' 2022-12-15](http://arxiv.org/abs/2212.07841v2)\]. Our findings confirm that in DPR tuning, pre-trained knowledge underpins retrieval performance, with fine-tuning primarily adjusting neuron activation rather than reorganizing knowledge \[[6' 2020-10-06](http://arxiv.org/abs/2010.02616v1)\]. However, this pattern does not hold for all models, such as mean-pooled (Contriever) and decoder-based (LLaMA) models, where fine-tuning plays a more significant role in shaping retrieval performance \[[7' 2021-04-30](http://arxiv.org/abs/2104.14939v1)\].

Several studies have explored the effectiveness of different fine-tuning strategies in dense retrievers. For instance, compositional fine-tuning (CFT) has been proposed as an approach that decomposes target tasks into component tasks and fine-tunes smaller models on a curriculum of these tasks, achieving better performance than end-to-end learning \[[8' 2022-10-27](http://arxiv.org/abs/2210.15133v1)\]. Similarly, contrastive learning has been widely used to train dense retrievers, with methods such as ANCE and Contriever demonstrating strong performance by constructing pseudo-positive examples from unlabeled data \[[9' 2021-12-16](http://arxiv.org/abs/2112.09118v4)\]. However, the effectiveness of these methods can vary depending on the dataset and the specific task \[[10' 2020-07-01](http://arxiv.org/abs/2007.00808v2)\].

Additionally, research has explored the use of contrastive learning in various domains, including image retrieval and semantic segmentation, where contrastive losses are used to improve the alignment between query and document representations \[[11' 2020-04-27](http://arxiv.org/abs/2004.12817v1)\].
