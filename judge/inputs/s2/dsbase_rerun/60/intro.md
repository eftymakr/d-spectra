## Related Works

Ranking models have traditionally focused on capturing the relative order of predictions, often at the expense of the accuracy of absolute values \[[1' 2021-12-09](http://arxiv.org/abs/2112.07428v2)\]. This limitation has led to a growing body of work on calibration techniques that aim to refine the absolute confidence scores of these models \[[2' 2019-08-14](http://arxiv.org/abs/1908.05164v3)\]. Calibration is essential for downstream tasks that require precise probability estimates, such as recommendation systems and business metrics optimization \[[3' 2022-08-12](http://arxiv.org/abs/2208.06164v2)\].

Several approaches have been proposed to calibrate ranking models. One line of work involves the use of parametric calibration methods, such as Gaussian and Gamma calibration, which map the ranking scores of pre-trained models to well-calibrated preference probabilities \[[1' 2021-12-09](http://arxiv.org/abs/2112.07428v2)\]. These methods are effective but often rely on fixed functional forms, such as piece-wise linear functions, which may limit their flexibility in complex scenarios \[[4' 2014-08-12](http://arxiv.org/abs/1408.2764v2)\]. In contrast, non-parametric approaches, such as temperature scaling, have also been explored, though they may not always achieve the desired level of calibration \[[5' 2023-03-19](http://arxiv.org/abs/2303.10761v1)\].

A more flexible approach is the use of neural networks to model the calibration function. This idea has been explored in the context of deep learning, where neural networks can learn arbitrary monotonic functions that preserve the relative ordering of predictions while adjusting their absolute values \[[6' 2015-09-01](http://arxiv.org/abs/1509.00174v1)\]. Specifically, the Unconstrained Monotonic Neural Network (UMNN) has been proposed as a powerful tool for this task. UMNNs enforce monotonicity by ensuring that the derivative of the output is strictly positive, allowing for greater flexibility in modeling the calibration function \[[2' 2019-08-14](http://arxiv.org/abs/1908.05164v3)\]. This approach has been applied in density estimation and variational inference, demonstrating its effectiveness in capturing complex relationships between inputs and outputs \[[7' 2018-09-11](http://arxiv.org/abs/1809.03941v6)\].

In the context of recommendation systems, joint optimization of ranking and calibration has been explored using contextualized hybrid models. These models aim to improve both ranking performance and calibration by jointly optimizing the logit values and predicted probabilities \[[3' 2022-08-12](http://arxiv.org/abs/2208.06164v2)\]. This approach has been shown to improve both ranking and calibration abilities, as demonstrated in experiments on public and industrial datasets \[[8' 2021-06-15](http://arxiv.org/abs/2106.07998v2)\].

Additionally, recent work has focused on improving the calibration of neural networks by addressing issues such as proximity bias, where models tend to be overconfident in low proximity data \[[9' 2001-05-03](http://arxiv.org/abs/astro-ph/0105045v1)\]. This has led to the development of new calibration algorithms, such as ProCal, which adjust confidence based on proximity to the training data \[[10' 2024-11-05](http://arxiv.org/abs/2411.02988v2)\]. These methods have been evaluated on a variety of datasets and architectures, showing significant improvements in calibration performance \[[11' 2018-02-15](http://arxiv.org/abs/1802.05792v2)\].
