Cross-domain sequential recommendation (CDSR) has gained significant attention in recent years due to its ability to leverage user behavior across multiple domains to improve recommendation performance. Early approaches primarily relied on self-attention mechanisms, which are central to transformer-based models, to capture sequential dependencies \[[1' 2020-12-01](http://arxiv.org/abs/2012.00485v3)\]. However, these methods often overlooked the potential of self-attention to learn cross-domain correlations \[[1' 2020-12-01](http://arxiv.org/abs/2012.00485v3)\]. Several works have attempted to enhance CDSR by incorporating domain-specific components, such as domain-aware modules or graph-based structures \[[2' 2021-08-25](http://arxiv.org/abs/2108.11333v1)\]\[[3' 2024-08-15](http://arxiv.org/abs/2408.08209v2)\]. Moreover, we demonstrate that the deployed KSVD optimization regularizes Primal-Attention with a sharper singular value decay than that of the canonical self-attention, further verifying the great potential of our method.
