## Related Works

Cross-domain sequential recommendation (CDSR) has gained significant attention in recent years due to its ability to leverage user behavior across multiple domains to improve recommendation performance. Early approaches primarily relied on self-attention mechanisms, which are central to transformer-based models, to capture sequential dependencies \[[1' 2020-12-01](http://arxiv.org/abs/2012.00485v3)\]. However, these methods often overlooked the potential of self-attention to learn cross-domain correlations \[[1' 2020-12-01](http://arxiv.org/abs/2012.00485v3)\]. Several works have attempted to enhance CDSR by incorporating domain-specific components, such as domain-aware modules or graph-based structures \[[2' 2021-08-25](http://arxiv.org/abs/2108.11333v1)\]\[[3' 2024-08-15](http://arxiv.org/abs/2408.08209v2)\]. For example, \[[4' 2024-10-17](http://arxiv.org/abs/2410.13588v1)\] introduced a mixed information flow network that considers both behavioral and knowledge transfer, while \[[5' 2022-06-16](http://arxiv.org/abs/2206.08050v1)\] proposed a lightweight self-attentive network to reduce computational overhead. 

In addition to self-attention-based methods, graph neural networks have been explored for modeling cross-domain interactions \[[6' 2018-10-10](http://arxiv.org/abs/1810.04650v2)\]. These approaches often focus on capturing collaborative relationships between items across domains \[[7' 2022-11-02](http://arxiv.org/abs/2211.01297v4)\]. However, they typically assume overlapping users or items, which is not always the case in real-world scenarios \[[8' 2023-04-08](http://arxiv.org/abs/2304.03891v1)\]. To address this, \[[9' 2019-10-06](http://arxiv.org/abs/1910.02448v4)\] proposed a method that leverages both domain and feedback transitions to model user behavior across domains. This work highlights the importance of considering feedback dynamics, such as shifts from negative to positive interactions, which can indicate improved user satisfaction \[[9' 2019-10-06](http://arxiv.org/abs/1910.02448v4)\]. 

Another line of research focuses on improving the efficiency and scalability of CDSR models. For instance, \[[10' 2023-05-31](http://arxiv.org/abs/2305.19798v2)\] introduced a lightweight self-attentive sequential recommendation model that reduces memory usage by leveraging compositional embeddings. Similarly, \[[11' 2020-09-10](http://arxiv.org/abs/2009.04695v3)\] proposed a time interval-enhanced graph neural network to capture temporal patterns in shared-account cross-domain scenarios. These methods aim to address the limitations of traditional RNN-based models, which struggle with modeling complex sequential dependencies \[[11' 2020-09-10](http://arxiv.org/abs/2009.04695v3)\]. 

Recent studies have also explored multi-objective optimization for recommendation tasks, which is relevant to the multi-objective formulation of CDSR \[[12' 2025-04-21](http://arxiv.org/abs/2504.15085v5)\]. For example, \[[13' 2023-10-07](http://arxiv.org/abs/2310.04633v3)\] proposed a multi-objective gradient method that improves the stability of gradient descent by incorporating first and second moments of the gradients. This approach has been applied to multi-objective recommendation systems, where conflicting objectives such as accuracy and diversity must be balanced \[[13' 2023-10-07](http://arxiv.org/abs/2310.04633v3)\]. 

Furthermore, contrastive learning has been used to enhance cross-domain recommendations by aligning representations across domains \[[14' 2022-09-21](http://arxiv.org/abs/2209.10163v1)\]. This approach leverages the mutual information between single-domain and cross-domain representations to improve the quality of recommendations \[[14' 2022-09-21](http://arxiv.org/abs/2209.10163v1)\].
