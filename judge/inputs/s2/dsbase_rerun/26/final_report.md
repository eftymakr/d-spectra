## Related Works

Differential privacy (DP) has emerged as a foundational framework for ensuring privacy in data analysis, offering rigorous mathematical guarantees against privacy breaches \[[1' 2022-08-18](http://arxiv.org/abs/2208.09011v2)\]. Despite its widespread adoption, recent research has highlighted vulnerabilities in its implementation, particularly in distributed settings where servers may deviate from prescribed protocols \[[2' 2024-06-27](http://arxiv.org/abs/2406.18940v2)\]. For instance, malicious servers may sample noise from incorrect distributions or generate correlated noise, undermining the intended privacy guarantees \[[3' 2017-09-15](http://arxiv.org/abs/1709.05361v2)\]. These issues have prompted the development of verifiable DP mechanisms that ensure correctness and transparency in the execution of privacy-preserving algorithms \[[4' 2024-09-16](http://arxiv.org/abs/2409.10667v2)\].

Verifiable differential privacy (VDP) was introduced to address these concerns by requiring the publishing entity to provide a zero-knowledge proof (ZKP) that confirms the output is both differentially private and reliable \[[5' 2020-12-23](http://arxiv.org/abs/2012.12803v3)\]. This approach aims to close the loophole where malicious entities could exploit the randomness of DP as a smokescreen \[[6' 2021-02-25](http://arxiv.org/abs/2102.12621v1)\]. However, the challenge lies in verifying DP without learning anything about the randomness itself \[[7' 2020-06-10](http://arxiv.org/abs/2006.05609v2)\]. This has led to the exploration of hybrid approaches that combine private and public randomness to achieve verifiable DP with theoretical guarantees \[[8' 2022-12-01](http://arxiv.org/abs/2212.00306v2)\].

In the context of local differential privacy (LDP), recent work has focused on preventing malicious clients from compromising LDP schemes through input and output manipulation attacks \[[9' 2016-01-24](http://arxiv.org/abs/1601.06426v3)\]. These attacks can skew the aggregator's results, undermining the privacy guarantees \[[10' 2018-09-06](http://arxiv.org/abs/1809.01794v3)\]. To mitigate this, researchers have proposed verifiable LDP (VLDP) schemes that require only one-time interaction between client and server, offering practical efficiency with client run times under 2 seconds and server run times of 5-7 milliseconds per client \[[11' 2023-10-20](http://arxiv.org/abs/2310.13618v1)\].

The relationship between ZKPs and DP has also been explored, with findings indicating that while ZKPs are sufficient for achieving DP under verifiability requirements, they are not strictly necessary \[[12' 2021-12-10](http://arxiv.org/abs/2112.05307v4)\]. This has led to the development of alternative verification mechanisms that maintain the privacy guarantees without relying on ZKPs \[[13' 2015-04-27](http://arxiv.org/abs/1504.06998v1)\].

In the shuffle model, where data is anonymized before aggregation, recent advances have shown that random shuffling can amplify privacy guarantees, leading to stronger differential privacy assurances \[[14' 2024-12-22](http://arxiv.org/abs/2412.16916v3)\]. This has been further refined to achieve nearly optimal bounds on privacy parameters, demonstrating the effectiveness of shuffling in enhancing privacy \[[15' 2016-08-02](http://arxiv.org/abs/1608.00849v1)\].

### <a id='category-verifiable-differential-privacy-mechanisms'></a>Verifiable Differential Privacy Mechanisms: 3 papers found.
Verifiable Differential Privacy Mechanisms have emerged as a critical area of research to address the limitations of traditional differential privacy (DP) in ensuring both privacy and correctness in data publishing. While DP introduces noise to protect individual privacy, it can be exploited by malicious entities who may manipulate the output to mask their actions \[[1' 2022-08-18](http://arxiv.org/abs/2208.09011v2)\]. To mitigate this, Verifiable Differential Privacy (VDP) was introduced, requiring the publisher to provide a zero-knowledge proof that confirms the output adheres to DP principles without revealing the randomness used \[[1' 2022-08-18](http://arxiv.org/abs/2208.09011v2)\]. This concept was further advanced by proposing methods to mix private and public randomness, enabling practical implementations with theoretical guarantees \[[1' 2022-08-18](http://arxiv.org/abs/2208.09011v2)\]. Subsequent work has focused on automating the verification of DP properties, such as $\varepsilon$-differential privacy, through novel proof techniques like coupling strategies and constraint-based formulations \[[2' 2024-06-27](http://arxiv.org/abs/2406.18940v2)\]. These approaches leverage probabilistic and logical constraints to verify complex algorithms, such as Report Noisy Max and the Exponential Mechanism, demonstrating the feasibility of automated verification \[[2' 2024-06-27](http://arxiv.org/abs/2406.18940v2)\]. Additionally, formal models have been developed to verify differentially private systems by tracking privacy leakage bounds through relation families, offering a sound basis for proving DP compliance in interactive systems \[[3' 2017-09-15](http://arxiv.org/abs/1709.05361v2)\]. These contributions collectively highlight the growing interest in verifiable DP mechanisms, emphasizing both theoretical advancements and practical implementations.

### <a id='category-local-differential-privacy-and-attack-mitigation'></a>Local Differential Privacy and Attack Mitigation: 3 papers found.
Local differential privacy (LDP) has been a focal point in privacy-preserving data aggregation, with significant research aimed at mitigating attacks and improving practicality \[[1' 2022-08-18](http://arxiv.org/abs/2208.09011v2)\]. LDP allows clients to add noise to their data before sending it to a central server, ensuring that the server cannot infer individual contributions \[[1' 2022-08-18](http://arxiv.org/abs/2208.09011v2)\]. However, LDP is vulnerable to malicious clients who can manipulate inputs or outputs to skew results \[[1' 2022-08-18](http://arxiv.org/abs/2208.09011v2)\]. To address this, verifiable LDP (VLDP) schemes have been proposed, enabling clients to prove the correctness of their contributions without revealing sensitive information \[[1' 2022-08-18](http://arxiv.org/abs/2208.09011v2)\]. These schemes typically require minimal interaction between clients and servers, making them suitable for real-world deployment \[[1' 2022-08-18](http://arxiv.org/abs/2208.09011v2)\]. For instance, the Basic RAPPOR algorithm has shown strong performance in high privacy regimes, while k-RR excels in low privacy settings \[[2' 2024-06-27](http://arxiv.org/abs/2406.18940v2)\]. Additionally, the shuffle model of LDP offers an extra layer of privacy by decoupling clients from the aggregator, though it remains susceptible to adversarial manipulation \[[1' 2022-08-18](http://arxiv.org/abs/2208.09011v2)\]. Recent work has focused on developing efficient VLDP mechanisms, such as the Verifiable Distributed Discrete Laplacian Mechanism (VDDLM), which significantly improves proof generation efficiency while maintaining acceptable accuracy \[[1' 2022-08-18](http://arxiv.org/abs/2208.09011v2)\]. These advancements highlight ongoing efforts to balance privacy guarantees with practical implementation in distributed systems \[[1' 2022-08-18](http://arxiv.org/abs/2208.09011v2)\].

### <a id='category-theoretical-guarantees-and-trade-offs'></a>Theoretical Guarantees and Trade-offs: 13 papers found.
The category "Theoretical Guarantees and Trade-offs" explores the balance between privacy and utility in differential privacy (DP) mechanisms, highlighting both theoretical foundations and practical implications. Existing research emphasizes that DP introduces a trade-off where increased privacy guarantees often reduce the utility of the data, as seen in the context of matrix factorization, where HDPMF achieves heterogeneous privacy protection while maintaining accuracy \[[3' 2017-09-15](http://arxiv.org/abs/1709.05361v2)\]. Similarly, in distributed settings, the challenge lies in efficiently sampling noise with Secure Multi-party Computation (MPC), where existing protocols vary in security assumptions and performance \[[1' 2022-08-18](http://arxiv.org/abs/2208.09011v2)\]. The Privacy Sandbox initiative from Google demonstrates how DP can be integrated into real-world applications like advertising, ensuring privacy while enabling ad measurement through mechanisms such as the Private Aggregation API (PAA) \[[6' 2021-02-25](http://arxiv.org/abs/2102.12621v1)\]. Additionally, the Gaussian mechanism, commonly used for numerical data, faces limitations in bounded domains, prompting the development of a bounded Gaussian mechanism to enhance both privacy and accuracy \[[9' 2016-01-24](http://arxiv.org/abs/1601.06426v3)\]. Furthermore, the integration of DP into deep learning models has revealed an unexpected trade-off between privacy and robustness, where differentially private networks may become more vulnerable to adversarial attacks \[[8' 2022-12-01](http://arxiv.org/abs/2212.00306v2)\]. These studies collectively underscore the need for rigorous theoretical analysis and practical implementation strategies to navigate the complex trade-offs inherent in DP.
