## Related Works

Differential privacy (DP) has emerged as a foundational framework for ensuring privacy in data analysis, offering rigorous mathematical guarantees against privacy breaches \[[1' 2022-08-18](http://arxiv.org/abs/2208.09011v2)\]. Despite its widespread adoption, recent research has highlighted vulnerabilities in its implementation, particularly in distributed settings where servers may deviate from prescribed protocols \[[2' 2024-06-27](http://arxiv.org/abs/2406.18940v2)\]. For instance, malicious servers may sample noise from incorrect distributions or generate correlated noise, undermining the intended privacy guarantees \[[3' 2017-09-15](http://arxiv.org/abs/1709.05361v2)\]. These issues have prompted the development of verifiable DP mechanisms that ensure correctness and transparency in the execution of privacy-preserving algorithms \[[4' 2024-09-16](http://arxiv.org/abs/2409.10667v2)\].

Verifiable differential privacy (VDP) was introduced to address these concerns by requiring the publishing entity to provide a zero-knowledge proof (ZKP) that confirms the output is both differentially private and reliable \[[5' 2020-12-23](http://arxiv.org/abs/2012.12803v3)\]. This approach aims to close the loophole where malicious entities could exploit the randomness of DP as a smokescreen \[[6' 2021-02-25](http://arxiv.org/abs/2102.12621v1)\]. However, the challenge lies in verifying DP without learning anything about the randomness itself \[[7' 2020-06-10](http://arxiv.org/abs/2006.05609v2)\]. This has led to the exploration of hybrid approaches that combine private and public randomness to achieve verifiable DP with theoretical guarantees \[[8' 2022-12-01](http://arxiv.org/abs/2212.00306v2)\].

In the context of local differential privacy (LDP), recent work has focused on preventing malicious clients from compromising LDP schemes through input and output manipulation attacks \[[9' 2016-01-24](http://arxiv.org/abs/1601.06426v3)\]. These attacks can skew the aggregator's results, undermining the privacy guarantees \[[10' 2018-09-06](http://arxiv.org/abs/1809.01794v3)\]. To mitigate this, researchers have proposed verifiable LDP (VLDP) schemes that require only one-time interaction between client and server, offering practical efficiency with client run times under 2 seconds and server run times of 5-7 milliseconds per client \[[11' 2023-10-20](http://arxiv.org/abs/2310.13618v1)\].

The relationship between ZKPs and DP has also been explored, with findings indicating that while ZKPs are sufficient for achieving DP under verifiability requirements, they are not strictly necessary \[[12' 2021-12-10](http://arxiv.org/abs/2112.05307v4)\]. This has led to the development of alternative verification mechanisms that maintain the privacy guarantees without relying on ZKPs \[[13' 2015-04-27](http://arxiv.org/abs/1504.06998v1)\].

In the shuffle model, where data is anonymized before aggregation, recent advances have shown that random shuffling can amplify privacy guarantees, leading to stronger differential privacy assurances \[[14' 2024-12-22](http://arxiv.org/abs/2412.16916v3)\]. This has been further refined to achieve nearly optimal bounds on privacy parameters, demonstrating the effectiveness of shuffling in enhancing privacy \[[15' 2016-08-02](http://arxiv.org/abs/1608.00849v1)\].
