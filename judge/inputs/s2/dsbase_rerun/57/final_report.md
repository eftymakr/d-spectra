## Related Works

Generative retrieval (GR) has gained significant attention in information retrieval (IR) due to its ability to directly predict document identifiers (docids) using parametric models \[[1' 2023-04-09](http://arxiv.org/abs/2304.04171v1)\]. However, most existing GR models are evaluated on static document collections, limiting their applicability to dynamic environments where documents evolve over time \[[1' 2023-04-09](http://arxiv.org/abs/2304.04171v1)\]. This section reviews related works that address these challenges and contribute to the development of GR models for dynamic corpora.

Early GR approaches, such as those proposed by \[[1' 2023-04-09](http://arxiv.org/abs/2304.04171v1)\], rely on fixed rule-based docids, such as document titles or BERT embeddings, which often fail to capture the full semantic information of a document. To address this, \[[1' 2023-04-09](http://arxiv.org/abs/2304.04171v1)\] introduced GenRet, a method that learns to tokenize documents into short discrete representations (docids) using a discrete auto-encoding framework. This approach enables end-to-end modeling of document retrieval tasks and establishes new state-of-the-art results on several benchmark datasets \[[1' 2023-04-09](http://arxiv.org/abs/2304.04171v1)\].

More recently, \[[2' 2024-08-04](http://arxiv.org/abs/2408.02152v3)\] proposed a few-shot indexing-based GR framework (Few-Shot GR), which eliminates the need for extensive training by prompting large language models (LLMs) to generate docids for all documents in a corpus. This method significantly reduces training costs and leverages pre-trained knowledge in LLMs more effectively than traditional training-based indexing approaches \[[2' 2024-08-04](http://arxiv.org/abs/2408.02152v3)\]. However, it does not explicitly address the challenges of dynamic corpora, where new documents are continuously added \[[1' 2023-04-09](http://arxiv.org/abs/2304.04171v1)\].

In the context of dynamic document collections, \[[3' 2023-08-29](http://arxiv.org/abs/2308.14968v2)\] introduced the CLEVER model, which enables continual learning for GR by incrementally indexing new documents while preserving the ability to answer queries with both previously and newly indexed documents. The model employs Incremental Product Quantization to encode new documents into docids with low computational cost and a memory-augmented learning mechanism to avoid catastrophic forgetting \[[3' 2023-08-29](http://arxiv.org/abs/2308.14968v2)\]. This work highlights the importance of adaptability in GR models for real-world applications \[[1' 2023-04-09](http://arxiv.org/abs/2304.04171v1)\].

Additionally, \[[4' 2023-05-27](http://arxiv.org/abs/2305.18952v5)\] explored the practicality of GR on dynamic corpora, demonstrating that GR systems are more adaptable to evolving knowledge and efficient in terms of inference FLOPs, indexing time, and storage footprint compared to Dual Encoders (DE) \[[4' 2023-05-27](http://arxiv.org/abs/2305.18952v5)\]. This study underscores the potential of GR for real-time information retrieval in dynamic environments \[[1' 2023-04-09](http://arxiv.org/abs/2304.04171v1)\].

In the realm of retrieval-augmented generation (RAG), \[[5' 2024-03-22](http://arxiv.org/abs/2404.07220v2)\] proposed Blended RAG, which combines semantic search techniques with hybrid query strategies to improve retrieval accuracy. This method leverages dense vector indexes and sparse encoder indexes to achieve better retrieval results on IR datasets

We also develop a progressive training scheme to capture the autoregressive nature of docids and to stabilize training. ### <a id='category-pretrained-language-model-integration'></a>Pretrained Language Model Integration: 4 papers found.
The integration of large language models (LLMs) into generative retrieval (GR) systems has been a focal point in recent research, with several studies addressing the limitations of traditional training-based indexing approaches. Existing GR methods often rely on training-based indexing, which fine-tunes models to memorize query-document associations, leading to high training costs and limited adaptability to dynamic corpora \[[1' 2023-04-09](http://arxiv.org/abs/2304.04171v1)\]. To mitigate these issues, researchers have proposed few-shot indexing-based GR frameworks, such as Few-Shot GR, which utilize LLMs to generate docids without additional training, thereby reducing costs and improving adaptability \[[1' 2023-04-09](http://arxiv.org/abs/2304.04171v1)\]. This approach creates a docid bank for the entire corpus, enabling efficient retrieval by constraining the LLM to generate docids within this bank \[[1' 2023-04-09](http://arxiv.org/abs/2304.04171v1)\]. Furthermore, the use of LLMs in GR systems has also been explored in the context of medical consultations, where frameworks like Terminology-Enhanced Information Retrieval (TEIR) and Emotional In-Context Learning (EICL) leverage LLMs to enhance retrieval accuracy and contextual understanding \[[3' 2023-08-29](http://arxiv.org/abs/2308.14968v2)\]. These studies highlight the potential of LLMs in overcoming domain knowledge limitations and improving performance in dynamic environments. Additionally, the evaluation of generative information retrieval (GenIR) systems has increasingly turned to LLM-based assessments, although challenges remain in ensuring the reliability and fairness of such evaluations \[[2' 2024-08-04](http://arxiv.org/abs/2408.02152v3)\]. Overall, the integration of LLMs into GR systems represents a significant shift towards more efficient and adaptable information retrieval methods.

Empirical results demonstrate the effectiveness and efficiency of the proposed model.»

### <a id='category-efficiency-vs.-effectiveness-trade-offs'></a>Efficiency vs. Effectiveness Trade-offs: 4 papers found.
In the context of efficiency versus effectiveness trade-offs in information retrieval, recent studies highlight the challenges and innovations in improving retrieval accuracy and system performance. For instance, the Blended RAG method combines semantic search techniques like Dense Vector and Sparse Encoder indexes with hybrid query strategies to enhance retrieval accuracy, particularly on large-scale datasets such as NQ and TREC-COVID \[[1' 2023-04-09](http://arxiv.org/abs/2304.04171v1)\]. Similarly, the Tree of Reviews (ToR) framework introduces a dynamic retrieval approach that uses a tree structure to manage retrieved paragraphs, reducing the impact of irrelevant information and improving multi-hop question answering performance \[[3' 2023-08-29](http://arxiv.org/abs/2308.14968v2)\]. Additionally, the use of topological graphic passwords (Topsnut-gpws) demonstrates an innovative approach to password security, leveraging algebraic matrices and graph theory for both authentication and encryption, which can be relevant in secure information retrieval systems \[[2' 2024-08-04](http://arxiv.org/abs/2408.02152v3)\]. Furthermore, the development of exact dynamic consensus algorithms addresses the limitations of existing methods by achieving zero steady-state error through high-order sliding modes, which is crucial for reliable information processing in dynamic environments \[[4' 2023-05-27](http://arxiv.org/abs/2305.18952v5)\]. These approaches collectively emphasize the balance between system efficiency and retrieval effectiveness, offering new strategies to enhance both in real-world applications.
