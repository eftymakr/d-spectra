## Related Works

Medical image segmentation, particularly for brain tumor segmentation, has seen significant advancements with the integration of deep learning techniques. Early approaches relied on traditional methods such as region growing and thresholding, but these were limited in their ability to capture complex anatomical structures \[[1' 2023-02-08](http://arxiv.org/abs/2302.04303v1)\]. With the advent of convolutional neural networks (CNNs), the field has evolved significantly, leading to the development of architectures like U-Net, which has become a standard for medical image segmentation \[[2' 2018-01-17](http://arxiv.org/abs/1801.05746v1)\]. U-Net's encoder-decoder structure, combined with skip connections, enables effective feature extraction and spatial localization \[[3' 2019-09-04](http://arxiv.org/abs/1909.06012v1)\].

Pre-training on large-scale datasets, such as ImageNet, has been shown to improve performance in medical image segmentation by leveraging transfer learning \[[4' 2023-04-20](http://arxiv.org/abs/2304.10517v3)\]. This is particularly important in medical imaging, where annotated data is often scarce and expensive to obtain \[[5' 2025-01-15](http://arxiv.org/abs/2501.09185v2)\]. For instance, TernausNet demonstrates how using a pre-trained VGG11 encoder can significantly enhance segmentation accuracy compared to models trained from scratch \[[6' 2021-11-30](http://arxiv.org/abs/2112.00113v1)\]. Similarly, the use of pre-trained models has been extended to 3D medical imaging, where challenges such as limited data and computational complexity arise \[[7' 2016-12-05](http://arxiv.org/abs/1612.01452v2)\].

Recent work has focused on adapting pre-trained models to 3D medical images, with strategies such as weight inflation and 2.5D approaches to balance computational efficiency and performance \[[8' 2022-11-21](http://arxiv.org/abs/2211.11801v3)\]. The 3D U$^2$-Net, for example, introduces a universal architecture that can handle multiple medical segmentation tasks with minimal parameters \[[9' 2020-10-13](http://arxiv.org/abs/2010.06163v2)\]. This aligns with the goal of reducing training requirements and improving efficiency, as seen in the proposed methods in this paper \[[10' 2019-11-29](http://arxiv.org/abs/1911.13273v2)\].

The integration of 3D convolutions and pre-trained weights has also been explored to enhance segmentation accuracy. For instance, the use of axial-coronal-sagittal convolutions in 3D medical imaging can help preserve depth-wise information while reducing computational overhead \[[11' 2019-10-24](http://arxiv.org/abs/1910.11456v1)\]. This is crucial for tasks like brain tumor segmentation, where accurate delineation of tumor boundaries is essential \[[12' 2018-03-23](http://arxiv.org/abs/1803.08691v1)\].

Moreover, the use of pre-trained encoders from related tasks, such as brain glioma grade classification, has shown promise in improving segmentation performance \[[13' 2023-04-09](http://arxiv.org/abs/2304.04225v1)\]. This approach leverages the shared features between classification and segmentation tasks, leading to better generalization and accuracy \[[14' 2021-11-19](http://arxiv.org/abs/2111.10480v6)\].

In addition to architectural improvements, the use of self-supervised pre-training and domain adaptation techniques has gained traction in medical image segmentation \[[15' 2018-10-04](http://arxiv.org/abs/1810.02113v1)\].
