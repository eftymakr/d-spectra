## Related Works

Personalized outfit generation has garnered significant attention in recent years, driven by the increasing demand for tailored fashion recommendations and the advancement of generative AI models. Existing approaches primarily focus on generating compatible fashion items either based on a given set of items or guided by user preferences \[[1' 2025-02-03](http://arxiv.org/abs/2502.00992v1)\]. However, these methods often suffer from limitations such as lack of diversity and reliance on supervised learning paradigms \[[2' 2024-09-18](http://arxiv.org/abs/2409.12150v1)\]. This section reviews related works that address these challenges and contribute to the field of fashion recommendation and outfit generation.

Outfit generation is a challenging task in fashion technology, requiring the creation of a collocated set of fashion items that complement a given set of items \[[1' 2025-02-03](http://arxiv.org/abs/2502.00992v1)\]. Previous studies have been limited to generating a unique set of fashion items based on a given set, without providing additional options to users \[[1' 2025-02-03](http://arxiv.org/abs/2502.00992v1)\]. This lack of diversity necessitates the development of more versatile frameworks \[[1' 2025-02-03](http://arxiv.org/abs/2502.00992v1)\]. FCBoost-Net, for instance, introduces a framework that leverages pre-trained generative models to produce multiple collocated and diversified outfits \[[1' 2025-02-03](http://arxiv.org/abs/2502.00992v1)\]. It randomly synthesizes multiple sets of fashion items and improves their compatibility through a novel fashion compatibility booster \[[1' 2025-02-03](http://arxiv.org/abs/2502.00992v1)\].

Personalized outfit recommendation remains a complex challenge, requiring both fashion compatibility understanding and trend awareness \[[2' 2024-09-18](http://arxiv.org/abs/2409.12150v1)\]. A novel framework has been proposed that harnesses the expressive power of large language models (LLMs) for this task, mitigating their "black box" and static nature through fine-tuning and direct feedback integration \[[2' 2024-09-18](http://arxiv.org/abs/2409.12150v1)\]. This framework bridges the item visual-textual gap by employing image captioning with a Multimodal Large Language Model (MLLM), enabling the LLM to extract style and color characteristics from human-curated fashion images \[[2' 2024-09-18](http://arxiv.org/abs/2409.12150v1)\].

Advancements in Generative AI offer new opportunities for FashionAI, surpassing traditional recommendation systems that often lack transparency and struggle to integrate expert knowledge \[[3' 2025-04-14](http://arxiv.org/abs/2504.09915v1)\]. To address these challenges, PAFA (Principle-Aware Fashion) is introduced, a multi-granular knowledge base that organizes professional styling expertise into three levels of metadata, domain principles, and semantic relationships \[[3' 2025-04-14](http://arxiv.org/abs/2504.09915v1)\]. Using PAFA, StePO-Rec is developed, a knowledge-guided method for multi-step outfit recommendation \[[3' 2025-04-14](http://arxiv.org/abs/2504.09915v1)\].

Outfit compatibility can also be modeled using graph neural networks (GNNs), where the compatibility of items is scored based on their interactions \[[4' 2024-04-28](http://arxiv.org/abs/2404.18040v1)\]. The use of Node-wise Graph Neural Networks (NGNN) and Hypergraph Neural Networks (HGNN) has shown promise in improving compatibility prediction and fill-in-the-blank tasks \[[4' 2024-04-28](http://arxiv.org/abs/2404.18040v1)\].

### <a id='category-generative-ai-models-in-fashion-generation'></a>Generative AI Models in Fashion Generation: 4 papers found.
In the realm of generative AI models for fashion generation, recent research has focused on improving the compatibility, diversity, and personalization of generated outfits. Existing methods often rely on item-wise compatibility checks but struggle to enforce global constraints such as style or occasion \[[2' 2024-09-18](http://arxiv.org/abs/2409.12150v1)\]. To address this, some studies have introduced multimodal embeddings that align image and text representations of fashion items, enabling the system to measure both item-query coherence and item-item compatibility \[[2' 2024-09-18](http://arxiv.org/abs/2409.12150v1)\]. These approaches aim to generate outfits that are not only visually coherent but also aligned with user-specified themes \[[3' 2025-04-14](http://arxiv.org/abs/2504.09915v1)\]. Additionally, there has been a growing emphasis on incorporating global style information into the generation process, with some models using unsupervised style extraction modules to learn and control the overall style of an outfit \[[4' 2024-04-28](http://arxiv.org/abs/2404.18040v1)\]. This trend highlights the shift towards more holistic and flexible outfit generation frameworks that consider both local item compatibility and global style coherence. Furthermore, the use of pre-trained generative models, combined with boosting techniques, has shown promise in improving the diversity and authenticity of generated outfits \[[1' 2025-02-03](http://arxiv.org/abs/2502.00992v1)\]. These advancements collectively contribute to the development of more effective and user-centric outfit generation systems.
