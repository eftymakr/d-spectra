## Related Works

Distributed training of large language models (LLMs) has attracted significant research interest due to the computational and memory demands of these models. Early efforts focused on model parallelism and data parallelism, with techniques such as pipeline parallelism and model sharding being widely adopted \[[1' 2024-12-13](http://arxiv.org/abs/2412.12178v2)\]. However, these approaches often lack fine-grained control over execution behavior, making it difficult to optimize performance in real-world settings \[[2' 2024-02-19](http://arxiv.org/abs/2402.12065v2)\]. 

Recent work has explored the use of quantization to reduce memory and computational overheads. For instance, WKVQuant introduces a PTQ framework that quantizes both weights and key/value caches, achieving memory savings comparable to weight-activation quantization while maintaining performance close to weight-only quantization \[[3' 2019-07-29](http://arxiv.org/abs/1907.12300v1)\]. Similarly, activation sparsity techniques have been proposed to reduce memory and computation by selectively pruning inactive activations in LLMs, achieving up to 50% reduction in main memory usage with minimal accuracy loss \[[4' 2022-06-01](http://arxiv.org/abs/2206.00774v1)\]. These methods highlight the importance of efficient resource management in distributed training.

The challenge of efficiently scheduling and managing resources in distributed systems has also been addressed in other domains. For example, predictive triggering frameworks have been developed for multi-agent systems to reduce network utilization by forecasting communication demands and allocating resources in advance \[[5' 2023-02-13](http://arxiv.org/abs/2302.06126v1)\]. In the context of edge computing, distributed training systems such as SROLE have been proposed to avoid action collisions by using shielding mechanisms that dynamically adjust task scheduling \[[6' 2019-05-22](http://arxiv.org/abs/1905.09219v1)\]. These approaches emphasize the need for adaptive and scalable resource management strategies in distributed environments.

Performance modeling and prediction remain critical for optimizing distributed training. Existing work has focused on empirical models and heuristic-based approaches to estimate execution times \[[7' 2021-09-14](http://arxiv.org/abs/2109.06440v1)\]. However, these methods often lack the granularity and accuracy needed for complex LLM training scenarios. Lumos addresses this gap by introducing a trace-driven performance modeling toolkit that captures and predicts execution behaviors with high accuracy, achieving an average error of just 3.3% across different models and configurations \[[8' 2020-03-11](http://arxiv.org/abs/2003.05168v1)\]. This approach is particularly valuable for exploring new model and deployment configurations without requiring extensive retraining or experimentation.

In addition to performance modeling, the field of distributed training has seen advancements in graph deployment and communication optimization. For example, TAG leverages device topology-aware graph deployment to minimize communication overhead in heterogeneous clusters, achieving up to 4.56x training speed-up \[[9' 2023-11-14](http://arxiv.org/abs/2401.02431v2)\]. Similarly, online resource utilization forecasting has been proposed to reduce the overhead of monitoring and predicting resource usage in large-scale distributed systems \[[10' 2019-11-21](http://arxiv.org/abs/1911.09561v1)\]. These techniques demonstrate the importance of combining accurate performance models with efficient resource management strategies to achieve high efficiency
