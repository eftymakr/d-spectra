## Related Works

Cross-domain Sequential Recommendation (CDSR) has gained significant attention in recent years due to its ability to model user preferences across multiple domains \[[1' 2023-04-08](http://arxiv.org/abs/2304.03891v1)\]. However, existing methods face two major challenges: the overlap dilemma and transition complexity. The overlap dilemma refers to the reliance on users with interactions across all domains, which limits the practicality of these methods \[[1' 2023-04-08](http://arxiv.org/abs/2304.03891v1)\]. Transition complexity arises from the difficulty of learning complex transition patterns from mixed behavior sequences \[[1' 2023-04-08](http://arxiv.org/abs/2304.03891v1)\]. 

To address these challenges, several approaches have been proposed. For instance, \[[2' 2022-06-16](http://arxiv.org/abs/2206.08050v1)\] introduces a graph-based solution called TiDA-GCN, which incorporates time interval information to enhance item and account-level representation learning. This method links users and items in each domain as a graph and uses domain-aware graph convolution networks to learn user-specific node representations. Additionally, it employs attention mechanisms to selectively guide the message passing process, improving the modeling of cross-domain relationships \[[2' 2022-06-16](http://arxiv.org/abs/2206.08050v1)\].

Another approach, \[[3' 2025-03-26](http://arxiv.org/abs/2503.20232v1)\], proposes a novel graph-based solution, DA-GCN, which also addresses the challenges of SCSR by leveraging domain-aware graph convolution networks. DA-GCN links users and items in each domain as a graph and employs attention mechanisms to guide message passing, thereby capturing domain-specific preferences \[[3' 2025-03-26](http://arxiv.org/abs/2503.20232v1)\].

In addition to graph-based methods, some works have focused on contrastive learning for sequential recommendation. \[[4' 2019-10-06](http://arxiv.org/abs/1910.02448v4)\] introduces LACLRec, which uses a learnable sequence augmenter to generate higher-quality augmented sequences and a ranking-based triplet contrastive loss to differentiate similarities between raw and augmented sequences. This approach improves recommendation accuracy by providing more fine-grained contrastive signals \[[4' 2019-10-06](http://arxiv.org/abs/1910.02448v4)\].

Furthermore, \[[5' 2021-05-07](http://arxiv.org/abs/2105.03300v1)\] proposes a method called CaDiRec, which leverages context-aware diffusion models to generate more reasonable augmented views for contrastive learning. This approach ensures that generated items align with their respective context information, thereby preserving semantic consistency and improving the quality of augmented sequences \[[5' 2021-05-07](http://arxiv.org/abs/2105.03300v1)\].

The use of graph contrastive learning has also been explored in sequential recommendation. \[[6' 2020-01-29](http://arxiv.org/abs/2002.00741v1)\] introduces GCL4SR, which employs a Weighted Item Transition Graph (WITG) to provide global context information and uses subgraphs to augment interaction sequence representations. This method enhances the consistency of augmented representations and improves the learning of sequence representations \[[6' 2020-01-29](http://arxiv.org/abs/2002.00741v1)\].

In addition, \[[7' 2023-10-21](http://arxiv.org/abs/2310.13925v1)\] proposes a method called XSimGCL, which simplifies graph contrastive learning by discarding ineffective graph augmentations and instead employing noise-based embedding augmentation to generate views for contrastive learning. This approach achieves significant improvements in recommendation accuracy and training efficiency \[[7' 2023-10-21](http://arxiv.org/abs/2310.13925v1)\].
