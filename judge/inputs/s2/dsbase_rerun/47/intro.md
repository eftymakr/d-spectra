## Related Works

Multi-Domain Recommendation (MDR) has emerged as a critical area of research in recommendation systems, aiming to leverage knowledge across multiple domains to improve recommendation performance \[[3' 2023-09-12](http://arxiv.org/abs/2309.06217v2)\]. Early approaches focused on transferring knowledge from a source domain to a target domain, often assuming that the domains were similar or had overlapping characteristics \[[7' 2012-10-26](http://arxiv.org/abs/1210.7056v1)\]. However, this approach can lead to the **Negative Transfer Problem (NTP)**, where the inclusion of dissimilar or low-quality domains degrades performance due to conflicting knowledge \[[2' 2023-03-02](http://arxiv.org/abs/2303.01003v1)\]. This issue has been extensively studied in the context of cross-domain recommendation, where the assumption of domain similarity is often violated \[[10' 2021-08-12](http://arxiv.org/abs/2108.05930v1)\].

Several works have attempted to mitigate NTP by introducing domain selection mechanisms. For instance, \[[7' 2012-10-26](http://arxiv.org/abs/1210.7056v1)\] proposed a selective transfer learning framework that uses empirical prediction error and variance to identify consistent source domains, thereby avoiding the negative impact of inconsistent data. Similarly, \[[12' 2023-12-17](http://arxiv.org/abs/2312.10602v1)\] introduced a weighted k-center algorithm for data subset selection, which could be adapted to prioritize domain selection based on uncertainty and diversity. However, these methods are typically domain-specific and lack a generalizable approach for multi-domain settings \[[1' 2023-02-12](http://arxiv.org/abs/2302.05990v2)\].

In the context of multi-domain recommendation, \[[3' 2023-09-12](http://arxiv.org/abs/2309.06217v2)\] and \[[8' 2022-02-25](http://arxiv.org/abs/2202.12524v4)\] have explored the challenges of modeling domain interactions. \[[3' 2023-09-12](http://arxiv.org/abs/2309.06217v2)\] introduced a graph-based approach to model sequential interactions across domains, while \[[8' 2022-02-25](http://arxiv.org/abs/2202.12524v4)\] proposed a model-agnostic learning framework (MAMDR) that allows for scalable and flexible multi-domain recommendation. These works highlight the importance of domain-specific modeling and the need for mechanisms to handle domain conflicts and sparsity. However, they do not explicitly address the dynamic selection of domains to avoid NTP \[[8' 2022-02-25](http://arxiv.org/abs/2202.12524v4)\].

More recently, \[[9' 2024-05-21](http://arxiv.org/abs/2405.12892v2)\] emphasized the importance of modeling domain-sensitive features, which are crucial for capturing the distinctions between domains. This work demonstrated that existing methods often neglect these features, leading to insufficient modeling of domain differences. To address this, \[[9' 2024-05-21](http://arxiv.org/abs/2405.12892v2)\] proposed a domain-sensitive feature attribution method and a memory architecture to enhance domain distinction awareness. This approach aligns with the need for dynamic domain selection to avoid NTP \[[9' 2024-05-21](http://arxiv.org/abs/2405.12892v2)\].

Additionally, \[[11' 2022-01-15](http://arxiv.org/abs/2201.05867v1)\] provides a comprehensive survey on transferability in deep learning, highlighting the challenges of negative transfer and the importance of domain adaptation. This work underscores the need for principled methods to assess and adapt knowledge across domains, which is essential for MDR systems \[[11' 2022-01-15](http://arxiv.org/abs/2201.05867v1)\].
