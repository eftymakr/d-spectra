## Related Works

Video frame interpolation (VFI) has been extensively studied in recent years, with a focus on synthesizing intermediate frames between existing ones to enhance temporal resolution. Early methods relied on optical flow estimation and warping techniques \[[18' 2017-08-05](http://arxiv.org/abs/1708.01692v1)\]. However, these approaches often struggled with large motion, occlusion, and blur, leading to artifacts in the interpolated frames. More recent works have leveraged deep learning to improve the quality and robustness of VFI. For example, \[[1' 2019-04-01](http://arxiv.org/abs/1904.00830v1)\] introduced a depth-aware flow projection layer to synthesize intermediate flows that preferentially sample closer objects, improving the quality of interpolation in complex scenes. Similarly, \[[10' 2022-02-10](http://arxiv.org/abs/2202.04901v4)\] proposed a single unified network that shares weights across scales and uses the Gram matrix loss to optimize for perceptual quality, outperforming state-of-the-art methods on the Xiph large motion benchmark.

Several approaches have focused on improving the efficiency and generalization of VFI models. \[[21' 2017-03-22](http://arxiv.org/abs/1703.07514v1)\] combined motion estimation and pixel synthesis into a single convolutional process, enabling the model to handle occlusion, blur, and abrupt brightness changes. This approach was trained end-to-end using widely available video data without requiring ground-truth optical flow. \[[18' 2017-08-05](http://arxiv.org/abs/1708.01692v1)\] further advanced this idea by using adaptive separable convolutions, reducing the number of parameters needed for large motion scenarios and enabling perceptual loss incorporation for visually pleasing results.

### <a id='category-video-frame-interpolation-techniques'></a>Video Frame Interpolation Techniques: 15 papers found.
Video frame interpolation techniques have seen significant advancements, particularly through the integration of deep learning methods. These techniques aim to synthesize intermediate frames between existing ones, enhancing temporal resolution in videos. Early approaches relied on optical flow and motion estimation, but recent methods leverage deep neural networks to improve quality and efficiency \[[1' 2019-04-01](http://arxiv.org/abs/1904.00830v1)\]. For instance, some works focus on incorporating depth information to better handle occlusions and large object motion by developing depth-aware flow projection layers \[[1' 2019-04-01](http://arxiv.org/abs/1904.00830v1)\]. Others propose fully convolutional autoencoder networks to generate multiple intermediate frames from two input images, demonstrating superior performance over existing methods \[[2' 2020-05-14](http://arxiv.org/abs/2005.06684v1)\]. Additionally, there is a growing interest in using implicit neural representations (INR) to enforce optical flow constraints, enabling high-quality interpolation without additional training data \[[4' 2022-10-04](http://arxiv.org/abs/2210.01879v1)\]. Furthermore, methods like latent diffusion models and neural fields are being explored to address perceptual quality and spatial consistency in interpolated frames \[[5' 2022-06-22](http://arxiv.org/abs/2206.10886v1)\]\[[6' 2023-02-28](http://arxiv.org/abs/2303.00050v1)\]. Some studies also emphasize the importance of unsupervised learning, such as cycle consistency, to synthesize high frame rate videos from low frame rate inputs without requiring ground truth data \[[7' 2023-03-16](http://arxiv.org/abs/2303.09508v3)\]. Event-based cameras are also being utilized to enhance interpolation quality by focusing on moving regions and reducing artifacts \[[8' 2022-06-27](http://arxiv.org/abs/2206.13454v1)\]. Overall, these approaches highlight the evolving landscape of video frame interpolation, driven by the need for higher quality, efficiency, and adaptability to diverse scenarios.

### <a id='category-performance-metrics-and-benchmarking'></a>Performance Metrics and Benchmarking: 2 papers found.
Recent advancements in video frame interpolation have focused on improving perceptual quality and temporal consistency, particularly for challenging scenarios like omnidirectional videos. To address this, researchers have explored deep learning-based perceptual quality metrics that better capture human judgment, though these models often neglect temporal information, limiting their effectiveness on videos \[[1' 2019-04-01](http://arxiv.org/abs/1904.00830v1)\]. In parallel, the need for specialized interpolation techniques has grown, especially for immersive media such as 360° videos, which require handling low frame rates and distortion artifacts \[[17' 2024-07-19](http://arxiv.org/abs/2407.14066v3)\]. These challenges have led to the development of novel datasets and models, such as the 360VFI benchmark, which incorporates distortion priors and equirectangular projection characteristics to enhance interpolation accuracy \[[17' 2024-07-19](http://arxiv.org/abs/2407.14066v3)\]. Additionally, efforts have been made to design lightweight yet high-quality interpolation models that can implicitly model camera movement and 3D geometry, as seen in the PS4PRO model \[[1' 2019-04-01](http://arxiv.org/abs/1904.00830v1)\]. These developments highlight the ongoing efforts to bridge the gap between traditional frame interpolation and the demands of modern video applications.

Overall, these developments highlight the growing synergy between video prediction and neural rendering, enabling more realistic and flexible scene reconstruction and manipulation.
