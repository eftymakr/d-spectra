## Related Works

Speech-driven 3D facial animation has been a focal point in recent research, with numerous approaches aiming to improve the realism and expressiveness of generated facial movements. Early methods primarily relied on handcrafted features and regression models, which often failed to capture the complex interplay between speech and facial expressions \[[1' 2023-12-18](http://arxiv.org/abs/2312.10877v1)\]. More recent approaches have leveraged deep learning techniques, particularly self-supervised audio models, to encode speech signals into meaningful representations that can be used to drive facial animations \[[2' 2023-10-17](http://arxiv.org/abs/2310.11295v1)\]. However, these methods often suffer from limitations such as the averaging effect, where phonetically similar syllables with distinct lip shapes lead to coupled audio features, resulting in less expressive and less accurate facial animations \[[3' 2023-12-05](http://arxiv.org/abs/2312.02781v1)\].

The averaging effect is a well-documented issue in self-supervised audio feature spaces, where near-homophone syllables tend to cluster, leading to reduced discriminability in lip motion generation \[[4' 2023-01-06](http://arxiv.org/abs/2301.02379v2)\]. This problem is exacerbated by the limited availability of large-scale, high-quality audio-visual datasets, which hinders the training of more robust and expressive models \[[5' 2021-12-04](http://arxiv.org/abs/2112.02214v2)\]. To address this, several works have proposed alternative approaches, such as incorporating visual and textual cues to improve the accuracy and coherence of facial animations \[[6' 2024-09-27](http://arxiv.org/abs/2409.19143v1)\]. For example, PMMTalk integrates visual and textual information extracted from speech to enhance the accuracy of facial blendshape coefficients \[[7' 2023-11-08](http://arxiv.org/abs/2311.04766v2)\]. Similarly, FaceFormer employs self-supervised pre-trained speech representations to capture long-term audio context, enabling more accurate and context-aware facial animation generation \[[8' 2018-05-23](http://arxiv.org/abs/1805.09313v4)\].

Despite these advancements, most existing methods still rely on deterministic frameworks that produce consistent outputs for the same input, limiting the diversity and naturalness of generated facial animations \[[9' 2012-09-22](http://arxiv.org/abs/1209.4982v1)\]. To overcome this limitation, researchers have explored non-deterministic approaches, such as diffusion-based models, which allow for the generation of diverse and realistic facial animations \[[10' 2024-09-12](http://arxiv.org/abs/2409.07966v4)\]. For instance, FaceDiffuser introduces a non-deterministic deep learning model trained with both 3D vertex and blendshape-based datasets, enabling the synthesis of varied facial expressions while maintaining lip synchronization \[[11' 2024-04-29](http://arxiv.org/abs/2404.18604v1)\]. Similarly, 3DiFACE proposes a lightweight audio-conditioned diffusion model that can be trained on small datasets and fine-tuned for specific subjects, resulting in more expressive and diverse facial animations \[[12' 2021-12-10](http://arxiv.org/abs/2112.05329v4)\].

In addition to addressing the averaging effect and improving expressiveness, recent works have also focused on modeling the relationship between speech and facial expressions in a more nuanced manner. For example, CorrTalk establishes a temporal correlation between hierarchical speech features and facial activity intensities, ensuring more accurate lip synchronization

The challenge of limited audio-visual data has also been tackled through self-supervised pre-trained speech representations and large-scale datasets, such as the 3D-CAVFA dataset, which provides a foundation for training more robust models \[[1' 2023-12-18](http://arxiv.org/abs/2312.10877v1)\]. Furthermore, recent approaches have introduced autoregressive models, such as FaceFormer, which encode long-term audio context and predict 3D face meshes sequentially, while also incorporating attention mechanisms to align audio and motion modalities \[[5' 2021-12-04](http://arxiv.org/abs/2112.02214v2)\]. These developments highlight the ongoing efforts to enhance the realism and expressiveness of speech-driven facial animation through advanced modeling strategies and multi-modal integration.

[Snippet]: «Audio-aware large language models (ALLMs) have recently made great strides in understanding and processing audio inputs. These models are typically adapted from text-based large language models (LLMs) through additional training on audio-related tasks. This adaptation process presents two major limitations. First, ALLMs often suffer from catastrophic forgetting, where crucial textual capabilities like instruction-following are lost after training on audio data. In some cases, models may even hallucinate sounds that are not present in the input audio, raising concerns about reliability. Second, achieving cross-modal alignment between audio and language typically relies on large collections of task-specific question-answer pairs for instruction tuning, making it resource-intensive. To address these issues, previous works have leveraged the backbone LLMs to synthesize general-purpose, caption-style alignment data. In this paper, we propose a data generation framework that produces contrastive-like training data, designed to enhance ALLMs' ability to differentiate between present and absent sounds. We further extend our approach to multi-audio scenarios, enabling the model to either explain differences between audio inputs or produce unified captions that describe all inputs, thereby enhancing audio-language alignment. We refer to the entire ALLM training framework as bootstrapping audio-language alignment via synthetic data generation from backbone LLMs (BALSa). Experimental results indicate that our method effectively mitigates audio hallucinations while reliably maintaining strong performance on audio understanding and reasoning benchmarks, as well as instruction-following skills. Moreover, incorporating multi-audio training further enhances the model's comprehension and reasoning capabilities. Overall, BALSa offers an efficient and scalable approach to developing ALLMs.»
