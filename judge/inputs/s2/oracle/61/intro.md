## Related Works

Personalized outfit generation has emerged as a critical area within fashion recommendation, aiming to produce sets of compatible and personalized fashion items that align with user preferences \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/1905.01866)\]. Early approaches focused on predefined outfit recommendations and personalized outfit composition, but these were constrained by existing fashion products, limiting their ability to address diverse user needs \[[6](http://arxiv.org/abs/2402.17279)\]. Recent advancements have leveraged AI-generated content to transcend these limitations, enabling more flexible and personalized outfit generation \[[6](http://arxiv.org/abs/2402.17279)\]. 

Several works have explored the use of generative models for fashion recommendation. For instance, the Hierarchical Fashion Graph Network (HFGN) integrates user-outfit and outfit-item relationships to improve compatibility and personalization \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/1905.01866)\]. Similarly, the Personalized Outfit Generation (POG) model connects user preferences with Transformer-based architectures to enhance both compatibility and personalization \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/1905.01866)\]. However, these models often rely on supervised learning paradigms, which can limit diversity and adaptability \[[6](http://arxiv.org/abs/2402.17279)\]. 

To address these limitations, researchers have proposed diffusion-based approaches for generative outfit recommendation. DiFashion, for example, uses diffusion models to generate multiple fashion images and compose them into visually compatible outfits \[[6](http://arxiv.org/abs/2402.17279)\]. This approach ensures high fidelity, compatibility, and personalization by incorporating conditions that guide the generation process \[[6](http://arxiv.org/abs/2402.17279)\]. Additionally, the use of Classifier-Free-Guidance helps align generated images with the specified conditions \[[6](http://arxiv.org/abs/2402.17279)\]. 

In the realm of virtual try-on, models like VITON and StableVITON have demonstrated the potential of diffusion models to generate realistic images by transferring clothing onto a person while preserving details \[[9](http://arxiv.org/abs/1711.08447)\]\[[11](http://arxiv.org/abs/2312.01725)\]. These models utilize latent spaces and warping techniques to ensure that the generated images are both visually appealing and semantically accurate \[[9](http://arxiv.org/abs/1711.08447)\]\[[11](http://arxiv.org/abs/2312.01725)\]. Furthermore, the Smart Fitting Room framework introduces a hybrid approach combining retrieval-based and generative methods to enhance the personalization and quality of virtual try-on \[[12](http://arxiv.org/abs/2401.16825)\]. 

Recent advances in direct preference optimization (DPO) have also influenced the fine-tuning of generative models for fashion tasks. DPO eliminates the need for reward models by directly optimizing on human comparison data, making the fine-tuning process more efficient and effective \[[15](http://arxiv.org/abs/2305.18290)\]. This approach has been adapted for diffusion models through methods like Diffusion-DPO and D3PO, which align models with human preferences without requiring extensive training or reward modeling \[[16](http://arxiv.org/abs/2311.12908)\]\[[17](http://arxiv.org/abs/2311.13231)\].
