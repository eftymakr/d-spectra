## Related Works

Brain tumor segmentation in medical imaging has been a focal point of research, with several approaches proposed to enhance the accuracy and efficiency of segmentation tasks. Early methods relied on traditional image processing techniques, but the advent of deep learning has significantly advanced the field. One of the most influential frameworks for biomedical image segmentation is the U-Net architecture, which has been widely adopted for its encoder-decoder structure and ability to capture contextual information \[[11]()\]. However, U-Net and its variants often require extensive training data and computational resources, which can be a limiting factor in clinical settings \[[13]()\].

Recent work has focused on improving the efficiency and performance of segmentation models by leveraging pre-trained models and advanced architectures. The nnU-Net framework, which automatically configures model parameters based on the input data, has demonstrated promising results in brain tumor segmentation tasks \[[14]()\]. This approach reduces the need for manual hyperparameter tuning and has been shown to achieve competitive performance on benchmark datasets such as BraTS \[[4](http://arxiv.org/abs/2011.00848)\]. However, nnU-Net still requires substantial training time and may not fully utilize pre-trained weights from other domains \[[4](http://arxiv.org/abs/2011.00848)\].

To address these limitations, researchers have explored the integration of pre-trained models from natural image datasets, such as ImageNet, into medical image segmentation frameworks. For instance, the Med3D model leverages transfer learning from large-scale natural image datasets to improve the performance of 3D medical image analysis tasks \[[9](http://arxiv.org/abs/1904.00625)\]. Similarly, the Models Genesis framework demonstrates that self-supervised learning can yield superior results compared to traditional transfer learning methods, particularly in 3D medical imaging \[[10](http://arxiv.org/abs/2004.07882)\]. These approaches highlight the potential of transfer learning in reducing training time and improving segmentation accuracy.

Another notable advancement is the use of attention mechanisms and transformer-based architectures for 3D medical image segmentation. The Swin UNETR model, which combines the strengths of vision transformers with the U-Net architecture, has shown promising results in capturing long-range dependencies in 3D MRI scans \[[8](http://arxiv.org/abs/2201.01266)\]. Similarly, the TransBTS model introduces a novel architecture that integrates 3D CNNs with transformers to enhance the modeling of both local and global features in brain tumor segmentation \[[7](http://arxiv.org/abs/2103.04430)\]. These models demonstrate the effectiveness of hybrid architectures in handling the complexity of 3D medical images.

### <a id='category-3d-medical-image-segmentation-frameworks'></a>3D Medical Image Segmentation Frameworks: 5 papers found.
The category "3D Medical Image Segmentation Frameworks" encompasses various approaches aimed at improving the accuracy and efficiency of brain tumor segmentation in 3D MRI data. Early methods relied on manual delineation, which is time-consuming and prone to human error \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/1811.02629)\]. To address these limitations, semantic segmentation networks based on encoder-decoder architectures have been developed, with notable success in the BraTS challenges \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/1810.11654)\]. For instance, a variational auto-encoder branch was integrated to regularize the decoder and improve segmentation performance \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/1810.11654)\]. Later advancements, such as nnU-Net, demonstrated significant improvements through region-based training, aggressive data augmentation, and postprocessing refinements, achieving top rankings in BraTS 2020 \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/2107.02314)\]. Building on these successes, further modifications like larger networks, group normalization, and axial attention in the decoder were explored, leading to improved quantitative metrics and first-place finishes in BraTS 2021 \[[4](http://arxiv.org/abs/2011.00848)\]. More recently, ensemble methods combining multiple frameworks such as DeepSeg, nnU-Net, and DeepSCAN have shown superior performance, achieving high Dice scores and low Hausdorff distances in both standard and unseen test datasets \[[5](http://arxiv.org/abs/2112.04653)\]. These developments highlight the ongoing evolution of 3D medical image segmentation frameworks, driven by innovations in architecture, training strategies, and data utilization.

In the realm of 3D medical image segmentation, particularly for brain tumor segmentation, recent advancements have focused on integrating transformer-based models with traditional convolutional architectures to leverage both local and global feature modeling \[[7](http://arxiv.org/abs/2103.04430)\]. While Fully Convolutional Neural Networks (FCNNs), especially the "U-shaped" architecture, have become the de facto standard for 3D medical image segmentation, they face limitations in capturing long-range dependencies due to the constrained kernel sizes of convolutional layers \[[8](http://arxiv.org/abs/2201.01266)\]. This limitation can hinder the accurate segmentation of tumors with variable sizes. In response, transformer models, which excel at modeling long-range dependencies through self-attention mechanisms, have been increasingly explored for medical imaging tasks \[[8](http://arxiv.org/abs/2201.01266)\]. For instance, the TransBTS model was introduced to address this gap by incorporating a Transformer into a 3D CNN framework, enabling the capture of global context while maintaining local 3D feature extraction \[[7](http://arxiv.org/abs/2103.04430)\]. Similarly, the Swin UNETR model reformulates 3D brain tumor segmentation as a sequence-to-sequence prediction problem, utilizing a hierarchical Swin transformer to extract features at multiple resolutions and combining them with an FCNN-based decoder \[[8](http://arxiv.org/abs/2201.01266)\]. These approaches highlight the growing trend of integrating transformer-based models into medical image segmentation pipelines to improve performance and efficiency. Additionally, the integration of pre-trained weights from ImageNet and the use of multi-modal input data further enhance the robustness of these models in handling complex medical imaging tasks \[[7](http://arxiv.org/abs/2103.04430)\]\[[8](http://arxiv.org/abs/2201.01266)\].
