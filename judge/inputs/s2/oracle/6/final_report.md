## Related Works

Long-form narrative summarization, encompassing books, movies, and TV scripts, remains a challenging task for existing large language models (LLMs) due to the complexity of capturing intricate plotlines, character interactions, and thematic coherence \[[13](http://arxiv.org/abs/2310.00785)\]. Prior work has explored various approaches to address these challenges, including abstractive summarization techniques, efficient attention mechanisms, and multi-agent collaboration frameworks. For instance, \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/2404.03561)\] introduced a two-stage abstractive summarization approach for movie scripts, where salient scenes are first identified and then used to generate summaries. This method improves the accuracy of summarizing narratives by focusing on the most relevant parts of the script. Similarly, \[[12](http://arxiv.org/abs/2406.02818)\] proposed the Chain of Agents (CoA) framework, which leverages multi-agent collaboration to process long contexts by dividing the input into segments and synthesizing the results. This approach mitigates the issue of long context focus by assigning each agent a short context, enhancing the model's ability to reason over extended narratives.

Efficient handling of long sequences is another critical aspect of narrative summarization. Several models have been proposed to reduce the computational and memory costs associated with processing long texts. For example, \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/2007.14062)\] introduced BigBird, a sparse attention mechanism that reduces the quadratic dependency on sequence length to linear, enabling the processing of longer documents. Similarly, \[[4](http://arxiv.org/abs/2004.05150)\] proposed Longformer, which uses a combination of local and global attention mechanisms to scale linearly with sequence length, making it suitable for long-document tasks. The Reformer \[[5](http://arxiv.org/abs/2001.04451)\] and Linformer \[[6](http://arxiv.org/abs/2006.04768)\] further improved efficiency by introducing locality-sensitive hashing and low-rank approximations of self-attention, respectively, reducing the complexity from $O(n^2)$ to $O(n)$. These models have been applied to various tasks, including long document summarization, demonstrating significant improvements in performance and efficiency.

Recent advancements in extending the context window of LLMs have also contributed to the field. Position Interpolation \[[7](http://arxiv.org/abs/2306.15595)\] enables extending the context window of RoPE-based models to up to 32768 tokens with minimal fine-tuning, while maintaining performance on tasks requiring long context. The Unlimiformer \[[10](http://arxiv.org/abs/2305.01625)\] further extends this capability by allowing unlimited input length through a k-nearest-neighbor (kNN) index, enabling the processing of very long documents without truncation. These developments have made it possible to handle the extended input lengths required for narrative summarization tasks.

In addition to model improvements, evaluation methodologies have also evolved. \[[13](http://arxiv.org/abs/2310.00785)\]

### <a id='category-efficient-attention-mechanisms'></a>Efficient Attention Mechanisms: 3 papers found.
Efficient attention mechanisms have been a focal point in improving the scalability and performance of transformer models, particularly for handling long sequences. Several approaches have been proposed to mitigate the quadratic computational and memory costs associated with full attention mechanisms. For instance, BigBird introduces a sparse attention mechanism that reduces the dependency from $O(L^2)$ to $O(L)$, enabling the model to handle sequences up to eight times longer than previously possible with similar hardware \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/2007.14062)\]. This mechanism also maintains the universal approximation and Turing completeness properties of full attention models, while leveraging $O(1)$ global tokens, such as the CLS token, to capture global context effectively \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/2007.14062)\]. Similarly, the Reformer employs locality-sensitive hashing to reduce attention complexity to $O(L \log L)$, and reversible residual layers to minimize memory usage during training \[[5](http://arxiv.org/abs/2001.04451)\]. The Linformer further improves efficiency by approximating self-attention with a low-rank matrix, achieving linear time and space complexity $O(n)$ while maintaining performance comparable to standard transformers \[[6](http://arxiv.org/abs/2006.04768)\]. These advancements collectively address the limitations of traditional attention mechanisms, making transformer models more viable for long-form narrative processing, which aligns with the challenges faced in narrative summarization tasks like those addressed by NexusSum \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/2007.14062)\]\[[5](http://arxiv.org/abs/2001.04451)\]\[[6](http://arxiv.org/abs/2006.04768)\].

[Snippet]: «Addressing the challenge of effectively processing long contexts has become a critical issue for Large Language Models (LLMs). Two common strategies have emerged: 1) reducing the input length, such as retrieving relevant chunks by Retrieval-Augmented Generation (RAG), and 2) expanding the context window limit of LLMs. However, both strategies have drawbacks: input reduction has no guarantee of covering the part with needed information, while window extension struggles with focusing on the pertinent information for solving the task. To mitigate these limitations, we propose Chain-of-Agents (CoA), a novel framework that harnesses multi-agent collaboration through natural language to enable information aggregation and context reasoning across various LLMs over long-context tasks. CoA consists of multiple worker agents who sequentially communicate to handle different segmented portions of the text, followed by a manager agent who synthesizes these contributions into a coherent final output. CoA processes the entire input by interleaving reading and reasoning, and it mitigates long context focus issues by assigning each agent a short context. We perform comprehensive evaluation of CoA on a wide range of long-context tasks in question answering, summarization, and code completion, demonstrating significant improvements by up to 10% over strong baselines of RAG, Full-Context, and multi-agent LLMs.»
