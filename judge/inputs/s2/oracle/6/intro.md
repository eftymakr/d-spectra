## Related Works

Long-form narrative summarization, encompassing books, movies, and TV scripts, remains a challenging task for existing large language models (LLMs) due to the complexity of capturing intricate plotlines, character interactions, and thematic coherence \[[13](http://arxiv.org/abs/2310.00785)\]. Prior work has explored various approaches to address these challenges, including abstractive summarization techniques, efficient attention mechanisms, and multi-agent collaboration frameworks. For instance, \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/2404.03561)\] introduced a two-stage abstractive summarization approach for movie scripts, where salient scenes are first identified and then used to generate summaries. This method improves the accuracy of summarizing narratives by focusing on the most relevant parts of the script. Similarly, \[[12](http://arxiv.org/abs/2406.02818)\] proposed the Chain of Agents (CoA) framework, which leverages multi-agent collaboration to process long contexts by dividing the input into segments and synthesizing the results. This approach mitigates the issue of long context focus by assigning each agent a short context, enhancing the model's ability to reason over extended narratives.

Efficient handling of long sequences is another critical aspect of narrative summarization. Several models have been proposed to reduce the computational and memory costs associated with processing long texts. For example, \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/2007.14062)\] introduced BigBird, a sparse attention mechanism that reduces the quadratic dependency on sequence length to linear, enabling the processing of longer documents. Similarly, \[[4](http://arxiv.org/abs/2004.05150)\] proposed Longformer, which uses a combination of local and global attention mechanisms to scale linearly with sequence length, making it suitable for long-document tasks. The Reformer \[[5](http://arxiv.org/abs/2001.04451)\] and Linformer \[[6](http://arxiv.org/abs/2006.04768)\] further improved efficiency by introducing locality-sensitive hashing and low-rank approximations of self-attention, respectively, reducing the complexity from $O(n^2)$ to $O(n)$. These models have been applied to various tasks, including long document summarization, demonstrating significant improvements in performance and efficiency.

Recent advancements in extending the context window of LLMs have also contributed to the field. Position Interpolation \[[7](http://arxiv.org/abs/2306.15595)\] enables extending the context window of RoPE-based models to up to 32768 tokens with minimal fine-tuning, while maintaining performance on tasks requiring long context. The Unlimiformer \[[10](http://arxiv.org/abs/2305.01625)\] further extends this capability by allowing unlimited input length through a k-nearest-neighbor (kNN) index, enabling the processing of very long documents without truncation. These developments have made it possible to handle the extended input lengths required for narrative summarization tasks.

In addition to model improvements, evaluation methodologies have also evolved. \[[13](http://arxiv.org/abs/2310.00785)\]
