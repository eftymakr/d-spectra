## Related Works

Active learning (AL) has been extensively studied for reducing human annotation costs by selecting the most informative unlabeled data for labeling \[[1]()\]. Early approaches such as uncertainty sampling \[[1]()\] and query-by-committee \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/2505.06825)\] aimed to reduce the amount of training data required by focusing on uncertain or ambiguous examples. More recent methods have explored uncertainty metrics to guide the selection of high-value examples in smaller training sets, particularly in image classification tasks \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/2505.06825)\]. These techniques have shown marked improvement over random sampling, especially on more complex tasks \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/2505.06825)\].

Federated learning (FL) has emerged as a promising approach for training models on decentralized data without sharing raw data \[[7](http://arxiv.org/abs/1511.03575)\]. This is particularly relevant in scenarios where data is distributed across many devices, such as mobile phones or edge devices \[[8](http://arxiv.org/abs/1602.05629)\]. However, existing FL methods often assume that data is identically and independently distributed (IID), which is not always the case in real-world applications \[[10](http://arxiv.org/abs/2002.05038)\]. To address this, federated active learning (FAL) has been proposed to combine the benefits of AL and FL, allowing for collaborative data selection and model training while preserving data privacy \[[9](http://arxiv.org/abs/2202.00195)\]. FAL methods have been shown to outperform random sampling strategies in image classification tasks \[[9](http://arxiv.org/abs/2202.00195)\].

Despite these advances, existing FAL methods often fail to account for the heterogeneity of data distribution across clients and the associated fluctuations in global and local model parameters, which can negatively impact model accuracy \[[1]()\]. To address these challenges, several studies have proposed novel approaches to improve the robustness of FAL to data heterogeneity. For example, Knowledge-Aware Federated Active Learning (KAFAL) introduces a novel active sampling method tailored for the federated active learning problem, which addresses the mismatch between the active sampling goals of the global model and the local clients \[[11](http://arxiv.org/abs/2211.13579)\]. Similarly, LoGo proposes a sampling strategy that integrates global and local selector models to resolve imbalances in data distribution and local heterogeneity \[[12](http://arxiv.org/abs/2303.12317)\].

Active Federated Learning (AFL) further improves the efficiency of FL by selecting clients based on their potential to improve model performance, rather than uniformly at random \[[13](http://arxiv.org/abs/1909.12641)\]. This approach reduces the number of required training iterations while maintaining model accuracy, demonstrating the potential of adaptive sampling in FL \[[13](http://arxiv.org/abs/1909.12641)\].

In summary, while AL and FL have been independently studied, their integration in FAL presents new opportunities and challenges. Recent work has focused on improving the robustness of FAL to data heterogeneity and improving the efficiency of data selection. These efforts have led to promising results

### <a id='category-active-learning-techniques'></a>Active Learning Techniques: 8 papers found.
Active learning (AL) is a technique that reduces the number of training examples needed to train a classifier by strategically selecting informative samples, which is particularly useful in scenarios where labeled data is scarce or expensive to obtain \[[1]()\]. Various uncertainty metrics have been proposed to guide this selection process, such as those based on entropy, margin, or confidence \[[1]()\]. These metrics help identify high-value examples that can improve model performance with fewer labeled samples \[[1]()\]. However, applying active learning to convolutional neural networks (CNNs) has faced challenges, as many traditional heuristics are not effective in batch settings \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/2505.06825)\]. Recent work has redefined active learning as a core-set selection problem, where the goal is to choose a subset of data that allows the model to generalize well to the remaining data \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/2505.06825)\]. Additionally, there has been a focus on developing task-agnostic methods that can be applied across different learning tasks without requiring task-specific tuning \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/1505.05424)\]. These methods often involve introducing auxiliary modules, such as loss prediction modules, to guide the selection of informative samples \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/1505.05424)\]. Overall, active learning remains a promising approach for improving the efficiency and effectiveness of training deep models with limited labeled data \[[1]()\]\[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/2505.06825)\]\[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/1505.05424)\].

### <a id='category-federated-learning-frameworks'></a>Federated Learning Frameworks: 4 papers found.
Federated Learning (FL) is a decentralized approach to training machine learning models where data remains on local devices, and only model updates are shared with a central server \[[8](http://arxiv.org/abs/1602.05629)\]. This framework is particularly useful when dealing with privacy-sensitive data or when data is distributed across a large number of devices \[[1]()\]. FL addresses the challenge of training models on non-IID (non-independent and identically distributed) data, which is common in real-world scenarios where data distributions across devices vary significantly \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/1505.05424)\]. Existing FL methods often struggle with data heterogeneity and the associated fluctuations in global and local model parameters, which can degrade model accuracy \[[1]()\]. To mitigate these issues, researchers have proposed various techniques, including methods that focus on robustness to biased sampling and data heterogeneity \[[10](http://arxiv.org/abs/2002.05038)\]. These approaches aim to improve the reliability and performance of FL systems by incorporating strategies such as adaptive communication schedules and alignment losses to calibrate decision boundaries \[[10](http://arxiv.org/abs/2002.05038)\]. Additionally, the communication efficiency of FL is a critical concern, with some methods achieving significant reductions in communication rounds compared to traditional approaches \[[8](http://arxiv.org/abs/1602.05629)\]. Overall, FL represents a promising direction for distributed machine learning, with ongoing research focused on enhancing its scalability, robustness, and practical applicability \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/1505.05424)\]\[[8](http://arxiv.org/abs/1602.05629)\]\[[10](http://arxiv.org/abs/2002.05038)\].

### <a id='category-federated-active-learning-approaches'></a>Federated Active Learning Approaches: 7 papers found.
Federated Active Learning (FAL) has emerged as a promising approach to reduce annotation costs in decentralized learning settings while preserving data privacy \[[1]()\]. Unlike traditional federated learning, which assumes fully labeled data \[[12](http://arxiv.org/abs/2303.12317)\], FAL integrates active learning strategies to select the most informative samples for annotation, addressing the challenge of limited annotation budgets \[[1]()\]. Several approaches have been proposed to enhance the efficiency and effectiveness of FAL. For instance, the F-AL method combines active learning with federated learning to collaboratively select informative instances in a distributed manner, outperforming baseline methods in image classification tasks \[[1]()\]. Similarly, the Knowledge-Aware Federated Active Learning (KAFAL) framework tackles the mismatch between global and local active sampling goals by introducing Knowledge-Specialized Active Sampling (KSAS) and Knowledge-Compensatory Federated Update (KCFU), which address client heterogeneity and non-IID data distributions \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/2505.06825)\]. Additionally, the LoGo framework explores the performance of global and local-only query selector models, demonstrating that their effectiveness depends on inter-class diversity and local heterogeneity, and proposes a sampling strategy that integrates both models to improve performance across varying conditions \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/1505.05424)\]. Active Federated Learning further enhances efficiency by selecting clients based on their data's potential to improve model performance, reducing training iterations by 20-70% while maintaining accuracy \[[4](http://arxiv.org/abs/1506.02142)\]. These approaches collectively highlight the growing interest in FAL as a solution to the annotation bottleneck in real-world applications.
