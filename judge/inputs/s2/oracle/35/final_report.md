Protein conformation sampling and structure prediction have long relied on physics-based simulations such as molecular dynamics (MD) and Monte Carlo (MC) methods \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/2306.03117)\]. To address these limitations, recent advances in deep learning have introduced generative modeling techniques, particularly denoising diffusion models, which enable efficient and accurate protein structure prediction and conformation sampling by learning distributions over crystallographic structures \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/1812.01729)\]. However, integrating physical supervision into these data-driven models remains a challenge, as standard energy-based objectives often lead to intractable optimization \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/1812.01729)\].

### <a id='category-reinforcement-learning-for-diffusion-models'></a>Reinforcement Learning for Diffusion Models: 3 papers found.
Reinforcement learning methods for diffusion models have been explored to directly optimize models for downstream objectives such as human-perceived quality or specific functional properties, rather than relying solely on likelihood-based training. These approaches frame denoising as a multi-step decision-making process, enabling policy gradient algorithms like denoising diffusion policy optimization (DDPO) that outperform traditional reward-weighted likelihood methods \[[10](http://arxiv.org/abs/2305.13301)\]. By incorporating human feedback, such as aesthetic quality or image compressibility, these models can adapt to complex objectives that are difficult to express via prompting alone \[[10](http://arxiv.org/abs/2305.13301)\]. Additionally, methods like Direct Preference Optimization (DPO) simplify the reinforcement learning from human feedback (RLHF) pipeline by introducing a new parameterization of the reward model that allows for closed-form policy extraction, reducing computational complexity and improving stability \[[11](http://arxiv.org/abs/2305.18290)\]. This approach eliminates the need for extensive sampling or hyperparameter tuning during fine-tuning, making it more efficient and easier to implement \[[11](http://arxiv.org/abs/2305.18290)\]. These advancements highlight the growing interest in aligning diffusion models with human preferences and physical constraints, which is particularly relevant for applications such as protein structure prediction where accurate conformational sampling is essential \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/1812.01729)\].
