## Related Works

The rapid growth of open-source machine learning (ML) resources has significantly impacted information retrieval (IR) research, particularly in the areas of resource management and advanced querying \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2305.11164)\]. While platforms like Hugging Face have become central hubs for sharing ML models and datasets, they lack structured representations that enable sophisticated analyses such as tracing model evolution and recommending relevant datasets \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2305.11164)\]. This limitation has motivated the development of knowledge graphs and benchmarking frameworks to enhance the usability of these resources.

Several studies have explored the characteristics and challenges of ML model repositories. For instance, \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/2311.13380)\] conducted a repository mining study on Hugging Face, revealing that only a small proportion of models report carbon emissions, and that there is a strong correlation between model size, dataset size, and carbon footprint. This highlights the need for more transparent and standardized reporting practices in the ML community. Similarly, \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/2303.02552)\] investigated the reuse of pre-trained models on Hugging Face, identifying challenges such as missing attributes, discrepancies between claimed and actual performance, and model risks. These findings underscore the importance of improving model documentation and metadata to support better reuse and evaluation.

The lack of structured data in ML repositories has also been a focus of other studies. \[[4](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/e9270f790459c84c3d16e2bb29bd1ab311ec5c78)\] analyzed the Hugging Face platform to assess the vulnerabilities of open-source AI models, revealing that a significant percentage of models contain high-severity vulnerabilities. This work emphasizes the need for improved security practices and automated vulnerability detection in ML ecosystems. Additionally, \[[5](http://arxiv.org/abs/2401.13822)\] examined dataset documentation on Hugging Face, finding that while dataset cards are widely used, there is significant variability in their completeness and quality. This variability affects the reproducibility and reliability of ML research, further highlighting the need for standardized documentation practices.

In the context of knowledge graphs, \[[6](http://arxiv.org/abs/2303.17580)\] introduced HuggingKG, the first large-scale knowledge graph built from the Hugging Face community, which captures domain-specific relations and textual attributes to support advanced querying and analysis. This work builds on earlier efforts to enhance the usability of ML resources, such as \[[7](http://arxiv.org/abs/2406.08205)\], which proposed HuggingGPT, an agent that leverages large language models to connect various AI models on Hugging Face to solve complex tasks. These initiatives demonstrate a growing trend towards integrating structured data and intelligent systems to improve the management and utilization of ML resources.

Moreover, \[[8](http://arxiv.org/abs/2401.13177)\] conducted a systematic literature review to quantify qualitative claims about PTM reuse on Hugging Face, finding that PTMs have a significantly higher turnover rate than traditional software and that documentation quality is strongly correlated with PTM popularity.
