The category "Network Cost Analysis in Cloud-Native Environments" focuses on the study of network structures and their implications for cost and efficiency in complex systems, particularly in cloud-native environments. Research in this area emphasizes the importance of understanding network topology, community structure, and weighted connections to better characterize and optimize resource usage \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2501.14753)\]. The analysis of weighted networks, where edges represent the intensity or capacity of connections, allows for a more accurate representation of real-world systems such as scientific collaboration and air-transportation networks \[[6](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/4665f10ba0006df8d4fec85e368bcc503d57ce44)\]. This approach enables the identification of hierarchies and organizational principles that underpin network architecture \[[6](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/4665f10ba0006df8d4fec85e368bcc503d57ce44)\]. Additionally, the detection of community structures—groups of nodes with dense internal connections—plays a critical role in understanding how networks function and how resources are distributed \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/2307.12479)\]. Various algorithms have been developed to detect these communities, often involving iterative edge removal based on betweenness measures, which helps in identifying densely connected subgroups \[[4](http://arxiv.org/abs/2207.01222)\]. The concept of modularity, which measures the strength of community structure, has also been leveraged to improve the accuracy of community detection in both synthetic and real-world networks \[[5](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/51e2cba5a013cbd11f2ab760a4e3906cfff44780)\]. These methods are essential for analyzing network costs in cloud-native environments, where efficient resource allocation and cost optimization are paramount \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2501.14753)\]. The integration of network science with financial management, as seen in the FinOps discipline, further underscores the relevance of these studies in addressing the challenges of managing network costs in modern cloud systems \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2501.14753)\].
