## Related Works

The growing adoption of cloud-native applications has led to increased interest in optimizing both resource usage and financial costs. Several studies have explored system-level optimizations for containerized environments, particularly focusing on resource efficiency and cost management. For instance, ABACUS introduces a FinOps solution that enables automated budget analysis and cloud usage surveillance, offering mechanisms to enforce budgets and alert teams when spending thresholds are breached \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2501.14753)\]. This aligns with the broader FinOps discipline, which combines financial management with cloud operations to optimize costs in cloud-based applications \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2501.14753)\]. 

In the context of cloud and AI infrastructure, researchers have examined cost optimization strategies, particularly for AI/ML workloads, which often consume significant computational resources. Techniques such as model quantization, GPU instance selection, and inference optimization have been explored to reduce costs \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/2307.12479)\]. Additionally, the paper highlights that large language model (LLM) inference costs have decreased by approximately 10x annually since 2021, indicating the potential for cost savings through optimized deployment strategies \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/2307.12479)\]. These findings underscore the importance of cost-aware design in modern cloud applications.

Kubernetes, a key platform for container orchestration, has also been the subject of extensive research. The KubeAdaptor framework was developed to address the challenge of inconsistent task scheduling between workflow systems and Kubernetes, ensuring the consistency of task execution order \[[4](http://arxiv.org/abs/2207.01222)\]. This is particularly relevant for applications requiring precise workflow execution, as it improves the efficiency of containerized processes on Kubernetes. Furthermore, the use of Kubernetes in scientific workflows, such as with Pachyderm, has been explored to enable reproducibility and scalability in data-intensive applications \[[5](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/51e2cba5a013cbd11f2ab760a4e3906cfff44780)\]. These studies highlight the role of Kubernetes in supporting complex, data-driven workflows while maintaining reproducibility.

Containerization technologies, such as Docker, have also been extensively studied for their role in enabling reproducibility in scientific research. Docker provides a portable environment for running applications, ensuring that computational workflows can be reproduced across different systems \[[11](http://arxiv.org/abs/1410.0846)\]. This is crucial for maintaining the integrity of scientific results, especially in fields like bioinformatics where data processing pipelines are often complex \[[5](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/51e2cba5a013cbd11f2ab760a4e3906cfff44780)\]. The use of containers in scientific workflows is further supported by frameworks like Pachyderm, which integrates with Kubernetes to manage data pipelines and versioning \[[5](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/51e2cba5a013cbd11f2ab760a4e3906cfff44780)\].

In addition to technical challenges, the financial implications of cloud usage have been explored in the context of FinOps.
