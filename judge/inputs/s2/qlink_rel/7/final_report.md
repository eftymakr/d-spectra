## Related Works

Recent advancements in large language models (LLMs) have focused on improving their alignment with human values and enhancing their reasoning capabilities. Prior work has explored various methods to align LLMs with user intent, including fine-tuning with human feedback \[[19](http://arxiv.org/abs/2203.02155)\], reinforcement learning from human preferences \[[9](http://arxiv.org/abs/2112.00861)\], and instruction tuning \[[20](http://arxiv.org/abs/2306.04387)\]. These approaches aim to improve the model's ability to follow user instructions and produce truthful, helpful outputs. However, many of these methods rely on predefined templates or manual analysis to detect refusal behaviors, which can be limiting in complex or adversarial settings \[[19](http://arxiv.org/abs/2203.02155)\].

The challenge of identifying refusal behaviors in LLMs has been addressed in several studies. For instance, \[[19](http://arxiv.org/abs/2203.02155)\] introduced InstructGPT, a model fine-tuned with human feedback to improve truthfulness and reduce toxic outputs. This approach demonstrated that fine-tuning with human feedback can significantly improve model alignment without sacrificing performance on standard benchmarks. Similarly, \[[9](http://arxiv.org/abs/2112.00861)\] explored the use of ranked preference modeling to enhance alignment, finding that this method scales more effectively with model size compared to imitation learning. These studies highlight the importance of human feedback in aligning LLMs with user intent.

In the context of reasoning, \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2309.02144)\] proposed the use of chain-of-thought (CoT) prompting to enhance the reasoning capabilities of LLMs. This technique involves generating step-by-step reasoning traces to improve the model's ability to solve complex problems. However, \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2309.02144)\] also noted that fine-tuned LLMs can suffer from an assessment misalignment problem, where they assign higher scores to subpar CoT responses. To address this, \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2309.02144)\] introduced an Alignment Fine-Tuning (AFT) paradigm that incorporates a constraint alignment loss to ensure that positive responses are consistently scored higher than negative ones. This approach has shown promising results in improving the reliability of CoT-based reasoning.

Another related work, \[[14](http://arxiv.org/abs/2304.08485)\], explored the use of visual instruction tuning to improve the zero-shot capabilities of LLMs on new tasks. This approach involves training models on instruction-following data to enhance their ability to generalize to unseen tasks. \[[14](http://arxiv.org/abs/2304.08485)\] demonstrated that models fine-tuned with visual instruction data can achieve state-of-the-art performance on tasks such as visual question answering and image captioning. This work highlights the potential of instruction tuning in improving the versatility of LLMs across different modalities.

### <a id='category-alignment-fine-tuning-paradigms'></a>Alignment Fine-Tuning Paradigms: 3 papers found.
The category "Alignment Fine-Tuning Paradigms" explores methods to enhance the reasoning capabilities of large language models (LLMs) while addressing issues such as assessment misalignment and model degradation. Recent studies highlight that fine-tuning LLMs on chain-of-thought (COT) reasoning data can significantly improve their reasoning abilities, but this approach often leads to suboptimal performance due to the model's tendency to assign higher scores to low-quality COTs \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2309.02144)\]. To mitigate this, the Alignment Fine-Tuning (AFT) paradigm was introduced, which involves three key steps: first, fine-tuning with COT data; second, generating and categorizing multiple COT responses into positive and negative based on their correctness; and third, calibrating scores using a constraint alignment loss that ensures positive responses outperform negative ones while keeping negative scores within a reasonable range \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2309.02144)\]. This approach not only improves the quality of reasoning but also adapts to ranking-based feedback scenarios. Furthermore, the category also examines existing ranking-based alignment methods like DPO, RRHF, and PRO, revealing that the constraint component—often overlooked in these methods—is crucial for maintaining model performance and preventing degradation \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2309.02144)\]. The broader context of LLM development includes advancements in pre-training, post-training, utilization, and evaluation methodologies, emphasizing the need for robust alignment techniques to ensure safe and effective model behavior \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/2205.11916)\].

### <a id='category-chain-of-thought-prompting'></a>Chain-of-Thought Prompting: 2 papers found.
Chain-of-thought (CoT) prompting has emerged as a powerful technique for enhancing the reasoning capabilities of large language models (LLMs), particularly in complex tasks such as arithmetic and symbolic reasoning \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2309.02144)\]. This approach involves eliciting multi-step reasoning by providing step-by-step answer examples, which has led to state-of-the-art performance on various benchmarks \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2309.02144)\]. Furthermore, research has shown that LLMs can also serve as effective zero-shot reasoners when prompted with "Let's think step by step," achieving significant improvements without the need for task-specific exemplars \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2309.02144)\]. The versatility of this method across diverse reasoning tasks suggests that LLMs possess untapped zero-shot reasoning capabilities that can be harnessed through simple prompting strategies \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2309.02144)\]. Additionally, recent work has explored the integration of reasoning with action generation, leading to the development of frameworks like ReAct, which combine reasoning traces with task-specific actions to improve performance in interactive decision-making tasks \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/2205.11916)\]. Such advancements highlight the growing interest in leveraging LLMs for reasoning and decision-making tasks through innovative prompting strategies.

### <a id='category-instruction-tuning-approaches'></a>Instruction Tuning Approaches: 4 papers found.
The Related Works section for the paper abstract focuses on recent advancements in instruction tuning for large language models (LLMs) and their extension to multimodal settings. Instruction tuning has significantly advanced LLMs such as ChatGPT, enabling them to align with human instructions across diverse tasks \[[20](http://arxiv.org/abs/2306.04387)\]. However, progress in open vision-language models (VLMs) has been limited due to the scarcity of high-quality instruction datasets \[[20](http://arxiv.org/abs/2306.04387)\]. To address this, the Multi-Modal, Multilingual Instruction Tuning (M$^3$IT) dataset was introduced, comprising 40 carefully curated datasets with 2.4 million instances and 400 manually written task instructions, reformatted into a vision-to-text structure \[[20](http://arxiv.org/abs/2306.04387)\]. This dataset is translated into 80 languages, ensuring broader accessibility \[[20](http://arxiv.org/abs/2306.04387)\]. In parallel, large multimodal models (LMMs) have shown encouraging progress with visual instruction tuning, with the LLaVA framework demonstrating that a fully-connected vision-language connector is surprisingly powerful and data-efficient \[[13](http://arxiv.org/abs/2310.03744)\]. The LLaVA model, trained using instruction-tuned data generated by GPT-4, achieves state-of-the-art performance on 11 benchmarks and exhibits impressive multimodal chat abilities \[[14](http://arxiv.org/abs/2304.08485)\]. Additionally, research has explored the use of machine-generated instruction-following data to improve zero-shot capabilities on new tasks, with early experiments showing that instruction tuning on such data can lead to significant improvements in performance \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/2303.18223)\]. These studies collectively highlight the growing interest in instruction tuning for both unimodal and multimodal models, emphasizing the importance of high-quality instruction datasets and efficient training strategies \[[13](http://arxiv.org/abs/2310.03744)\]\[[14](http://arxiv.org/abs/2304.08485)\]\[[20](http://arxiv.org/abs/2306.04387)\].
