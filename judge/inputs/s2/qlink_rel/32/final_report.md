## Related Works

Recent advancements in large language models (LLMs) have emphasized the importance of understanding how feature representations evolve across layers, as this knowledge is critical for improving interpretability and robustness \[[16](http://arxiv.org/abs/2012.13255)\]. While prior work has identified critical layers linked to specific functions or behaviors, these studies often rely on data-dependent analyses of fine-tuned models, limiting their utility to post-hoc settings \[[16](http://arxiv.org/abs/2012.13255)\]. In contrast, our approach is data-oblivious, leveraging Centered Kernel Alignment (CKA) to analyze representation dynamics in pre-fine-tuned models \[[16](http://arxiv.org/abs/2012.13255)\]. This enables us to identify intrinsic critical layers without requiring fine-tuning, offering a more generalizable framework for understanding model behavior.

The use of CKA for analyzing representation dynamics has been explored in several works. For instance, \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2210.05643)\] investigates the Neural Tangent Kernel (NTK) to understand fine-tuning dynamics in pre-trained language models, showing that the NTK can describe fine-tuning updates under certain conditions. This aligns with our findings that layers with significant shifts in representation space are most affected during fine-tuning \[[16](http://arxiv.org/abs/2012.13255)\]. Similarly, \[[12](http://arxiv.org/abs/2110.04366)\] presents a unified framework for parameter-efficient transfer learning, highlighting the importance of modifying specific hidden states in pre-trained models to achieve strong performance with minimal parameter updates. Our work extends this by identifying which layers are most critical for fine-tuning, offering a complementary perspective \[[12](http://arxiv.org/abs/2110.04366)\].

Efficient fine-tuning methods, such as LoRA and adapters, have also been widely studied. LoRA, for example, freezes pre-trained model weights and injects trainable rank decomposition matrices into each layer, significantly reducing the number of trainable parameters while maintaining performance \[[8](http://arxiv.org/abs/2106.09685)\]. This is consistent with our findings that fine-tuning critical layers can lead to greater loss reduction compared to non-critical layers \[[16](http://arxiv.org/abs/2012.13255)\]. Adapters, on the other hand, add only a few trainable parameters per task, enabling parameter sharing across tasks \[[14](http://arxiv.org/abs/1902.00751)\]. These methods are closely related to our work, as they also aim to optimize fine-tuning efficiency while maintaining model performance \[[14](http://arxiv.org/abs/1902.00751)\].

In addition to fine-tuning, the role of pre-training in shaping model behavior has been extensively studied. For example, \[[16](http://arxiv.org/abs/2012.13255)\] shows that pre-training implicitly minimizes intrinsic dimensionality, which may explain the effectiveness of large models in low-data regimes. This is relevant to our analysis of representation shifts during fine-tuning, as it suggests that pre-trained models are already optimized for efficient adaptation \[[16](http://arxiv.org/abs/2012.13255)\]. ### <a id='category-efficient-fine-tuning-techniques-(e.g.-lora-adapters)'></a>Efficient Fine-Tuning Techniques (e.g., LoRA, Adapters): 4 papers found.
Efficient fine-tuning techniques such as LoRA and QLoRA have emerged as critical approaches for adapting large language models (LLMs) to specific tasks without retraining all parameters, significantly reducing computational costs \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/2307.09288)\]. LoRA introduces trainable rank decomposition matrices into each Transformer layer, allowing for parameter-efficient adaptation while maintaining model quality comparable to full fine-tuning \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/2307.09288)\]. This method drastically reduces the number of trainable parameters and GPU memory usage, making it feasible to fine-tune models like GPT-3 175B \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/2307.09288)\]. QLoRA further advances this by combining 4-bit quantization with LoRA, enabling the fine-tuning of massive models (e.g., 65B parameters) on a single GPU while preserving performance \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/2303.18223)\]. This approach introduces innovations such as 4-bit NormalFloat (NF4) data types and paged optimizers to manage memory efficiently \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/2303.18223)\]. Additionally, research into intrinsic dimensionality has shown that pre-trained models often operate in a low-dimensional subspace, suggesting that only a subset of parameters needs adjustment during fine-tuning \[[4](http://arxiv.org/abs/2305.18290)\]. These findings support the effectiveness of parameter-efficient methods by highlighting the potential for significant performance gains with minimal parameter updates \[[4](http://arxiv.org/abs/2305.18290)\]. Such techniques are essential for practical deployment and scalability in real-world applications \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/2307.09288)\]\[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/2303.18223)\]\[[4](http://arxiv.org/abs/2305.18290)\].
