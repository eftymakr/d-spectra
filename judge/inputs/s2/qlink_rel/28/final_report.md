## Related Works

Federated Learning (FL) has emerged as a promising paradigm for training machine learning models while preserving data privacy by keeping training data decentralized \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/1912.04977)\]. FL enables multiple clients to collaboratively train a model under the orchestration of a central server, without sharing raw data \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/1912.04977)\]. This approach is particularly relevant in scenarios where data is distributed across devices, such as mobile phones or IoT devices, and where privacy and regulatory constraints are significant \[[10](http://arxiv.org/abs/1602.05629)\]. However, existing FL methods face challenges due to the heterogeneity of data distributions across clients and the associated fluctuations in global and local model parameters, which can negatively impact model accuracy \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/1812.01097)\].

Several works have addressed these challenges. For instance, FedProx introduces a framework to handle heterogeneity in federated networks by generalizing and re-parameterizing the FedAvg algorithm, allowing for more robust convergence in highly heterogeneous settings \[[8](http://arxiv.org/abs/1812.06127)\]. Similarly, Adaptive Federated Optimization proposes federated versions of adaptive optimizers such as Adagrad, Adam, and Yogi, which improve performance by addressing the convergence issues caused by client heterogeneity \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/2003.00295)\]. These methods demonstrate that adaptive optimization techniques can significantly enhance the performance of FL in non-convex settings.

Non-IID data is another critical challenge in FL, as it can lead to significant accuracy degradation. Research has shown that non-IID data can reduce accuracy by up to 55% for neural networks trained on highly skewed data, where each client trains only on a single class \[[7](http://arxiv.org/abs/1806.00582)\]. To mitigate this, strategies such as sharing a small subset of data across clients have been proposed, which can improve accuracy by up to 30% \[[7](http://arxiv.org/abs/1806.00582)\]. Additionally, the work on Federated Semi-Supervised Learning (FSSL) addresses the issue of partially labeled data, proposing a novel method called FedMatch that improves upon naive approaches by introducing an inter-client consistency loss \[[18](http://arxiv.org/abs/2006.12097)\].

The problem of non-identical data distribution across clients has also been explored in the context of federated visual classification. Studies have shown that performance degrades as the distribution of data across devices becomes more heterogeneous, and mitigation strategies such as server momentum have been proposed to improve classification accuracy \[[13](http://arxiv.org/abs/1909.06335)\]. Furthermore, the work on Clustered Federated Learning (CFL) introduces a framework that groups clients based on their data distribution, allowing for more efficient and accurate model training by leveraging the geometric properties of the loss surface \[[6](http://arxiv.org/abs/1910.01991)\].

### <a id='category-federated-learning-frameworks'></a>Federated Learning Frameworks: 11 papers found.
Federated learning (FL) has emerged as a critical paradigm for training machine learning models while preserving data privacy by keeping training data decentralized \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/1912.04977)\]. This approach is particularly relevant in scenarios involving heterogeneous data distributions across clients, such as mobile devices or autonomous systems, where data is often non-identically distributed \[[4](http://arxiv.org/abs/1804.08333)\]. Existing FL frameworks, such as FedAvg, face challenges in convergence and performance when dealing with statistical heterogeneity, as local updates can drift apart, leading to slow convergence and difficulty in tuning \[[10](http://arxiv.org/abs/1602.05629)\]. To address these issues, researchers have proposed advanced techniques like FedProx, which generalizes FedAvg by incorporating proximal terms to handle device-level constraints and data heterogeneity, resulting in more stable and accurate convergence \[[4](http://arxiv.org/abs/1804.08333)\]. Additionally, clustered FL (CFL) introduces a novel federated multitask learning framework that groups clients based on data distribution without requiring prior knowledge of the number of clusters, offering flexibility and strong mathematical guarantees \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/2003.00295)\]. Furthermore, methods like ensemble distillation have been introduced to enable flexible aggregation of heterogeneous client models, improving training efficiency and reducing communication rounds \[[5](http://arxiv.org/abs/1610.05492)\]. These developments highlight the ongoing efforts to enhance FL's robustness, scalability, and adaptability in real-world applications.

### <a id='category-adaptive-optimization-techniques'></a>Adaptive Optimization Techniques: 1 papers found.
Adaptive optimization techniques have gained significant attention in both non-federated and federated learning settings due to their ability to handle heterogeneous data and improve convergence behavior \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/2003.00295)\]. In non-federated scenarios, adaptive optimizers such as Adagrad, Adam, and Yogi have been successful in addressing issues related to varying data distributions and model parameter fluctuations \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/2003.00295)\]. These methods adjust learning rates dynamically based on historical gradients, which can lead to more stable and efficient training. In the context of federated learning, where client datasets are often non-IID (independent and identically distributed), the use of adaptive optimizers has been shown to enhance model performance by mitigating the challenges posed by data heterogeneity \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/2003.00295)\]. This is particularly important as standard methods like Federated Averaging (FedAvg) often struggle with convergence and tuning in such environments. The integration of adaptive optimization into federated learning frameworks not only improves the robustness of the training process but also contributes to better communication efficiency and overall model accuracy. Therefore, the development of federated versions of adaptive optimizers represents a promising direction for advancing the field of distributed machine learning \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/2003.00295)\].

### <a id='category-communication-efficiency-in-federated-learning'></a>Communication Efficiency in Federated Learning: 3 papers found.
Communication efficiency in federated learning is a critical challenge, particularly due to the resource constraints of mobile devices and the need to minimize uplink communication costs \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/1812.01097)\]. Existing approaches focus on reducing the amount of data transmitted between clients and the central server, with methods such as structured updates and sketched updates being proposed to achieve significant reductions in communication overhead \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/1912.04977)\]. Structured updates involve learning model parameters from a restricted space, such as low-rank matrices or masked representations, while sketched updates employ compression techniques like quantization, random rotations, and subsampling to reduce the size of transmitted updates \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/1812.01097)\]. These strategies have been shown to decrease communication costs by up to two orders of magnitude, making them essential for practical deployment on mobile platforms \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/1812.01097)\]. Additionally, federated learning frameworks must address the challenges of non-IID data distributions and communication constraints, which can limit the effectiveness of traditional training methods \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/1912.04977)\]. Recent work has also explored multi-task learning and systems-aware optimization to improve robustness against stragglers and enhance fault tolerance in distributed settings \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/2003.00295)\]. These advancements highlight the growing interest in optimizing communication efficiency while maintaining model performance in federated learning environments.
