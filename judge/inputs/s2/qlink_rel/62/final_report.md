## Related Works

Generative retrieval has emerged as a promising paradigm in information retrieval, shifting from traditional index-based methods to end-to-end neural network models that directly generate document identifiers \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2204.10628)\]. This approach leverages large language models to encode entire document corpora, enabling seamless integration with generative models \[[9](http://arxiv.org/abs/2202.06991)\]. Unlike conventional retrieval systems that rely on separate indexing and retrieval stages, generative retrieval models operate as a single differentiable search index, generating identifiers through constrained decoding \[[10](http://arxiv.org/abs/2311.09134)\]. This paradigm has been explored in various studies, each addressing specific challenges in scalability, effectiveness, and generalization.

Early work on generative retrieval focused on autoregressive models that generate document identifiers by leveraging n-grams or semantic representations \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2204.10628)\]. These models often use hierarchical structures to partition the search space, but recent approaches have moved toward using all n-grams in a passage as potential identifiers, allowing for more flexible and effective retrieval \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2204.10628)\]. This shift has led to significant improvements in performance, with some models achieving up to a 10-point improvement over traditional retrieval systems on benchmark datasets \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2204.10628)\].

The integration of generative retrieval with large language models has also been explored in the context of knowledge-intensive tasks, where models must retrieve and generate answers based on supporting evidence \[[8](http://arxiv.org/abs/2005.11401)\]. Retrieval-augmented generation (RAG) combines pre-trained models with dense vector indexes to enhance the ability of models to access and manipulate knowledge \[[8](http://arxiv.org/abs/2005.11401)\]. This approach has been shown to outperform both parametric and task-specific architectures, particularly in open-domain question answering \[[8](http://arxiv.org/abs/2005.11401)\]. Additionally, the use of query generation models to improve the alignment between indexed documents and retrieval queries has been proposed to address data distribution mismatches \[[11](http://arxiv.org/abs/2206.10128)\].

Another critical challenge in generative retrieval is the optimization of retrieval performance through beam search and constrained decoding. While beam search is commonly used to generate document identifiers, recent studies have shown that the use of marginal distributions during generation may not be optimal \[[12](http://arxiv.org/abs/2306.15222)\]. This has led to the development of learning-to-rank frameworks that directly optimize passage ranking, bridging the gap between autoregressive generation and the final retrieval target \[[12](http://arxiv.org/abs/2306.15222)\].

The scalability of generative retrieval to large corpora has also been a focus of recent research. Studies have explored how generative models can be trained to handle millions of passages, emphasizing the importance of synthetic queries and efficient indexing strategies \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/2305.11841)\]. These findings highlight the potential of generative retrieval but also underscore the challenges in scaling to real-world applications \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/2305.11841)\].

### <a id='category-scalability-and-indexing-strategies'></a>Scalability and Indexing Strategies: 4 papers found.
The category "Scalability and Indexing Strategies" explores the challenges and advancements in generative retrieval systems, particularly focusing on how these models handle large-scale document corpora and indexing strategies. Generative retrieval models, such as those based on the Differentiable Search Index (DSI), aim to replace traditional index structures with a single transformer model that performs both indexing and retrieval \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/2305.11841)\]. However, these models face limitations when scaling to large datasets, as they often struggle with data distribution mismatches between indexing and retrieval phases \[[11](http://arxiv.org/abs/2206.10128)\]. For instance, during indexing, documents are typically represented based on their full text, while retrieval queries are often shorter, leading to a mismatch in the data distribution \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/2305.11841)\]. To address this, researchers have proposed innovative indexing frameworks, such as DSI-QG, which incorporates query generation models to create more relevant document representations during indexing \[[11](http://arxiv.org/abs/2206.10128)\]. Additionally, other approaches like RIPOR introduce optimization frameworks that improve the accuracy of relevance scoring during sequential document ID generation \[[10](http://arxiv.org/abs/2311.09134)\]. These strategies highlight the ongoing efforts to enhance the scalability and effectiveness of generative retrieval models, particularly in large-scale and cross-lingual retrieval tasks \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/2305.11841)\]\[[10](http://arxiv.org/abs/2311.09134)\]\[[11](http://arxiv.org/abs/2206.10128)\].

[Snippet]: «Conventional document retrieval techniques are mainly based on the index-retrieve paradigm. It is challenging to optimize pipelines based on this paradigm in an end-to-end manner. As an alternative, generative retrieval represents documents as identifiers (docid) and retrieves documents by generating docids, enabling end-to-end modeling of document retrieval tasks. However, it is an open question how one should define the document identifiers. Current approaches to the task of defining document identifiers rely on fixed rule-based docids, such as the title of a document or the result of clustering BERT embeddings, which often fail to capture the complete semantic information of a document. We propose GenRet, a document tokenization learning method to address the challenge of defining document identifiers for generative retrieval. GenRet learns to tokenize documents into short discrete representations (i.e., docids) via a discrete auto-encoding approach. Three components are included in GenRet: (i) a tokenization model that produces docids for documents; (ii) a reconstruction model that learns to reconstruct a document based on a docid; and (iii) a sequence-to-sequence retrieval model that generates relevant document identifiers directly for a designated query. We also develop a progressive training scheme to capture the autoregressive nature of docids and to stabilize training. We conduct experiments on the NQ320K, MS MARCO, and BEIR datasets to assess the effectiveness of GenRet. GenRet establishes the new state-of-the-art on the NQ320K dataset. Especially, compared to generative retrieval baselines, GenRet can achieve significant improvements on the unseen documents. GenRet also outperforms comparable baselines on MS MARCO and BEIR, demonstrating the method's generalizability.»

[Snippet]: «Generative retrieval stands out as a promising new paradigm in text retrieval that aims to generate identifier strings of relevant passages as the retrieval target. This generative paradigm taps into powerful generative language models, distinct from traditional sparse or dense retrieval methods. However, only learning to generate is insufficient for generative retrieval. Generative retrieval learns to generate identifiers of relevant passages as an intermediate goal and then converts predicted identifiers into the final passage rank list. The disconnect between the learning objective of autoregressive models and the desired passage ranking target leads to a learning gap. To bridge this gap, we propose a learning-to-rank framework for generative retrieval, dubbed LTRGR. LTRGR enables generative retrieval to learn to rank passages directly, optimizing the autoregressive model toward the final passage ranking target via a rank loss. This framework only requires an additional learning-to-rank training phase to enhance current generative retrieval systems and does not add any burden to the inference stage. We conducted experiments on three public benchmarks, and the results demonstrate that LTRGR achieves state-of-the-art performance among generative retrieval methods. The code and checkpoints are released at https://github.com/liyongqi67/LTRGR.»
