## Related Works

Narrative summarization, which involves condensing long-form narratives such as books, movies, and TV scripts, has been a challenging task for existing language models due to the complexity of capturing intricate plotlines, character interactions, and thematic coherence \[[15](http://arxiv.org/abs/2005.14165)\]. Early approaches to text summarization relied on extractive methods, which simply rephrase or restructure existing content without generating new information \[[9](http://arxiv.org/abs/1611.04230)\]. However, these methods often fail to capture the deeper meaning and structure of narratives \[[16](http://arxiv.org/abs/1509.00685)\]. More recent work has explored abstractive summarization using sequence-to-sequence models, which can generate novel content while preserving the key elements of the original text \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/1602.06023)\]. These models have been further improved with attention mechanisms that allow for better alignment between input and output \[[5](http://arxiv.org/abs/1409.3215)\]. 

A key limitation of many sequence-to-sequence models is their tendency to repeat information or produce incoherent outputs, especially when dealing with longer texts \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/1704.04368)\]. To address this, researchers have proposed hybrid architectures such as pointer-generator networks, which allow models to copy words from the source text while also generating new content \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/1704.04368)\]. This approach has been shown to improve the accuracy and fluency of generated summaries \[[10](http://arxiv.org/abs/1705.04304)\]. Additionally, coverage mechanisms have been introduced to prevent repetition by tracking what has been summarized \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/1704.04368)\]. 

Another challenge in narrative summarization is the need for structured processing of long-form content. Recent work has explored hierarchical summarization pipelines that process text in chunks, allowing for better control over output length and quality \[[11](http://arxiv.org/abs/1808.10792)\]. This approach has been further enhanced with multi-agent frameworks that leverage multiple language models to improve coherence and accuracy \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/1602.06023)\]. Such frameworks avoid the need for fine-tuning, making them more scalable and adaptable to different domains \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/1602.06023)\]. 

The use of pre-trained models has also gained traction in narrative summarization. For instance, models such as BERT have been adapted for both extractive and abstractive summarization tasks, leveraging their ability to capture semantic meaning \[[13](http://arxiv.org/abs/1908.08345)\]. Additionally, large-scale pre-training on diverse corpora has enabled models to generalize better across different domains, including narrative texts . These models have also been fine-tuned with novel training strategies, such as reinforcement learning, to improve the quality of generated summaries \[[10](http://arxiv.org/abs/1705.04304)\]. 

Finally, the evaluation of summarization models remains a critical area of research. While traditional metrics such as ROUGE have been widely used, recent work has highlighted their limitations in capturing human judgment and factual correctness \[[6](http://arxiv.org/abs/1908.08960)\]. The evaluation of text summarization has been a focal point of recent research, with a growing emphasis on improving the alignment between automatic metrics and human judgment. Existing studies highlight that current evaluation protocols are weakly correlated with human assessment and fail to account for critical aspects such as factual accuracy \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/1602.06023)\]. This shortcoming is exacerbated by the use of automatically collected datasets, which often introduce noise and inconsistencies that hinder model training and evaluation \[[6](http://arxiv.org/abs/1908.08960)\]. Additionally, models tend to overfit to layout biases present in these datasets, leading to limited diversity in their outputs \[[6](http://arxiv.org/abs/1908.08960)\]. To address these issues, researchers have undertaken comprehensive evaluations of 14 automatic metrics, benchmarking them against neural summarization models and human annotations \[[12](http://arxiv.org/abs/2007.12626)\]. These efforts have led to the development of a unified toolkit for evaluating summarization models across a broad range of metrics, promoting more standardized and reliable evaluation practices \[[12](http://arxiv.org/abs/2007.12626)\]. The work also emphasizes the importance of diverse human judgments, incorporating both expert and crowd-sourced annotations to enhance the robustness of evaluation protocols \[[12](http://arxiv.org/abs/2007.12626)\]. These advancements are crucial for improving the quality and reliability of summarization systems, particularly in handling complex narrative structures that require capturing intricate plotlines and thematic coherence \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/1602.06023)\].

### <a id='category-attention-mechanisms'></a>Attention Mechanisms: 2 papers found.
Attention mechanisms have been widely explored in neural summarization to improve the quality and coherence of generated summaries. Early approaches, such as RNN-based encoder-decoder models with attention, showed promise for short texts but struggled with longer documents, often producing repetitive or incoherent outputs \[[10](http://arxiv.org/abs/1705.04304)\]. To address these limitations, researchers introduced intra-attention mechanisms that allow models to focus on both the input and the continuously generated output, enhancing the model's ability to maintain coherence during long sequence generation \[[10](http://arxiv.org/abs/1705.04304)\]. Additionally, combining supervised learning with reinforcement learning (RL) has been shown to improve summary readability by aligning the model's output with global sequence prediction objectives \[[10](http://arxiv.org/abs/1705.04304)\]. Another line of research focuses on content selection, where data-efficient content selectors are used to identify key phrases from the source document, ensuring that summaries are both concise and informative \[[11](http://arxiv.org/abs/1808.10792)\]. These selectors are often integrated as a bottom-up attention step, helping to constrain the model to likely phrases and improve compression while maintaining fluency \[[11](http://arxiv.org/abs/1808.10792)\]. These advancements highlight the ongoing efforts to refine attention mechanisms for better performance in abstractive summarization tasks.
