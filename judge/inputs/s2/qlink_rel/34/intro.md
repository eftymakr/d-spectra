## Related Works

Time series forecasting has been a cornerstone in financial applications, with recent advancements focusing on improving accuracy and robustness in the presence of uncertainty. Traditional approaches such as prediction-only or predict-then-optimize (PnO) methods suffer from goal mismatch, where the forecasting and allocation objectives are not aligned \[[24](http://arxiv.org/abs/1905.10437)\]. This issue is further exacerbated by the introduction of state-of-the-art (SOTA) forecasting models, which can introduce additional uncertainty into the predicted results \[[25](http://arxiv.org/abs/1506.01497)\]. To address these challenges, several works have explored hybrid models that integrate forecasting with optimization, while others have focused on improving the reliability of forecasts through uncertainty quantification.

The concept of end-to-end training with objective alignment has been explored in the context of time series forecasting. For instance, the N-BEATS model \[[24](http://arxiv.org/abs/1905.10437)\] demonstrates that deep learning primitives such as residual blocks can be sufficient to solve a wide range of forecasting problems without the need for domain-specific components. Similarly, the iTransformer \[[9](http://arxiv.org/abs/2310.06625)\] introduces an architecture that applies attention and feed-forward networks on inverted dimensions, enabling better utilization of arbitrary lookback windows and improved generalization across different variates. These models highlight the potential of deep learning to capture complex temporal patterns while maintaining interpretability.

Uncertainty calibration in time series forecasting has also been a focus of recent research. The DeepAR model \[[4](http://arxiv.org/abs/1704.04110)\] is a probabilistic forecasting method based on autoregressive recurrent networks, which provides accurate forecasts by estimating the probability distribution of future values. This approach has been shown to improve accuracy by around 15% compared to state-of-the-art methods. Similarly, the Informer \[[22](http://arxiv.org/abs/2012.07436)\] model introduces a ProbSparse self-attention mechanism that reduces time and memory complexity while maintaining performance on sequence dependency alignment. These models demonstrate the importance of efficient attention mechanisms in handling long sequences and reducing computational overhead.

In the context of financial applications, the Temporal Fusion Transformer (TFT) \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/1912.09363)\] has been designed for multi-horizon forecasting, incorporating interpretable self-attention layers and specialized components for feature selection. The TFT model is particularly effective in handling complex inputs such as static covariates and exogenous time series, making it suitable for financial forecasting tasks. Additionally, the TimesNet \[[6](http://arxiv.org/abs/2210.02186)\] model extends the analysis of temporal variations into a 2D space, enabling the modeling of both intraperiod and interperiod variations. This approach has led to significant improvements in forecasting accuracy across multiple tasks, including short- and long-term forecasting, imputation, and anomaly detection.
