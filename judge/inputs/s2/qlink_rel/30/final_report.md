## Related Works

Modern databases often trade strong transaction isolation for weak isolation guarantees to achieve low latency and high throughput, particularly in distributed systems \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/1908.04509)\]. This trade-off has led to the emergence of weak isolation levels such as Read Committed (RC), Read Atomic (RA), and Causal Consistency (CC), which are increasingly adopted in production environments \[[3](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/cbe191bda20907994d45bc835d20446968370add)\]. However, these weaker isolation levels can introduce bugs that violate data consistency, as evidenced by several real-world cases \[[4](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/615ff2896b52bee2cbfd64ced324106a29b73397)\]. Black-box testing has emerged as a critical technique for detecting such isolation bugs by verifying whether transaction histories adhere to a given isolation level \[[5](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/38c443a37914e287c42a3238ccebd7372847d5f9)\].

The complexity of checking transactional consistency has been extensively studied. For instance, serializability is known to be NP-complete, while certain models like read committed and read atomic are polynomial-time checkable \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/1908.04509)\]. More recently, snapshot isolation (SI) has been shown to be NP-complete in general, but polynomial-time algorithms exist under certain parameter constraints \[[2](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/9f96a48d2bc0e77004b32960495685be6c301c63)\]. These findings highlight the computational challenges in verifying isolation properties, especially for weak models.

Efforts to improve the efficiency of isolation testing have led to the development of specialized tools. Viper, for example, is a fast and sound SI checker that leverages BC-polygraphs to represent transaction dependencies, enabling efficient verification \[[2](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/9f96a48d2bc0e77004b32960495685be6c301c63)\]. Similarly, Plume introduces a novel approach for checking weak isolation levels by combining vectors and tree clocks to accelerate the process \[[3](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/cbe191bda20907994d45bc835d20446968370add)\]. These tools are essential for validating the correctness of database implementations under weak isolation, particularly in large-scale systems.

Despite these advances, existing testers often suffer from high polynomial complexity, limiting their applicability to large workloads \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/1908.04509)\]. This limitation is addressed by AWDIT, a new tester that achieves significant improvements in efficiency. AWDIT tests RC, RA, and CC in time complexities of $O(n^(3/2))$, $O(n^(3/2))$, and $O(n \cdot k)$, respectively, which is a substantial improvement over the state of the art \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/1908.04509)\]. Furthermore, AWDIT is proven to be essentially optimal, with a conditional lower bound of $n^(3/2)$ for any weak isolation level between RC and CC \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/1908.04509)\].
