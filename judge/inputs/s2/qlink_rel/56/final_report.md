## Related Works

Cross-domain Sequential Recommendation (CDSR) has gained significant attention in recent years due to its ability to leverage user behavior across multiple domains to improve recommendation performance. Early approaches primarily focused on single-domain sequential recommendation, often relying on traditional models such as Markov Chains (MCs) and Recurrent Neural Networks (RNNs) to capture sequential patterns \[[27](http://arxiv.org/abs/1808.09781)\]. However, these methods struggled with the data sparsity issue and were limited in their ability to transfer knowledge across domains \[[5](http://arxiv.org/abs/2103.01696)\]. 

To address these limitations, several studies have explored cross-domain recommendation techniques. For instance, CoNet introduced a deep transfer learning approach using neural networks to learn complex user-item interaction relationships by connecting hidden layers across domains \[[4](http://arxiv.org/abs/1804.06769)\]. This approach enabled dual knowledge transfer across domains through cross connections, leading to significant improvements in recommendation performance \[[4](http://arxiv.org/abs/1804.06769)\]. Similarly, DDTCDR proposed a dual learning mechanism that iteratively transfers information between domains until the learning process stabilizes, improving cross-domain recommendation accuracy \[[6](http://arxiv.org/abs/1910.05189)\]. 

Another line of research focuses on graph-based models for cross-domain sequential recommendation. DA-GCN, for example, constructs a domain-aware graph convolution network to learn user-specific node representations and employs attention mechanisms to selectively guide message passing \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/2105.03300)\]. This approach effectively captures domain-specific preferences and improves recommendation accuracy by leveraging inter-domain relationships. 

In addition to graph-based models, contrastive learning has emerged as a promising technique for sequential recommendation. Contrastive Cross-Domain Sequential Recommendation (C2DSR) leverages intra- and inter-sequence item relationships to jointly learn single- and cross-domain user preferences \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2304.03891)\]. By maximizing mutual information between single- and cross-domain representations, C2DSR enhances the correlation between these representations, leading to more accurate recommendations. 

Furthermore, the integration of Large Language Models (LLMs) into recommendation systems has shown promise in capturing semantic relationships between items and users. BERT4Rec, for example, employs bidirectional self-attention to model user behavior sequences, allowing each item to fuse information from both left and right contexts \[[8](http://arxiv.org/abs/1904.06690)\]. This approach improves recommendation performance by capturing more nuanced user preferences.

[Snippet]: «Large Language Models (LLMs) have demonstrated remarkable performance across diverse domains, thereby prompting researchers to explore their potential for use in recommendation systems. Initial attempts have leveraged the exceptional capabilities of LLMs, such as rich knowledge and strong generalization through In-context Learning, which involves phrasing the recommendation task as prompts. Nevertheless, the performance of LLMs in recommendation tasks remains suboptimal due to a substantial disparity between the training tasks for LLMs and recommendation tasks, as well as inadequate recommendation data during pre-training. To bridge the gap, we consider building a Large Recommendation Language Model by tunning LLMs with recommendation data. To this end, we propose an efficient and effective Tuning framework for Aligning LLMs with Recommendations, namely TALLRec. We have demonstrated that the proposed TALLRec framework can significantly enhance the recommendation capabilities of LLMs in the movie and book domains, even with a limited dataset of fewer than 100 samples. Additionally, the proposed framework is highly efficient and can be executed on a single RTX 3090 with LLaMA-7B. Furthermore, the fine-tuned LLM exhibits robust cross-domain generalization. Our code and data are available at https://github.com/SAI990323/TALLRec.»
