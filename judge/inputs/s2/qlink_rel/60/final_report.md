## Related Works

Calibration of deep learning models has been extensively studied, with a focus on improving the reliability of predicted probabilities. Early work on neural network calibration highlighted that modern architectures, unlike older models, tend to be poorly calibrated, often overconfident in their predictions \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/1706.04599)\]. This issue is particularly problematic for applications where accurate uncertainty estimation is critical, such as safety-critical systems or decision-making under uncertainty \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/2002.09437)\]. Several post-hoc calibration techniques have been proposed to address this, including temperature scaling, which applies a single parameter to adjust the output logits \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/1706.04599)\]. While effective, temperature scaling is limited in its expressiveness, as it only allows for a single scalar adjustment \[[9](http://arxiv.org/abs/1910.12656)\].

More recently, researchers have explored more flexible calibration methods. Focal loss has been shown to produce well-calibrated models by modifying the loss function to reduce the impact of easy examples, thereby promoting more balanced confidence distributions \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/2002.09437)\]. Additionally, mixup training has been found to improve calibration by encouraging the model to learn more robust and less overconfident predictions \[[4](http://arxiv.org/abs/1905.11001)\]. This is attributed to the label smoothing inherent in mixup, which helps prevent overfitting and improves the model's ability to generalize \[[19](http://arxiv.org/abs/1906.02629)\].

Another approach, Bayesian Binning, offers a non-parametric calibration method that can be applied to binary classification models, allowing for more flexible and accurate recalibration \[[5](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/8d115c3b2ee80e0754360a154a9369bc1658b607)\]. However, it is computationally intensive and less scalable compared to parametric methods. In contrast, Dirichlet calibration has been proposed as a multiclass calibration method that generalizes the beta calibration approach, offering improved calibration performance across various models and datasets \[[9](http://arxiv.org/abs/1910.12656)\].

The need for flexible and expressive calibration methods has led to the development of neural network-based calibrators. For instance, the use of unconstrained monotonic neural networks (UMNNs) has been proposed to learn arbitrary monotonic functions, thereby improving flexibility and expressiveness while maintaining the necessary order-preserving properties \[[23](http://arxiv.org/abs/1902.02476)\]. This approach is particularly relevant to the current work, as it addresses the limitations of fixed-form calibration functions \[[23](http://arxiv.org/abs/1902.02476)\].

Furthermore, the importance of accurate calibration has been emphasized in the context of uncertainty estimation, where models must provide reliable uncertainty estimates for both aleatoric and epistemic uncertainties \[[21](http://arxiv.org/abs/1703.04977)\]. This has led to the development of methods such as Bayesian neural networks and deep ensembles, which offer more robust uncertainty estimates \[[6](http://arxiv.org/abs/1612.01474)\]. However, these methods often come with increased computational costs or require significant hyperparameter tuning \[[6](http://arxiv.org/abs/1612.01474)\].

Modern deep learning models often exhibit poor calibration, meaning their predicted probabilities do not accurately reflect the true likelihood of correct predictions \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/1706.04599)\]. This issue has been extensively studied, revealing that factors such as model depth, width, weight decay, and Batch Normalization significantly influence calibration \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/1706.04599)\]. Various post-processing methods have been proposed to recalibrate predictions, including temperature scaling, Platt scaling, and histogram binning \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/1706.04599)\]\[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/1904.01685)\]. While temperature scaling is simple and effective, it may not always achieve perfect calibration, and histogram binning, though more accurate, is sample inefficient \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/1904.01685)\]\[[8](http://arxiv.org/abs/1909.10155)\]. Recent work has introduced more sophisticated approaches, such as the scaling-binning calibrator, which combines parametric function fitting with binning to improve calibration while reducing sample requirements \[[8](http://arxiv.org/abs/1909.10155)\]. Additionally, methods like the spine-fitting approach use differentiable functions to approximate cumulative distributions, offering a binning-free alternative that outperforms existing methods in terms of calibration error \[[7](http://arxiv.org/abs/1906.02530)\]. Furthermore, researchers have explored the use of Bayesian methods and uncertainty quantification to improve calibration, proposing techniques that ensure accurate credible intervals while maintaining model performance \[[4](http://arxiv.org/abs/1905.11001)\]. These advancements highlight the ongoing efforts to develop more reliable and flexible calibration techniques for deep learning models.
