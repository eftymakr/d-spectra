## Related Works

The integration of knowledge graphs (KGs) with large language models (LLMs) to enhance reasoning capabilities has gained significant attention in recent years. While LLMs have demonstrated strong reasoning abilities, they often struggle with multi-step reasoning and leveraging structured knowledge from KGs without adaptation \[[16](http://arxiv.org/abs/2201.11903)\]. KGs, on the other hand, provide structured, semantically rich representations that are well-suited for reasoning tasks \[[13](http://arxiv.org/abs/2003.02320)\]. However, most existing claim verification methods rely on unstructured text corpora, limiting their ability to effectively leverage KGs \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2306.08302)\]. 

Several approaches have explored the synergy between LLMs and KGs. For instance, KEPLER proposes a unified model that integrates knowledge embedding with pre-trained language representation, allowing for better integration of factual knowledge into LLMs and producing effective text-enhanced KG embeddings \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/1911.06136)\]. Similarly, StructGPT introduces a general framework for LLMs to reason over structured data, using an iterative reading-then-reasoning approach to improve zero-shot reasoning performance \[[4](http://arxiv.org/abs/2305.09645)\]. 

Retrieval-augmented generation (RAG) has also been proposed as a method to enhance LLMs with external knowledge, combining pre-trained parametric and non-parametric memory for language generation \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/2005.11401)\]. This approach has been successfully applied to knowledge-intensive NLP tasks, such as open-domain question answering, where RAG models generate more specific, diverse, and factual language compared to parametric-only models \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/2005.11401)\]. 

The use of KGs for reasoning has also been explored in the context of commonsense reasoning. KagNet, for example, introduces a knowledge-aware graph network module that enables explainable inferences by grounding questions in external knowledge graphs \[[14](http://arxiv.org/abs/1909.02151)\]. This approach has achieved state-of-the-art performance on the CommonsenseQA dataset. 

In addition, QA-GNN proposes a novel model that addresses the challenges of reasoning over KGs and QA contexts by using relevance scoring and joint reasoning through graph-based message passing \[[5](http://arxiv.org/abs/2104.06378)\]. This model has been evaluated on the CommonsenseQA and OpenBookQA datasets, showing improvements over existing LM and LM+KG models.

This approach has been shown to significantly improve performance on arithmetic and symbolic reasoning tasks, achieving state-of-the-art results on benchmarks such as GSM8K.

The integration of large language models (LLMs) and knowledge graphs (KGs) has emerged as a critical area of research, aiming to enhance the reasoning capabilities of LLMs by leveraging the structured, factual knowledge stored in KGs \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2306.08302)\]. While LLMs excel in natural language understanding and generation, they often lack the ability to accurately reason with structured knowledge, leading to hallucinations and inaccuracies \[[4](http://arxiv.org/abs/2305.09645)\]. KGs, on the other hand, provide rich, semantically grounded representations that can improve the interpretability and factual accuracy of LLMs \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2306.08302)\]. Recent approaches have explored various frameworks to unify these two paradigms, including KG-enhanced LLMs, where KGs are incorporated during pre-training or inference to improve knowledge understanding \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2306.08302)\], and LLM-augmented KGs, where LLMs are used to enhance KG tasks such as embedding, completion, and question answering \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2306.08302)\]. Additionally, synergized models have been proposed, where LLMs and KGs work collaboratively to enable bidirectional reasoning \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2306.08302)\]. For instance, models like KEPLER integrate textual information with KGs to produce more accurate embeddings and improve performance on NLP tasks \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/1911.06136)\]. Similarly, QA-GNN introduces methods for joint reasoning over questions, answers, and KGs, using relevance scoring and graph-based message passing to enhance interpretability \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/2005.11401)\]. The RoG framework further advances this integration by using KGs to generate faithful reasoning paths, improving the reliability of LLMs in complex reasoning tasks \[[4](http://arxiv.org/abs/2305.09645)\]. These efforts highlight the growing interest in combining the strengths of LLMs and KGs to achieve more robust and interpretable reasoning systems \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2306.08302)\]\[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/1911.06136)\]\[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/2005.11401)\]\[[4](http://arxiv.org/abs/2305.09645)\].

### <a id='category-retrieval-augmented-generation'></a>Retrieval-Augmented Generation: 3 papers found.
Retrieval-Augmented Generation (RAG) has emerged as a critical approach to enhance the knowledge-intensive capabilities of large language models (LLMs), addressing limitations in their ability to access, manipulate, and update world knowledge \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/2005.11401)\]. By integrating non-parametric memory, such as dense vector indexes of external databases like Wikipedia, RAG models can dynamically retrieve relevant information during generation, improving factual accuracy and reducing hallucinations \[[11](http://arxiv.org/abs/2312.10997)\]. This approach allows LLMs to combine their inherent knowledge with up-to-date, structured data, making them more effective for open-domain question-answering and other tasks requiring precise information retrieval \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/2005.11401)\]. Research has shown that RAG models generate more specific, diverse, and factual responses compared to parametric-only models, particularly in scenarios involving long-tail entities or less common factual knowledge \[[15](http://arxiv.org/abs/2212.10511)\]. Furthermore, advancements in RAG frameworks have led to the development of modular architectures that separate retrieval, generation, and augmentation components, enabling more flexible and efficient knowledge integration \[[11](http://arxiv.org/abs/2312.10997)\]. These innovations highlight the growing importance of RAG in bridging the gap between LLMs and structured knowledge sources, offering a promising direction for improving reasoning and generalization across diverse NLP tasks.

### <a id='category-kgs-for-structured-reasoning'></a>KGs for Structured Reasoning: 4 papers found.
Knowledge graphs (KGs) have emerged as a critical tool for structured reasoning, enabling more effective reasoning over complex, relational data \[[13](http://arxiv.org/abs/2003.02320)\]. KGs are represented as multi-relational graphs, where entities are nodes and relations are typed edges, allowing for rich semantic representations that support advanced reasoning tasks \[[18](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/0c06bd20403a204bcd95dae1f176c10894fe7139)\]. This structured format is particularly beneficial for tasks like question answering, where multi-hop reasoning over multiple edges is required to arrive at accurate answers \[[18](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/0c06bd20403a204bcd95dae1f176c10894fe7139)\]. However, KGs often suffer from sparsity, with many missing links, which complicates reasoning, especially in multi-hop scenarios \[[18](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/0c06bd20403a204bcd95dae1f176c10894fe7139)\]. To address this, KG embedding methods have been proposed to predict missing links, improving the completeness of the graph \[[18](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/0c06bd20403a204bcd95dae1f176c10894fe7139)\]. Additionally, KGs have been widely applied across various domains, driving advancements in knowledge representation and reasoning for both human and machine \[[13](http://arxiv.org/abs/2003.02320)\]. Despite these benefits, challenges remain in defining and constructing domain-specific KGs, with current approaches often lacking consistency and completeness \[[19](http://arxiv.org/abs/2011.00235)\]. Recent research has also explored the integration of KGs with large language models (LLMs) to enhance reasoning capabilities, such as in claim verification, where structured knowledge from KGs can complement the unstructured text-based approaches typically used \[[13](http://arxiv.org/abs/2003.02320)\]. This integration has led to the development of frameworks like ClaimPKG, which combines LLMs with KGs to improve reasoning performance and generalizability \[[13](http://arxiv.org/abs/2003.02320)\]. Overall, the field continues to evolve, with ongoing efforts to refine KG representation, improve reasoning over sparse data, and expand the applicability of KGs across diverse domains \[[13](http://arxiv.org/abs/2003.02320)\]\[[18](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/0c06bd20403a204bcd95dae1f176c10894fe7139)\]\[[19](http://arxiv.org/abs/2011.00235)\].
