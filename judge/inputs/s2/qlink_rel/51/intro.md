## Related Works

Federated unlearning has emerged as a critical research area in the context of privacy-preserving machine learning, particularly in response to the "right to be forgotten" (RTBF) legislation \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2203.07320)\]. This concept enables users to remove their data contributions from a global model trained across decentralized data sources, ensuring compliance with privacy regulations \[[2](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/eadeffdec9fac8fd7f9aea732ca410eb082b7dcf)\]. The development of unlearning methods has been driven by the need to address both the technical challenges of removing data influence and the ethical implications of data privacy \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/2207.05521)\].

Several approaches have been proposed to enable efficient federated unlearning. One notable method is FedEraser, which leverages the historical parameter updates stored at the central server to reconstruct an unlearned model without requiring full retraining \[[4](http://arxiv.org/abs/2201.09441)\]. This approach trades off server storage for faster unlearning times, significantly reducing the computational overhead \[[5](http://arxiv.org/abs/2310.20448)\]. Similarly, the work in \[[6](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/39da2d48f2132b919405e3bb6df6ece44ac6e15a)\] introduces a method that removes a client's contribution by subtracting historical updates from the global model and using knowledge distillation to maintain performance without accessing client data. This method is particularly effective for neural networks and does not rely on client participation \[[7](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/06edb1f6eadb6c141d720fcca4c854578dc5d670)\].

Another line of research focuses on the challenges of verifying unlearning effects, especially in distributed settings where the influence of one client's data can be canceled by contributions from others \[[8](http://arxiv.org/abs/2209.02299)\]. The VeriFi framework addresses this by introducing verifiable federated unlearning, allowing clients to independently verify the unlearning effect through marking and checking mechanisms \[[9](http://arxiv.org/abs/2205.12709)\]. This ensures transparency and accountability in the unlearning process \[[10](http://arxiv.org/abs/2210.10958)\].

The problem of unlearning in federated learning is further complicated by the need to balance efficiency with accuracy. For instance, the work in \[[11](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/a52c3ec7325e81bd87fdf8d74bf9acfd1e1ffe5f)\] proposes a method that uses Bayesian inference and parameter self-sharing to achieve efficient unlearning while maintaining model performance. This approach is particularly useful in scenarios where the server cannot access raw client data \[[12](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/efab3b30d6f67f55c68f9dc7c65b2b54b1789030)\]. Additionally, the study in \[[13](http://arxiv.org/abs/2110.11794)\] introduces a class-discriminative pruning method that allows for selective forgetting of categories without requiring full retraining, offering a significant speedup compared to traditional methods \[[14](http://arxiv.org/abs/2401.11018)\].

Efforts to improve the efficiency and accuracy of federated unlearning have also included the development of communication-efficient algorithms. The FATS framework, for example, introduces total variation (TV) stability to ensure exact unlearning while minimizing communication costs \[[15](http://arxiv.org/abs/2401.05146)\]. This framework guarantees that the unlearned model is statistically indistinguishable from the one trained without the deleted data, providing strong theoretical guarantees \[[16](http://arxiv.org/abs/2404.09724)\].
