## Related Works

Instruction-following text embedding is a critical area of research in natural language processing (NLP), where the goal is to generate dynamic text embeddings that adapt to user instructions, highlighting specific attributes of text \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/1910.10683)\]. This task has gained significant attention due to its applicability in various downstream tasks such as question answering, text classification, and semantic similarity \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/1910.10683)\]. However, existing approaches often suffer from significant computational overhead, as they require re-encoding the entire corpus for each new instruction \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/1910.10683)\].

Several works have explored the use of pre-trained models for instruction-following tasks. For instance, BERT \[[5](http://arxiv.org/abs/1810.04805)\] introduced a new language representation model that is capable of fine-tuning with just one additional output layer to create state-of-the-art models for a wide range of tasks. Similarly, the Transformer architecture \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/1706.03762)\] has been shown to outperform recurrent and convolutional neural networks in sequence transduction tasks, offering better parallelizability and faster training times.

Instruction tuning has also been a focus of recent research. InstructGPT \[[8](http://arxiv.org/abs/2203.02155)\] demonstrated that fine-tuning large language models with human feedback can significantly improve their ability to follow instructions. This approach was further refined with the introduction of Self-Instruct \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/2212.10560)\], which generates instruction data from the model's own outputs, reducing the need for human annotation. Similarly, the Unnatural Instructions dataset \[[12](http://arxiv.org/abs/2212.09689)\] was created by prompting a language model to generate diverse and creative instructions, which were then used to train models without human supervision.

The importance of instruction-aware embeddings has also been highlighted in the development of INSTRUCTOR \[[9](http://arxiv.org/abs/2212.09741)\], a method that generates text embeddings tailored to different downstream tasks by incorporating task instructions. This approach allows for a single embedder to be used across various tasks, improving efficiency and performance.

Recent advancements in contrastive learning have also contributed to the field. SimCSE \[[20](http://arxiv.org/abs/2104.08821)\] introduced a simple contrastive learning framework that significantly improved sentence embeddings, achieving state-of-the-art results on semantic textual similarity tasks. Similarly, E5 \[[14](http://arxiv.org/abs/2212.03533)\] demonstrated that contrastive pre-training with weak supervision signals can produce high-quality text embeddings that transfer well to a wide range of tasks.

The challenge of aligning models with user intent has also been addressed in the context of vision-and-language navigation. For example, the Speaker-Follower Models \[[13](http://arxiv.org/abs/1806.02724)\] approach synthesizes new instructions for data augmentation and implements pragmatic reasoning to evaluate how well candidate action sequences explain an instruction. This has led to significant improvements in navigation performance.
