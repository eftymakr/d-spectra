## Related Works

Deep learning has significantly impacted the field of inverse problems in imaging, with two prominent approaches being deep-unrolling and plug-and-play (PnP) methods \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/1704.03488)\]. PnP approaches are iterative algorithms where regularization is implicitly performed by an off-the-shelf deep denoiser \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/1912.10557)\]. These methods are flexible for varying compression ratios (CRs) but are limited in reconstruction accuracy and speed \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/1611.03679)\]. On the other hand, unrolling approaches transform a truncated iterative optimization process into an end-to-end trainable network \[[4](http://arxiv.org/abs/1704.03264)\]. These networks typically achieve better accuracy and faster inference but require fine-tuning or retraining when CR changes \[[5](http://arxiv.org/abs/1801.06756)\].

The integration of deep learning with traditional optimization methods has led to the development of algorithm unrolling techniques, which connect iterative algorithms used in signal processing with deep neural networks \[[6](http://arxiv.org/abs/2003.10428)\]. This approach allows for the creation of efficient and interpretable networks that can be trained on reasonably sized datasets \[[7](http://arxiv.org/abs/1712.02862)\]. The idea of unrolling has been applied to various problems, including image deblurring and super-resolution \[[8](http://arxiv.org/abs/1905.05406)\]. For instance, the Deep Unfolding for Blind Deblurring (DUBLID) network was proposed to connect an iterative algorithm with a neural network, achieving significant performance gains while maintaining interpretability \[[9](http://arxiv.org/abs/1508.02848)\].

The use of deep neural networks for image restoration has also been explored in the context of denoising priors. Learning deep CNN denoisers and integrating them into model-based optimization methods has shown promising results \[[10](http://arxiv.org/abs/1809.04693)\]. These denoisers can be used as priors to solve various inverse problems, such as deblurring and super-resolution \[[11](http://arxiv.org/abs/2008.13751)\]. The success of these methods is attributed to the ability of deep networks to learn complex image structures and patterns \[[12](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/b954a94af132294a4a4e2efce15027758a535742)\].

Another approach to integrating deep learning with optimization methods is the use of proximal operators. The Learning Proximal Operators paper proposed replacing the proximal operator of the regularization used in convex energy minimization algorithms with a denoising neural network \[[13](http://arxiv.org/abs/1611.02862)\]. This allows for the use of a fixed denoising neural network in various inverse problems, reducing the need for problem-specific training \[[14](http://arxiv.org/abs/1902.05399)\]. Similarly, the Learned Primal-Dual algorithm for tomographic reconstruction unrolls a proximal primal-dual optimization method, replacing proximal operators with convolutional neural networks \[[15](http://arxiv.org/abs/1608.03981)\].

### <a id='category-unrolling-approaches-and-their-advantages'></a>Unrolling Approaches and Their Advantages: 10 papers found.
The category "Unrolling Approaches and Their Advantages" explores the integration of iterative algorithms with deep neural networks to enhance performance, interpretability, and efficiency in solving inverse problems. Unrolling methods, which transform iterative optimization processes into trainable neural network layers, have gained significant traction due to their ability to combine the strengths of model-based and data-driven approaches \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/1611.03679)\]. These methods allow for the incorporation of physical models into the network architecture, leading to improved performance and reduced reliance on large training datasets \[[5](http://arxiv.org/abs/1801.06756)\]. For instance, unrolling iterative algorithms such as total-variation regularization or half-quadratic splitting enables the creation of deep networks that maintain the interpretability of traditional methods while benefiting from the flexibility and power of deep learning \[[6](http://arxiv.org/abs/2003.10428)\]\[[7](http://arxiv.org/abs/1712.02862)\]. Furthermore, unrolled networks can be trained end-to-end, allowing for joint optimization of both the network parameters and the iterative steps, which enhances their adaptability to different problem settings \[[5](http://arxiv.org/abs/1801.06756)\]\[[8](http://arxiv.org/abs/1905.05406)\]. This approach has been successfully applied to tasks such as image deblurring, super-resolution, and tomographic reconstruction, where unrolled networks demonstrate superior performance compared to purely data-driven or model-based methods \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/1611.03679)\]\[[6](http://arxiv.org/abs/2003.10428)\]\[[9](http://arxiv.org/abs/1508.02848)\]. Additionally, unrolling techniques have been shown to reduce computational costs and improve convergence rates, particularly when dealing with constrained environments such as limited GPU memory \[[5](http://arxiv.org/abs/1801.06756)\]\[[10](http://arxiv.org/abs/1809.04693)\]. Overall, unrolling provides a principled framework for bridging the gap between traditional iterative algorithms and deep learning, offering a versatile and effective solution for a wide range of inverse problems \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/1611.03679)\]\[[5](http://arxiv.org/abs/1801.06756)\]\[[6](http://arxiv.org/abs/2003.10428)\].

Recent works have demonstrated that denoisers trained via deep CNNs can serve as powerful priors for solving various inverse problems, enabling a plug-and-play integration into iterative algorithms like half quadratic splitting (HQS) or ADMM \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/1611.03679)\]. [Snippet]: «Alternating direction method of multiplier (ADMM) is a widely used algorithm for solving constrained optimization problems in image restoration. Among many useful features, one critical feature of the ADMM algorithm is its modular structure, which allows one to plug in any off-the-shelf image denoising algorithm for a subproblem in the ADMM algorithm. Because of the plug-in nature, this type of ADMM algorithms is coined the name “Plug-and-Play ADMM.” Plug-and-Play ADMM has demonstrated promising empirical results in a number of recent papers. However, it is unclear under what conditions and by using what denoising algorithms would it guarantee convergence. Also, since Plug-and-Play ADMM uses a specific way to split the variables, it is unclear if fast implementation can be made for common Gaussian and Poissonian image restoration problems. In this paper, we propose a Plug-and-Play ADMM algorithm with provable fixed-point convergence. We show that for any denoising algorithm satisfying an asymptotic criteria, called bounded denoisers, Plug-and-Play ADMM converges to a fixed point under a continuation scheme. We also present fast implementations for two image restoration problems on superresolution and single-photon imaging. We compare Plug-and-Play ADMM with state-of-the-art algorithms in each problem type and demonstrate promising experimental results of the algorithm.»
