## Related Works

Neural rendering has emerged as a powerful paradigm for reconstructing 3D scenes from 2D images, leveraging deep learning to model both geometry and appearance \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/1708.01692)\]. The core idea involves taking multiple views as input and optimizing the reconstructed scene by minimizing the uncertainty in geometry and appearance across these views \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/1708.01692)\]. However, the quality of reconstruction is often limited by the number of input views, especially in complex and dynamic scenes where certain angles of objects are never captured \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/1708.01692)\]. To address this limitation, researchers have explored various data augmentation techniques, including video frame interpolation, to generate additional views and improve the robustness of neural rendering models \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/1708.01692)\].

Video frame interpolation has been extensively studied, with several approaches focusing on combining motion estimation and pixel synthesis into a single process \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/1712.00080)\]. For example, the method proposed in \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/1703.07514)\] formulates frame interpolation as a local separable convolution over input frames, using pairs of 1D kernels to reduce the number of parameters and improve computational efficiency. Similarly, \[[4](http://arxiv.org/abs/2106.10689)\] introduces a deep fully convolutional neural network that estimates spatially adaptive convolution kernels for each pixel, allowing for high-quality frame synthesis while avoiding the need for explicit optical flow estimation. These methods demonstrate that integrating motion and synthesis into a single network can lead to more efficient and effective video interpolation \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/1703.07514)\]\[[4](http://arxiv.org/abs/2106.10689)\].

Another line of work focuses on incorporating depth information to improve the accuracy of frame interpolation, particularly in the presence of occlusions and large motion \[[5](http://arxiv.org/abs/1603.06041)\]. For instance, \[[6](http://arxiv.org/abs/2007.11571)\] proposes a depth-aware flow projection layer that prioritizes the synthesis of intermediate flows for closer objects, leading to more accurate and visually pleasing results. This approach highlights the importance of leveraging geometric cues to enhance the quality of interpolated frames \[[6](http://arxiv.org/abs/2007.11571)\].

In addition to improving interpolation quality, researchers have also explored the use of unsupervised learning to train frame interpolation models without requiring ground truth intermediate frames \[[7](http://arxiv.org/abs/1904.00830)\]. For example, \[[8](http://arxiv.org/abs/1912.07372)\] introduces a cycle consistency-based approach that optimizes models to minimize the discrepancy between the center frame and its cycle reconstruction, achieving results comparable to supervised methods \[[8](http://arxiv.org/abs/1912.07372)\]. This work demonstrates that unsupervised techniques can be effective for video interpolation, especially when high-quality training data is scarce \[[8](http://arxiv.org/abs/1912.07372)\].
