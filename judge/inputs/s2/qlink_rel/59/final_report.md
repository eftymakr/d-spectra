## Related Works

Fairness in ranking and recommendation systems has been a growing area of research, with multiple studies addressing the trade-off between accuracy and fairness. Early work on fairness in rankings, such as \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/1805.01788)\], highlighted the inherent position bias in rankings, where lower-ranked items receive disproportionately less attention. This bias can lead to unfair distribution of opportunities and resources. To address this, \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/1805.01788)\] proposed measures and mechanisms to quantify and mitigate unfairness at the individual level, rather than just group-level fairness. This approach is more comprehensive and subsumes group fairness.

The concept of fairness in rankings has also been extended to consider exposure allocation, as discussed in \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/1802.07281)\]. This work introduced a framework for fairness constraints in rankings, focusing on exposure distribution. It demonstrated how fairness goals can be implemented using various constraints, including demographic parity, disparate treatment, and disparate impact. The framework allows for the maximization of user utility while satisfying fairness constraints, which is a key consideration in fair ranking systems.

In the context of recommendation systems, \[[4](http://arxiv.org/abs/2002.10764)\] introduced the idea of two-sided fairness, considering both customer and producer fairness. This work showed that a customer-centric design may lead to unfair exposure distribution for producers, while a producer-centric design might negatively affect customer satisfaction. The paper proposed a novel approach to fairly allocate exposure, ensuring at least Maximin Share (MMS) for producers and Envy-Free up to One Good (EF1) fairness for customers. This approach was validated through extensive experiments on real-world datasets.

Another significant contribution to fair recommendation is \[[6](http://arxiv.org/abs/1903.00780)\], which proposed a method for evaluating fairness in recommender systems based on pairwise comparisons. This approach allows for a tractable means to reason about fairness in rankings and introduces a regularizer to improve fairness during model training. The method was applied to a large-scale production recommender system, showing significant improvements in pairwise fairness.

Fairness-aware re-ranking has also been explored in \[[7](http://arxiv.org/abs/1809.02921)\], where the authors proposed a re-ranking algorithm that balances ranking quality and provider-side fairness. The algorithm incorporates personalized user tolerance towards provider diversification, ensuring that fairness is considered without overly sacrificing accuracy. This work demonstrated that fairness can be improved with only a slight loss in accuracy, while being attentive to individual user differences.
