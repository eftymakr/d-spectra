## Related Works

Label noise is a pervasive issue in deep learning, particularly in large-scale datasets where manual annotation is impractical. Several approaches have been proposed to address this challenge. Early methods focused on modifying network architectures or estimating true labels from noisy ones \[[8](http://arxiv.org/abs/1712.09482)\]. For instance, \[[8](http://arxiv.org/abs/1712.09482)\] introduced loss functions that are inherently robust to label noise, such as mean absolute error (MAE), which allows standard back propagation to learn the true classifier even under label noise. This approach has been further generalized to handle multiclass classification problems \[[11](http://arxiv.org/abs/1805.07836)\]. 

Another line of research focuses on modifying the training process to mitigate the impact of noisy labels. \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/1412.6596)\] proposed a method that enhances the prediction objective with a notion of consistency, ensuring that similar inputs yield consistent predictions. This approach has been shown to be robust to label corruption on datasets such as MNIST and Toronto Face Database. Similarly, \[[7](http://arxiv.org/abs/1806.02612)\] introduced a dimensionality-driven learning strategy that monitors the dimensionality of subspaces during training and adapts the loss function accordingly, leading to improved tolerance to noisy labels.

More recently, semi-supervised learning techniques have been leveraged to address label noise. \[[5](http://arxiv.org/abs/2002.07394)\] proposed DivideMix, a framework that models the per-sample loss distribution with a mixture model to dynamically divide the training data into labeled and unlabeled sets. This allows for semi-supervised training that improves robustness to noisy labels. \[[14](http://arxiv.org/abs/1901.04215)\] extended this idea by introducing Co-teaching+, a method that bridges the "Update by Disagreement" strategy with the original Co-teaching approach, improving robustness by leveraging disagreement between two networks during training.

In addition to semi-supervised methods, loss correction approaches have been proposed to handle class-dependent label noise. \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/1609.03683)\] introduced a theoretically grounded approach that corrects the loss function by estimating the probability of each class being corrupted into another. This method has been shown to be effective across various architectures and datasets, including MNIST, CIFAR-10, and clothing images. \[[11](http://arxiv.org/abs/1805.07836)\] further generalized this idea by proposing a set of noise-robust loss functions that can be applied to any DNN architecture, achieving good performance in a wide range of noisy label scenarios.

Another approach focuses on preventing the memorization of noisy labels during training. \[[9](http://arxiv.org/abs/2007.00151)\] introduced early-learning regularization, which exploits the early learning phase to prevent memorization of false labels. This approach leverages semi-supervised learning techniques to produce target probabilities and uses a regularization term to steer the model toward these targets, thereby improving robustness to noisy annotations.

[Snippet]: «Deep learning with noisy labels is practically challenging, as the capacity of deep models is so high that they can totally memorize these noisy labels sooner or later during training. Nonetheless, recent studies on the memorization effects of deep neural networks show that they would first memorize training data of clean labels and then those of noisy labels. Therefore in this paper, we propose a new deep learning paradigm called Co-teaching for combating with noisy labels. Namely, we train two deep neural networks simultaneously, and let them teach each other given every mini-batch: firstly, each network feeds forward all data and selects some data of possibly clean labels; secondly, two networks communicate with each other what data in this mini-batch should be used for training; finally, each network back propagates the data selected by its peer network and updates itself. Empirical results on noisy versions of MNIST, CIFAR-10 and CIFAR-100 demonstrate that Co-teaching is much superior to the state-of-the-art methods in the robustness of trained deep models.»

### <a id='category-robust-loss-functions'></a>Robust Loss Functions: 2 papers found.
In the context of robust loss functions for deep learning under label noise, recent research has focused on developing loss functions that are inherently tolerant to noisy labels, rather than relying on complex architectures or label correction techniques \[[8](http://arxiv.org/abs/1712.09482)\]. For binary classification, theoretical results have established conditions for loss functions that ensure robustness to label noise, and these have been extended to multiclass settings \[[8](http://arxiv.org/abs/1712.09482)\]. The mean absolute error (MAE) loss has been shown to be inherently robust, allowing standard backpropagation to learn the true classifier even with noisy labels \[[8](http://arxiv.org/abs/1712.09482)\]. However, MAE has also been found to perform poorly in certain scenarios, particularly with deep neural networks and challenging datasets, prompting the development of more generalized noise-robust loss functions that extend both MAE and categorical cross-entropy (CCE) \[[11](http://arxiv.org/abs/1805.07836)\]. These new loss functions are designed to be compatible with existing DNN architectures while maintaining performance across a range of noisy label conditions \[[11](http://arxiv.org/abs/1805.07836)\].

[Snippet]: «We propose a novel framework to perform classification via deep learning in the presence of noisy annotations. When trained on noisy labels, deep neural networks have been observed to first fit the training data with clean labels during an "early learning" phase, before eventually memorizing the examples with false labels. We prove that early learning and memorization are fundamental phenomena in high-dimensional classification tasks, even in simple linear models, and give a theoretical explanation in this setting. Motivated by these findings, we develop a new technique for noisy classification tasks, which exploits the progress of the early learning phase. In contrast with existing approaches, which use the model output during early learning to detect the examples with clean labels, and either ignore or attempt to correct the false labels, we take a different route and instead capitalize on early learning via regularization. There are two key elements to our approach. First, we leverage semi-supervised learning techniques to produce target probabilities based on the model outputs. Second, we design a regularization term that steers the model towards these targets, implicitly preventing memorization of the false labels. The resulting framework is shown to provide robustness to noisy annotations on several standard benchmarks and real-world datasets, where it achieves results comparable to the state of the art.»
