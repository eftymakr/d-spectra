## Related Works

Label noise is a pervasive issue in deep learning, particularly in large-scale datasets where manual annotation is impractical. Several approaches have been proposed to address this challenge. Early methods focused on modifying network architectures or estimating true labels from noisy ones \[[8](http://arxiv.org/abs/1712.09482)\]. For instance, \[[8](http://arxiv.org/abs/1712.09482)\] introduced loss functions that are inherently robust to label noise, such as mean absolute error (MAE), which allows standard back propagation to learn the true classifier even under label noise. This approach has been further generalized to handle multiclass classification problems \[[11](http://arxiv.org/abs/1805.07836)\]. 

Another line of research focuses on modifying the training process to mitigate the impact of noisy labels. \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/1412.6596)\] proposed a method that enhances the prediction objective with a notion of consistency, ensuring that similar inputs yield consistent predictions. This approach has been shown to be robust to label corruption on datasets such as MNIST and Toronto Face Database. Similarly, \[[7](http://arxiv.org/abs/1806.02612)\] introduced a dimensionality-driven learning strategy that monitors the dimensionality of subspaces during training and adapts the loss function accordingly, leading to improved tolerance to noisy labels.

More recently, semi-supervised learning techniques have been leveraged to address label noise. \[[5](http://arxiv.org/abs/2002.07394)\] proposed DivideMix, a framework that models the per-sample loss distribution with a mixture model to dynamically divide the training data into labeled and unlabeled sets. This allows for semi-supervised training that improves robustness to noisy labels. \[[14](http://arxiv.org/abs/1901.04215)\] extended this idea by introducing Co-teaching+, a method that bridges the "Update by Disagreement" strategy with the original Co-teaching approach, improving robustness by leveraging disagreement between two networks during training.

In addition to semi-supervised methods, loss correction approaches have been proposed to handle class-dependent label noise. \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/1609.03683)\] introduced a theoretically grounded approach that corrects the loss function by estimating the probability of each class being corrupted into another. This method has been shown to be effective across various architectures and datasets, including MNIST, CIFAR-10, and clothing images. \[[11](http://arxiv.org/abs/1805.07836)\] further generalized this idea by proposing a set of noise-robust loss functions that can be applied to any DNN architecture, achieving good performance in a wide range of noisy label scenarios.

Another approach focuses on preventing the memorization of noisy labels during training. \[[9](http://arxiv.org/abs/2007.00151)\] introduced early-learning regularization, which exploits the early learning phase to prevent memorization of false labels. This approach leverages semi-supervised learning techniques to produce target probabilities and uses a regularization term to steer the model toward these targets, thereby improving robustness to noisy annotations.
