## Related Works

Taxation policy design has been extensively studied, with a focus on both theoretical models and empirical implementations. Traditional approaches, such as the U.S. federal income tax, aim to reduce economic inequality but often lack adaptability to dynamic economic conditions \[[1](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/12dba28ded03c013923430994d1aac347c647c8d)\]. More sophisticated models, like the Saez Optimal Taxation framework, attempt to dynamically adjust tax rates based on economic indicators, yet they struggle to account for taxpayer heterogeneity and irrational behavior \[[1](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/12dba28ded03c013923430994d1aac347c647c8d)\]. This limitation highlights the need for more flexible and adaptive taxation models that can better reflect real-world complexities.

Agent-based modeling (ABM) has emerged as a promising approach to simulate and analyze economic systems, including taxation. ABMs allow for the representation of heterogeneous agents and their interactions, which is critical for modeling taxpayer behavior \[[7](http://arxiv.org/abs/1101.1847)\]. For instance, studies have explored the use of ABMs to simulate tax evasion dynamics, incorporating enforcement mechanisms such as audits to curb evasion \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/0805.0998)\]. These models demonstrate that even minimal audit rates can significantly reduce tax evasion, suggesting that policy interventions can have a measurable impact on taxpayer behavior \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/0805.0998)\]. Similarly, the Ising model has been used to analyze tax evasion dynamics, showing that punishment-based enforcement mechanisms can effectively control evasion \[[4](http://arxiv.org/abs/0801.2980)\].

Recent advances in large language models (LLMs) have also influenced the development of more sophisticated policy-making tools. LLMs are capable of generating reasoning traces and task-specific actions, which can be leveraged to improve decision-making in complex environments \[[6](http://arxiv.org/abs/2210.03629)\]. The ReAct framework, which synergizes reasoning and acting in LLMs, has been applied to various decision-making tasks, demonstrating improved performance over traditional methods \[[6](http://arxiv.org/abs/2210.03629)\]. Furthermore, reinforcement learning (RL) has been proposed as a method to enhance the behavioral models of agents in ABMs, allowing for more realistic and adaptive decision-making \[[9](http://arxiv.org/abs/2006.05048)\]. RL-based models have been shown to outperform traditional behavioral models in policy simulations, particularly in scenarios involving behavioral heterogeneity and population dynamics \[[9](http://arxiv.org/abs/2006.05048)\].

In the context of public health, agent-based modeling has been applied to simulate disease transmission and health behaviors, highlighting the versatility of ABMs in modeling complex systems \[[10](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/ebc3c689a69f536d0b089d00cb19c8e8cce0f648)\]. Similarly, opinion dynamics models have been analyzed using Markov chains, providing a rigorous framework for understanding the evolution of opinions and behaviors in social systems \[[11](http://arxiv.org/abs/1108.1716)\]. These studies underscore the broader applicability of ABMs and their potential for integration with advanced AI techniques such as LLMs.
