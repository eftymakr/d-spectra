## Related Works

Diffusion models have become a cornerstone in generative modeling, particularly for image and video synthesis, due to their ability to produce high-quality, diverse outputs \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2112.10752)\]. However, the computational and memory demands of these models, especially diffusion transformers (DiTs), pose significant challenges for deployment on edge devices \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/2204.03458)\]. Early works on diffusion models, such as \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/2304.08818)\], demonstrated the effectiveness of latent diffusion models (LDMs) in reducing computational complexity by operating in a compressed latent space. This approach has been extended to video generation, where models like MagicVideo \[[16](http://arxiv.org/abs/2211.11018)\] and Video LDM \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/2304.08818)\] leverage pre-trained variational autoencoders (VAEs) to map video data into a lower-dimensional latent space, enabling efficient training and inference.

Despite these advances, quantization of diffusion models remains a non-trivial task. Post-training quantization (PTQ) has been explored for image generation models, such as in \[[17](http://arxiv.org/abs/2405.16005)\], where the authors propose PTQ4DiT to address the unique challenges of DiTs. However, existing quantization methods often fail to generalize to video generation due to the temporal dynamics of video data and the complexity of spatiotemporal correlations \[[18](http://arxiv.org/abs/1712.05877)\]. This issue is further compounded by the need to maintain high fidelity in both spatial and temporal dimensions \[[19](http://arxiv.org/abs/2206.01861)\].

Recent works have attempted to address these challenges. For instance, Q-DiT \[[20](http://arxiv.org/abs/2406.17343)\] introduces a novel quantization framework that handles the spatial and temporal variance in DiTs by employing automatic quantization granularity allocation and sample-wise dynamic activation quantization. Similarly, PTQ4ViT \[[11](http://arxiv.org/abs/2111.12293)\] focuses on vision transformers, proposing twin uniform quantization and a Hessian-guided metric to reduce quantization error. These methods, while effective for image-based models, have not been specifically tailored for video DiTs, which require handling both spatial and temporal information simultaneously.

These works collectively highlight the potential of latent space approaches in enabling high-quality, efficient, and versatile video generation, addressing challenges such as computational complexity and temporal consistency.

### <a id='category-post-training-quantization-for-diffusion-models'></a>Post-Training Quantization for Diffusion Models: 7 papers found.
Post-training quantization (PTQ) has emerged as a critical technique for accelerating diffusion models while maintaining their generative performance, particularly for deployment on edge devices and in low-latency applications \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2112.10752)\]. However, traditional PTQ methods face significant challenges when applied to diffusion models due to the temporal variation in activation distributions across multiple time steps and the unique architecture of diffusion transformers (DiTs) \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/2204.03458)\]. For instance, quantization noise can accumulate over denoising steps, leading to a degradation in output quality and a mismatch with the variance schedule \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/2304.08818)\]. To address these issues, researchers have proposed specialized PTQ methods tailored to diffusion models, such as PTQ4DM, which introduces timestep-aware calibration and split shortcut quantization to maintain performance during quantization \[[4](http://arxiv.org/abs/2211.15736)\]. Similarly, PTQ4DiT tackles the challenges of DiTs by employing channel-wise salience balancing and spearmen's ρ-guided salience calibration to handle extreme channel magnitudes and temporal activation variations \[[5](http://arxiv.org/abs/2302.04304)\]. Additionally, Q-DiT introduces automatic quantization granularity allocation and sample-wise dynamic activation quantization to adapt to the spatial and temporal variance in DiTs \[[6](http://arxiv.org/abs/2212.09748)\]. These approaches highlight the need for PTQ methods that are specifically designed for the unique characteristics of diffusion models, ensuring that quantization does not compromise the quality of generated outputs \[[7](http://arxiv.org/abs/2305.10657)\].

### <a id='category-quantization-error-mitigation-techniques'></a>Quantization Error Mitigation Techniques: 2 papers found.
Quantization error mitigation techniques have been extensively studied to improve the accuracy of quantized neural networks, particularly in vision transformers where previous post-training quantization methods led to significant accuracy drops \[[11](http://arxiv.org/abs/2111.12293)\]. These methods often rely on addressing the mismatch between activation distributions and Gaussian assumptions, as well as improving the selection of scaling factors through more accurate metrics \[[11](http://arxiv.org/abs/2111.12293)\]. For instance, the twin uniform quantization method was proposed to better handle the non-Gaussian activation distributions in vision transformers, leading to near-lossless prediction accuracy at 8-bit quantization \[[11](http://arxiv.org/abs/2111.12293)\]. In addition, hardware-driven approaches have been developed to enable efficient quantization, such as PTQ4ViT, which provides a fast and effective framework for vision transformers \[[11](http://arxiv.org/abs/2111.12293)\]. Other studies have explored post-training quantization (PTQ) as a lightweight approach that requires no re-training, achieving close-to-floating-point accuracy for 8-bit quantization \[[13](http://arxiv.org/abs/2106.08295)\]. Meanwhile, quantization-aware training (QAT) has also been considered, allowing for lower bit quantization with competitive results when labeled data is available \[[13](http://arxiv.org/abs/2106.08295)\]. These techniques highlight the ongoing efforts to balance computational efficiency with model accuracy in quantization processes.
