## Related Works

De novo genome assembly has been a focal point of bioinformatics research, driven by the advent of next-generation sequencing technologies that generate massive volumes of short reads \[[1](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/7279acd9c61a8de019ea2d55d23124dfbb6b5269)\]. Early approaches, such as the overlap-layout-consensus (OLC) method, relied on pairwise alignment and consensus building, but these methods struggled with the computational demands of large datasets \[[1](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/7279acd9c61a8de019ea2d55d23124dfbb6b5269)\]. In contrast, de Bruijn graph-based assemblers, such as SOAPdenovo, have become more prevalent due to their scalability and efficiency in handling large short-read datasets \[[1](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/7279acd9c61a8de019ea2d55d23124dfbb6b5269)\]. However, these tools often require significant computational and memory resources, limiting their applicability to large-scale genomic studies \[[1](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/7279acd9c61a8de019ea2d55d23124dfbb6b5269)\].

Recent advancements in long-read sequencing technologies, such as PacBio and Oxford Nanopore, have introduced new challenges and opportunities for de novo assembly \[[3](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/2b59fd18ada769ef76fb7fcc7382f063791abfab)\]. These technologies produce longer reads but are accompanied by higher error rates, necessitating the development of specialized assemblers like Canu and Flye \[[3](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/2b59fd18ada769ef76fb7fcc7382f063791abfab)\]. Canu, for instance, employs adaptive k-mer weighting and repeat separation to improve the accuracy of assemblies, particularly for complex genomes \[[3](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/2b59fd18ada769ef76fb7fcc7382f063791abfab)\]. Similarly, Flye leverages repeat graphs to resolve repetitive regions, significantly enhancing the contiguity of assemblies \[[21](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/e0f860a608ecfb4ac6d0010e16db5c13cca14da1)\]. These tools demonstrate the importance of addressing both error correction and repeat resolution in long-read assembly.

The computational complexity of de novo assembly has also spurred research into parallel and distributed computing frameworks. For example, SWAP-Assembler and Ray have been developed to handle the massive data volumes generated by high-throughput sequencing technologies \[[14](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/fe8d86bc5520b5049104ff58ee7e980982816ebf)\]\[[19](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/38410204a2977a9484e6d1205697b4103b5482f7)\]. SWAP-Assembler, in particular, utilizes a scalable parallel model to process data across thousands of cores, significantly reducing runtime and improving contig quality \[[14](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/fe8d86bc5520b5049104ff58ee7e980982816ebf)\]. Ray, on the other hand, is designed to assemble reads from multiple sequencing platforms, demonstrating the potential for hybrid approaches in improving assembly accuracy \[[19](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/38410204a2977a9484e6d1205697b4103b5482f7)\].

Efforts to reduce memory usage and improve computational efficiency have also been a key focus. For instance, the use of succinct data structures, such as those described in "Succinct data structures for assembling large genomes," has shown promise in reducing storage requirements while maintaining assembly accuracy \[[6](http://arxiv.org/abs/1008.2555)\]. These structures enable the efficient representation of de Bruijn graphs, which are critical for large-scale genome assembly \[[6](http://arxiv.org/abs/1008.2555)\]. Additionally, the integration of probabilistic models, such as those used in BELLA, has helped in detecting overlaps and reducing false positives in long-read assemblies \[[10](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/f27decfab0389e2003e7ca66260b34eb3c1b8827)\].
