## Related Works

Vision Transformers (ViTs) have emerged as a powerful alternative to convolutional neural networks (CNNs) in computer vision tasks, leveraging the transformer architecture to effectively capture global context \[[4](http://arxiv.org/abs/2010.11929)\]. However, the computational complexity and memory requirements of ViTs pose significant challenges for hardware acceleration, particularly due to the sensitivity of operations like Softmax and LayerNorm to model accuracy \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2208.05163)\]. Existing approaches often rely on CPU for these complex operations, resulting in suboptimal performance due to communication overhead between the CPU and accelerator \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2208.05163)\]. This section reviews related works that address these challenges through hardware acceleration, quantization, and architectural improvements.

Several studies have explored hardware acceleration for ViTs. Auto-ViT-Acc introduces an FPGA-aware framework for ViT acceleration using mixed-scheme quantization, achieving a 5.6× improvement in frame rate with minimal accuracy loss \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2208.05163)\]. ViTCoD proposes a co-design framework that integrates algorithmic and hardware-level optimizations, significantly reducing data movement costs and achieving speedups of up to 235.3× \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/2210.09573)\]. DynamicViT employs dynamic token sparsification to reduce computational load while maintaining accuracy, achieving up to 40% throughput improvements \[[12](http://arxiv.org/abs/2106.02034)\]. HeatViT further enhances hardware efficiency by introducing a learnable token selector and 8-bit quantization, achieving up to 65.3% computation reduction with minimal accuracy loss \[[13](http://arxiv.org/abs/2211.08110)\]. These works highlight the importance of both algorithmic and hardware-level optimizations in improving ViT performance.

Quantization has also been explored as a means to reduce computational complexity. FQ-ViT introduces a post-training quantization method that mitigates performance degradation in fully quantized ViTs, achieving lossless accuracy degradation of ~1% \[[11](http://arxiv.org/abs/2111.13824)\]. VAQF presents a fully automatic framework for low-bit ViT acceleration on FPGAs, achieving frame rates of up to 30 FPS with 6-bit activation quantization \[[15](http://arxiv.org/abs/2201.06618)\]. These approaches demonstrate the potential of quantization to enable efficient ViT deployment on hardware platforms.

Architectural improvements have also contributed to ViT efficiency. T2T-ViT introduces a Tokens-to-Token transformation to reduce parameter count and computational cost while improving accuracy \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/2101.11986)\]. CvT integrates convolutions into ViTs to leverage the strengths of both architectures, achieving state-of-the-art performance with fewer parameters \[[10](http://arxiv.org/abs/2103.15808)\]. CrossViT utilizes cross-attention for multi-scale feature fusion, improving accuracy while maintaining efficiency \[[7](http://arxiv.org/abs/2103.14899)\]. ### <a id='category-fpga-based-vit-acceleration-approaches'></a>FPGA-Based ViT Acceleration Approaches: 3 papers found.
Recent FPGA-based ViT acceleration approaches have focused on addressing the computational and memory demands of ViTs for efficient deployment on resource-constrained edge devices \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2208.05163)\]. One key contribution is the introduction of mixed-scheme quantization, which enables higher accuracy compared to state-of-the-art quantization methods without hardware acceleration \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2208.05163)\]. Additionally, hardware-efficient token pruning frameworks, such as HeatViT, have been proposed to dynamically identify and remove non-informative tokens, leading to significant computation reduction while maintaining high accuracy \[[13](http://arxiv.org/abs/2211.08110)\]. These methods leverage the inherent computational patterns in ViTs to optimize both hardware utilization and performance. Furthermore, fully automatic quantization frameworks, such as VAQF, have been developed to guide both software-side quantization strategies and hardware-side accelerator implementations, enabling real-time execution of ViTs on FPGAs with minimal compilation time \[[15](http://arxiv.org/abs/2201.06618)\]. These approaches collectively highlight the importance of integrating both algorithmic and hardware-level optimizations to achieve high throughput and accuracy in ViT acceleration on FPGAs.

[Snippet]: «Vision Transformers (ViTs) have achieved state-of-the-art performance on various vision tasks. However, ViTs’ self-attention module is still arguably a major bottleneck, limiting their achievable hardware efficiency and more extensive applications to resource constrained platforms. Meanwhile, existing accelerators dedicated to NLP Transformers are not optimal for ViTs. This is because there is a large difference between ViTs and Transformers for natural language processing (NLP) tasks: ViTs have a relatively fixed number of input tokens, whose attention maps can be pruned by up to 90% even with fixed sparse patterns, without severely hurting the model accuracy (e.g., <=1.5% under 90% pruning ratio); while NLP Transformers need to handle input sequences of varying numbers of tokens and rely on on-the-fly predictions of dynamic sparse attention patterns for each input to achieve a decent sparsity (e.g., >=50%). To this end, we propose a dedicated algorithm and accelerator co-design framework dubbed ViTCoD for accelerating ViTs. Specifically, on the algorithm level, ViTCoD prunes and polarizes the attention maps to have either denser or sparser fixed patterns for regularizing two levels of workloads without hurting the accuracy, largely reducing the attention computations while leaving room for alleviating the remaining dominant data movements; on top of that, we further integrate a lightweight and learnable auto-encoder module to enable trading the dominant high-cost data movements for lower-cost computations. On the hardware level, we develop a dedicated accelerator to simultaneously coordinate the aforementioned enforced denser and sparser workloads for boosted hardware utilization, while integrating on-chip encoder and decoder engines to leverage ViTCoD’s algorithm pipeline for much reduced data movements. Extensive experiments and ablation studies validate that ViTCoD largely reduces the dominant data movement costs, achieving speedups of up to 235.3×, 142.9×, 86.0×, 10.1×, and 6.8× over general computing platforms CPUs, EdgeGPUs, GPUs, and prior-art Transformer accelerators SpAtten and Sanger under an attention sparsity of 90%, respectively. Our code implementation is available at https://github.com/GATECH-EIC/ViTCoD.»

### <a id='category-vision-transformer-(vit)-architecture-and-performance'></a>Vision Transformer (ViT) Architecture and Performance: 11 papers found.
Vision Transformer (ViT) architectures have gained significant traction in computer vision due to their ability to capture global context effectively, as demonstrated in various image classification tasks \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/2210.09573)\]. However, early ViT models faced challenges in performance compared to CNNs, primarily due to inefficient tokenization and attention mechanisms \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2208.05163)\]. To address these limitations, researchers proposed improvements such as Tokens-To-Token Vision Transformer (T2T-ViT), which enhances local structure modeling and reduces computational overhead \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2208.05163)\]. Additionally, efforts have been made to optimize ViT efficiency through sparsity-aware training, dynamic token sparsification, and unified compression frameworks that combine pruning, layer skipping, and knowledge distillation \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/2101.11986)\]\[[9](http://arxiv.org/abs/2103.00112)\]. These approaches aim to reduce computational costs while maintaining or improving accuracy, as seen in models like DynamicViT and CvT, which integrate convolutional elements or hierarchical attention mechanisms \[[7](http://arxiv.org/abs/2103.14899)\]\[[12](http://arxiv.org/abs/2106.02034)\]. Furthermore, novel architectures such as Transformer iN Transformer (TNT) and Pooling-based Vision Transformer (PiT) have been introduced to enhance feature representation and spatial dimension handling, leading to improved performance on benchmarks like ImageNet \[[6](http://arxiv.org/abs/2106.02852)\]\[[10](http://arxiv.org/abs/2103.15808)\]. These advancements highlight the ongoing evolution of ViT models toward more efficient and effective vision tasks.

[Snippet]: «This paper studies the efficiency problem for visual transformers by excavating redundant calculation in given networks. The recent transformer architecture has demonstrated its effectiveness for achieving excellent performance on a series of computer vision tasks. However, similar to that of convolutional neural networks, the huge computational cost of vision transformers is still a severe issue. Considering that the attention mechanism aggregates different patches layer-by-layer, we present a novel patch slimming approach that discards useless patches in a topdown paradigm. We first identify the effective patches in the last layer and then use them to guide the patch selection process of previous layers. For each layer, the impact of a patch on the final output feature is approximated and patches with less impacts will be removed. Experimental results on benchmark datasets demonstrate that the proposed method can significantly reduce the computational costs of vision transformers without affecting their performances. For example, over 45% FLOPs of the ViT-Ti model can be reduced with only 0.2% top-1 accuracy drop on the ImageNet dataset.»

[Snippet]: «Network quantization significantly reduces model inference complexity and has been widely used in real-world deployments. However, most existing quantization methods have been developed mainly on Convolutional Neural Networks (CNNs), and suffer severe degradation when applied to fully quantized vision transformers. In this work, we demonstrate that many of these difficulties arise because of serious inter-channel variation in LayerNorm inputs, and present, Power-of-Two Factor (PTF), a systematic method to reduce the performance degradation and inference complexity of fully quantized vision transformers. In addition, observing an extreme non-uniform distribution in attention maps, we propose Log-Int-Softmax (LIS) to sustain that and simplify inference by using 4-bit quantization and the BitShift operator. Comprehensive experiments on various transformer-based architectures and benchmarks show that our Fully Quantized Vision Transformer (FQ-ViT) outperforms previous works while even using lower bit-width on attention maps. For instance, we reach 84.89% top-1 accuracy with ViT-L on ImageNet and 50.8 mAP with Cascade Mask R-CNN (Swin-S) on COCO. To our knowledge, we are the first to achieve lossless accuracy degradation (~1%) on fully quantized vision transformers. The code is available at https://github.com/megvii-research/FQ-ViT.»
