## Related Works

Long-tailed recognition remains a challenging problem in visual recognition due to the skewed class distribution in real-world datasets \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/1910.09217)\]. Prior work has primarily focused on class-balancing strategies such as loss re-weighting, data re-sampling, or transfer learning from head- to tail-classes \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/1910.09217)\]. However, these methods often jointly learn representations and classifiers, which can lead to suboptimal performance \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/1910.09217)\]. Recent studies have explored the potential of contrastive learning for long-tailed recognition, showing promising results in improving feature representations \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/2207.09052)\]. For instance, supervised contrastive learning (SCL) has been shown to effectively pull samples from the same class together in the embedding space while pushing apart samples from different classes \[[22](http://arxiv.org/abs/2004.11362)\]. However, SCL struggles with long-tailed data due to its inability to form a regular simplex, which is ideal for representation learning \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/2207.09052)\]. To address this, researchers have proposed variants such as balanced contrastive learning (BCL) and targeted supervised contrastive learning (TSC), which aim to improve the uniformity of feature distributions and enhance class boundaries \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/2207.09052)\]\[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/2111.13998)\]. These methods demonstrate that contrastive learning can be adapted to long-tailed scenarios by modifying the loss function and training strategy \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/2207.09052)\]\[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/2111.13998)\].

The effectiveness of contrastive learning in long-tailed recognition has also been explored in the context of self-supervised learning. SimCLR, a simple framework for contrastive learning of visual representations, has shown that data augmentation and normalized embeddings play a critical role in learning useful representations \[[8](http://arxiv.org/abs/2002.05709)\]. Similarly, PaCo introduces parametric class-wise learnable centers to rebalance the optimization process and improve performance on long-tailed datasets \[[4](http://arxiv.org/abs/2107.12028)\]. These approaches highlight the importance of addressing class imbalance during the learning process, either through loss design or representation learning \[[4](http://arxiv.org/abs/2107.12028)\]\[[8](http://arxiv.org/abs/2002.05709)\].

Another line of research focuses on improving the calibration of models trained on long-tailed data. Techniques such as label-aware smoothing and shifted batch normalization have been proposed to reduce miscalibration and improve classifier learning \[[9](http://arxiv.org/abs/2104.00466)\]. These methods aim to address the issue of over-confidence in dominant classes and ensure that the model generalizes well to rare classes \[[9](http://arxiv.org/abs/2104.00466)\]. Additionally, distribution alignment strategies have been introduced to balance the class prior during training, leading to improved performance across various tasks \[[10](http://arxiv.org/abs/2103.16370)\]. These approaches demonstrate that addressing class imbalance requires a holistic strategy that considers both the representation and the classifier \[[10](http://arxiv.org/abs/2103.16370)\].
