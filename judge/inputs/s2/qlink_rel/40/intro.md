## Related Works

Zero-Knowledge Proofs (ZKPs) have garnered significant attention for their ability to enable secure and private computation, allowing a prover to demonstrate the truth of a statement without revealing additional information \[[16](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/b179cf70666c9f67fc8c330be3a55a5dd2266c2e)\]. Despite their potential, the computational complexity of generating ZKP proofs has hindered their widespread adoption in real-world applications \[[1](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/e149d93531d8c07cf43f6cae327a4795144d1304)\]. Recent efforts have focused on accelerating the key primitives of ZKP protocols using hardware, particularly GPUs and ASICs, to reduce the time required for proof generation \[[1](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/e149d93531d8c07cf43f6cae327a4795144d1304)\]. However, existing accelerations often face limitations such as requiring a trusted setup for each application or generating proofs with larger sizes and higher verification costs, which restrict their use in scenarios with numerous verifiers or strict time constraints \[[1](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/e149d93531d8c07cf43f6cae327a4795144d1304)\].

Several works have explored hardware acceleration for ZKP protocols. For instance, GZKP leverages GPU acceleration to improve performance for polynomial computations and multi-scalar multiplications, achieving speedups of up to 48.1× and 17.6× over existing systems \[[1](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/e149d93531d8c07cf43f6cae327a4795144d1304)\]. Similarly, PipeZK introduces a pipelined architecture with two subsystems to handle intensive compute tasks, achieving 10× speedup on cryptographic benchmarks and 5× on Zcash \[[1](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/e149d93531d8c07cf43f6cae327a4795144d1304)\]. These approaches highlight the importance of optimizing memory access patterns and parallelism for efficient ZKP generation \[[1](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/e149d93531d8c07cf43f6cae327a4795144d1304)\].

The challenge of accelerating entire ZKP protocols, rather than just individual primitives, has also been addressed. For example, SZKP is the first ASIC to accelerate an entire ZKP proof on-chip, leveraging structured dataflows for both Number Theoretic Transform (NTT) and Multi-scalar Multiplication (MSM) operations. It achieves over 400× speedup over CPU implementations and 12× over GPU implementations \[[1](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/e149d93531d8c07cf43f6cae327a4795144d1304)\]. These results underscore the potential of hardware acceleration to significantly reduce proof generation time, making ZKPs more practical for real-world applications \[[1](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/e149d93531d8c07cf43f6cae327a4795144d1304)\].

In addition to hardware acceleration, there have been efforts to improve the efficiency of ZKP protocols themselves. For example, zkCNN introduces a zero-knowledge proof scheme for convolutional neural networks (CNNs), enabling the owner of a model to prove the correctness of predictions without leaking model details \[[1](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/e149d93531d8c07cf43f6cae327a4795144d1304)\]. This work also introduces a new sumcheck protocol for fast Fourier transforms and convolutions, achieving a 1264× speedup over existing schemes \[[1](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/e149d93531d8c07cf43f6cae327a4795144d1304)\]. Such advancements demonstrate the growing interest in applying ZKPs to machine learning and other complex computations \[[1](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/e149d93531d8c07cf43f6cae327a4795144d1304)\].
