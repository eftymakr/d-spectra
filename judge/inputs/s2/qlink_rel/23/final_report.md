## Related Works

Sensor calibration is a critical step in autonomous driving systems, enabling accurate data fusion from heterogeneous sensors such as radar, camera, and LiDAR. Prior work has focused on both target-based and targetless calibration methods. Target-based approaches, such as those using calibration boards or retroreflectors, are widely used due to their simplicity and reliability \[[3](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/4d4dbbcf27d991aeab43dafb82a105bb5512b799)\]. However, they are not suitable for real-time or online calibration, as they require dedicated calibration targets \[[5](http://arxiv.org/abs/1904.08743)\]. Targetless methods, on the other hand, aim to achieve automatic calibration without the need for external targets, which is essential for long-term system reliability \[[1](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/0955326c67a947b5fac5fbae7238d04fc9adc251)\]. 

Several studies have explored radar-camera calibration, particularly in challenging environments. A continuous-time approach for 3D radar-to-camera extrinsic calibration was proposed, which utilizes radar velocity measurements and does not require specialized radar retroreflectors \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/2103.07505)\]. This method addresses the sparsity of radar data and improves the robustness of the calibration process. Similarly, a data-driven method for automatic rotational radar-camera calibration was introduced, which does not require dedicated calibration targets and is trained on real-world data \[[5](http://arxiv.org/abs/1904.08743)\]. This approach leverages a coarse and fine convolutional neural network to achieve precise and robust sensor registration.

The importance of sensor fusion for robust perception has also been emphasized in recent work. For example, CenterFusion \[[4](http://arxiv.org/abs/2011.04841)\] proposes a middle-fusion approach that exploits both radar and camera data for 3D object detection, improving detection accuracy and velocity estimation. Another study, CRN \[[15](http://arxiv.org/abs/2304.00670)\], presents a novel camera-radar fusion framework that generates a semantically rich and spatially accurate bird’s-eye-view (BEV) feature map, which is crucial for tasks such as object detection and tracking. This framework addresses the limitations of camera-only approaches in adverse weather conditions by leveraging the precise long-range measurements of radar.

Online calibration methods have also gained attention, particularly those based on moving object tracking. A method that uses detection and tracking of moving objects for online multi-sensor calibration was proposed, which is computationally lightweight and resource-efficient \[[6](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/c80265fa719eb85c8a56a6b36c74b99b793f39b2)\]. This approach is particularly useful for autonomous systems that require long-term reliability without the need for manual calibration. Additionally, a method that integrates millimeter wave radar with a monocular vision sensor for on-road obstacle detection was presented, which uses a three-level fusion strategy to improve detection performance \[[24](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/fbd0a52edc6ee6c8dd8a6992eaee20d66b982d65)\].

In terms of feature-level fusion, RVDet \[[23](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/a94f8a34a9c4fce90727f62d652f2420fd1021a2)\] proposes a framework that aligns radar and camera features in the bird’s-eye view (BEV) domain

### <a id='category-sensor-fusion-for-perception'></a>Sensor Fusion for Perception: 13 papers found.
The related works in sensor fusion for perception focus on improving the accuracy and robustness of autonomous vehicle systems by integrating data from multiple sensors, such as radar and camera. Early approaches primarily relied on single-sensor data, which led to limitations in performance under adverse conditions \[[1](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/0955326c67a947b5fac5fbae7238d04fc9adc251)\]. To address these challenges, researchers have proposed various fusion strategies, including middle-fusion and feature-level fusion, to leverage the strengths of different modalities \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/2103.07505)\]. For instance, CenterFusion introduces a middle-fusion approach that combines radar and camera data to improve 3D object detection by associating radar detections with image features \[[3](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/4d4dbbcf27d991aeab43dafb82a105bb5512b799)\]. Similarly, CameraRadarFusion Net (CRF-Net) enhances 2D object detection by fusing camera and radar data at network layers, demonstrating significant improvements over image-only methods \[[4](http://arxiv.org/abs/2011.04841)\]. Another notable work, BEVFusion, proposes a framework that unifies multi-modal features in a shared bird's-eye view (BEV) representation space, preserving both geometric and semantic information \[[5](http://arxiv.org/abs/1904.08743)\]. Additionally, the nuScenes dataset, which includes data from 6 cameras, 5 radars, and 1 lidar, has become a benchmark for evaluating multi-sensor fusion techniques \[[6](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/c80265fa719eb85c8a56a6b36c74b99b793f39b2)\]. These studies highlight the importance of sensor fusion in achieving reliable perception, especially in challenging environments where single-sensor data may be insufficient \[[7](http://arxiv.org/abs/2209.06535)\].
