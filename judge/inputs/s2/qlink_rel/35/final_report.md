## Related Works

Protein dynamics are essential for understanding biological functions and properties, yet traditional methods such as molecular dynamics (MD) simulations are computationally expensive and time-consuming \[[1](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/21a4afa74caf78e28880b7fe4c30a09a5f9c14e3)\]. Recent advances in generative modeling have enabled more efficient and accurate protein structure prediction and conformation sampling by learning distributions over crystallographic structures \[[2](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/2eb1ed9d4baf548391d8220c0a8a3d0be79845ed)\]. However, integrating physical supervision into these data-driven approaches remains a challenge, as standard energy-based objectives often lead to intractable optimization \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/2402.04845)\]. This section reviews related works that address these challenges and contribute to the field of protein conformation generation and dynamics modeling.

Several studies have explored the use of deep learning for protein structure prediction and conformation sampling. For instance, AlphaFold has demonstrated high accuracy in predicting protein structures from sequences, achieving atomic-level precision even in cases where no homologous structures are available \[[4](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/dc32a984b651256a8ec282be52310e6bd33d9815)\]. Similarly, AlphaFold Meets Flow Matching introduces a flow-based generative modeling approach for learning and sampling the conformational landscapes of proteins, leveraging highly accurate single-state predictors like AlphaFold \[[5](http://arxiv.org/abs/2304.02198)\]. These models have shown superior performance in capturing conformational flexibility and generating diverse protein structures \[[5](http://arxiv.org/abs/2304.02198)\].

Another line of research focuses on diffusion models for protein conformation generation. The SE(3) diffusion model, for example, has been applied to protein backbone generation, demonstrating the ability to generate designable monomers up to 500 amino acids without relying on pretrained protein structure prediction networks \[[6](http://arxiv.org/abs/2209.15611)\]. Similarly, the ConfDiff model incorporates a force-guided network with a mixture of data-based score models to generate protein conformations with rich diversity while preserving high fidelity \[[7](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/c49a0912595a1cc70aab63524f64ed08c92194a8)\]. These models address the challenge of incorporating physical prior knowledge into the generation process, ensuring that sampled conformations remain close to the equilibrium distribution \[[7](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/c49a0912595a1cc70aab63524f64ed08c92194a8)\].

Additionally, the development of Boltzmann generators has enabled the direct generation of equilibrium states of many-body systems using deep learning and statistical mechanics \[[8](http://arxiv.org/abs/2306.03117)\]. These models are trained on energy functions of molecular systems and learn to provide unbiased, one-shot samples from their equilibrium states, overcoming the limitations of traditional sampling methods \[[8](http://arxiv.org/abs/2306.03117)\]. This approach has been applied to protein conformation sampling, demonstrating the ability to generate statistically independent samples from different metastable states \[[8](http://arxiv.org/abs/2306.03117)\].

### <a id='category-score-based-frameworks-for-protein-conformation-sampling'></a>Score-Based Frameworks for Protein Conformation Sampling: 1 papers found.
The category "Score-Based Frameworks for Protein Conformation Sampling" focuses on the challenges of exploring protein conformational space using traditional methods like Monte Carlo (MC) and molecular dynamics (MD) simulations, which are limited by high-energy barriers and the need for extensive computational resources \[[8](http://arxiv.org/abs/2306.03117)\]. These methods rely on empirically derived force fields and either Markov chain or Newtonian mechanics to evolve the system, but they often fail to adequately sample the conformational space due to the rare event problem \[[8](http://arxiv.org/abs/2306.03117)\]. Recent advances have introduced learning-based approaches that perform direct sampling, but these are target-specific and require large amounts of simulation data for training, making them less generalizable \[[8](http://arxiv.org/abs/2306.03117)\]. To address these limitations, the Str2Str framework was proposed, inspired by simulated annealing, which enables zero-shot conformation sampling without relying on simulation data during training or inference \[[8](http://arxiv.org/abs/2306.03117)\]. This framework utilizes an amortized denoising score matching objective trained on general crystal structures and possesses roto-translation equivariant properties, allowing it to generate high-quality protein conformations efficiently \[[8](http://arxiv.org/abs/2306.03117)\]. Experimental results show that Str2Str outperforms existing generative models and significantly reduces computational time compared to traditional MD simulations \[[8](http://arxiv.org/abs/2306.03117)\]. This approach represents a significant advancement in the field by integrating generalizable learning-based methods with physical principles to improve the accuracy and efficiency of protein conformation sampling.
