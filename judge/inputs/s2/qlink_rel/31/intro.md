## Related Works

Unsupervised pre-training has emerged as a powerful paradigm for learning general-purpose representations from raw data. This approach has been widely applied across various domains, including computer vision and natural language processing. However, its application to DNA pre-training has faced challenges due to the limited information content of pure DNA sequences \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/1807.03748)\]. In contrast, supervised training for genomic profile prediction has been shown to be more effective, as it leverages additional regulatory signals such as chromatin accessibility \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/1206.5538)\]. This is supported by prior work that highlights the importance of incorporating domain-specific knowledge to improve representation learning \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/1505.05192)\].

Several methods have been proposed for unsupervised visual representation learning, such as Contrastive Predictive Coding \[[4](http://arxiv.org/abs/1905.09272)\], which uses a probabilistic contrastive loss to learn latent representations that are useful for predicting future samples. Similarly, the SimCLR framework \[[5](http://arxiv.org/abs/1603.09246)\] simplifies contrastive learning by eliminating the need for specialized architectures or memory banks, and it demonstrates that data augmentation and larger batch sizes can significantly improve representation quality. These methods have been successful in learning robust representations from unlabeled data, but they often require careful design of the predictive tasks.

In the context of DNA representation learning, the use of supervised signals from genomic profiles has been explored. For example, the work on Embedding Earth \[[6](http://arxiv.org/abs/1609.02907)\] presents a self-supervised contrastive pre-training method for dense land cover classification, leveraging the large availability of satellite imagery. This approach is particularly relevant when ground truth data is scarce, as it allows for the transfer of learned features to downstream tasks with minimal labeled data. Similarly, the work on Unsupervised Feature Learning via Non-parametric Instance Discrimination \[[7](http://arxiv.org/abs/1907.11692)\] shows that learning representations that capture instance-level similarity can outperform state-of-the-art methods on ImageNet classification.

The introduction of the SPACE model builds upon these ideas by leveraging a Mixture of Experts (MoE) architecture to better capture the relationships between DNA sequences across different species and genomic profiles. This approach is motivated by the need to handle the multi-species and multi-profile nature of genomic data, which is a critical challenge in DNA representation learning. The effectiveness of this approach is demonstrated through extensive experiments, where the model achieves state-of-the-art performance on various tasks \[[8](http://arxiv.org/abs/2203.06041)\].

In addition to these methods, other approaches such as the use of gapped k-mer features \[[9](http://arxiv.org/abs/1805.01978)\] and the development of deep residual networks \[[10](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/42d3ae61ca296558cba61446bd95b8a6e5b1082d)\] have also contributed to the field of representation learning.
