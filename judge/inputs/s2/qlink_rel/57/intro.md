## Related Works

Generative retrieval (GR) has gained significant attention in information retrieval (IR) due to its ability to directly generate document identifiers (docids) for a given query, bypassing the need for traditional index-retrieve pipelines \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/2202.06991)\]. However, most existing GR models are primarily evaluated on static document collections, with limited exploration of their performance in dynamic corpora where documents are continuously added \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2308.14968)\]. This section reviews related works that address GR, dynamic corpus handling, and docid design, providing a foundation for the current study.

Early GR approaches, such as those based on the Differentiable Search Index (DSI), encode all document information into model parameters, allowing queries to be answered directly using the model \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/2202.06991)\]. While DSI has shown strong performance on small-scale datasets, its scalability to large corpora remains a challenge \[[6](http://arxiv.org/abs/2305.11841)\]. Similarly, the Neural Corpus Indexer (NCI) introduces a sequence-to-sequence model that generates docids directly for queries, improving recall performance over traditional methods \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/2206.02743)\]. However, these models often rely on static docid definitions, which may not generalize well to dynamic settings \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2308.14968)\].

The importance of docid design in GR has been highlighted in several works. For instance, the *text-based* docid approach, where identifiers are generated as semantic tokens, has shown superior generalization to unseen documents compared to *numeric-based* docids \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2308.14968)\]. This is attributed to the semantic alignment of text-based docids with the pretrained knowledge of language models \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2308.14968)\]. In contrast, numeric-based docids, while efficient, suffer from overfitting to the initial document set, leading to poor performance in dynamic environments \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2308.14968)\]. This issue is further exacerbated by the lack of semantic richness in numeric identifiers, which limits their ability to capture document relationships \[[12](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/6682a3679a17d090830635f1c420126d20eae54a)\].

Several works have attempted to address these limitations. The GenRet method introduces a discrete auto-encoding framework to learn semantic docids in an end-to-end manner, improving retrieval performance on benchmark datasets \[[4](http://arxiv.org/abs/2304.04171)\]. Similarly, the NOVO approach proposes learnable docids optimized through retrieval tasks, enhancing semantic representation and model performance \[[12](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/6682a3679a17d090830635f1c420126d20eae54a)\]. The DSI++ model further advances continual learning for DSI, enabling incremental indexing of new documents while mitigating forgetting of previously indexed ones \[[11](http://arxiv.org/abs/2212.09744)\]. These efforts highlight the growing interest in dynamic GR models that can adapt to evolving document collections.
