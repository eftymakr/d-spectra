## Related Works

Generative retrieval (GR) has gained significant attention in information retrieval (IR) due to its ability to directly generate document identifiers (docids) for a given query, bypassing the need for traditional index-retrieve pipelines \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/2202.06991)\]. However, most existing GR models are primarily evaluated on static document collections, with limited exploration of their performance in dynamic corpora where documents are continuously added \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2308.14968)\]. This section reviews related works that address GR, dynamic corpus handling, and docid design, providing a foundation for the current study.

Early GR approaches, such as those based on the Differentiable Search Index (DSI), encode all document information into model parameters, allowing queries to be answered directly using the model \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/2202.06991)\]. While DSI has shown strong performance on small-scale datasets, its scalability to large corpora remains a challenge \[[6](http://arxiv.org/abs/2305.11841)\]. Similarly, the Neural Corpus Indexer (NCI) introduces a sequence-to-sequence model that generates docids directly for queries, improving recall performance over traditional methods \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/2206.02743)\]. However, these models often rely on static docid definitions, which may not generalize well to dynamic settings \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2308.14968)\].

The importance of docid design in GR has been highlighted in several works. For instance, the *text-based* docid approach, where identifiers are generated as semantic tokens, has shown superior generalization to unseen documents compared to *numeric-based* docids \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2308.14968)\]. This is attributed to the semantic alignment of text-based docids with the pretrained knowledge of language models \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2308.14968)\]. In contrast, numeric-based docids, while efficient, suffer from overfitting to the initial document set, leading to poor performance in dynamic environments \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2308.14968)\]. This issue is further exacerbated by the lack of semantic richness in numeric identifiers, which limits their ability to capture document relationships \[[12](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/6682a3679a17d090830635f1c420126d20eae54a)\].

Several works have attempted to address these limitations. The GenRet method introduces a discrete auto-encoding framework to learn semantic docids in an end-to-end manner, improving retrieval performance on benchmark datasets \[[4](http://arxiv.org/abs/2304.04171)\]. Similarly, the NOVO approach proposes learnable docids optimized through retrieval tasks, enhancing semantic representation and model performance \[[12](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/6682a3679a17d090830635f1c420126d20eae54a)\]. The DSI++ model further advances continual learning for DSI, enabling incremental indexing of new documents while mitigating forgetting of previously indexed ones \[[11](http://arxiv.org/abs/2212.09744)\]. These efforts highlight the growing interest in dynamic GR models that can adapt to evolving document collections.

However, most existing GR models are evaluated on static document collections, limiting their applicability to dynamic corpora where new documents are continuously added \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2308.14968)\]. Recent works have addressed this challenge by proposing methods that allow incremental indexing and querying of new documents while preserving retrieval performance \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2308.14968)\]. For instance, CLEVER introduces Incremental Product Quantization to efficiently encode new documents and a memory-augmented learning mechanism to prevent catastrophic forgetting \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2308.14968)\]. Additionally, SEAL proposes using all ngrams in a passage as possible identifiers, enabling autoregressive models to generate and score distinctive ngrams for retrieval, which outperforms prior approaches and established retrieval solutions \[[4](http://arxiv.org/abs/2304.04171)\]. NOVO introduces learnable docids as unique n-gram sets that can be optimized through query denoising modeling and retrieval tasks, enhancing semantic and token representations for better performance \[[6](http://arxiv.org/abs/2305.11841)\]. ### <a id='category-semantic-alignment-with-pretrained-models'></a>Semantic Alignment with Pretrained Models: 4 papers found.
The category "Semantic Alignment with Pretrained Models" explores how modern information retrieval systems can better align with the semantic understanding of language models. Research in this area highlights the limitations of traditional index-retrieve paradigms, where the index is difficult to optimize for the final retrieval target \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2308.14968)\]. To address this, the Neural Corpus Indexer (NCI) proposes an end-to-end deep neural network that unifies training and indexing stages, improving recall performance through a prefix-aware weight-adaptive decoder and semantic document identifiers \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/2206.02743)\]. Similarly, the Differentiable Search Index (DSI) introduces a paradigm where a single Transformer model encodes all corpus information, enabling direct query-to-docid mapping without separate indexing \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/2202.06991)\]. However, DSI models face challenges due to data distribution mismatches between indexing and retrieval, especially in cross-lingual settings \[[4](http://arxiv.org/abs/2304.04171)\]. To mitigate this, DSI-QG generates relevant queries during indexing, allowing the model to connect document identifiers to a set of queries, thus improving alignment and performance \[[5](http://arxiv.org/abs/2206.10128)\]. These approaches underscore the importance of semantic alignment in enhancing retrieval accuracy, particularly when leveraging pretrained models for more effective and generalized information retrieval.

### <a id='category-generative-retrieval-(gr)-overview'></a>Generative Retrieval (GR) Overview: 4 papers found.
Generative retrieval (GR) has gained significant attention as a promising paradigm in information retrieval, shifting from traditional index-based methods to sequence-to-sequence modeling \[[6](http://arxiv.org/abs/2305.11841)\]. This approach encodes an entire document corpus within a single Transformer, eliminating the need for external indices \[[6](http://arxiv.org/abs/2305.11841)\]. However, most existing GR models are evaluated on static document collections, with limited exploration of their performance on dynamic corpora where document sets evolve continuously \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2308.14968)\]. Recent studies have begun to address this gap, revealing that GR models with text-based docids demonstrate superior generalization to unseen documents compared to numeric-based docids \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2308.14968)\]. The finer granularity of text-based docids aligns better with the semantic understanding of language models, enhancing performance in dynamic settings \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2308.14968)\]. Additionally, the lexical diversity of text-based docids contributes to their effectiveness, allowing for more robust retrieval in evolving document collections \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2308.14968)\]. In contrast, numeric-based docids, while efficient, suffer from overfitting to the initial training set, leading to performance degradation in dynamic environments \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2308.14968)\]. These findings highlight the importance of docid design in GR models, with implications for improving retrieval accuracy in real-world applications \[[6](http://arxiv.org/abs/2305.11841)\]. Several works have also explored the integration of generative models with search systems, proposing unified approaches that combine retrieval and reasoning for knowledge-intensive tasks \[[7](http://arxiv.org/abs/2208.07652)\]. These efforts underscore the ongoing evolution of GR, emphasizing the need for models that balance efficiency with adaptability in dynamic settings \[[15](http://arxiv.org/abs/2204.05511)\].

Across different model scales and document identifier representations, we show that continual indexing of new documents leads to considerable forgetting of previously indexed documents.
