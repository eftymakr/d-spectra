## Related Works

Video super-resolution (VSR) has been a focal point in computer vision, aiming to reconstruct high-resolution (HR) videos from low-resolution (LR) inputs. Early approaches relied on explicit motion estimation and compensation, which often introduced inaccuracies and computational overhead \[[1](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/c6a8ff4d9dcdf9e5d82310b847af999ed6655f65)\]. More recently, deep learning methods have emerged as a powerful alternative, leveraging convolutional neural networks (CNNs) to learn complex mappings between LR and HR frames \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/1611.05250)\]. However, these methods often struggle with temporal consistency and computational efficiency, especially when dealing with large motions or complex scenes \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/1801.04590)\].

Several works have attempted to address these challenges by incorporating recurrent architectures or attention mechanisms. For instance, \[[4](http://arxiv.org/abs/1812.02898)\] proposed a frame-recurrent video super-resolution framework that uses previously inferred HR estimates to super-resolve subsequent frames, thereby improving temporal consistency while reducing computational cost. Similarly, \[[5](http://arxiv.org/abs/1707.02921)\] introduced a temporally-deformable alignment network (TDAN) that aligns reference and supporting frames at the feature level without computing optical flow, thus alleviating artifacts caused by inaccurate flow estimation. This approach has shown significant improvements in handling occlusions and maintaining temporal alignment \[[6](http://arxiv.org/abs/2201.12288)\].

Another line of research focuses on the use of transformers for VSR, capitalizing on their parallel computing capabilities and ability to model long-range dependencies. However, standard transformer architectures often fail to exploit spatial-temporal locality effectively, leading to suboptimal performance \[[7](http://arxiv.org/abs/1905.02716)\]. To address this, \[[8](http://arxiv.org/abs/1909.08080)\] proposed a spatial-temporal convolutional self-attention layer, which better captures local information while maintaining the benefits of transformers. This has led to notable improvements in both accuracy and efficiency \[[9](http://arxiv.org/abs/2008.05765)\].

In addition to architectural innovations, training strategies have also been refined to improve the stability and quality of VSR models. For example, \[[10](http://arxiv.org/abs/1511.04587)\] introduced a three-stage training strategy that combines HR-LR videos to stabilize the training process, leading to better perceptual quality. This approach has been further extended in \[[11](http://arxiv.org/abs/2007.10595)\], where a progressive fusion network is designed to exploit non-local spatio-temporal correlations, resulting in more efficient and effective fusion of temporal information \[[12](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/a2c50f93f6d0892918e0ff653750d198ccab48d0)\].

Moreover, recurrent structures have been widely adopted to model temporal dependencies in VSR. \[[13](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/755227d7ea41b1d90df31f411f9ecbc998c20d26)\] proposed a bidirectional recurrent convolutional network that reduces the number of parameters and enables finer-level modeling of temporal dependencies. This has led to faster inference times and better performance on videos with complex motions \[[14](http://arxiv.org/abs/2008.00455)\].

### <a id='category-deep-learning-approaches-for-vsr'></a>Deep Learning Approaches for VSR: 11 papers found.
Video super-resolution (VSR) has seen significant advancements through deep learning approaches, with various methods addressing challenges such as motion estimation, temporal consistency, and computational efficiency \[[1](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/c6a8ff4d9dcdf9e5d82310b847af999ed6655f65)\]. Early methods relied heavily on explicit motion compensation, but recent works have introduced end-to-end neural networks that implicitly handle motion through dynamic upsampling filters and residual learning \[[1](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/c6a8ff4d9dcdf9e5d82310b847af999ed6655f65)\]. For instance, some approaches utilize spatio-temporal sub-pixel convolutions to exploit temporal redundancies while maintaining real-time performance \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/1611.05250)\]. Others, like the enhanced deep super-resolution network (EDSR), focus on improving performance by removing unnecessary modules and expanding model size while stabilizing training \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/1801.04590)\]. Similarly, the very deep residual channel attention network (RCAN) addresses training difficulties in deep networks by introducing residual in residual (RIR) structures and channel attention mechanisms \[[7](http://arxiv.org/abs/1905.02716)\]. Additionally, methods such as the multi-correspondence aggregation network (MuCAN) leverage self-similarity across frames to improve reconstruction accuracy \[[6](http://arxiv.org/abs/2201.12288)\]. These approaches collectively highlight the shift towards more efficient and accurate VSR techniques that balance performance with computational cost \[[8](http://arxiv.org/abs/1909.08080)\].

### <a id='category-temporally-deformable-alignment-networks'></a>Temporally-Deformable Alignment Networks: 3 papers found.
In the realm of video super-resolution (VSR), recent approaches have focused on improving temporal alignment and reducing artifacts during frame synthesis. Traditional methods often rely on optical flow to align reference and supporting frames, but this can lead to inaccuracies and visual artifacts \[[1](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/c6a8ff4d9dcdf9e5d82310b847af999ed6655f65)\]. To address these limitations, temporally-deformable alignment networks such as TDAN and EDVR have been proposed. TDAN eliminates the need for optical flow by dynamically predicting offsets for convolutional kernels at the feature level, enabling more accurate alignment without introducing artifacts \[[1](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/c6a8ff4d9dcdf9e5d82310b847af999ed6655f65)\]. Similarly, EDVR introduces a Pyramid, Cascading, and Deformable (PCD) alignment module that handles large motions through deformable convolutions in a coarse-to-fine manner, improving alignment accuracy \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/1611.05250)\]. Furthermore, EDVR incorporates a Temporal and Spatial Attention (TSA) fusion module to enhance feature fusion across frames with diverse motion and blur, leading to superior performance in video restoration tasks \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/1611.05250)\]. These methods highlight the importance of feature-level alignment and attention mechanisms in achieving high-quality VSR results. Additionally, one-stage frameworks have been developed to directly synthesize high-resolution slow-motion videos from low-frame-rate, low-resolution inputs, leveraging temporal interpolation and deformable ConvLSTM to better capture global temporal contexts \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/1801.04590)\]. These advancements demonstrate the growing trend toward more efficient and effective VSR techniques that address both alignment and quality challenges.

### <a id='category-transformer-based-vsr-architectures'></a>Transformer-Based VSR Architectures: 3 papers found.
Video restoration and super-resolution have seen significant advancements with the integration of transformer-based architectures, which leverage parallel processing and long-range dependencies. These models address the challenge of temporal information fusion by utilizing mechanisms such as sliding windows, recurrent structures, or self-attention layers \[[20](http://arxiv.org/abs/2206.02146)\]. For instance, the Video Restoration Transformer (VRT) introduces temporal reciprocal self attention (TRSA) and parallel warping to enable joint motion estimation and feature alignment across multiple video clips \[[6](http://arxiv.org/abs/2201.12288)\]. Similarly, the Recurrent Video Restoration Transformer (RVRT) combines the advantages of parallel and sequential processing by using a globally recurrent framework with guided deformable attention for cross-clip alignment \[[20](http://arxiv.org/abs/2206.02146)\]. These approaches aim to improve performance on tasks like video super-resolution, deblurring, and denoising while managing computational efficiency \[[6](http://arxiv.org/abs/2201.12288)\]. Additionally, efforts have been made to adapt standard transformer designs for video super-resolution by addressing limitations such as lack of data locality and feature alignment \[[24](http://arxiv.org/abs/2106.06847)\]. These innovations highlight the growing trend of using transformers to handle the complexities of video restoration tasks.
