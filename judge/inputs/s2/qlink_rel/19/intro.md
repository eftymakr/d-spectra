## Related Works

Speech-driven 3D facial animation has been a focal point in computer vision and machine learning research, aiming to generate realistic facial expressions that match speech content and emotional tone. Early approaches often relied on handcrafted features and rule-based systems, which were limited in their ability to capture the complex dynamics of facial motion \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2112.05329)\]. With the advent of deep learning, several works have explored neural networks to model the relationship between audio and visual data.

A significant body of work has focused on improving lip synchronization and facial expression accuracy. For instance, VOCA \[[8](http://arxiv.org/abs/1905.03079)\] introduced a 4D face dataset and a neural network that factors identity from facial motion, enabling realistic animation of a wide range of adult faces. This work laid the foundation for later methods that emphasized identity-specific speaking styles and facial idiosyncrasies \[[4](http://arxiv.org/abs/2301.00023)\]. Similarly, Imitator \[[4](http://arxiv.org/abs/2301.00023)\] proposed a style-agnostic transformer to learn identity-specific details from a short input video, significantly improving Lip-Sync performance by 49%.

Another line of research has addressed the challenge of emotional disentanglement in speech-driven facial animation. EmoTalk \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/2303.11089)\] introduced an end-to-end neural network to disentangle emotions from speech content, generating rich 3D facial expressions. This approach was further refined in EMOTE \[[9](http://arxiv.org/abs/2306.08990)\], which explicitly controls emotional expression while maintaining lip-sync, using decoupled losses for speech and emotion. These methods highlight the importance of disentangling emotional and content-related features for more expressive animations.

The integration of self-supervised learning has also been a key advancement. FaceFormer \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2112.05329)\] proposed a Transformer-based autoregressive model that encodes long-term audio context and autoregressively predicts 3D face meshes. This model leverages self-supervised pre-trained speech representations to address data scarcity issues. Similarly, SelfTalk \[[6](http://arxiv.org/abs/2306.10799)\] introduced a self-supervised framework that learns 3D talking faces through a commutative training diagram, enabling cross-modal feature exchange between audio, text, and lip shape.

Recent methods have also explored the use of diffusion models for non-deterministic facial animation. FaceDiffuser \[[24](http://arxiv.org/abs/2309.11306)\] introduced a diffusion-based approach to generate speech-driven facial animations, trained on both 3D vertex and blendshape datasets. This method addresses the deterministic nature of previous models, allowing for more natural and varied facial expressions.
