## Related Works

Opinion mining has garnered significant attention for its ability to extract insights from textual data, particularly in the context of user feedback \[[17](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/96761dc3d8adaeff44eb9c07501eb3109802ee4b)\]. While much of the research focuses on sentiment polarity—such as classifying text as positive, negative, or neutral—fine-grained emotion classification remains underexplored, especially in app reviews \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2005.00547)\]. This gap is addressed by several recent works that have attempted to develop more nuanced emotion detection systems.

The GoEmotions dataset \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2005.00547)\] represents a significant contribution to the field, offering a large-scale, manually annotated corpus of Reddit comments with fine-grained emotion labels. This dataset includes 58k English comments labeled for 27 emotion categories or neutral, and it demonstrates high annotation quality through Principal Component Analysis. However, this dataset is not tailored specifically for app reviews, which present unique challenges in terms of language and context \[[18](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/3fb416be9e59b35c7cc91cd11b5b3f3f5491f13a)\].

Similarly, the EmoBank dataset \[[5](http://arxiv.org/abs/2205.01996)\] focuses on dimensional emotion analysis using the Valence-Arousal-Dominance (VAD) framework. It distinguishes between writer’s and reader’s emotions, which is an important distinction for understanding how emotions are perceived rather than expressed. While this work is valuable, it does not address the specific challenges of app review analysis, such as the need for domain-specific annotations \[[12](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/7d0531aaaa4dbf4033da964289843e7812262702)\].

The SemEval-2018 Task 1: Affect in Tweets \[[10](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/66c8ab0b3b7698cf5333c3f7350a6eaa5302294b)\] explores the classification of affect in social media content, including emotion intensity, valence, and emotion classification. This work highlights the importance of multi-lingual and multi-task approaches, but again, it is not focused on app reviews. Nevertheless, the techniques developed for this task could be adapted for app review analysis.

In the context of app reviews, the work by \[[18](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/3fb416be9e59b35c7cc91cd11b5b3f3f5491f13a)\] explores the classification of app reviews into bug reports, feature requests, or praise. This task is closely related to sentiment analysis, as it involves identifying the intent behind user feedback. However, it does not focus on fine-grained emotion classification, which is the core of this study.

The CROWDSOURCING A WORD–EMOTION ASSOCIATION LEXICON \[[13](http://arxiv.org/abs/1308.6297)\] presents a method for generating a large, high-quality word-emotion lexicon using crowdsourcing. This work is relevant to the development of lexicons for emotion analysis, which can be used to improve the performance of sentiment and emotion classification models.

### <a id='category-fine-grained-emotion-classification'></a>Fine-Grained Emotion Classification: 5 papers found.
The category of fine-grained emotion classification has seen significant advancements, particularly in the development of large-scale annotated datasets and the application of deep learning models to capture nuanced emotional expressions. Researchers have highlighted the importance of fine-grained emotion labeling for improving applications such as empathetic chatbots and detecting harmful online behavior \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2005.00547)\]. The GoEmotions dataset, containing 58k Reddit comments labeled for 27 emotion categories, represents a major contribution, demonstrating high annotation quality and generalization across domains \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2005.00547)\]. Similarly, another study introduced a very large dataset achieving state-of-the-art results on 24 fine-grained emotion types with an average accuracy of 87.58%, and further extended the task to model Plutchik’s 8 primary emotion dimensions with superior accuracy \[[7](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/6a6f1b8ef3eefaf8027a76134859b44886a5f795)\]. These efforts address the lack of large labeled datasets for social media and other domains, where emotion detection remains challenging \[[4](http://arxiv.org/abs/2005.11882)\]. Additionally, researchers have explored the annotation of aesthetic emotions in poetry, focusing on reader elicited responses rather than textual content, and developed a structured annotation framework with acceptable inter-annotator agreement \[[5](http://arxiv.org/abs/2205.01996)\]. These studies collectively emphasize the need for more detailed emotion categorization and the challenges of capturing mixed and complex emotional responses in real-world data.

### <a id='category-emotion-intensity-and-valence'></a>Emotion Intensity and Valence: 1 papers found.
The category "Emotion Intensity and Valence" is central to the task of analyzing affect in textual data, particularly in the context of social media and app reviews. This category encompasses both the intensity of emotions expressed and the valence, or sentiment, associated with those emotions. As highlighted in the SemEval-2018 Task 1, emotion intensity regression and ordinal classification, along with valence regression and classification, are critical subtasks in understanding the emotional states conveyed in tweets \[[10](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/66c8ab0b3b7698cf5333c3f7350a6eaa5302294b)\]. These tasks require not only identifying the presence of emotions but also quantifying their strength and overall sentiment, which can range from highly positive to highly negative. The complexity of these tasks is further compounded by the need to account for variations in language, cultural context, and individual expression, making them challenging to automate. While some studies have explored the use of large language models to assist in emotion annotation, as seen in the evaluation of automated pipelines for app reviews, the accuracy and consistency of these models remain limited due to the nuanced and often ambiguous nature of emotional expression \[[10](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/66c8ab0b3b7698cf5333c3f7350a6eaa5302294b)\]. Therefore, the development of structured annotation frameworks and annotated datasets, as emphasized in the paper, is essential for improving the reliability and feasibility of emotion analysis in real-world applications.
