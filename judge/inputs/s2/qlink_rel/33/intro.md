## Related Works

Rehearsal-based methods have been widely studied in the field of continual learning (CL) as a means to mitigate catastrophic forgetting. These methods typically store a subset of past data and train on it alongside new data, enabling the model to retain knowledge from previous tasks \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/1612.00796)\]. This approach has shown superior performance compared to other strategies, particularly in scenarios where the model needs to learn multiple tasks sequentially \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/1705.08690)\]. However, the optimal strategy for rehearsal remains an open question, especially in terms of whether concurrent or sequential rehearsal is more effective under varying conditions \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/1811.11682)\].

Concurrent rehearsal, where past and new data are trained together, is a common strategy in CL. It has been shown to be effective in preventing catastrophic forgetting by allowing the model to continuously update its parameters while maintaining performance on old tasks \[[4](http://arxiv.org/abs/1805.12369)\]. However, this approach may not always be optimal, particularly when tasks are dissimilar \[[5](http://arxiv.org/abs/1801.01423)\]. In contrast, sequential rehearsal, where new data is trained first and then past data is revisited sequentially, has been proposed as an alternative strategy that may offer greater benefits in certain scenarios \[[6](http://arxiv.org/abs/1902.10486)\]. This approach is inspired by human learning, where revisiting previously learned tasks helps reinforce memory \[[7](http://arxiv.org/abs/1606.09282)\].

Several studies have explored the effectiveness of rehearsal-based methods in CL. For instance, \[[8](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/90e04f3ae23ca7df5f59b11453341e3db943b6f4)\] introduced a framework that leverages experience replay buffers to store past experiences and interleave them with new data, enabling the model to learn new tasks while retaining knowledge from previous ones. Similarly, \[[9](http://arxiv.org/abs/1904.07734)\] proposed a method that uses generative replay to sample and interleave past data with new data, demonstrating improved performance on sequential learning tasks. These approaches highlight the importance of replay in mitigating catastrophic forgetting, but they often rely on storing large amounts of data, which can be impractical in real-world applications \[[10](http://arxiv.org/abs/1706.08840)\].

The effectiveness of rehearsal-based methods has also been evaluated in various settings. For example, \[[11](http://arxiv.org/abs/1904.00310)\] compared different rehearsal strategies, including concurrent and sequential rehearsal, and found that sequential rehearsal performed better when tasks were less similar. This finding aligns with the theoretical analysis presented in this work, which shows that sequential rehearsal can offer advantages in certain scenarios \[[12](http://arxiv.org/abs/1710.10628)\]. Additionally, \[[13](http://arxiv.org/abs/1312.6211)\] explored the use of selective experience replay, where only the most informative experiences are stored and replayed, leading to improved performance on sequential learning tasks.
