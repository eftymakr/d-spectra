## Related Works

Speech-driven facial animation has been a focal point in computer vision and audio processing research, with numerous approaches aiming to generate realistic 3D facial animations from audio inputs. Early methods primarily focused on lip synchronization, often using handcrafted features or simple regression models \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2303.11089)\]. However, these approaches lacked the ability to capture complex facial expressions and emotional nuances \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/2112.05329)\]. More recent works have explored deep learning techniques, leveraging neural networks to map audio signals to facial movements \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/2104.08223)\]. For instance, \[[4](http://arxiv.org/abs/2104.07452)\] introduced a Transformer-based model, FaceFormer, which encodes long-term audio context and autoregressively predicts 3D face meshes, significantly improving lip synchronization and facial expression accuracy.

The challenge of disentangling content and emotion in speech-driven facial animation has also been addressed by several studies. EmoTalk \[[5](http://arxiv.org/abs/1807.07860)\] proposed an end-to-end neural network that disentangles emotions from speech content, enabling the generation of rich 3D facial expressions. This approach uses an emotion disentangling encoder (EDE) and an emotion-guided feature fusion decoder to achieve controllable emotional styles \[[5](http://arxiv.org/abs/1807.07860)\]. Similarly, MeshTalk \[[6](http://arxiv.org/abs/2306.10799)\] introduced a categorical latent space for facial animation, disentangling audio-correlated and audio-uncorrelated information to produce realistic facial movements, including eye blinks and brow motion \[[6](http://arxiv.org/abs/2306.10799)\].

Another key challenge in this field is the scarcity of 3D emotional talking face data. To address this, researchers have explored the use of facial blendshapes and self-supervised learning. For example, \[[7](http://arxiv.org/abs/2104.11116)\] proposed a method that leverages facial blendshapes to reconstruct plausible 3D faces from 2D emotional data, contributing a large-scale 3D emotional talking face dataset (3D-ETF) \[[7](http://arxiv.org/abs/2104.11116)\]. Additionally, \[[8](http://arxiv.org/abs/2205.15278)\] introduced a self-supervised framework, SelfTalk, that involves cross-modal learning to generate 3D talking faces without relying on labeled data, enhancing visual accuracy and reducing domain gaps \[[8](http://arxiv.org/abs/2205.15278)\].

The importance of emotional expression in facial animation has also been emphasized in recent works. Emotional Video Portraits (EVP) \[[9](http://arxiv.org/abs/2301.02379)\] introduced a system for synthesizing high-quality video portraits with vivid emotional dynamics, using cross-reconstructed emotion disentanglement to generate dynamic 2D emotional facial landmarks \[[9](http://arxiv.org/abs/2301.02379)\]. This approach enables the synthesis of realistic emotional expressions while maintaining lip synchronization \[[9](http://arxiv.org/abs/2301.02379)\].
