## Related Works

Deep sequential recommendation models have seen significant advancements, driven by the need to capture complex user behavior patterns and efficiently handle variable-length sequences. Traditional approaches such as Markov Chains (MCs) and Recurrent Neural Networks (RNNs) have been widely used, but they face limitations in modeling long-range dependencies and capturing diverse user preferences \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/1808.09781)\]. MC-based methods excel in sparse datasets due to their simplicity, while RNNs offer better performance in denser environments \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/1808.09781)\]. However, both struggle with computational efficiency and capturing nuanced interaction patterns \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/1808.09781)\].

Recent work has introduced attention-based models, such as SASRec \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/1808.09781)\], which leverages self-attention to capture long-term semantics while maintaining model efficiency. SASRec improves upon MC and RNN approaches by using attention mechanisms to identify relevant items from a user’s history, leading to better performance on both sparse and dense datasets \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/1808.09781)\]. Similarly, BERT4Rec extends this idea by employing bidirectional self-attention to model user behavior sequences from both left and right contexts, improving representation power and avoiding information leakage \[[4](http://arxiv.org/abs/1904.06690)\]. This bidirectional approach is further refined in LinRec, which introduces a linear complexity attention mechanism to address the quadratic computational cost of traditional attention methods, while preserving learning capabilities \[[5](http://arxiv.org/abs/2411.01537)\].

Efforts to improve efficiency have also led to the development of state space models (SSMs), which offer linear-time sequence modeling with structured state matrices \[[9](http://arxiv.org/abs/2312.00752)\]. Mamba, a selective SSM, has been adapted for sequential recommendation, offering significant improvements in both effectiveness and efficiency \[[8](http://arxiv.org/abs/2403.03900)\]. Mamba4Rec, for instance, leverages the Mamba block to process long sequences efficiently, outperforming RNN- and attention-based baselines \[[8](http://arxiv.org/abs/2403.03900)\]. Similarly, SSD4Rec integrates Mamba into a structured state space duality model, achieving near-linear scalability and state-of-the-art performance on benchmark datasets \[[10](http://arxiv.org/abs/2409.01192)\].

To further enhance modeling capabilities, hybrid approaches have been proposed. For example, Caser uses convolutional filters to extract local features from user sequences, enabling the capture of both general preferences and sequential patterns \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/1809.07426)\]. ### <a id='category-attention-based-sequential-recommendation-models'></a>Attention-Based Sequential Recommendation Models: 4 papers found.
Attention-based sequential recommendation models have emerged as a critical area of research, aiming to better capture the complex dynamics of user behavior. These models leverage self-attention mechanisms to effectively model long-range dependencies and uncover both similar and diverse item relationships \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/1808.09781)\]. For instance, SASRec introduces a self-attention based approach that balances the strengths of Markov Chains and RNNs by focusing on relevant actions in a user's history, leading to improved performance on both sparse and dense datasets \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/1808.09781)\]. Similarly, BERT4Rec extends this idea by employing a bidirectional self-attention mechanism, allowing the model to consider both left and right contexts of items in a sequence, thereby overcoming the limitations of unidirectional architectures \[[4](http://arxiv.org/abs/1904.06690)\]. The NARM framework further enhances this by integrating an attention mechanism with a hybrid encoder to capture the user's main purpose in a session, demonstrating the potential of attention mechanisms to model both sequential behavior and session-specific intent \[[12](http://arxiv.org/abs/1711.04725)\]. Additionally, FEARec introduces a novel approach by shifting the focus to the frequency domain, using a ramp structure and auto-correlation to explicitly learn both low- and high-frequency information, as well as periodic patterns in user behavior \[[14](http://arxiv.org/abs/2304.09184)\]. These models collectively highlight the importance of attention mechanisms in sequential recommendation, addressing challenges such as sequence length variations and diverse interaction patterns.

### <a id='category-temporal-modeling-in-sequential-recommendation'></a>Temporal Modeling in Sequential Recommendation: 5 papers found.
Temporal modeling in sequential recommendation has been a focal point in recent research, with various approaches aiming to capture the dynamic nature of user interactions. Traditional methods such as Markov Chains and Recurrent Neural Networks (RNNs) have been widely used to model sequential patterns, but they often neglect the temporal aspect by treating interaction histories as ordered sequences without considering the time intervals between actions \[[7](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/9c7a455b1e48d01a99e58e884a6c1acb75074ad0)\]. To address this limitation, models like TiSASRec have been introduced, which explicitly incorporate timestamps and time intervals into the modeling process, enhancing the accuracy of next-item predictions by accounting for the actual timing of user interactions \[[7](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/9c7a455b1e48d01a99e58e884a6c1acb75074ad0)\]. Similarly, the Caser model leverages convolutional filters to capture local sequential patterns from item sequences, effectively embedding recent interactions into a time-latent space to better reflect user preferences \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/1809.07426)\]. The SASRec model, inspired by the Transformer architecture, has also gained traction for its ability to handle long-range dependencies in sequential data, though it lacks personalized user embeddings \[[25](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/5aed438fecf702ead9c0cddfa9a80bd00cff0138)\]. To overcome this, the Personalized Transformer (SSE-PT) model was developed, integrating personalized embeddings and achieving significant improvements in recommendation accuracy \[[25](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/5aed438fecf702ead9c0cddfa9a80bd00cff0138)\]. Additionally, session-based recommendation approaches, such as those using RNNs, have been adapted to handle short session data effectively, offering more accurate recommendations by modeling the entire session rather than relying on long user histories \[[19](http://arxiv.org/abs/1511.06939)\]. These advancements highlight the ongoing efforts to improve temporal modeling in sequential recommendation, emphasizing the importance of capturing both the order and timing of user interactions for more accurate and personalized recommendations.

### <a id='category-state-space-models-for-sequential-recommendation'></a>State Space Models for Sequential Recommendation: 7 papers found.
State space models (SSMs) have emerged as a promising approach for sequential recommendation, addressing the limitations of traditional attention-based and RNN-based models in terms of computational efficiency and long-range dependency modeling \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/2406.02638)\]. These models, inspired by control theory, provide a structured framework for capturing temporal dynamics through state compression, enabling efficient parallel computation and linear complexity in sequence processing \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/2406.02638)\]. Recent works such as EchoMamba4Rec \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/2406.02638)\], RecMamba \[[6](http://arxiv.org/abs/2403.16371)\], and Mamba4Rec \[[8](http://arxiv.org/abs/2403.03900)\] have demonstrated the effectiveness of SSMs in handling lifelong user sequences, with notable improvements in both efficiency and performance. For instance, RecMamba achieves performance comparable to existing models while significantly reducing training time and memory costs \[[6](http://arxiv.org/abs/2403.16371)\]. Similarly, Mamba4Rec leverages selective SSMs to balance effectiveness and efficiency, outperforming both RNN- and attention-based baselines \[[8](http://arxiv.org/abs/2403.03900)\]. Additionally, SSD4Rec introduces a novel framework that integrates Mamba with hardware-aware designs, enabling efficient variable-length sequence modeling \[[10](http://arxiv.org/abs/2409.01192)\]. The S4 model further advances SSMs by introducing a structured parameterization that allows for efficient computation while preserving theoretical strengths, achieving state-of-the-art results on long-range sequence tasks \[[11](http://arxiv.org/abs/2111.00396)\]. These developments highlight the growing interest in SSMs for sequential recommendation, emphasizing their potential to address the challenges of modeling user behavior in real-world applications \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/2406.02638)\]\[[6](http://arxiv.org/abs/2403.16371)\]\[[8](http://arxiv.org/abs/2403.03900)\]\[[10](http://arxiv.org/abs/2409.01192)\]\[[11](http://arxiv.org/abs/2111.00396)\].

Efficiency improvements in sequential recommendation have been a focal point in recent research, with several approaches addressing the computational challenges of traditional attention mechanisms. For instance, LinRec introduces an L2-Normalized Linear Attention mechanism that reduces the computational complexity from quadratic to linear while preserving the learning capabilities of dot-product attention \[[5](http://arxiv.org/abs/2411.01537)\]. Similarly, LRURec leverages linear recurrent units to enable fast inference and incremental processing, further enhancing efficiency through parallelizable training and reduced model size \[[15](http://arxiv.org/abs/2310.02367)\]. These methods are complemented by FMLP-Rec, which integrates filtering algorithms to mitigate noise in logged user behavior data, improving model robustness and performance \[[16](http://arxiv.org/abs/2202.13556)\]. Together, these works highlight the ongoing efforts to balance efficiency with effectiveness in sequential recommendation, particularly in handling long sequences and diverse user behaviors.

### <a id='category-graph-based-sequential-recommendation-models'></a>Graph-Based Sequential Recommendation Models: 2 papers found.
Graph-based sequential recommendation models have been increasingly explored to address the challenges of modeling complex user behavior and dynamic preferences. These models aim to capture both implicit and explicit interactions between users and items, as well as among items themselves, to improve the accuracy of next-item recommendations \[[13](http://arxiv.org/abs/2106.14226)\]. One key challenge is the noise and sparsity in user historical sequences, which makes it difficult to extract meaningful patterns \[[13](http://arxiv.org/abs/2106.14226)\]. To tackle this, researchers have proposed methods such as SURGE, which leverages graph neural networks to re-construct item sequences into interest graphs, enabling the identification of core user preferences through clustering and graph convolutional propagation \[[13](http://arxiv.org/abs/2106.14226)\]. Similarly, TransRec introduces a unified approach to model third-order interactions between users, items, and sequential patterns by embedding items into a 'transition space' and using translation vectors to represent user behavior \[[17](http://arxiv.org/abs/1707.02410)\]. These approaches highlight the importance of integrating both user-item and item-item interactions to better capture sequential dynamics and improve recommendation accuracy. Additionally, they emphasize the need for efficient modeling of long sequences and dynamic user preferences, which remains an active research area in sequential recommendation systems.
