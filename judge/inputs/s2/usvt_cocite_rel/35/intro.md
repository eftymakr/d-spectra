## Related Works

Protein dynamics are essential for understanding biological functions and properties, yet traditional methods such as molecular dynamics (MD) simulations are computationally expensive and time-consuming \[[1](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/21a4afa74caf78e28880b7fe4c30a09a5f9c14e3)\]. Recent advances in generative modeling have enabled more efficient and accurate protein structure prediction and conformation sampling by learning distributions over crystallographic structures \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/2402.04845)\]. However, integrating physical supervision into these data-driven approaches remains a challenge, as standard energy-based objectives often lead to intractable optimization \[[3](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/2eb1ed9d4baf548391d8220c0a8a3d0be79845ed)\]. This section reviews related works that address these challenges and contribute to the field of protein conformation generation.

Several studies have explored the use of generative models for protein structure prediction and conformation sampling. For instance, Boltzmann generators use deep learning and statistical mechanics to sample equilibrium states of many-body systems \[[4](http://arxiv.org/abs/2304.02198)\]. These models are trained on energy functions and learn to generate unbiased equilibrium samples from their equilibrium state \[[5](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/dc32a984b651256a8ec282be52310e6bd33d9815)\]. Similarly, diffusion models have been applied to protein structure generation, such as EigenFold, which models the structure as a system of harmonic oscillators and generates a cascading-resolution generative process \[[6](http://arxiv.org/abs/2306.03117)\]. Another approach, Str2Str, leverages a score-based framework for zero-shot protein conformation sampling without relying on simulation data during training \[[7](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/2cfc26b4af99f195b433fa7aa00f221b111c7cd4)\].

AlphaFold and its variants have also contributed significantly to the field of protein structure prediction. AlphaFold2 demonstrated high accuracy in predicting protein structures, even in cases where no similar structure is available \[[8](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/c49a0912595a1cc70aab63524f64ed08c92194a8)\]. More recently, AlphaFold3 was introduced with an updated diffusion-based architecture capable of predicting the joint structure of protein complexes \[[9](http://arxiv.org/abs/2403.14088)\]. These models have been further extended to generate protein ensembles, such as AlphaFlow and ESMFlow, which use flow-based generative modeling to learn and sample conformational landscapes \[[10](http://arxiv.org/abs/2209.15611)\].

In addition to structure prediction, there has been growing interest in modeling protein conformational ensembles and flexibility. For example, the idpGAN model uses a generative adversarial network to predict sequence-dependent coarse-grained ensembles of intrinsically disordered peptides \[[11](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/98926d43356e87c22c82efc132dcaaac1ff40ebe)\]. Similarly, the ConfDiff model incorporates a force-guided network to generate protein conformations with high fidelity and diversity \[[12](http://arxiv.org/abs/2302.00600)\]. These models highlight the importance of incorporating physical priors to guide the generation process and ensure the sampled conformations are physically plausible.
