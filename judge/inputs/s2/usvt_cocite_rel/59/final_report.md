## Related Works

Fairness in recommendation systems has gained significant attention in recent years, with a growing body of research addressing the trade-offs between recommendation accuracy and fairness. Early work focused primarily on user-side fairness, often neglecting the impact on item providers \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2204.08085)\]. However, recent studies have emphasized the importance of considering both user and item fairness in recommendation systems, as they operate in two-sided markets where both sides are stakeholders \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/2005.14713)\]. 

Several approaches have been proposed to incorporate fairness into the re-ranking process. One notable work introduces a learning-to-rank approach that enforces merit-based fairness guarantees for groups of items, ensuring amortized group fairness while learning the ranking function from implicit feedback data \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/1903.00780)\]. This method dynamically adapts fairness and utility constraints as more data becomes available, demonstrating robustness and practicality \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/1903.00780)\]. 

Another line of research focuses on fairness through pairwise comparisons, proposing novel metrics for evaluating fairness in rankings and regularizers to improve fairness during model training \[[4](http://arxiv.org/abs/1809.02921)\]. This approach has been applied to large-scale production systems, resulting in significant improvements in pairwise fairness \[[4](http://arxiv.org/abs/1809.02921)\]. 

The concept of provider fairness has also been explored, with some works addressing the need to ensure fair exposure for item providers while maintaining recommendation quality \[[5](http://arxiv.org/abs/2002.10764)\]. A recent study proposes a fairness-aware re-ranking algorithm that balances accuracy and provider-side fairness, incorporating personalized user tolerance towards diversity \[[6](http://arxiv.org/abs/2005.12974)\]. This approach shows that fairness can be improved with only a slight sacrifice in accuracy, while being attentive to individual user differences \[[6](http://arxiv.org/abs/2005.12974)\].

In addition to user and provider fairness, there has been growing interest in long-term fairness in recommendation systems, where fairness constraints must adapt to dynamic changes in item popularity over time \[[7](http://arxiv.org/abs/1802.07281)\]. A novel framework has been proposed that uses fairness-constrained reinforcement learning to model the recommendation problem as a Constrained Markov Decision Process (CMDP), allowing the model to dynamically adjust its recommendation policy to maintain fairness \[[7](http://arxiv.org/abs/1802.07281)\].

Furthermore, the idea of fairness through awareness has been explored, where fairness is achieved by ensuring similar individuals are treated similarly in the ranking process \[[8](http://arxiv.org/abs/2101.03584)\]. This approach has been extended to include fairness-aware ranking in search and recommendation systems, with applications in domains such as LinkedIn Talent Search \[[9](http://arxiv.org/abs/1805.01788)\]. These frameworks allow for the enforcement of fairness criteria such as equality of opportunity and demographic parity \[[9](http://arxiv.org/abs/1805.01788)\].
