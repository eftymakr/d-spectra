## Related Works

Time series forecasting has been a cornerstone in financial applications, with recent advancements focusing on improving accuracy and robustness in the presence of uncertainty. Traditional approaches such as autoregressive models and Gaussian processes have been widely used but often struggle with capturing complex patterns in high-dimensional and long-term sequences \[[13](http://arxiv.org/abs/1703.07015)\]. This has led to the emergence of deep learning models, particularly those based on Transformer architectures, which have demonstrated superior performance in modeling long-range dependencies \[[21](http://arxiv.org/abs/2012.07436)\]. 

Several works have explored the integration of deep learning with forecasting tasks, emphasizing the importance of handling uncertainty and improving model interpretability. For instance, the Temporal Fusion Transformer (TFT) introduces an interpretable architecture that combines recurrent layers with self-attention mechanisms to learn temporal relationships at different scales \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/1912.09363)\]. Similarly, the Informer model addresses the computational challenges of Transformers in long-sequence forecasting by introducing a ProbSparse self-attention mechanism, which reduces time and memory complexity while maintaining performance \[[21](http://arxiv.org/abs/2012.07436)\]. 

The challenge of aligning forecasting with optimization in financial applications has also been addressed in prior research. The N-BEATS model, for example, proposes a deep neural architecture with backward and forward residual links, achieving state-of-the-art performance across diverse time series datasets \[[27](http://arxiv.org/abs/1905.10437)\]. This model is notable for its interpretability and adaptability to various domains. Additionally, the DeepAR model employs autoregressive recurrent networks to produce probabilistic forecasts, demonstrating significant accuracy improvements over classical methods \[[7](http://arxiv.org/abs/1704.04110)\]. 

In the context of financial forecasting, the introduction of uncertainty-aware models has become critical. The Risk-aware Time-Series Predict-and-Allocate (RTS-PnO) framework, which is the focus of this paper, builds upon these advancements by introducing a novel approach that aligns forecasting with allocation decisions while being agnostic to the underlying forecasting model \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2211.14730)\]. This framework incorporates end-to-end training with objective alignment measurement and adaptive forecasting uncertainty calibration, addressing the limitations of prediction-only and Predict-then-Optimize approaches \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2211.14730)\].

Furthermore, the importance of multi-scale modeling in time series forecasting has been highlighted in several studies. The Multi-Scale Transformer Pyramid Network (MTPNet) and the TimeMixer model both propose architectures that effectively capture temporal dependencies at multiple scales, improving performance on datasets with rich fine-scale information \[[25](http://arxiv.org/abs/2308.11946)\]\[[19](http://arxiv.org/abs/2405.14616)\]. These models demonstrate the value of decomposing time series into multiple scales to better understand and predict complex patterns.

Deep learning approaches have significantly advanced time series forecasting by addressing challenges such as long-term dependencies, non-stationarity, and computational efficiency \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/2205.14415)\]. Models like PatchTST introduce efficient Transformer-based designs that segment time series into subseries-level patches, enabling better computation and memory usage while retaining local semantic information \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2211.14730)\]. Similarly, Non-stationary Transformers tackle over-stationarization by incorporating De-stationary Attention to recover non-stationary patterns, leading to improved performance on real-world datasets \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/2205.14415)\]. The iTransformer model further enhances Transformer-based forecasting by inverting dimensions to capture multivariate correlations and improve generalization across different variates \[[9](http://arxiv.org/abs/2310.06625)\]. For long-term forecasting, Autoformer introduces an Auto-Correlation mechanism that outperforms self-attention in both efficiency and accuracy, achieving state-of-the-art results on multiple benchmarks \[[10](http://arxiv.org/abs/2106.13008)\]. Additionally, lightweight models like SparseTSF focus on minimizing computational resources while maintaining competitive performance through techniques like cross-period sparse forecasting \[[11](http://arxiv.org/abs/2405.00946)\]. These advancements highlight the growing importance of deep learning in handling complex time series data across diverse applications \[[16](http://arxiv.org/abs/2004.10240)\].

### <a id='category-performance-evaluation-and-baselines'></a>Performance Evaluation and Baselines: 5 papers found.
The Related Works section for the paper's category on Performance Evaluation and Baselines highlights the evolving landscape of time series forecasting and its challenges. Recent studies have shown that while Transformer-based models have gained popularity for their ability to capture semantic correlations in long sequences, they face inherent limitations in preserving temporal order due to their permutation-invariant self-attention mechanism, leading to significant temporal information loss \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2211.14730)\]. This issue is further exacerbated when applying large language models (LLMs) to time series forecasting, as ablation studies reveal that LLMs do not effectively represent sequential dependencies and often perform similarly to or worse than simpler attention-based models \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/1912.09363)\]. To address these challenges, researchers have proposed alternative approaches, such as one-layer linear models like LTSF-Linear, which surprisingly outperform complex Transformer-based models in empirical evaluations across multiple datasets \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/2205.14415)\]. Additionally, the lack of comprehensive time series archives for forecasting has hindered the development and comparison of global forecasting models, prompting efforts to create standardized benchmarks with diverse datasets and evaluation metrics \[[4](http://arxiv.org/abs/1809.04206)\]. The M4 Competition further emphasizes the need for rigorous evaluation frameworks, expanding on previous competitions by increasing the number of series, forecasting methods, and incorporating prediction intervals \[[5](http://arxiv.org/abs/2205.13504)\]. These studies collectively underscore the importance of evaluating forecasting models not only on accuracy but also on their ability to handle uncertainty and adapt to different time series characteristics.
