## Related Works

Anomaly detection in industrial settings has garnered significant attention due to its critical role in ensuring operational safety and quality control. Early approaches focused on process-based anomaly detection, where physical properties of the system were used to identify deviations \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2012.03586)\]. These methods relied on predefined invariant rules derived from domain knowledge, which were manually intensive and often sub-optimal in complex systems \[[3](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/de39d8209e4848bdb9e7268080c87d7384525d11)\]. Recent advancements have shifted toward data-driven approaches, leveraging machine learning and deep learning techniques to automatically learn and detect anomalies from operational data \[[5](http://arxiv.org/abs/2301.11514)\].

Several studies have explored the use of deep learning for industrial anomaly detection. For instance, \[[6](http://arxiv.org/abs/1911.04831)\] introduced a sequence-to-sequence neural network approach for detecting anomalies in operational data of industrial control systems, achieving a high detection rate on the SWaT dataset. Similarly, \[[8](http://arxiv.org/abs/1806.08110)\] proposed a method using 1D convolutional networks to detect cyber attacks in ICS, demonstrating superior performance over recurrent networks in time series prediction tasks. These methods rely on normal datasets to understand the system's behavior and detect outliers, which aligns with the cold-start problem addressed in \[[4](http://arxiv.org/abs/2106.08265)\], where PatchCore was introduced to achieve state-of-the-art performance on the MVTec AD benchmark with an AUROC score of up to 99.6%.

The MVTec 3D-AD dataset \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/2112.09045)\] has become a benchmark for unsupervised 3D anomaly detection, providing a comprehensive set of test samples with precise ground-truth annotations. This dataset has been used to evaluate various methods, including \[[7](http://arxiv.org/abs/2303.00601)\], which proposed M3DM, a multimodal anomaly detection model that outperforms existing methods on both detection and segmentation precision. The use of hybrid fusion schemes and point feature alignment in M3DM highlights the importance of integrating multiple modalities for improved detection accuracy.

Another important direction is the development of robust anomaly detection frameworks that can resist adversarial attacks. \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2012.03586)\] demonstrated that many existing anomaly detectors are vulnerable to synthetic sensor spoofing, reducing their recall and making them unable to reliably detect anomalies. This highlights the need for more complete attacks in datasets and more critical analysis of process-based detectors. \[[9](http://arxiv.org/abs/1709.06397)\] further explored stealthy attacks using deep learning frameworks, showing that attackers can generate high-quality spoofing signals that bypass anomaly detectors while achieving specific attack goals.

In summary, while many deep learning-based approaches have shown promising results in industrial anomaly detection, challenges remain in terms of computational efficiency, deployment on embedded devices, and robustness against adversarial attacks.
