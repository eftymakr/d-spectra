## Related Works

Anomaly detection in industrial settings has garnered significant attention due to its critical role in ensuring operational safety and quality control. Early approaches focused on process-based anomaly detection, where physical properties of the system were used to identify deviations \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2012.03586)\]. These methods relied on predefined invariant rules derived from domain knowledge, which were manually intensive and often sub-optimal in complex systems \[[3](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/de39d8209e4848bdb9e7268080c87d7384525d11)\]. Recent advancements have shifted toward data-driven approaches, leveraging machine learning and deep learning techniques to automatically learn and detect anomalies from operational data \[[5](http://arxiv.org/abs/2301.11514)\].

Several studies have explored the use of deep learning for industrial anomaly detection. For instance, \[[6](http://arxiv.org/abs/1911.04831)\] introduced a sequence-to-sequence neural network approach for detecting anomalies in operational data of industrial control systems, achieving a high detection rate on the SWaT dataset. Similarly, \[[8](http://arxiv.org/abs/1806.08110)\] proposed a method using 1D convolutional networks to detect cyber attacks in ICS, demonstrating superior performance over recurrent networks in time series prediction tasks. These methods rely on normal datasets to understand the system's behavior and detect outliers, which aligns with the cold-start problem addressed in \[[4](http://arxiv.org/abs/2106.08265)\], where PatchCore was introduced to achieve state-of-the-art performance on the MVTec AD benchmark with an AUROC score of up to 99.6%.

The MVTec 3D-AD dataset \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/2112.09045)\] has become a benchmark for unsupervised 3D anomaly detection, providing a comprehensive set of test samples with precise ground-truth annotations. This dataset has been used to evaluate various methods, including \[[7](http://arxiv.org/abs/2303.00601)\], which proposed M3DM, a multimodal anomaly detection model that outperforms existing methods on both detection and segmentation precision. The use of hybrid fusion schemes and point feature alignment in M3DM highlights the importance of integrating multiple modalities for improved detection accuracy.

Another important direction is the development of robust anomaly detection frameworks that can resist adversarial attacks. \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2012.03586)\] demonstrated that many existing anomaly detectors are vulnerable to synthetic sensor spoofing, reducing their recall and making them unable to reliably detect anomalies. This highlights the need for more complete attacks in datasets and more critical analysis of process-based detectors. \[[9](http://arxiv.org/abs/1709.06397)\] further explored stealthy attacks using deep learning frameworks, showing that attackers can generate high-quality spoofing signals that bypass anomaly detectors while achieving specific attack goals.

In summary, while many deep learning-based approaches have shown promising results in industrial anomaly detection, challenges remain in terms of computational efficiency, deployment on embedded devices, and robustness against adversarial attacks. ### <a id='category-sensor-spoofing-and-attack-resistance'></a>Sensor Spoofing and Attack Resistance: 2 papers found.
In the context of sensor spoofing and attack resistance in industrial control systems, recent research has focused on evaluating the vulnerabilities of process-based anomaly detection schemes. A systematic analysis of such systems has revealed that many detectors are susceptible to adversarial spoofing attacks, such as Synthetic Sensor Spoofing, which can manipulate sensor data to evade detection while still achieving attack objectives \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2012.03586)\]. These attacks often reduce the recall of anomaly detection systems, making them unreliable for real-world applications \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2012.03586)\]. Similarly, a deep learning-based framework has been introduced that enables attackers to generate stealthy attacks with minimal prior knowledge of the target system, bypassing black-box anomaly detectors by learning from intercepted sensor and control signals \[[9](http://arxiv.org/abs/1709.06397)\]. These findings highlight the limitations of current detection methods and the need for more robust frameworks that can better capture and enforce physical properties of the system \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2012.03586)\]. The effectiveness of these attacks has been demonstrated through real-world case studies, further emphasizing the urgency for improved security measures in industrial environments \[[9](http://arxiv.org/abs/1709.06397)\].

### <a id='category-adversarial-robustness-in-anomaly-detection'></a>Adversarial Robustness in Anomaly Detection: 1 papers found.
Context: 
	Document 1: [Snippet]: «Recently, reconstruction-based anomaly detection was proposed as an effective technique to detect attacks in dynamic industrial control networks. Unlike classical network anomaly detectors that observe the network traffic, reconstruction-based detectors operate on the measured sensor data, leveraging physical process models learned a priori. In this work, we investigate different approaches to evade prior-work reconstruction-based anomaly detectors by manipulating sensor data so that the attack is concealed. We find that replay attacks (commonly assumed to be very strong) show bad performance (i.e., increasing the number of alarms) if the attacker is constrained to manipulate less than 95% of all features in the system, as hidden correlations between the features are not replicated well. To address this, we propose two novel attacks that manipulate a subset of the sensor readings, leveraging learned physical constraints of the system. Our attacks feature two different attacker models: A white box attacker, which uses an optimization approach with a detection oracle, and a black box attacker, which uses an autoencoder to translate anomalous data into normal data. We evaluate our implementation on two different datasets from the water distribution domain, showing that the detector’s Recall drops from 0.68 to 0.12 by manipulating 4 sensors out of 82 in WADI dataset. In addition, we show that our black box attacks are transferable to different detectors: They work against autoencoder-, LSTM-, and CNN-based detectors. Finally, we implement and demonstrate our attacks on a real industrial testbed to demonstrate their feasibility in real-time.»
