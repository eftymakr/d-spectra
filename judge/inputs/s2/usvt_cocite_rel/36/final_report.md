## Related Works

Deploying large language models (LLMs) on edge devices has been a focal point of recent research due to the increasing demand for privacy-preserving and low-latency AI applications \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2306.00978)\]. Several approaches have been proposed to address the challenges of memory, computation, and energy constraints on resource-limited edge hardware. One notable effort is the development of quantization techniques that reduce model size while preserving performance. AWQ \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2306.00978)\] introduces an activation-aware quantization method that identifies and protects salient weights, enabling up to 4x model size reduction and 3-4x acceleration across various edge platforms. Similarly, SmoothQuant \[[4](http://arxiv.org/abs/2211.10438)\] enables 8-bit weight and activation quantization without significant accuracy loss, achieving up to 1.56x speedup and 2x memory reduction for LLMs. These methods are crucial for enabling efficient inference on edge devices with limited resources.

Efficient memory management is another critical area of research.
