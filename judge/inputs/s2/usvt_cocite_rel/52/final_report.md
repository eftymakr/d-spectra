## Related Works

Dense retrievers have emerged as a powerful alternative to traditional sparse retrieval methods in information retrieval tasks. These models utilize pre-trained language models (e.g., BERT, LLaMA) as backbone encoders, which are fine-tuned via contrastive learning to generate dense representations that enable efficient semantic matching \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2002.08909)\]. Recent studies have questioned the role of fine-tuning versus pre-training in dense retrievers, arguing that retrieval knowledge is primarily acquired during pre-training, and that fine-tuning mainly adjusts neuron activations rather than reorganizing knowledge \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2002.08909)\]. This claim was initially tested using a BERT-based encoder with the Dense Passage Retriever (DPR) as a representative model \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2002.08909)\]. 

This work extends the previous analysis by evaluating different representation approaches, such as CLS token usage versus mean pooling, and exploring various backbone architectures, including encoder-only BERT and decoder-only LLaMA \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2002.08909)\]. Additionally, we test the claim on multiple datasets, including MSMARCO and Natural Questions, to determine if the pattern holds universally \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2002.08909)\]. Our findings confirm that pre-trained knowledge significantly underpins retrieval performance in DPR tuning, with fine-tuning primarily adjusting activation patterns rather than reorganizing knowledge \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2002.08909)\]. However, this pattern does not universally apply, as seen in models like Contriever (mean-pooled) and LLaMA (decoder-based), where fine-tuning plays a more substantial role in knowledge acquisition \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2002.08909)\].

Several studies have explored the effectiveness of different pre-training and fine-tuning strategies for dense retrievers. For instance, coCondenser introduces an unsupervised corpus-level contrastive loss to warm up the passage embedding space, reducing the need for heavy data engineering and large batch training \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/2108.05540)\]. Similarly, the Condenser architecture is designed to pre-train dense encoders by conditioning LM prediction on dense representations, leading to improved performance on various retrieval tasks \[[5](http://arxiv.org/abs/2104.08253)\]. Contrastive learning has also been applied to unsupervised dense retrievers, with models like CLEAR demonstrating strong performance on the BEIR benchmark \[[7](http://arxiv.org/abs/2112.09118)\]. 

The importance of pre-training in dense retrievers is further supported by the work of RoBERTa, which highlights the impact of training data size and hyperparameter choices on model performance \[[4](http://arxiv.org/abs/1907.11692)\]. Additionally, the effectiveness of contrastive learning in dense retrieval is demonstrated by the ANCE model, which constructs negatives from an Approximate Nearest Neighbor (ANN) index to improve retrieval accuracy \[[6](http://arxiv.org/abs/2007.00808)\].
