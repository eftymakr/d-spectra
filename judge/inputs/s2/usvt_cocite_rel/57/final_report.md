## Related Works

Generative retrieval (GR) has gained significant attention in information retrieval (IR) as an alternative to traditional index-retrieve frameworks \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2206.02743)\]. Unlike conventional methods that rely on static indices, GR models directly generate document identifiers (docids) for a given query, enabling end-to-end optimization \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/2308.14968)\]. However, most existing GR approaches are evaluated on static document collections, with limited exploration of their performance in dynamic environments where documents are continuously added \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/2202.06991)\]. This section reviews relevant works that address the challenges of GR in static and dynamic settings, focusing on docid design, model efficiency, and generalization across different corpus types.

Early GR models, such as the Differentiable Search Index (DSI), encode all documents into a single transformer model, allowing direct query-to-docid mapping \[[4](http://arxiv.org/abs/2206.10128)\]. DSI has demonstrated strong performance on benchmark datasets, outperforming dense retrieval methods like BM25 \[[5](http://arxiv.org/abs/2304.04171)\]. However, DSI faces limitations in dynamic settings, as retraining is required when new documents are added \[[6](http://arxiv.org/abs/2208.09257)\]. To address this, DSI++ was proposed, enabling continual learning by incrementally updating the model with new documents while preserving retrieval accuracy for previously indexed documents \[[7](http://arxiv.org/abs/2311.09134)\]. This work highlights the importance of continual learning in GR models for evolving corpora.

The design of docids plays a critical role in GR performance. Numeric-based docids, while efficient, often suffer from poor generalization in dynamic settings due to overfitting on the training set \[[8](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/6682a3679a17d090830635f1c420126d20eae54a)\]. In contrast, text-based docids, which are more semantically aligned with language models, show better performance in dynamic environments \[[9](http://arxiv.org/abs/2208.07652)\]. This aligns with findings from the GenRet model, which learns to tokenize documents into short, semantic docids using a discrete auto-encoding approach \[[10](http://arxiv.org/abs/2204.10628)\]. The semantic richness of text-based docids allows for better alignment with language models, improving retrieval accuracy in dynamic settings \[[11](http://arxiv.org/abs/2212.09744)\].

Several works have explored the use of query generation to improve GR performance, particularly in cross-lingual and dynamic settings. DSI-QG introduces a framework where documents are indexed with potentially relevant queries, enabling the model to connect docids to a broader set of queries \[[12](http://arxiv.org/abs/2305.11841)\]. This approach mitigates data distribution mismatches between indexing and retrieval phases, improving performance on cross-lingual tasks \[[13](http://arxiv.org/abs/2305.15115)\]. Similarly, the NOVO model proposes learnable, interpretable docids that are optimized through training, allowing for better semantic representation and retrieval \[[14](http://arxiv.org/abs/2204.05511)\].

### <a id='category-fine-grained-docid-design-and-its-advantages'></a>Fine-grained DocID Design and Its Advantages: 3 papers found.
The category "Fine-grained DocID Design and Its Advantages" focuses on the design and optimization of document identifiers (docids) in model-based information retrieval systems. Existing approaches often rely on static, numeric-based docids, which are not optimized for retrieval tasks and suffer from semantic limitations \[[8](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/6682a3679a17d090830635f1c420126d20eae54a)\]. To address these issues, researchers have proposed more semantically rich docid designs, such as the two types of docids introduced in Ultron, which are richer in semantics and easier for model inference \[[6](http://arxiv.org/abs/2208.09257)\]. Similarly, the NOVO docids are unique n-gram sets that can be generated in any order and optimized through training to better capture document semantics and relationships \[[8](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/6682a3679a17d090830635f1c420126d20eae54a)\]. These fine-grained docid designs enable more effective retrieval by aligning with the semantic knowledge of language models and improving the memorization of document features \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/2202.06991)\]. Additionally, the Semantic-Enhanced DSI model (SE-DSI) further advances the design of docids by incorporating elaborative descriptions and rehearsal strategies inspired by cognitive psychology, leading to improved retrieval performance \[[13](http://arxiv.org/abs/2305.15115)\]. These studies collectively highlight the importance of fine-grained docid design in enhancing retrieval effectiveness, especially in dynamic corpora where document collections evolve continuously \[[6](http://arxiv.org/abs/2208.09257)\]\[[8](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/6682a3679a17d090830635f1c420126d20eae54a)\]\[[13](http://arxiv.org/abs/2305.15115)\].
