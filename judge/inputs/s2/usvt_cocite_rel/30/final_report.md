## Related Works

Modern databases often trade strong transaction isolation for weak isolation guarantees to achieve low latency and high throughput, particularly in distributed systems \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/1908.04509)\]. This trade-off has led to the emergence of weak isolation levels such as Read Committed (RC), Read Atomic (RA), and Causal Consistency (CC), which are widely used in production databases \[[2](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/cbe191bda20907994d45bc835d20446968370add)\]. However, these weak isolation levels are not without challenges. Several production databases have been found to suffer from isolation bugs, which can break their data-consistency guarantees \[[3](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/9f96a48d2bc0e77004b32960495685be6c301c63)\].

Black-box testing has emerged as a prominent technique for detecting these isolation bugs by verifying whether transaction histories adhere to a prescribed isolation level \[[4](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/615ff2896b52bee2cbfd64ced324106a29b73397)\]. This approach is particularly useful because it does not require access to the internal implementation of the database, making it transparent to users and developers \[[5](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/38c443a37914e287c42a3238ccebd7372847d5f9)\]. However, testing large-scale databases on realistic workloads requires isolation testers to be highly efficient \[[6](http://arxiv.org/abs/2103.02830)\].

The complexity of isolation testing has been a subject of study. While testing strong isolation levels has been known to be NP-complete \[[7](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/e7ab23d011e5183db78cfea48e303210f6e57e2e)\], recent research has shown that weak isolation levels can be tested in polynomial time \[[8](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/32257d8d2b08c87e58c7b7f4b2430d58e4b51a81)\]. This has enabled the scalability of testing tools, but existing testers often have large polynomial complexities, limiting their applicability to moderate-sized workloads \[[9](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/592950c96053778e9b58ce4c461c1cfa8d32e334)\].

In this work, we develop AWDIT, a highly-efficient and provably optimal tester for weak database isolation. AWDIT improves upon existing methods by achieving polynomial-time complexity for testing RC, RA, and CC, with time complexities of $O(n^(3/2))$, $O(n^(3/2))$, and $O(n \cdot k)$, respectively \[[10](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/11cc8e2d4678ba88b43bf3f9b7db5290a5cf9dc4)\]. This represents a significant improvement over the state of the art, where existing testers often have higher complexities . Furthermore, we prove that AWDIT is essentially optimal, as there is a conditional lower bound of $n^(3/2)$ for any weak isolation level between RC and CC . Our experiments demonstrate that AWDIT is significantly faster than existing, highly optimized testers, achieving speedups of up to $245\times$ for the largest histories .
