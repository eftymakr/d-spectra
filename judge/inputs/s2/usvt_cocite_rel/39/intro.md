## Related Works

De novo genome assembly has been a focal point of computational genomics, driven by the advent of next-generation sequencing technologies that produce vast amounts of short reads \[[1](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/d04b3cc774151260a59a5e6768b6cfae7fc3e512)\]. Early assemblers, such as SOAPdenovo and ABySS, relied on de Bruijn graphs and overlap-layout-consensus (OLC) strategies to reconstruct genomes from fragmented data \[[2](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/7279acd9c61a8de019ea2d55d23124dfbb6b5269)\]. These methods, while effective for small genomes, struggled with scalability and accuracy for large, complex genomes due to memory constraints and computational inefficiencies \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/1512.01801)\]. The introduction of long-read sequencing technologies, such as PacBio and Oxford Nanopore, has significantly improved assembly continuity, but these technologies also introduce high error rates that require sophisticated error correction and assembly strategies \[[4](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/2b59fd18ada769ef76fb7fcc7382f063791abfab)\].

Several studies have explored the parametric complexity of the assembly problem, highlighting the interplay between repeat complexity, read lengths, and coverage in determining the difficulty of assembly \[[5](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/618d82a202bb0408e388eba250c66c8a90700c81)\]. This has led to the development of more advanced assemblers that leverage distributed computing and parallel processing to handle large datasets. For example, PASHA utilizes hybrid parallelism to improve scalability and efficiency in assembling large genomes \[[6](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/3db83bc91d0aa801301a9c063bbfd9d4202f89f2)\]. Similarly, SWAP-Assembler is designed for massive parallel processing using thousands of cores, enabling the assembly of large datasets in a fraction of the time required by traditional methods \[[7](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/c6e319a023f932d6d2ff8897c2c91e56e872db8e)\].

The use of graph-based assemblers has also gained traction, particularly for handling the challenges of long-read sequencing. Canu, for instance, employs adaptive k-mer weighting and repeat separation to improve the accuracy of assemblies from noisy single-molecule sequences \[[8](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/f27decfab0389e2003e7ca66260b34eb3c1b8827)\]. Similarly, hifiasm introduces phased assembly graphs to resolve haplotype diversity, enabling more accurate representation of genetic variation \[[9](http://arxiv.org/abs/2010.10055)\]. These approaches demonstrate the importance of tailoring assembly strategies to the specific characteristics of the sequencing data.

Efforts to reduce memory usage and improve computational efficiency have also been a key focus. Minimap and miniasm, for example, provide fast mapping and de novo assembly for noisy long sequences without requiring an error correction stage, significantly reducing computational time \[[10](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/eff7a7b6916b7584b2184b3aa30601d158648be8)\]. Additionally, succinct data structures have been proposed to compress de Bruijn graphs, enabling more efficient storage and processing of large genomic datasets \[[11](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/f9d407ce133ec096ab4321e6d98e8f0081f5bdcc)\]. These innovations highlight the ongoing challenges and advancements in de novo assembly, particularly in balancing accuracy, speed, and resource utilization.
