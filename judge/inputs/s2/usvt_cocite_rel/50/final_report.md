## Related Works

The rapid growth of open-source machine learning (ML) resources has significantly impacted information retrieval (IR) research, as noted in the paper’s abstract \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2305.11164)\]. However, existing platforms like Hugging Face have limitations in utilizing structured representations, which hinder advanced querying and analysis \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2305.11164)\]. This section reviews related works that address these challenges and contribute to the development of knowledge graphs and benchmarks for ML resource management.

Several studies have explored the characteristics and usage patterns of models and datasets on Hugging Face. For instance, a repository mining study analyzed 1,417 ML models and found that carbon emissions reporting is inconsistent, with a slight decrease in reported footprints over the past two years \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/2311.13380)\]. This highlights the need for more structured and transparent documentation, which aligns with the goals of HuggingKG and HuggingBench. Additionally, the study emphasized the importance of categorizing models based on their carbon efficiency, which could inform the design of more sustainable and transparent ML ecosystems \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/2311.13380)\].

Another study focused on the evolution and maintenance of ML models on Hugging Face, examining over 380,000 models and identifying trends in community engagement and model maintenance \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/2303.02552)\]. The research classified commit messages into categories such as corrective, perfective, and adaptive, offering insights into how models are updated and maintained. These findings underscore the importance of structured data and metadata for tracking model evolution, a key aspect of HuggingKG.

A mixed-methods empirical study identified challenges such as limited guidance for beginners, difficulties in understanding model outputs, and a lack of comprehensive documentation \[[4](http://arxiv.org/abs/2401.13822)\]. These issues highlight the need for better documentation practices, which are essential for the success of knowledge graphs like HuggingKG. The study also noted that documentation quality is strongly correlated with model popularity, reinforcing the importance of structured metadata in resource management.

### <a id='category-pre-trained-model-reuse-challenges'></a>Pre-Trained Model Reuse Challenges: 2 papers found.
The category "Pre-Trained Model Reuse Challenges" highlights several key issues faced by practitioners when reusing pre-trained models (PTMs). These challenges include the lack of essential attributes such as provenance, reproducibility, and portability, which are critical for effective model reuse \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/2303.02552)\]. Additionally, discrepancies between claimed and actual model performance, as well as potential risks associated with model usage, pose significant hurdles \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/2303.02552)\]. These challenges are further exacerbated by limited guidance for beginner users, difficulties in understanding model outputs, and a lack of comprehensive documentation, which hinders model comprehension and selection \[[7](http://arxiv.org/abs/2401.13177)\]. The absence of standardized practices and infrastructure for model registries also contributes to the complexity of PTM reuse \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/2303.02552)\]. These findings underscore the need for improved tools and frameworks to support model management and reuse in the growing PTM ecosystem.

### <a id='category-model-and-dataset-documentation-quality'></a>Model and Dataset Documentation Quality: 3 papers found.
The Category "Model and Dataset Documentation Quality" highlights the critical role of thorough documentation in ensuring the reliability, reproducibility, and transparency of machine learning resources. Existing studies reveal that while dataset documentation is widely recognized as essential, there is a lack of systematic understanding of current practices \[[4](http://arxiv.org/abs/2401.13822)\]. For instance, analysis of Hugging Face dataset cards shows significant variation in completion rates, with more popular datasets often having better documentation \[[4](http://arxiv.org/abs/2401.13822)\]. Practitioners tend to prioritize sections like Dataset Description and Dataset Structure, while sections such as Considerations for Using the Data are often underdeveloped \[[4](http://arxiv.org/abs/2401.13822)\]. This trend is echoed in the documentation of large language models for code (LLM4Code), where documentation is found to be less detailed compared to general AI repositories on GitHub, and licensing practices differ from other software repositories \[[11](http://arxiv.org/abs/2405.16746)\]. Additionally, the concept of model cards has been introduced to address the need for transparency in model performance and usage, particularly in high-impact applications \[[15](http://arxiv.org/abs/1810.03993)\]. These findings underscore the importance of improving documentation standards to enhance the quality and usability of machine learning resources.

### <a id='category-community-engagement-and-model-popularity'></a>Community Engagement and Model Popularity: 3 papers found.
The category "Community Engagement and Model Popularity" highlights the dynamics of collaboration, model adoption, and the structural characteristics of the Hugging Face (HF) Hub. Research indicates that activity on the HF Hub is highly imbalanced, with a small percentage of repositories accounting for the majority of downloads, reflecting a Pareto distribution similar to open-source software development \[[9](http://arxiv.org/abs/2405.13058)\]. This imbalance is further exacerbated by licensing differences, which influence collaboration patterns, with permissive licenses fostering more open collaboration \[[9](http://arxiv.org/abs/2405.13058)\]. Additionally, the social network structure of model repositories reveals a core-periphery configuration, where a core group of prolific developers interacts extensively, while the majority of developers operate in isolation \[[9](http://arxiv.org/abs/2405.13058)\]. This structure suggests that community engagement is concentrated among a few key contributors, limiting broader participation. Furthermore, studies have shown that PTM reuse on the Hugging Face platform exhibits a high turnover rate, indicating rapid evolution compared to traditional software \[[8](http://arxiv.org/abs/2406.08205)\]. Documentation quality has also been found to strongly correlate with model popularity, underscoring the importance of clear and comprehensive resources in driving adoption \[[8](http://arxiv.org/abs/2406.08205)\]. These findings collectively emphasize the role of community engagement, licensing, and documentation in shaping the popularity and evolution of models on the HF Hub.
