## Related Works

The rapid growth of open-source machine learning (ML) resources has significantly impacted information retrieval (IR) research, as noted in the paper’s abstract \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2305.11164)\]. However, existing platforms like Hugging Face have limitations in utilizing structured representations, which hinder advanced querying and analysis \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2305.11164)\]. This section reviews related works that address these challenges and contribute to the development of knowledge graphs and benchmarks for ML resource management.

Several studies have explored the characteristics and usage patterns of models and datasets on Hugging Face. For instance, a repository mining study analyzed 1,417 ML models and found that carbon emissions reporting is inconsistent, with a slight decrease in reported footprints over the past two years \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/2311.13380)\]. This highlights the need for more structured and transparent documentation, which aligns with the goals of HuggingKG and HuggingBench. Additionally, the study emphasized the importance of categorizing models based on their carbon efficiency, which could inform the design of more sustainable and transparent ML ecosystems \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/2311.13380)\].

Another study focused on the evolution and maintenance of ML models on Hugging Face, examining over 380,000 models and identifying trends in community engagement and model maintenance \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/2303.02552)\]. The research classified commit messages into categories such as corrective, perfective, and adaptive, offering insights into how models are updated and maintained. These findings underscore the importance of structured data and metadata for tracking model evolution, a key aspect of HuggingKG.

The reuse of pre-trained models (PTMs) on Hugging Face has also been extensively studied. A mixed-methods empirical study identified challenges such as limited guidance for beginners, difficulties in understanding model outputs, and a lack of comprehensive documentation \[[4](http://arxiv.org/abs/2401.13822)\]. These issues highlight the need for better documentation practices, which are essential for the success of knowledge graphs like HuggingKG. The study also noted that documentation quality is strongly correlated with model popularity, reinforcing the importance of structured metadata in resource management.

A systematic literature review on Hugging Face revealed that PTMs have a significantly higher turnover rate compared to traditional software, indicating rapid evolution \[[8](http://arxiv.org/abs/2406.08205)\]. This finding supports the need for a knowledge graph that captures the dynamic nature of ML resources. Furthermore, the review found that documentation quality is strongly correlated with PTM popularity, which aligns with the goals of HuggingKG in providing structured and comprehensive metadata.
