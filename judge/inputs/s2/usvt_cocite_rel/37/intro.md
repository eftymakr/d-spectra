## Related Works

Vision Transformers (ViTs) have emerged as a powerful alternative to convolutional neural networks (CNNs) in computer vision tasks, leveraging the transformer architecture to effectively capture global context \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/2010.11929)\]. However, the deployment of ViTs on hardware, especially FPGAs, faces significant challenges due to the complex arithmetic operations such as Softmax and LayerNorm, which are sensitive to model accuracy and difficult to map onto efficient hardware with low precision \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2208.05163)\]. Existing approaches often exploit parallelism in matrix multiplication operations while keeping these complex operations on the CPU, resulting in suboptimal performance due to communication overhead between the CPU and accelerator \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2208.05163)\].

Several works have explored hardware acceleration for ViTs. Auto-ViT-Acc introduces an FPGA-aware automatic acceleration framework based on mixed-scheme quantization, achieving higher accuracy than state-of-the-art quantization methods while improving frame rates \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2208.05163)\]. ViTCoD proposes a co-design framework that integrates algorithmic and hardware-level optimizations, achieving significant speedups by reducing dominant data movement costs \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/2210.09573)\]. I-ViT enables integer-only inference for ViTs by approximating non-linear operations with lightweight integer arithmetic methods, achieving comparable or slightly higher accuracy than full-precision models \[[4](http://arxiv.org/abs/2207.01405)\]. FQ-ViT addresses the challenges of post-training quantization for ViTs by introducing Power-of-Two Factor (PTF) and Log-Int-Softmax (LIS) to reduce performance degradation and inference complexity \[[7](http://arxiv.org/abs/2111.13824)\]. Patch Slimming reduces computational costs by discarding redundant patches in a top-down manner, achieving over 45% FLOPs reduction with minimal accuracy drop \[[8](http://arxiv.org/abs/2106.02852)\]. HeatViT introduces hardware-efficient token pruning for ViTs, achieving higher accuracy and significant computation reduction on embedded FPGAs \[[9](http://arxiv.org/abs/2211.08110)\]. VAQF presents a fully automatic software-hardware co-design framework for low-bit ViTs, enabling real-time execution on FPGAs \[[10](http://arxiv.org/abs/2201.06618)\]. Chasing Sparsity in Vision Transformers explores end-to-end sparsity integration, achieving significant computational cost reduction without sacrificing accuracy \[[11](http://arxiv.org/abs/2106.04533)\]. Transformer in Transformer (TNT) improves ViT performance by introducing a hierarchical attention mechanism, achieving higher accuracy with similar computational cost \[[12](http://arxiv.org/abs/2103.00112)\]. Unified Visual Transformer Compression proposes a framework combining pruning, layer skipping, and knowledge distillation, achieving substantial model size reduction without accuracy loss \[[13](http://arxiv.org/abs/2203.08243)\]. Swin Transformer introduces a hierarchical architecture with shifted windows, achieving state-of-the-art performance on various vision tasks \[[14](http://arxiv.org/abs/2103.14030)\].
