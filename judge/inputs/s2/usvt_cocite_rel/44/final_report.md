## Related Works

Representation learning has become a cornerstone in modern recommender systems, enabling efficient and effective user-item matching. Early approaches primarily relied on matrix factorization \[[13](http://arxiv.org/abs/1409.2944)\], which decomposes user-item interaction matrices into latent user and item embeddings. However, these methods struggle with scalability and sparsity, motivating the exploration of deep learning-based techniques. Neural Collaborative Filtering (NCF) \[[9](http://arxiv.org/abs/1708.05031)\] was one of the first to apply deep neural networks to collaborative filtering, using a multi-layer perceptron to model user-item interactions. This approach significantly improved recommendation performance by capturing non-linear patterns in user preferences.

Recent advances in graph neural networks have further enhanced the modeling of complex interactions. GraphSAGE \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/1706.02216)\] introduced an inductive framework for generating node embeddings by aggregating features from a node's local neighborhood, making it suitable for large-scale and evolving graphs. Similarly, GCN-based methods, such as the one proposed in \[[19](http://arxiv.org/abs/1704.06803)\], have been applied to web-scale recommendation tasks, leveraging graph convolutional networks to incorporate both graph structure and node features. These models have demonstrated superior performance on large datasets, particularly when combined with efficient training strategies \[[19](http://arxiv.org/abs/1704.06803)\].

Another line of research focuses on integrating side information, such as item attributes or user behavior, into the recommendation process. Variational Autoencoders for Collaborative Filtering \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/1802.05814)\] extended traditional factorization models by introducing a probabilistic framework that allows for more flexible modeling of user-item interactions. This approach also incorporates Bayesian inference, which has been shown to improve model robustness and generalization. Additionally, the use of knowledge graphs has gained traction in recommendation systems, as demonstrated by KGAT \[[7](http://arxiv.org/abs/1905.07854)\], which explicitly models high-order relations in knowledge graphs to improve recommendation accuracy.

Graph representation binarization has emerged as a critical technique for improving the efficiency of collaborative filtering in recommender systems, particularly in terms of memory usage and computational overhead \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/1706.02216)\]. This approach involves converting high-dimensional embeddings into compact binary sequences, which can significantly enhance the scalability of recommendation models \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/1706.02216)\]. However, existing methods often focus on numerical quantization without adequately addressing the information loss that occurs during this process, leading to performance degradation \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/1706.02216)\]. To mitigate this issue, recent work has emphasized the importance of explicitly reducing information loss at various stages of the binarization process, which has been shown to yield substantial performance improvements \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/1706.02216)\]. Additionally, methods such as embedding sample synthesis have been proposed to generate high-quality binary representations by leveraging both real item data and latent embedding samples \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/1706.02216)\]. These techniques are closely related to broader efforts in embedding sample synthesis, which aim to improve the quality and generalization of node embeddings in large graphs \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/1802.05814)\]. For instance, approaches like GraphSAGE and continuous Skip-gram models have demonstrated the effectiveness of sampling and aggregating local neighborhood information to generate more accurate and robust embeddings \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/1802.05814)\]\[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/1905.08108)\]. These methods highlight the importance of incorporating both discriminative and generative strategies to enhance the performance of embedding-based models \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/1802.05814)\]. By combining these insights, the proposed BiGeaR++ framework introduces a fine-grained inference distillation mechanism and an effective embedding sample synthesis approach, leading to significant improvements in recommendation accuracy \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/1706.02216)\].

### <a id='category-graph-neural-networks-in-recommendations'></a>Graph Neural Networks in Recommendations: 7 papers found.
Graph Neural Networks (GNNs) have emerged as a powerful paradigm for recommendation systems, enabling the modeling of complex user-item interactions through graph structures \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/1905.08108)\]. Traditional methods, such as matrix factorization, often fail to capture the collaborative signal inherent in user-item interactions, leading to suboptimal embeddings \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/1905.08108)\]. In contrast, GNN-based approaches, like Neural Graph Collaborative Filtering (NGCF), explicitly integrate the bipartite graph structure into the embedding process, allowing for the propagation of embeddings and the modeling of high-order connectivity \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/1905.08108)\]. Similarly, the Multi-Graph Convolution Collaborative Filtering (Multi-GCCF) framework extends this idea by incorporating multiple graphs, including user-user and item-item interactions, to better capture proximal relationships \[[12](http://arxiv.org/abs/2001.00267)\]. At a larger scale, Pinterest's implementation of a data-efficient Graph Convolutional Network (GCN) demonstrates how efficient random walks and novel training strategies can enable the embedding of massive graphs with billions of nodes \[[4](http://arxiv.org/abs/1806.01973)\]. Furthermore, the BiRank algorithm introduces a regularization-based approach for ranking vertices in bipartite graphs, leveraging both graph structure and vertex features to produce high-quality recommendations \[[18](http://arxiv.org/abs/1708.04396)\]. These advancements highlight the growing importance of GNNs in recommendation systems, offering more expressive and scalable solutions compared to earlier methods \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/1905.08108)\]\[[4](http://arxiv.org/abs/1806.01973)\]\[[12](http://arxiv.org/abs/2001.00267)\]\[[18](http://arxiv.org/abs/1708.04396)\].
