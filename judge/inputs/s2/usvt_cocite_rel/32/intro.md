## Related Works

Recent advancements in large language models (LLMs) have emphasized the importance of understanding how feature representations evolve across layers, as this knowledge is critical for improving interpretability and robustness \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2307.09288)\]. Several studies have explored the dynamics of fine-tuning, revealing that pre-trained models can be effectively adapted to downstream tasks with minimal parameter updates \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/2012.13255)\]. For instance, BitFit demonstrates that modifying only the bias terms of a model can achieve competitive performance with full fine-tuning, suggesting that fine-tuning is primarily about exposing knowledge from language modeling rather than learning new task-specific knowledge \[[4](http://arxiv.org/abs/2106.10199)\]. Similarly, LoRA introduces a method that injects trainable rank decomposition matrices into each layer of the Transformer architecture, significantly reducing the number of trainable parameters while maintaining performance \[[9](http://arxiv.org/abs/2106.09685)\]. This approach aligns with the broader trend of parameter-efficient transfer learning, which aims to fine-tune only a small subset of parameters to achieve strong performance \[[7](http://arxiv.org/abs/2110.04366)\].

The effectiveness of fine-tuning has also been explained through the lens of intrinsic dimensionality. Research shows that pre-trained models have a low intrinsic dimension, meaning that a small number of parameters can capture the essential information needed for fine-tuning \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/2012.13255)\]. This concept is further supported by the Neural Tangent Kernel (NTK), which provides a theoretical framework for understanding how fine-tuning updates affect model behavior \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/2210.05643)\]. These insights highlight the importance of understanding how model representations change during fine-tuning and how this relates to performance improvements.

Instruction tuning has also emerged as a promising approach to enhance zero-shot learning capabilities. By fine-tuning models on a collection of tasks described via natural language instructions, researchers have achieved significant improvements in performance on unseen tasks \[[14](http://arxiv.org/abs/2109.01652)\]. This method demonstrates the value of incorporating diverse task descriptions into the training process, which can improve the model's ability to generalize across different domains.

Additionally, the use of prompt tuning has been explored as a lightweight alternative to full fine-tuning. Prompt tuning involves learning continuous prompts that condition frozen language models to perform specific tasks, offering a way to reuse a single model for multiple downstream tasks without retraining \[[16](http://arxiv.org/abs/2104.08691)\]. This approach is particularly relevant given the high costs associated with training large models and the need for efficient reuse across tasks.

These works collectively highlight the ongoing efforts to understand and improve the fine-tuning process, emphasizing the importance of parameter efficiency, model adaptability, and generalization capabilities.
