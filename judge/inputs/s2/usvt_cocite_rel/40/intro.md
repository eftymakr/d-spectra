## Related Works

Zero-Knowledge Proofs (ZKPs) have garnered significant attention for their ability to enable secure and private computation, allowing a prover to demonstrate the truth of a statement without revealing additional information \[[1](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/e149d93531d8c07cf43f6cae327a4795144d1304)\]. Despite their potential, ZKPs have not yet achieved widespread adoption due to the computational complexity involved in the proving process \[[1](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/e149d93531d8c07cf43f6cae327a4795144d1304)\]. Recent efforts have focused on accelerating key primitives of ZKP protocols using hardware, particularly GPUs and ASICs \[[1](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/e149d93531d8c07cf43f6cae327a4795144d1304)\]. However, these approaches often face limitations, such as requiring a trusted setup for each application or generating larger proof sizes with higher verification costs, which restrict their use in scenarios with numerous verifiers or strict time constraints \[[1](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/e149d93531d8c07cf43f6cae327a4795144d1304)\].

Several works have addressed the performance bottlenecks in ZKP generation. GZKP \[[1](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/e149d93531d8c07cf43f6cae327a4795144d1304)\] is a GPU-accelerated ZKP system that significantly improves the speed of proof generation by optimizing polynomial computation and multi-scalar multiplication. It achieves up to 48.1× and 17.6× speedups over existing systems \[[1](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/e149d93531d8c07cf43f6cae327a4795144d1304)\]. Similarly, PipeZK \[[2](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/6ee84674b7c426849c3da56cb278c08eb7a903cf)\] introduces a pipelined architecture with two subsystems to handle large polynomial computations and multi-scalar multiplications on elliptic curves, achieving 10× speedup on cryptographic benchmarks and 5× on Zcash \[[2](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/6ee84674b7c426849c3da56cb278c08eb7a903cf)\]. SZKP \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/2408.05890)\] presents a scalable accelerator framework that is the first ASIC to accelerate an entire proof on-chip, achieving over 400×, 3×, and 12× speedups over CPU, ASIC, and GPU implementations \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/2408.05890)\].

In the realm of machine learning, zkCNN \[[5](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/6336149196f02d0eb6c1a89bc6b662e4422bd794)\] is a zero-knowledge proof scheme designed for convolutional neural networks (CNNs), allowing model owners to prove prediction correctness without leaking model details. It leverages a new sumcheck protocol for fast Fourier transforms and convolutions, achieving 1264× faster proof generation than existing schemes \[[5](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/6336149196f02d0eb6c1a89bc6b662e4422bd794)\]. Additionally, the use of ZK-friendly hash functions, such as MiMC, has been explored in AMAZE \[[12](http://arxiv.org/abs/2411.06350)\], which provides a hardware-optimized framework for efficient ZKP generation on edge devices, achieving over 13× speedup compared to standard CPU implementations \[[12](http://arxiv.org/abs/2411.06350)\].

While these accelerators focus on specific primitives, the work presented here, zkSpeed, addresses the acceleration of an entire ZKP protocol, namely HyperPlonk, which supports one-time, universal setup and small proof sizes.
