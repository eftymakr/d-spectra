## Related Works

Zero-Knowledge Proofs (ZKPs) have garnered significant attention for their ability to enable secure and private computation, allowing a prover to demonstrate the truth of a statement without revealing additional information \[[1](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/e149d93531d8c07cf43f6cae327a4795144d1304)\]. Despite their potential, ZKPs have not yet achieved widespread adoption due to the computational complexity involved in the proving process \[[1](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/e149d93531d8c07cf43f6cae327a4795144d1304)\]. Recent efforts have focused on accelerating key primitives of ZKP protocols using hardware, particularly GPUs and ASICs \[[1](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/e149d93531d8c07cf43f6cae327a4795144d1304)\]. However, these approaches often face limitations, such as requiring a trusted setup for each application or generating larger proof sizes with higher verification costs, which restrict their use in scenarios with numerous verifiers or strict time constraints \[[1](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/e149d93531d8c07cf43f6cae327a4795144d1304)\].

Several works have addressed the performance bottlenecks in ZKP generation. GZKP \[[1](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/e149d93531d8c07cf43f6cae327a4795144d1304)\] is a GPU-accelerated ZKP system that significantly improves the speed of proof generation by optimizing polynomial computation and multi-scalar multiplication. It achieves up to 48.1× and 17.6× speedups over existing systems \[[1](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/e149d93531d8c07cf43f6cae327a4795144d1304)\]. Similarly, PipeZK \[[2](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/6ee84674b7c426849c3da56cb278c08eb7a903cf)\] introduces a pipelined architecture with two subsystems to handle large polynomial computations and multi-scalar multiplications on elliptic curves, achieving 10× speedup on cryptographic benchmarks and 5× on Zcash \[[2](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/6ee84674b7c426849c3da56cb278c08eb7a903cf)\]. SZKP \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/2408.05890)\] presents a scalable accelerator framework that is the first ASIC to accelerate an entire proof on-chip, achieving over 400×, 3×, and 12× speedups over CPU, ASIC, and GPU implementations \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/2408.05890)\].

### <a id='category-hardware-acceleration-techniques-for-zkps'></a>Hardware Acceleration Techniques for ZKPs: 5 papers found.
Hardware acceleration techniques for zero-knowledge proofs (ZKPs) have gained significant attention due to the computational intensity of proof generation, which remains a bottleneck for practical deployment \[[1](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/e149d93531d8c07cf43f6cae327a4795144d1304)\]. Several approaches have been proposed to address this challenge, focusing on optimizing key primitives such as polynomial computations and multi-scalar multiplications (MSMs) \[[2](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/6ee84674b7c426849c3da56cb278c08eb7a903cf)\]. For instance, GZKP leverages GPU acceleration to improve performance by promoting cache-friendly memory access and eliminating costly external shuffles \[[1](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/e149d93531d8c07cf43f6cae327a4795144d1304)\]. Similarly, PipeZK introduces a pipelined accelerator with two subsystems, one handling large polynomial computations and the other managing MSMs, achieving speedups of up to 10x on cryptographic benchmarks \[[2](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/6ee84674b7c426849c3da56cb278c08eb7a903cf)\]. SZKP, on the other hand, presents an ASIC-based solution that accelerates the entire proof generation process by leveraging structured dataflows for both Number Theoretic Transforms (NTTs) and MSMs, achieving over 400× speedups compared to CPU implementations \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/2408.05890)\]. Additionally, the need for efficient cryptographic hashing in ZKP protocols has led to the development of ZK-friendly hash functions like MiMC, which are optimized for hardware acceleration to enhance performance in resource-constrained environments \[[12](http://arxiv.org/abs/2411.06350)\]. These advancements highlight the growing emphasis on hardware-centric solutions to overcome the performance limitations of ZKPs, enabling their broader adoption in privacy-preserving applications \[[1](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/e149d93531d8c07cf43f6cae327a4795144d1304)\]\[[2](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/6ee84674b7c426849c3da56cb278c08eb7a903cf)\]\[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/2408.05890)\]\[[12](http://arxiv.org/abs/2411.06350)\].

### <a id='category-zkp-protocol-overview-and-challenges'></a>ZKP Protocol Overview and Challenges: 9 papers found.
Zero-knowledge proofs (ZKPs) are a critical component in privacy-preserving systems, enabling a prover to demonstrate the validity of a statement without disclosing additional information \[[1](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/e149d93531d8c07cf43f6cae327a4795144d1304)\]. The concept of zero-knowledge proofs was formalized with the introduction of interactive proof systems that ensure no extra knowledge is revealed beyond the correctness of the proposition \[[4](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/9ccf7b6cb32cf89752a35bd910555adac54773e0)\]. These proofs have found applications in blockchain technologies, verifiable machine learning, and electronic voting, yet their adoption has been limited by computational complexity \[[1](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/e149d93531d8c07cf43f6cae327a4795144d1304)\]. Recent advancements have focused on optimizing ZKP protocols, such as HyperPlonk, which supports one-time, universal setup and small proof sizes \[[1](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/e149d93531d8c07cf43f6cae327a4795144d1304)\]. Researchers have developed accelerators like zkSpeed to enhance the performance of ZKP protocols by leveraging hardware, achieving significant speedups over CPU-based implementations \[[1](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/e149d93531d8c07cf43f6cae327a4795144d1304)\]. Additionally, the design of zero-knowledge proof systems has been explored in terms of their knowledge complexity, with studies showing that for languages with perfect zero-knowledge proofs, their complements can be verified with short interactive protocols \[[21](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/d5ccb8b351118b35b1a565ad27de4cd2ebdd0287)\]. The implementation of ZKPs remains challenging, as evidenced by the need for robust libraries like zksk, which provide a Python-based domain-specific language for defining and computing sigma protocols \[[17](http://arxiv.org/abs/1911.02459)\]. Despite these efforts, challenges persist, including the trade-off between proof size, verification time, and the requirement for trusted setups in some protocols \[[1](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/e149d93531d8c07cf43f6cae327a4795144d1304)\]. These developments highlight the ongoing research into improving the efficiency and practicality of zero-knowledge proofs for real-world applications.
