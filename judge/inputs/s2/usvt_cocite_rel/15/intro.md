## Related Works

Brain tumor segmentation is a critical task in medical image analysis, with numerous approaches proposed over the years to improve accuracy and efficiency. Early methods relied on traditional image processing techniques, but recent advances have shifted toward deep learning models, particularly convolutional neural networks (CNNs), which have demonstrated superior performance in handling the complexity of 3D medical data \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/1505.04597)\]. One of the earliest and most influential architectures for biomedical image segmentation is the U-Net, which employs a contracting path to capture context and an expanding path for precise localization \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/1505.04597)\]. This architecture has been widely adopted and adapted for various segmentation tasks, including brain tumor segmentation \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/1603.05959)\]. 

The U-Net's success has led to the development of several variants tailored for 3D medical imaging. For instance, the 3D U-Net extends the original U-Net by replacing 2D operations with their 3D counterparts, enabling dense volumetric segmentation from sparse annotations \[[19](http://arxiv.org/abs/1606.06650)\]. This is particularly useful in scenarios where annotated data is limited, as is often the case in medical imaging \[[19](http://arxiv.org/abs/1606.06650)\]. Similarly, the V-Net is a fully convolutional neural network designed for 3D MRI segmentation, trained end-to-end on MRI volumes and optimized using a Dice coefficient-based objective function \[[9](http://arxiv.org/abs/1606.04797)\]. These models have been instrumental in improving the accuracy of tumor segmentation, especially in challenging cases with class imbalance \[[9](http://arxiv.org/abs/1606.04797)\].

To further enhance performance, researchers have explored the integration of pre-trained weights from other domains, such as ImageNet, into medical segmentation models. For example, the nnU-Net framework has been shown to achieve state-of-the-art results on the BraTS challenge, particularly when combined with region-based training and aggressive data augmentation \[[5](http://arxiv.org/abs/2011.00848)\]. The nnU-Net also incorporates a self-configuring approach, which automatically adjusts the network architecture based on the input data, leading to improved generalization and efficiency \[[5](http://arxiv.org/abs/2011.00848)\]. 

In addition to architectural improvements, the use of advanced post-processing techniques has also contributed to better segmentation outcomes. For instance, the application of 3D fully connected conditional random fields (CRFs) has been shown to effectively reduce false positives and improve the quality of segmentation maps \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/1603.05959)\]. Furthermore, the use of attention mechanisms, such as scale attention, has enabled models to better integrate information across different scales, leading to more accurate and robust segmentation \[[25](http://arxiv.org/abs/2011.03188)\].
