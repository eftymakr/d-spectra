## Related Works

Video super-resolution (VSR) has been a focal point in computer vision, with recent advances leveraging deep learning to enhance low-resolution (LR) videos into high-resolution (HR) counterparts. Early methods primarily relied on single-image super-resolution (SISR) techniques, which were extended to video by incorporating temporal information \[[1](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/c6a8ff4d9dcdf9e5d82310b847af999ed6655f65)\]. However, these methods often suffered from computational inefficiency and poor temporal consistency due to the need for explicit motion estimation and compensation \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/1801.04590)\]. 

To address these limitations, researchers have proposed various architectures that integrate recurrent structures or attention mechanisms to better exploit temporal dependencies \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/1611.05250)\]. For instance, frame-recurrent models have been introduced to reduce computational costs by warping only one image per step, while maintaining temporal consistency through the use of previously inferred HR estimates \[[4](http://arxiv.org/abs/1707.02921)\]. Similarly, spatio-temporal networks have been developed to effectively utilize temporal redundancies, improving reconstruction accuracy while maintaining real-time speed \[[5](http://arxiv.org/abs/1812.02898)\]. These methods, however, often require complex motion compensation strategies, which can introduce artifacts and increase computational overhead \[[6](http://arxiv.org/abs/2201.12288)\].

More recently, attention-based approaches have gained traction. For example, the Temporally-Deformable Alignment Network (TDAN) avoids optical flow computation by dynamically predicting offsets of sampling convolution kernels at the feature level, leading to more accurate alignment \[[7](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/755227d7ea41b1d90df31f411f9ecbc998c20d26)\]. Similarly, the Video Restoration Transformer (VRT) introduces a parallel frame prediction ability, utilizing temporal reciprocal self attention (TRSA) and parallel warping to enable cross-clip interactions and efficient information fusion \[[8](http://arxiv.org/abs/1511.04587)\]. These models demonstrate improved performance across multiple video restoration tasks, including super-resolution, deblurring, and denoising \[[9](http://arxiv.org/abs/1909.08080)\].

In addition to attention mechanisms, recurrent neural networks (RNNs) have been explored for their ability to model long-term temporal dependencies. The Bidirectional Recurrent Convolutional Network (BRCN) was proposed to handle complex motions by using weight-sharing convolutional connections, reducing parameters and enabling finer-level temporal modeling \[[10](http://arxiv.org/abs/1905.02716)\]. Similarly, the Recurrent Structure-Detail Network (RSDN) divides the input into structure and detail components, feeding them to a recurrent unit for more effective exploitation of previous frames \[[11](http://arxiv.org/abs/2007.10595)\]. These approaches have shown promising results in achieving high-quality VSR while maintaining computational efficiency \[[12](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/a2c50f93f6d0892918e0ff653750d198ccab48d0)\].
