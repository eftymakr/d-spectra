## Related Works

Federated unlearning has emerged as a critical research area in the context of privacy-preserving machine learning, particularly in response to the "right to be forgotten" (RTBF) legislation \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2203.07320)\]. This concept enables users to request the removal of their data from a trained model without compromising the overall model performance \[[2](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/eadeffdec9fac8fd7f9aea732ca410eb082b7dcf)\]. In federated learning (FL), where data remains decentralized, unlearning becomes more complex due to the distributed nature of training and the lack of global access to training data \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/2207.05521)\]. 

Several studies have explored federated unlearning techniques, with a focus on efficient and verifiable methods. For instance, \[[4](http://arxiv.org/abs/2310.20448)\] introduced FedEraser, which leverages historical parameter updates to reconstruct an unlearned model, significantly reducing the time required compared to retraining from scratch. Similarly, \[[5](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/06edb1f6eadb6c141d720fcca4c854578dc5d670)\] proposed a method that eliminates the influence of a client's data by performing local unlearning and then running a few rounds of federated learning with the remaining clients. This approach avoids the need for storing historical updates on the server, making it more scalable \[[6](http://arxiv.org/abs/2205.12709)\].

The importance of verifiability in federated unlearning has also been emphasized. \[[7](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/39da2d48f2132b919405e3bb6df6ece44ac6e15a)\] introduced VeriFi, a framework that allows users to verify the unlearning effect by marking and checking the global model's performance on specific data points. This ensures that the unlearning process is both effective and transparent \[[8](http://arxiv.org/abs/2210.10958)\]. Additionally, \[[9](http://arxiv.org/abs/2209.02299)\] proposed a method that uses knowledge distillation to restore model performance after unlearning, without requiring any data from the clients, making it suitable for FL systems \[[10](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/a52c3ec7325e81bd87fdf8d74bf9acfd1e1ffe5f)\].

Efforts to improve the efficiency and accuracy of federated unlearning have also been made. \[[11](http://arxiv.org/abs/2401.11018)\] introduced a framework that ensures the unlearned model is statistically indistinguishable from the one trained without the deleted data, providing a rigorous guarantee of exact unlearning. This approach is particularly useful in scenarios where data privacy is paramount \[[12](http://arxiv.org/abs/2401.05146)\]. Furthermore, \[[13](http://arxiv.org/abs/2201.09441)\] proposed a method that uses subspace-based techniques to eliminate a target client's contribution without requiring additional storage, making it suitable for resource-constrained environments \[[14](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/a08f1c5c9f0bdb24f0dc75c0d969eb353899156b)\].

In addition to these methods, there has been a growing interest in evaluating the effectiveness of federated unlearning. \[[15](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/efab3b30d6f67f55c68f9dc7c65b2b54b1789030)\] introduced a set of evaluation metrics to assess both under-unlearning and over-unlearning scenarios, which are critical for maintaining model performance while respecting user privacy \[[16](http://arxiv.org/abs/2304.10638)\].
