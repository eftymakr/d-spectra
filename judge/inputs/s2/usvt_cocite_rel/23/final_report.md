## Related Works

Sensor calibration is a critical step in autonomous systems that integrate heterogeneous sensors such as radar, camera, and LiDAR. Prior work has focused on both offline and online calibration methods, with varying degrees of success in addressing the challenges posed by sensor sparsity and measurement uncertainty. For instance, \[[1](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/0955326c67a947b5fac5fbae7238d04fc9adc251)\] presents a continuous-time approach for 3D radar-to-camera extrinsic calibration that does not require specialized radar retroreflectors, making it suitable for real-world deployment. Similarly, \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/1904.08743)\] introduces a targetless rotational auto-calibration method for radar and camera, leveraging deep learning to achieve robust sensor registration without the need for dedicated calibration targets. These methods, however, typically rely on either manual calibration or limited real-time adaptation.

In contrast, \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/2103.07505)\] proposes an online multi-sensor calibration method based on moving object tracking, which utilizes detection and tracking information to continuously update calibration parameters. This approach is particularly useful for long-term system reliability, as it avoids the need for periodic manual recalibration. However, it relies on the presence of moving objects, which may not always be available in all environments. Additionally, \[[5](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/c80265fa719eb85c8a56a6b36c74b99b793f39b2)\] explores the use of radar and camera data for online calibration, focusing on the detection of decalibration events and estimating rotation with error from a 20-second long scenario. While this method is effective in detecting drift, it may not be suitable for high-speed or highly dynamic environments.

Recent work has also focused on the integration of radar and camera data for object detection and depth estimation. For example, \[[6](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/fa7f4199640500d48c0671181ea6a8dce3141123)\] presents a feature-level fusion detection framework called RVDet, which aligns radar and camera features in the bird's-eye view (BEV) domain to improve detection performance. This method addresses the spatial alignment issue between multi-modal data, which is a common challenge in radar-camera fusion. Similarly, \[[7](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/0e88fc24ae7694d0b1a2fe78322936fc568bc7d2)\] introduces a radar-camera fusion approach based on representation learning, aiming to improve data association through deep feature interaction and global reasoning. Related Works in Sensor Calibration Methods: The field of sensor calibration for autonomous systems has seen significant advancements, particularly in addressing the challenges posed by heterogeneous sensor fusion. Early approaches focused on extrinsic calibration of radar–LiDAR–camera systems, highlighting the limitations of radar due to its sparse informativeness and lack of elevation resolution \[[1](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/0955326c67a947b5fac5fbae7238d04fc9adc251)\]. This issue has been tackled through innovative methods such as the use of special targets and two-step optimization procedures to improve the accuracy of six extrinsic parameters \[[1](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/0955326c67a947b5fac5fbae7238d04fc9adc251)\]. Similarly, the integration of 3D millimetre-wavelength radars with cameras has been explored, with methods that do not require specialized retroreflectors, relying instead on radar velocity measurements \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/2103.07505)\]. The importance of robust sensor fusion in adverse weather conditions has also been emphasized, where accurate calibration is essential for reliable autonomous vehicle operation \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/2103.07505)\]. Recent studies have also focused on automatic calibration methods without dedicated targets, such as the use of deep learning techniques with convolutional neural networks to achieve precise and robust sensor registration \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/1904.08743)\]. Additionally, the development of open-source tools for joint extrinsic calibration of radar, camera, and lidar has been a notable contribution, enabling simultaneous calibration of multiple sensing modalities \[[4](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/4d4dbbcf27d991aeab43dafb82a105bb5512b799)\]. Furthermore, online calibration methods based on detection and tracking of moving objects have been proposed, leveraging existing system data to maintain long-term reliability \[[5](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/c80265fa719eb85c8a56a6b36c74b99b793f39b2)\]. These approaches highlight the ongoing efforts to improve the accuracy and robustness of sensor calibration in dynamic and challenging environments.
