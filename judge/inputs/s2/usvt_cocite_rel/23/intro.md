## Related Works

Sensor calibration is a critical step in autonomous systems that integrate heterogeneous sensors such as radar, camera, and LiDAR. Prior work has focused on both offline and online calibration methods, with varying degrees of success in addressing the challenges posed by sensor sparsity and measurement uncertainty. For instance, \[[1](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/0955326c67a947b5fac5fbae7238d04fc9adc251)\] presents a continuous-time approach for 3D radar-to-camera extrinsic calibration that does not require specialized radar retroreflectors, making it suitable for real-world deployment. Similarly, \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/1904.08743)\] introduces a targetless rotational auto-calibration method for radar and camera, leveraging deep learning to achieve robust sensor registration without the need for dedicated calibration targets. These methods, however, typically rely on either manual calibration or limited real-time adaptation.

In contrast, \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/2103.07505)\] proposes an online multi-sensor calibration method based on moving object tracking, which utilizes detection and tracking information to continuously update calibration parameters. This approach is particularly useful for long-term system reliability, as it avoids the need for periodic manual recalibration. However, it relies on the presence of moving objects, which may not always be available in all environments. Additionally, \[[5](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/c80265fa719eb85c8a56a6b36c74b99b793f39b2)\] explores the use of radar and camera data for online calibration, focusing on the detection of decalibration events and estimating rotation with error from a 20-second long scenario. While this method is effective in detecting drift, it may not be suitable for high-speed or highly dynamic environments.

Recent work has also focused on the integration of radar and camera data for object detection and depth estimation. For example, \[[6](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/fa7f4199640500d48c0671181ea6a8dce3141123)\] presents a feature-level fusion detection framework called RVDet, which aligns radar and camera features in the bird's-eye view (BEV) domain to improve detection performance. This method addresses the spatial alignment issue between multi-modal data, which is a common challenge in radar-camera fusion. Similarly, \[[7](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/0e88fc24ae7694d0b1a2fe78322936fc568bc7d2)\] introduces a radar-camera fusion approach based on representation learning, aiming to improve data association through deep feature interaction and global reasoning. This method is trained with noisy labels generated by a rule-based algorithm and achieves a performance of 92.2% F1 score, which is significantly higher than traditional rule-based methods.

Another notable work is \[[8](http://arxiv.org/abs/2008.05711)\], which proposes a method for full-velocity radar returns using radar-camera fusion. This approach estimates the full velocity of Doppler returns by combining optical flow from camera images with radar measurements, addressing the issue of missing tangential velocity components.
