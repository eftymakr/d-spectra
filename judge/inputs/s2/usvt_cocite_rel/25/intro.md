## Related Works

The integration of knowledge graphs (KGs) with large language models (LLMs) to enhance reasoning capabilities has gained significant attention in recent years. While LLMs have demonstrated strong reasoning abilities in complex tasks, they often lack up-to-date knowledge and suffer from hallucinations during reasoning \[[18](http://arxiv.org/abs/2005.14165)\]. KGs, on the other hand, provide structured, semantically rich representations that are well-suited for reasoning \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2104.06378)\]. However, most existing verification methods rely on unstructured text corpora, limiting their ability to effectively leverage KGs \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2104.06378)\]. 

Several approaches have attempted to bridge the gap between LLMs and KGs. QA-GNN \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2104.06378)\] proposes a model that uses LMs to estimate the importance of KG nodes and performs joint reasoning over the QA context and KG. KEPLER \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/1911.06136)\] integrates factual knowledge into pre-trained language models by encoding textual entity descriptions with a PLM and jointly optimizing the KE and language modeling objectives. RoG \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/2310.01061)\] synergizes LLMs with KGs to enable faithful and interpretable reasoning, using a planning-retrieval-reasoning framework. LARK \[[4](http://arxiv.org/abs/2305.01157)\] formulates complex KG reasoning as a combination of contextual KG search and logical query reasoning. 

The use of KGs for reasoning has also been explored in the context of commonsense reasoning. KagNet \[[16](http://arxiv.org/abs/1909.02151)\] proposes a textual inference framework that utilizes external, structured commonsense knowledge graphs to perform explainable inferences. ERNIE \[[7](http://arxiv.org/abs/1905.07129)\] enhances language representation with informative entities from KGs, achieving significant improvements on various knowledge-driven tasks. K-BERT \[[10](http://arxiv.org/abs/1909.07606)\] enables language representation with knowledge graphs by injecting domain knowledge into the model during pre-training. 

Recent work has also focused on unifying LLMs and KGs for more effective reasoning. The paper \[[12](http://arxiv.org/abs/2306.08302)\] presents a roadmap for unifying LLMs and KGs, proposing three frameworks: KG-enhanced LLMs, LLM-augmented KGs, and synergized LLMs + KGs. CoLAKE \[[9](http://arxiv.org/abs/2010.00309)\] jointly learns contextualized representation for both language and knowledge with the extended MLM objective. 

In addition to these approaches, several studies have explored the use of LLMs for knowledge graph construction and reasoning. The paper \[[5](http://arxiv.org/abs/2305.13168)\] presents an exhaustive evaluation of LLMs for KG construction and reasoning, showing that LLMs are more suited as inference assistants rather than few-shot information extractors.
