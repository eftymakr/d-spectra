## Related Works

Cross-domain sequential recommendation (CDSR) has gained significant attention in recent years due to its ability to alleviate data sparsity and cold-start problems in recommender systems \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2304.03891)\]. Traditional approaches often rely on self-attention mechanisms, such as those found in the Transformer architecture, to model sequential user interactions \[[14](http://arxiv.org/abs/1904.06690)\]. However, these methods typically incorporate domain-specific components as additional modules, which may not fully leverage the inherent power of the self-attention mechanism \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2304.03891)\]. 

Several works have explored the use of self-attention in CDSR. For instance, C2DSR proposes a method that simultaneously leverages intra- and inter-sequence item relationships to capture precise user preferences \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2304.03891)\]. The model employs a graph neural network to mine inter-sequence collaborative relationships and a sequential attentive encoder to capture intra-sequence patterns \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2304.03891)\]. Similarly, DASL introduces a dual learning mechanism to iteratively transfer information between domains, enhancing cross-domain CTR predictions \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/2106.02768)\]. 

Another line of research focuses on the use of graph-based models for cross-domain sequential recommendation. DA-GCN addresses the challenge of modeling shared-account scenarios by introducing a domain-aware graph convolution network \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/2105.03300)\]. This approach constructs graphs for each domain and uses attention mechanisms to guide message passing, effectively capturing domain-specific preferences \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/2105.03300)\]. 

The use of contrastive learning has also been explored in the context of sequential recommendation. CL4SRec introduces a multi-task framework that combines next-item prediction with contrastive learning to derive self-supervision signals from user behavior sequences \[[19](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/a8f2764a76772ab957b31f5d7bb88527d46fbdc8)\]. This approach improves the quality of user representations by capturing more meaningful patterns \[[19](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/a8f2764a76772ab957b31f5d7bb88527d46fbdc8)\]. 

In addition, the problem of data sparsity and cold-starts has been addressed through various techniques. DDTCDR proposes a deep dual transfer model that transfers information between domains in an iterative manner, improving recommendation performance in both domains \[[6](http://arxiv.org/abs/1910.05189)\]. The model uses latent orthogonal mapping to preserve relations between users across different latent spaces \[[6](http://arxiv.org/abs/1910.05189)\]. 

Recent work has also focused on the challenges of open-world assumptions in CDSR. AMID introduces an adaptive multi-interest debiasing framework that accounts for non-overlapping users and dynamic data distribution shifts \[[11](http://arxiv.org/abs/2311.04590)\]. This approach uses a multi-interest information module and a doubly robust estimator to improve model performance under open-world conditions \[[11](http://arxiv.org/abs/2311.04590)\].
