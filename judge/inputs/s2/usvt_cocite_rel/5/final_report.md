## Related Works

Instruction-following text embedding is a critical area of research that aims to generate dynamic text embeddings capable of adapting to user instructions, highlighting specific attributes of text \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/1506.06726)\]. This task is particularly relevant in applications such as information retrieval, semantic similarity, and natural language understanding, where embeddings must be tailored to the context or intent of the user \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/1506.06726)\]. Existing approaches often suffer from significant computational overhead, as they require re-encoding the entire corpus for each new instruction, which is both time-consuming and resource-intensive \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/1506.06726)\].

Several prior works have explored methods for generating task-specific embeddings. Skip-Thought Vectors \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/1706.03762)\] introduced an encoder-decoder model that reconstructs surrounding sentences, enabling the creation of generic sentence representations that are robust and perform well in practice. Similarly, BERT \[[4](http://arxiv.org/abs/1810.04805)\] introduced a new language representation model based on bidirectional transformers, which can be fine-tuned for a wide range of tasks, including question answering and language inference. These models, however, typically require retraining or re-encoding for each new instruction, which limits their efficiency \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/1506.06726)\].

To address this limitation, recent works have focused on improving the efficiency of instruction-following text embeddings. Sentence-T5 \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/2108.08877)\] explored sentence embeddings from pre-trained text-to-text transformers, demonstrating that encoder-only models can outperform previous best models on semantic textual similarity tasks. SimCSE \[[5](http://arxiv.org/abs/2104.08821)\] introduced a contrastive learning framework that significantly advances the state-of-the-art in sentence embeddings, achieving strong performance on semantic textual similarity tasks with both unsupervised and supervised approaches. These methods, while effective, still require re-encoding or fine-tuning for each instruction, which is computationally expensive \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/1506.06726)\].

In contrast, our proposed GSTransform framework introduces a lightweight transformation mechanism that adapts pre-computed embeddings in real time to align with user instructions, guided by a small amount of instruction-focused labeled data \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/1506.06726)\]. This approach is inspired by the idea that instruction-relevant information is inherently encoded in generic embeddings but remains underutilized \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/1506.06726)\]. By leveraging a guided space transformation, GSTransform avoids the need to re-encode the entire corpus for each instruction, significantly reducing computational overhead \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/1506.06726)\].

The concept of instruction-following text embeddings is also related to the broader field of instruction tuning. InstructGPT \[[18](http://arxiv.org/abs/2203.02155)\] demonstrated that fine-tuning large language models with human feedback can improve their ability to follow user instructions, leading to better performance in tasks such as question answering and text generation.
