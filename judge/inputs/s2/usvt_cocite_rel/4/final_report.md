## Related Works

Label noise is a pervasive issue in deep learning, significantly impacting model performance and generalization. Prior work has explored various strategies to mitigate its effects, including loss correction, semi-supervised learning, and robust loss functions. Early approaches focused on modifying the loss function to be more resilient to noisy labels. For instance, \[[10](http://arxiv.org/abs/1712.09482)\] proposed that the mean absolute error (MAE) loss is inherently robust to label noise, suggesting that standard back propagation could suffice for learning the true classifier. However, this approach was later found to perform poorly on challenging datasets, prompting the development of more sophisticated loss functions \[[14](http://arxiv.org/abs/1805.07836)\]. The Generalized Cross Entropy (GCE) loss, a generalization of both MAE and categorical cross entropy, was introduced to better handle noisy label scenarios, showing promising results on CIFAR-10, CIFAR-100, and FASHION-MNIST datasets \[[14](http://arxiv.org/abs/1805.07836)\].

Another line of research has focused on semi-supervised learning techniques to improve robustness against noisy labels. Co-teaching, for example, trains two neural networks simultaneously, allowing them to teach each other by selecting potentially clean samples and refining their training data iteratively \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/1804.06872)\]. This approach demonstrated superior robustness compared to state-of-the-art methods on noisy versions of MNIST, CIFAR-10, and CIFAR-100. Similarly, DivideMix leverages semi-supervised learning by modeling the per-sample loss distribution with a mixture model to dynamically divide the training data into clean and noisy subsets, and trains the model on both \[[5](http://arxiv.org/abs/2002.07394)\]. This method was shown to achieve substantial improvements over existing techniques.

In addition to semi-supervised learning, some approaches have focused on estimating the noise distribution and correcting the loss accordingly. The work in \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/1609.03683)\] proposed a loss correction approach that assumes knowledge of the probability of each class being corrupted into another, and showed that this can be estimated using a recent noise estimation technique. This led to an end-to-end framework that demonstrated robustness across various architectures and datasets. Another approach, CleanNet, introduced a joint neural embedding network that requires only a fraction of the classes to be manually verified to provide knowledge of label noise, which can then be transferred to other classes \[[4](http://arxiv.org/abs/1711.07131)\]. This method was effective in reducing label noise detection error rates and improving classification performance on large-scale datasets.

### <a id='category-label-noise-impact-on-deep-learning'></a>Label Noise Impact on Deep Learning: 11 papers found.
The category "Label Noise Impact on Deep Learning" encompasses a range of approaches aimed at mitigating the adverse effects of noisy labels in training deep neural networks. Researchers have explored various strategies, including modifying network architectures \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/1804.06872)\], estimating true labels from noisy ones \[[4](http://arxiv.org/abs/1711.07131)\], and designing loss functions that are inherently robust to label noise \[[7](http://arxiv.org/abs/2007.00151)\]. For instance, some studies propose loss correction procedures that rely on matrix inversion and multiplication, assuming knowledge of class-dependent corruption probabilities \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/1609.03683)\]. Others, such as CleanNet, introduce joint neural embedding networks that require only a fraction of manually verified labels to infer label noise patterns \[[4](http://arxiv.org/abs/1711.07131)\]. Additionally, methods like Co-teaching and Co-teaching+ have been developed to train multiple networks that teach each other, improving robustness to noisy labels by leveraging small-loss instances \[[11](http://arxiv.org/abs/2012.11854)\]. Furthermore, some works focus on the dimensionality of deep representations, proposing strategies that adapt loss functions based on subspace monitoring during training to enhance tolerance to noisy labels \[[6](http://arxiv.org/abs/1806.02612)\]. These approaches highlight the diverse ways in which label noise impacts deep learning and the various techniques being developed to address this challenge.

### <a id='category-regularization-for-noise-mitigation'></a>Regularization for Noise Mitigation: 1 papers found.

	Document 1: [Snippet]: «We propose a novel framework to perform classification via deep learning in the presence of noisy annotations. When trained on noisy labels, deep neural networks have been observed to first fit the training data with clean labels during an "early learning" phase, before eventually memorizing the examples with false labels. We prove that early learning and memorization are fundamental phenomena in high-dimensional classification tasks, even in simple linear models, and give a theoretical explanation in this setting. Motivated by these findings, we develop a new technique for noisy classification tasks, which exploits the progress of the early learning phase. In contrast with existing approaches, which use the model output during early learning to detect the examples with clean labels, and either ignore or attempt to correct the false labels, we take a different route and instead capitalize on early learning via regularization. There are two key elements to our approach. First, we leverage semi-supervised learning techniques to produce target probabilities based on the model outputs. Second, we design a regularization term that steers the model towards these targets, implicitly preventing memorization of the false labels. The resulting framework is shown to provide robustness to noisy annotations on several standard benchmarks and real-world datasets, where it achieves results comparable to the state of the art.»

### <a id='category-loss-function-modifications-for-robustness'></a>Loss Function Modifications for Robustness: 2 papers found.

	Document 1: [Snippet]: «Training accurate deep neural networks (DNNs) in the presence of noisy labels is an important and challenging task. Though a number of approaches have been proposed for learning with noisy labels, many open issues remain. In this paper, we show that DNN learning with Cross Entropy (CE) exhibits overfitting to noisy labels on some classes (``easy" classes), but more surprisingly, it also suffers from significant under learning on some other classes (``hard" classes). Intuitively, CE requires an extra term to facilitate learning of hard classes, and more importantly, this term should be noise tolerant, so as to avoid overfitting to noisy labels. Inspired by the symmetric KL-divergence, we propose the approach of Symmetric cross entropy Learning (SL), boosting CE symmetrically with a noise robust counterpart Reverse Cross Entropy (RCE). Our proposed SL approach simultaneously addresses both the under learning and overfitting problem of CE in the presence of noisy labels. We provide a theoretical analysis of SL and also empirically show, on a range of benchmark and real-world datasets, that SL outperforms state-of-the-art methods. We also show that SL can be easily incorporated into existing methods in order to further enhance their performance.»
