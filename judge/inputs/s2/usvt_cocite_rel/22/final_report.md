## Related Works

Unsupervised domain adaptation (UDA) for semantic segmentation has attracted significant research interest due to the high cost of obtaining pixel-level annotations for real-world data \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/1909.13589)\]. Traditional UDA methods assume that the category settings between the source and target domains are known, which is often not the case in real-world applications \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/1909.13589)\]. This assumption can lead to performance degradation when private classes exist in the target domain \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/1909.13589)\]. To address this limitation, several approaches have been proposed to improve domain adaptation without prior knowledge of category settings.

One line of research focuses on improving the quality of pseudo-labels generated during the adaptation process. For instance, \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/2003.03773)\] introduces a method that estimates prediction uncertainty to rectify pseudo label learning, which helps in reducing the impact of noisy pseudo labels. Similarly, \[[14](http://arxiv.org/abs/2101.10979)\] proposes a method that uses representative prototypes to denoise pseudo labels and align the target feature space, which enhances the robustness of the adaptation process. These methods highlight the importance of pseudo-label quality in UDA.

Another approach is to address the domain shift by aligning the marginal distributions of features between the source and target domains. For example, \[[16](http://arxiv.org/abs/1811.12833)\] introduces an adversarial entropy minimization method that reduces the domain gap by using entropy loss and adversarial loss. This approach has shown effectiveness in synthetic-to-real domain adaptation tasks. Similarly, \[[18](http://arxiv.org/abs/2007.09222)\] proposes a fine-grained adversarial learning strategy that aligns features at the class level while preserving the internal structure of semantics across domains, which improves the alignment of both common and private classes.

Several works also focus on improving the adaptation process by incorporating additional information such as depth \[[20](http://arxiv.org/abs/1904.01886)\] or instance-level features \[[17](http://arxiv.org/abs/2003.08040)\]. For instance, \[[20](http://arxiv.org/abs/1904.01886)\] proposes a depth-aware domain adaptation framework that leverages dense depth information from the source domain to improve the performance of semantic segmentation on the target domain. This approach demonstrates that incorporating additional information can significantly enhance the adaptation process.

Moreover, some studies have explored the use of self-training techniques to improve the quality of pseudo labels. \[[6](http://arxiv.org/abs/2008.12197)\] introduces an instance adaptive self-training framework that generates high-quality pseudo labels by using an instance adaptive selector. This method also incorporates region-guided regularization to smooth the pseudo-label region and sharpen the non-pseudo-label region, which enhances the robustness of the adaptation process.

### <a id='category-pseudo-label-quality-improvement-techniques'></a>Pseudo-Label Quality Improvement Techniques: 6 papers found.
In the context of pseudo-label quality improvement techniques for unsupervised domain adaptation in semantic segmentation, several approaches have been proposed to address the challenges posed by noisy pseudo-labels and domain shifts. Existing methods often rely on pseudo-labels generated from the source domain to train on the target domain, but these labels can be unreliable due to domain discrepancies \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/2003.03773)\]. To mitigate this, researchers have introduced techniques such as uncertainty estimation, where models output both segmentation predictions and their confidence levels, allowing for dynamic thresholding of pseudo-labels based on prediction variance \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/2004.07703)\]. Additionally, methods like DACS (Domain Adaptation via Cross-domain mixed Sampling) have been developed to improve pseudo-label quality by mixing labeled source data with pseudo-labeled target data, enabling more robust training \[[4](http://arxiv.org/abs/2007.08702)\]. Another approach involves leveraging representative prototypes, such as class feature centroids, to refine pseudo-labels by estimating their likelihood and aligning feature distributions across domains \[[5](http://arxiv.org/abs/1910.13049)\]. Furthermore, recent work has emphasized the importance of network architecture in UDA, with Transformers showing promise in capturing long-range dependencies and improving feature transfer \[[6](http://arxiv.org/abs/2008.12197)\]. These techniques collectively aim to enhance the reliability of pseudo-labels and improve adaptation performance across diverse domains.

### <a id='category-domain-distribution-alignment-strategies'></a>Domain Distribution Alignment Strategies: 9 papers found.
In the context of unsupervised domain adaptation for semantic segmentation, recent works have focused on addressing the domain shift between source and target domains by aligning feature distributions and improving generalization. Many approaches rely on adversarial learning to enforce domain invariance, such as aligning marginal distributions or category-level features \[[5](http://arxiv.org/abs/1910.13049)\]. However, these methods often overlook the intra-domain variability within the target data, leading to suboptimal performance \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/1909.13589)\]. To mitigate this, some studies propose two-step strategies, first reducing the inter-domain gap and then addressing the intra-domain gap by leveraging self-supervised adaptation from an easy to a hard subdomain \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/1909.13589)\]. Others introduce category-aware alignment mechanisms, such as category anchor-guided models, which explicitly enforce feature alignment at the class level to improve discriminative power \[[5](http://arxiv.org/abs/1910.13049)\]. Additionally, methods based on Fourier transforms have been proposed to simplify domain alignment by swapping spectral components between domains, achieving competitive results without complex training \[[8](http://arxiv.org/abs/2004.05498)\]. Furthermore, some works focus on fine-grained adversarial learning, introducing domain discriminators that operate at the class level to preserve semantic structure across domains \[[18](http://arxiv.org/abs/2007.09222)\]. These strategies highlight the ongoing efforts to enhance domain adaptation by addressing both inter- and intra-domain discrepancies, with a growing emphasis on robustness in real-world scenarios where category settings may not be known \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/1909.13589)\].

### <a id='category-adversarial-perturbation-based-adaptation-methods'></a>Adversarial Perturbation-Based Adaptation Methods: 4 papers found.
Adversarial Perturbation-Based Adaptation Methods focus on improving domain adaptation in semantic segmentation by addressing the challenges of domain shift and class distribution mismatches. These methods leverage adversarial learning to align feature distributions between source and target domains, ensuring that models can generalize better to unseen environments \[[11](http://arxiv.org/abs/1802.10349)\]. Specifically, they introduce adversarial perturbations at the feature level to make classifiers invariant to domain-specific variations, which is crucial for handling scenarios where the target domain has different characteristics from the source \[[16](http://arxiv.org/abs/1811.12833)\]. For instance, some approaches generate and defend against pointwise adversarial perturbations in the feature space, allowing the model to maintain performance even when dealing with tail classes or small objects that are often overlooked in traditional alignment strategies \[[19](http://arxiv.org/abs/1912.08954)\]. Additionally, methods such as Cycle-Consistent Adversarial Domain Adaptation (CyCADA) combine adversarial learning with cycle-consistency constraints to adapt representations at both the pixel and feature levels, enabling effective domain transfer without requiring aligned image pairs \[[22](http://arxiv.org/abs/1711.03213)\]. These techniques collectively enhance the robustness of semantic segmentation models in real-world applications where domain adaptation is essential.
