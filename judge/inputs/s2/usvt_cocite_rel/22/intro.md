## Related Works

Unsupervised domain adaptation (UDA) for semantic segmentation has attracted significant research interest due to the high cost of obtaining pixel-level annotations for real-world data \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/1909.13589)\]. Traditional UDA methods assume that the category settings between the source and target domains are known, which is often not the case in real-world applications \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/1909.13589)\]. This assumption can lead to performance degradation when private classes exist in the target domain \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/1909.13589)\]. To address this limitation, several approaches have been proposed to improve domain adaptation without prior knowledge of category settings.

One line of research focuses on improving the quality of pseudo-labels generated during the adaptation process. For instance, \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/2003.03773)\] introduces a method that estimates prediction uncertainty to rectify pseudo label learning, which helps in reducing the impact of noisy pseudo labels. Similarly, \[[14](http://arxiv.org/abs/2101.10979)\] proposes a method that uses representative prototypes to denoise pseudo labels and align the target feature space, which enhances the robustness of the adaptation process. These methods highlight the importance of pseudo-label quality in UDA.

Another approach is to address the domain shift by aligning the marginal distributions of features between the source and target domains. For example, \[[16](http://arxiv.org/abs/1811.12833)\] introduces an adversarial entropy minimization method that reduces the domain gap by using entropy loss and adversarial loss. This approach has shown effectiveness in synthetic-to-real domain adaptation tasks. Similarly, \[[18](http://arxiv.org/abs/2007.09222)\] proposes a fine-grained adversarial learning strategy that aligns features at the class level while preserving the internal structure of semantics across domains, which improves the alignment of both common and private classes.

Several works also focus on improving the adaptation process by incorporating additional information such as depth \[[20](http://arxiv.org/abs/1904.01886)\] or instance-level features \[[17](http://arxiv.org/abs/2003.08040)\]. For instance, \[[20](http://arxiv.org/abs/1904.01886)\] proposes a depth-aware domain adaptation framework that leverages dense depth information from the source domain to improve the performance of semantic segmentation on the target domain. This approach demonstrates that incorporating additional information can significantly enhance the adaptation process.

Moreover, some studies have explored the use of self-training techniques to improve the quality of pseudo labels. \[[6](http://arxiv.org/abs/2008.12197)\] introduces an instance adaptive self-training framework that generates high-quality pseudo labels by using an instance adaptive selector. This method also incorporates region-guided regularization to smooth the pseudo-label region and sharpen the non-pseudo-label region, which enhances the robustness of the adaptation process.
