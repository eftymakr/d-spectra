## Related Works

Rehearsal-based methods have been widely studied in continual learning (CL) to mitigate catastrophic forgetting, where models lose knowledge of previous tasks after learning new ones \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/1612.00796)\]. These methods typically store a subset of past data and train it alongside new data, enabling the model to retain prior knowledge while adapting to new tasks \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/1705.08690)\]. However, the optimality of concurrent rehearsal—where past and new data are trained together—remains an open question. Recent work has explored alternative strategies, such as sequential rehearsal, where new data is trained first, followed by revisiting past data sequentially \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/1811.11682)\]. This approach is inspired by human learning, where revisiting tasks in sequence helps reinforce memory \[[4](http://arxiv.org/abs/1805.12369)\].

Theoretical analysis of rehearsal-based CL has been limited, but recent studies have begun to address this gap. For instance, \[[5](http://arxiv.org/abs/1801.01423)\] investigates the impact of task similarity on forgetting and generalization, showing that task similarity significantly influences the effectiveness of different rehearsal strategies. Similarly, \[[6](http://arxiv.org/abs/1902.10486)\] introduces a theoretical framework to analyze forgetting in overparameterized linear models, providing insights into how task similarity and model capacity affect performance. These findings align with our theoretical analysis, which demonstrates that sequential rehearsal performs better when tasks are less similar \[[7](http://arxiv.org/abs/2302.05836)\].

Several methods have been proposed to enhance rehearsal-based CL. For example, \[[8](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/90e04f3ae23ca7df5f59b11453341e3db943b6f4)\] introduces a framework that leverages generative models to replay past data, enabling efficient and effective training on previous tasks. This approach is particularly useful in real-world applications where storing all past data is infeasible \[[9](http://arxiv.org/abs/1606.09282)\]. Additionally, \[[10](http://arxiv.org/abs/2010.04003)\] explores selective experience replay, where only the most informative experiences are stored and replayed, improving performance while reducing memory usage. This strategy is closely related to our hybrid rehearsal approach, which combines concurrent and sequential rehearsal based on task similarity \[[11](http://arxiv.org/abs/1904.07734)\].

In the realm of continual learning, \[[12](http://arxiv.org/abs/1904.00310)\] proposes a method that uses memory buffers to store past experiences, allowing models to replay them during training. This approach has been shown to reduce catastrophic forgetting in both image classification and reinforcement learning tasks \[[13](http://arxiv.org/abs/2107.04384)\]. Similarly, \[[14](http://arxiv.org/abs/1706.08840)\] introduces a framework that separates neural structure learning from parameter estimation, enabling models to evolve their architecture while maintaining knowledge of previous tasks \[[15](http://arxiv.org/abs/1908.04742)\].

The effectiveness of rehearsal-based methods has also been evaluated in various benchmarks. For example, \[[16](http://arxiv.org/abs/2104.07446)\] compares different rehearsal strategies on the split MNIST and CIFAR-100 datasets, demonstrating that methods that selectively replay past experiences outperform standard approaches \[[17](http://arxiv.org/abs/2105.07385)\]. These methods store and reuse a subset of past data alongside new data during training, enabling the model to retain previously learned knowledge while adapting to new tasks \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/1705.08690)\]. Inspired by human learning, where revisiting tasks helps reinforce memory, researchers have proposed hybrid rehearsal strategies that combine concurrent and sequential approaches, training similar tasks together and revisiting dissimilar ones sequentially \[[5](http://arxiv.org/abs/1801.01423)\]. Such hybrid methods have demonstrated superior performance compared to standard concurrent rehearsal, as evidenced by experiments with deep neural networks \[[6](http://arxiv.org/abs/1902.10486)\]. Additionally, the effectiveness of rehearsal strategies is influenced by factors such as task similarity, data distribution, and the use of memory buffers \[[7](http://arxiv.org/abs/2302.05836)\]. These findings highlight the importance of tailoring rehearsal strategies to the specific characteristics of the task sequence, offering new directions for improving continual learning systems \[[8](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/90e04f3ae23ca7df5f59b11453341e3db943b6f4)\].

### <a id='category-theoretical-frameworks-for-continual-learning'></a>Theoretical Frameworks for Continual Learning: 7 papers found.
Theoretical frameworks for continual learning (CL) have been extensively explored to address catastrophic forgetting, a phenomenon where learning new tasks erases knowledge of previous ones. Researchers have focused on understanding the factors influencing forgetting and generalization, such as overparameterization, task similarity, and task ordering \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/1612.00796)\]. For instance, under overparameterized linear models, explicit forms of expected forgetting and generalization error have been derived, offering insights into how these factors interact \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/1612.00796)\]. Additionally, the teacher-student setup has been used to disentangle task similarity at the feature and readout levels, revealing complex relationships between task similarity, transfer, and forgetting \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/1705.08690)\]. Theoretical analyses have also shown that when input distributions and input-output relationships of target functions are dissimilar, networks can avoid catastrophic forgetting \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/1811.11682)\]. Moreover, studies have highlighted the role of temporal trade-offs between transfer and interference, proposing methods like Meta-Experience Replay (MER) that optimize these dynamics through gradient alignment \[[4](http://arxiv.org/abs/1805.12369)\]. These theoretical advancements have informed practical approaches, such as hybrid rehearsal strategies that combine concurrent and sequential rehearsal based on task similarity, leading to improved performance in deep neural networks \[[5](http://arxiv.org/abs/1801.01423)\]. Overall, these frameworks provide a foundation for both theoretical understanding and algorithmic innovation in continual learning.
