## Related Works

3D human pose estimation from monocular videos has attracted significant research interest due to its applications in human-computer interaction, action recognition, and virtual reality. Early methods primarily relied on 2D pose estimation followed by 3D reconstruction using geometric constraints \[[1](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/50167a9e6bdc1f64a58fe8a3c990105fec0bd2bc)\]. However, these approaches often suffer from depth ambiguity and self-occlusions, which are challenging to resolve without explicit 3D information \[[2](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/555a23a05777a0b03614b195c7c72f7c626a97e9)\]. Recent advances have focused on leveraging spatial and temporal dependencies to improve accuracy and robustness \[[3](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/30de7fecb146356d7ae6cafa072997674601e0f5)\].

Several works have explored the use of graph convolutional networks (GCNs) to model the human skeleton as a graph, where joints are nodes and bones are edges. For instance, \[[4](http://arxiv.org/abs/2007.09389)\] introduced a graph-based method that explicitly incorporates domain knowledge about human body configurations into graph convolutional operations. This approach enables the model to better capture the spatial relationships between joints. Similarly, \[[5](http://arxiv.org/abs/2103.10455)\] proposed a novel graph convolutional network architecture, Graph Stacked Hourglass Networks, which processes features across three different scales of human skeletal representations, enabling the model to learn both local and global feature representations.

Temporal consistency is another critical aspect in 3D human pose estimation. \[[6](http://arxiv.org/abs/2103.16385)\] introduced an attention-based framework that uses multi-scale dilated convolutions to model long-range dependencies among frames, leading to more accurate 3D pose estimation. \[[7](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/db466d02ec9f7f47ee11590d0feeb6330256c98d)\] further proposed a sequence-to-sequence network with LSTM units and shortcut connections to enforce temporal smoothness, improving the temporal coherence of 3D pose sequences. Additionally, \[[8](http://arxiv.org/abs/1603.06937)\] introduced a motion loss function that captures keypoint motion patterns, enhancing the accuracy of 3D pose estimation from 2D pose data.

To address the issue of limited 3D annotations, \[[9](http://arxiv.org/abs/1705.03098)\] proposed ordinal depth supervision, using weaker supervision signals provided by the ordinal depths of human joints. ### <a id='category-graph-convolutional-networks-for-human-pose'></a>Graph Convolutional Networks for Human Pose: 8 papers found.
Graph Convolutional Networks (GCNs) have been extensively explored for 3D human pose estimation, with various approaches addressing challenges such as depth ambiguity, self-occlusion, and the need for modeling complex spatial and temporal dependencies \[[1](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/50167a9e6bdc1f64a58fe8a3c990105fec0bd2bc)\]. Early methods represented the human skeleton as an undirected graph, where joints are nodes and bones are edges, but this approach failed to capture the hierarchical relationships inherent in articulated structures \[[26](http://arxiv.org/abs/2107.07797)\]. To overcome this, later works introduced directed graphs, explicitly encoding parent-child relationships between joints, thereby improving the modeling of hierarchical dependencies \[[26](http://arxiv.org/abs/2107.07797)\]. Additionally, some studies focused on improving the representation power of GCNs by introducing variants like Locally Connected Networks (LCN) and Modulated GCNs, which allow for more flexible feature transformations and dynamic graph structures \[[16](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/442928cd95d7bca15800b29ce2e2f8db5461bd58)\]\[[15](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/0646857f867d9a624f2b53d1ffb4de99e225b2d3)\]. These models also incorporated domain knowledge about human body configurations, enhancing the accuracy of pose estimation \[[1](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/50167a9e6bdc1f64a58fe8a3c990105fec0bd2bc)\]. Furthermore, methods such as Semantic Graph Convolutional Networks (SemGCN) were proposed to capture semantic relationships between nodes, enabling better generalization and performance with fewer parameters \[[17](http://arxiv.org/abs/1904.03345)\]. Another line of research emphasized the importance of temporal consistency and local-to-global feature learning, introducing architectures like the local-to-global network and hierarchical channel-squeezing fusion layers to effectively extract relevant information while suppressing noise \[[1](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/50167a9e6bdc1f64a58fe8a3c990105fec0bd2bc)\]\[[20](http://arxiv.org/abs/2108.07181)\]. These advancements collectively highlight the ongoing efforts to refine GCN-based approaches for 3D human pose estimation, focusing on improved accuracy, efficiency, and adaptability to complex human movements.

In the context of temporal consistency in 3D pose estimation, prior work has focused on leveraging temporal information across frames to improve accuracy and coherence. Additionally, CNN-based frameworks have been developed to process all input frames concurrently, avoiding the limitations of RNNs while still producing temporally coherent results \[[28](http://arxiv.org/abs/1908.08289)\]. These methods highlight the importance of temporal modeling in 3D human pose estimation, particularly in handling the inherent challenges of independent frame errors and long-range dependencies.
