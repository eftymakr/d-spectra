## Related Works

Recent advancements in large language models (LLMs) have focused on improving their reasoning capabilities through various techniques such as chain-of-thought (CoT) prompting \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2205.11916)\], in-context learning \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/2303.18223)\], and multimodal instruction tuning \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/2310.03744)\]. These approaches aim to enhance the models' ability to perform complex reasoning tasks, often leveraging both textual and visual modalities \[[4](http://arxiv.org/abs/2302.00923)\]. For instance, the introduction of CoT prompting has significantly improved performance on arithmetic and symbolic reasoning tasks by encouraging step-by-step reasoning \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2205.11916)\]. Similarly, in-context learning allows models to adapt to new tasks with minimal fine-tuning, using task-specific examples directly within the input \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/2303.18223)\].

Multimodal instruction tuning has also gained traction, with models like LLaVA demonstrating the ability to handle visual inputs effectively through instruction tuning \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/2310.03744)\]. This approach involves training models on visual instruction datasets, enabling them to understand and respond to complex visual and textual prompts \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/2310.03744)\]. Furthermore, the development of models such as Flamingo has enabled rapid adaptation to novel tasks using few-shot learning, by leveraging pre-trained vision and language models \[[7](http://arxiv.org/abs/2204.14198)\]. These models are trained on large-scale multimodal web corpora, allowing them to generalize across a wide range of tasks \[[7](http://arxiv.org/abs/2204.14198)\].

In addition to improving reasoning and adaptability, researchers have also explored ways to enhance the alignment of LLMs with human values and safety considerations. Techniques such as alignment fine-tuning have been proposed to address issues like assessment misalignment, where models may assign higher scores to subpar reasoning chains \[[19](http://arxiv.org/abs/2309.02144)\]. This approach involves fine-tuning models on chain-of-thought (COT) training data, generating multiple responses, and calibrating scores using a constraint alignment loss to ensure high-quality reasoning \[[19](http://arxiv.org/abs/2309.02144)\].

Moreover, the development of vision-language models (VLMs) has been driven by the need to better understand and interact with multimodal inputs. Models like ViLBERT and LXMERT have been designed to learn joint representations of image content and natural language, enabling them to perform tasks such as visual question answering and image captioning \[[8](http://arxiv.org/abs/1908.02265)\]\[[10](http://arxiv.org/abs/1908.07490)\]. These models use attention mechanisms to align visual and textual information, improving their ability to reason about complex scenes \[[8](http://arxiv.org/abs/1908.02265)\].

In the context of safety and alignment, recent work has also focused on developing frameworks that allow models to identify and steer toward safer behaviors without relying on predefined refusal tokens or manual analysis \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2205.11916)\]. The COSMIC framework, for example, uses cosine similarity metrics to identify viable steering directions and target layers, enabling automated direction selection
