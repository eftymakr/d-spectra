## Related Works

Generative retrieval has emerged as a promising paradigm that replaces traditional search index data structures with a single large-scale neural network, enabling improved efficiency and seamless integration with generative large language models \[[12](http://arxiv.org/abs/2311.09134)\]. This approach directly generates document identifiers through corpus-specific constrained decoding, offering a unified end-to-end solution \[[12](http://arxiv.org/abs/2311.09134)\]. Prior work has explored various aspects of this paradigm, including scalability, diversity in generation, and the effectiveness of different decoding strategies \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2404.07220)\]\[[5](http://arxiv.org/abs/1610.02424)\]\[[11](http://arxiv.org/abs/2501.11034)\].

Several studies have investigated the scalability of generative retrieval techniques. For instance, \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/2305.11841)\] conducted the first empirical study of generative retrieval across various corpus scales, ultimately scaling up to the entire MS MARCO passage ranking task with a corpus of 8.8M passages. They found that while generative retrieval is competitive with state-of-the-art dual encoders on small corpora, scaling to millions of passages remains an important and unsolved challenge. This highlights the need for more robust and scalable generative retrieval methods \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/2305.11841)\].

The use of beam search in generative retrieval has also been extensively studied. Beam search, a common decoding strategy, explores the search space in a greedy left-right fashion, retaining only the top-B candidates \[[5](http://arxiv.org/abs/1610.02424)\]. However, \[[5](http://arxiv.org/abs/1610.02424)\] found that producing nearly identical sequences is computationally wasteful and fails to capture the inherent ambiguity of complex AI tasks. To address this, they proposed Diverse Beam Search (DBS), an alternative that decodes a list of diverse outputs by optimizing for a diversity-augmented objective. DBS was shown to outperform beam search on tasks such as image captioning and machine translation \[[5](http://arxiv.org/abs/1610.02424)\].

In addition to beam search, other decoding strategies have been explored. \[[6](http://arxiv.org/abs/2104.05336)\] investigated numerous decoding algorithms, including those that rely on a value function parameterized by a neural network, and introduced a Monte-Carlo Tree Search (MCTS) based method. They found that the choice of decoding algorithm heavily depends on the characteristics of the goal metric, suggesting that further research is needed to optimize decoding for specific tasks \[[6](http://arxiv.org/abs/2104.05336)\].

RAG models were shown to outperform parametric seq2seq models and task-specific retrieve-and-extract architectures on a wide range of knowledge-intensive NLP tasks \[[9](http://arxiv.org/abs/2005.11401)\]. ### <a id='category-generative-retrieval-frameworks'></a>Generative Retrieval Frameworks: 5 papers found.
Generative retrieval has emerged as a promising paradigm in text retrieval, aiming to replace traditional index-based methods with a single large-scale neural network that directly generates document identifiers \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2404.07220)\]. This approach leverages the power of generative language models to encode corpus information into model parameters, eliminating the need for separate indexing structures \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2404.07220)\]. However, existing generative retrieval models face challenges in accurately capturing relevance distributions and optimizing retrieval performance \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2404.07220)\]. Recent efforts have focused on improving the learning-to-rank capabilities of generative retrieval systems, such as the LTRGR framework, which bridges the gap between autoregressive generation and passage ranking by introducing a rank loss during training \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/2305.11841)\]. Additionally, frameworks like RIPOR have been developed to enhance the scalability and effectiveness of generative retrieval on large-scale benchmarks by incorporating novel ranking optimization algorithms and document ID construction strategies \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/2204.10628)\]. The GBS framework further advances generative retrieval for book search by addressing the unique challenges of complex, hierarchical book information through data augmentation and outline-oriented encoding \[[4](http://arxiv.org/abs/2208.07652)\]. These developments highlight the ongoing efforts to refine generative retrieval models, improve their generalization capabilities, and enhance their performance on diverse tasks and datasets \[[5](http://arxiv.org/abs/1610.02424)\].

### <a id='category-beam-search-optimization-strategies'></a>Beam Search Optimization Strategies: 1 papers found.

	Document 1: [Snippet]: «Neural sequence models are widely used to model time-series data. Equally ubiquitous is the usage of beam search (BS) as an approximate inference algorithm to decode output sequences from these models. BS explores the search space in a greedy left-right fashion retaining only the top-B candidates - resulting in sequences that differ only slightly from each other. Producing lists of nearly identical sequences is not only computationally wasteful but also typically fails to capture the inherent ambiguity of complex AI tasks. To overcome this problem, we propose Diverse Beam Search (DBS), an alternative to BS that decodes a list of diverse outputs by optimizing for a diversity-augmented objective. We observe that our method finds better top-1 solutions by controlling for the exploration and exploitation of the search space - implying that DBS is a better search algorithm. Moreover, these gains are achieved with minimal computational or memory over- head as compared to beam search. To demonstrate the broad applicability of our method, we present results on image captioning, machine translation and visual question generation using both standard quantitative metrics and qualitative human studies. Further, we study the role of diversity for image-grounded language generation tasks as the complexity of the image changes. We observe that our method consistently outperforms BS and previously proposed techniques for diverse decoding from neural sequence models.»

	Document 1: [Snippet]: «Large pre-trained language models have been shown to store factual knowledge in their parameters, and achieve state-of-the-art results when fine-tuned on downstream NLP tasks. Pre-trained models with a differentiable access mechanism to explicit non-parametric memory can overcome this issue, but have so far been only investigated for extractive downstream tasks. We explore a general-purpose fine-tuning recipe for retrieval-augmented generation (RAG) -- models which combine pre-trained parametric and non-parametric memory for language generation. We fine-tune and evaluate our models on a wide range of knowledge-intensive NLP tasks and set the state-of-the-art on three open domain QA tasks, outperforming parametric seq2seq models and task-specific retrieve-and-extract architectures. For language generation tasks, we find that RAG models generate more specific, diverse and factual language than a state-of-the-art parametric-only seq2seq baseline.»
