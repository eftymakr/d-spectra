## Related Works

Generative retrieval has emerged as a promising paradigm that replaces traditional search index data structures with a single large-scale neural network, enabling improved efficiency and seamless integration with generative large language models \[[12](http://arxiv.org/abs/2311.09134)\]. This approach directly generates document identifiers through corpus-specific constrained decoding, offering a unified end-to-end solution \[[12](http://arxiv.org/abs/2311.09134)\]. Prior work has explored various aspects of this paradigm, including scalability, diversity in generation, and the effectiveness of different decoding strategies \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2404.07220)\]\[[5](http://arxiv.org/abs/1610.02424)\]\[[11](http://arxiv.org/abs/2501.11034)\].

Several studies have investigated the scalability of generative retrieval techniques. For instance, \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/2305.11841)\] conducted the first empirical study of generative retrieval across various corpus scales, ultimately scaling up to the entire MS MARCO passage ranking task with a corpus of 8.8M passages. They found that while generative retrieval is competitive with state-of-the-art dual encoders on small corpora, scaling to millions of passages remains an important and unsolved challenge. This highlights the need for more robust and scalable generative retrieval methods \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/2305.11841)\].

The use of beam search in generative retrieval has also been extensively studied. Beam search, a common decoding strategy, explores the search space in a greedy left-right fashion, retaining only the top-B candidates \[[5](http://arxiv.org/abs/1610.02424)\]. However, \[[5](http://arxiv.org/abs/1610.02424)\] found that producing nearly identical sequences is computationally wasteful and fails to capture the inherent ambiguity of complex AI tasks. To address this, they proposed Diverse Beam Search (DBS), an alternative that decodes a list of diverse outputs by optimizing for a diversity-augmented objective. DBS was shown to outperform beam search on tasks such as image captioning and machine translation \[[5](http://arxiv.org/abs/1610.02424)\].

In addition to beam search, other decoding strategies have been explored. \[[6](http://arxiv.org/abs/2104.05336)\] investigated numerous decoding algorithms, including those that rely on a value function parameterized by a neural network, and introduced a Monte-Carlo Tree Search (MCTS) based method. They found that the choice of decoding algorithm heavily depends on the characteristics of the goal metric, suggesting that further research is needed to optimize decoding for specific tasks \[[6](http://arxiv.org/abs/2104.05336)\].

The effectiveness of generative retrieval in knowledge-intensive tasks has also been explored. \[[9](http://arxiv.org/abs/2005.11401)\] introduced Retrieval-Augmented Generation (RAG), which combines pre-trained parametric and non-parametric memory for language generation. RAG models were shown to outperform parametric seq2seq models and task-specific retrieve-and-extract architectures on a wide range of knowledge-intensive NLP tasks \[[9](http://arxiv.org/abs/2005.11401)\].
