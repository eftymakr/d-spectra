## Related Works

Federated learning (FL) has emerged as a promising approach to train machine learning models on decentralized data while preserving user privacy \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/1910.01991)\]. However, the increasing demand for data privacy, particularly under regulations such as the "right to be forgotten," has led to the development of federated unlearning (FU) techniques that allow users to remove their data from the global model \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/2310.20448)\]. This section reviews related works in the areas of federated learning, machine unlearning, and evaluation practices for unlearning methods.

Federated learning enables collaborative training of models across multiple clients without sharing raw data \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/1910.01991)\]. However, existing FL approaches often fail to address the issue of data erasure, which is critical for compliance with privacy regulations \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/2310.20448)\]. In response, federated unlearning has been introduced as a mechanism to remove the influence of specific clients or data samples from the global model \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/2310.20448)\]. Several methods have been proposed to achieve this, including retraining from scratch, which is computationally expensive \[[13](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/39da2d48f2132b919405e3bb6df6ece44ac6e15a)\], and more efficient techniques such as local unlearning and parameter update reconstruction \[[17](http://arxiv.org/abs/2207.05521)\].

Machine unlearning, a broader concept, refers to the ability of a model to "forget" certain data points or classes \[[23](http://arxiv.org/abs/2209.02299)\]. This is particularly important in the context of deep learning, where models can memorize sensitive information \[[23](http://arxiv.org/abs/2209.02299)\]. Recent approaches have focused on developing efficient unlearning methods that minimize the need for full retraining \[[14](http://arxiv.org/abs/2201.09441)\]. For example, FedEraser leverages historical parameter updates to reconstruct an unlearned model without requiring full retraining \[[18](http://arxiv.org/abs/2012.13891)\]. Similarly, the SISA training framework improves unlearning efficiency by strategically limiting the influence of specific data points during training \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/1912.03817)\].

Evaluation of unlearning methods remains a challenging task, as current practices often rely on single metrics, which may not fully capture the effectiveness of unlearning \[[29](http://arxiv.org/abs/2412.19069)\]. This paper addresses this limitation by proposing adapted and newly developed evaluation metrics to assess unlearning strategies in a more comprehensive manner \[[29](http://arxiv.org/abs/2412.19069)\]. The study evaluates five unlearning strategies, uncovering their strengths and limitations in managing under-unlearning and over-unlearning scenarios \[[29](http://arxiv.org/abs/2412.19069)\].

The concept of verifiable federated unlearning has also gained attention, as it ensures that unlearning claims can be independently verified \[[16](http://arxiv.org/abs/2205.12709)\].
