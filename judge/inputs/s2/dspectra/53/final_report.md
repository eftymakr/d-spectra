## Related Works

Deep sequential recommendation models have seen significant advancements, driven by the need to capture dynamic user preferences and complex interaction patterns. Traditional approaches such as Markov Chains (MCs) and Recurrent Neural Networks (RNNs) have been widely used, but they often struggle with modeling long-term dependencies and handling sparse or dense datasets effectively \[[4](http://arxiv.org/abs/1808.09781)\]. More recently, self-attention based models like SASRec have emerged as a powerful alternative, enabling the capture of long-term semantics while maintaining computational efficiency \[[4](http://arxiv.org/abs/1808.09781)\]. However, these models typically treat interaction histories as ordered sequences without considering the actual time intervals between interactions, which can lead to suboptimal predictions \[[1](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/9c7a455b1e48d01a99e58e884a6c1acb75074ad0)\].

To address the limitations of traditional models, several works have introduced time-aware mechanisms. For instance, TiSASRec explicitly models the timestamps of interactions within a sequential modeling framework, incorporating both the absolute positions of items and the time intervals between them \[[1](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/9c7a455b1e48d01a99e58e884a6c1acb75074ad0)\]. Similarly, MEANTIME proposes a mixture of attention mechanisms with multi-temporal embeddings to better capture the temporal dynamics of user behavior \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/2008.08273)\]. These models highlight the importance of leveraging temporal information to enhance recommendation accuracy, particularly in scenarios where user behavior evolves over time \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/2008.08273)\].

Efficiency remains a critical challenge for attention-based models, especially when dealing with long sequences. Mamba4Rec introduces a selective state space model (SSM) that enables linear complexity for sequence processing, making it suitable for real-time applications \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/2403.03900)\]. EchoMamba4Rec further enhances this by integrating bi-directional processing with spectral filtering to capture complex patterns in user interaction data \[[16](http://arxiv.org/abs/2406.02638)\]. These models demonstrate that SSMs can effectively balance performance and efficiency, offering a promising direction for sequential recommendation \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/2403.03900)\].

Another key challenge in sequential recommendation is the modeling of diverse user behaviors and preferences. KATRec incorporates knowledge graphs to enhance item representation by capturing higher-order connections and user-specific patterns \[[15](http://arxiv.org/abs/2012.03323)\]. Similarly, ARERec introduces an attentive local-interaction model that considers both user and item behavior within the interaction sequence, leading to more accurate and personalized recommendations \[[17](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/656670a2f0b632a41eae8b20e28d1f80ec12b697)\]. These approaches emphasize the importance of modeling both user and item dynamics to better understand the underlying patterns in sequential interactions \[[17](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/656670a2f0b632a41eae8b20e28d1f80ec12b697)\].

Time-aware sequential recommendation models have gained significant attention for their ability to capture the temporal dynamics of user interactions. Traditional approaches, such as Markov Chains and Recurrent Neural Networks, have been widely used, but they often neglect the actual timestamps of interactions, focusing only on the order of events \[[1](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/9c7a455b1e48d01a99e58e884a6c1acb75074ad0)\]. More recently, self-attention based models, inspired by the Transformer architecture, have emerged as a powerful alternative, offering better performance in capturing sequential patterns \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/2008.08273)\]. Additionally, the Personalized Transformer (SSE-PT) model has been introduced to enhance personalization by integrating user-specific embeddings, leading to improved performance in recommendation tasks \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/2403.03900)\]. These advancements highlight the ongoing efforts to better model temporal aspects in sequential recommendation, aiming to improve both accuracy and interpretability.

### <a id='category-state-space-modeling-for-temporal-dynamics'></a>State-Space Modeling for Temporal Dynamics: 4 papers found.
State-space modeling for temporal dynamics has emerged as a promising approach to address the challenges of sequential recommendation, particularly in handling long behavior sequences and improving inference efficiency \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/2403.03900)\]. Traditional attention-based models, while effective, suffer from quadratic computational complexity, making them inefficient for long sequences \[[16](http://arxiv.org/abs/2406.02638)\]. In contrast, state space models (SSMs), such as Mamba, offer a more efficient alternative by leveraging hardware-aware parallel algorithms and selective mechanisms to model temporal dependencies with linear complexity \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/2403.03900)\]. This allows for better scalability and performance in scenarios involving lifelong user sequences \[[25](http://arxiv.org/abs/2403.16371)\]. Recent works, such as Mamba4Rec and EchoMamba4Rec, have further advanced this field by integrating SSMs with control theory principles, enabling bi-directional processing and frequency-domain filtering to capture complex user interaction patterns \[[16](http://arxiv.org/abs/2406.02638)\]. Additionally, SSD4Rec demonstrates the potential of Mamba for recommendation tasks by introducing a novel Masked Bidirectional Structured State Space Duality block, which enhances variable-length sequence modeling \[[19](http://arxiv.org/abs/2409.01192)\]. These developments highlight the growing interest in state-space modeling as a viable solution for improving both the efficiency and effectiveness of sequential recommendation systems.

### <a id='category-attention-mechanisms-for-item-relationships'></a>Attention Mechanisms for Item Relationships: 7 papers found.
Attention mechanisms have become a cornerstone in sequential recommendation systems, enabling models to effectively capture complex interactions between users and items \[[1](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/9c7a455b1e48d01a99e58e884a6c1acb75074ad0)\]. These mechanisms allow for the dynamic weighting of item relevance based on user behavior, with models like SASRec leveraging self-attention to identify relevant actions in a user's history \[[1](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/9c7a455b1e48d01a99e58e884a6c1acb75074ad0)\]. Similarly, Category-aware Collaborative Sequential Recommenders utilize item categories to enhance dependency modeling and collaborative learning, particularly in sparse datasets \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/2008.08273)\]. TransRec extends this by modeling third-order interactions between users, items, and next items through a unified transition space \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/2403.03900)\]. ARERec introduces local-interaction modeling by incorporating both user and item behavior within specific interaction periods, improving the accuracy of recommendations by focusing on local rather than global representations \[[4](http://arxiv.org/abs/1808.09781)\]. STOSA further advances the field by introducing stochastic item embeddings and Wasserstein self-attention to better capture uncertainty and collaborative transitivity \[[5](http://arxiv.org/abs/2008.07873)\]. MARank unifies individual- and union-level item interactions through multi-order attentive ranking, enhancing preference inference from multiple perspectives \[[6](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/0b42168f22713c48d8b0ce3bc8f680d347184d88)\]. Finally, AC-TSR addresses attention calibration issues in Transformer-based models, improving the accuracy of attention weight assignment through spatial and adversarial calibration techniques \[[7](http://arxiv.org/abs/2109.05261)\]. These works collectively highlight the evolving use of attention mechanisms to better model user behavior, item relationships, and interaction patterns in sequential recommendation systems.
