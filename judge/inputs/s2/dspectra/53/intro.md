## Related Works

Deep sequential recommendation models have seen significant advancements, driven by the need to capture dynamic user preferences and complex interaction patterns. Traditional approaches such as Markov Chains (MCs) and Recurrent Neural Networks (RNNs) have been widely used, but they often struggle with modeling long-term dependencies and handling sparse or dense datasets effectively \[[4](http://arxiv.org/abs/1808.09781)\]. More recently, self-attention based models like SASRec have emerged as a powerful alternative, enabling the capture of long-term semantics while maintaining computational efficiency \[[4](http://arxiv.org/abs/1808.09781)\]. However, these models typically treat interaction histories as ordered sequences without considering the actual time intervals between interactions, which can lead to suboptimal predictions \[[1](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/9c7a455b1e48d01a99e58e884a6c1acb75074ad0)\].

To address the limitations of traditional models, several works have introduced time-aware mechanisms. For instance, TiSASRec explicitly models the timestamps of interactions within a sequential modeling framework, incorporating both the absolute positions of items and the time intervals between them \[[1](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/9c7a455b1e48d01a99e58e884a6c1acb75074ad0)\]. Similarly, MEANTIME proposes a mixture of attention mechanisms with multi-temporal embeddings to better capture the temporal dynamics of user behavior \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/2008.08273)\]. These models highlight the importance of leveraging temporal information to enhance recommendation accuracy, particularly in scenarios where user behavior evolves over time \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/2008.08273)\].

Efficiency remains a critical challenge for attention-based models, especially when dealing with long sequences. Mamba4Rec introduces a selective state space model (SSM) that enables linear complexity for sequence processing, making it suitable for real-time applications \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/2403.03900)\]. EchoMamba4Rec further enhances this by integrating bi-directional processing with spectral filtering to capture complex patterns in user interaction data \[[16](http://arxiv.org/abs/2406.02638)\]. These models demonstrate that SSMs can effectively balance performance and efficiency, offering a promising direction for sequential recommendation \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/2403.03900)\].

Another key challenge in sequential recommendation is the modeling of diverse user behaviors and preferences. KATRec incorporates knowledge graphs to enhance item representation by capturing higher-order connections and user-specific patterns \[[15](http://arxiv.org/abs/2012.03323)\]. Similarly, ARERec introduces an attentive local-interaction model that considers both user and item behavior within the interaction sequence, leading to more accurate and personalized recommendations \[[17](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/656670a2f0b632a41eae8b20e28d1f80ec12b697)\]. These approaches emphasize the importance of modeling both user and item dynamics to better understand the underlying patterns in sequential interactions \[[17](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/656670a2f0b632a41eae8b20e28d1f80ec12b697)\].

DuoRec introduces a contrastive regularization to reshape the distribution of sequence representations, ensuring that item embeddings maintain meaningful semantic relationships \[[11](http://arxiv.org/abs/2110.05730)\].
