## Related Works

Recent research has explored the dynamics of fine-tuning large language models (LLMs) and how pre-trained features are modified during this process. A key challenge in understanding fine-tuning is the lack of theoretical understanding of why models with millions of parameters can be effectively fine-tuned on small datasets without overfitting \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2210.05643)\]. This issue has motivated the investigation of Neural Tangent Kernels (NTK) to analyze the gradient descent dynamics of fine-tuned models \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2210.05643)\]. The NTK approach has been extended to Adam optimization and used to characterize conditions under which kernel-based dynamics emerge during fine-tuning \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2210.05643)\]. This aligns with our findings that certain layers in LLMs exhibit significant representation shifts during fine-tuning, suggesting a potential connection between kernel-based dynamics and the evolution of feature representations \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2210.05643)\].

Fine-tuning has also been shown to have trade-offs between in-distribution (ID) and out-of-distribution (OOD) performance. Studies have demonstrated that fine-tuning can lead to worse OOD accuracy compared to linear probing, especially when the distribution shift is large \[[10](http://arxiv.org/abs/2202.10054)\]. This suggests that fine-tuning may distort pretrained features, leading to reduced generalization. Our work complements these findings by identifying critical layers in pre-fine-tuned models that are most affected during fine-tuning, offering insights into how to optimize fine-tuning strategies \[[10](http://arxiv.org/abs/2202.10054)\].

The effectiveness of parameter-efficient fine-tuning methods, such as Low-Rank Adaptation (LoRA), has also been widely studied. This aligns with our approach of identifying critical layers that can be fine-tuned with minimal parameter updates, improving both efficiency and robustness \[[8](http://arxiv.org/abs/2407.11046)\]. Additionally, the use of intrinsic dimensionality to analyze fine-tuning has been proposed as a way to understand how models can be tuned effectively on small datasets \[[13](http://arxiv.org/abs/2012.13255)\]. Our work extends this by showing that representation shifts in critical layers are driven by changes in top principal components, which encode semantic transitions \[[13](http://arxiv.org/abs/2012.13255)\].

### <a id='category-intrinsic-dimensionality-and-model-adaptation'></a>Intrinsic Dimensionality and Model Adaptation: 2 papers found.
The concept of intrinsic dimensionality plays a crucial role in understanding how models adapt during fine-tuning, particularly in the context of large language models (LLMs). Recent work has shown that pre-trained models often exhibit a low intrinsic dimension, meaning that a small subset of parameters can capture most of the model's performance when fine-tuned on downstream tasks \[[13](http://arxiv.org/abs/2012.13255)\]. This finding aligns with broader discussions on representation learning, where the effectiveness of models is closely tied to how well they can learn and adapt representations from data \[[5](http://arxiv.org/abs/1206.5538)\]. The low intrinsic dimensionality suggests that models may implicitly minimize this dimension during pre-training, which could explain their effectiveness even when fine-tuned on small datasets \[[13](http://arxiv.org/abs/2012.13255)\]. This insight has implications for improving model efficiency and generalization, as it implies that focusing on key parameters or layers could yield significant performance gains without requiring full parameter updates \[[13](http://arxiv.org/abs/2012.13255)\]. Additionally, the connection between intrinsic dimensionality and task representations highlights the potential for developing more robust generalization bounds that are independent of the full parameter space \[[13](http://arxiv.org/abs/2012.13255)\]. These ideas are part of a growing body of research that seeks to better understand the mechanisms underlying model adaptation and representation learning \[[5](http://arxiv.org/abs/1206.5538)\].

Context: 
	Document 1: [Snippet]: «When transferring a pretrained model to a downstream task, two popular methods are full fine-tuning (updating all the model parameters) and linear probing (updating only the last linear layer—the “head”). It is well known that fine-tuning leads to better accuracy in-distribution (ID). However, in this paper, we find that fine-tuning can achieve worse accuracy than linear probing out-of-distribution (OOD) when the pretrained features are good and the distribution shift is large. On 10 distribution shift datasets, fine-tuning obtains on average 2% higher accuracy ID but 7% lower accuracy OOD than linear probing. We show theoretically that this tradeoff between ID and OOD accuracy arises even in a simple setting: fine-tuning overparameterized two-layer linear networks. We prove that the OOD error of fine-tuning is high when we initialize with a fixed or random head—this is because while fine-tuning learns the head, the lower layers of the neural network change simultaneously and distort the pretrained features. Our analysis suggests that the easy two-step strategy of linear probing then full fine-tuning (LP-FT), sometimes used as a fine-tuning heuristic, combines the benefits of both fine-tuning and linear probing. Empirically, LP-FT outperforms both fine-tuning and linear probing on the above datasets (1% better ID, 10% better OOD than full fine-tuning).»
