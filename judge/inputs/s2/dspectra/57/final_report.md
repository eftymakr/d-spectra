## Related Works

Generative retrieval (GR) has recently gained traction as a promising paradigm in information retrieval (IR), offering a direct mapping from queries to document identifiers (docids) without the need for traditional indexing or reranking \[[2](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/5beaeee1e27007bd12967480bb9c379cebd15e6e)\]. While many GR models have been developed and evaluated using static document collections, their performance in dynamic environments where document corpora evolve continuously remains underexplored \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2308.14968)\]. This section reviews related works that address the challenges of GR in dynamic settings, as well as alternative approaches to docid design and retrieval efficiency.

Several studies have focused on the design of docids for GR models. For instance, \[[4](http://arxiv.org/abs/2304.04171)\] proposes GenRet, a method that learns to tokenize documents into short discrete representations (i.e., docids) via a discrete auto-encoding approach. This method enables end-to-end modeling of document retrieval tasks and achieves state-of-the-art results on benchmark datasets. Similarly, \[[6](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/6682a3679a17d090830635f1c420126d20eae54a)\] introduces NOVO docids, which are unique n-gram sets that can be generated in any order to retrieve the corresponding document. These docids are optimized through training to better learn semantics and relationships between documents, leading to improved retrieval performance.

Efforts to improve the scalability of GR models have also been explored. \[[10](http://arxiv.org/abs/2305.11841)\] investigates how generative retrieval scales to millions of passages, revealing that synthetic queries are crucial for indexing, while existing architectural modifications often fail to account for computational costs. \[[8](http://arxiv.org/abs/2311.09134)\] introduces RIPOR, an optimization framework that enhances generative retrieval by introducing a novel prefix-oriented ranking optimization algorithm and constructing document IDs based on relevance associations between queries and documents. These approaches demonstrate the potential of GR to handle large-scale retrieval tasks.

In dynamic environments, the ability to adapt to evolving document collections is critical. \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2308.14968)\] addresses this by proposing CLEVER, a model that enables incremental indexing of new documents while preserving the ability to answer queries with both previously and newly indexed relevant documents. This work highlights the importance of incremental learning and memory-augmented mechanisms for continual learning in GR. \[[5](http://arxiv.org/abs/2408.02152)\] further explores few-shot indexing, where an LLM is prompted to generate docids for all documents in a corpus without any training, offering a promising direction for reducing training costs.

Additionally, \[[16](http://arxiv.org/abs/2403.12499)\] introduces a listwise generative retrieval approach that optimizes the relevance of entire docid lists rather than individual docids. This method improves retrieval performance by considering the sequential nature of ranking, offering a more principled approach to GR.

### <a id='category-generative-retrieval-(gr)-frameworks'></a>Generative Retrieval (GR) Frameworks: 3 papers found.
Generative retrieval (GR) has gained significant traction in information retrieval (IR) as a paradigm that integrates document retrieval and ranking into a unified model \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2308.14968)\]. Unlike traditional approaches that rely on an index-retrieve-then-rank pipeline, GR leverages sequence-to-sequence models to directly map queries to relevant document identifiers (docids), often using text-based docids for semantic alignment with language models \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2308.14968)\]. Recent advancements have focused on improving the performance of GR models, particularly in dynamic corpora where document collections evolve continuously \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2308.14968)\]. For instance, research has shown that GR models with fine-grained text-based docids exhibit superior generalization to unseen documents, surpassing BM25 and even matching dense retrieval methods \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2308.14968)\]. This is attributed to the semantic alignment of text-based docids with pretrained language models, as well as their high lexical diversity and fine-grained design \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2308.14968)\]. In contrast, numeric-based docids, while efficient, suffer from performance degradation in dynamic settings due to overfitting on the training set and an excessive tendency to favor the initial document set \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2308.14968)\]. To address these limitations, studies have proposed frameworks like GR$^2$ \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/2305.18952)\], which incorporate multi-graded constrained contrastive training to enhance the relevance and distinctness of generated docids. Additionally, optimization frameworks such as RIPOR \[[2](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/5beaeee1e27007bd12967480bb9c379cebd15e6e)\] have been introduced to improve the accuracy of relevance score estimation during sequential docid generation. These developments highlight the ongoing efforts to refine GR models for both static and dynamic environments, emphasizing the balance between efficiency and effectiveness \[[2](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/5beaeee1e27007bd12967480bb9c379cebd15e6e)\]\[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/2305.18952)\].

### <a id='category-docid-design-approaches'></a>DocID Design Approaches: 4 papers found.
Document ID design approaches in generative retrieval have been a focal point of recent research, with various methods proposed to enhance the effectiveness of document retrieval in dynamic environments. Traditional approaches rely on static, rule-based docids such as document titles or BERT-based clustering results, which often fail to capture the full semantic information of documents \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2308.14968)\]. In contrast, methods like GenRet introduce a discrete auto-encoding framework to learn semantic docids through a tokenization model, reconstruction model, and sequence-to-sequence retrieval model, enabling fully end-to-end modeling \[[2](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/5beaeee1e27007bd12967480bb9c379cebd15e6e)\]. Similarly, NOVO proposes learnable docids as unique n-gram sets that can be optimized through query denoising and retrieval tasks, improving both semantic and token representations \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/2305.18952)\]. Ultron further advances this by designing semantically rich docids and introducing a three-stage training workflow to better capture document knowledge and query-docid associations \[[4](http://arxiv.org/abs/2304.04171)\]. These approaches highlight the importance of flexible, learnable docids that can adapt to dynamic corpora, as evidenced by the superior generalization of text-based docids over unseen documents in dynamic settings \[[5](http://arxiv.org/abs/2408.02152)\]. The emphasis on fine-grained docid design and semantic alignment with language models' knowledge underscores the ongoing evolution in document identifier strategies \[[6](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/6682a3679a17d090830635f1c420126d20eae54a)\].
