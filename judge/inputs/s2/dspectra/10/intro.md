## Related Works

Multi-token prediction (MTP) has emerged as an alternative to the traditional next-token prediction (NTP) objective in training large language models (LLMs). While NTP focuses on predicting one token at a time, MTP extends this by predicting multiple future tokens simultaneously, often using multiple independent output heads that share a common model trunk \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2404.19737)\]. This approach has been shown to improve downstream performance, inference speed, and training efficiency, particularly for large models \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2404.19737)\]. However, smaller language models (SLMs) face challenges in effectively leveraging the MTP objective \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2404.19737)\]. To address this, we propose a curriculum learning strategy for MTP training, exploring two variants: a forward curriculum, which gradually increases the complexity of the pre-training objective from NTP to MTP, and a reverse curriculum, which does the opposite \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2404.19737)\].

Curriculum learning has been widely explored in the context of neural networks, where it aims to improve learning efficiency by structuring the training data in an order that aligns with the model's learning capacity \[[12](http://arxiv.org/abs/1704.03003)\]. This concept has been applied to various tasks, including natural language understanding \[[7](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/2d315208dd88eed245dc4ae1ae398af75aa291f8)\] and neural machine translation \[[9](http://arxiv.org/abs/1903.09848)\]. For example, in the case of neural machine translation, curriculum learning has been shown to reduce training time and improve performance by gradually increasing the difficulty of the training examples \[[9](http://arxiv.org/abs/1903.09848)\]. Similarly, in the context of language models, curriculum learning has been used to improve the performance of smaller models by introducing tasks in an order that matches their capacity \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2404.19737)\].

In the context of MTP, the forward curriculum has been shown to enable SLMs to better leverage the MTP objective during pre-training, improving downstream NTP performance and generative output quality while retaining the benefits of self-speculative decoding \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2404.19737)\]. In contrast, the reverse curriculum, which starts with MTP and gradually reduces the number of tokens predicted, achieves stronger NTP performance and output quality but fails to provide any self-speculative decoding benefits \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2404.19737)\]. This suggests that the order in which the model is trained can significantly impact its ability to generalize and perform well on downstream tasks.

The use of curriculum learning in MTP training is not without precedent. For instance, in the context of sequence prediction with recurrent neural networks, scheduled sampling has been proposed as a way to gradually shift the training process from fully guided to less guided, thereby improving performance \[[13](http://arxiv.org/abs/1506.03099)\].
