## Related Works

Diffusion models have become a cornerstone in generative AI, particularly in image and video generation, due to their ability to produce high-quality, realistic outputs \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2406.02540)\]. However, the computational and memory demands of these models, especially diffusion transformers (DiTs), pose significant challenges for deployment on edge devices \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/2406.17343)\]. Several works have explored quantization techniques to reduce these demands, but most focus on image generation, leaving video generation tasks underexplored \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/2312.00845)\].

Post-training quantization (PTQ) has emerged as a promising approach to compress models without retraining, enabling faster inference and reduced memory usage \[[4](http://arxiv.org/abs/2405.16005)\]. For instance, PTQ4DiT addresses the unique challenges of DiT models by handling salient channels and temporal activation variations through channel-wise salience balancing and spearmen’s $\rho$-guided calibration \[[5](http://arxiv.org/abs/2106.14156)\]. Similarly, Q-DiT introduces dynamic activation quantization to adapt to temporal and spatial variations in DiTs, achieving significant performance improvements \[[6](http://arxiv.org/abs/2111.14822)\].

In the context of video generation, the complexity is further heightened by the need to maintain spatiotemporal coherence across frames \[[7](http://arxiv.org/abs/2302.04304)\]. MagicVideo demonstrates efficient video generation using latent diffusion models, leveraging a 3D U-Net design and low-dimensional latent space to reduce computational load \[[8](http://arxiv.org/abs/2306.02316)\]. Similarly, Latte introduces a latent diffusion transformer for video generation, achieving state-of-the-art performance across multiple datasets \[[9](http://arxiv.org/abs/2211.01324)\].

The issue of quantization in video diffusion models has been addressed by QVD, which introduces high temporal discriminability quantization (HTDQ) and scattered channel range integration (SCRI) to handle temporal features and inter-channel disparities \[[10](http://arxiv.org/abs/2408.06072)\]. This work highlights the importance of temporal consistency in video generation, a challenge also addressed by Lumiere, which employs a space-time U-Net to generate full temporal duration in a single pass \[[11](http://arxiv.org/abs/2307.10373)\].

Furthermore, the need for efficient and accurate quantization in video diffusion models has led to the development of Q-VDiT, which specifically targets the challenges of quantization in video DiT models \[[12](http://arxiv.org/abs/1406.2199)\]. The framework includes a token-aware quantization estimator (TQE) to compensate for quantization errors and a temporal maintenance distillation (TMD) module to preserve spatiotemporal correlations \[[13](http://arxiv.org/abs/2401.03048)\].

In addition to quantization, other works have explored the use of diffusion models for video generation, such as CogVideoX, which employs an expert transformer for better text-video alignment \[[14](http://arxiv.org/abs/2305.10657)\].
