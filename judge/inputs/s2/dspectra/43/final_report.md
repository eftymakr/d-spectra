## Related Works

Large language models (LLMs) have seen significant advancements in recent years, particularly in their ability to perform zero-shot and few-shot learning. Early work on zero-shot learning demonstrated that instruction tuning—finetuning models on a collection of tasks described via natural language instructions—can substantially improve performance on unseen tasks \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2109.01652)\]. This approach was further refined with the introduction of FLAN, a 137B parameter model that outperformed GPT-3 on multiple benchmarks \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2109.01652)\]. Scaling instruction tuning to larger models, such as PaLM 540B, has shown even greater improvements, with Flan-PaLM 540B achieving state-of-the-art results on benchmarks like MMLU \[[4](http://arxiv.org/abs/2210.11416)\].

The training of large models also presents significant challenges, particularly in distributed environments. Techniques such as model parallelism have been developed to train models with billions of parameters, enabling the training of models up to 8.3 billion parameters using 512 GPUs \[[5](http://arxiv.org/abs/1909.08053)\]. These techniques are crucial for scaling LLMs and enabling their deployment on large-scale systems.

Recent work has also focused on improving the reasoning capabilities of LLMs. Chain-of-thought (COT) prompting has been shown to enhance reasoning performance, although fine-tuned models often suffer from assessment misalignment, where they assign higher scores to subpar COTs \[[18](http://arxiv.org/abs/2309.02144)\]. To address this, Alignment Fine-Tuning (AFT) has been proposed, which involves fine-tuning models on COT data, generating multiple responses, and calibrating scores using a constraint alignment loss \[[18](http://arxiv.org/abs/2309.02144)\]. This approach has been shown to improve performance on reasoning benchmarks.

In the realm of code generation, Codex, a GPT model fine-tuned on GitHub code, has demonstrated strong capabilities in writing Python code, solving 28.8% of problems on the HumanEval benchmark \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/2107.03374)\]. Repeated sampling from the model has also been found to be an effective strategy for improving performance on difficult prompts \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/2107.03374)\].

Multimodal models have also been explored, with efforts to extend instruction tuning to vision and language tasks. LLaVA, a multimodal model trained using visual instruction tuning, has shown impressive performance on tasks involving images and text \[[10](http://arxiv.org/abs/2304.08485)\]. This work highlights the potential of instruction tuning in the multimodal setting.

### <a id='category-instruction-tuning-and-zero-shot-learning'></a>Instruction Tuning and Zero-Shot Learning: 5 papers found.
Instruction Tuning and Zero-Shot Learning has been a focal area in improving the adaptability and performance of large language models (LLMs) on unseen tasks. Instruction tuning, which involves finetuning models on a collection of tasks described via natural language instructions, has shown substantial improvements in zero-shot performance \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2109.01652)\]. This approach not only enhances the model's ability to generalize across diverse tasks but also surpasses the performance of larger models like GPT-3 in several benchmarks \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2109.01652)\]. Scaling instruction tuning by increasing the number of tasks and model size further amplifies these benefits, leading to state-of-the-art results on various benchmarks such as MMLU and BBH \[[4](http://arxiv.org/abs/2210.11416)\]. Additionally, generating complex instruction data using AI-driven methods, such as Evol-Instruct, allows for the creation of high-quality training data without manual effort, resulting in models like WizardLM that outperform human-created instruction-based models \[[6](http://arxiv.org/abs/2304.12244)\]. Furthermore, integrating hybrid reasoning strategies, such as combining chain-of-thought (CoT) and program-of-thought (PoT) rationales, has led to significant improvements in mathematical reasoning tasks, with models like MAmmoTH achieving competitive performance against much larger models like GPT-4 \[[11](http://arxiv.org/abs/2309.05653)\]. These advancements highlight the critical role of instruction tuning in enhancing the versatility and effectiveness of LLMs across a wide range of applications.

### <a id='category-model-scaling-and-optimization'></a>Model Scaling and Optimization: 3 papers found.
Model scaling and optimization have been pivotal in advancing large language models (LLMs), with recent studies focusing on improving efficiency and performance across different hardware and task constraints \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/2204.02311)\]. Researchers have demonstrated that increasing model size, such as the 540-billion parameter PaLM, leads to significant improvements in few-shot learning and multilingual tasks, often surpassing human performance on standardized benchmarks like BIG-bench \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/2204.02311)\]. Additionally, efforts to optimize LLM inference on memory-constrained devices have led to innovative techniques such as "windowing" and "row-column bundling," which reduce data transfer and improve inference speed by up to 25x \[[9](http://arxiv.org/abs/2312.11514)\]. These methods are crucial for deploying LLMs on edge devices and in resource-limited environments. Furthermore, the development of models like GPT-4 highlights the importance of scalable infrastructure and optimization strategies that enable predictable performance across varying scales, allowing for efficient model training and deployment \[[13](http://arxiv.org/abs/2303.08774)\]. Such advancements underscore the ongoing research into both model architecture and system-level optimizations to enhance the practicality and effectiveness of LLMs.

### <a id='category-reasoning-and-chain-of-thought-prompting'></a>Reasoning and Chain-of-Thought Prompting: 1 papers found.
Context: 
	Document 1: [Snippet]: «Reasoning is a cognitive process of using evidence to reach a sound conclusion. The reasoning capability is essential for large language models (LLMs) to serve as the brain of the artificial general intelligence agent. Recent studies reveal that fine-tuning LLMs on data with the chain of thought (COT) reasoning process can significantly enhance their reasoning capabilities. However, we find that the fine-tuned LLMs suffer from an \textit(Assessment Misalignment) problem, i.e., they frequently assign higher scores to subpar COTs, leading to potential limitations in their reasoning abilities. To address this problem, we introduce an \textit(Alignment Fine-Tuning (AFT)) paradigm, which involves three steps: 1) fine-tuning LLMs with COT training data; 2) generating multiple COT responses for each question, and categorizing them into positive and negative ones based on whether they achieve the correct answer; 3) calibrating the scores of positive and negative responses given by LLMs with a novel constraint alignment loss. Specifically, the constraint alignment loss has two objectives: a) Alignment, which guarantees that positive scores surpass negative scores to encourage answers with high-quality COTs; b) Constraint, which keeps the negative scores confined to a reasonable range to prevent the model degradation. Beyond just the binary positive and negative feedback, the constraint alignment loss can be seamlessly adapted to the ranking situations when ranking feedback is accessible. Furthermore, we also delve deeply into recent ranking-based alignment methods, such as DPO, RRHF, and PRO, and discover that the constraint, which has been overlooked by these approaches, is also crucial for their performance. Extensive experiments on four reasoning benchmarks with both binary and ranking feedback demonstrate the effectiveness of AFT.»
