## Related Works

Maximum Inner Product Search (MIPS) has been extensively studied in recent years due to its critical role in machine learning and information retrieval, particularly in high-dimensional data applications \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/1405.5869)\]. Early approaches to MIPS primarily relied on inner product similarity, which, while straightforward, faced challenges such as local optima and redundant computations \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/1405.5869)\]. To address these issues, researchers have explored reducing the MIPS problem to the Nearest Neighbor Search (NNS) under the Euclidean metric through space projection, although this often leads to topology destruction and information loss \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/1405.5869)\]. 

Despite the divergence between these two paradigms, recent work has argued that there is no inherent binary opposition between inner product and Euclidean metrics \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/1405.5869)\]. Instead, by integrating both metrics in the design of indexing and search algorithms, significant performance improvements can be achieved \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/1405.5869)\]. This idea has been supported by several studies that have explored the theoretical and empirical connections between these two metrics from the MIPS perspective \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/1405.5869)\]. For instance, the work by \[[19](http://arxiv.org/abs/1202.6101)\] proposed a general branch-and-bound algorithm based on a tree data structure, which was later extended to a dual-tree algorithm for multiple queries \[[19](http://arxiv.org/abs/1202.6101)\]. These algorithms introduced novel inner-product bounds that improved the efficiency of the search process \[[19](http://arxiv.org/abs/1202.6101)\].

Graph-based approaches have also gained traction in the field of MIPS. The inner-product navigable small world graph (ip-NSW) has been shown to achieve an order of magnitude speedup over existing baseline methods \[[5](http://arxiv.org/abs/1909.13459)\]. However, the exceptional performance of ip-NSW is attributed to its ability to match the norm bias of the MIPS problem, where large norm items are more likely to be the result of MIPS \[[5](http://arxiv.org/abs/1909.13459)\]. Building on this, the ip-NSW+ algorithm was introduced, which further improves performance by incorporating an additional angular proximity graph \[[5](http://arxiv.org/abs/1909.13459)\]. 

Other approaches have focused on improving the efficiency and accuracy of MIPS through hashing and quantization techniques. Asymmetric Locality Sensitive Hashing (ALSH) has been proposed as a method for sublinear time MIPS, leveraging asymmetric transformations to convert the problem into an approximate near neighbor search \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/1405.5869)\]. Similarly, quantization-based methods have been developed to approximate inner products by decomposing database vectors into multiple subspaces \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/1509.01469)\]. These methods have shown significant improvements over traditional LSH approaches, particularly when combined with learned codebooks \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/1509.01469)\].

### <a id='category-hashing-and-quantization-techniques'></a>Hashing and Quantization Techniques: 7 papers found.
The category "Hashing and Quantization Techniques" encompasses a range of methods aimed at improving the efficiency of Maximum Inner Product Search (MIPS), a critical task in machine learning and information retrieval. Several approaches have been proposed to address the computational challenges of MIPS, including asymmetric hashing schemes and quantization-based methods. For instance, early work demonstrated that asymmetric transformations can convert MIPS into an approximate near neighbor search problem, enabling the use of hashing techniques \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/1405.5869)\]. This idea was further extended with the development of the Asymmetric LSH (ALSH) framework, which allows for more efficient and accurate hashing for MIPS by leveraging asymmetric transformations \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/1610.03317)\]. Additionally, quantization-based approaches have emerged as a promising alternative, where database vectors are quantized into multiple subspaces, and inner products are approximated using these quantizers, avoiding the need for higher-dimensional feature space augmentation \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/1509.01469)\]. Recent advancements have also introduced novel hashing techniques, such as spherical $k$-means, which adapt existing methods for approximate Maximum Cosine Similarity Search (MCSS) to solve MIPS more effectively \[[8](http://arxiv.org/abs/1507.05910)\]. Furthermore, the issue of normalization in real-world datasets has been addressed through methods like NORM-RANGING LSH, which partitions datasets into sub-datasets to handle long-tailed distributions and improve query efficiency \[[10](http://arxiv.org/abs/1809.08782)\]. These techniques collectively highlight the ongoing efforts to develop more efficient and accurate hashing and quantization methods for MIPS, addressing both theoretical and practical challenges.

### <a id='category-inner-product-similarity-approaches'></a>Inner Product Similarity Approaches: 10 papers found.
The category "Inner Product Similarity Approaches" encompasses a range of methods aimed at improving the efficiency and effectiveness of Maximum Inner Product Search (MIPS), a critical task in machine learning and information retrieval. Existing approaches to MIPS often face challenges such as computational inefficiency, suboptimal search quality, and lack of flexibility in balancing search speed and accuracy \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/1610.03317)\]. For instance, brute-force methods are computationally expensive, prompting the development of novel indexing and pruning techniques \[[11](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/0b2790c2c1e23e5535421696e1ec763bbc651bbd)\]. Some works have explored approximate methods, such as clustering-based retrieval, which partitions vectors into clusters to reduce the search space and improve efficiency, particularly for sparse vectors \[[9](http://arxiv.org/abs/2309.09013)\]. Others have introduced budgeted MIPS settings, where the goal is to find the best approximate top-k results under a computational budget, leveraging sampling algorithms like wedge and diamond sampling \[[12](http://arxiv.org/abs/1908.08656)\]. Additionally, there is research on preprocessing-free methods that allow users to control the suboptimality of results with theoretical guarantees, casting MIPS as a Best Arm Identification problem \[[17](http://arxiv.org/abs/1812.06360)\]. Furthermore, the problem of diversity in recommendation lists has been addressed by proposing algorithms that incorporate diversity objectives alongside relevance, such as the diversity-aware k-MIPS problem \[[20](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/87bfc511e4ef4a8aeddad903b06036dbe7177625)\]. These approaches highlight the ongoing efforts to enhance MIPS by addressing computational constraints, improving search quality, and incorporating additional objectives like diversity and fairness \[[23](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/0a27db33fcdb1117c971263ada20718828a250d1)\].

### <a id='category-graph-based-search-algorithms'></a>Graph-Based Search Algorithms: 4 papers found.
The category "Graph-Based Search Algorithms" encompasses several approaches to efficiently solving the Maximum Inner Product Search (MIPS) problem, which is critical in high-dimensional data applications such as recommendation systems and information retrieval. Existing methods often rely on graph-based indexing strategies that leverage the properties of inner product similarity. For instance, the inner-product navigable small world graph (ip-NSW) has been shown to achieve significant speedups over traditional baselines by exploiting the norm bias in MIPS, where items with larger norms are more likely to be relevant \[[5](http://arxiv.org/abs/1909.13459)\]. This bias is effectively captured by ip-NSW, as it constructs a proximity graph where large norm items have high in-degrees, allowing the search process to focus on these critical points \[[5](http://arxiv.org/abs/1909.13459)\]. Similarly, the norm adjusted proximity graph (NAPG) improves upon existing graph-based methods by incorporating adjustment factors derived from sampled data, enabling more meaningful connections during graph construction \[[7](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/25454f456c514219c9d6eeb42200ebbf378db9ca)\]. Another approach, the Inner Product Delaunay Graph (IPDG), transforms MIPS into a graph search problem, offering efficiency gains by leveraging geometric properties of the data \[[15](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/4d0dce74babcf393b5a4cbcb11c89ccca6a3b92b)\]. These methods collectively highlight the importance of graph-based indexing in MIPS, with each approach offering distinct advantages depending on the data topology \[[15](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/4d0dce74babcf393b5a4cbcb11c89ccca6a3b92b)\]. The development of these algorithms reflects a growing trend toward hybrid strategies that combine insights from both inner product and Euclidean metrics to enhance search performance \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/1405.5869)\].
