## Related Works

Maximum Inner Product Search (MIPS) has been extensively studied in recent years due to its critical role in machine learning and information retrieval, particularly in high-dimensional data applications \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/1405.5869)\]. Early approaches to MIPS primarily relied on inner product similarity, which, while straightforward, faced challenges such as local optima and redundant computations \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/1405.5869)\]. To address these issues, researchers have explored reducing the MIPS problem to the Nearest Neighbor Search (NNS) under the Euclidean metric through space projection, although this often leads to topology destruction and information loss \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/1405.5869)\]. 

Despite the divergence between these two paradigms, recent work has argued that there is no inherent binary opposition between inner product and Euclidean metrics \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/1405.5869)\]. Instead, by integrating both metrics in the design of indexing and search algorithms, significant performance improvements can be achieved \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/1405.5869)\]. This idea has been supported by several studies that have explored the theoretical and empirical connections between these two metrics from the MIPS perspective \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/1405.5869)\]. For instance, the work by \[[19](http://arxiv.org/abs/1202.6101)\] proposed a general branch-and-bound algorithm based on a tree data structure, which was later extended to a dual-tree algorithm for multiple queries \[[19](http://arxiv.org/abs/1202.6101)\]. These algorithms introduced novel inner-product bounds that improved the efficiency of the search process \[[19](http://arxiv.org/abs/1202.6101)\].

Graph-based approaches have also gained traction in the field of MIPS. The inner-product navigable small world graph (ip-NSW) has been shown to achieve an order of magnitude speedup over existing baseline methods \[[5](http://arxiv.org/abs/1909.13459)\]. However, the exceptional performance of ip-NSW is attributed to its ability to match the norm bias of the MIPS problem, where large norm items are more likely to be the result of MIPS \[[5](http://arxiv.org/abs/1909.13459)\]. Building on this, the ip-NSW+ algorithm was introduced, which further improves performance by incorporating an additional angular proximity graph \[[5](http://arxiv.org/abs/1909.13459)\]. 

Other approaches have focused on improving the efficiency and accuracy of MIPS through hashing and quantization techniques. Asymmetric Locality Sensitive Hashing (ALSH) has been proposed as a method for sublinear time MIPS, leveraging asymmetric transformations to convert the problem into an approximate near neighbor search \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/1405.5869)\]. Similarly, quantization-based methods have been developed to approximate inner products by decomposing database vectors into multiple subspaces \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/1509.01469)\]. These methods have shown significant improvements over traditional LSH approaches, particularly when combined with learned codebooks \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/1509.01469)\].
