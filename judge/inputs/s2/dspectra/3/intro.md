## Related Works

Recent advancements in Text-to-Speech (TTS) systems have significantly enhanced speech naturalness, leading to increased demand for precise prosody control and mispronunciation correction \[[25](http://arxiv.org/abs/2110.02854)\]. Traditional approaches for prosody manipulation often rely on specialized modules or additional training, which limits their ability to perform post-hoc adjustments \[[25](http://arxiv.org/abs/2110.02854)\]. Similarly, conventional mispronunciation correction methods depend on grapheme-to-phoneme dictionaries, which are less practical in low-resource settings \[[25](http://arxiv.org/abs/2110.02854)\]. This section reviews related works that address these challenges, focusing on methods for prosody control, mispronunciation correction, and post-hoc TTS modifications.

Several studies have explored prosody modeling in TTS systems. For instance, \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/1909.10302)\] introduces a fully automatic method to enable sentence-wise speaking pace and expressiveness control, while \[[8](http://arxiv.org/abs/2011.06465)\] proposes a hierarchical architecture that conditions phoneme-level prosody features on word-level prosody features. These approaches aim to improve the naturalness and controllability of synthesized speech. However, they often require explicit prosody annotations or are limited in their ability to perform post-hoc adjustments \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/1909.10302)\]\[[8](http://arxiv.org/abs/2011.06465)\].

Mispronunciation correction in TTS has also been a focus of recent research. \[[10](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/fbf2a6a887ea92311cf207d522c535daf867a6ba)\] explores the use of pre-trained text embeddings, such as BERT, to encode input phrases and improve prosody and pronunciation in Tacotron2-based systems. This approach leverages semantic information from the text to enhance the naturalness of the synthesized speech. Similarly, \[[19](http://arxiv.org/abs/2011.05161)\] investigates the use of cross-utterance BERT embeddings to improve prosody generation for end-to-end speech synthesis, demonstrating that incorporating contextual information from neighboring sentences can enhance the naturalness and expressiveness of the output \[[10](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/fbf2a6a887ea92311cf207d522c535daf867a6ba)\]\[[19](http://arxiv.org/abs/2011.05161)\].

Non-autoregressive TTS models have also been proposed to improve inference efficiency and controllability. \[[11](http://arxiv.org/abs/2010.11439)\] introduces Parallel Tacotron, a non-autoregressive model that uses a variational autoencoder-based residual encoder to enable efficient synthesis. This model relaxes the one-to-many mapping nature of TTS and improves naturalness by allowing diverse rhythms and pitches. \[[16](http://arxiv.org/abs/2110.02952)\] further extends this idea by introducing hierarchical prosody modeling in non-autoregressive parallel neural TTS, enabling fine-grained control over prosody dimensions such as pitch, duration, and energy \[[11](http://arxiv.org/abs/2010.11439)\]\[[16](http://arxiv.org/abs/2110.02952)\].
