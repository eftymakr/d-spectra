## Related Works

Recent advancements in Text-to-Speech (TTS) systems have significantly enhanced speech naturalness, leading to increased demand for precise prosody control and mispronunciation correction \[[25](http://arxiv.org/abs/2110.02854)\]. Traditional approaches for prosody manipulation often rely on specialized modules or additional training, which limits their ability to perform post-hoc adjustments \[[25](http://arxiv.org/abs/2110.02854)\]. Similarly, conventional mispronunciation correction methods depend on grapheme-to-phoneme dictionaries, which are less practical in low-resource settings \[[25](http://arxiv.org/abs/2110.02854)\]. This section reviews related works that address these challenges, focusing on methods for prosody control, mispronunciation correction, and post-hoc TTS modifications.

Several studies have explored prosody modeling in TTS systems. These approaches aim to improve the naturalness and controllability of synthesized speech. However, they often require explicit prosody annotations or are limited in their ability to perform post-hoc adjustments \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/1909.10302)\]\[[8](http://arxiv.org/abs/2011.06465)\].

Furthermore, approaches such as Prosody-TTS explicitly model phoneme duration and fundamental frequency ($f_0$) to achieve finer-level control over prosody during synthesis \[[25](http://arxiv.org/abs/2110.02854)\]. These methods highlight the ongoing efforts to improve prosody control in TTS systems, either through enhanced modeling techniques or by introducing more flexible and controllable architectures.

### <a id='category-non-autoregressive-tts-models'></a>Non-Autoregressive TTS Models: 4 papers found.
Non-autoregressive text-to-speech (TTS) models have gained significant attention for their ability to generate speech more efficiently than traditional autoregressive models. These models, such as Transformer TTS \[[2](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/9f2dd5cc190fc713f1339fca838a5537931744f8)\], FastSpeech \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/2006.04558)\], and Parallel Tacotron \[[11](http://arxiv.org/abs/2010.11439)\], leverage parallel processing and advanced architectures like the Transformer and variational autoencoder to improve both training efficiency and speech quality. For instance, Transformer TTS replaces recurrent structures with multi-head attention mechanisms, enabling parallel computation and better modeling of long-range dependencies \[[2](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/9f2dd5cc190fc713f1339fca838a5537931744f8)\]. Similarly, FastSpeech introduces a teacher-student distillation pipeline to simplify the one-to-many mapping problem, though it faces limitations in voice quality due to information loss during distillation \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/2006.04558)\]. Parallel Tacotron further enhances efficiency by using a variational autoencoder-based residual encoder, allowing for parallelizable training and inference while improving naturalness through local context modeling \[[11](http://arxiv.org/abs/2010.11439)\]. These models collectively demonstrate the potential of non-autoregressive approaches to achieve high-quality speech synthesis with reduced computational costs.
