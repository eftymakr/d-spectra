## Related Works

De novo genome assembly has been a focal point of computational genomics, driven by the advent of next-generation sequencing (NGS) technologies that generate massive volumes of short reads \[[1](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/c6e319a023f932d6d2ff8897c2c91e56e872db8e)\]. Early efforts focused on developing algorithms capable of handling the computational complexity of assembling genomes from these short sequences \[[2](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/f8d1052e2c9d1cdd63093427ccc9169e848d777d)\]. For instance, SOAPdenovo and its successor SOAPdenovo2 have been instrumental in improving the continuity, accuracy, and coverage of de novo assemblies, particularly in repeat regions \[[3](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/2b59fd18ada769ef76fb7fcc7382f063791abfab)\]. These tools have demonstrated significant improvements in assembly quality, with SOAPdenovo2 achieving a contig N50 of ~20.9 kbp and scaffold N50 of ~22 Mbp for the 2008 Asian genome \[[4](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/d04b3cc774151260a59a5e6768b6cfae7fc3e512)\].

The challenges of de novo assembly are not limited to algorithmic improvements. The inherent limitations of short-read sequencing, such as the inability to resolve repetitive regions and the high computational demands, have spurred the development of more sophisticated assemblers \[[5](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/fbd59e17f72c3aedb3cd06af2e50a91526ccd81a)\]. For example, SHARCGS has been successfully applied to assemble short-read data with high accuracy and speed, achieving N50 sizes greater than 20 kbp for Drosophila and Arabidopsis \[[6](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/7279acd9c61a8de019ea2d55d23124dfbb6b5269)\]. Similarly, ALLPATHS has been used to generate high-quality assemblies from whole-genome shotgun microreads, demonstrating the potential of graph-based approaches to retain intrinsic ambiguities in the assembly process \[[7](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/9cbccbe327e51dffd5c9b8b3199b6958d000b902)\].

Long-read sequencing technologies, such as PacBio and Oxford Nanopore, have further transformed de novo assembly by enabling the reconstruction of reference-quality genomes \[[8](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/f5cfb387a6bd8a1cb7bdf07562fadb9a351e37cd)\]. Canu, designed specifically for noisy single-molecule sequences, has shown remarkable performance in assembling complete microbial genomes and near-complete eukaryotic chromosomes, achieving a contig NG50 of greater than 21 Mbp on human and Drosophila PacBio datasets \[[9](http://arxiv.org/abs/1304.3752)\]. These advancements have been complemented by tools like Minimap2, which provides efficient alignment of long reads and assembly contigs, offering significant speed improvements over existing mappers \[[10](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/3266b66caeb3986cbe6a299620af43c10eb2dee3)\].

Despite these advancements, the computational challenges of de novo assembly persist. The high memory footprint and irregular data patterns of short-read assemblies have led to the development of parallel and distributed assemblers, such as PASHA, which leverages hybrid computing architectures to improve scalability and efficiency \[[11](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/d7827222e6d751e13b1fe74b97ce413a037abfaf)\]. Similarly, SWAP-Assembler has been designed for processing massive sequencing data using thousands of cores, demonstrating scalability and contig quality comparable to existing assemblers \[[12](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/dbb41f80dc43d139761a863eb7d6da773a726261)\].

### <a id='category-computational-challenges-in-de-novo-assembly'></a>Computational Challenges in De Novo Assembly: 9 papers found.
The computational challenges in de novo assembly are well-documented, with multiple studies highlighting the limitations of current methods in handling large datasets and complex genomic structures \[[1](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/c6e319a023f932d6d2ff8897c2c91e56e872db8e)\]. Short-read sequencing technologies, while offering high throughput and cost efficiency, result in fragmented assemblies due to their limited read lengths, making de novo assembly particularly challenging \[[4](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/d04b3cc774151260a59a5e6768b6cfae7fc3e512)\]. This issue is exacerbated by the presence of repetitive sequences, which introduce ambiguities and biases in both alignment and assembly processes \[[18](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/2501576dc02c6ca1504b045885eb1cec8d4d3f16)\]. As a result, existing assemblers often struggle with memory constraints, irregular data patterns, and the inability to fully resolve structural variations \[[8](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/f5cfb387a6bd8a1cb7bdf07562fadb9a351e37cd)\]. Several studies have evaluated the performance of different assemblers, noting that string-based and overlap-layout-consensus (OLC) methods are more suitable for short reads and small genomes, whereas De Bruijn graph-based assemblers are better suited for large datasets \[[6](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/7279acd9c61a8de019ea2d55d23124dfbb6b5269)\]. Additionally, the accuracy and completeness of de novo assemblies are often compromised, with significant portions of the genome missing due to repeat regions and heterozygosity \[[10](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/3266b66caeb3986cbe6a299620af43c10eb2dee3)\]. To address these challenges, there is a growing need for improved theoretical models and heuristic approaches that can better capture the parametric complexity of the assembly problem \[[4](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/d04b3cc774151260a59a5e6768b6cfae7fc3e512)\]. Furthermore, recent work has focused on developing assembly-independent methods for estimating genomic characteristics, such as genome size and repeat content, which can aid in the design of more effective sequencing strategies and assembly algorithms \[[17](http://arxiv.org/abs/1308.2012)\]. These findings underscore the ongoing need for innovative solutions to enhance the efficiency and accuracy of de novo genome assembly.

### <a id='category-graph-based-assembly-tools'></a>Graph-Based Assembly Tools: 3 papers found.
Graph-based assembly tools have been pivotal in addressing the challenges of de novo genome assembly, particularly with the advent of high-throughput sequencing technologies that generate vast amounts of short reads \[[7](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/9cbccbe327e51dffd5c9b8b3199b6958d000b902)\]. These tools leverage de Bruijn graphs to represent overlapping k-mer sequences, enabling efficient traversal and assembly of complex genomic regions \[[15](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/91c964330beeed2c5c875d18633793a548a987cf)\]. For instance, early approaches demonstrated that bacterial genomes like *Campylobacter jejuni* and *Escherichia coli* can be assembled into single, perfect contigs using graph-based methods, while larger genomes produce highly connected and accurate assemblies represented as intricate graphs \[[7](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/9cbccbe327e51dffd5c9b8b3199b6958d000b902)\]. More recent advancements, such as the use of colored de Bruijn graphs, have enabled the detection and genotyping of both simple and complex genetic variants, including structural variations and sequences absent from reference genomes \[[15](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/91c964330beeed2c5c875d18633793a548a987cf)\]. Additionally, the integration of graph simplification techniques, such as those based on message passing neural networks (MPNN), has further improved the scalability and efficiency of these tools, allowing them to handle large genomic datasets with dynamic and interdependent structures \[[26](http://arxiv.org/abs/2011.05013)\]. These developments highlight the ongoing evolution of graph-based assembly tools, which continue to play a critical role in advancing genome sequencing and analysis.

### <a id='category-parallel-and-distributed-assemblers'></a>Parallel and Distributed Assemblers: 4 papers found.
Parallel and distributed assemblers have emerged as critical tools for addressing the computational challenges of de novo genome assembly, particularly for large and complex genomes. These assemblers leverage parallelization strategies to improve efficiency and scalability, often utilizing distributed-memory architectures or hybrid computing models that combine shared-memory multi-core CPUs with message-passing systems \[[22](http://arxiv.org/abs/2010.10055)\]. For instance, diBELLA 2D introduces new distributed-memory parallel algorithms for overlap detection and layout simplification, achieving near-linear scaling with over 80% parallel efficiency for the human genome \[[22](http://arxiv.org/abs/2010.10055)\]. Similarly, SWAP-Assembler employs a scalable computational framework based on a small-world asynchronous parallel model, enabling efficient processing of massive sequencing data across thousands of cores \[[25](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/fe8d86bc5520b5049104ff58ee7e980982816ebf)\]. PASHA further demonstrates the effectiveness of hybrid parallelism by integrating multi-threading on multi-core CPUs with message passing on compute clusters, resulting in faster and more contiguous assemblies compared to existing tools like Velvet, ABySS, and SOAPdenovo \[[27](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/7bf2f1c55f90e11a4d1df4dd608ac621a08f8b33)\]. Additionally, HipMer focuses on efficient parallelization of the Meraculous code to enable extreme-scale analysis, showcasing performance results on large genomes such as the human genome and hexaploid wheat \[[19](http://arxiv.org/abs/1705.11147)\].
