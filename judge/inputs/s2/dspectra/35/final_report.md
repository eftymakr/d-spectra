## Related Works

Protein structure prediction and conformational sampling have seen significant advancements with the integration of deep learning techniques. Traditional methods such as molecular dynamics (MD) simulations are computationally intensive and often limited in their ability to sample rare events \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2402.04845)\]. Recent approaches have leveraged generative models, particularly denoising diffusion models, to predict protein structures and sample conformational ensembles more efficiently \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/2004.13167)\]. These models learn distributions over crystallographic structures, enabling accurate and rapid prediction of protein conformations \[[3](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/2eb1ed9d4baf548391d8220c0a8a3d0be79845ed)\]. However, integrating physical constraints into these data-driven models remains a challenge, as standard energy-based objectives often lead to intractable optimization \[[4](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/21a4afa74caf78e28880b7fe4c30a09a5f9c14e3)\].

Several studies have explored energy-based models for protein conformations, operating at atomic scale and trained solely on crystallized protein data \[[5](http://arxiv.org/abs/2205.15019)\]. These models have demonstrated performance comparable to established methods like Rosetta, capturing physicochemical properties relevant to protein energy \[[6](http://arxiv.org/abs/2206.13517)\]. Additionally, Boltzmann generators have been developed to sample equilibrium states of many-body systems using deep learning and statistical mechanics, enabling efficient computation of free-energy differences and discovery of new configurations \[[7](http://arxiv.org/abs/2209.15611)\]. These models learn coordinate transformations to simplify sampling in complex systems \[[8](http://arxiv.org/abs/1503.03585)\].

The use of diffusion models for protein structure generation has also gained traction. A latent diffusion model has been proposed to reduce the complexity of protein modeling while flexibly capturing the distribution of natural protein structures in a condensed latent space \[[9](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/dc32a984b651256a8ec282be52310e6bd33d9815)\]. Equivariant protein autoencoders have been employed to embed proteins into a latent space, followed by an equivariant diffusion model to learn the distribution of latent representations \[[10](http://arxiv.org/abs/2311.11459)\]. This approach enables the generation of novel protein backbone structures with high designability and efficiency \[[11](http://arxiv.org/abs/2403.14088)\].

This method mirrors the native twisting of proteins into energetically favorable conformations, alleviating the need for complex equivariant networks \[[13](http://arxiv.org/abs/1501.03971)\].
