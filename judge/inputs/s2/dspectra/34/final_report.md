## Related Works

Time series forecasting has been a central challenge in various domains, including finance, energy, and traffic management. Early approaches relied on classical statistical models such as ARIMA and exponential smoothing, but these models often struggled with capturing complex, non-linear patterns in high-dimensional and long-term data \[[27](http://arxiv.org/abs/2205.13504)\]. With the advent of deep learning, recurrent neural networks (RNNs) and long short-term memory (LSTM) networks became popular for their ability to model temporal dependencies \[[27](http://arxiv.org/abs/2205.13504)\]. However, RNNs and LSTMs suffer from vanishing gradients and are inefficient for long-term forecasting \[[13](http://arxiv.org/abs/2308.11200)\]. 

To address these limitations, Transformer-based models have gained significant traction due to their ability to capture long-range dependencies through self-attention mechanisms \[[27](http://arxiv.org/abs/2205.13504)\]. However, Transformers face challenges in time series forecasting, such as the locality-agnostic issue and memory bottlenecks, where the quadratic complexity of attention mechanisms makes them impractical for long sequences \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/1907.00235)\]. To mitigate these issues, several works have proposed modifications, such as convolutional self-attention \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/1907.00235)\], LogSparse Transformers \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/1907.00235)\], and Autoformer with Auto-Correlation \[[10](http://arxiv.org/abs/2106.13008)\]. These models aim to improve efficiency and accuracy by incorporating local patterns and reducing computational overhead.

In the financial domain, time series forecasting is crucial for tasks like asset allocation and risk management. Traditional approaches often use prediction-only or predict-then-optimize strategies, which can lead to goal mismatch and uncertainty in forecasts \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2211.14730)\]. Recent work has focused on developing more robust frameworks that integrate forecasting and allocation in a unified manner. For instance, the Risk-aware Time-Series Predict-and-Allocate (RTS-PnO) framework \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2211.14730)\] introduces an end-to-end approach that aligns objectives, calibrates uncertainty, and is agnostic to forecasting models. This framework has been evaluated on multiple financial datasets, showing significant improvements over existing baselines \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2211.14730)\].

Other works have explored the use of pre-trained models for time series analysis, leveraging large-scale data to improve generalization and performance \[[6](http://arxiv.org/abs/2302.11939)\]. For example, the Frozen Pretrained Transformer (FPT) has been used to fine-tune on various time series tasks, achieving comparable or state-of-the-art results \[[6](http://arxiv.org/abs/2302.11939)\]. Similarly, the Temporal Fusion Transformer (TFT) has been introduced for interpretable multi-horizon forecasting, combining recurrent layers with self-attention mechanisms to model both local and global dependencies \[[4](http://arxiv.org/abs/1912.09363)\].

### <a id='category-transformer-based-models'></a>Transformer-Based Models: 7 papers found.
Recent advancements in Transformer-based models have significantly impacted time series forecasting, addressing challenges such as long-term dependency modeling, computational efficiency, and handling non-stationary data \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2211.14730)\]\[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/1907.00235)\]\[[10](http://arxiv.org/abs/2106.13008)\]. Models like PatchTST introduce efficient patching strategies to retain local semantic information while reducing computational overhead \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2211.14730)\]. Similarly, LogSparse Transformer mitigates the memory bottleneck by achieving $O(L(\log L)^2)$ complexity, enabling better performance on fine-grained time series \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/1907.00235)\]. Autoformer further improves long-term forecasting by introducing an Auto-Correlation mechanism that outperforms traditional self-attention in both efficiency and accuracy \[[10](http://arxiv.org/abs/2106.13008)\]. Meanwhile, Non-stationary Transformers tackle the issue of over-stationarization by incorporating modules that preserve inherent non-stationarity, leading to significant performance gains \[[14](http://arxiv.org/abs/2205.14415)\]. Additionally, ScaleFormer demonstrates that multi-scale refinement with shared weights can enhance forecasting accuracy across various architectures \[[24](http://arxiv.org/abs/2206.04038)\]. However, some studies question the validity of Transformer-based approaches for long-term forecasting, highlighting that simple linear models can outperform complex Transformers in certain scenarios \[[27](http://arxiv.org/abs/2205.13504)\]. These developments collectively highlight the ongoing evolution of Transformer-based models in time series forecasting, emphasizing both their strengths and limitations.

### <a id='category-time-series-forecasting-models'></a>Time Series Forecasting Models: 10 papers found.
Time Series Forecasting Models have seen significant advancements with the integration of deep learning techniques, particularly in addressing the challenges of multivariate and long-term forecasting. Traditional methods often struggle with capturing complex temporal dependencies and inter-series correlations, while also being limited in scalability and adaptability \[[15](http://arxiv.org/abs/2103.07719)\]. Recent approaches, such as the Spectral Temporal Graph Neural Network (StemGNN), have introduced innovative methods to jointly model inter-series correlations and temporal dependencies in the spectral domain, improving forecast accuracy by learning these relationships automatically from data rather than relying on pre-defined priors \[[15](http://arxiv.org/abs/2103.07719)\]. Similarly, the TPA-LSTM model enhances forecasting by incorporating a novel attention mechanism that leverages frequency domain information to better capture temporal patterns across multiple time steps \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/1809.04206)\]. These models are complemented by frameworks like DeepAR, which utilize auto-regressive recurrent networks trained on large datasets to produce accurate probabilistic forecasts, achieving notable improvements over classical methods \[[9](http://arxiv.org/abs/1704.04110)\]. Additionally, the CoST framework applies contrastive learning to disentangle seasonal and trend components, enabling more robust and accurate forecasting across various domains \[[26](http://arxiv.org/abs/2202.01575)\]. The introduction of hybrid models that combine the strengths of classical methods with deep learning, such as those that integrate uncertainty estimation and scalable forecasting capabilities, further highlights the evolving landscape of time series forecasting \[[16](http://arxiv.org/abs/1905.12417)\]. These developments underscore the growing emphasis on models that can handle the complexity of real-world time series data while maintaining interpretability and adaptability \[[17](http://arxiv.org/abs/1711.11053)\].

### <a id='category-pre-trained-models-for-time-series'></a>Pre-trained Models for Time Series: 3 papers found.
The category "Pre-trained Models for Time Series" explores the application of pre-trained models originally developed for natural language processing (NLP) and computer vision (CV) to time series analysis. These models, such as the Frozen Pretrained Transformer (FPT), leverage the self-attention mechanism to capture temporal dependencies without altering the original architecture, enabling them to be fine-tuned for various tasks like classification, anomaly detection, and forecasting \[[6](http://arxiv.org/abs/2302.11939)\]. The self-attention module has been shown to behave similarly to principal component analysis (PCA), which helps explain how these models bridge the domain gap and achieve universality across different time series tasks \[[6](http://arxiv.org/abs/2302.11939)\]. Additionally, frameworks like Chronos tokenize time series data into a fixed vocabulary and train transformer-based models on this tokenized data, achieving state-of-the-art performance on diverse datasets \[[20](http://arxiv.org/abs/2403.07815)\]. These approaches demonstrate that pre-trained models can significantly improve zero-shot performance on unseen forecasting tasks, simplifying the forecasting pipeline by leveraging knowledge from multiple domains \[[20](http://arxiv.org/abs/2403.07815)\]. The broader field of Foundation Models (FMs) has also contributed to time series analysis by introducing novel architectures, pre-training techniques, and adaptation methods that enhance the generalization and effectiveness of models across various downstream tasks \[[28](http://arxiv.org/abs/2403.14735)\]. These developments highlight the growing importance of pre-trained models in addressing the unique challenges of time series analysis.
