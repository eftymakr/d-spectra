## Related Works

Time series forecasting has been a central challenge in various domains, including finance, energy, and traffic management. Early approaches relied on classical statistical models such as ARIMA and exponential smoothing, but these models often struggled with capturing complex, non-linear patterns in high-dimensional and long-term data \[[27](http://arxiv.org/abs/2205.13504)\]. With the advent of deep learning, recurrent neural networks (RNNs) and long short-term memory (LSTM) networks became popular for their ability to model temporal dependencies \[[27](http://arxiv.org/abs/2205.13504)\]. However, RNNs and LSTMs suffer from vanishing gradients and are inefficient for long-term forecasting \[[13](http://arxiv.org/abs/2308.11200)\]. 

To address these limitations, Transformer-based models have gained significant traction due to their ability to capture long-range dependencies through self-attention mechanisms \[[27](http://arxiv.org/abs/2205.13504)\]. However, Transformers face challenges in time series forecasting, such as the locality-agnostic issue and memory bottlenecks, where the quadratic complexity of attention mechanisms makes them impractical for long sequences \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/1907.00235)\]. To mitigate these issues, several works have proposed modifications, such as convolutional self-attention \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/1907.00235)\], LogSparse Transformers \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/1907.00235)\], and Autoformer with Auto-Correlation \[[10](http://arxiv.org/abs/2106.13008)\]. These models aim to improve efficiency and accuracy by incorporating local patterns and reducing computational overhead.

In the financial domain, time series forecasting is crucial for tasks like asset allocation and risk management. Traditional approaches often use prediction-only or predict-then-optimize strategies, which can lead to goal mismatch and uncertainty in forecasts \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2211.14730)\]. Recent work has focused on developing more robust frameworks that integrate forecasting and allocation in a unified manner. For instance, the Risk-aware Time-Series Predict-and-Allocate (RTS-PnO) framework \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2211.14730)\] introduces an end-to-end approach that aligns objectives, calibrates uncertainty, and is agnostic to forecasting models. This framework has been evaluated on multiple financial datasets, showing significant improvements over existing baselines \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2211.14730)\].

Other works have explored the use of pre-trained models for time series analysis, leveraging large-scale data to improve generalization and performance \[[6](http://arxiv.org/abs/2302.11939)\]. For example, the Frozen Pretrained Transformer (FPT) has been used to fine-tune on various time series tasks, achieving comparable or state-of-the-art results \[[6](http://arxiv.org/abs/2302.11939)\]. Similarly, the Temporal Fusion Transformer (TFT) has been introduced for interpretable multi-horizon forecasting, combining recurrent layers with self-attention mechanisms to model both local and global dependencies \[[4](http://arxiv.org/abs/1912.09363)\].
