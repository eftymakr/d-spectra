## Related Works

Unsupervised pre-training has emerged as a powerful paradigm for learning rich representations from unlabeled data. Several approaches have been proposed to leverage this paradigm in various domains, including computer vision and natural language processing. For instance, \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/1505.05192)\] explores the use of spatial context as a supervisory signal for training a rich visual representation, where a convolutional neural network is trained to predict the position of one patch relative to another. This approach demonstrates that such models can capture visual similarity across images and improve performance in object detection tasks. Similarly, \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/1807.03748)\] introduces Contrastive Predictive Coding, a universal unsupervised learning approach that leverages autoregressive models to predict future samples in latent space, achieving strong performance across speech, images, text, and reinforcement learning domains.

In the field of computer vision, \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/1206.5538)\] presents a method for unsupervised visual representation learning by solving jigsaw puzzles, where the model learns to recognize the geometric arrangement of image tiles. This approach outperforms existing unsupervised features in object detection and classification tasks. Additionally, \[[4](http://arxiv.org/abs/2203.06041)\] proposes a method for unsupervised feature learning by predicting image rotations, which provides a powerful supervisory signal for semantic feature learning. The model achieves state-of-the-art performance on various benchmarks, including PASCAL VOC 2007 detection, demonstrating that unsupervised pre-training can significantly close the gap with supervised learning.

In the context of natural language processing, \[[5](http://arxiv.org/abs/1901.09005)\] introduces SpanBERT, which improves pre-training by representing and predicting spans of text. This approach extends BERT by masking contiguous random spans and training the model to predict the entire content of the masked span, leading to substantial gains in tasks such as question answering and coreference resolution. Furthermore, \[[6](http://arxiv.org/abs/1606.04586)\] presents a robustly optimized BERT pretraining approach, highlighting the importance of hyperparameter choices and training data size in achieving state-of-the-art results on GLUE, RACE, and SQuAD benchmarks.

In the domain of semi-supervised learning, \[[7](http://arxiv.org/abs/1905.09272)\] explores techniques such as randomized data augmentation and dropout to improve generalization and stability for classifiers trained with limited labeled data. The proposed method minimizes the difference between predictions from multiple passes of a training sample through the network, leading to better performance on benchmark datasets. Additionally, \[[8](http://arxiv.org/abs/1609.02907)\] introduces a scalable approach for semi-supervised learning on graph-structured data using graph convolutional networks, which outperforms related methods by a significant margin.
