## Related Works

Cross-domain sequential recommendation (CDSR) has gained significant attention in recent years due to its ability to alleviate data sparsity and cold-start problems in traditional sequential recommendation systems \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2304.03891)\]. Several approaches have been proposed to address these challenges, primarily leveraging self-attention mechanisms and domain-specific modules to enhance performance \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/1808.09781)\]. For instance, the Self-Attentive Sequential Recommendation (SASRec) model \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/1808.09781)\] introduced a self-attention mechanism to capture long-term dependencies in user behavior sequences, outperforming traditional methods like Markov Chains and RNNs. However, these models often rely on domain-specific components, which may not fully exploit the inherent power of the self-attention mechanism \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/2311.04590)\].

To address this gap, the Contrastive Cross-Domain Sequential Recommendation (C2DSR) model \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2304.03891)\] was proposed to simultaneously leverage intra- and inter-sequence item relationships, aiming to capture precise user preferences across domains. The model employs a graph neural network to mine inter-sequence collaborative relationships and a sequential attentive encoder to capture intra-sequence patterns \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2304.03891)\]. Additionally, the paper introduces a contrastive cross-domain infomax objective to enhance the correlation between single- and cross-domain user representations \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2304.03891)\]. These efforts highlight the importance of modeling both intra- and inter-sequence interactions for effective cross-domain learning.

Another notable work, the Adaptive Multi-Interest Debiasing framework (AMID) \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/2311.04590)\], addresses the open-world assumptions in CDSR by designing a framework that adapts to real-world scenarios with long-tailed users and cold-start items. The framework includes a multi-interest information module (MIM) and a doubly robust estimator (DRE) to alleviate biases across domains \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/2311.04590)\]. This approach demonstrates the necessity of considering both overlapping and non-overlapping users in cross-domain scenarios \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/2311.04590)\].

This model is particularly beneficial for deployment on resource-constrained devices \[[6](http://arxiv.org/abs/2108.11333)\]. ### <a id='category-self-attention-mechanisms-in-sequential-recommendation'></a>Self-Attention Mechanisms in Sequential Recommendation: 6 papers found.
Sequential recommendation models have increasingly adopted self-attention mechanisms to capture complex sequential patterns in user behavior, with various approaches addressing limitations such as oversmoothing, uncertainty, and the need for explicit domain-aware components \[[12](http://arxiv.org/abs/2312.10325)\]. For instance, SASRec introduces a self-attention based model that balances long-term semantics with model efficiency by focusing on relevant actions \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/1808.09781)\]. Similarly, STOSA incorporates stochastic Gaussian distributions and Wasserstein self-attention to better handle uncertainty and collaborative transitivity, particularly beneficial for cold start items \[[7](http://arxiv.org/abs/2201.06035)\]. BSARec tackles the oversmoothing issue by leveraging Fourier transforms to integrate low and high-frequency information, enhancing the model's ability to capture fine-grained sequential patterns \[[12](http://arxiv.org/abs/2312.10325)\]. Feature-level models like FDSA further enrich sequential recommendations by considering both item-level and feature-level transitions, improving performance through explicit modeling of feature interactions \[[14](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/63555b4f363ee72dd8d49d8deca68760542c0cd2)\]. Additionally, FEARec extends self-attention into the frequency domain, enabling the explicit learning of both low- and high-frequency information while capturing periodic behavior patterns \[[20](http://arxiv.org/abs/2304.09184)\]. These approaches collectively highlight the evolving use of self-attention in sequential recommendation, emphasizing the need for models that can adapt to diverse data characteristics and improve robustness in real-world scenarios.

Recent works have explored various strategies to enhance cross-domain knowledge transfer, such as dual learning mechanisms that iteratively transfer information between domains \[[13](http://arxiv.org/abs/2106.02768)\], and frameworks that incorporate multi-interest modeling to capture both overlapping and non-overlapping user behaviors \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/2311.04590)\].
