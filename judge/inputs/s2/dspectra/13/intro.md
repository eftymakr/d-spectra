## Related Works

Long-tailed recognition remains a challenging problem in visual recognition due to the skewed class distribution in real-world datasets, where a few classes dominate the training data while others are severely underrepresented \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2207.09052)\]. Early approaches attempted to address this issue through data re-sampling, loss re-weighting, or transfer learning from head to tail classes \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/1910.09217)\]. However, these methods often fail to effectively capture the complex relationships between classes and do not consistently improve model generalization \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/2111.13998)\]. 

Recent studies have explored contrastive learning as a promising approach for long-tailed recognition. Supervised Contrastive Learning (SCL) has shown strong performance on balanced data by pulling positive samples together and pushing negative samples apart \[[4](http://arxiv.org/abs/2103.14267)\]. However, in long-tailed scenarios, SCL struggles with imbalanced attraction and repulsion gradients between positive and negative pairs, leading to poor generalization \[[5](http://arxiv.org/abs/2012.08548)\]. This issue has been further analyzed in \[[6](http://arxiv.org/abs/2306.15925)\], where the authors demonstrate that SCL fails to form a regular simplex in the feature space, which is essential for effective representation learning. 

To address these limitations, several works have proposed modifications to contrastive learning. Balanced Contrastive Learning (BCL) introduces class-averaging and class-complement strategies to balance gradient contributions and ensure all classes appear in every mini-batch \[[7](http://arxiv.org/abs/2110.04596)\]. Targeted Supervised Contrastive Learning (TSC) improves the uniformity of feature distributions by forcing all classes to converge to uniformly distributed targets on a hypersphere \[[8](http://arxiv.org/abs/2107.12028)\]. Similarly, Parametric Contrastive Learning (PaCo) introduces class-wise learnable centers to rebalance the optimization process and improve performance on long-tailed datasets \[[9](http://arxiv.org/abs/2203.15359)\]. 

Decoupling representation and classifier learning has also been explored as a way to improve long-tailed recognition. Decoupling Contrastive Learning (DCL) decouples the training objective to address the imbalanced intra-category distance and leverages patch-based self-distillation to transfer knowledge from head to tail classes \[[10](http://arxiv.org/abs/2305.01160)\]. Additionally, Equalization Loss (EQL) and its improved version, EQL v2, aim to balance gradients between positive and negative samples, achieving significant improvements on long-tailed object detection and classification tasks \[[11](http://arxiv.org/abs/2110.10048)\]. 

In addition to contrastive learning, other methods have focused on improving feature representation and classifier learning separately. Distribution Alignment \[[12](http://arxiv.org/abs/1904.05160)\] proposes a unified framework to adjust classification scores and balance class priors, achieving state-of-the-art results across multiple tasks.
