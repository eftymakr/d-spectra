## Related Works

Radar and camera sensor fusion has been extensively studied in the context of autonomous driving due to their complementary advantages in robustness and cost-effectiveness \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2205.02111)\]. Early approaches primarily focused on 2D object detection, with methods like MV3D \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/1611.07759)\] proposing a multi-view 3D object detection framework that integrates LIDAR point clouds and RGB images. These methods often rely on bird's-eye-view (BEV) representations to enable efficient 3D object detection \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/2008.05711)\]. However, the sparsity and uncertainty in radar data pose significant challenges to accurate calibration and fusion \[[4](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/31cddde4f300fe043e0de0d0373affedd0539685)\].

Recent efforts have sought to improve radar-camera fusion by addressing the issue of spatial alignment and feature fusion. For example, CRAFT \[[5](http://arxiv.org/abs/2103.07505)\] introduces a spatio-contextual fusion transformer that effectively exploits both spatial and contextual properties of camera and radar data. This method is particularly effective in handling the discrepancy between coordinate systems and spatial properties, leading to improved 3D object detection performance \[[6](http://arxiv.org/abs/2007.14366)\]. Similarly, RCM-Fusion \[[7](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/0955326c67a947b5fac5fbae7238d04fc9adc251)\] proposes a multi-level fusion approach that combines both modalities at feature and instance levels, achieving state-of-the-art results on the nuScenes dataset \[[8](http://arxiv.org/abs/2307.10249)\].

The calibration of radar and camera systems remains a critical challenge, especially in the presence of data sparsity and measurement uncertainty \[[9](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/4d4dbbcf27d991aeab43dafb82a105bb5512b799)\]. Several methods have been proposed to address this, including the use of target-based calibration techniques \[[10](http://arxiv.org/abs/2209.06535)\]. However, these methods often require specialized hardware and controlled environments, limiting their applicability in real-world scenarios \[[11](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/b5c3469beab918993c1c113cce97eb4e56b09df1)\].
