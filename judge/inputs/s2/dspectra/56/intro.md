## Related Works

Cross-domain Sequential Recommendation (CDSR) has gained significant attention in recent years due to its ability to alleviate the data sparsity and cold-start problems in recommender systems by leveraging information from multiple domains \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2406.03085)\]. Early approaches focused on transferring knowledge between domains using matrix factorization or neural networks, but they often struggled with capturing complex sequential patterns and cross-domain interactions \[[18](http://arxiv.org/abs/2103.01696)\]. More recent methods have incorporated graph neural networks and attention mechanisms to better model sequential behaviors and inter-domain relationships \[[19](http://arxiv.org/abs/2104.07368)\]. However, these methods typically rely on overlapping users and items across domains, which limits their applicability in real-world scenarios where such overlaps are rare \[[24](http://arxiv.org/abs/2311.04590)\].

To address the limitations of traditional CDSR methods, several works have explored the use of deep learning techniques to model sequential interactions more effectively. For instance, the Dynamic Graph Neural Network for Sequential Recommendation (DGSR) integrates dynamic graph structures to capture both user and item interactions over time, improving the modeling of sequential patterns \[[19](http://arxiv.org/abs/2104.07368)\]. Similarly, the Contrastive Cross-Domain Sequential Recommendation (C2DSR) framework leverages intra- and inter-sequence item relationships to jointly learn single- and cross-domain user preferences, enhancing the accuracy of recommendations \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/2304.03891)\]. These methods demonstrate the importance of considering both domain-specific and cross-domain interactions in sequential recommendation tasks.

Another line of research has focused on contrastive learning to improve the quality of user and item representations. The Contrastive Learning for Sequential Recommendation (CL4SRec) framework introduces a multi-task learning approach that combines next-item prediction with contrastive learning, enabling the extraction of more meaningful user patterns and better encoding of user representations \[[3](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/a8f2764a76772ab957b31f5d7bb88527d46fbdc8)\]. This approach has shown promising results in improving recommendation performance by leveraging self-supervised signals from user behavior sequences.

In addition to deep learning and contrastive learning, some studies have explored the use of large language models (LLMs) to enhance CDSR. The LLMCDSR framework, for example, utilizes LLMs to generate pseudo items and improve cross-domain interaction prediction by incorporating collaborative and textual information \[[23](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/e33f51c13728bedeeb54a76b398949a381d6152f)\]. Similarly, the BERT4Rec model employs bidirectional encoder representations from Transformers to model user behavior sequences, allowing for more accurate recommendations by capturing both left and right context \[[15](http://arxiv.org/abs/1904.06690)\]. These works highlight the potential of LLMs in capturing semantic information and improving the modeling of user preferences across domains.
