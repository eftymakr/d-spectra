## Related Works

Recent advancements in multimodal learning have sought to bridge the gap between language and vision, enabling models to understand and generate content across multiple modalities. Early work in this area focused on aligning text with visual inputs through pre-training on large-scale image-text datasets, such as Conceptual Captions \[[17](http://arxiv.org/abs/2102.08981)\], which provided a foundation for vision-and-language pretraining. These datasets, however, were limited in scale and diversity, often constrained by the requirements of their original tasks \[[17](http://arxiv.org/abs/2102.08981)\]. To address this, researchers introduced larger, more diverse datasets such as Conceptual 12M (CC12M), which contains 12 million image-text pairs and is specifically designed for vision-and-language pretraining \[[17](http://arxiv.org/abs/2102.08981)\]. This expansion allowed for better generalization and improved performance on tasks involving long-tail visual concepts.

Several models have been developed to integrate vision and language, including ViLBERT \[[6](http://arxiv.org/abs/1908.02265)\], which extends the BERT architecture to a multi-modal two-stream model, processing both visual and textual inputs in separate streams that interact through co-attentional transformer layers. ViLBERT was pretrained on the Conceptual Captions dataset and achieved state-of-the-art results on tasks such as visual question answering and referring expressions \[[6](http://arxiv.org/abs/1908.02265)\]. Similarly, PaLM-E \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2303.03378)\] introduced a framework for embodied language models that incorporate real-world continuous sensor modalities into language models, enabling tasks such as robotic manipulation planning and visual question answering. PaLM-E demonstrated the potential of multimodal models to perform complex reasoning tasks across different modalities \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2303.03378)\].

The integration of vision and language has also been explored in the context of dialogue systems. For instance, Visual ChatGPT \[[4](http://arxiv.org/abs/2303.04671)\] combines ChatGPT with visual foundation models to enable users to interact with the system by sending and receiving both text and images. This system allows for complex visual questions and multi-step visual editing instructions, demonstrating the potential of multimodal models in interactive settings \[[4](http://arxiv.org/abs/2303.04671)\]. Similarly, Video-ChatGPT \[[5](http://arxiv.org/abs/2306.05424)\] extends this concept to video-based conversation, merging a video-adapted visual encoder with a large language model (LLM) to enable detailed conversations about videos \[[5](http://arxiv.org/abs/2306.05424)\].

Another significant contribution is the development of models that can understand and generate nonverbal cues, such as facial expressions and body language. ### <a id='category-multi-modal-model-architectures'></a>Multi-Modal Model Architectures: 10 papers found.

These advancements highlight the growing emphasis on creating unified frameworks that can effectively handle and generate across multiple modalities, thereby improving the realism and interactivity of conversational AI systems.
