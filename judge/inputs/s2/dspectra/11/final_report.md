## Related Works

3D human pose estimation from monocular images has been a challenging task due to the inherent ambiguity in depth information and the complex dependencies between body joints \[[1](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/ec3eddefc3c658ed8de35ed18bfa7e6ce675019a)\]. Early approaches relied on hand-crafted features and geometric models, but recent advances have leveraged deep learning to achieve more accurate and robust results \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/1411.4280)\]. A key challenge in modeling long-range dependencies between body parts is that joints are not only constrained by other individual joints but also modulated by the surrounding body parts \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/1708.01101)\]. This necessitates the use of more sophisticated architectures that can effectively capture both local and global relationships \[[4](http://arxiv.org/abs/1406.2984)\].

Several methods have attempted to model long-range dependencies by increasing the depth of neural networks. However, this approach often introduces uncorrelated noise and increases the model size, leading to inefficiencies \[[5](http://arxiv.org/abs/1511.09439)\]. To address this, researchers have explored alternative strategies such as using graph-based models that explicitly encode spatial relationships between joints \[[6](http://arxiv.org/abs/1407.3399)\]. For example, the work by \[[7](http://arxiv.org/abs/1406.2282)\] introduced a hybrid architecture combining a deep convolutional network with a Markov Random Field to exploit structural domain constraints \[[8](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/50167a9e6bdc1f64a58fe8a3c990105fec0bd2bc)\]. Similarly, \[[9](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/db466d02ec9f7f47ee11590d0feeb6330256c98d)\] proposed a method that integrates a sparsity-driven 3D geometric prior with temporal smoothness to improve pose estimation accuracy \[[10](http://arxiv.org/abs/1312.4659)\].

Another line of research has focused on improving the efficiency of 3D pose estimation by incorporating pyramid structures or multi-scale feature representations \[[11](http://arxiv.org/abs/2203.13387)\]. For instance, \[[12](http://arxiv.org/abs/1711.08585)\] designed a Pyramid Residual Module (PRMs) to enhance scale invariance in deep convolutional neural networks, enabling better handling of scale variations in human body parts \[[13](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/0646857f867d9a624f2b53d1ffb4de99e225b2d3)\]. Additionally, \[[14](http://arxiv.org/abs/2206.06420)\] introduced a novel architecture that uses a position refinement model to improve joint location estimation in human pose estimation tasks \[[15](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/555a23a05777a0b03614b195c7c72f7c626a97e9)\].

Recent works have also explored the use of transformers for modeling spatial and temporal dependencies in 3D pose estimation \[[16](http://arxiv.org/abs/1602.00134)\]. For example, \[[17](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/22e305b7b66bdedf379fa678802cb46e8bb546b1)\] proposed a spatial-temporal transformer structure to comprehensively model human joint relations within each frame as well as the temporal correlations across frames \[[18](http://arxiv.org/abs/1705.03098)\]. Affinity modulation adjusts the graph structure in a GCN so that it can model additional edges beyond the human skeleton.
