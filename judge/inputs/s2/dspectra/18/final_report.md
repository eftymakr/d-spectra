## Related Works

Anomaly detection has become a critical component in modern industrial systems, particularly in the context of intelligent manufacturing, where maintaining quality control is essential \[[11](http://arxiv.org/abs/1901.04997)\]. Traditional approaches often rely on threshold-based methods or supervised learning, which are limited by the lack of labeled data and the inability to capture complex temporal dependencies \[[11](http://arxiv.org/abs/1901.04997)\]. Recent advances in deep learning have enabled more sophisticated anomaly detection methods, particularly in image and time-series data \[[11](http://arxiv.org/abs/1901.04997)\].

In the field of image-based anomaly detection, several deep learning approaches have been proposed. For instance, \[[6](http://arxiv.org/abs/2106.08265)\] introduces PatchCore, a method that uses a maximally representative memory bank of nominal patch-features to achieve state-of-the-art performance on the MVTec AD benchmark. This approach is particularly effective in the cold-start scenario, where only nominal examples are available \[[6](http://arxiv.org/abs/2106.08265)\]. Similarly, \[[9](http://arxiv.org/abs/2107.12571)\] presents CFLOW-AD, a real-time model based on conditional normalizing flows that achieves high accuracy while being computationally efficient. CFLOW-AD is notable for its ability to provide localization information, which is crucial for industrial applications \[[9](http://arxiv.org/abs/2107.12571)\]. The MVTec 3D-AD dataset \[[10](http://arxiv.org/abs/2112.09045)\] further expands the scope of anomaly detection to 3D data, enabling the detection of geometric deviations in manufactured products. This dataset is particularly relevant for scenarios where anomalies manifest as structural changes \[[10](http://arxiv.org/abs/2112.09045)\].

For time-series data, methods such as LSTM-based Encoder-Decoder schemes \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/1607.00148)\] have been developed to address the challenges of detecting anomalies in inherently unpredictable time-series. These models learn to reconstruct 'normal' time-series behavior and use reconstruction error as a metric for anomaly detection. The approach has been tested on both synthetic and real-world datasets, demonstrating robustness across various time-series patterns \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/1607.00148)\]. Additionally, \[[11](http://arxiv.org/abs/1901.04997)\] proposes MAD-GAN, an unsupervised multivariate anomaly detection method based on Generative Adversarial Networks (GANs). MAD-GAN considers the entire variable set concurrently, capturing latent interactions among sensors and actuators, which is essential for detecting anomalies in complex systems \[[11](http://arxiv.org/abs/1901.04997)\].

### <a id='category-anomaly-detection-in-industrial-systems'></a>Anomaly Detection in Industrial Systems: 4 papers found.
In the realm of anomaly detection in industrial systems, recent research has focused on addressing the limitations of traditional methods, particularly in handling unpredictable time-series data and improving robustness against adversarial attacks. For instance, the EncDec-AD approach leverages Long Short Term Memory Networks to reconstruct normal behavior and detect anomalies through reconstruction error, demonstrating effectiveness across various time-series types \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/1607.00148)\]. Similarly, studies have highlighted vulnerabilities in process-based anomaly detection systems, where adversarial spoofing attacks can significantly reduce recall, indicating a need for more rigorous analysis and robustness in detection frameworks \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/2012.03586)\]. Additionally, the development of large-scale, real-world datasets like Real-IAD has been crucial in bridging the gap between public benchmarks and practical applications, offering more challenging and diverse anomaly detection scenarios \[[5](http://arxiv.org/abs/2403.12580)\]. Furthermore, the integration of generative models, such as MAD-GAN, has enabled more effective multivariate anomaly detection by capturing spatial-temporal correlations and latent variable interactions in complex systems \[[11](http://arxiv.org/abs/1901.04997)\]. These advancements collectively underscore the evolving landscape of anomaly detection, emphasizing the importance of adaptability, robustness, and real-world applicability.

### <a id='category-unsupervised-and-semi-supervised-methods'></a>Unsupervised and Semi-Supervised Methods: 2 papers found.
In the realm of unsupervised and semi-supervised anomaly detection, recent advancements have focused on leveraging both unlabeled and limited labeled data to improve detection performance. While traditional methods often rely solely on unlabeled data, semi-supervised approaches have gained traction by incorporating a small number of labeled samples, typically normal instances verified by domain experts \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/1607.00148)\]. However, most existing deep methods are limited to using labeled normal data, with few studies exploring the utilization of labeled anomalies \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/1607.00148)\]. This limitation is addressed by Deep SAD, an end-to-end deep learning framework that integrates both normal and anomalous labeled samples, offering a more comprehensive approach to anomaly detection \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/1607.00148)\]. Additionally, the introduction of a 3D dataset for unsupervised anomaly detection and localization has highlighted the challenges of detecting geometric anomalies in real-world manufacturing scenarios, where models are trained only on anomaly-free data \[[10](http://arxiv.org/abs/2112.09045)\]. This dataset provides a valuable benchmark for evaluating the effectiveness of anomaly detection algorithms in identifying defects such as scratches, dents, and deformations \[[10](http://arxiv.org/abs/2112.09045)\]. These developments underscore the growing interest in semi-supervised methods that can effectively utilize limited labeled data to enhance anomaly detection performance, particularly in industrial settings where computational resources are constrained \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/1607.00148)\].

Through comprehensive evaluations, we demonstrate the relevance of our dataset and method.
