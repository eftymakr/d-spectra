## Related Works

Neural retrieval models have shown significant promise in Web search tasks, particularly in improving the relevance of query-document pairs \[[12](http://arxiv.org/abs/1610.08136)\]. However, training these models typically requires a large amount of labeled query-document pairs, which are expensive to obtain \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2305.18290)\]. Recent advances have explored the use of synthetic queries generated by large language models as a scalable alternative \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2305.18290)\]. Despite this, synthetic queries often vary in quality, leading to suboptimal performance in downstream retrieval tasks \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2305.18290)\]. Existing approaches typically filter out noisy query-document pairs using signals from an external re-ranker \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2305.18290)\].

Direct Preference Optimization (DPO) has emerged as a promising technique for aligning large language models with human preferences without the need for a reward model or reinforcement learning \[[4](http://arxiv.org/abs/2402.10571)\]. DPO fine-tunes the model to increase the likelihood of preferred responses over dispreferred ones, using only a simple classification loss \[[4](http://arxiv.org/abs/2402.10571)\]. This approach has been shown to be stable, performant, and computationally lightweight \[[4](http://arxiv.org/abs/2402.10571)\]. However, not all preference pairs are equal; some may require a larger margin between preferred and dispreferred responses \[[17](http://arxiv.org/abs/2405.14734)\]. To address this, DPO with an offset (ODPO) was proposed, which introduces an offset value to ensure the difference between the likelihood of the preferred and dispreferred responses is greater than a specified threshold \[[4](http://arxiv.org/abs/2402.10571)\].

In the context of retrieval, several works have explored the use of neural ranking models that can effectively capture the relevance between queries and documents \[[12](http://arxiv.org/abs/1610.08136)\]. For instance, K-NRM uses a kernel-based approach to extract multi-level soft match features and combines them into a final ranking score \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/1706.06613)\]. Similarly, the Deep Relevance Matching Model (DRMM) employs a joint deep architecture at the query term level to handle exact matching signals, query term importance, and diverse matching requirements \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/1711.08611)\]. These models have shown significant improvements over traditional retrieval methods \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/1711.08611)\].

Another approach to improving retrieval effectiveness is through the use of weak supervision signals, such as those obtained from unsupervised ranking models like BM25 \[[11](http://arxiv.org/abs/1704.08803)\]. ### <a id='category-direct-preference-optimization-(dpo)-framework'></a>Direct Preference Optimization (DPO) Framework: 4 papers found.
Direct Preference Optimization (DPO) is a framework designed to improve the alignment of large language models (LLMs) with human preferences by simplifying the reinforcement learning from human feedback (RLHF) process. Unlike traditional RLHF, which requires training a separate reward model and then fine-tuning the LLM using reinforcement learning, DPO introduces a new parameterization of the reward model that allows for the extraction of the optimal policy in closed form, eliminating the need for complex reinforcement learning procedures \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2305.18290)\]. This approach relies on a simple classification loss, making it computationally lightweight and easier to implement \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2305.18290)\]. However, DPO still depends on the assumption that pairwise preferences can be substituted with pointwise rewards, a key approximation that limits its generalization to out-of-distribution data \[[7](http://arxiv.org/abs/2310.12036)\]. To address this, researchers have proposed extensions such as filtered DPO (fDPO), which uses a trained reward model to monitor and filter the quality of the preference dataset during training, leading to improved model performance \[[19](http://arxiv.org/abs/2404.13846)\]. Additionally, alternative approaches like Direct Nash Optimization (DNO) have emerged, which directly optimize over pairwise preferences without relying on reward maximization, offering a more theoretically grounded and scalable solution \[[23](http://arxiv.org/abs/2404.03715)\]. These developments highlight the ongoing efforts to refine and enhance DPO and related frameworks, aiming to achieve better alignment with human preferences while maintaining stability and efficiency.

The category "Neural Retrieval Models and Their Training Challenges" encompasses various approaches aimed at improving the effectiveness and efficiency of neural retrieval systems. Existing methods, such as generative information retrieval (GenIR), formulate document retrieval as a document identifier (docid) generation task, but they often suffer from token-level misalignment, which hinders effective document-level relevance capture \[[5](http://arxiv.org/abs/2504.05181)\]. To address this, reinforcement learning-based methods like reinforcement learning from relevance feedback (RLRF) have been proposed, but they introduce significant complexity through reward modeling and reinforcement fine-tuning, which is computationally expensive and unstable \[[5](http://arxiv.org/abs/2504.05181)\]. In contrast, direct document relevance optimization (DDRO) aligns token-level generation with document-level relevance estimation through pairwise ranking, eliminating the need for explicit reward modeling and reinforcement learning, thereby simplifying the optimization pipeline \[[5](http://arxiv.org/abs/2504.05181)\]. Similarly, in query-by-document (QBD) retrieval, improving the BERT re-ranker's effectiveness through multi-task optimization by incorporating document-level representation learning objectives has shown promising results without altering the re-ranker or requiring additional training data \[[8](http://arxiv.org/abs/2202.00373)\]. Furthermore, dense retrieval (DR) models have gained traction due to their ability to better rank documents, but their performance heavily relies on effective training strategies such as hard negative sampling, which lacks theoretical analysis and may introduce risks \[[21](http://arxiv.org/abs/2104.08051)\]. To mitigate these issues, researchers have proposed training strategies like STAR and ADORE, which enhance stability and directly optimize ranking performance through dynamic sampling methods \[[21](http://arxiv.org/abs/2104.08051)\]. These approaches collectively highlight the ongoing efforts to refine neural retrieval models by addressing training challenges through innovative optimization techniques and theoretical insights \[[5](http://arxiv.org/abs/2504.05181)\]\[[8](http://arxiv.org/abs/2202.00373)\]\[[21](http://arxiv.org/abs/2104.08051)\].
