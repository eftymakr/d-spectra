## Related Works

Unsupervised domain adaptation (UDA) for semantic segmentation has been extensively studied to address the challenge of transferring knowledge from a labeled source domain to an unlabeled target domain \[[1](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/6518b0e6334c6ffb9b94c551c57ad79c7008cbb5)\]. Traditional UDA methods typically assume that the category settings between the source and target domains are known, which is often not the case in real-world scenarios \[[2](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/5f9ce93f82dcd9b0f9ffe4274b81682f40d86253)\]. This assumption leads to performance degradation when private classes exist in the target domain, as the model cannot distinguish between common and private classes \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/2103.16372)\]. To address this limitation, several works have explored methods that do not rely on prior knowledge of category settings \[[4](http://arxiv.org/abs/2211.07609)\].

One line of research focuses on improving the alignment between source and target domains by leveraging adversarial learning. For instance, Domain Separation Networks (DSN) explicitly model the shared and private components of the data, enabling better feature alignment \[[5](http://arxiv.org/abs/2004.08878)\]. Similarly, the DIRT-T approach integrates the cluster assumption into domain adversarial training, improving the robustness of the model against domain shifts \[[6](http://arxiv.org/abs/2012.12545)\]. These methods, however, still require access to source domain data or models, which may not be available in privacy-sensitive scenarios \[[7](http://arxiv.org/abs/1910.13049)\].

Another approach, such as the work by Zhang et al. \[[8](http://arxiv.org/abs/2007.08702)\], introduces a framework that constructs virtual source domain data to mimic the source distribution, enabling adaptation without access to the original source data. This method uses high-confidence target samples to generate virtual data and applies adversarial learning and self-training strategies to align the domains \[[9](http://arxiv.org/abs/1904.01886)\]. Similarly, the PiPa framework \[[10](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/a2e433da83c6e1eb4f4486c900fdfbf1951c4d30)\] proposes a pixel- and patch-wise self-supervised learning approach to enhance intra-image correlations and semantic consistency across different contexts \[[11](http://arxiv.org/abs/1608.06019)\].

Recent works have also focused on improving the reliability of pseudo-labels during domain adaptation. For example, the work by Chen et al. \[[12](http://arxiv.org/abs/1602.04433)\] introduces an uncertainty-aware consistency regularization method that exploits the latent uncertainty of target samples to improve the learning process \[[13](http://arxiv.org/abs/2111.14887)\]. This approach helps the student model learn more reliable knowledge from the teacher model, leading to better performance on benchmark datasets \[[14](http://arxiv.org/abs/2011.08502)\]. Similarly, the work by Li et al. \[[15](http://arxiv.org/abs/2005.10876)\] proposes a method that uses uncertainty estimation to rectify pseudo label learning, leading to significant improvements in performance \[[16](http://arxiv.org/abs/2004.07703)\].

In addition to these methods, some works have explored the use of depth information to improve domain adaptation. For instance, DADA \[[17](http://arxiv.org/abs/1802.08735)\] leverages dense depth maps from the source domain to enhance the performance of semantic segmentation models on the target domain \[[18](http://arxiv.org/abs/2101.10979)\].
