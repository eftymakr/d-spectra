## Related Works

Video super-resolution (VSR) has been a focal point in computer vision, aiming to enhance the spatial resolution of low-resolution (LR) videos while preserving temporal consistency. Early approaches relied on motion estimation and compensation techniques, which, while effective, often introduced computational overhead and inaccuracies \[[1](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/c6a8ff4d9dcdf9e5d82310b847af999ed6655f65)\]. Recent advances have shifted towards deep learning-based methods, leveraging convolutional neural networks (CNNs) and recurrent architectures to exploit temporal correlations \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/1611.05250)\]. These methods, however, still face challenges in handling complex motion and maintaining high fidelity in real-world scenarios \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/2008.05765)\].

Deep learning-based VSR methods have seen significant progress, particularly with the introduction of spatio-temporal networks and motion compensation techniques. For instance, the use of 3D convolutions and early fusion strategies has enabled more efficient exploitation of temporal redundancies \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/1611.05250)\]. Similarly, recurrent neural networks (RNNs), such as bidirectional recurrent convolutional networks, have been proposed to model long-term temporal dependencies, offering faster inference and better performance on complex motion \[[18](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/a2c50f93f6d0892918e0ff653750d198ccab48d0)\]. However, these methods often require extensive computational resources, limiting their deployment on mobile devices \[[16](http://arxiv.org/abs/2208.11608)\].

The integration of transformers into VSR has also gained traction, as they offer parallel computing capabilities and better handling of long-range dependencies. However, traditional transformer architectures, with their fully connected self-attention layers, lack the ability to exploit spatial locality, which is crucial for VSR \[[21](http://arxiv.org/abs/2106.06847)\]. To address this, novel attention mechanisms have been introduced, such as spatial-temporal convolutional self-attention layers, which better capture local information \[[21](http://arxiv.org/abs/2106.06847)\]. These advancements have led to improved performance on benchmark datasets, though they often come at the cost of increased computational complexity \[[21](http://arxiv.org/abs/2106.06847)\].

Another key challenge in VSR is the presence of artifacts and inconsistencies, especially in real-world scenarios where degradation is non-uniform and complex. To mitigate this, recent work has focused on introducing self-supervised learning and control mechanisms. For example, the use of a self-supervised ControlNet, which leverages high-resolution (HR) features as guidance and employs contrastive learning to extract degradation-insensitive features from LR videos, has shown promise in reducing artifacts \[[25](http://arxiv.org/abs/2407.13987)\]. Additionally, the incorporation of recurrent latent space propagation and temporal modulation networks has enabled more efficient and accurate alignment of frames, improving temporal consistency \[[8](http://arxiv.org/abs/1909.08080)\]\[[10](http://arxiv.org/abs/2104.10642)\].

Recent advancements in video super-resolution (VSR) have focused on leveraging deep learning techniques to improve the quality and efficiency of generating high-resolution videos from low-resolution inputs. Many approaches rely on convolutional neural networks (CNNs) and recurrent architectures to model temporal dependencies, such as the 3D CNN with slow fusion, Recurrent Neural Networks (RNNs), and the proposed Recurrent Residual Network (RRN) \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/2008.05765)\]. These methods aim to stabilize training and enhance performance by incorporating residual learning \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/2008.05765)\]. Efficient recurrent models, like the proposed Recurrent Latent Space Propagation (RLSP), have also been developed to handle VSR with high efficiency and effectiveness, achieving significant speed improvements over existing methods \[[8](http://arxiv.org/abs/1909.08080)\]. Furthermore, one-stage space-time video super-resolution frameworks have been proposed to directly synthesize high-resolution slow-motion videos from low frame rate, low-resolution inputs, combining temporal interpolation and spatial super-resolution in a unified approach \[[11](http://arxiv.org/abs/2002.11616)\]. These methods highlight the ongoing efforts to improve both the quality and efficiency of VSR, addressing challenges such as motion compensation, computational complexity, and temporal consistency \[[1](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/c6a8ff4d9dcdf9e5d82310b847af999ed6655f65)\].

Real-World Degradation Handling in VSR has been a focal point for improving the performance of video super-resolution (VSR) models in practical scenarios. Existing methods often struggle with the propagation of artifacts and degradations, especially in real-world settings where videos are subject to complex and varied degradations \[[14](http://arxiv.org/abs/2111.12704)\]. To address these challenges, researchers have explored various strategies, such as incorporating image precleaning stages to reduce noise and artifacts before propagation \[[14](http://arxiv.org/abs/2111.12704)\], and training models on real-world datasets that capture the diversity of degradation types \[[15](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/2d5f68c536b12c2ac52168c8928e5ec887679211)\]. Additionally, the use of advanced attention mechanisms, such as channel attention, has been shown to be less sensitive to artifacts compared to spatial attention, although it can lead to feature redundancy \[[25](http://arxiv.org/abs/2407.13987)\]. To mitigate this, techniques like the squeeze-excite mechanism and covariance-based rescaling have been proposed to counteract the effects of high channel covariance \[[25](http://arxiv.org/abs/2407.13987)\]. Furthermore, the integration of stochastic degradation schemes and longer sequence training has been introduced to improve efficiency and performance \[[14](http://arxiv.org/abs/2111.12704)\]. These approaches collectively aim to enhance the robustness and quality of VSR models in real-world applications.

### <a id='category-temporal-modulation-and-frame-alignment-techniques'></a>Temporal Modulation and Frame Alignment Techniques: 4 papers found.
In the context of temporal modulation and frame alignment techniques, recent research has focused on improving the quality and efficiency of video super-resolution (VSR) by addressing the challenges of temporal alignment and motion compensation. Several approaches have been proposed to enhance the alignment of reference and supporting frames, which is critical for generating high-resolution videos from low-resolution inputs. For instance, the Temporal Modulation Network (TMNet) introduces a Temporal Modulation Block (TMB) to modulate deformable convolution kernels, enabling controllable feature interpolation and accurate intermediate frame generation \[[10](http://arxiv.org/abs/2104.10642)\]. Similarly, the Temporally-deformable Alignment Network (TDAN) avoids the need for optical flow by dynamically predicting offsets of sampling convolution kernels at the feature level, leading to more robust temporal alignment without artifacts \[[26](http://arxiv.org/abs/1812.02898)\]. Another method, based on hierarchical temporal information, divides input sequences into groups corresponding to different frame rates, leveraging attention modules and intra-group fusion to recover missing details and handle large motion \[[13](http://arxiv.org/abs/2007.10595)\]. Additionally, the Video LDM approach extends latent diffusion models to video generation by introducing a temporal dimension to the latent space and finetuning on encoded image sequences, enabling efficient and high-resolution video synthesis \[[9](http://arxiv.org/abs/2304.08818)\]. These techniques collectively highlight the importance of temporal modulation and frame alignment in achieving high-quality video super-resolution, with varying strategies aimed at improving accuracy, efficiency, and robustness in real-world applications.
