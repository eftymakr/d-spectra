## Related Works

Zero-Knowledge Proofs (ZKPs) have emerged as a critical cryptographic primitive for privacy-preserving computation, enabling a prover to demonstrate the truth of a statement without revealing additional information \[[6](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/9ccf7b6cb32cf89752a35bd910555adac54773e0)\]. This concept has found applications in blockchain technologies, verifiable machine learning, and electronic voting \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2408.05890)\]. However, the computational complexity of generating ZKPs has hindered their widespread adoption \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2408.05890)\]. Recent efforts have focused on accelerating key primitives of ZKP protocols, particularly on GPU and ASIC platforms \[[18](http://arxiv.org/abs/2412.12481)\]. For instance, GZKP \[[2](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/e149d93531d8c07cf43f6cae327a4795144d1304)\] and PipeZK \[[3](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/6ee84674b7c426849c3da56cb278c08eb7a903cf)\] have demonstrated significant speedups by optimizing polynomial computations and multi-scalar multiplications (MSMs), which are computationally intensive components of ZKP generation \[[3](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/6ee84674b7c426849c3da56cb278c08eb7a903cf)\]. These works have improved performance, but they often require trusted setups or generate larger proof sizes, limiting their applicability in scenarios with strict verification constraints \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2408.05890)\].

The challenge of achieving both efficiency and universality in ZKP protocols has led to the development of systems like HyperPlonk, which supports one-time, universal setup and small proof sizes \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2408.05890)\]. Accelerating such protocols is essential for practical deployment. The proposed accelerator, zkSpeed, addresses this by targeting the entire proof generation process, including the SumCheck and MSM primitives \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2408.05890)\]. This approach contrasts with prior works that often focus on individual components \[[3](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/6ee84674b7c426849c3da56cb278c08eb7a903cf)\]\[[18](http://arxiv.org/abs/2412.12481)\]. For example, SZKP \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2408.05890)\] presents an ASIC-based accelerator that achieves speedups of over 400× over CPU implementations, while GZKP \[[2](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/e149d93531d8c07cf43f6cae327a4795144d1304)\] achieves up to 48.1× speedup using GPU acceleration \[[2](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/e149d93531d8c07cf43f6cae327a4795144d1304)\]. However, these accelerators typically focus on specific components rather than the full protocol \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2408.05890)\].

Hardware acceleration for Zero-Knowledge Proof (ZKP) primitives has emerged as a critical area of research to address the computational bottlenecks in proof generation, which is a key challenge in deploying ZKPs for real-world applications \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2408.05890)\]. The proof generation process, particularly in zkSNARKs, involves intensive computations such as Number Theoretic Transform (NTT) and Multi-scalar Multiplication (MSM), which are highly resource-intensive and time-consuming \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2408.05890)\]. Prior works have explored hardware acceleration using GPUs and custom RTL designs, but these approaches often struggle with the irregular memory access patterns and data-dependent operations inherent in NTT and MSM computations \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2408.05890)\]. To overcome these challenges, researchers have proposed specialized accelerators like SZKP, which leverages structured dataflows to achieve significant speedups over CPU, ASIC, and GPU implementations \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2408.05890)\]. Similarly, PipeZK introduces a pipelined architecture with two subsystems to efficiently handle large polynomial computations and multi-scalar multiplications on elliptic curves, achieving up to 10x speedup on cryptographic benchmarks \[[3](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/6ee84674b7c426849c3da56cb278c08eb7a903cf)\]. Another notable work presents an FPGA-based architecture tailored for accelerating the MSM operation in zk-SNARKs, exploiting parallelism and optimized modular arithmetic to achieve 110x–150x speedups over software implementations \[[18](http://arxiv.org/abs/2412.12481)\]. These efforts highlight the growing interest in dedicated hardware solutions to enhance the performance and scalability of ZKP systems, making them more viable for applications such as blockchain and verifiable computing.

The category "Quantum Security in ZKP Systems" explores advancements in zero-knowledge proof (ZKP) systems that are secure against quantum attacks. Recent research has demonstrated that several classical interactive proof systems, such as the Goldreich-Micali-Wigderson protocols for Graph Isomorphism and Graph 3-Coloring, can be proven zero-knowledge against general quantum attacks, assuming the existence of quantum computationally concealing commitment schemes \[[4](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/c3d1262160d759f424a83aae1d053b59d3352e7d)\]. Additionally, it has been established that honest-verifier and general quantum statistical zero-knowledge are equivalent, with QSZK = QSZKHV \[[4](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/c3d1262160d759f424a83aae1d053b59d3352e7d)\]. In the context of quantum Merlin-Arthur (QMA) proof systems, significant progress has been made in proving the hardness of the Consistency of Local Density Matrices (CLDM) problem under Karp reductions, which is crucial for constructing zero-knowledge protocols for QMA \[[5](http://arxiv.org/abs/1911.07782)\]. Furthermore, the paper shows that any quantum zero-knowledge proof system can be transformed into a three-message public-coin quantum zero-knowledge proof system with polynomially small error in soundness, without relying on computational assumptions \[[9](http://arxiv.org/abs/0705.1129)\]. These results highlight the theoretical foundations of quantum zero-knowledge, including the equivalence of different variants like honest-verifier and general quantum zero-knowledge \[[9](http://arxiv.org/abs/0705.1129)\]. Moreover, the work emphasizes that quantum zero-knowledge protocols can be made secure against quantum attacks, even in the presence of shared quantum entanglement, by leveraging homomorphic properties of commitment schemes \[[10](http://arxiv.org/abs/2304.09784)\]. These developments contribute to the broader goal of ensuring privacy and security in quantum computing environments \[[10](http://arxiv.org/abs/2304.09784)\].
