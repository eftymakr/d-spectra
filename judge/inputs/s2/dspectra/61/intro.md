## Related Works

Personalized outfit generation has garnered significant attention in recent years, driven by the increasing demand for tailored fashion recommendations and the rise of generative AI models. Early approaches focused on compatibility and personalization, often using rule-based systems or collaborative filtering \[[15](http://arxiv.org/abs/1804.09979)\]. However, these methods lacked the flexibility and creativity required to generate diverse and context-aware outfits. With the advent of deep learning, several studies have explored the use of neural networks for outfit generation and recommendation, leveraging large-scale datasets and advanced architectures.

One notable work is POG, which introduces a Personalized Outfit Generation model that connects user preferences with Transformer architecture to ensure compatibility and personalization \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/1905.01866)\]. This model is deployed on a platform to generate personalized outfits for users, demonstrating the feasibility of integrating personalization into outfit generation. Similarly, FashionNet proposes a system that combines feature extraction and compatibility computation using deep convolutional and fully-connected networks, enabling personalized outfit recommendations \[[5](http://arxiv.org/abs/1810.02443)\]. The two-stage training strategy in FashionNet allows for the transfer of a general compatibility model to a personalized one, improving recommendation accuracy.

Another key contribution is the use of deep learning for outfit compatibility prediction. The work in \[[14](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/b1db62044adca574e4cf88b23c170f3cc089d513)\] introduces GP-BPR, a personalized compatibility modeling scheme that incorporates both general aesthetics and user preferences. This model uses a comprehensive personal preference modeling method, considering both image and context descriptions to capture user-specific preferences. The IQON3000 dataset, created for evaluation, highlights the importance of incorporating user preferences in compatibility modeling \[[14](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/b1db62044adca574e4cf88b23c170f3cc089d513)\].

In addition to compatibility, several studies have focused on the generation of new fashion items. The work in \[[12](http://arxiv.org/abs/1807.03133)\] introduces an unsupervised style extraction module that enables the generation of outfits while controlling their styles. This model outperforms baseline methods in fashion item prediction and style extraction tasks, demonstrating the potential of incorporating global style information into outfit generation \[[12](http://arxiv.org/abs/1807.03133)\]. Similarly, the Diffusion Models for Generative Outfit Recommendation \[[16](http://arxiv.org/abs/2402.17279)\] propose a novel task called Generative Outfit Recommendation (GOR), aiming to generate a set of fashion images and compose them into a visually compatible outfit tailored to specific users. This work introduces DiFashion, a model that uses diffusion models to generate high-fidelity, compatible, and personalized outfits.

The use of attention mechanisms has also been explored in outfit generation.
