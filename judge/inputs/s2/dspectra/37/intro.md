## Related Works

Vision Transformers (ViTs) have gained significant traction in computer vision due to their ability to capture global context effectively \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2211.08110)\]. However, deploying ViTs on resource-constrained hardware remains challenging due to their high computational and memory demands \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2211.08110)\]. Prior work has focused on optimizing ViT performance through various techniques, including quantization, pruning, and architectural modifications \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/2208.05163)\]\[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/2210.09573)\]\[[4](http://arxiv.org/abs/2302.09108)\].

Several studies have explored quantization strategies to reduce the computational overhead of ViTs. For example, Auto-ViT-Acc introduces a mixed-scheme quantization approach that improves accuracy while maintaining high inference speeds on FPGAs \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/2208.05163)\]. Similarly, VAQF proposes a fully automatic framework for low-bit ViT acceleration, enabling real-time execution on FPGAs by optimizing quantization precision and accelerator settings \[[10](http://arxiv.org/abs/2201.06618)\]. These approaches demonstrate the potential of quantization in reducing the computational burden of ViT models.

Token pruning has also been widely investigated as a means to improve hardware efficiency. HeatViT introduces an adaptive token pruning framework that dynamically identifies and consolidates non-informative tokens, achieving significant computation reduction without sacrificing accuracy \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2211.08110)\]. Similarly, EViT reorganizes image tokens during inference to reduce redundant computations, improving both speed and accuracy \[[11](http://arxiv.org/abs/2202.07800)\]. These methods highlight the importance of efficient token management in accelerating ViT inference.

Hardware-specific optimizations have been another key focus in ViT acceleration. ViTA proposes a configurable FPGA accelerator that minimizes off-chip memory accesses, achieving high hardware utilization efficiency and reasonable frame rates for edge applications \[[4](http://arxiv.org/abs/2302.09108)\]. ViTCoD further advances this by integrating algorithm and accelerator co-design, achieving speedups of up to 235.3× over general computing platforms \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/2210.09573)\]. These accelerators demonstrate the potential of custom hardware solutions in improving ViT performance.

Recent work has also explored hybrid architectures to enhance both accuracy and efficiency. EfficientViT combines convolutional and transformer layers, improving hardware efficiency while maintaining high accuracy \[[9](http://arxiv.org/abs/2403.20230)\]. The proposed FPGA-based accelerator for EfficientViT further exploits its hybrid architecture to achieve high throughput and energy efficiency \[[9](http://arxiv.org/abs/2403.20230)\]. These hybrid models represent a promising direction for future ViT designs.

Moreover, dynamic token configuration has been introduced to adaptively adjust the number of tokens based on input characteristics. Dynamic Transformers automatically configure the number of tokens for each input image, reducing computational costs while maintaining accuracy \[[13](http://arxiv.org/abs/2105.15075)\]. This approach aligns with the goal of achieving a better trade-off between accuracy and speed.
