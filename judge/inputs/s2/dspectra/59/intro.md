## Related Works

Fairness in re-ranking has garnered significant attention as machine learning systems increasingly influence high-stakes decisions. Prior research has identified a trade-off between ranking accuracy and item fairness, with fairness considerations often leading to a decline in user-side accuracy \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2005.12974)\]. This trade-off is analogous to the dynamics of economic transactions, where fairness constraints on item-side fairness (similar to a commodity tax on suppliers) translate into a cost passed on to users (a decline in accuracy) \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2005.12974)\]. In economics, the extent to which such taxes transfer to users is formalized using the concept of elasticity \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2005.12974)\]. Similarly, the re-ranking fairness-accuracy trade-off is governed by the elasticity of utility between item groups \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2005.12974)\].

Several works have explored fairness-aware ranking, focusing on balancing accuracy and fairness. For instance, \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/1809.02921)\] proposes a fairness-aware re-ranking algorithm (FAR) that balances ranking quality and provider-side fairness, incorporating personalized user tolerance towards provider diversification. The algorithm iteratively generates ranking lists by trading off between accuracy and provider coverage. This approach aligns with the idea that fairness constraints can affect overall performance, but it does not explicitly model the elasticity of utility between item groups \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/1809.02921)\].

Other approaches, such as \[[5](http://arxiv.org/abs/2404.17826)\], consider fairness as a taxation process, reformulating previous fair re-ranking methods as item-level tax policies. However, these policies often fail to meet the ideal requirements of continuity and controllability over accuracy loss \[[5](http://arxiv.org/abs/2404.17826)\]. In contrast, \[[25](http://arxiv.org/abs/1810.01943)\] introduces a comprehensive toolkit, AI Fairness 360, which provides a wide range of fairness metrics and mitigation algorithms, facilitating the evaluation and implementation of fair algorithms. This toolkit supports the analysis of fairness-accuracy trade-offs, which is critical for understanding the limitations of current fair re-ranking evaluations \[[25](http://arxiv.org/abs/1810.01943)\].

The concept of elasticity has also been explored in the context of fairness and accuracy trade-offs. \[[27](http://arxiv.org/abs/2405.15443)\] introduces a causal perspective to analyze the fairness-accuracy trade-off, proposing the notion of path-specific excess loss (PSEL) and total excess loss (TEL) to quantify the impact of fairness constraints on model performance. This work highlights the importance of considering causal relationships when evaluating fairness-accuracy trade-offs \[[27](http://arxiv.org/abs/2405.15443)\].

In addition, \[[29](http://arxiv.org/abs/2204.08085)\] presents a re-ranking approach that integrates fairness constraints from both the consumer and producer sides in a joint objective framework, improving both consumer and producer fairness without reducing overall recommendation quality. This approach demonstrates the potential of algorithms to minimize data biases in recommender systems \[[29](http://arxiv.org/abs/2204.08085)\].
