## Related Works

Deep-unrolling and plug-and-play (PnP) approaches have emerged as dominant methods for solving single-pixel imaging (SPI) inverse problems \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/1809.04693)\]. PnP algorithms, which replace proximal operators with off-the-shelf deep denoisers, offer flexibility in handling varying compression ratios (CRs) but often struggle with reconstruction accuracy and speed \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/1912.10557)\]. These methods rely on iterative algorithms where the denoiser is plugged into the optimization process, allowing for the integration of powerful pre-trained denoisers \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/1801.06756)\]. However, their performance can be limited by the quality and adaptability of the denoiser \[[4](http://arxiv.org/abs/2002.09611)\].

On the other hand, unrolling approaches transform truncated iterative optimization processes into end-to-end trainable neural networks, typically achieving better accuracy and faster inference \[[5](http://arxiv.org/abs/1903.08616)\]. These methods, such as those based on the half-quadratic splitting (HQS) or alternating direction method of multipliers (ADMM), require fine-tuning or retraining when the compression ratio changes, which limits their adaptability \[[6](http://arxiv.org/abs/2012.05703)\]. This limitation is addressed in this work by proposing a unified framework that integrates the strengths of both PnP and unrolling approaches \[[7](http://arxiv.org/abs/1704.03488)\].

Several studies have explored the use of deep learning for image restoration, emphasizing the importance of denoising priors and the integration of physical models \[[8](http://arxiv.org/abs/1611.03679)\]. For example, the work by \[[9](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/b954a94af132294a4a4e2efce15027758a535742)\] introduced a denoising-based image restoration algorithm that was unfolded into a deep neural network, combining denoisers with back-projection modules to ensure observation consistency \[[10](http://arxiv.org/abs/2008.13751)\]. Similarly, \[[11](http://arxiv.org/abs/2008.00226)\] proposed a method where the proximal operator of a regularization term was replaced by a denoising neural network, enabling the use of deep learning for image reconstruction while maintaining the flexibility of variational methods \[[12](http://arxiv.org/abs/2104.10348)\].

The concept of algorithm unrolling has also been widely adopted, connecting iterative algorithms with deep neural networks to create interpretable and efficient models \[[13](http://arxiv.org/abs/1902.05399)\]. This approach has been applied to various tasks, including image deblurring, where the iterative algorithm was unrolled into a neural network to improve performance while maintaining interpretability \[[14](http://arxiv.org/abs/2205.13051)\]. Additionally, the work by \[[15](http://arxiv.org/abs/1905.05113)\] demonstrated that unrolling methods could be used to develop fast neural network approximations for sparse coding, showing the potential of this approach for a wide range of imaging tasks \[[16](http://arxiv.org/abs/1511.04587)\].
