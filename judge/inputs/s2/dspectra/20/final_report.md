## Related Works

Deep-unrolling and plug-and-play (PnP) approaches have emerged as dominant methods for solving single-pixel imaging (SPI) inverse problems \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/1809.04693)\]. PnP algorithms, which replace proximal operators with off-the-shelf deep denoisers, offer flexibility in handling varying compression ratios (CRs) but often struggle with reconstruction accuracy and speed \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/1912.10557)\]. These methods rely on iterative algorithms where the denoiser is plugged into the optimization process, allowing for the integration of powerful pre-trained denoisers \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/1801.06756)\]. However, their performance can be limited by the quality and adaptability of the denoiser \[[4](http://arxiv.org/abs/2002.09611)\].

On the other hand, unrolling approaches transform truncated iterative optimization processes into end-to-end trainable neural networks, typically achieving better accuracy and faster inference \[[5](http://arxiv.org/abs/1903.08616)\]. These methods, such as those based on the half-quadratic splitting (HQS) or alternating direction method of multipliers (ADMM), require fine-tuning or retraining when the compression ratio changes, which limits their adaptability \[[6](http://arxiv.org/abs/2012.05703)\]. This limitation is addressed in this work by proposing a unified framework that integrates the strengths of both PnP and unrolling approaches \[[7](http://arxiv.org/abs/1704.03488)\].

Efficiency and Speed in Image Restoration: Recent advances in image restoration have focused on developing algorithms that balance computational efficiency with reconstruction accuracy. Plug-and-play (PnP) methods, which integrate deep denoisers into iterative optimization frameworks, have gained popularity for their flexibility and scalability, particularly in handling large datasets \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/1809.04693)\]. These methods often rely on proximal gradient approaches, allowing for the use of arbitrary denoisers without requiring them to be proximal operators \[[14](http://arxiv.org/abs/2205.13051)\]. However, traditional PnP approaches can be computationally intensive, especially when applied to high-resolution or large-scale imaging problems \[[15](http://arxiv.org/abs/1905.05113)\]. To address these challenges, researchers have proposed online variants of PnP algorithms that process only a subset of measurements at each iteration, significantly improving scalability \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/1809.04693)\]. Additionally, methods like ODER (Optimized Deep Equilibrium Reconstruction) have been introduced to enhance the efficiency of deep equilibrium models by incorporating stochastic approximations of measurement models, reducing memory and computational demands \[[14](http://arxiv.org/abs/2205.13051)\]. In parallel, block coordinate RED (Regularization by Denoising) algorithms have been developed to decompose large-scale estimation problems into smaller, manageable updates, enabling efficient and accurate image recovery \[[15](http://arxiv.org/abs/1905.05113)\]. Furthermore, models such as TNRD (Trainable Nonlinear Reaction Diffusion) have demonstrated high efficiency by learning time-dependent parameters through a loss-based approach, leading to fast inference and superior performance in tasks like denoising and super-resolution \[[18](http://arxiv.org/abs/1508.02848)\]. These developments highlight the ongoing efforts to create faster, more adaptable image restoration techniques that maintain high accuracy across varying conditions.

This has led to the development of plug-and-play frameworks, where pre-trained denoisers are integrated into iterative algorithms to solve various restoration tasks such as deblurring, super-resolution, and denoising \[[10](http://arxiv.org/abs/2008.13751)\]. 	Document 1: [Snippet]: «Learning-based single image super-resolution (SISR) methods are continuously showing superior effectiveness and efficiency over traditional model-based methods, largely due to the end-to-end training. However, different from model-based methods that can handle the SISR problem with different scale factors, blur kernels and noise levels under a unified MAP (maximum a posteriori) framework, learning-based methods generally lack such flexibility. To address this issue, this paper proposes an end-to-end trainable unfolding network which leverages both learningbased methods and model-based methods. Specifically, by unfolding the MAP inference via a half-quadratic splitting algorithm, a fixed number of iterations consisting of alternately solving a data subproblem and a prior subproblem can be obtained. The two subproblems then can be solved with neural modules, resulting in an end-to-end trainable, iterative network. As a result, the proposed network inherits the flexibility of model-based methods to super-resolve blurry, noisy images for different scale factors via a single model, while maintaining the advantages of learning-based methods. Extensive experiments demonstrate the superiority of the proposed deep unfolding network in terms of flexibility, effectiveness and also generalizability.»
