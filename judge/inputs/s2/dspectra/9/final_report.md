## Related Works

The segmentation of multiple sclerosis (MS) lesions from magnetic resonance imaging (MRI) has been a focal point of research due to its critical role in disease monitoring and treatment evaluation. Early approaches relied on manual segmentation, which is time-consuming and subject to inter- and intra-observer variability \[[1](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/71b5e8d9970f3b725127eb7da3d5183b921ad017)\]. To address these limitations, automated methods have been proposed, ranging from traditional image processing techniques to more recent deep learning-based approaches \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/1803.09172)\].

A significant challenge in MS lesion segmentation is the high variability in lesion appearance, size, and location, which complicates the development of universally applicable algorithms \[[1](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/71b5e8d9970f3b725127eb7da3d5183b921ad017)\]. This variability is further exacerbated by differences in MRI acquisition protocols and the inherent heterogeneity of MS lesions \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/2104.10029)\]. As a result, many studies have focused on improving segmentation accuracy by leveraging advanced machine learning techniques \[[4](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/45bf918158d3f779cb41b982fa9237682c11dd96)\].

Deep learning has emerged as a powerful tool for MS lesion segmentation, particularly through the use of convolutional neural networks (CNNs). For instance, a fully convolutional neural network (CNN) was proposed to segment white matter lesions from multi-contrast MRI images, achieving significant improvements in segmentation quality compared to existing methods \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/1803.09172)\]. Similarly, a multi-branch CNN was introduced to handle the complexity of MS lesion segmentation by integrating information from multiple modalities and scales \[[14](http://arxiv.org/abs/1811.02942)\]. These approaches have demonstrated the potential of deep learning to improve the accuracy and robustness of lesion segmentation.

Another important aspect of MS lesion analysis is the identification of specific lesion types, such as paramagnetic rim lesions (PRLs), which are associated with more severe disease progression \[[10](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/bb5b80dc4165d0ac3e28d1882b898633e84779c2)\]. Detecting PRLs is challenging due to their rarity and the need for specialized MRI sequences \[[15](http://arxiv.org/abs/2208.02135)\]. Recent studies have explored the use of machine learning to classify PRLs based on radiomic features extracted from MRI data \[[5](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/09b2a6040c15e8672169531fc76bba0b5a04296b)\]. Additionally, a fully automated method was developed to detect PRLs using T2*-phase images, demonstrating high correlation with expert annotations \[[5](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/09b2a6040c15e8672169531fc76bba0b5a04296b)\].

The class imbalance between rim and non-rim lesions poses a significant challenge in training effective classifiers \[[10](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/bb5b80dc4165d0ac3e28d1882b898633e84779c2)\]. To address this, synthetic data generation has been proposed as a solution. A study introduced a generative adversarial network (GAN) to create synthetic quantitative susceptibility maps of PRLs, which were used to improve classifier performance \[[26](http://arxiv.org/abs/1809.01410)\]. This approach was further extended to include multi-channel extensions for generating accompanying contrasts and probabilistic segmentation maps \[[26](http://arxiv.org/abs/1809.01410)\].

Automatic segmentation of multiple sclerosis (MS) lesions from magnetic resonance imaging (MRI) remains a challenging task due to the high variability in lesion appearance, size, and location across patients \[[1](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/71b5e8d9970f3b725127eb7da3d5183b921ad017)\]. Despite significant advancements, no single method has been widely adopted in clinical practice due to performance limitations in handling the heterogeneity of MS data \[[1](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/71b5e8d9970f3b725127eb7da3d5183b921ad017)\]. Recent studies have explored deep learning techniques, particularly convolutional neural networks (CNNs), to improve segmentation accuracy \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/1803.09172)\]. Another study introduced a deep learning-based approach that combines information from two time points to segment lesion activity, demonstrating improved performance over classic methods \[[6](http://arxiv.org/abs/2008.02001)\]. Additionally, a method based on non-local means (NLM) was developed to segment MS lesions from 3D MRI data, showing good similarity measures and strong correlation with expert segmentations \[[12](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/1562d113593782a0ce01347b594e05abae02fbfa)\]. These approaches highlight the ongoing efforts to develop robust and accurate automated segmentation techniques for MS lesions, with a focus on improving sensitivity, specificity, and generalizability across diverse MRI protocols and patient populations \[[1](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/71b5e8d9970f3b725127eb7da3d5183b921ad017)\]\[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/1803.09172)\]\[[6](http://arxiv.org/abs/2008.02001)\]\[[10](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/bb5b80dc4165d0ac3e28d1882b898633e84779c2)\]\[[12](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/1562d113593782a0ce01347b594e05abae02fbfa)\].

### <a id='category-automated-lesion-detection-methods'></a>Automated Lesion Detection Methods: 7 papers found.
The Related Works section for the paper focuses on automated lesion detection methods in multiple sclerosis, particularly emphasizing the use of quantitative susceptibility mapping (QSM) and machine learning techniques to identify paramagnetic rim lesions (PRLs). Recent studies have highlighted the importance of PRLs as emerging biomarkers, characterized by paramagnetic rims and associated with increased disability and tissue damage \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/2104.10029)\]. Traditional methods rely on manual annotation, which is time-consuming and prone to inter-observer variability \[[8](http://arxiv.org/abs/2201.07463)\]. To address these challenges, deep learning approaches have been proposed, such as QSMRim-Net, which integrates lesion-level radiomic and convolutional features to improve detection accuracy \[[1](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/71b5e8d9970f3b725127eb7da3d5183b921ad017)\]. This method was trained on a dataset of 177 rim+ lesions and 3986 rim-negative lesions, achieving a pROC AUC of 0.760 and PR AUC of 0.704, outperforming existing state-of-the-art methods \[[1](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/71b5e8d9970f3b725127eb7da3d5183b921ad017)\]. Additionally, synthetic data generation has been explored to mitigate class imbalance, with techniques like generative adversarial networks (GANs) producing synthetic QSM maps to enhance classifier performance \[[25](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/6827f01eea292991099b6b5acc59fde8269da9d5)\]. These approaches aim to improve longitudinal monitoring of lesion evolution and provide more reliable clinical insights \[[10](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/bb5b80dc4165d0ac3e28d1882b898633e84779c2)\]. Furthermore, the use of advanced MRI techniques, such as susceptibility-weighted imaging and high-resolution phase images, has enabled better contrast between rim and non-rim lesions, offering more accurate tissue characterization \[[16](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/ee11ea6f0d9f3cfa8d4a4eeae39ae1fd74ec030a)\]. Overall, these studies underscore the growing interest in combining advanced imaging modalities with machine learning to automate and improve the accuracy of lesion detection in MS \[[8](http://arxiv.org/abs/2201.07463)\].
