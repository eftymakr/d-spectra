## Related Works

The segmentation of multiple sclerosis (MS) lesions from magnetic resonance imaging (MRI) has been a focal point of research due to its critical role in disease monitoring and treatment evaluation. Early approaches relied on manual segmentation, which is time-consuming and subject to inter- and intra-observer variability \[[1](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/71b5e8d9970f3b725127eb7da3d5183b921ad017)\]. To address these limitations, automated methods have been proposed, ranging from traditional image processing techniques to more recent deep learning-based approaches \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/1803.09172)\].

A significant challenge in MS lesion segmentation is the high variability in lesion appearance, size, and location, which complicates the development of universally applicable algorithms \[[1](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/71b5e8d9970f3b725127eb7da3d5183b921ad017)\]. This variability is further exacerbated by differences in MRI acquisition protocols and the inherent heterogeneity of MS lesions \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/2104.10029)\]. As a result, many studies have focused on improving segmentation accuracy by leveraging advanced machine learning techniques \[[4](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/45bf918158d3f779cb41b982fa9237682c11dd96)\].

Deep learning has emerged as a powerful tool for MS lesion segmentation, particularly through the use of convolutional neural networks (CNNs). For instance, a fully convolutional neural network (CNN) was proposed to segment white matter lesions from multi-contrast MRI images, achieving significant improvements in segmentation quality compared to existing methods \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/1803.09172)\]. Similarly, a multi-branch CNN was introduced to handle the complexity of MS lesion segmentation by integrating information from multiple modalities and scales \[[14](http://arxiv.org/abs/1811.02942)\]. These approaches have demonstrated the potential of deep learning to improve the accuracy and robustness of lesion segmentation.

Another important aspect of MS lesion analysis is the identification of specific lesion types, such as paramagnetic rim lesions (PRLs), which are associated with more severe disease progression \[[10](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/bb5b80dc4165d0ac3e28d1882b898633e84779c2)\]. Detecting PRLs is challenging due to their rarity and the need for specialized MRI sequences \[[15](http://arxiv.org/abs/2208.02135)\]. Recent studies have explored the use of machine learning to classify PRLs based on radiomic features extracted from MRI data \[[5](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/09b2a6040c15e8672169531fc76bba0b5a04296b)\]. Additionally, a fully automated method was developed to detect PRLs using T2*-phase images, demonstrating high correlation with expert annotations \[[5](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/09b2a6040c15e8672169531fc76bba0b5a04296b)\].

The class imbalance between rim and non-rim lesions poses a significant challenge in training effective classifiers \[[10](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/bb5b80dc4165d0ac3e28d1882b898633e84779c2)\]. To address this, synthetic data generation has been proposed as a solution. A study introduced a generative adversarial network (GAN) to create synthetic quantitative susceptibility maps of PRLs, which were used to improve classifier performance \[[26](http://arxiv.org/abs/1809.01410)\]. This approach was further extended to include multi-channel extensions for generating accompanying contrasts and probabilistic segmentation maps \[[26](http://arxiv.org/abs/1809.01410)\].
