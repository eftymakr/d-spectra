## Related Works

Instruction-following text embedding has emerged as a critical area in natural language processing (NLP), aiming to generate dynamic text embeddings that adapt to user instructions and highlight specific attributes of text \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/1910.10683)\]. Early approaches, such as those based on traditional encoder-decoder architectures, often required re-encoding the entire corpus for each new instruction, leading to significant computational overhead \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/1810.04805)\]. This limitation has spurred research into more efficient methods that can adapt pre-computed embeddings in real time \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/1706.03762)\].

Recent work has explored the use of pre-trained language models to generate instruction-following data, enabling zero-shot generalization on new tasks \[[4](http://arxiv.org/abs/2104.08773)\]. For instance, BERT, a deep bidirectional transformer model, has demonstrated strong performance on a variety of NLP tasks by jointly conditioning on both left and right context in all layers \[[5](http://arxiv.org/abs/2304.12244)\]. Similarly, the Transformer architecture, which relies solely on attention mechanisms, has shown superior performance in sequence transduction tasks, such as machine translation \[[6](http://arxiv.org/abs/2212.10560)\]. These models have laid the groundwork for more advanced instruction-tuning techniques.

Instruction tuning has been further advanced through methods like Self-Instruct, which generates instruction, input, and output samples from a language model and uses them to fine-tune the original model \[[7](http://arxiv.org/abs/2212.09741)\]. This approach has led to significant improvements in instruction-following capabilities, achieving a 33% absolute improvement over the original model on Super-NaturalInstructions \[[8](http://arxiv.org/abs/2110.08207)\]. Similarly, the Flan Collection has demonstrated the importance of task balancing and enrichment techniques in effective instruction tuning, with Flan-T5 outperforming prior work by 3-17% across evaluation settings \[[9](http://arxiv.org/abs/2304.03277)\].

The concept of using instruction-focused label annotation to guide the adaptation of pre-computed embeddings has also been explored. For example, the INSTRUCTOR method generates text embeddings tailored to different downstream tasks and domains without further training, achieving state-of-the-art performance on a wide range of tasks \[[10](http://arxiv.org/abs/2304.08485)\]. This aligns with the core idea of GSTransform, which leverages a small amount of instruction-focused label annotation to adapt embeddings in real time \[[11](http://arxiv.org/abs/2202.12837)\].

Moreover, the use of multitask prompted training to enable zero-shot task generalization has shown promise. By converting tasks into human-readable prompted forms, models can achieve strong zero-shot performance on completely held-out tasks \[[12](http://arxiv.org/abs/2303.08774)\]. This approach has been applied to a wide variety of tasks, including classification, information retrieval, and semantic textual similarity \[[13](http://arxiv.org/abs/2308.06259)\].

Instruction tuning has emerged as a critical approach to enhance the ability of large language models (LLMs) to follow instructions and perform new tasks without explicit training on those tasks \[[5](http://arxiv.org/abs/2304.12244)\]. This technique involves fine-tuning models on a collection of tasks described via natural language instructions, which significantly improves zero-shot performance on unseen tasks \[[18](http://arxiv.org/abs/2109.01652)\]. Prior studies have demonstrated that instruction-tuned models, such as FLAN, outperform both unmodified pretrained models and even few-shot GPT-3 on various benchmarks \[[18](http://arxiv.org/abs/2109.01652)\]. However, these methods often rely on human-written instruction data, which can be limited in quantity and diversity, thereby constraining the generality of the tuned models \[[6](http://arxiv.org/abs/2212.10560)\]. To address this limitation, researchers have proposed methods like Self-Instruct, which generates synthetic instruction-following data from the model itself, reducing the need for human annotations while improving performance \[[6](http://arxiv.org/abs/2212.10560)\]. Similarly, the use of GPT-4 to generate instruction-following data has shown superior results compared to previous state-of-the-art models, highlighting the potential of machine-generated data in instruction tuning \[[9](http://arxiv.org/abs/2304.03277)\]. Additionally, the introduction of Unnatural Instructions, a dataset of creative and diverse instructions generated with minimal human labor, has shown that model-generated data can rival manually curated datasets in effectiveness \[[15](http://arxiv.org/abs/2212.09689)\]. These advancements underscore the importance of instruction tuning in enabling LLMs to generalize across tasks and improve their zero-shot capabilities, while also addressing the challenges of data scarcity and annotation costs \[[6](http://arxiv.org/abs/2212.10560)\]\[[9](http://arxiv.org/abs/2304.03277)\]\[[15](http://arxiv.org/abs/2212.09689)\].
