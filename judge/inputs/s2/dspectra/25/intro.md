## Related Works

The integration of knowledge graphs (KGs) with large language models (LLMs) to enhance reasoning capabilities has gained significant attention in recent research \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2306.08302)\]. KGs provide structured, semantically rich representations that are ideal for reasoning tasks, while LLMs excel in natural language understanding and generation \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/2310.01061)\]. However, existing verification methods often rely on unstructured text corpora, limiting their ability to effectively leverage KGs \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/2305.01157)\]. This limitation is particularly evident in tasks requiring multi-step reasoning over structured data, where LLMs struggle without adaptation \[[4](http://arxiv.org/abs/2305.13168)\].

Several approaches have explored the synergy between LLMs and KGs. For instance, \[[5](http://arxiv.org/abs/2405.20139)\] proposed a framework that incorporates KGs during the pre-training and inference phases of LLMs to enhance their understanding of knowledge. Similarly, \[[6](http://arxiv.org/abs/2402.04978)\] introduced a method called reasoning on graphs (RoG), which combines LLMs with KGs to enable faithful and interpretable reasoning by generating relation paths grounded in KGs. This approach uses a planning-retrieval-reasoning framework to ensure that reasoning paths are both valid and interpretable \[[7](http://arxiv.org/abs/1911.06136)\].

Another line of research focuses on using LLMs to improve KG construction and reasoning. \[[8](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/08cc15080d093fa776e9b3377bc96a67ea2693e4)\] conducted an exhaustive evaluation of LLMs for KG construction and reasoning, showing that LLMs like GPT-4 are more suited as inference assistants rather than few-shot information extractors. They also proposed AutoKG, a multi-agent-based approach that leverages LLMs and external sources for KG construction and reasoning \[[9](http://arxiv.org/abs/2307.07697)\]. Similarly, \[[10](http://arxiv.org/abs/2305.09645)\] introduced GNN-RAG, a method that combines the reasoning abilities of graph neural networks (GNNs) with the language understanding of LLMs in a retrieval-augmented generation (RAG) style. This approach significantly improves performance on KGQA benchmarks \[[11](http://arxiv.org/abs/2110.14168)\].

To address the challenges of hallucinations and knowledge gaps in LLMs, \[[12](http://arxiv.org/abs/2104.06378)\] proposed a collaborative training-free reasoning scheme that integrates LLMs with KGs. This scheme involves using LLMs to iteratively explore KGs, selectively retrieving task-relevant knowledge subgraphs, and combining implicit knowledge to perform reasoning while explicitly explaining the process \[[13](http://arxiv.org/abs/2402.11163)\].
