## Related Works

Graph Convolutional Networks (GCNs) have become a cornerstone in collaborative filtering (CF) for recommendation systems, owing to their ability to model high-order user-item interactions \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/1905.08108)\]. Early approaches such as Neural Graph Collaborative Filtering (NGCF) aimed to integrate user-item graphs into the embedding process, propagating embeddings to capture collaborative signals \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/1905.08108)\]. However, these models often rely on complex architectures that include nonlinear transformations and activation functions, which can introduce training difficulties and degrade performance \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/2002.02126)\]. LightGCN simplifies this by focusing on linear propagation and neighborhood aggregation, achieving significant improvements over NGCF \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/2002.02126)\]. Similarly, the Linear Residual Graph Convolutional Network (LR-GCCF) also removes non-linearities to enhance recommendation performance, demonstrating that simpler models can yield better results \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/2001.10167)\].

The effectiveness of GCNs in CF is often attributed to their ability to capture high-order connectivity through spectral graph filtering \[[4](http://arxiv.org/abs/1704.02901)\]. However, existing methods suffer from limitations such as over-smoothing and poor generalization, especially in sparse recommendation scenarios \[[5](http://arxiv.org/abs/1509.09292)\]. The Multi-Component Graph Convolutional Collaborative Filtering (MCCF) model addresses this by decomposing and recombining latent purchasing motivations, improving fine-grained preference modeling \[[6](http://arxiv.org/abs/1911.10699)\]. Likewise, the Disentangled Graph Collaborative Filtering (DGCF) model disentangles user intents by modeling intent-aware interaction graphs, leading to more interpretable and accurate representations \[[7](http://arxiv.org/abs/2001.00267)\].
