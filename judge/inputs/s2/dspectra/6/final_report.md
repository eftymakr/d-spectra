## Related Works

Narrative summarization, which involves condensing long-form texts such as books, movies, and TV scripts, has been a challenging task for existing language models due to the complexity of plotlines, character interactions, and thematic coherence \[[17](http://arxiv.org/abs/2301.09790)\]. Early approaches focused on extractive methods, such as SummaRuNNer, which leverages recurrent neural networks to produce interpretable summaries by highlighting features like information content and salience \[[15](http://arxiv.org/abs/1611.04230)\]. However, these models often struggle with abstractive summarization and content selection, leading to verbose or incoherent outputs \[[13](http://arxiv.org/abs/1808.10792)\]. More recently, researchers have explored the use of attention mechanisms and hierarchical structures to improve summarization quality. For instance, Hierarchical Transformers for Multi-Document Summarization introduced a model that processes multiple documents by encoding them in a hierarchical manner, allowing for better cross-document relationship modeling \[[9](http://arxiv.org/abs/1905.13164)\]. Similarly, PEGASUS proposed a pre-training objective tailored for abstractive summarization, where important sentences are masked and generated from the remaining content, achieving state-of-the-art performance across diverse domains \[[4](http://arxiv.org/abs/1912.08777)\].

The use of multi-agent systems has also gained traction in addressing complex summarization tasks. MetaGPT, for example, introduces a framework that incorporates human workflows into multi-agent collaboration, enabling structured coordination and domain-specific validation \[[12](http://arxiv.org/abs/2308.00352)\]. This aligns with the growing interest in transfer learning, where models are pre-trained on large datasets before being fine-tuned for specific tasks \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/1910.10683)\]. The success of transfer learning has led to the development of unified text-to-text frameworks, such as the Transformer, which has been widely adopted for various NLP tasks \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/1910.10683)\]. However, the effectiveness of these models in handling long-form narratives remains limited, as they often fail to capture the intricate structure of such texts \[[17](http://arxiv.org/abs/2301.09790)\].

To address these limitations, recent work has focused on improving content selection and coherence. Bottom-Up Abstractive Summarization introduced a content selector that identifies key phrases from the source document, enabling more efficient compression while maintaining fluency \[[13](http://arxiv.org/abs/1808.10792)\]. This approach has been further refined by models like Longformer, which processes long sequences by introducing a linear attention mechanism, making it suitable for long-document summarization tasks \[[14](http://arxiv.org/abs/2004.05150)\]. These advancements highlight the ongoing efforts to enhance both the structural and semantic aspects of narrative summarization.
