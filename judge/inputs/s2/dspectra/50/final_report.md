## Related Works

The rapid growth of open-source machine learning (ML) resources has significantly impacted information retrieval (IR) research, prompting the need for advanced querying and analysis capabilities such as tracing model evolution and recommending relevant datasets \[[13](http://arxiv.org/abs/2405.13058)\]. Existing platforms like Hugging Face have become central hubs for sharing ML models and datasets, but they lack structured representations that could enable such advanced analyses \[[13](http://arxiv.org/abs/2405.13058)\]. This gap has motivated the development of HuggingKG, a large-scale knowledge graph constructed from the Hugging Face community to enhance ML resource management \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2311.13380)\]. HuggingKG contains 2.6 million nodes and 6.2 million edges, capturing domain-specific relations and textual attributes to support complex queries \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2311.13380)\]. Alongside HuggingKG, HuggingBench is introduced as a multi-task benchmark with three novel test collections for IR tasks, including resource recommendation, classification, and tracing \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2311.13380)\].

Several studies have explored the characteristics and challenges of the Hugging Face ecosystem. For instance, a repository mining study revealed that the majority of ML models on Hugging Face lack comprehensive documentation, which hinders reproducibility and transparency \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/2401.13822)\]. Another study found that the carbon footprint of ML models is often underreported, highlighting the need for improved energy reporting practices \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/2305.11164)\]. These findings underscore the importance of structured representations for better resource management and analysis \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2311.13380)\].

The development of HuggingKG aligns with broader efforts to enhance the usability of ML resources. For example, the Open Graph Benchmark (OGB) provides a set of challenging and realistic benchmark datasets for graph machine learning, emphasizing scalability and reproducibility \[[5](http://arxiv.org/abs/2005.00687)\]. Similarly, the MS-Celeb-1M dataset is a large-scale benchmark for face recognition, showcasing the importance of comprehensive and well-documented datasets \[[10](http://arxiv.org/abs/1607.08221)\]. These works highlight the necessity of structured and well-documented resources for advancing research in ML and IR \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2311.13380)\]\[[5](http://arxiv.org/abs/2005.00687)\]\[[10](http://arxiv.org/abs/1607.08221)\].

Moreover, the use of knowledge graphs in ML has been explored in prior works. A review of relational machine learning for knowledge graphs discussed how statistical models can be trained on large knowledge graphs to predict new facts \[[12](http://arxiv.org/abs/1503.00759)\]. This concept is closely related to the integration of knowledge graphs with large language models (LLMs), as seen in the roadmap for unifying LLMs and knowledge graphs \[[14](http://arxiv.org/abs/2306.08302)\]. These studies emphasize the potential of combining structured knowledge with flexible models to improve reasoning and inference \[[12](http://arxiv.org/abs/1503.00759)\]\[[14](http://arxiv.org/abs/2306.08302)\].

### <a id='category-documentation-and-reproducibility-in-ml-models'></a>Documentation and Reproducibility in ML Models: 4 papers found.
The category "Documentation and Reproducibility in ML Models" highlights critical issues surrounding the documentation and reproducibility of machine learning models and datasets on platforms like Hugging Face. Existing studies reveal that while Hugging Face has become a central hub for sharing ML models and datasets, there are significant gaps in the completeness and quality of documentation. For instance, dataset cards on Hugging Face show marked heterogeneity in completion rates, with less popular datasets often lacking detailed information \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/2401.13822)\]. This lack of thorough documentation affects the reproducibility and transparency of research, as datasets may not be fully described or structured in a way that facilitates reuse \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/2305.11164)\]. Additionally, the documentation often prioritizes technical details over considerations for ethical or social impacts, which are crucial for responsible AI development \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/2401.13822)\]. Furthermore, the reuse of pre-trained models on Hugging Face faces challenges such as missing attributes, discrepancies between claimed and actual performance, and potential risks associated with model usage \[[11](http://arxiv.org/abs/2303.02552)\]. These issues underscore the need for improved documentation standards and tools to enhance the reliability and accessibility of ML resources. The analysis of Hugging Face's supply chain also reveals shortcomings in documentation, licensing, and consistency, which can have implications for compliance and security \[[15](http://arxiv.org/abs/2502.04484)\]. Overall, these findings emphasize the importance of structured documentation and standardized practices to support the growth and responsible use of open-source ML resources.

Research conducted on platforms like Hugging Face has revealed that while there is a growing awareness of the issue, the reporting of carbon emissions remains inconsistent, with only a minority of models providing detailed information on their environmental impact \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/2305.11164)\]. The study also identified correlations between carbon emissions and various factors, such as model size, dataset size, and application domain, suggesting that these attributes play a crucial role in determining the environmental footprint of ML systems \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2311.13380)\]. To address these challenges, researchers have proposed frameworks for categorizing models based on their carbon emission reporting practices and carbon efficiency, aiming to improve transparency and encourage more sustainable model development \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2311.13380)\]. Additionally, the use of repository mining techniques has enabled deeper analysis of carbon emissions across large-scale ML model repositories, providing valuable insights into the trends and patterns of energy consumption in the field \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/2305.11164)\]. These findings underscore the importance of developing more robust measurement tools and reporting standards to support the responsible growth of ML technologies.

### <a id='category-integration-of-llms-with-knowledge-graphs'></a>Integration of LLMs with Knowledge Graphs: 2 papers found.
The integration of large language models (LLMs) with knowledge graphs (KGs) represents a promising direction for enhancing both the interpretability and factual accuracy of AI systems \[[14](http://arxiv.org/abs/2306.08302)\]. LLMs, such as ChatGPT and GPT-4, exhibit strong generalization capabilities but are often criticized for their black-box nature and limited ability to access structured factual knowledge \[[14](http://arxiv.org/abs/2306.08302)\]. In contrast, KGs, like Wikipedia or Huapu, provide rich, structured representations of knowledge that can be leveraged to improve the reasoning and inference capabilities of LLMs \[[14](http://arxiv.org/abs/2306.08302)\]. This synergy is particularly relevant in the context of managing open-source machine learning resources, where structured knowledge can facilitate advanced queries and analyses \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2311.13380)\]. For instance, the HuggingKG project, built from the Hugging Face community, demonstrates how KGs can be used to organize and relate ML resources, enabling tasks such as model evolution tracing and dataset recommendation \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2311.13380)\]. By combining the strengths of LLMs and KGs, researchers can develop more robust and interpretable AI systems capable of handling complex tasks across multiple domains \[[14](http://arxiv.org/abs/2306.08302)\].
