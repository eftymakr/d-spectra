## Related Works

Brain tumor segmentation is a critical task in medical image analysis, with numerous approaches proposed to improve segmentation accuracy and efficiency. Early methods relied on traditional image processing techniques, but recent advances in deep learning have significantly enhanced performance \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/1603.05959)\]. Fully convolutional neural networks (FCNs), such as U-Net, have become the de facto standard for 3D medical image segmentation due to their ability to capture spatial relationships and provide end-to-end segmentation \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/1812.01049)\]. The U-Net architecture, with its symmetric encoder-decoder structure and skip connections, has been widely adopted and adapted for brain tumor segmentation tasks \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/1802.10508)\]. 

Several studies have focused on improving the performance of U-Net-based models by incorporating additional features and modifications. For instance, the use of 3D convolutions and dense training schemes has been shown to enhance the ability of models to process volumetric data and handle class imbalance \[[4](http://arxiv.org/abs/2201.01266)\]. The introduction of 3D fully connected CRFs for post-processing has also been effective in reducing false positives and improving segmentation accuracy \[[5](http://arxiv.org/abs/1810.11654)\]. 

Ensemble methods have also gained traction, as they can reduce random errors and improve performance by combining predictions from multiple models \[[6](http://arxiv.org/abs/2210.13336)\]. The BraTS challenge has served as a benchmark for evaluating these methods, with various approaches achieving high Dice scores on different sub-regions of brain tumors \[[7](http://arxiv.org/abs/2011.00848)\]. For example, the 3D U-Net with ensemble strategies achieved 9th place in the 2018 BraTS challenge \[[8](http://arxiv.org/abs/2011.11052)\]. 

More recently, transformer-based models have been explored for 3D medical image segmentation due to their ability to capture long-range dependencies \[[9](http://arxiv.org/abs/2012.06760)\]. The Swin UNETR, which combines the strengths of vision transformers with FCNNs, has shown promising results on the BraTS 2021 dataset \[[10](http://arxiv.org/abs/2010.13082)\]. Similarly, the TransBTS model, which integrates 3D CNNs with transformers, has achieved comparable or higher results than previous state-of-the-art methods \[[11](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/f89fe7d194c63a62b2cb76fac08ef525b483b964)\]. 

Efforts to improve computational efficiency and reduce training requirements have also been a focus of recent research. The nnU-Net framework, which is self-configuring and adapts to different segmentation tasks, has been widely adopted in the field \[[12](http://arxiv.org/abs/2103.04430)\]. Modifications to nnU-Net, such as region-based training and aggressive data augmentation, have further improved its performance on the BraTS 2020 challenge \[[13](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/b9c3631120c171570b94f946273b98c32505fc9a)\].

This capability has been leveraged in models such as Swin UNETR, which integrates a hierarchical Swin transformer encoder with an FCNN-based decoder to enhance segmentation performance \[[4](http://arxiv.org/abs/2201.01266)\]. These models address limitations of traditional FCNNs, which struggle with long-range information modeling due to their limited kernel sizes \[[4](http://arxiv.org/abs/2201.01266)\]. By incorporating transformers, these approaches improve the accuracy of tumor segmentation, especially for complex and variable-sized tumors. The integration of pre-trained weights and novel architectural strategies further enhances efficiency and performance, aligning with the broader trend of leveraging transformer architectures in medical imaging \[[4](http://arxiv.org/abs/2201.01266)\]\[[12](http://arxiv.org/abs/2103.04430)\].
