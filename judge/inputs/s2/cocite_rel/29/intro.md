## Related Works

Maximum Inner Product Search (MIPS) has been a focal point in machine learning and information retrieval, particularly in high-dimensional data applications \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/1405.5869)\]. Traditional approaches to MIPS often rely solely on inner product (IP) similarity, which can suffer from local optima and redundant computations \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/1405.5869)\]. Alternatively, some methods reduce MIPS to nearest neighbor search (NNS) under the Euclidean metric via space projection, which can lead to topology destruction and information loss \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/1405.5869)\]. However, recent studies suggest that these two paradigms are not inherently opposed and that integrating IP and Euclidean metrics in indexing and search algorithms can significantly enhance MIPS performance \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/1405.5869)\].

Asymmetric LSH (ALSH) was introduced to address the limitations of standard LSH for MIPS, by converting inner product search into approximate near neighbor search through asymmetric transformations \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/1610.03317)\]. This approach enables sublinear time algorithms for MIPS and has been applied in collaborative filtering tasks \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/1610.03317)\]. Similarly, the XBOX transformation, used in the Xbox recommender system, provides a Euclidean transformation for inner-product spaces, improving search efficiency \[[10](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/d8aad5d9c2c336c550ca9621b73c7688f184d50b)\]. These methods demonstrate the potential of asymmetric transformations in bridging the gap between IP and Euclidean metrics \[[10](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/d8aad5d9c2c336c550ca9621b73c7688f184d50b)\].

Graph-based methods have also gained traction in MIPS. The inner-product navigable small world graph (ip-NSW) represents a state-of-the-art method for approximate MIPS, achieving significant speedups over existing baselines \[[4](http://arxiv.org/abs/1909.13459)\]. However, its performance is attributed to the norm bias in MIPS, where large norm items dominate the search process \[[4](http://arxiv.org/abs/1909.13459)\]. To improve upon ip-NSW, ip-NSW+ was proposed, combining angular and inner-product proximity graphs to enhance search accuracy and robustness \[[4](http://arxiv.org/abs/1909.13459)\]. Similarly, the norm-adjusted proximity graph (NAPG) was introduced to construct more meaningful connections in graph-based indexes, improving the efficiency of inner product search \[[3](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/25454f456c514219c9d6eeb42200ebbf378db9ca)\].

Efforts to optimize exact MIPS have also been explored. The blocked matrix multiply (BMM) approach, which leverages hardware efficiency, outperforms state-of-the-art solvers by an order of magnitude for certain inputs \[[9](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/0b2790c2c1e23e5535421696e1ec763bbc651bbd)\]. Additionally, the MAX-IMUS algorithm, which integrates pruning techniques and hardware efficiency, further improves performance \[[9](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/0b2790c2c1e23e5535421696e1ec763bbc651bbd)\]. A data-dependent optimizer, OPTIMUS, was introduced to dynamically select the best solver for a given input, enhancing overall efficiency \[[9](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/0b2790c2c1e23e5535421696e1ec763bbc651bbd)\].
