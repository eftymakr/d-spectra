## Related Works

Protein dynamics are essential for understanding biological functions and properties, yet traditional methods such as molecular dynamics (MD) simulations are computationally expensive and time-consuming \[[1](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/21a4afa74caf78e28880b7fe4c30a09a5f9c14e3)\]. Recent advances in generative modeling have enabled more efficient and accurate protein structure prediction and conformation sampling by learning distributions over crystallographic structures \[[2](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/2cfc26b4af99f195b433fa7aa00f221b111c7cd4)\]. However, integrating physical supervision into these data-driven approaches remains a challenge, as standard energy-based objectives often lead to intractable optimization \[[3](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/2eb1ed9d4baf548391d8220c0a8a3d0be79845ed)\]. This section reviews related works that address these challenges and contribute to the field of protein structure generation and conformational sampling.

Several studies have explored the use of machine learning for protein conformational ensemble generation. For instance, idpGAN leverages a generative adversarial network (GAN) based on a transformer architecture to predict sequence-dependent coarse-grained ensembles for intrinsically disordered peptides \[[4](http://arxiv.org/abs/2304.02198)\]. Similarly, Chroma introduces a diffusion process that respects the conformational statistics of polymer ensembles, enabling the direct sampling of novel protein structures and sequences \[[5](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/dc32a984b651256a8ec282be52310e6bd33d9815)\]. These models demonstrate the potential of deep learning to generate physically realistic protein conformations without relying on extensive simulation data \[[6](http://arxiv.org/abs/2209.15611)\].

Diffusion models have also been applied to protein structure prediction. EigenFold, for example, uses a diffusion process that models the structure as a system of harmonic oscillators, naturally inducing a cascading-resolution generative process along the eigenmodes of the system \[[7](http://arxiv.org/abs/2306.05445)\]. This approach enables the generation of diverse and accurate protein structures, as demonstrated on recent CAMEO targets \[[8](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/c49a0912595a1cc70aab63524f64ed08c92194a8)\]. Similarly, the work by \[[9](http://arxiv.org/abs/2306.03117)\] presents a diffusion-based generative model that generates protein backbone structures by denoising from a random, unfolded state towards a stable folded structure, mimicking the natural folding process \[[10](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/d9bb6d9590949392cae342b75323826188b1b8a2)\].

Recent advancements in deep learning have also focused on improving the accuracy of protein structure prediction. AlphaFold, for instance, has achieved atomic accuracy in predicting protein structures, even in cases where no similar structure is known \[[11](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/1ccd3a55ec1200295ce99ec8d934668dc8f753d8)\]. This success is attributed to a novel machine learning approach that incorporates physical and biological knowledge about protein structure \[[12](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/18a93dc1558bf9d7534d0b416633cebaf75c1145)\]. ### <a id='category-diffusion-models-for-protein-conformation-sampling'></a>Diffusion Models for Protein Conformation Sampling: 4 papers found.
Document 1 introduces Chroma, a generative model for proteins and protein complexes that can directly sample novel protein structures and sequences, and can be conditioned to steer the generative process towards desired properties and functions \[[1](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/21a4afa74caf78e28880b7fe4c30a09a5f9c14e3)\]. This model utilizes a diffusion process that respects the conformational statistics of polymer ensembles, an efficient neural architecture for molecular systems, and a general low-temperature sampling algorithm for diffusion models \[[1](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/21a4afa74caf78e28880b7fe4c30a09a5f9c14e3)\]. The experimental results show that Chroma generates proteins that are highly expressed, fold, and have favorable biophysical properties \[[1](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/21a4afa74caf78e28880b7fe4c30a09a5f9c14e3)\]. 

Document 2 presents EigenFold, a diffusion generative modeling framework for sampling a distribution of structures from a given protein sequence \[[4](http://arxiv.org/abs/2304.02198)\]. This model defines a diffusion process that models the structure as a system of harmonic oscillators, naturally inducing a cascading-resolution generative process along the eigenmodes of the system \[[4](http://arxiv.org/abs/2304.02198)\]. EigenFold achieves a median TMScore of 0.84 on recent CAMEO targets and provides a more comprehensive picture of model uncertainty via the ensemble of sampled structures \[[4](http://arxiv.org/abs/2304.02198)\].

Document 3 describes a diffusion-based generative model that generates protein backbone structures via a procedure inspired by the natural folding process \[[6](http://arxiv.org/abs/2209.15611)\]. This model describes a protein backbone structure as a sequence of angles capturing the relative orientation of the constituent backbone atoms and generates structures by denoising from a random, unfolded state towards a stable folded structure \[[6](http://arxiv.org/abs/2209.15611)\]. This approach mirrors how proteins natively twist into energetically favorable conformations and alleviates the need for complex equivariant networks \[[6](http://arxiv.org/abs/2209.15611)\].

Document 4 addresses the challenge of designing novel protein structures by developing theoretical foundations of SE(3) invariant diffusion models on multiple frames followed by a novel framework, FrameDiff, for learning the SE(3) equivariant score over multiple frames \[[18](http://arxiv.org/abs/2302.02277)\]. FrameDiff is applied to monomer backbone generation and can generate designable monomers up to 500 amino acids without relying on a pretrained protein structure prediction network \[[18](http://arxiv.org/abs/2302.02277)\]. The samples are capable of generalizing beyond any known protein structure \[[18](http://arxiv.org/abs/2302.02277)\].

Recent advancements in conformational sampling of proteins have focused on overcoming the limitations of traditional Monte Carlo (MC) and molecular dynamics (MD) simulations, which are often hindered by high-energy barriers and the need for extensive computational resources \[[1](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/21a4afa74caf78e28880b7fe4c30a09a5f9c14e3)\]. Learning-based approaches have emerged as a promising alternative, enabling direct sampling of conformational spaces through generative models \[[2](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/2cfc26b4af99f195b433fa7aa00f221b111c7cd4)\]. However, these methods typically require large amounts of simulation data for training, limiting their generalizability and increasing data acquisition costs \[[3](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/2eb1ed9d4baf548391d8220c0a8a3d0be79845ed)\]. To address these challenges, researchers have explored zero-shot conformation sampling techniques that do not rely on simulation data during training or inference \[[4](http://arxiv.org/abs/2304.02198)\]. One such approach, inspired by simulated annealing, is the Str2Str framework, which leverages an amortized denoising score matching objective trained on general crystal structures \[[5](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/dc32a984b651256a8ec282be52310e6bd33d9815)\]. This method enables efficient and accurate conformation sampling without the need for extensive simulation data, offering significant speed improvements over traditional MD simulations \[[6](http://arxiv.org/abs/2209.15611)\]. Additionally, efforts to integrate physical supervision into generative models have led to methods like Energy-based Alignment (EBA), which aligns generative models with feedback from physical models to improve the physical plausibility of generated structures \[[7](http://arxiv.org/abs/2306.05445)\]. These developments highlight the growing trend toward data-efficient and physically informed approaches in protein conformation sampling \[[8](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/c49a0912595a1cc70aab63524f64ed08c92194a8)\].

and holds promise for applications in structural biology and drug discovery.
