## Related Works

The growing adoption of cloud-native applications has led to increased interest in optimizing costs and resource usage in containerized environments. FinOps, an emerging discipline that combines financial management with cloud operations, has gained traction as a means to achieve cost efficiency in cloud-based systems \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2501.14753)\]. Prior research has focused on system-level optimization strategies for resource efficiency and cost reduction in containerized systems \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2501.14753)\]. However, the analysis of network costs in Kubernetes clusters remains underexplored, despite the significant role that network usage plays in overall cloud expenditures \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2501.14753)\].

Several studies have examined cloud cost optimization techniques, particularly in the context of AI and machine learning workloads, where GPU compute represents a substantial portion of technical budgets \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/2307.12479)\]. These studies highlight the importance of resource allocation strategies, such as model quantization, GPU instance selection, and inference optimization, to reduce costs \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/2307.12479)\]. For instance, large language model (LLM) inference costs have decreased by approximately 10x annually since 2021, demonstrating the potential for cost savings through strategic optimization \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/2307.12479)\]. Additionally, case studies from Amazon Prime Video, Pinterest, Cloudflare, and Netflix illustrate practical applications of these techniques, achieving cost savings of up to 90% \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/2307.12479)\].

In the context of container orchestration, Kubernetes has emerged as a de facto standard for managing containerized applications \[[10](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/d11e5c5c08af88ee8abb9ebaa166b1d6fd198501)\]. Research on Kubernetes scheduling has identified key challenges in resource allocation and scheduling, emphasizing the need for efficient and well-defined orchestration systems \[[10](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/d11e5c5c08af88ee8abb9ebaa166b1d6fd198501)\]. The scheduling process in Kubernetes is influenced by Quality of Service (QoS) parameters, aiming to provide limited response time, efficient energy consumption, and better resource utilization \[[10](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/d11e5c5c08af88ee8abb9ebaa166b1d6fd198501)\]. These findings underscore the importance of scheduling algorithms in achieving cost-effective and efficient resource management in cloud-native environments \[[10](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/d11e5c5c08af88ee8abb9ebaa166b1d6fd198501)\].

Moreover, the integration of Kubernetes with cloud platforms has enabled the development of scalable and flexible infrastructure for modern applications \[[12](http://arxiv.org/abs/2010.10350)\]. For example, Kubernetes allows for rapid application development and management by deploying containers on Kubernetes Engine, leveraging the benefits of containerization such as faster deployments and reduced resource usage \[[3](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/b5634b15badc73f3cda72b960fd51ef03ac1265d)\]. Additionally, the use of Kubernetes in bioinformatics workflows has demonstrated its ability to scale complex data-processing tasks while maintaining reproducibility and auditability \[[18](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/d32b4e8ef3fbde009efa47249081feaa4aac8b27)\].

In the realm of FinOps, ABACUS has been proposed as a solution for optimizing cloud costs by setting budgets, enforcing them through blocking new deployments, and alerting teams when spending breaches thresholds \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2501.14753)\].
