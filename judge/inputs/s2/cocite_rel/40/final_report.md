## Related Works

Zero-Knowledge Proofs (ZKPs) have emerged as a critical cryptographic primitive for privacy-preserving computation, enabling a prover to demonstrate the truth of a statement without revealing additional information \[[3](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/9ccf7b6cb32cf89752a35bd910555adac54773e0)\]. Despite their theoretical promise, ZKPs have faced significant adoption barriers due to the computational intensity of the proving process \[[1](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/e149d93531d8c07cf43f6cae327a4795144d1304)\]. Recent research has focused on accelerating key primitives of ZKP protocols using hardware, such as GPUs and ASICs, to reduce the time required for proof generation \[[13](http://arxiv.org/abs/2408.05890)\]. However, existing accelerators often suffer from limitations such as requiring a trusted setup for each application or generating proofs with larger sizes and higher verification costs, which restrict their use in scenarios with many verifiers or strict time constraints \[[1](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/e149d93531d8c07cf43f6cae327a4795144d1304)\].

Several works have explored hardware acceleration for ZKP protocols. For instance, GZKP leverages GPU acceleration to improve performance for polynomial computations and multi-scalar multiplications (MSMs), achieving up to 48.1× speedup over standard cryptographic benchmarks \[[1](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/e149d93531d8c07cf43f6cae327a4795144d1304)\]. Similarly, PipeZK introduces a pipelined architecture with two subsystems to handle large polynomial computations and MSMs, achieving 10× speedup on cryptographic benchmarks and 5× on Zcash \[[2](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/6ee84674b7c426849c3da56cb278c08eb7a903cf)\]. The ReZK accelerator focuses on optimizing NTT/INTT and MSM operations by employing a reconfigurable design that supports variable bit-widths and sizes \[[22](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/8b12309fd3c5a1851ff8b6d9cfff018811d08d93)\]. This flexibility allows ReZK to adapt to different elliptic curves and computation requirements. Another notable work, if-ZKP, presents an FPGA-based accelerator for the multi-scalar multiplication operation in zk-SNARKs, achieving 110×–150× speedup over software implementations \[[16](http://arxiv.org/abs/2412.12481)\].

In addition to hardware acceleration, there has been significant research on improving the efficiency and usability of ZKP frameworks. A recent survey of ZKP frameworks highlights the growing number of open-source systems and their applications in various domains, including blockchain and privacy-preserving machine learning \[[17](http://arxiv.org/abs/2502.07063)\]. However, these frameworks often face challenges in terms of usability and performance, particularly for complex computations \[[17](http://arxiv.org/abs/2502.07063)\]. The development of zkSpeed, an accelerator for HyperPlonk, addresses these limitations by supporting one-time, universal setup and generating small proof sizes, which are essential for publicly verifiable, consensus-based systems \[[1](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/e149d93531d8c07cf43f6cae327a4795144d1304)\]. By accelerating the entire protocol, including SumCheck and MSMs, zkSpeed achieves geometric mean speedups of 801× over CPU baselines, demonstrating the potential of full-chip acceleration for ZKP

### <a id='category-hardware-acceleration-techniques'></a>Hardware Acceleration Techniques: 5 papers found.
Hardware acceleration techniques for cryptographic protocols have seen significant advancements, particularly in addressing the computational bottlenecks of zero-knowledge proofs (ZKPs) and fully homomorphic encryption (FHE). For ZKPs, the primary challenge lies in the time-consuming proof generation, which involves large polynomial computations and multi-scalar multiplications on elliptic curves. To tackle this, PipeZK was introduced as a pipelined accelerator with two subsystems, one optimizing polynomial computations through efficient dataflow and memory management, and the other handling multi-scalar multiplications with a dynamic work dispatch mechanism \[[2](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/6ee84674b7c426849c3da56cb278c08eb7a903cf)\]. This approach achieves substantial speedups, up to 10x on cryptographic benchmarks and 5x on Zcash \[[2](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/6ee84674b7c426849c3da56cb278c08eb7a903cf)\]. Similarly, FHE faces prohibitive overheads, with prior accelerators only supporting simple programs and struggling with complex computations \[[10](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/b718a4b59ee6ee21c7c2ee1aa64085231853535e)\]. CraterLake addresses this by introducing a scalable architecture for large ciphertexts and novel functional units, achieving up to 4,600X speedup over CPUs for deep neural networks \[[10](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/b718a4b59ee6ee21c7c2ee1aa64085231853535e)\]. F1, another FHE accelerator, is a programmable wide-vector processor optimized for FHE primitives like modular arithmetic and number-theoretic transforms, achieving up to 17,000X speedup over software implementations \[[15](http://arxiv.org/abs/2109.05371)\]. Additionally, BTS focuses on supporting bootstrapping as a first-class citizen in FHE, addressing the significant computational and memory bandwidth demands of this process \[[18](http://arxiv.org/abs/2112.15479)\]. These accelerators collectively highlight the growing importance of hardware-centric solutions to overcome the performance limitations of cryptographic protocols, enabling practical deployment in privacy-preserving applications.

### <a id='category-zkp-fundamentals-and-applications'></a>ZKP Fundamentals and Applications: 12 papers found.
The category "ZKP Fundamentals and Applications" explores the theoretical underpinnings and practical implementations of zero-knowledge proofs (ZKPs), emphasizing their role in privacy-preserving computation and secure verification. ZKPs allow a prover to demonstrate the truth of a statement without revealing any additional information, a concept illustrated by the idea that proving a graph is Hamiltonian through a Hamiltonian tour contains more knowledge than simply stating the graph is Hamiltonian \[[3](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/9ccf7b6cb32cf89752a35bd910555adac54773e0)\]. This foundational principle is further refined by defining zero-knowledge proofs as those that convey no additional knowledge beyond the correctness of the proposition \[[7](http://arxiv.org/abs/1912.08939)\]. The development of zero-knowledge protocols has been extended to languages not efficiently recognizable, such as quadratic residuosity and nonresiduosity, marking a significant advancement in cryptographic security \[[3](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/9ccf7b6cb32cf89752a35bd910555adac54773e0)\]. In multi-prover environments, novel protocols for problems like 3-colorability have been introduced, leveraging two or three provers to achieve efficient and secure verification with minimal computational resources \[[5](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/27463b98f47a73de3b77549985aae80c344ddf69)\]. Additionally, the use of bit commitment and homomorphic properties in commitment schemes has enabled the creation of polynomial-time ZKPs for NP-Complete problems like Subset Sum and 3SAT, ensuring security even against quantum adversaries \[[10](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/b718a4b59ee6ee21c7c2ee1aa64085231853535e)\]. The theoretical framework also addresses the limitations of NP-complete languages, showing that perfect zero-knowledge protocols for such languages would imply a collapse of the polynomial hierarchy \[[7](http://arxiv.org/abs/1912.08939)\]. These advancements underscore the evolving landscape of ZKP research, balancing theoretical rigor with practical applicability across domains such as blockchain, machine learning, and secure voting \[[9](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/d5ccb8b351118b35b1a565ad27de4cd2ebdd0287)\].
