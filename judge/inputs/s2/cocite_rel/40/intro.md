## Related Works

Zero-Knowledge Proofs (ZKPs) have emerged as a critical cryptographic primitive for privacy-preserving computation, enabling a prover to demonstrate the truth of a statement without revealing additional information \[[3](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/9ccf7b6cb32cf89752a35bd910555adac54773e0)\]. Despite their theoretical promise, ZKPs have faced significant adoption barriers due to the computational intensity of the proving process \[[1](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/e149d93531d8c07cf43f6cae327a4795144d1304)\]. Recent research has focused on accelerating key primitives of ZKP protocols using hardware, such as GPUs and ASICs, to reduce the time required for proof generation \[[13](http://arxiv.org/abs/2408.05890)\]. However, existing accelerators often suffer from limitations such as requiring a trusted setup for each application or generating proofs with larger sizes and higher verification costs, which restrict their use in scenarios with many verifiers or strict time constraints \[[1](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/e149d93531d8c07cf43f6cae327a4795144d1304)\].

Several works have explored hardware acceleration for ZKP protocols. For instance, GZKP leverages GPU acceleration to improve performance for polynomial computations and multi-scalar multiplications (MSMs), achieving up to 48.1× speedup over standard cryptographic benchmarks \[[1](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/e149d93531d8c07cf43f6cae327a4795144d1304)\]. Similarly, PipeZK introduces a pipelined architecture with two subsystems to handle large polynomial computations and MSMs, achieving 10× speedup on cryptographic benchmarks and 5× on Zcash \[[2](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/6ee84674b7c426849c3da56cb278c08eb7a903cf)\]. The ReZK accelerator focuses on optimizing NTT/INTT and MSM operations by employing a reconfigurable design that supports variable bit-widths and sizes \[[22](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/8b12309fd3c5a1851ff8b6d9cfff018811d08d93)\]. This flexibility allows ReZK to adapt to different elliptic curves and computation requirements. Another notable work, if-ZKP, presents an FPGA-based accelerator for the multi-scalar multiplication operation in zk-SNARKs, achieving 110×–150× speedup over software implementations \[[16](http://arxiv.org/abs/2412.12481)\].

In addition to hardware acceleration, there has been significant research on improving the efficiency and usability of ZKP frameworks. A recent survey of ZKP frameworks highlights the growing number of open-source systems and their applications in various domains, including blockchain and privacy-preserving machine learning \[[17](http://arxiv.org/abs/2502.07063)\]. However, these frameworks often face challenges in terms of usability and performance, particularly for complex computations \[[17](http://arxiv.org/abs/2502.07063)\]. The development of zkSpeed, an accelerator for HyperPlonk, addresses these limitations by supporting one-time, universal setup and generating small proof sizes, which are essential for publicly verifiable, consensus-based systems \[[1](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/e149d93531d8c07cf43f6cae327a4795144d1304)\]. By accelerating the entire protocol, including SumCheck and MSMs, zkSpeed achieves geometric mean speedups of 801× over CPU baselines, demonstrating the potential of full-chip acceleration for ZKP
