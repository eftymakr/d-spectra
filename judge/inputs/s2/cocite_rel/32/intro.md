## Related Works

Recent advancements in large language models (LLMs) have emphasized the importance of understanding how feature representations evolve across layers, as this knowledge is critical for improving interpretability and robustness \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2307.09288)\]. Several studies have explored the dynamics of representation learning in LLMs, focusing on how fine-tuning affects model behavior. For example, Llama 2 introduces a collection of pretrained and fine-tuned large language models optimized for dialogue use cases, demonstrating that fine-tuned models can outperform open-source chat models on various benchmarks \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2307.09288)\]. Similarly, LLaMA presents a series of foundation models that are trained on publicly available datasets, achieving state-of-the-art performance without reliance on proprietary data \[[9](http://arxiv.org/abs/2302.13971)\]. These models highlight the importance of efficient fine-tuning strategies, as training large models from scratch is computationally prohibitive.

Parameter-efficient transfer learning methods have emerged as a critical area of research, aiming to reduce the number of trainable parameters while maintaining performance. Techniques such as LoRA, which injects low-rank matrices into Transformer layers, have shown that fine-tuning can be achieved with a fraction of the parameters, leading to significant memory and computational savings \[[7](http://arxiv.org/abs/2106.09685)\]. BitFit, another sparse fine-tuning method, modifies only the bias terms of the model, achieving competitive results with smaller training data \[[8](http://arxiv.org/abs/2106.10199)\]. These methods align with the broader trend of exploring low-rank adaptations to make fine-tuning more feasible for large-scale models.

The effectiveness of fine-tuning has also been linked to the concept of intrinsic dimensionality. Research shows that pre-trained models often have a low intrinsic dimension, meaning that only a small subset of parameters is sufficient for fine-tuning \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/2012.13255)\]. This insight has led to the development of methods like Compacter, which inserts task-specific weight matrices into pretrained models, achieving performance on par with full fine-tuning while significantly reducing the number of trainable parameters \[[10](http://arxiv.org/abs/2106.04647)\]. These findings support the idea that fine-tuning is not merely about learning new task-specific knowledge but rather about exposing knowledge already induced during pre-training.

In addition to efficiency, the alignment of LLMs with human preferences has become a focal point. Direct Preference Optimization (DPO) introduces a new parameterization of the reward model in reinforcement learning from human feedback (RLHF), enabling the extraction of optimal policies in closed form \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/2305.18290)\]. This approach simplifies the fine-tuning process, eliminating the need for complex reinforcement learning procedures.
