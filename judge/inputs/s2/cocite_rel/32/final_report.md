## Related Works

Recent advancements in large language models (LLMs) have emphasized the importance of understanding how feature representations evolve across layers, as this knowledge is critical for improving interpretability and robustness \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2307.09288)\]. Several studies have explored the dynamics of representation learning in LLMs, focusing on how fine-tuning affects model behavior. For example, Llama 2 introduces a collection of pretrained and fine-tuned large language models optimized for dialogue use cases, demonstrating that fine-tuned models can outperform open-source chat models on various benchmarks \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2307.09288)\]. Similarly, LLaMA presents a series of foundation models that are trained on publicly available datasets, achieving state-of-the-art performance without reliance on proprietary data \[[9](http://arxiv.org/abs/2302.13971)\]. These models highlight the importance of efficient fine-tuning strategies, as training large models from scratch is computationally prohibitive.

Parameter-efficient transfer learning methods have emerged as a critical area of research, aiming to reduce the number of trainable parameters while maintaining performance. Techniques such as LoRA, which injects low-rank matrices into Transformer layers, have shown that fine-tuning can be achieved with a fraction of the parameters, leading to significant memory and computational savings \[[7](http://arxiv.org/abs/2106.09685)\]. BitFit, another sparse fine-tuning method, modifies only the bias terms of the model, achieving competitive results with smaller training data \[[8](http://arxiv.org/abs/2106.10199)\]. These methods align with the broader trend of exploring low-rank adaptations to make fine-tuning more feasible for large-scale models.

The effectiveness of fine-tuning has also been linked to the concept of intrinsic dimensionality. Research shows that pre-trained models often have a low intrinsic dimension, meaning that only a small subset of parameters is sufficient for fine-tuning \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/2012.13255)\]. This insight has led to the development of methods like Compacter, which inserts task-specific weight matrices into pretrained models, achieving performance on par with full fine-tuning while significantly reducing the number of trainable parameters \[[10](http://arxiv.org/abs/2106.04647)\]. These findings support the idea that fine-tuning is not merely about learning new task-specific knowledge but rather about exposing knowledge already induced during pre-training.

In addition to efficiency, the alignment of LLMs with human preferences has become a focal point. This approach simplifies the fine-tuning process, eliminating the need for complex reinforcement learning procedures. Why can we use relatively vanilla gradient descent algorithms (e.g., without strong regularization) to tune a model with hundreds of millions of parameters on datasets with only hundreds or thousands of labeled examples? In this paper, we argue that analyzing fine-tuning through the lens of intrinsic dimension provides us with empirical and theoretical intuitions to explain this remarkable phenomenon. We empirically show that common pre-trained models have a very low intrinsic dimension; in other words, there exists a low dimension reparameterization that is as effective for fine-tuning as the full parameter space. ### <a id='category-parameter-efficient-transfer-learning-methods'></a>Parameter-Efficient Transfer Learning Methods: 6 papers found.
Parameter-efficient transfer learning methods have gained significant attention in NLP due to their ability to reduce computational costs while maintaining strong performance. These methods typically involve fine-tuning only a small subset of parameters, such as bias terms \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/2305.18290)\], low-rank matrices \[[7](http://arxiv.org/abs/2106.09685)\], or task-specific vectors \[[12](http://arxiv.org/abs/2101.00190)\], rather than retraining all model parameters. For instance, LoRA introduces trainable rank decomposition matrices into each Transformer layer, drastically reducing the number of trainable parameters and memory usage \[[7](http://arxiv.org/abs/2106.09685)\]. Similarly, BitFit focuses on modifying only the bias terms of the model, achieving competitive results with full fine-tuning \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/2305.18290)\]. Prefix-tuning, on the other hand, keeps the model weights frozen and instead optimizes a sequence of task-specific vectors, referred to as the prefix, which influences the model's output during generation \[[12](http://arxiv.org/abs/2101.00190)\]. Compacter further advances this field by integrating ideas from adapters, low-rank optimization, and hypercomplex multiplication layers, allowing for efficient fine-tuning with minimal parameter updates \[[10](http://arxiv.org/abs/2106.04647)\]. These approaches collectively highlight the importance of strategic parameter modifications and their impact on model performance, offering a unified framework for understanding and improving parameter-efficient transfer learning \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2307.09288)\].
