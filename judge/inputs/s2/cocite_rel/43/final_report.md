## Related Works

The field of large language model (LLM) training and optimization has seen significant advancements, particularly in addressing the challenges of scalability, efficiency, and performance prediction. Early work on LLMs focused on improving model architecture and training efficiency, with notable contributions in quantization and sparsity-aware techniques \[[9](http://arxiv.org/abs/1712.05877)\]. For instance, quantization methods have been proposed to reduce computational and memory costs, enabling efficient inference on resource-constrained devices \[[9](http://arxiv.org/abs/1712.05877)\]. These techniques are crucial for deploying LLMs on edge devices, where memory and processing power are limited.

Instruction tuning has emerged as a powerful approach to enhance the versatility and performance of LLMs. Several studies have explored instruction-tuned models, demonstrating that fine-tuning on diverse instruction datasets significantly improves model capabilities \[[15](http://arxiv.org/abs/2210.11416)\]. For example, the Flan-PaLM model, instruction-finetuned on a large number of tasks, outperforms its base version by a substantial margin \[[15](http://arxiv.org/abs/2210.11416)\]. Similarly, the WizardLM model leverages AI-generated instruction data to improve the complexity and diversity of training examples, leading to better performance on complex tasks \[[6](http://arxiv.org/abs/2304.12244)\]. This approach has also been extended to multilingual and multimodal settings, where instruction tuning helps models generalize across different domains and modalities \[[16](http://arxiv.org/abs/2304.08485)\].

Efforts to improve the efficiency of LLM inference have also been a key area of research. Techniques such as "windowing" and "row-column bundling" have been introduced to reduce data transfer and improve inference speed by leveraging hardware characteristics \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2312.11514)\]. These methods enable running models up to twice the size of available DRAM, with significant speed improvements over naive loading approaches \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2312.11514)\]. Additionally, quantization and training techniques have been developed to enable integer-only arithmetic inference, improving the trade-off between accuracy and on-device latency \[[9](http://arxiv.org/abs/1712.05877)\].

The importance of accurate performance modeling for LLM training has also been recognized. Recent work has focused on developing performance models that can predict execution behaviors and guide optimization efforts \[[14](http://arxiv.org/abs/2306.05685)\]. For example, the MT-Bench benchmark evaluates LLMs based on human preferences, highlighting the need for scalable and explainable evaluation methods \[[14](http://arxiv.org/abs/2306.05685)\]. These benchmarks are essential for assessing the real-world effectiveness of LLMs and guiding their deployment.

However, their substantial computational and memory requirements present challenges, especially for devices with limited DRAM capacity. This paper tackles the challenge of efficiently running LLMs that exceed the available DRAM capacity by storing the model parameters in flash memory, but bringing them on demand to DRAM. Our method involves constructing an inference cost model that takes into account the characteristics of flash memory, guiding us to optimize in two critical areas: reducing the volume of data transferred from flash and reading data in larger, more contiguous chunks. These methods collectively enable running models up to twice the size of the available DRAM, with a 4-5x and 20-25x increase in inference speed compared to naive loading approaches in CPU and GPU, respectively. Our integration of sparsity awareness, context-adaptive loading, and a hardware-oriented design paves the way for effective inference of LLMs on devices with limited memory.»

### <a id='category-instruction-tuning-techniques'></a>Instruction Tuning Techniques: 6 papers found.
Instruction tuning techniques have been extensively explored to enhance the performance of large language models (LLMs) on a wide range of tasks. Recent studies have demonstrated that training LLMs on instruction-following data significantly improves their zero-shot and few-shot capabilities, enabling them to generalize better to unseen tasks \[[15](http://arxiv.org/abs/2210.11416)\]. For instance, instruction-tuned models like Flan-PaLM 540B, trained on 1.8K tasks, outperform baseline models by a large margin, achieving state-of-the-art results on benchmarks such as MMLU \[[15](http://arxiv.org/abs/2210.11416)\]. Similarly, the MAmmoTH series, trained on a hybrid dataset combining chain-of-thought (CoT) and program-of-thought (PoT) rationales, has shown substantial improvements in mathematical reasoning, with MAmmoTH-7B achieving 33% accuracy on the MATH dataset, surpassing existing open-source models \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/2309.05653)\]. Instruction tuning has also been extended to multimodal settings, where models like LLaVA leverage generated instruction data to improve visual and language understanding, achieving performance comparable to GPT-4 on synthetic multimodal tasks \[[16](http://arxiv.org/abs/2304.08485)\]. Furthermore, efforts to automate instruction data generation, such as the Evol-Instruct method, have enabled the creation of complex instruction datasets, leading to models like WizardLM that outperform human-created instruction data and ChatGPT in certain aspects \[[6](http://arxiv.org/abs/2304.12244)\]. The importance of diverse and high-quality instruction datasets has also been emphasized, with models like T\"ulu and UltraLLaMA demonstrating that carefully curated instruction data can significantly enhance model performance across multiple dimensions, including reasoning, coding, and multilingual understanding \[[12](http://arxiv.org/abs/2306.04751)\]\[[17](http://arxiv.org/abs/2305.14233)\]. These findings highlight the critical role of instruction tuning in advancing the capabilities of LLMs and suggest that further research into instruction data generation and model fine-tuning is essential for continued progress in the field.

These frameworks collectively underscore the need for robust, diverse, and scalable evaluation methods to accurately measure LLM capabilities and identify areas for improvement.

While there is emerging work on relieving this pressure via model compression, the applicability and performance of existing compression techniques is limited by the scale and complexity of GPT models.
