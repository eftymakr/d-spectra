## Related Works

The problem of learning from noisy labels has been extensively studied in recent years, with a variety of approaches proposed to mitigate the adverse effects of label noise on deep learning models. Early works focused on modifying the loss function to make it more robust to label corruption. For instance,  introduced the mean absolute error (MAE) as a noise-robust alternative to the categorical cross entropy (CCE) loss, showing that MAE can yield better performance under label noise. However, MAE was found to perform poorly with deep neural networks and challenging datasets, motivating further research into more generalized noise-robust loss functions \[[14](http://arxiv.org/abs/1805.07836)\]. The Generalized Cross Entropy (GCE) loss was proposed as a theoretically grounded alternative that generalizes both MAE and CCE, offering improved performance across a wide range of noisy label scenarios \[[14](http://arxiv.org/abs/1805.07836)\].

Another line of work focuses on semi-supervised learning techniques to address noisy labels. The DivideMix framework leverages a mixture model to dynamically divide training data into clean and noisy subsets, then trains the model in a semi-supervised manner \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2002.07394)\]. This approach also introduces two diverged networks that train on each other’s dataset divisions, helping to avoid confirmation bias. Similarly, the Co-teaching framework trains two neural networks simultaneously, allowing them to teach each other by selecting data with potentially clean labels and back propagating the selected data from the peer network \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/1804.06872)\]. This method has shown superior robustness against noisy labels compared to state-of-the-art methods.

In addition to loss function modifications and semi-supervised learning, several works have explored the use of consistency-based regularization to improve robustness to label noise. The early-learning regularization approach capitalizes on the early learning phase, where models first fit clean labels before memorizing noisy ones. By leveraging semi-supervised learning to produce target probabilities and designing a regularization term that steers the model toward these targets, this method prevents the memorization of false labels \[[6](http://arxiv.org/abs/2007.00151)\]. Similarly, the work by \[[13](http://arxiv.org/abs/1904.11238)\] proposes an unsupervised label noise modeling approach using a beta mixture to estimate the probability that a sample is mislabeled, and corrects the loss using bootstrapping techniques.

Recent advances have also explored the use of distillation and knowledge transfer to improve learning from noisy labels. The Learning from Noisy Labels with Distillation approach utilizes a small clean dataset and label relations in a knowledge graph to "hedge the risk" of learning from noisy labels \[[12](http://arxiv.org/abs/1703.02391)\]. This method has been shown to be effective across various domains

	Document 1: [Snippet]: «Deep learning with noisy labels is practically challenging, as the capacity of deep models is so high that they can totally memorize these noisy labels sooner or later during training. Nonetheless, recent studies on the memorization effects of deep neural networks show that they would first memorize training data of clean labels and then those of noisy labels. Therefore in this paper, we propose a new deep learning paradigm called Co-teaching for combating with noisy labels. Namely, we train two deep neural networks simultaneously, and let them teach each other given every mini-batch: firstly, each network feeds forward all data and selects some data of possibly clean labels; secondly, two networks communicate with each other what data in this mini-batch should be used for training; finally, each network back propagates the data selected by its peer network and updates itself. Empirical results on noisy versions of MNIST, CIFAR-10 and CIFAR-100 demonstrate that Co-teaching is much superior to the state-of-the-art methods in the robustness of trained deep models.»

### <a id='category-noise-robust-loss-functions'></a>Noise Robust Loss Functions: 5 papers found.
In the context of noise robust loss functions for deep learning, several approaches have been proposed to address the challenges posed by noisy labels. One notable method is Symmetric cross entropy Learning (SL), which addresses both overfitting to noisy labels on easy classes and under learning on hard classes by combining Cross Entropy (CE) with a noise-robust Reverse Cross Entropy (RCE) \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/1908.06112)\]. Another approach involves loss correction procedures that are agnostic to network architecture and application domain, relying on matrix inversion and multiplication to adjust for class-dependent label noise \[[4](http://arxiv.org/abs/1609.03683)\]. Additionally, research has focused on identifying loss functions that are inherently robust to label noise, such as the mean absolute error (MAE) loss, which has been shown to enable effective learning even under noisy conditions \[[8](http://arxiv.org/abs/1712.09482)\]. A more recent development is the introduction of a novel information-theoretic loss function, $\mathcal(L)_(DMI)$, based on Determinant based Mutual Information (DMI), which is provably robust to instance-independent label noise and can be applied to any classification neural network without requiring auxiliary information \[[9](http://arxiv.org/abs/1909.03388)\]. Furthermore, theoretical analyses have shown that certain loss functions, when used for risk minimization, can yield robust performance in the presence of label noise, especially when combined with appropriate training strategies \[[5](http://arxiv.org/abs/1711.07131)\]. These methods collectively highlight the ongoing efforts to develop loss functions that are both theoretically grounded and practically effective in handling noisy labels in deep learning.

### <a id='category-consistency-based-regularization'></a>Consistency-Based Regularization: 1 papers found.
Context: 
	Document 1: [Snippet]: «Current state-of-the-art deep learning systems for visual object recognition and detection use purely supervised training with regularization such as dropout to avoid overfitting. The performance depends critically on the amount of labeled examples, and in current practice the labels are assumed to be unambiguous and accurate. However, this assumption often does not hold; e.g. in recognition, class labels may be missing; in detection, objects in the image may not be localized; and in general, the labeling may be subjective. In this work we propose a generic way to handle noisy and incomplete labeling by augmenting the prediction objective with a notion of consistency. We consider a prediction consistent if the same prediction is made given similar percepts, where the notion of similarity is between deep network features computed from the input data. In experiments we demonstrate that our approach yields substantial robustness to label noise on several datasets. On MNIST handwritten digits, we show that our model is robust to label corruption. On the Toronto Face Database, we show that our model handles well the case of subjective labels in emotion recognition, achieving state-of-the- art results, and can also benefit from unlabeled face images with no modification to our method. On the ILSVRC2014 detection challenge data, we show that our approach extends to very deep networks, high resolution images and structured outputs, and results in improved scalable detection.»

Context: 
	Document 1: [Snippet]: «Despite being robust to small amounts of label noise, convolutional neural networks trained with stochastic gradient methods have been shown to easily fit random labels. When there are a mixture of correct and mislabelled targets, networks tend to fit the former before the latter. This suggests using a suitable two-component mixture model as an unsupervised generative model of sample loss values during training to allow online estimation of the probability that a sample is mislabelled. Specifically, we propose a beta mixture to estimate this probability and correct the loss by relying on the network prediction (the so-called bootstrapping loss). We further adapt mixup augmentation to drive our approach a step further. Experiments on CIFAR-10/100 and TinyImageNet demonstrate a robustness to label noise that substantially outperforms recent state-of-the-art.
