## Related Works

Federated learning (FL) has emerged as a promising paradigm for collaborative machine learning while preserving data privacy by keeping training data decentralized \[[22](http://arxiv.org/abs/2401.11018)\]. The increasing regulatory focus on data privacy, particularly the "right to be forgotten" (RTBF), has spurred research into federated unlearning (FU), which aims to remove the influence of specific clients or data points from the global model \[[22](http://arxiv.org/abs/2401.11018)\]. This section reviews existing work on federated unlearning, highlighting key approaches, challenges, and evaluation practices.

Several studies have proposed methods for federated unlearning. FedEraser \[[1](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/eadeffdec9fac8fd7f9aea732ca410eb082b7dcf)\] introduces a technique that reconstructs an unlearned model by leveraging historical parameter updates stored at the central server, enabling faster unlearning compared to retraining from scratch. Similarly, the paper \[[8](http://arxiv.org/abs/2207.05521)\] proposes a method that first performs local unlearning at the client and then uses the locally unlearned model as initialization for a few rounds of federated learning to obtain the unlearned global model. This approach avoids the need for storing historical updates and reduces computational overhead.

The concept of verifiable federated unlearning has also been explored. VeriFi \[[6](http://arxiv.org/abs/2205.12709)\] introduces a framework that allows clients to verify the unlearning effect by marking and checking the global model's performance on specific fingerprints. This ensures that the unlearning process is transparent and verifiable, addressing a critical gap in existing methods. Additionally, the paper \[[26](http://arxiv.org/abs/2405.17462)\] presents Ferrari, a federated feature unlearning framework that minimizes feature sensitivity, enabling the removal of sensitive or biased features from the global model.

Efforts to ensure privacy during federated unlearning have also been made. Starfish \[[28](http://arxiv.org/abs/2404.09724)\] proposes a privacy-preserving federated unlearning scheme using Two-Party Computation (2PC) techniques to protect client data during the unlearning process. This method ensures that historical client data cannot be reconstructed from the uploaded information, mitigating information leakage risks.

Evaluation practices for federated unlearning remain a challenge. While some studies focus on single metrics \[[1](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/eadeffdec9fac8fd7f9aea732ca410eb082b7dcf)\], others advocate for multiple evaluation metrics to comprehensively assess unlearning effectiveness \[[30](http://arxiv.org/abs/2005.02205)\]. The paper \[[30](http://arxiv.org/abs/2005.02205)\] highlights that current evaluation practices may inadvertently introduce privacy risks, emphasizing the need for rigorous evaluation frameworks. This aligns with the findings of the current study, which proposes adapted and newly proposed evaluation metrics to address both under-unlearning and over-unlearning scenarios.

### <a id='category-unlearning-methodologies'></a>Unlearning Methodologies: 21 papers found.
The category of Unlearning Methodologies in federated learning encompasses a range of approaches aimed at enabling the removal of specific data contributions from global models while maintaining model performance and efficiency. These methods are driven by the "right to be forgotten" and the need to counter data poisoning attacks, ensuring compliance with privacy regulations such as GDPR \[[1](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/eadeffdec9fac8fd7f9aea732ca410eb082b7dcf)\]. Existing techniques often rely on retraining-based model calibration, which poses privacy risks and is computationally expensive \[[5](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/06edb1f6eadb6c141d720fcca4c854578dc5d670)\]. To address these challenges, researchers have proposed alternative strategies, such as FedEraser, which leverages historical parameter updates to reconstruct unlearned models without retraining from scratch, achieving a 4× speed-up \[[1](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/eadeffdec9fac8fd7f9aea732ca410eb082b7dcf)\]. Similarly, FRU introduces a log-based rollback mechanism inspired by database transactions, enabling efficient unlearning in federated recommendation systems \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/2210.10958)\]. Other methods, such as FedRecovery, utilize differential privacy to erase client influence by removing weighted gradient residuals and adding Gaussian noise, ensuring statistical indistinguishability between unlearned and retrained models \[[5](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/06edb1f6eadb6c141d720fcca4c854578dc5d670)\]. ### <a id='category-regulatory-context-and-right-to-be-forgotten'></a>Regulatory Context and Right to Be Forgotten: 5 papers found.
The concept of federated unlearning has emerged as a critical component in realizing the "right to be forgotten" within federated learning (FL) frameworks, enabling the removal of user data from both the training data and the resulting FL model \[[4](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/39da2d48f2132b919405e3bb6df6ece44ac6e15a)\]. This paradigm addresses the challenge of ensuring that revoked data no longer influences the model, a process termed federated unlearning \[[7](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/a52c3ec7325e81bd87fdf8d74bf9acfd1e1ffe5f)\]. While the most straightforward approach involves retraining the model from scratch after data removal, this method is computationally expensive and impractical for large-scale FL systems \[[4](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/39da2d48f2132b919405e3bb6df6ece44ac6e15a)\]. Consequently, researchers have explored more efficient methods, such as reverse stochastic gradient ascent (SGA) and elastic weight consolidation (EWC), to achieve effective unlearning without full retraining \[[4](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/39da2d48f2132b919405e3bb6df6ece44ac6e15a)\]. These approaches aim to minimize the impact of revoked data while maintaining model performance \[[4](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/39da2d48f2132b919405e3bb6df6ece44ac6e15a)\]. However, recent studies have highlighted potential privacy risks, such as information leakage, where unlearning may inadvertently retain traces of the data in the model \[[30](http://arxiv.org/abs/2005.02205)\]. This has led to the development of privacy-preserving frameworks that incorporate techniques like differential privacy and temperature scaling to mitigate these risks \[[30](http://arxiv.org/abs/2005.02205)\]. Additionally, the field has seen efforts to categorize and evaluate existing federated unlearning algorithms, identifying common challenges and proposing taxonomies to guide future research \[[7](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/a52c3ec7325e81bd87fdf8d74bf9acfd1e1ffe5f)\]. These contributions collectively highlight the growing interest in federated unlearning as a means to balance data privacy and model efficiency in FL systems \[[4](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/39da2d48f2132b919405e3bb6df6ece44ac6e15a)\]\[[7](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/a52c3ec7325e81bd87fdf8d74bf9acfd1e1ffe5f)\]\[[30](http://arxiv.org/abs/2005.02205)\].
