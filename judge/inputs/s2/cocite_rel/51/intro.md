## Related Works

Federated learning (FL) has emerged as a promising paradigm for collaborative machine learning while preserving data privacy by keeping training data decentralized \[[22](http://arxiv.org/abs/2401.11018)\]. The increasing regulatory focus on data privacy, particularly the "right to be forgotten" (RTBF), has spurred research into federated unlearning (FU), which aims to remove the influence of specific clients or data points from the global model \[[22](http://arxiv.org/abs/2401.11018)\]. This section reviews existing work on federated unlearning, highlighting key approaches, challenges, and evaluation practices.

Several studies have proposed methods for federated unlearning. FedEraser \[[1](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/eadeffdec9fac8fd7f9aea732ca410eb082b7dcf)\] introduces a technique that reconstructs an unlearned model by leveraging historical parameter updates stored at the central server, enabling faster unlearning compared to retraining from scratch. Similarly, the paper \[[8](http://arxiv.org/abs/2207.05521)\] proposes a method that first performs local unlearning at the client and then uses the locally unlearned model as initialization for a few rounds of federated learning to obtain the unlearned global model. This approach avoids the need for storing historical updates and reduces computational overhead.

The concept of verifiable federated unlearning has also been explored. VeriFi \[[6](http://arxiv.org/abs/2205.12709)\] introduces a framework that allows clients to verify the unlearning effect by marking and checking the global model's performance on specific fingerprints. This ensures that the unlearning process is transparent and verifiable, addressing a critical gap in existing methods. Additionally, the paper \[[26](http://arxiv.org/abs/2405.17462)\] presents Ferrari, a federated feature unlearning framework that minimizes feature sensitivity, enabling the removal of sensitive or biased features from the global model.

Efforts to ensure privacy during federated unlearning have also been made. Starfish \[[28](http://arxiv.org/abs/2404.09724)\] proposes a privacy-preserving federated unlearning scheme using Two-Party Computation (2PC) techniques to protect client data during the unlearning process. This method ensures that historical client data cannot be reconstructed from the uploaded information, mitigating information leakage risks.

Evaluation practices for federated unlearning remain a challenge. While some studies focus on single metrics \[[1](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/eadeffdec9fac8fd7f9aea732ca410eb082b7dcf)\], others advocate for multiple evaluation metrics to comprehensively assess unlearning effectiveness \[[30](http://arxiv.org/abs/2005.02205)\]. The paper \[[30](http://arxiv.org/abs/2005.02205)\] highlights that current evaluation practices may inadvertently introduce privacy risks, emphasizing the need for rigorous evaluation frameworks. This aligns with the findings of the current study, which proposes adapted and newly proposed evaluation metrics to address both under-unlearning and over-unlearning scenarios.
