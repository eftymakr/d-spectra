## Related Works

Neural retrieval methods using transformer-based pre-trained language models have significantly advanced multilingual and cross-lingual retrieval \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2004.12832)\]. However, their effectiveness for low-resource, morphologically rich languages such as Amharic remains underexplored due to data scarcity and suboptimal tokenization \[[23](http://arxiv.org/abs/2503.18570)\]. This gap is addressed by introducing Amharic-specific dense retrieval models based on pre-trained Amharic BERT and RoBERTa backbones \[[23](http://arxiv.org/abs/2503.18570)\]. These models demonstrate notable improvements over existing multilingual baselines, such as Arctic Embed 2.0 \[[23](http://arxiv.org/abs/2503.18570)\].

The use of pre-trained language models for cross-lingual retrieval has been extensively explored. For instance, ColBERT \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2004.12832)\] introduces a late interaction architecture that independently encodes queries and documents using BERT, then employs a cheap yet powerful interaction step to model fine-grained similarity. This approach enables efficient retrieval while maintaining high effectiveness \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2004.12832)\]. Similarly, ColBERT-X \[[4](http://arxiv.org/abs/2201.08471)\] extends this idea to support cross-language information retrieval by using the XLM-RoBERTa (XLM-R) encoder. These models have shown significant improvements over traditional lexical CLIR baselines \[[4](http://arxiv.org/abs/2201.08471)\].

Pre-training multilingual language models at scale has also led to significant performance gains for cross-lingual transfer tasks \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/1911.02116)\]. The XLM-R model, trained on one hundred languages using more than two terabytes of filtered CommonCrawl data, significantly outperforms multilingual BERT (mBERT) on a variety of cross-lingual benchmarks \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/1911.02116)\]. This is particularly important for low-resource languages, where performance improvements are critical \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/1911.02116)\].

The development of dense retrieval models for low-resource languages has also been a focus of recent research. For example, the study on Amharic dense retrieval models \[[23](http://arxiv.org/abs/2503.18570)\] constructs training datasets tailored to dense retrieval models and evaluates model performance by comparing dense and sparse retrieval approaches on Amharic information retrieval. This highlights the challenges and efforts involved in advancing retrieval systems for low-resource languages \[[23](http://arxiv.org/abs/2503.18570)\].

Additionally, the use of transfer learning approaches for building cross-language dense retrieval models has been explored \[[4](http://arxiv.org/abs/2201.08471)\]. The ColBERT-X model can be trained in two ways: zero-shot training, where the system is trained on the English MS MARCO collection, and translate-train, where the system is trained on the MS MARCO English queries coupled with machine translations of the associated MS MARCO passages. Results on ad hoc document ranking tasks in several languages demonstrate substantial improvements over traditional lexical CLIR baselines \[[4](http://arxiv.org/abs/2201.08471)\].
