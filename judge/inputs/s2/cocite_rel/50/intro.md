## Related Works

The rapid growth of open-source machine learning (ML) resources has significantly impacted information retrieval (IR) research, particularly in the areas of resource management and recommendation systems. Existing platforms, such as Hugging Face, have become central hubs for sharing and collaborating on ML models and datasets \[[15](http://arxiv.org/abs/2307.14841)\]. However, these platforms often lack structured representations that enable advanced queries and analyses, such as tracing model evolution and recommending relevant datasets \[[15](http://arxiv.org/abs/2307.14841)\]. This limitation has motivated recent efforts to enhance the capabilities of such platforms through the development of knowledge graphs and benchmarking frameworks.

Several studies have explored the characteristics and usage patterns of Hugging Face. For instance, \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2305.11164)\] conducted a repository mining study on Hugging Face Hub API, analyzing the measurement of carbon emissions for 1,417 ML models and associated datasets. The study found that while there is a slight decrease in reported carbon footprint over the past two years, the majority of models still lack comprehensive carbon emission reporting. This highlights a gap in transparency and sustainability practices within the ML community \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2305.11164)\].

Similarly, \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/2311.13380)\] examined the evolution and maintenance of ML models on Hugging Face, analyzing over 380,000 models. The study identified trends in ML domains, framework usage, and author grouping, as well as the evolution of tags and datasets. It also introduced a new classification system for model maintenance based on commit messages and other attributes \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/2311.13380)\].

In addition, \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/2303.02552)\] investigated the reuse of pre-trained models (PTMs) on Hugging Face, identifying challenges such as missing attributes, discrepancies between claimed and actual performance, and model risks. The study emphasized the need for improved documentation and standardization in the PTM ecosystem \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/2303.02552)\].

The importance of dataset documentation has also been highlighted in several studies. \[[4](http://arxiv.org/abs/2401.13822)\] conducted a large-scale analysis of dataset cards on Hugging Face, revealing that while dataset descriptions and structures are well-documented, sections like "Considerations for Using the Data" receive less attention. This suggests a need for more comprehensive and accessible documentation to improve reproducibility and transparency \[[4](http://arxiv.org/abs/2401.13822)\].

Moreover, \[[5](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/e9270f790459c84c3d16e2bb29bd1ab311ec5c78)\] assessed the vulnerabilities of the open-source AI landscape by analyzing Hugging Face models and their associated GitHub repositories. The study found a significant percentage of high-severity vulnerabilities, underscoring the importance of security in the AI supply chain \[[5](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/e9270f790459c84c3d16e2bb29bd1ab311ec5c78)\].

These studies collectively underscore the need for structured representations and improved documentation in ML platforms like Hugging Face. Our work, HuggingKG, addresses these gaps by constructing a large-scale knowledge graph
