## Related Works

The detection and segmentation of multiple sclerosis (MS) lesions, particularly paramagnetic rim lesions (PRLs), have been extensively studied in recent years. These lesions, characterized by a paramagnetic rim due to iron deposition, are associated with more severe disease progression and are increasingly recognized as a key biomarker in MS \[[4](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/bb5b80dc4165d0ac3e28d1882b898633e84779c2)\]. Automated methods for identifying these lesions have been proposed to overcome the limitations of manual segmentation, which is time-consuming and subject to inter- and intra-rater variability \[[2](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/71b5e8d9970f3b725127eb7da3d5183b921ad017)\]. 

Several deep learning approaches have been developed for MS lesion segmentation, with a focus on improving accuracy and robustness. For instance, a cascaded 3D convolutional neural network (CNN) approach has been introduced to improve automated segmentation of white matter (WM) lesions in MS patients. This method uses two CNNs in sequence, where the first network identifies potential lesion candidates and the second reduces misclassification \[[7](http://arxiv.org/abs/1702.04869)\]. Similarly, the RSANet, a recurrent slice-wise attention network, has been proposed to model 3D MRI images as sequences of slices, capturing long-range dependencies to improve segmentation performance \[[14](http://arxiv.org/abs/2002.12470)\]. 

Another significant advancement is the development of a fully automated technique for detecting PRLs using standard T1 and FLAIR sequences along with T2* phase images. This method leverages radiomic features and machine learning algorithms to classify lesions as PRLs or not, achieving an area under the curve (AUC) of 0.80 \[[3](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/09b2a6040c15e8672169531fc76bba0b5a04296b)\]. This approach is particularly important given the rarity of PRLs, which makes training models on imbalanced datasets a major challenge \[[4](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/bb5b80dc4165d0ac3e28d1882b898633e84779c2)\].

To address class imbalance, synthetic data generation has been proposed. In this work, synthetic quantitative susceptibility maps (QSMs) of PRLs are created to improve classifier performance and enable multi-channel extensions for generating accompanying contrasts and probabilistic segmentation maps \[[1](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/45bf918158d3f779cb41b982fa9237682c11dd96)\]. This approach is inspired by similar efforts in other medical imaging tasks, where synthetic data is used to augment training datasets and improve model generalization \[[20](http://arxiv.org/abs/2108.06883)\].

Furthermore, the use of uncertainty measures in deep learning models has been explored to enhance the reliability of lesion detection and segmentation. Monte Carlo dropout has been applied to estimate uncertainty in predictions, allowing for better clinical interpretation and refinement by clinicians \[[13](http://arxiv.org/abs/1808.01200)\]. This is especially important for small lesions, which are challenging to segment and often result in high false-positive rates \[[13](http://arxiv.org/abs/1808.01200)\].

The related works section focuses on the segmentation techniques for multiple sclerosis (MS) lesions, particularly in the context of automated methods and deep learning approaches. Automated segmentation of MS lesions has been extensively studied due to the challenges posed by lesion variability, anatomical differences, and the need for efficient and reproducible methods \[[1](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/45bf918158d3f779cb41b982fa9237682c11dd96)\]. Traditional methods often rely on manual segmentation, which is time-consuming and subject to inter- and intra-observer variability \[[2](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/71b5e8d9970f3b725127eb7da3d5183b921ad017)\]. To address these limitations, various automated techniques have been proposed, including atlas-based segmentation, which leverages topological and statistical atlases to improve accuracy and consistency \[[3](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/09b2a6040c15e8672169531fc76bba0b5a04296b)\]. Recent advancements have also incorporated deep learning models, such as recurrent slice-wise attention networks (RSANet), which model 3D MRI images as sequences of slices to capture long-range dependencies and contextual information \[[4](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/bb5b80dc4165d0ac3e28d1882b898633e84779c2)\]. These models have shown promising results in improving segmentation accuracy, particularly in handling the class imbalance between rim and non-rim lesions \[[5](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/691751bfa44017d8041e17409411f9e9ffc3dec6)\]. Additionally, synthetic data generation has been introduced to enhance classifier performance by addressing the scarcity of rare lesion types, such as paramagnetic rim lesions (PRLs) \[[6](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/e9df6d7fa6b380c5db0ad4f29b9c32219aca05fe)\]. The integration of generative adversarial networks (GANs) has further enabled the creation of synthetic quantitative susceptibility maps, which aid in improving detection and segmentation of PRLs \[[7](http://arxiv.org/abs/1702.04869)\]. Despite these advancements, challenges remain in achieving consistent performance across diverse MRI protocols and lesion characteristics, highlighting the need for more robust and standardized validation frameworks \[[8](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/8611112d0c105f752e239fe8729e652f8ee52ce3)\].

To overcome the computational burden of processing 3D medical scans, we have devised an efficient and effective dense training scheme which joins the processing of adjacent image patches into one pass through the network while automatically adapting to the inherent class imbalance present in the data. Further, we analyze the development of deeper, thus more discriminative 3D CNNs. In order to incorporate both local and larger contextual information, we employ a dual pathway architecture that processes the input images at multiple scales simultaneously. For post‐processing of the network's soft segmentation, we use a 3D fully connected Conditional Random Field which effectively removes false positives. Our pipeline is extensively evaluated on three challenging tasks of lesion segmentation in multi‐channel MRI patient data with traumatic brain injuries, brain tumours, and ischemic stroke. We improve on the state‐of‐the‐art for all three applications, with top ranking performance on the public benchmarks BRATS 2015 and ISLES 2015. Our method is computationally efficient, which allows its adoption in a variety of research and clinical settings. The source code of our implementation is made publicly available.»
