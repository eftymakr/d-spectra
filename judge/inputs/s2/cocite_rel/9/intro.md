## Related Works

The detection and segmentation of multiple sclerosis (MS) lesions, particularly paramagnetic rim lesions (PRLs), have been extensively studied in recent years. These lesions, characterized by a paramagnetic rim due to iron deposition, are associated with more severe disease progression and are increasingly recognized as a key biomarker in MS \[[4](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/bb5b80dc4165d0ac3e28d1882b898633e84779c2)\]. Automated methods for identifying these lesions have been proposed to overcome the limitations of manual segmentation, which is time-consuming and subject to inter- and intra-rater variability \[[2](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/71b5e8d9970f3b725127eb7da3d5183b921ad017)\]. 

Several deep learning approaches have been developed for MS lesion segmentation, with a focus on improving accuracy and robustness. For instance, a cascaded 3D convolutional neural network (CNN) approach has been introduced to improve automated segmentation of white matter (WM) lesions in MS patients. This method uses two CNNs in sequence, where the first network identifies potential lesion candidates and the second reduces misclassification \[[7](http://arxiv.org/abs/1702.04869)\]. Similarly, the RSANet, a recurrent slice-wise attention network, has been proposed to model 3D MRI images as sequences of slices, capturing long-range dependencies to improve segmentation performance \[[14](http://arxiv.org/abs/2002.12470)\]. 

Another significant advancement is the development of a fully automated technique for detecting PRLs using standard T1 and FLAIR sequences along with T2* phase images. This method leverages radiomic features and machine learning algorithms to classify lesions as PRLs or not, achieving an area under the curve (AUC) of 0.80 \[[3](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/09b2a6040c15e8672169531fc76bba0b5a04296b)\]. This approach is particularly important given the rarity of PRLs, which makes training models on imbalanced datasets a major challenge \[[4](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/bb5b80dc4165d0ac3e28d1882b898633e84779c2)\].

To address class imbalance, synthetic data generation has been proposed. In this work, synthetic quantitative susceptibility maps (QSMs) of PRLs are created to improve classifier performance and enable multi-channel extensions for generating accompanying contrasts and probabilistic segmentation maps \[[1](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/45bf918158d3f779cb41b982fa9237682c11dd96)\]. This approach is inspired by similar efforts in other medical imaging tasks, where synthetic data is used to augment training datasets and improve model generalization \[[20](http://arxiv.org/abs/2108.06883)\].

Furthermore, the use of uncertainty measures in deep learning models has been explored to enhance the reliability of lesion detection and segmentation. Monte Carlo dropout has been applied to estimate uncertainty in predictions, allowing for better clinical interpretation and refinement by clinicians \[[13](http://arxiv.org/abs/1808.01200)\]. This is especially important for small lesions, which are challenging to segment and often result in high false-positive rates \[[13](http://arxiv.org/abs/1808.01200)\].
