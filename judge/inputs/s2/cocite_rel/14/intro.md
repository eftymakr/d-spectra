## Related Works

Video super-resolution (VSR) has been a focal point in computer vision, particularly with the advent of deep learning techniques that enable high-quality video upscaling. Early approaches primarily relied on motion estimation and compensation to align frames and improve resolution \[[1](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/c6a8ff4d9dcdf9e5d82310b847af999ed6655f65)\]. However, these methods often suffered from computational inefficiency and limited temporal consistency \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/1801.04590)\]. Recent advances have shifted towards end-to-end deep learning frameworks that either eliminate the need for explicit motion compensation or integrate it more efficiently \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/1611.05250)\].

Several works have explored recurrent neural networks (RNNs) for VSR, leveraging their ability to model long-term temporal dependencies. For instance, the Bidirectional Recurrent Convolutional Network (BRCN) was introduced to handle multi-frame SR efficiently by replacing full feedforward and recurrent connections with weight-sharing convolutional connections, thus reducing parameters and improving performance \[[4](http://arxiv.org/abs/1909.08080)\]. Similarly, the Recurrent Back-Projection Network (RBPN) integrates spatial and temporal contexts through a recurrent encoder-decoder module, allowing for iterative refinement of frame information \[[5](http://arxiv.org/abs/1812.02898)\]. These methods demonstrate that recurrent architectures can effectively capture temporal dynamics while maintaining computational efficiency.

Another line of research focuses on the use of attention mechanisms to improve alignment and feature fusion. The Temporally-Deformable Alignment Network (TDAN) proposes a feature-level alignment strategy without computing optical flow, thereby reducing artifacts caused by inaccurate flow estimation \[[6](http://arxiv.org/abs/2007.10595)\]. The Video Restoration Transformer (VRT) introduces a parallel frame prediction ability using temporal reciprocal self attention (TRSA) and parallel warping, enabling cross-clip interactions and better feature fusion \[[7](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/a2c50f93f6d0892918e0ff653750d198ccab48d0)\]. These attention-based methods highlight the importance of aligning frames and extracting relevant features for high-quality VSR.

Efforts have also been made to address the computational burden of VSR. The Efficient Video Super-Resolution through Recurrent Latent Space Propagation (RLSP) algorithm introduces high-dimensional latent states to propagate temporal information implicitly, achieving significant speed improvements over previous methods \[[8](http://arxiv.org/abs/1707.02921)\]. The Real-Time Video Super-Resolution with Spatio-Temporal Networks and Motion Compensation method employs 3D convolutions and a fast multi-resolution spatial transformer module to improve efficiency while maintaining accuracy \[[9](http://arxiv.org/abs/2201.12288)\]. These methods demonstrate that efficient VSR can be achieved through better exploitation of temporal correlations and reduced computational overhead.
