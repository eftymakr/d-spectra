## Related Works

Multi-token prediction (MTP) has emerged as a promising alternative to traditional next-token prediction (NTP) in the training of large language models (LLMs). While NTP involves predicting one token at a time, MTP extends this by predicting multiple future tokens simultaneously, often using multiple independent output heads that operate on top of a shared model trunk \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2404.19737)\]. This approach has been shown to improve downstream performance, inference speed, and training efficiency, especially for large models \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2404.19737)\]. However, smaller language models (SLMs) have struggled with the MTP objective, necessitating the development of curriculum learning strategies to gradually adapt them to MTP \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2404.19737)\].

Curriculum learning has been widely used in machine learning to improve training efficiency and model performance. In the context of sequence prediction, scheduled sampling has been proposed to gradually shift the training process from fully guided prediction using true previous tokens to less guided prediction using generated tokens \[[7](http://arxiv.org/abs/1506.03099)\]. This approach helps mitigate error accumulation during inference. Similarly, in the context of MTP, a forward curriculum strategy has been introduced, which gradually increases the complexity of the pre-training objective from NTP to MTP \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2404.19737)\]. This strategy enables SLMs to better leverage MTP during pre-training, improving downstream NTP performance and generative output quality while retaining the benefits of self-speculative decoding \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2404.19737)\].

In contrast, a reverse curriculum strategy has also been explored, which starts with MTP and gradually reduces the number of tokens predicted \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2404.19737)\]. This approach achieves stronger NTP performance and output quality but fails to provide the self-speculative decoding benefits observed with the forward curriculum \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2404.19737)\]. The effectiveness of curriculum learning in MTP training highlights the importance of carefully designing the training process to accommodate the unique challenges of multi-token prediction.

Several prior works have investigated the implications of MTP on model behavior and performance. For example, the use of pause tokens during training and inference has been shown to improve the quality of generated responses by allowing the model to process more computation before committing to an answer \[[4](http://arxiv.org/abs/2310.02226)\]. Additionally, the introduction of the Medusa framework, which adds extra decoding heads to predict multiple subsequent tokens in parallel, has demonstrated significant speedups in inference while maintaining generation quality \[[6](http://arxiv.org/abs/2401.10774)\]. These approaches align with the broader trend of exploring parallel and speculative decoding techniques to enhance the efficiency of LLMs.
