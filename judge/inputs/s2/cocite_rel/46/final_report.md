## Related Works

Cross-domain sequential recommendation (CDSR) has gained significant attention in recent years due to its ability to alleviate data sparsity and cold-start problems in traditional sequential recommendation systems \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2106.02768)\]. Several approaches have been proposed to address these challenges, often leveraging self-attention mechanisms and domain-specific components to improve performance \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/2304.03891)\]. However, many existing frameworks primarily focus on adding domain-aware modules without fully exploiting the inherent power of the self-attention mechanism in transformers \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/2105.03300)\]. This section reviews related works that have contributed to the development of CDSR, highlighting their methodologies, limitations, and how they relate to our proposed approach.

Early work on cross-domain recommendation primarily relied on transfer learning techniques, such as matrix factorization and deep neural networks, to bridge the gap between domains \[[4](http://arxiv.org/abs/2311.04590)\]. These methods often assume overlapping users across domains and transfer latent representations from a source domain to a target domain \[[5](http://arxiv.org/abs/1808.09781)\]. However, they typically ignore the complex sequential dynamics of user behavior and fail to capture the interplay between intra- and inter-sequence item interactions \[[6](http://arxiv.org/abs/1804.06769)\]. More recent approaches have attempted to model sequential patterns by integrating self-attention mechanisms, such as SASRec and BERT4Rec, which are capable of capturing long-term dependencies in user behavior \[[7](http://arxiv.org/abs/2012.00485)\]. Despite their effectiveness, these models are often limited by their unidirectional nature and inability to handle the sparsity and cold-start problems in cross-domain settings \[[8](http://arxiv.org/abs/2311.04760)\].

To address these limitations, several studies have proposed hybrid models that combine self-attention with domain-specific components. For instance, DASL introduces a dual learning mechanism to simultaneously transfer information between two domains, improving cross-domain CTR predictions by leveraging bidirectional latent relations \[[9](http://arxiv.org/abs/1904.06690)\]. Similarly, CoNet employs a deep transfer learning approach using neural networks to capture complex user-item interactions across domains \[[10](http://arxiv.org/abs/2103.01696)\]. However, these models often rely on overlapping users and assume a closed-world setting, which may not hold in real-world scenarios where user behavior is sparse and non-overlapping \[[11](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/fa990aee9a8f157b5d393f5f3eaa014e1e5c67aa)\].

Another line of research focuses on modeling both intra- and inter-sequence item relationships to enhance cross-domain user preference learning \[[12](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/6dd0605f7de7581c69b4acf0ab78000748a09f3e)\]. ### <a id='category-self-attention-mechanisms-in-recommender-systems'></a>Self-Attention Mechanisms in Recommender Systems: 3 papers found.
Sequential recommendation systems aim to predict user actions based on their interaction history, with recent approaches leveraging self-attention mechanisms to capture complex sequential patterns \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2106.02768)\]. Traditional methods such as Markov Chains (MCs) and Recurrent Neural Networks (RNNs) have been widely used, but they face limitations in modeling long-term dependencies and capturing contextual relevance \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2106.02768)\]. To address these challenges, self-attention based models like SASRec and BERT4Rec have emerged, offering more flexible and powerful representations by allowing the model to focus on relevant items in the history \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2106.02768)\]\[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/2304.03891)\]. SASRec introduces a self-attention mechanism that balances model efficiency with the ability to capture long-term semantics, making it effective across both sparse and dense datasets \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2106.02768)\]. Similarly, BERT4Rec employs a bidirectional self-attention architecture, enabling the model to consider both left and right contexts of items in the sequence, thereby improving the richness of the hidden representations \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/2304.03891)\]. However, these models often assume that interaction sequences are strictly ordered without considering the time intervals between actions, which can affect the accuracy of predictions \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/2105.03300)\]. To further enhance sequential recommendation, TiSASRec was proposed to explicitly model time intervals between interactions, improving the model's ability to adapt to varying temporal dynamics \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/2105.03300)\]. These developments highlight the growing importance of self-attention mechanisms in sequential recommendation, particularly in handling cross-domain scenarios where the ability to learn correlations across different domains is crucial \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2106.02768)\]\[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/2304.03891)\]\[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/2105.03300)\].
