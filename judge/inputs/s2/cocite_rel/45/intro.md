## Related Works

Fine-grained emotion classification in text has been a growing area of research, particularly in the context of social media and user-generated content. While sentiment analysis has been extensively studied, with datasets such as those used in SemEval tasks \[[9](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/66c8ab0b3b7698cf5333c3f7350a6eaa5302294b)\], the detection of discrete emotions remains less explored. This is especially true for domains like app reviews, where emotional nuances are often overlooked \[[16](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/3fb416be9e59b35c7cc91cd11b5b3f3f5491f13a)\]. Several studies have attempted to bridge this gap by developing annotated datasets and exploring machine learning approaches for emotion detection.

The SemEval-2016 dataset was extended with emotion annotations to support more detailed analysis \[[1](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/7d0531aaaa4dbf4033da964289843e7812262702)\]. This work also explored the relationship between emotion, stance, and sentiment, laying the groundwork for future research. Similarly, the GoEmotions dataset \[[7](http://arxiv.org/abs/2005.00547)\] provides a large-scale, manually annotated corpus of Reddit comments, covering 27 emotion categories, which has been used to evaluate models for emotion classification. While these datasets are valuable, they often focus on general text rather than app reviews, which have unique linguistic and emotional characteristics.

Efforts to automate emotion annotation have also been explored. For instance, the use of large language models (LLMs) in sentiment and emotion detection has been investigated, revealing both potential and limitations \[[11](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/8eb9a8d756e93530eb35e9f0e26a2a0190c1dd7c)\]. These studies highlight that while LLMs can provide reasonable performance, they often exhibit biases related to label classes, prompt templates, and word forms. This is particularly relevant when applying such models to app reviews, where the language is more informal and context-dependent.

Several frameworks have been proposed to improve the analysis of user reviews. AR-Miner \[[3](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/ff921a209cc9e1c2120669d3d81e627ca5e53572)\] introduces a computational framework for mining informative app reviews, which includes filtering, grouping, and prioritizing reviews based on their content. This work is crucial for developers aiming to understand user sentiment and identify areas for improvement. However, it does not directly address emotion classification, which is a more complex task.

The development of emotion detection models has also been supported by large-scale datasets. EmoNet \[[5](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/6a6f1b8ef3eefaf8027a76134859b44886a5f795)\] introduces a dataset with 24 fine-grained emotion types and achieves state-of-the-art results using gated recurrent neural networks. EmoBank \[[6](http://arxiv.org/abs/2205.01996)\] further explores dimensional emotion analysis using the VAD representation, distinguishing between writer’s and reader’s emotions. These datasets and models are foundational for understanding emotional content in text, but they often lack the specificity required for app reviews.
