## Related Works

Deep learning has significantly impacted the field of inverse problems in imaging, with two prominent approaches being deep-unrolling and plug-and-play (PnP) methods \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/1704.03488)\]. PnP approaches, which integrate off-the-shelf deep denoisers into iterative algorithms, offer flexibility in handling varying compression ratios (CRs) but often suffer from limitations in reconstruction accuracy and speed \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/1704.03488)\]. These methods rely on the assumption that the denoiser can implicitly regularize the solution, leveraging the power of deep learning for image restoration \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/1704.03488)\]. However, the performance of PnP methods can be sensitive to the choice of denoiser and the specific problem at hand \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/1704.03488)\].

On the other hand, unrolling approaches transform a truncated iterative optimization process into an end-to-end trainable neural network \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/1704.03488)\]. These networks typically achieve better accuracy and faster inference compared to PnP methods, but they often require fine-tuning or retraining when the CR changes, which limits their adaptability \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/1704.03488)\]. This challenge has motivated the development of more flexible and efficient architectures that can handle varying CRs without retraining \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/1704.03488)\].

Recent work has explored the integration of both PnP and unrolling approaches to leverage their respective strengths \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/1704.03488)\]. For instance, the work in \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/1902.05399)\] proposes an algorithm unrolling approach to deep image deblurring, where an iterative algorithm is unrolled into a neural network to achieve interpretability and performance gains \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/1902.05399)\]. Similarly, \[[3](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/b954a94af132294a4a4e2efce15027758a535742)\] introduces a neural network architecture based on algorithm unrolling, which enables efficient and interpretable blind image deblurring \[[3](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/b954a94af132294a4a4e2efce15027758a535742)\]. These methods demonstrate that unrolling can provide a balance between flexibility and performance, especially when combined with well-designed denoisers \[[3](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/b954a94af132294a4a4e2efce15027758a535742)\].

Another line of research focuses on the use of denoising priors within model-based optimization frameworks \[[4](http://arxiv.org/abs/1611.03679)\]. For example, \[[5](http://arxiv.org/abs/1704.03264)\] discusses the integration of fast and effective CNN denoisers into model-based optimization methods to solve various inverse problems, including image restoration and deblurring \[[5](http://arxiv.org/abs/1704.03264)\]. This approach allows for the use of pre-trained denoisers, reducing the need for extensive training while maintaining performance \[[5](http://arxiv.org/abs/1704.03264)\].

Additionally, the work in \[[6](http://arxiv.org/abs/1801.06756)\] introduces a denoising-based image restoration algorithm that can be unfolded into a deep neural network, combining the benefits of iterative methods with the efficiency of deep learning \[[6](http://arxiv.org/abs/1801.06756)\]. This method leverages the power of denoisers to implicitly enforce regularization, leading to improved performance in tasks such as image denoising and deblurring \[[6](http://arxiv.org/abs/1801.06756)\].

To address this issue, we present Dynamic Convolution, a new design that increases model complexity without increasing the network depth or width. Instead of using a single convolution kernel per layer, dynamic convolution aggregates multiple parallel convolution kernels dynamically based upon their attentions, which are input dependent. Assembling multiple kernels is not only computationally efficient due to the small kernel size, but also has more representation power since these kernels are aggregated in a non-linear way via attention.
