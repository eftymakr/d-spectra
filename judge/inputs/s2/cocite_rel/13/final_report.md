## Related Works

Long-tailed recognition remains a challenging problem in visual recognition due to the skewed class distribution in real-world data \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/1910.09217)\]. Many prior works have focused on addressing this issue through class re-balancing strategies, such as loss re-weighting, data re-sampling, or transfer learning from head to tail classes \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/1910.09217)\]. However, these methods often follow a joint learning scheme of representation and classification, which may not be optimal for long-tailed scenarios \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/1910.09217)\]. Recent studies have explored the decoupling of representation and classifier learning, showing that it can lead to better performance \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/1910.09217)\]. For example, the work by \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/1910.09217)\] found that data imbalance might not be an issue in learning high-quality representations, and that a simple instance-balanced sampling approach can achieve strong long-tailed recognition ability by adjusting only the classifier.

Contrastive learning has also been explored for long-tailed recognition. Supervised contrastive learning (SCL) has shown promising performance on balanced data, but it tends to bias towards high-frequency classes, increasing the difficulty of imbalanced learning \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/2107.12028)\]. To address this, \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/2107.12028)\] proposed Parametric Contrastive Learning (PaCo), which introduces class-wise learnable centers to rebalance the optimization process. This approach adaptsively enhances the intensity of pushing samples of the same class close together and benefits hard example learning. Experiments on long-tailed datasets like CIFAR, ImageNet, and iNaturalist showed that PaCo outperforms standard SCL.

Another approach, Balanced Contrastive Learning (BCL), was proposed to address the failure of SCL to form a regular simplex in long-tailed data \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/2207.09052)\]. BCL introduces two improvements: class-averaging, which balances the gradient contribution of negative classes, and class-complement, which ensures all classes appear in every mini-batch. These changes help the model form a regular simplex and improve cross-entropy optimization. BCL was tested on datasets such as CIFAR-10-LT and ImageNet-LT, showing competitive performance.

Targeted Supervised Contrastive Learning (TSC) was introduced to improve the uniformity of feature distributions in the hypersphere \[[4](http://arxiv.org/abs/2111.13998)\]. TSC generates targets uniformly distributed on a hypersphere and forces features of different classes to converge to these targets during training. This improves class boundaries and generalization on long-tailed data. Experiments on multiple datasets demonstrated that TSC achieves state-of-the-art performance.
