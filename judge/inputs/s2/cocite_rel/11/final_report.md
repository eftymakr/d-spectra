## Related Works

3D human pose estimation has been extensively studied, with a variety of approaches addressing the challenges of depth ambiguity, self-occlusion, and temporal consistency. Early methods relied on 2D pose estimation followed by 3D lifting, where 2D joint locations are mapped to 3D space \[[8](http://arxiv.org/abs/1705.03098)\]. This approach has been refined by incorporating temporal information to improve consistency across frames \[[7](http://arxiv.org/abs/1711.08585)\]. For instance, sequence-to-sequence networks with LSTM units have been used to enforce temporal smoothness, leading to more coherent 3D pose sequences \[[7](http://arxiv.org/abs/1711.08585)\]. However, these methods often suffer from limited receptive fields and computational inefficiencies.

Recent advances have leveraged graph-based models to better capture spatial and temporal dependencies. Graph Convolutional Networks (GCNs) have been used to model human skeletons, learning both local and global features \[[5](http://arxiv.org/abs/2103.16385)\]. These models incorporate domain knowledge about human body configurations, enabling more accurate 3D pose estimation \[[2](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/50167a9e6bdc1f64a58fe8a3c990105fec0bd2bc)\]. Additionally, attention mechanisms have been introduced to exploit temporal contexts, allowing models to adaptively identify significant frames and tensor outputs \[[3](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/30de7fecb146356d7ae6cafa072997674601e0f5)\]. This has led to improved performance on benchmark datasets such as Human3.6M and MPI-INF-3DHP \[[3](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/30de7fecb146356d7ae6cafa072997674601e0f5)\].

Another line of research focuses on improving generalization, especially for rare or unseen poses. The Split-and-Recombine (SRNet) approach addresses this by dividing the body into local regions and processing them separately, while maintaining global coherence \[[4](http://arxiv.org/abs/2007.09389)\]. This method has shown significant improvements in predicting rare poses without sacrificing performance on common ones. Similarly, the use of ordinal depth supervision has been proposed to reduce the dependency on precise 3D annotations, allowing for training with weaker supervision signals \[[20](http://arxiv.org/abs/1805.04095)\].

Temporal modeling has also seen significant improvements with the introduction of Transformer-based architectures. PoseFormer, a purely transformer-based approach, has been shown to outperform traditional convolutional models by effectively modeling spatial and temporal dependencies \[[6](http://arxiv.org/abs/2103.10455)\]. To alleviate the need for accurate 3D ground truth, we propose to use a weaker supervision signal provided by the ordinal depths of human joints. Simultaneously, these ordinal annotations can be easily incorporated in the training procedure of typical ConvNets for 3D human pose.
