## Related Works

Federated Learning (FL) has emerged as a promising paradigm for training machine learning models on decentralized data while preserving privacy \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/1912.04977)\]. Early works such as \[[11](http://arxiv.org/abs/1602.05629)\] introduced the concept of federated learning, emphasizing the challenges of non-IID data and communication efficiency. This framework enables multiple clients to collaboratively train a model without sharing raw data, which is particularly useful in settings like mobile devices and IoT systems \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/1912.04977)\]. However, existing FL approaches often assume homogeneous data distributions and fail to account for the heterogeneity across clients, which can significantly degrade model accuracy \[[14](http://arxiv.org/abs/1806.00582)\].

To address the issue of non-IID data, several works have proposed modifications to the FL framework. For instance, \[[14](http://arxiv.org/abs/1806.00582)\] showed that accuracy can drop by up to 55% in highly skewed non-IID settings and proposed strategies to improve training by sharing a small subset of data globally. Similarly, \[[15](http://arxiv.org/abs/1909.06335)\] explored the impact of non-identical data distributions on visual classification and introduced server momentum as a mitigation strategy. These studies highlight the importance of addressing data heterogeneity in FL.

Recent advances in FL have also focused on improving communication efficiency. \[[4](http://arxiv.org/abs/1610.05492)\] introduced structured and sketched updates to reduce the uplink communication costs, achieving a reduction in communication cost by two orders of magnitude. \[[13](http://arxiv.org/abs/1812.06127)\] proposed FedProx, a framework that generalizes FedAvg to handle both statistical and system heterogeneity, demonstrating improved convergence behavior in highly heterogeneous settings. These methods have been instrumental in making FL more practical for real-world applications.

Personalization in FL has also been a focus of recent research. \[[8](http://arxiv.org/abs/2002.07948)\] introduced a meta-learning approach to enable personalized federated learning, allowing users to adapt a shared model to their local data. \[[20](http://arxiv.org/abs/2102.07078)\] further extended this idea by proposing a framework that learns a shared representation across clients and unique local heads for each client, improving performance in heterogeneous settings. These approaches demonstrate the potential of FL to provide both global and personalized models.

Client selection in FL has also been explored to improve training efficiency and model accuracy. \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/1804.08333)\] introduced FedCS, a protocol that actively manages clients based on their resource conditions, leading to faster convergence. \[[17](http://arxiv.org/abs/2105.05883)\] proposed clustered sampling, which improves client representativeness and reduces variance in stochastic aggregation weights. These methods highlight the importance of optimizing client selection in FL to achieve better performance.
