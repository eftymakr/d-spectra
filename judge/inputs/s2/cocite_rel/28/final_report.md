## Related Works

Federated Learning (FL) has emerged as a promising paradigm for training machine learning models on decentralized data while preserving privacy \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/1912.04977)\]. Early works such as \[[11](http://arxiv.org/abs/1602.05629)\] introduced the concept of federated learning, emphasizing the challenges of non-IID data and communication efficiency. This framework enables multiple clients to collaboratively train a model without sharing raw data, which is particularly useful in settings like mobile devices and IoT systems \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/1912.04977)\]. However, existing FL approaches often assume homogeneous data distributions and fail to account for the heterogeneity across clients, which can significantly degrade model accuracy \[[14](http://arxiv.org/abs/1806.00582)\].

To address the issue of non-IID data, several works have proposed modifications to the FL framework. For instance, \[[14](http://arxiv.org/abs/1806.00582)\] showed that accuracy can drop by up to 55% in highly skewed non-IID settings and proposed strategies to improve training by sharing a small subset of data globally. Similarly, \[[15](http://arxiv.org/abs/1909.06335)\] explored the impact of non-identical data distributions on visual classification and introduced server momentum as a mitigation strategy. These studies highlight the importance of addressing data heterogeneity in FL.

Recent advances in FL have also focused on improving communication efficiency. \[[4](http://arxiv.org/abs/1610.05492)\] introduced structured and sketched updates to reduce the uplink communication costs, achieving a reduction in communication cost by two orders of magnitude. \[[13](http://arxiv.org/abs/1812.06127)\] proposed FedProx, a framework that generalizes FedAvg to handle both statistical and system heterogeneity, demonstrating improved convergence behavior in highly heterogeneous settings. These methods have been instrumental in making FL more practical for real-world applications.

These approaches demonstrate the potential of FL to provide both global and personalized models.

### <a id='category-federated-learning-frameworks'></a>Federated Learning Frameworks: 9 papers found.
Federated learning (FL) has emerged as a critical paradigm for training machine learning models on decentralized data while preserving privacy \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/1804.08333)\]. It enables multiple clients, such as mobile devices or organizations, to collaboratively train a shared model under the orchestration of a central server without sharing raw data \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/1912.04977)\]. However, FL faces significant challenges due to the heterogeneity of client data distributions and system capabilities \[[8](http://arxiv.org/abs/2002.07948)\]. For instance, standard federated optimization methods like FedAvg often struggle with convergence and performance in non-convex settings when data is non-IID \[[5](http://arxiv.org/abs/2003.00295)\]. To address these issues, researchers have proposed adaptive optimization techniques such as federated versions of Adagrad, Adam, and Yogi, which improve convergence and communication efficiency by adapting to client heterogeneity \[[5](http://arxiv.org/abs/2003.00295)\]. Additionally, frameworks like LEAF provide modular benchmarks and evaluation tools to better capture the complexities of practical federated environments \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/1812.01097)\]. Furthermore, recent work has explored clustered FL (CFL), which leverages geometric properties of the loss surface to group clients with similar data distributions, enhancing model performance without requiring changes to the communication protocol \[[6](http://arxiv.org/abs/1910.01991)\]. Similarly, HeteroFL addresses heterogeneous clients with varying computational and communication capabilities by enabling the training of diverse local models while producing a unified global inference model \[[18](http://arxiv.org/abs/2010.01264)\]. These advancements highlight the ongoing efforts to improve the robustness, efficiency, and scalability of federated learning in real-world scenarios.

### <a id='category-client-selection-strategies'></a>Client Selection Strategies: 5 papers found.
Client selection strategies in federated learning (FL) have been extensively studied to improve training efficiency and model performance. Existing approaches often rely on uniform or random sampling, which can lead to slow convergence due to system and statistical heterogeneity \[[10](http://arxiv.org/abs/2112.11256)\]. To address this, several works have proposed adaptive client selection methods. For instance, FedCS introduces a client selection mechanism that actively manages clients based on their resource conditions, allowing the server to aggregate more updates and accelerate model improvement \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/1804.08333)\]. Similarly, CSFedAvg identifies clients with lower degrees of non-IID data and prioritizes their participation to enhance training accuracy \[[7](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/514b57ac2810be1242395b15d5e57635c93635de)\]. Clustered sampling has also been introduced to improve client representativeness and reduce variance in stochastic aggregation weights by grouping clients based on sample size or model similarity \[[17](http://arxiv.org/abs/2105.05883)\]. These strategies collectively aim to optimize the FL process by addressing challenges such as non-IID data, heterogeneous resources, and communication constraints. Further, they demonstrate that adaptive and biased sampling can significantly reduce convergence time and improve model performance compared to traditional methods \[[10](http://arxiv.org/abs/2112.11256)\].

Recent work has focused on developing personalized variants of federated learning, where each user can adapt a shared model to their local dataset with minimal computational overhead \[[8](http://arxiv.org/abs/2002.07948)\]. This approach retains the benefits of federated learning, such as data privacy and distributed computation, while enabling more tailored model performance \[[8](http://arxiv.org/abs/2002.07948)\]. These methods leverage distributed computational power to perform local updates on low-dimensional parameters, improving convergence and reducing dimensionality \[[20](http://arxiv.org/abs/2102.07078)\]. The effectiveness of these personalized approaches is influenced by the similarity of user data distributions, with performance degrading as distribution distances increase \[[8](http://arxiv.org/abs/2002.07948)\]. Experimental evaluations have demonstrated that these methods outperform traditional federated learning and other personalized variants in heterogeneous settings \[[8](http://arxiv.org/abs/2002.07948)\]\[[20](http://arxiv.org/abs/2102.07078)\].
