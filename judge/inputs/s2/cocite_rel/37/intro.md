## Related Works

Vision Transformers (ViTs) have emerged as a powerful alternative to convolutional neural networks (CNNs) in computer vision tasks, leveraging the self-attention mechanism to capture global context effectively \[[4](http://arxiv.org/abs/2010.11929)\]. However, the deployment of ViTs on hardware, especially FPGAs, faces significant challenges due to the complexity of arithmetic operations such as Softmax and LayerNorm, which are sensitive to model accuracy and difficult to map onto low-precision hardware \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2208.05163)\]. Existing methods often exploit parallelism in matrix multiplication operations but leave these complex operations on the CPU, resulting in suboptimal performance due to communication overhead between the CPU and accelerator \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2208.05163)\].

Several works have explored hardware acceleration for ViTs. Auto-ViT-Acc proposes an FPGA-aware acceleration framework with mixed-scheme quantization, achieving higher accuracy than state-of-the-art quantization methods while improving frame rates \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2208.05163)\]. VAQF introduces a fully automatic software-hardware co-design framework for low-bit ViTs, enabling real-time execution on FPGAs with binary weights and low-precision activations \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/2201.06618)\]. Both of these works highlight the importance of quantization and hardware-specific optimizations for efficient ViT deployment.

Efforts to improve the efficiency of ViTs have also focused on architectural modifications. LeViT integrates principles from CNNs into ViTs to achieve faster inference while maintaining accuracy \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/2104.01136)\]. CvT introduces convolutions into ViTs to leverage the benefits of both CNNs and transformers, achieving state-of-the-art performance with fewer parameters and lower FLOPs \[[6](http://arxiv.org/abs/2103.15808)\]. CrossViT proposes a dual-branch transformer to combine small- and large-patch tokens for improved feature representation \[[7](http://arxiv.org/abs/2103.14899)\]. These architectural innovations demonstrate the potential for hybrid models to balance efficiency and performance.

Quantization has also been explored as a means to reduce computational overhead. Q-ViT develops a fully quantized ViT with an information rectification module and distribution-guided distillation to maintain accuracy \[[13](http://arxiv.org/abs/2210.06707)\]. PackQViT introduces a sub-8-bit quantization-aware training framework with activation-aware quantization and integer-only computation flow, achieving significant speedups on mobile devices \[[17](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/5fd1b67482e4b9fe70b480c0fd0467900d6db436)\]. These works highlight the importance of quantization strategies and hardware-aware design in enabling efficient ViT deployment.
