## Related Works

Rehearsal-based methods have emerged as a prominent approach to address catastrophic forgetting in continual learning (CL) by storing and training on a subset of past data alongside new data in current tasks \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/1612.00796)\]. These methods aim to mitigate the degradation of performance on previously learned tasks, a phenomenon well-documented in neural networks \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/1811.11682)\]. While concurrent rehearsal, where past and new data are trained together, is widely used, recent work has questioned whether this approach is always optimal \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/1705.08690)\]. Inspired by human learning, where sequentially revisiting tasks helps mitigate forgetting, researchers have explored whether sequential rehearsal can offer greater benefits for CL compared to standard concurrent rehearsal \[[4](http://arxiv.org/abs/1801.01423)\].

Several studies have investigated the effectiveness of different rehearsal strategies. For instance, \[[5](http://arxiv.org/abs/2012.12631)\] proposed a method that leverages experience replay buffers to store past events, allowing models to learn new tasks while retaining knowledge of previous ones. This approach has been shown to reduce catastrophic forgetting in both Atari and DMLab domains \[[6](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/90e04f3ae23ca7df5f59b11453341e3db943b6f4)\]. Similarly, \[[7](http://arxiv.org/abs/1606.09282)\] introduced a framework called Deep Generative Replay, which uses a generative model to sample and interleave data from previous tasks with new tasks, enabling efficient sequential learning \[[8](http://arxiv.org/abs/1903.04476)\]. These methods highlight the potential of replay-based strategies in mitigating forgetting, though they often require significant memory or computational resources \[[9](http://arxiv.org/abs/1904.07734)\].

The idea of sequential rehearsal has also been explored in the context of reinforcement learning, where agents are exposed to tasks in a sequence. \[[10](http://arxiv.org/abs/1902.10486)\] demonstrated that using experience replay buffers with a mixture of on- and off-policy learning can substantially reduce catastrophic forgetting, even matching the performance of methods that require task identities \[[11](http://arxiv.org/abs/1908.04742)\]. Additionally, \[[12](http://arxiv.org/abs/1802.10269)\] proposed a method that selectively stores experiences in a long-term memory, showing that distribution matching can prevent catastrophic forgetting and is consistently the best approach on all domains tested \[[13](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/7bfb4ef17eabec1acd266958bdb08622eebfbb05)\].

More recently, \[[14](http://arxiv.org/abs/1706.08840)\] introduced a hybrid approach that combines concurrent and sequential rehearsal, training similar tasks concurrently and revisiting dissimilar tasks sequentially. This method was shown to outperform standard concurrent rehearsal in deep neural networks \[[15](http://arxiv.org/abs/1809.10635)\]. The theoretical analysis of rehearsal-based CL in overparameterized linear models further supports the effectiveness of sequential rehearsal when tasks are less similar \[[16](http://arxiv.org/abs/1905.12588)\]. These findings suggest that the choice of rehearsal strategy significantly impacts the performance of CL systems, and that sequential rehearsal may offer advantages in certain scenarios \[[17](http://arxiv.org/abs/1910.06562)\].
