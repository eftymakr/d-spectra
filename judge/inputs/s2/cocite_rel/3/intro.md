## Related Works

Recent advancements in Text-to-Speech (TTS) systems have significantly enhanced speech naturalness, leading to increased demand for precise prosody control and mispronunciation correction \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/1909.10302)\]. Traditional approaches for prosody manipulation often rely on specialized modules or additional training, which limits their capacity for post-hoc adjustments \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/1909.10302)\]. Similarly, conventional mispronunciation correction methods depend on grapheme-to-phoneme dictionaries, making them less practical in low-resource settings \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/1909.10302)\]. This section reviews related works that address these challenges.

Several studies have explored methods for prosody control in TTS systems. For example, \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/2011.06465)\] introduces a hierarchical prosody modeling approach for non-autoregressive TTS, enabling the prediction of phoneme-level prosody features conditioned on word-level features. This approach improves audio quality and prosody naturalness \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/2011.06465)\]. Similarly, \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/2110.02952)\] proposes a non-autoregressive parallel neural TTS front-end model hierarchically conditioned on both coarse and fine-grained acoustic speech features, allowing for effective control of each prosodic dimension \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/2110.02952)\]. These methods demonstrate the potential of hierarchical modeling for prosody control.

In addition to prosody control, several works have focused on improving the quality and expressiveness of synthesized speech. \[[4](http://arxiv.org/abs/2009.06775)\] introduces a sequence-to-sequence neural network conditioned on acoustic speech features to learn a latent prosody space with intuitive and meaningful dimensions, enabling the generation of a wide variety of speaking styles \[[4](http://arxiv.org/abs/2009.06775)\]. \[[5](http://arxiv.org/abs/2006.04558)\] presents FastSpeech 2, which addresses the limitations of FastSpeech by directly training the model with ground-truth targets and introducing more variation information as conditional inputs, resulting in improved voice quality and faster inference speed \[[5](http://arxiv.org/abs/2006.04558)\].

Another line of research has focused on controllable TTS systems that allow for fine-grained prosody transfer. \[[6](http://arxiv.org/abs/2206.13443)\] presents CopyCat2, a model capable of synthesizing speech with different speaker identities and transferring prosody at a fine-grained level between any pair of seen speakers \[[6](http://arxiv.org/abs/2206.13443)\]. This work demonstrates the feasibility of multi-speaker TTS with contextually appropriate prosody. Similarly, \[[7](http://arxiv.org/abs/2010.04301)\] introduces Non-Attentive Tacotron, which replaces the attention mechanism with an explicit duration predictor, enabling both utterance-wide and per-phoneme control of duration \[[7](http://arxiv.org/abs/2010.04301)\].
