## Related Works

Sequential recommendation systems aim to predict the next item a user will interact with, based on their historical behavior. These systems have evolved significantly, incorporating advanced techniques such as recurrent neural networks (RNNs), self-attention mechanisms, and state-space models to better capture user preferences and sequential patterns \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/1808.09781)\]. Early approaches, such as Markov Chains, assumed that the next action depends only on the last few actions, while RNNs introduced the ability to model longer-term dependencies \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/1808.09781)\]. However, RNNs suffer from high computational complexity and are often limited in their ability to capture diverse interaction patterns \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/1809.07426)\].

Self-attention mechanisms, such as those used in SASRec and BERT4Rec, have been introduced to address these limitations by allowing models to dynamically weigh the importance of different items in a user’s history \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/1808.09781)\]\[[4](http://arxiv.org/abs/1904.06690)\]. SASRec, for instance, uses self-attention to identify relevant items from a user’s action history and predict the next item, outperforming traditional RNN-based models \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/1808.09781)\]. Similarly, BERT4Rec employs a bidirectional self-attention mechanism to model user behavior sequences, allowing each item to be influenced by both left and right context \[[4](http://arxiv.org/abs/1904.06690)\]. This bidirectional approach improves the model’s ability to capture complex sequential dependencies \[[4](http://arxiv.org/abs/1904.06690)\].

Despite these advancements, many models still struggle with computational efficiency, especially when dealing with long sequences \[[13](http://arxiv.org/abs/2403.03900)\]. To address this, state-space models (SSMs) such as S4 and Mamba have been proposed, offering linear complexity and efficient handling of long-range dependencies \[[11](http://arxiv.org/abs/2111.00396)\]\[[13](http://arxiv.org/abs/2403.03900)\]. Mamba, in particular, has shown strong performance in modeling long sequences, achieving state-of-the-art results on various tasks while maintaining linear scalability \[[13](http://arxiv.org/abs/2403.03900)\]. These models are particularly useful in scenarios where user interactions are numerous and sequences are long, such as in short video platforms \[[10](http://arxiv.org/abs/2409.01192)\].

Another challenge in sequential recommendation is the issue of data sparsity and the need to capture diverse user behaviors \[[18](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/a8f2764a76772ab957b31f5d7bb88527d46fbdc8)\]. Contrastive learning methods, such as CL4SRec, have been proposed to improve representation learning by leveraging self-supervision signals from user behavior sequences \[[18](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/a8f2764a76772ab957b31f5d7bb88527d46fbdc8)\]. Additionally, methods like DuoRec and DCRec have been developed to address representation degeneration and popularity bias, respectively \[[12](http://arxiv.org/abs/2110.05730)\]\[[8](http://arxiv.org/abs/2303.11780)\]. These approaches aim to improve the quality of user and item embeddings by incorporating additional constraints and regularization techniques \[[12](http://arxiv.org/abs/2110.05730)\]\[[8](http://arxiv.org/abs/2303.11780)\].

In summary, while significant progress has been made in sequential recommendation, challenges remain in efficiently modeling long sequences

### <a id='category-attention-mechanisms-in-recommendations'></a>Attention Mechanisms in Recommendations: 4 papers found.
Sequential recommendation models have evolved to better capture the dynamic nature of user interactions, with recent approaches leveraging attention mechanisms to address limitations in traditional methods like Markov Chains (MCs) and Recurrent Neural Networks (RNNs) \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/1808.09781)\]. While MCs assume predictions based on recent actions and are effective in sparse datasets, RNNs offer greater flexibility for dense data but often face computational challenges \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/1808.09781)\]. To balance these trade-offs, self-attention based models such as SASRec have been introduced, allowing for efficient modeling of long-term semantics while focusing on relevant actions \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/1808.09781)\]. Similarly, LinRec improves efficiency in Transformer-based models by introducing linear complexity through L2-normalized linear attention, maintaining performance comparable to traditional dot-product attention \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/2411.01537)\]. Furthermore, models like TiSASRec explicitly incorporate time intervals between interactions, enhancing the modeling of temporal dynamics \[[5](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/9c7a455b1e48d01a99e58e884a6c1acb75074ad0)\]. These advancements highlight the growing trend of integrating attention mechanisms with temporal and structural considerations to improve the accuracy and efficiency of sequential recommendation systems.

### <a id='category-model-architecture-innovations'></a>Model Architecture Innovations: 10 papers found.
Recent advancements in sequential recommendation have focused on addressing challenges such as modeling dynamic user preferences, capturing diverse interaction patterns, and efficiently handling variable-length sequences \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/1808.09781)\]. Several approaches have been proposed to improve the effectiveness of sequential recommendation models. For instance, Caser employs convolutional filters to learn local features from sequences, enabling the capture of both general preferences and sequential patterns \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/1809.07426)\]. Similarly, BERT4Rec introduces a bidirectional self-attention mechanism, allowing the model to consider both left and right contexts of items in a sequence, thereby improving the representation of user behavior \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/2411.01537)\]. The Mamba model, based on structured state space models (SSMs), offers fast inference and linear scaling with sequence length, demonstrating strong performance across various modalities \[[4](http://arxiv.org/abs/1904.06690)\]. Additionally, SURGE integrates graph neural networks to address noisy and dynamic user preferences by constructing interest graphs and performing cluster-aware propagation \[[5](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/9c7a455b1e48d01a99e58e884a6c1acb75074ad0)\]. Temporal patterns such as absolute and relative time signals have also been incorporated into models to better capture user behavior dynamics \[[6](http://arxiv.org/abs/2001.04830)\]. Furthermore, models like NARM and FMLP-Rec have been developed to handle session-based recommendations and noise in logged data, respectively, by leveraging attention mechanisms and filtering techniques \[[7](http://arxiv.org/abs/2312.00752)\]\[[8](http://arxiv.org/abs/2303.11780)\]. These approaches collectively highlight the ongoing efforts to enhance sequential recommendation through innovative architectures and modeling strategies.

### <a id='category-state-space-models-for-long-sequences'></a>State-Space Models for Long Sequences: 5 papers found.
State-space models (SSMs) have emerged as a promising approach for addressing the challenges of long-sequence modeling in sequential recommendation systems \[[10](http://arxiv.org/abs/2409.01192)\]. Traditional methods like RNNs, CNNs, and Transformers face limitations in handling long-range dependencies and computational efficiency, especially for sequences with thousands of steps \[[11](http://arxiv.org/abs/2111.00396)\]. Recent work has focused on improving SSMs by introducing structured parameterizations, such as the S4 model, which conditions the state matrix $ A $ with a low-rank correction to enable efficient computation while preserving theoretical strengths \[[11](http://arxiv.org/abs/2111.00396)\]. This allows for stable diagonalization and reduces the SSM to a Cauchy kernel computation, significantly improving efficiency \[[11](http://arxiv.org/abs/2111.00396)\]. Building on this, the Mamba architecture, based on selective SSMs, has demonstrated superior performance in modeling long behavior sequences with hardware-aware designs, enabling efficient and effective sequential recommendations \[[10](http://arxiv.org/abs/2409.01192)\]. Similarly, the S5 model extends the S4 framework by using a multi-input, multi-output SSM, achieving state-of-the-art results on long-range sequence modeling tasks while maintaining computational efficiency \[[24](http://arxiv.org/abs/2208.04933)\]. These advancements highlight the potential of SSMs in overcoming the limitations of traditional models, particularly in scenarios requiring variable-length sequence processing and capturing diverse user interactions \[[10](http://arxiv.org/abs/2409.01192)\]\[[11](http://arxiv.org/abs/2111.00396)\]\[[24](http://arxiv.org/abs/2208.04933)\].
