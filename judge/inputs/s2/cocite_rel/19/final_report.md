## Related Works

Speech-driven 3D facial animation has been a focal point in computer vision and machine learning research, with numerous approaches aiming to generate realistic facial animations from audio inputs. Early methods primarily relied on phoneme-level features and rule-based systems, often resulting in static or uncanny facial movements \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2104.08223)\]. More recent approaches have leveraged deep learning to improve the realism and expressiveness of generated animations. For instance, MeshTalk introduced a categorical latent space to disentangle audio-correlated and audio-uncorrelated facial movements, achieving more natural lip synchronization and plausible upper face animations \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/2112.05329)\]. Similarly, FaceFormer utilized a Transformer-based autoregressive model to encode long-term audio context and predict 3D face meshes, improving the accuracy of lip movements \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/2303.11089)\].

The importance of emotional and contextual disentanglement in facial animation has also been emphasized. EmoTalk proposed an end-to-end neural network to disentangle emotions from speech content, enabling the generation of emotionally expressive 3D faces \[[4](http://arxiv.org/abs/2301.06059)\]. This approach was further extended in 3D-TalkEmo, which introduced a 3D face representation structure to synthesize emotional talking heads with various emotional states \[[5](http://arxiv.org/abs/2104.07452)\]. Additionally, EAMM proposed an emotion-aware motion model to generate one-shot emotional talking faces by incorporating emotion source videos \[[6](http://arxiv.org/abs/2301.00023)\].

The challenge of lip synchronization and the impact of phonetic variations on facial motion have also been addressed. The work in \[[7](http://arxiv.org/abs/2205.15278)\] demonstrated that phonetically similar syllables can lead to significant coupling in self-supervised audio feature spaces, resulting in an averaging effect during lip motion generation. This issue is closely related to the problem tackled in the current paper, where the Wav2Sem module is proposed to decorrelate audio encodings by extracting semantic features \[[8](http://arxiv.org/abs/2301.02379)\]. This approach is similar to the work in \[[9](http://arxiv.org/abs/1807.07860)\], which emphasized the importance of disentangling audio representations to improve the accuracy of lip movements in the presence of noise and emotional variations.

Furthermore, the use of self-supervised learning for audio representation has been explored in several works. wav2vec 2.0, for example, demonstrated the feasibility of learning powerful speech representations from unlabeled data, which can be used to improve the quality of audio-driven facial animations \[[10](http://arxiv.org/abs/1905.03079)\]. This aligns with the current paper's use of self-supervised audio models as encoders, highlighting the importance of robust audio representations in generating realistic facial animations.

### <a id='category-latent-space-disentanglement-methods'></a>Latent Space Disentanglement Methods: 6 papers found.
In the context of latent space disentanglement methods for audio-driven facial animation, recent research has focused on improving the accuracy and realism of generated facial movements by explicitly separating audio-related and non-audio-related information. For instance, one approach introduces a categorical latent space that disentangles audio-correlated and audio-uncorrelated information using a novel cross-modality loss, enabling more realistic lip motion and plausible animation of uncorrelated facial features such as eye blinks \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2104.08223)\]. Similarly, other works have emphasized the importance of disentangled audio-visual representations to enable arbitrary-subject talking face generation, where both subject-related and speech-related information are explicitly separated through an associative-and-adversarial training process \[[9](http://arxiv.org/abs/1807.07860)\]. Another study addresses the challenge of pose control in talking face generation by introducing an implicit low-dimensional pose code, allowing for controllable head poses while maintaining accurate lip synchronization \[[12](http://arxiv.org/abs/2104.11116)\]. Furthermore, efforts have been made to enhance robustness against variations in audio input, such as background noise and emotional tone, by disentangling audio sequences into factors like phonetic content, emotional tone, and background noise \[[15](http://arxiv.org/abs/1910.00726)\]. Additionally, methods have been proposed to generalize across different people by leveraging 3D face models and neural rendering, enabling photo-realistic facial reenactment with arbitrary source and target speakers \[[24](http://arxiv.org/abs/1912.05566)\]. These approaches collectively highlight the significance of disentangled latent spaces in achieving more accurate, controllable, and robust audio-driven facial animation.

### <a id='category-transformer-based-audio-driven-models'></a>Transformer-Based Audio-Driven Models: 4 papers found.
Transformers have become a cornerstone in speech-driven 3D facial animation, with several approaches leveraging their ability to model long-range dependencies and cross-modal interactions \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/2112.05329)\]. For instance, Face-Former introduces a Transformer-based autoregressive model that encodes long-term audio context and predicts 3D face meshes, addressing the limitations of phoneme-level feature learning that often leads to inaccurate lip movements \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/2112.05329)\]. Similarly, Imitator focuses on capturing identity-specific speaking styles by training a style-agnostic transformer on a large facial expression dataset, enabling the generation of realistic facial animations that preserve the target actor’s unique characteristics \[[6](http://arxiv.org/abs/2301.00023)\]. Another approach, Codetalker, reframes the problem as a code query task in a learned codebook space, reducing cross-modal mapping uncertainty and improving the vividness of generated motions \[[8](http://arxiv.org/abs/2301.02379)\]. These methods collectively highlight the importance of modeling both audio-visual alignment and identity-specific characteristics to achieve more natural and expressive facial animations. Additionally, the Audio-Visual Correlation Transformer (AVCT) emphasizes the need for consistent audio-visual correlations, particularly when generalizing across different speakers, by incorporating phonemes and motion field transfer modules to ensure accurate lip-sync and natural mouth shapes \[[27](http://arxiv.org/abs/2112.02749)\]. Overall, these works demonstrate the growing trend of using Transformer-based models to enhance the realism and accuracy of speech-driven facial animation, while addressing key challenges such as data scarcity, identity-specific variations, and cross-modal alignment.

### <a id='category-emotion-and-context-disentanglement'></a>Emotion and Context Disentanglement: 3 papers found.
In the realm of emotion and context disentanglement for speech-driven 3D facial animation, recent research has focused on separating emotional expressions from speech content to generate more expressive and emotionally accurate facial animations \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/2303.11089)\]. For instance, the Emotalk framework introduces an emotion disentangling encoder (EDE) that decouples emotion and content in speech by cross-reconstructed speech signals with different emotion labels, enabling the generation of 3D faces with enhanced emotional expression \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/2303.11089)\]. Similarly, the Emotional Video Portraits (EVP) system employs a Cross-Reconstructed Emotion Disentanglement technique to decompose speech into emotion and content spaces, allowing for dynamic emotional facial landmark deduction \[[5](http://arxiv.org/abs/2104.07452)\]. These approaches aim to address the limitations of previous methods that often neglected emotional expressions or failed to disentangle them from speech content \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/2303.11089)\]. Additionally, other works have proposed deep learning frameworks that map speech spectrograms to micro facial action unit intensities, enabling the automatic estimation of emotional intensity and the reproduction of affective states through facial unit activations \[[26](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/263ad0264a19ac58905804715f14fb53fe2f181a)\]. These studies collectively highlight the importance of disentangling emotional and contextual information in speech to improve the realism and expressiveness of 3D facial animations.

### <a id='category-lip-synchronization-challenges'></a>Lip Synchronization Challenges: 2 papers found.
In the realm of lip synchronization and speech-driven facial animation, prior research has primarily focused on aligning lip movements with audio inputs, often using static images or pre-trained audio models \[[13](http://arxiv.org/abs/2008.10010)\]. However, these approaches struggle with dynamic, unconstrained talking face videos, especially when dealing with arbitrary identities, leading to significant lip-sync errors \[[13](http://arxiv.org/abs/2008.10010)\]. This challenge is exacerbated by the lack of high-resolution audio-visual datasets and limitations in facial landmark detection, which hinder the generation of realistic, high-definition videos \[[23](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/c9e8cb4bb2c8e5d6dd4e631fc347f7f1415f2150)\]. To address these issues, recent works have introduced advanced frameworks that leverage 3D morphable models and flow-guided generation techniques to improve both the quality and synchronization of synthesized videos \[[23](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/c9e8cb4bb2c8e5d6dd4e631fc347f7f1415f2150)\]. Additionally, efforts have been made to develop rigorous evaluation benchmarks and metrics to accurately measure lip synchronization in unconstrained settings \[[13](http://arxiv.org/abs/2008.10010)\]. These advancements highlight the ongoing challenges and innovations in the field, particularly in achieving natural, accurate lip movements across diverse and dynamic scenarios.
