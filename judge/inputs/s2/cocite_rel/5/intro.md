## Related Works

Instruction-following text embedding has emerged as a critical area in natural language processing, aiming to generate dynamic text embeddings that adapt to user instructions. This task requires models to understand and incorporate specific attributes of text based on given instructions, which is a departure from static embedding approaches \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2212.09741)\]. Prior work has explored various methods to achieve this, but existing approaches often suffer from significant computational overhead, as they require re-encoding the entire corpus for each new instruction \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2212.09741)\]. This limitation has motivated the development of more efficient and adaptive embedding frameworks.

One notable approach is INSTRUCTOR, which introduces a method for computing text embeddings given task instructions by embedding text together with instructions explaining the use case \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2212.09741)\]. This method allows for generating embeddings tailored to different downstream tasks and domains without further training, which is a significant advantage over traditional encoders \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2212.09741)\]. However, INSTRUCTOR still requires training on a diverse set of tasks and may not be as efficient in real-time applications \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2212.09741)\].

Another relevant work is E5, which is a family of state-of-the-art text embeddings that transfer well to a wide range of tasks \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/2212.03533)\]. E5 is trained in a contrastive manner with weak supervision signals and can be used as a general-purpose embedding model for tasks such as retrieval, clustering, and classification \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/2212.03533)\]. While E5 achieves strong performance in both zero-shot and fine-tuned settings, it does not explicitly address the challenge of adapting embeddings to specific instructions in real-time \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/2212.03533)\].

Self-Instruct is another framework that improves the instruction-following capabilities of pretrained language models by bootstrapping off their own generations \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/2212.10560)\]. This method generates instructions, input, and output samples from a language model and uses them to fine-tune the original model, leading to significant improvements in instruction-following performance \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/2212.10560)\]. However, Self-Instruct relies on a large amount of synthetic data and may not be as effective in scenarios where real-time adaptation is required \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/2212.10560)\].

The work on Vision-and-Language Navigation (VLN) has also contributed to the understanding of how models can interpret and follow natural language instructions in complex environments \[[8](http://arxiv.org/abs/1711.07280)\]. These models often use reinforcement learning and cross-modal matching to align instructions with visual inputs, demonstrating the importance of grounding instructions in perceptual context \[[8](http://arxiv.org/abs/1711.07280)\]. While these approaches are effective in specific domains, they are not directly applicable to the general task of instruction-following text embedding \[[8](http://arxiv.org/abs/1711.07280)\].
