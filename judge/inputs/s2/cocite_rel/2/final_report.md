## Related Works

Nonverbal communication plays a crucial role in human interaction, encompassing gestures, facial expressions, and body language that convey intent and emotion \[[2](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/86f81c8a46a84cd969ed80e88395ba5de709794f)\]. Existing large language models (LLMs) have primarily focused on text-based understanding and generation, often neglecting the integration of nonverbal cues \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2302.14045)\]. This limitation restricts their ability to create fully immersive conversational experiences \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2302.14045)\]. Several recent works have explored multimodal extensions to LLMs, aiming to incorporate visual and auditory modalities for more comprehensive understanding \[[10](http://arxiv.org/abs/2204.14198)\]. For instance, Kosmos-1 is a Multimodal Large Language Model (MLLM) that can perceive general modalities, learn in context, and follow instructions \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2302.14045)\]. Similarly, ChatBridge introduces a novel multimodal language model that leverages language as a catalyst to bridge different modalities \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/2305.16103)\]. These models demonstrate the potential of integrating nonverbal cues into LLMs, although they often rely on limited or curated datasets \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/2305.16103)\].

The importance of multimodal data for training models that can understand and generate nonverbal cues has been emphasized in several studies. For example, ViLBERT is a model that learns task-agnostic joint representations of image content and natural language, enabling it to perform well on various vision-and-language tasks \[[9](http://arxiv.org/abs/1908.02265)\]. Similarly, BLIP is a vision-language pre-training framework that transfers flexibly to both vision-language understanding and generation tasks \[[8](http://arxiv.org/abs/2201.12086)\]. These models highlight the need for large-scale, diverse datasets that include annotated videos with aligned text, facial expressions, and body language \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2302.14045)\]. The VENUS dataset introduced in this work addresses this need by providing a large-scale, annotated dataset with time-aligned nonverbal cues \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2302.14045)\].

Recent advances in multimodal learning have also focused on improving the alignment between different modalities. For example, Flamingo is a family of Visual Language Models (VLMs) that can be rapidly adapted to novel tasks using only a handful of annotated examples \[[10](http://arxiv.org/abs/2204.14198)\]. This is achieved through architectural innovations that allow the models to handle sequences of interleaved visual and textual data \[[10](http://arxiv.org/abs/2204.14198)\]. Similarly, mPLUG-Owl introduces a modularized training paradigm that equips LLMs with multi-modal abilities through modularized learning of foundation LLMs, a visual knowledge module, and a visual abstractor module \[[13](http://arxiv.org/abs/2304.14178)\]. These models demonstrate the potential of combining different modalities to enhance the understanding and generation capabilities of LLMs \[[13](http://arxiv.org/abs/2304.14178)\].

### <a id='category-multimodal-extensions-to-llms'></a>Multimodal Extensions to LLMs: 18 papers found.
Recent advancements in multimodal extensions to large language models (LLMs) have focused on integrating diverse modalities such as text, images, audio, and video to enhance understanding and generation capabilities. Models like Kosmos-1 \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2302.14045)\] and ChatBridge \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/2305.16103)\] have demonstrated the ability to process and generate content across multiple modalities, leveraging pre-trained LLMs and cross-modal alignment techniques. These models often utilize large-scale multimodal datasets, such as those containing interleaved text and images, to train on a wide range of tasks, including vision-language understanding, visual question answering, and image captioning \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2302.14045)\]\[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/2305.16103)\]. Additionally, models like Flamingo \[[10](http://arxiv.org/abs/2204.14198)\] and Video-LLaMA \[[5](http://arxiv.org/abs/2306.02858)\] have been developed to handle complex tasks such as video comprehension and audio-visual integration, emphasizing the importance of temporal and cross-modal alignment. Furthermore, research has explored the use of instruction tuning and few-shot learning to improve the adaptability of these models to new tasks, as seen in LLaVA \[[12](http://arxiv.org/abs/2304.08485)\] and InstructBLIP \[[14](http://arxiv.org/abs/2305.06500)\]. These efforts highlight the growing interest in creating more robust and versatile multimodal models capable of understanding and generating content across various modalities, thereby bridging the gap between human-like interaction and machine intelligence.
