## Related Works

Anomaly detection in industrial settings has garnered significant attention due to its critical role in quality control and fault prevention in manufacturing processes. Early approaches relied on handcrafted features and classical machine learning models, but recent advances have leveraged deep learning to improve detection accuracy and robustness \[[5](http://arxiv.org/abs/2301.11514)\]. However, most deep learning-based methods suffer from high computational demands, making them unsuitable for deployment on resource-constrained embedded devices commonly used in Small and Medium Enterprises (SMEs) \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2106.08265)\]. This challenge has motivated the development of lightweight and efficient models that maintain competitive performance while enabling real-time deployment \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/2011.08785)\].

Several recent works have focused on improving the efficiency of anomaly detection models. PatchCore \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2106.08265)\] addresses the cold-start problem by using a maximally representative memory bank of nominal patch features, achieving state-of-the-art performance on the MVTec AD benchmark with an AUROC score of 99.6%. Similarly, PaDiM \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/2011.08785)\] employs a probabilistic representation of the normal class using multivariate Gaussian distributions and exploits semantic correlations in CNN features to improve localization accuracy. Both methods are designed for one-class learning, where models are trained only on normal data, a common scenario in industrial settings \[[5](http://arxiv.org/abs/2301.11514)\]. However, these methods often require substantial computational resources, limiting their applicability in embedded systems \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2106.08265)\].

To further reduce computational overhead, multimodal approaches have been proposed to integrate information from multiple data sources, such as RGB images and 3D point clouds \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/2303.00601)\]. The M3DM method \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/2303.00601)\] introduces a hybrid fusion scheme that aligns features across modalities and avoids information loss during fusion. This approach outperforms existing methods on the MVTec-3D AD dataset, demonstrating the potential of multimodal learning for industrial anomaly detection. However, such methods may still be too complex for deployment on embedded hardware \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/2303.00601)\].

Another line of research focuses on improving the generalization and robustness of anomaly detection models. DRÆM \[[7](http://arxiv.org/abs/2108.07610)\] proposes a discriminatively trained reconstruction embedding model that enables direct anomaly localization without requiring post-processing steps. This method outperforms existing unsupervised methods on the MVTec dataset and achieves performance close to fully supervised approaches on the DAGM dataset \[[7](http://arxiv.org/abs/2108.07610)\]. Similarly, the Asymmetric Student-Teacher Networks (AST) \[[8](http://arxiv.org/abs/2210.07829)\] leverage normalizing flows for density estimation and conventional feed-forward networks to achieve large distances between normal and anomalous outputs, leading to state-of-the-art results on MVTec AD and MVTec 3D-AD datasets \[[8](http://arxiv.org/abs/2210.07829)\].
