## Related Works

Anomaly detection in industrial settings has garnered significant attention due to its critical role in quality control and fault prevention in manufacturing processes. Early approaches relied on handcrafted features and classical machine learning models, but recent advances have leveraged deep learning to improve detection accuracy and robustness \[[5](http://arxiv.org/abs/2301.11514)\]. However, most deep learning-based methods suffer from high computational demands, making them unsuitable for deployment on resource-constrained embedded devices commonly used in Small and Medium Enterprises (SMEs) \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2106.08265)\]. This challenge has motivated the development of lightweight and efficient models that maintain competitive performance while enabling real-time deployment \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/2011.08785)\].

Several recent works have focused on improving the efficiency of anomaly detection models. PatchCore \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2106.08265)\] addresses the cold-start problem by using a maximally representative memory bank of nominal patch features, achieving state-of-the-art performance on the MVTec AD benchmark with an AUROC score of 99.6%. Similarly, PaDiM \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/2011.08785)\] employs a probabilistic representation of the normal class using multivariate Gaussian distributions and exploits semantic correlations in CNN features to improve localization accuracy. Both methods are designed for one-class learning, where models are trained only on normal data, a common scenario in industrial settings \[[5](http://arxiv.org/abs/2301.11514)\]. However, these methods often require substantial computational resources, limiting their applicability in embedded systems \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2106.08265)\].

To further reduce computational overhead, multimodal approaches have been proposed to integrate information from multiple data sources, such as RGB images and 3D point clouds \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/2303.00601)\]. The M3DM method \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/2303.00601)\] introduces a hybrid fusion scheme that aligns features across modalities and avoids information loss during fusion. This approach outperforms existing methods on the MVTec-3D AD dataset, demonstrating the potential of multimodal learning for industrial anomaly detection. However, such methods may still be too complex for deployment on embedded hardware \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/2303.00601)\].

Another line of research focuses on improving the generalization and robustness of anomaly detection models. DRÆM \[[7](http://arxiv.org/abs/2108.07610)\] proposes a discriminatively trained reconstruction embedding model that enables direct anomaly localization without requiring post-processing steps. This method outperforms existing unsupervised methods on the MVTec dataset and achieves performance close to fully supervised approaches on the DAGM dataset \[[7](http://arxiv.org/abs/2108.07610)\]. Similarly, the Asymmetric Student-Teacher Networks (AST) \[[8](http://arxiv.org/abs/2210.07829)\] leverage normalizing flows for density estimation and conventional feed-forward networks to achieve large distances between normal and anomalous outputs, leading to state-of-the-art results on MVTec AD and MVTec 3D-AD datasets \[[8](http://arxiv.org/abs/2210.07829)\].

### <a id='category-deep-learning-based-anomaly-detection-methods'></a>Deep Learning-Based Anomaly Detection Methods: 3 papers found.
In the realm of deep learning-based anomaly detection methods, recent advancements have focused on improving efficiency and performance for industrial applications \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2106.08265)\]. A key challenge is the cold-start problem, where models must be trained using only nominal (non-defective) examples, making it difficult to detect anomalies without prior knowledge of defect types \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2106.08265)\]. To address this, approaches such as PatchCore have been proposed, which utilize maximally representative memory banks of nominal patch-features to achieve state-of-the-art performance on benchmarks like MVTec AD, achieving an AUROC score of up to 99.6% \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2106.08265)\]. Additionally, the use of student-teacher frameworks has been explored, where asymmetric architectures are employed to enhance anomaly detection by leveraging the divergence of outputs between normal and anomalous data \[[8](http://arxiv.org/abs/2210.07829)\]. These methods aim to improve both detection accuracy and computational efficiency, which is crucial for deployment on resource-constrained embedded devices \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2106.08265)\]. Furthermore, comprehensive reviews have highlighted the importance of considering various neural network architectures, levels of supervision, and loss functions to optimize anomaly detection in industrial settings \[[5](http://arxiv.org/abs/2301.11514)\]. Overall, these studies emphasize the need for robust, efficient, and adaptable models that can be deployed in real-world manufacturing environments.

### <a id='category-anomaly-localization-techniques'></a>Anomaly Localization Techniques: 4 papers found.
In the realm of anomaly localization techniques, recent advancements have focused on improving both detection accuracy and computational efficiency, particularly for industrial applications. Methods such as PaDiM leverage pretrained convolutional neural networks (CNNs) for patch embedding and multivariate Gaussian distributions to model normal patterns, achieving state-of-the-art performance on datasets like MVTec AD and STC \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/2011.08785)\]. Similarly, the introduction of a comprehensive 3D dataset for unsupervised anomaly detection has highlighted the need for robust methods capable of identifying geometric deviations in manufactured objects, emphasizing the importance of high-resolution data and precise ground-truth annotations \[[4](http://arxiv.org/abs/2112.09045)\]. The MVTec AD dataset, which contains 5354 high-resolution images with over 70 types of defects, has become a benchmark for evaluating unsupervised anomaly detection methods, revealing significant room for improvement in both detection and localization \[[6](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/3aa681914a7da79f7d7293f51a058eefe61c8bb7)\]. Additionally, discriminative approaches like DRÆM have emerged, offering a novel way to directly localize anomalies by learning a joint representation of an image and its reconstruction, thus eliminating the need for complex post-processing steps \[[7](http://arxiv.org/abs/2108.07610)\]. These developments underscore the growing emphasis on methods that are not only accurate but also efficient, especially for deployment in real-world industrial settings where computational resources are limited.

### <a id='category-deployment-and-real-world-testing-of-anomaly-detection-systems'></a>Deployment and Real-World Testing of Anomaly Detection Systems: 2 papers found.
In the realm of anomaly detection for industrial control systems, recent research has focused on improving the reliability and adaptability of detection mechanisms in dynamic environments. Reconstruction-based anomaly detection has emerged as a promising approach, leveraging physical process models to detect attacks by analyzing sensor data rather than network traffic \[[9](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/c67e39cce9253f2f78ebbc818cc85547dc4fc40b)\]. This method has been shown to be effective in identifying anomalies in systems like water distribution networks, where subtle changes in sensor readings can indicate malicious activity \[[9](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/c67e39cce9253f2f78ebbc818cc85547dc4fc40b)\]. However, attackers have developed strategies to evade these detectors by manipulating sensor data, particularly through replay attacks, which are often assumed to be highly effective \[[9](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/c67e39cce9253f2f78ebbc818cc85547dc4fc40b)\]. To counteract such evasion techniques, researchers have proposed novel attack methods that exploit learned physical constraints of the system, such as white box and black box attacks, which use optimization and autoencoder-based approaches to generate normal-looking data \[[9](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/c67e39cce9253f2f78ebbc818cc85547dc4fc40b)\]. These attacks have been tested on real-world datasets, demonstrating their effectiveness against various anomaly detection models, including autoencoder, LSTM, and CNN-based detectors \[[9](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/c67e39cce9253f2f78ebbc818cc85547dc4fc40b)\]. Additionally, there is growing interest in systematically generating invariant rules from operational data logs to enhance anomaly detection, as manually defined rules are often limited in scope and accuracy \[[10](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/de39d8209e4848bdb9e7268080c87d7384525d11)\]. These efforts highlight the ongoing evolution of anomaly detection techniques tailored to the unique challenges of industrial control environments.
