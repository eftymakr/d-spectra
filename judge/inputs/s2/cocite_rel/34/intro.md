## Related Works

Time series forecasting and asset allocation have been extensively studied in recent years, with a growing focus on integrating advanced models to improve accuracy and robustness. Traditional approaches to financial asset allocation typically involve two stages: first, forecasting asset returns, and second, optimizing the portfolio based on these forecasts \[[18](http://arxiv.org/abs/2206.03246)\]. However, errors in the forecasting stage can significantly reduce the accuracy of the resulting asset weights, thereby affecting the profitability of the overall portfolio \[[18](http://arxiv.org/abs/2206.03246)\]. This challenge has motivated the development of end-to-end frameworks that directly optimize the portfolio without relying on intermediate forecasting steps \[[18](http://arxiv.org/abs/2206.03246)\].

Several studies have explored the use of deep learning models for time series forecasting, including Transformers, which have shown promise in capturing long-term dependencies \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2211.14730)\]. However, Transformers face challenges in handling long lookback windows due to computational and memory constraints \[[23](http://arxiv.org/abs/2308.11200)\]. To address this, models such as PatchTST \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2211.14730)\] and iTransformer \[[9](http://arxiv.org/abs/2310.06625)\] have been proposed, which leverage efficient attention mechanisms and novel architectures to improve forecasting performance. These models have demonstrated significant improvements in accuracy, particularly for long-term forecasting tasks \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2211.14730)\].

In the context of financial time series, hybrid models combining Convolutional Neural Networks (CNNs) and Transformers have also been explored \[[27](http://arxiv.org/abs/2304.04912)\]. These models aim to capture both short-term and long-term dependencies by leveraging the local pattern recognition capabilities of CNNs and the global context modeling of Transformers. This approach has shown promising results in forecasting stock price changes \[[27](http://arxiv.org/abs/2304.04912)\].

The integration of pre-trained models into time series analysis has also gained traction. For instance, the "One Fits All" framework \[[16](http://arxiv.org/abs/2302.11939)\] leverages pre-trained language models to perform various time series tasks, including forecasting, classification, and anomaly detection. This approach demonstrates that pre-trained models can generalize across different time series tasks, offering comparable or state-of-the-art performance \[[16](http://arxiv.org/abs/2302.11939)\].

Additionally, the use of probabilistic models for forecasting has been explored, with DeepAR \[[7](http://arxiv.org/abs/1704.04110)\] being a notable example. DeepAR is a probabilistic forecasting model based on autoregressive recurrent networks, which has been shown to improve accuracy compared to classical methods \[[7](http://arxiv.org/abs/1704.04110)\]. Similarly, the Chronos framework \[[11](http://arxiv.org/abs/2403.07815)\] introduces a pre-trained probabilistic time series model that tokenizes time series data and leverages transformer-based architectures for forecasting. This approach has demonstrated strong performance across diverse datasets \[[11](http://arxiv.org/abs/2403.07815)\].
