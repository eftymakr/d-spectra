## Related Works

Generative retrieval (GR) has gained significant attention in information retrieval (IR) due to its ability to directly generate document identifiers (docids) from queries using a single model \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/2202.06991)\]. However, most existing GR models are evaluated on static document collections, and their performance on dynamic corpora where documents are continuously added remains underexplored \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2308.14968)\]. This section reviews related works that address GR, dynamic corpora, and docid design, providing a foundation for the current study.

Early GR models, such as the Differentiable Search Index (DSI), encode all documents into model parameters and use the same model to answer queries directly \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/2202.06991)\]. This approach simplifies the retrieval process by eliminating the need for separate indexing and retrieval components \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/2202.06991)\]. However, deploying DSI models in dynamic environments is computationally expensive, as retraining is required whenever new documents are added \[[7](http://arxiv.org/abs/2212.09744)\]. To address this, DSI++ was introduced, enabling continual learning by incrementally updating the model with new documents while preserving retrieval performance for both old and new documents \[[7](http://arxiv.org/abs/2212.09744)\]. Despite these advances, the issue of forgetting previously indexed documents remains a challenge \[[7](http://arxiv.org/abs/2212.09744)\].

Docid design is a critical factor in GR performance. Traditional approaches use numeric-based docids, which are efficient but lack semantic richness \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2308.14968)\]. In contrast, text-based docids, such as those generated by the Neural Corpus Indexer (NCI) and the CorpusBrain model, offer fine-grained representations that align better with language models' pretrained knowledge \[[6](http://arxiv.org/abs/2208.07652)\]. This alignment is crucial for dynamic corpora, where the ability to generalize to unseen documents is essential \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2308.14968)\]. The use of semantic docids has been shown to improve retrieval performance, especially in zero-shot and low-resource settings \[[6](http://arxiv.org/abs/2208.07652)\].

Recent work has also explored the use of autoregressive models to generate docids, such as in the SEAL system, which uses ngrams as identifiers \[[8](http://arxiv.org/abs/2204.10628)\]. This approach allows for more diverse and meaningful docid representations, leading to improved retrieval performance \[[8](http://arxiv.org/abs/2204.10628)\]. Additionally, the GenRet method learns to tokenize documents into semantic docids through a discrete auto-encoding framework, achieving state-of-the-art results on benchmark datasets \[[9](http://arxiv.org/abs/2304.04171)\].

The importance of docid design in dynamic environments is further highlighted by the findings of the current study, which show that text-based docids outperform numeric-based ones in terms of generalization and performance over evolving corpora \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2308.14968)\]. This is attributed to their semantic alignment with language models and their ability to capture fine-grained document characteristics \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2308.14968)\].

	Document 1: [Snippet]: «The Differentiable Search Index (DSI) is an emerging paradigm for information retrieval. Unlike traditional retrieval architectures where index and retrieval are two different and separate components, DSI uses a single transformer model to perform both indexing and retrieval. In this paper, we identify and tackle an important issue of current DSI models: the data distribution mismatch that occurs between the DSI indexing and retrieval processes. Specifically, we argue that, at indexing, current DSI methods learn to build connections between the text of long documents and the identifier of the documents, but then retrieval of document identifiers is based on queries that are commonly much shorter than the indexed documents. To address this fundamental problem of current DSI models, we propose a simple yet effective indexing framework for DSI, called DSI-QG. When indexing, DSI-QG represents documents with a number of potentially relevant queries generated by a query generation model and re-ranked and filtered by a cross-encoder ranker. The presence of these queries at indexing allows the DSI models to connect a document identifier to a set of queries, hence mitigating data distribution mismatches present between the indexing and the retrieval phases. Empirical results on popular mono-lingual and cross-lingual passage retrieval datasets show that DSI-QG significantly outperforms the original DSI model.»
