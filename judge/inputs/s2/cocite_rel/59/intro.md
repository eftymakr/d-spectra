## Related Works

Fairness in recommendation systems has garnered significant attention in recent years, with a growing body of research addressing the trade-offs between recommendation accuracy and fairness \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2204.08085)\]\[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/1104.3913)\]\[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/1704.06840)\]. Prior work has identified that fairness constraints can lead to a decline in recommendation accuracy, particularly when fairness is enforced at the item or provider level \[[4](http://arxiv.org/abs/1802.07281)\]\[[5](http://arxiv.org/abs/2002.10764)\]\[[6](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/1e213c3cde47d2ae4bf4492b30874e3fc47e0d92)\]. This trade-off is often framed in terms of economic principles, where fairness considerations can be likened to a tax on suppliers or producers, ultimately impacting users \[[7](http://arxiv.org/abs/1712.09752)\]\[[8](http://arxiv.org/abs/2104.10671)\]\[[9](http://arxiv.org/abs/1609.05807)\]. 

Several studies have explored fairness in rankings, focusing on both user-side and item-side fairness \[[10](http://arxiv.org/abs/1610.08559)\]\[[11](http://arxiv.org/abs/2110.15781)\]\[[12](http://arxiv.org/abs/1809.02921)\]. For instance, \[[13](http://arxiv.org/abs/1707.00093)\] highlights the importance of considering fairness from both customer and producer perspectives in two-sided platforms, noting that traditional approaches often prioritize one side at the expense of the other. Similarly, \[[14](http://arxiv.org/abs/2104.09024)\] proposes a two-sided fairness-aware recommendation model (TFROM) that ensures fairness for both customers and providers while maintaining personalization. This aligns with the broader trend of addressing fairness in recommender systems from a multisided perspective \[[15](http://arxiv.org/abs/1903.00780)\].

Fairness in recommendation ranking has also been approached through the lens of pairwise comparisons and utility maximization \[[16](http://arxiv.org/abs/2205.13619)\]. For example, \[[17](http://arxiv.org/abs/1701.08230)\] introduces a framework for evaluating fairness in rankings based on pairwise comparisons, which allows for a more nuanced understanding of fairness in recommendation systems. This approach has been further developed in \[[18](http://arxiv.org/abs/2303.06660)\], where the authors propose a fairness-aware re-ranking algorithm that balances accuracy and provider-side fairness. 

The concept of elasticity has been introduced as a way to formalize the trade-off between fairness and accuracy in re-ranking tasks \[[19](http://arxiv.org/abs/1706.06368)\]. This idea is closely related to economic principles, where the elasticity of utility between item groups determines how much accuracy is lost when fairness constraints are applied \[[20](http://arxiv.org/abs/1712.03586)\]. This insight has led to the development of the Elastic Fairness Curve (EF-Curve), an evaluation framework that enables a comparative analysis of algorithm performance across different elasticity levels \[[21](http://arxiv.org/abs/2201.01180)\]. 

In addition to these contributions, several studies have focused on the practical implementation of fairness-aware recommendation algorithms. For example, \[[22](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/d3d050c39fe41f2b17d4bdeb95cb10868761c204)\] proposes a fair re-ranking algorithm that incorporates elasticity calculations to adjust inter-item distances within a curved space. This approach has been validated on real-world datasets, demonstrating its effectiveness in improving fairness while maintaining recommendation quality \[[23](http://arxiv.org/abs/1711.05144)\].
