## Related Works

Diffusion models have gained significant traction in image and video generation due to their ability to produce high-quality, diverse outputs \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2211.15736)\]. However, the computational and memory demands of these models hinder their deployment on edge devices \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/2302.04304)\]. Post-training quantization (PTQ) has emerged as a promising technique to reduce model size and accelerate inference without retraining \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/2304.08818)\]. Early PTQ methods, however, were not tailored for diffusion models, which exhibit unique characteristics such as multi-time-step structures and bimodal activation distributions \[[4](http://arxiv.org/abs/2204.03458)\]. 

Several works have attempted to address these challenges. Q-Diffusion \[[5](http://arxiv.org/abs/2112.10752)\] introduced a novel PTQ method specifically tailored for diffusion models, focusing on timestep-aware calibration and split shortcut quantization. This approach enabled the quantization of full-precision models into 4-bit while maintaining performance \[[5](http://arxiv.org/abs/2112.10752)\]. Similarly, PTQ4DiT \[[6](http://arxiv.org/abs/2305.10657)\] was proposed to handle the unique challenges of diffusion transformers, including salient channels with extreme magnitudes and temporal variability in activation distributions. It introduced Channel-wise Salience Balancing (CSB) and Spearmen's ρ-guided Salience Calibration (SSC) to mitigate quantization errors \[[6](http://arxiv.org/abs/2305.10657)\]. 

Temporal Dynamic Quantization \[[7](http://arxiv.org/abs/2406.17343)\] further advanced the field by dynamically adjusting the quantization interval based on time step information, significantly improving output quality while maintaining low computational overhead \[[7](http://arxiv.org/abs/2406.17343)\]. This method is compatible with both PTQ and quantization-aware training (QAT), offering flexibility in deployment scenarios \[[7](http://arxiv.org/abs/2406.17343)\]. 

In the realm of vision transformers, PTQ4ViT \[[8](http://arxiv.org/abs/2405.16005)\] proposed twin uniform quantization to reduce quantization error on activation values, achieving near-lossless prediction accuracy at 8-bit quantization \[[8](http://arxiv.org/abs/2405.16005)\]. FQ-ViT \[[9](http://arxiv.org/abs/2212.09748)\] further improved this by introducing Power-of-Two Factor (PTF) and Log-Int-Softmax (LIS) to sustain extreme non-uniform distributions in attention maps \[[9](http://arxiv.org/abs/2212.09748)\]. 

For diffusion transformers, ViDiT-Q \[[10](http://arxiv.org/abs/2306.02316)\] was designed to address the challenges of text-to-image and video tasks, achieving W8A8 and W4A8 quantization with negligible degradation in visual quality \[[10](http://arxiv.org/abs/2306.02316)\]. This work highlighted the importance of tailoring quantization schemes to the specific characteristics of diffusion transformers \[[10](http://arxiv.org/abs/2306.02316)\].

These works collectively demonstrate the ongoing efforts to develop efficient quantization methods for diffusion models, addressing the unique challenges posed by their architecture and generation process.
