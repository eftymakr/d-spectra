## Related Works

Sensor calibration is a fundamental step in multi-sensor fusion for autonomous driving systems, ensuring accurate alignment between heterogeneous sensors such as radar, camera, and LiDAR \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2103.07505)\]. Early approaches for radar-camera calibration relied on dedicated calibration targets, which limited their applicability in dynamic environments \[[2](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/fa7f4199640500d48c0671181ea6a8dce3141123)\]. More recently, targetless methods have emerged to address these limitations, leveraging data-driven techniques for automatic calibration \[[3](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/0955326c67a947b5fac5fbae7238d04fc9adc251)\]. For instance, \[[4](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/4d4dbbcf27d991aeab43dafb82a105bb5512b799)\] proposed a data-driven method for automatic rotational radar-camera calibration without dedicated calibration targets, using a coarse and fine convolutional neural network to achieve precise and robust sensor registration. Similarly, \[[5](http://arxiv.org/abs/1904.08743)\] introduced an automatic targetless method for extrinsic calibration of multiple 3D LiDAR and radar sensors, requiring only a short driving distance and demonstrating high accuracy and repeatability.

The challenge of radar data sparsity and height uncertainty has motivated the development of novel representation and fusion strategies. For example, \[[6](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/0e88fc24ae7694d0b1a2fe78322936fc568bc7d2)\] proposed a dual-perspective representation that combines frontal and bird's-eye views to mitigate height uncertainty, aligning with the approach of the current work. The use of multi-modal cross-attention mechanisms to find location correspondences through cross-modal matching has also been explored in prior work, such as in \[[7](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/c80265fa719eb85c8a56a6b36c74b99b793f39b2)\], where a cross-attention based feature fusion was used to adaptively exchange spatio-contextual information between camera and radar for 3D object detection. Additionally, \[[8](http://arxiv.org/abs/2011.04841)\] introduced a feature-level fusion detection framework for radar and camera, where radar features were transformed into bird's-eye view (BEV) and aligned with image features to achieve better performance.

To enhance robustness against sparsity and height uncertainty, noise-resistant matching strategies have been proposed. For example, \[[9](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/0681cea243e4fb4d5ba9d8d08bec107615714bb4)\] designed a noise-resistant matcher to provide better supervision and improve the robustness of the matching mechanism. This aligns with the approach in the current work, where a Noise-Resistant Matcher was introduced during the training phase to enhance the robustness of the matching mechanism. Furthermore, \[[10](http://arxiv.org/abs/2304.00670)\] proposed a method for joint radar alignment and odometry calibration, demonstrating the importance of accurate sensor alignment for reliable perception.
