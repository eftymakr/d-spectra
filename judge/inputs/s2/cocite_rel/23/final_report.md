## Related Works

Sensor calibration is a fundamental step in multi-sensor fusion for autonomous driving systems, ensuring accurate alignment between heterogeneous sensors such as radar, camera, and LiDAR \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2103.07505)\]. Early approaches for radar-camera calibration relied on dedicated calibration targets, which limited their applicability in dynamic environments \[[2](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/fa7f4199640500d48c0671181ea6a8dce3141123)\]. More recently, targetless methods have emerged to address these limitations, leveraging data-driven techniques for automatic calibration \[[3](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/0955326c67a947b5fac5fbae7238d04fc9adc251)\]. For instance, \[[4](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/4d4dbbcf27d991aeab43dafb82a105bb5512b799)\] proposed a data-driven method for automatic rotational radar-camera calibration without dedicated calibration targets, using a coarse and fine convolutional neural network to achieve precise and robust sensor registration. Similarly, \[[5](http://arxiv.org/abs/1904.08743)\] introduced an automatic targetless method for extrinsic calibration of multiple 3D LiDAR and radar sensors, requiring only a short driving distance and demonstrating high accuracy and repeatability.

The challenge of radar data sparsity and height uncertainty has motivated the development of novel representation and fusion strategies. For example, \[[6](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/0e88fc24ae7694d0b1a2fe78322936fc568bc7d2)\] proposed a dual-perspective representation that combines frontal and bird's-eye views to mitigate height uncertainty, aligning with the approach of the current work. ### <a id='category-radar-and-camera-fusion-frameworks'></a>Radar and Camera Fusion Frameworks: 14 papers found.
The related works in radar and camera fusion for autonomous driving focus on improving 3D object detection, velocity estimation, and robustness in adverse conditions. Early approaches often relied on late fusion strategies, which combine sensor outputs at the result level, limiting the ability to fully exploit complementary information \[[14](http://arxiv.org/abs/2209.06535)\]. More recent methods, such as CenterFusion, introduce middle-fusion techniques that align radar and camera data through novel association methods, such as frustum-based matching, to improve depth and velocity estimation \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2103.07505)\]. Similarly, GRIF employs a gated RoI fusion mechanism to adaptively select data from radar and camera, enhancing performance in sparse or abnormal conditions \[[2](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/fa7f4199640500d48c0671181ea6a8dce3141123)\]. Camera Radar Net (CRN) further advances this by transforming image features to bird’s-eye-view (BEV) using radar points and applying multi-modal deformable attention to address spatial misalignment \[[3](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/0955326c67a947b5fac5fbae7238d04fc9adc251)\]. Other frameworks, like RadarNet, combine early and late fusion strategies, leveraging voxel-based early fusion and attention-based late fusion to capture geometric and dynamic information \[[4](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/4d4dbbcf27d991aeab43dafb82a105bb5512b799)\]. The RVDet framework addresses spatial alignment challenges by using occupancy grid maps and adaptive projection networks to integrate radar and camera features in the BEV domain \[[5](http://arxiv.org/abs/1904.08743)\]. Additionally, methods like CRF-Net introduce network-level fusion of camera and radar data, learning optimal fusion levels and employing training strategies like BlackIn to enhance detection accuracy \[[6](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/0e88fc24ae7694d0b1a2fe78322936fc568bc7d2)\]. The challenge of data association remains a key focus, with methods such as the proposed deep representation learning approach achieving high F1 scores by improving radar-camera alignment \[[7](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/c80265fa719eb85c8a56a6b36c74b99b793f39b2)\].
