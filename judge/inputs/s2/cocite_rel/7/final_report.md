## Related Works

Recent advancements in large language models (LLMs) have focused on improving their reasoning capabilities and multimodal understanding. Several studies have explored how LLMs can be enhanced through various techniques, including chain-of-thought (CoT) prompting, visual instruction tuning, and cross-modal alignment. These approaches aim to improve the models' ability to reason, understand, and generate content across multiple modalities.

Chain-of-thought prompting has been widely studied as a method to elicit complex reasoning from LLMs. For instance, \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/2205.11916)\] demonstrates that adding a simple prompt like "Let's think step by step" significantly improves zero-shot reasoning performance on arithmetic and symbolic reasoning tasks. This approach has been shown to outperform traditional few-shot learning methods by leveraging the model's internal reasoning process without requiring task-specific examples \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/2205.11916)\]. Similarly, \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2302.00923)\] introduces Multimodal-CoT, which extends CoT reasoning to incorporate both text and vision modalities, enabling more robust reasoning in tasks such as ScienceQA and A-OKVQA. This work highlights the importance of integrating multiple modalities to enhance reasoning capabilities \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2302.00923)\].

In the realm of multimodal models, several frameworks have been proposed to bridge the gap between vision and language. LXMERT \[[6](http://arxiv.org/abs/1908.07490)\] is one such model that uses a Transformer-based architecture with three encoders—object relationship, language, and cross-modality—to learn vision-and-language connections. It is pre-trained on image-and-sentence pairs using tasks like masked language modeling and cross-modality matching, leading to state-of-the-art results on visual question answering datasets \[[6](http://arxiv.org/abs/1908.07490)\]. Similarly, ViLBERT \[[10](http://arxiv.org/abs/1908.02265)\] extends the BERT architecture to a two-stream model that processes visual and textual inputs separately before integrating them through co-attentional transformer layers. This approach enables the model to perform well on tasks such as visual question answering and referring expressions \[[10](http://arxiv.org/abs/1908.02265)\].

Visual instruction tuning has also gained traction as a method to improve the zero-shot performance of LLMs on visual tasks. LLaVA \[[17](http://arxiv.org/abs/2304.08485)\] is an example of this, where a large language model is fine-tuned using instruction-following data generated by GPT-4. This approach allows the model to understand and respond to visual inputs without requiring extensive task-specific training \[[17](http://arxiv.org/abs/2304.08485)\]. Similarly, \[[18](http://arxiv.org/abs/2309.05519)\] introduces NExT-GPT, an any-to-any multimodal LLM that can accept and generate content in multiple modalities, including text, images, videos, and audio. ### <a id='category-cross-modal-alignment-models'></a>Cross-Modal Alignment Models: 11 papers found.
Cross-Modal Alignment Models have been extensively explored to bridge the gap between different modalities such as vision, language, and audio. These models aim to align and integrate information from multiple modalities to enable comprehensive understanding and generation. For instance, LXMERT \[[6](http://arxiv.org/abs/1908.07490)\] introduces a framework that uses a Transformer model with three encoders to learn vision-and-language connections through diverse pre-training tasks. Similarly, Video-LLaMA \[[9](http://arxiv.org/abs/2306.02858)\] extends LLMs to comprehend video content by integrating visual and auditory signals, addressing challenges like temporal changes in visual scenes and cross-modal integration. LLaVA \[[17](http://arxiv.org/abs/2304.08485)\] further demonstrates the effectiveness of instruction tuning using generated multimodal data to enhance visual and language understanding. Additionally, models like BLIP \[[14](http://arxiv.org/abs/2201.12086)\] and ViLBERT \[[10](http://arxiv.org/abs/1908.02265)\] focus on vision-language pre-training, achieving state-of-the-art results on various tasks by leveraging large-scale datasets and innovative architectures. These approaches collectively highlight the importance of cross-modal alignment in enabling multimodal models to perform complex tasks with high accuracy and generalization.
