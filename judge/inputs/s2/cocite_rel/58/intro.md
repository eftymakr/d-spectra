## Related Works

Table discovery and semantic parsing have seen significant advancements in recent years, driven by the increasing availability of structured and tabular data. Early approaches to table retrieval focused on keyword-based matching, often relying on simple string similarity or inverted indexes \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/1802.06159)\]. These methods, while effective for basic queries, struggled with complex or ambiguous user requirements, leading to suboptimal results when dealing with large or heterogeneous datasets \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/1802.06159)\]. More recent work has introduced semantic similarity measures to improve the accuracy of table retrieval by capturing the meaning of queries and tables rather than relying solely on surface-level matches \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/1802.06159)\]. This approach involves representing both queries and tables in multiple semantic spaces, including discrete sparse and continuous dense vector representations, and using various similarity metrics to compare them \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/1802.06159)\]. This method has shown substantial improvements over traditional keyword-based approaches, particularly in scenarios involving ad hoc table retrieval \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/1802.06159)\].

Semantic parsing has also been explored in the context of structured tables, with models like TaBERT being developed to jointly learn representations for natural language and tabular data \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2005.08314)\]. TaBERT is trained on a large corpus of 26 million tables and their English contexts, enabling it to perform well on tasks such as semantic parsing and text-to-SQL \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2005.08314)\]. This model has achieved state-of-the-art results on the WikiTableQuestions benchmark, demonstrating its effectiveness in handling complex semantic parsing tasks \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2005.08314)\]. Similarly, the Compositional Semantic Parsing task has been addressed by leveraging question-answer pairs to train models that can handle complex queries over semi-structured tables \[[6](http://arxiv.org/abs/1508.00305)\]. This work introduced a new dataset of 22,033 complex questions, which has since become a valuable resource for evaluating semantic parsing models \[[6](http://arxiv.org/abs/1508.00305)\].

In addition to semantic parsing, the field of table detection and recognition has also seen progress, with datasets like TableBank being introduced to benchmark and evaluate models on real-world scenarios \[[5](http://arxiv.org/abs/1903.01949)\]. TableBank contains 417K high-quality labeled tables and is designed to support the development of robust deep learning models for table detection and recognition \[[5](http://arxiv.org/abs/1903.01949)\]. This dataset has enabled researchers to build strong baselines and explore the generalization capabilities of models trained on out-of-domain data \[[5](http://arxiv.org/abs/1903.01949)\].

The proposed nlcTD task builds on these advancements by introducing a new paradigm for table discovery that combines natural language requirements with query tables to refine search results . This approach addresses the limitations of existing methods, which often rely on vague keywords or single-table queries, by enabling users to specify more nuanced and complex search criteria .
