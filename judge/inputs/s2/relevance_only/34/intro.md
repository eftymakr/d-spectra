## Related Works

Fund allocation in financial domains has attracted significant research interest, with a focus on improving the accuracy and robustness of allocation strategies under various constraints. Traditional approaches often rely on prediction-only or Predict-Then-Optimize (PTO) frameworks, which may suffer from goal mismatch and uncertainty in forecasting \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2503.03165)\]. Recent works have attempted to address these challenges by integrating advanced time series forecasting models with optimization techniques. For instance, PTOFA \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2503.03165)\] introduces a framework that combines prediction and optimization stages to maximize revenue under constraints, demonstrating the effectiveness of data-driven approaches. Similarly, the Portfolio Transformer (PT) \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/2206.03246)\] directly optimizes the Sharpe ratio without requiring explicit return forecasts, leveraging attention mechanisms to capture complex dependencies in financial data.

The integration of deep learning models with financial forecasting has also been explored. For example, the use of Transformers in time series forecasting has gained traction due to their ability to model long-term dependencies \[[18](http://arxiv.org/abs/2211.14730)\]. However, some studies highlight the limitations of relying heavily on patching techniques, which can restrict model applicability in certain forecasting tasks \[[27](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/5161e1cf2c3df491458ceb6f565e26af8747d954)\]. In response, DeformableTST \[[27](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/5161e1cf2c3df491458ceb6f565e26af8747d954)\] proposes a sparse attention mechanism that reduces reliance on patching, thereby improving flexibility and performance in diverse forecasting scenarios. Additionally, the introduction of risk-aware frameworks has become increasingly important, as financial applications often require balancing profitability with risk management \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/2503.04662)\].

The importance of uncertainty calibration in forecasting has also been emphasized. For example, the Residual Boosting Network (RBN) \[[22](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/151942b084ece11084dec2970598fd5d495e1bbc)\] leverages backcast residuals to improve probabilistic forecasts, while the Retrieval-Augmented Diffusion Model (RATD) \[[21](http://arxiv.org/abs/2410.18712)\] enhances forecasting accuracy by incorporating relevant historical data. These approaches highlight the growing trend of combining advanced forecasting techniques with uncertainty quantification to improve decision-making in financial contexts.

In the realm of asset allocation, several studies have focused on optimizing portfolio weights using deep learning models. The Portfolio Transformer (PT) \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/2206.03246)\] is one such example, where the model directly optimizes the Sharpe ratio without requiring explicit return forecasts. Similarly, the Risk-Aware Trading Portfolio Optimization (RATPO) \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/2503.04662)\] integrates market risk and profit measures into the optimization process, demonstrating the importance of incorporating risk factors into financial decision-making.
