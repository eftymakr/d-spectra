## Related Works

Recent advancements in neural retrieval methods, particularly those leveraging transformer-based pre-trained language models, have significantly improved multilingual and cross-lingual retrieval capabilities \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2011.01154)\]. However, the effectiveness of these models for low-resource, morphologically rich languages such as Amharic remains underexplored due to data scarcity and suboptimal tokenization strategies \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2011.01154)\]. This gap is addressed by introducing Amharic-specific dense retrieval models based on pre-trained Amharic BERT and RoBERTa backbones \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2011.01154)\]. These models demonstrate notable improvements over existing multilingual baselines, highlighting the importance of language-specific adaptation for effective retrieval in low-resource settings \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2011.01154)\].

Several studies have investigated the development of pre-trained models for Amharic, focusing on semantic models and their application in various NLP tasks \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/2503.18570)\]. For instance, the introduction of Word2Vec embeddings, distributional thesaurus (DT), BERT-like contextual embeddings, and DT embeddings via network embedding algorithms has enabled better performance in tasks such as Named Entity Recognition (NER) and Part-of-Speech (POS) tagging \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/2503.18570)\]. These models have been shown to outperform multilingual models, especially when trained on language-specific data \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/2503.18570)\]. Additionally, the use of contextual embeddings from FLAIR and RoBERTa has demonstrated superior results for NER and POS tagging \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/2503.18570)\].

The challenges of adapting multilingual models to low-resource languages and unseen scripts have also been explored \[[7](http://arxiv.org/abs/2012.15562)\]. Techniques such as matrix factorization and leveraging lexically overlapping tokens have been proposed to improve the performance of models for under-resourced languages \[[7](http://arxiv.org/abs/2012.15562)\]. These methods have shown promise in adapting pretrained models to new languages and scripts with minimal labeled data \[[7](http://arxiv.org/abs/2012.15562)\]. Similarly, the development of dense retrieval models for Amharic has been explored, with studies focusing on training datasets tailored for dense retrieval and evaluating model performance against sparse retrieval approaches \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/2503.18570)\].

The importance of fine-tuning strategies and dataset composition for improving Automatic Speech Recognition (ASR) in low-resource languages has also been emphasized \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/2503.18485)\]. Fine-tuning the Whisper model using datasets such as Mozilla Common Voice and FLEURS has led to significant improvements in transcription accuracy for Amharic \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/2503.18485)\].
