## Related Works

Narrative summarization, particularly for long-form texts such as books, movies, and TV scripts, remains a challenging task due to the complexity of plotlines, character interactions, and thematic coherence \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/1905.13164)\]. Existing approaches often rely on either extractive or abstractive methods, but they struggle with capturing the nuanced structure of narratives \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/2004.12727)\]. For instance, general-purpose extractive models are biased toward sentence positions and often fail to capture the deeper structure of long narratives \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/2004.12727)\]. Similarly, abstractive models may suffer from hallucinations or fail to maintain coherence when summarizing complex texts \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/2410.02603)\].

Several works have attempted to address these challenges by incorporating narrative structure into summarization models. One approach involves explicitly modeling key narrative events or turning points, treating them as latent variables to guide the summarization process \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/2004.12727)\]. This method improves the quality of summaries by ensuring that important narrative elements are included. Another work explores the use of hierarchical Transformers to encode documents in a structured manner, allowing for better cross-document relationship modeling \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/1905.13164)\]. This hierarchical approach enables the model to capture both local and global dependencies, which is crucial for summarizing long narratives.

Plan-guided summarization has also been investigated as a way to improve faithfulness and reduce hallucinations in small language models \[[4](http://arxiv.org/abs/2504.09071)\]. However, this approach has shown limited success in narrative summarization, as plans themselves can contain hallucinations, leading to unfaithful summaries \[[4](http://arxiv.org/abs/2504.09071)\]. This highlights the difficulty of aligning high-level plans with the detailed content of narratives.

To better capture the structure of narratives, some researchers have proposed using multi-agent frameworks, where different agents specialize in specific tasks such as content selection, language generation, and coherence checking \[[9](http://arxiv.org/abs/2401.07324)\]. These frameworks allow for more structured and collaborative summarization, improving both quality and diversity of outputs. For example, the *Agents' Room* framework decomposes narrative writing into subtasks and uses specialized agents to handle each component \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/2410.02603)\]. This modular approach enables more effective collaboration and better handling of complex narrative structures.

Another promising direction is the use of causal graph generation from narrative texts, which aims to capture the underlying causal relationships between events \[[7](http://arxiv.org/abs/2504.07459)\]. This method uses linguistically informed features to identify causal links and constructs interpretable causal graphs, offering a more structured way to represent narrative content. This approach has shown superior performance compared to purely language model-based methods.
