## Related Works

Generative retrieval (GR) has gained significant attention in information retrieval (IR) as an alternative to traditional retrieval paradigms \[[1](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/5beaeee1e27007bd12967480bb9c379cebd15e6e)\]. Unlike conventional methods that rely on index-retrieve-then-rank pipelines, GR models directly generate document identifiers (docids) based on a query, leveraging the power of sequence-to-sequence models \[[1](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/5beaeee1e27007bd12967480bb9c379cebd15e6e)\]. This approach allows for end-to-end optimization of retrieval tasks without explicit indexing \[[13](http://arxiv.org/abs/2404.14851)\]. However, most existing GR models have been primarily evaluated on static document collections, with limited exploration of their performance in dynamic environments \[[1](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/5beaeee1e27007bd12967480bb9c379cebd15e6e)\].

Recent studies have begun to address the challenges of applying GR to dynamic corpora, where document collections evolve over time \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/2305.18952)\]. For instance, \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/2305.18952)\] evaluates the adaptability and robustness of GR models on dynamic scenarios, showing that GR outperforms Dual Encoders (DE) in terms of inference efficiency and adaptability to evolving knowledge. Similarly, \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/2308.14968)\] introduces a continual learning framework for GR, proposing the CLEVER model that enables incremental indexing of new documents while preserving retrieval performance on previously indexed documents. This work highlights the importance of continual learning in dynamic settings \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/2308.14968)\].

The design of docids plays a crucial role in the effectiveness of GR models \[[1](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/5beaeee1e27007bd12967480bb9c379cebd15e6e)\]. While numeric-based docids are often favored for their efficiency, they suffer from poor generalization in dynamic settings due to their tendency to overfit the initial document set \[[1](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/5beaeee1e27007bd12967480bb9c379cebd15e6e)\]. In contrast, text-based docids, which are often derived from document content or titles, exhibit superior performance in dynamic environments due to their semantic alignment with language models' pre-trained knowledge \[[1](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/5beaeee1e27007bd12967480bb9c379cebd15e6e)\]. This is supported by \[[9](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/6682a3679a17d090830635f1c420126d20eae54a)\], which proposes NOVO docids—learnable and interpretable identifiers that are optimized through retrieval tasks to better capture document semantics \[[9](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/6682a3679a17d090830635f1c420126d20eae54a)\].

Furthermore, the granularity and diversity of docid design significantly impact retrieval performance \[[1](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/5beaeee1e27007bd12967480bb9c379cebd15e6e)\]. \[[14](http://arxiv.org/abs/2304.04171)\] introduces GenRet, a method that learns to tokenize documents into short discrete representations (docids) using a discrete auto-encoding approach, enabling more effective and interpretable GR models \[[14](http://arxiv.org/abs/2304.04171)\]. ### <a id='category-generative-retrieval-(gr)-overview-and-prior-work'></a>Generative Retrieval (GR) Overview and Prior Work: 5 papers found.
Generative retrieval (GR) has emerged as a transformative approach in information retrieval, shifting from traditional index-retrieve-rank pipelines to models that directly generate document identifiers (DocIDs) from queries \[[1](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/5beaeee1e27007bd12967480bb9c379cebd15e6e)\]. This paradigm leverages sequence-to-sequence architectures, enabling models to encode queries and generate relevant DocIDs without explicit indexing \[[1](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/5beaeee1e27007bd12967480bb9c379cebd15e6e)\]. Recent work has focused on improving DocID design, training strategies, and scalability, with some studies highlighting the limitations of numeric-based DocIDs in dynamic environments \[[4](http://arxiv.org/abs/2406.01197)\]. For instance, numeric-based DocIDs, while efficient, exhibit poor performance in dynamic corpora due to overfitting on the training set, leading to a tendency to favor initial document sets \[[4](http://arxiv.org/abs/2406.01197)\]. In contrast, text-based DocIDs demonstrate superior generalization, particularly in dynamic settings, owing to their fine-grained design and alignment with language models' pretrained knowledge \[[4](http://arxiv.org/abs/2406.01197)\]. Additionally, research has explored multi-graded relevance in GR, introducing frameworks like GR$^2$ that address challenges in generating distinct and relevant DocIDs \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/2308.14968)\]. These advancements underscore the evolving landscape of GR, emphasizing the need for scalable, efficient, and adaptable models that can handle real-world, evolving document collections \[[5](http://arxiv.org/abs/2409.18409)\].

### <a id='category-empirical-evidence-for-dynamic-gr-improvements'></a>Empirical Evidence for Dynamic GR Improvements: 6 papers found.
Generative retrieval (GR) has been increasingly explored as a promising paradigm in information retrieval, particularly for its ability to dynamically adapt to evolving document collections \[[1](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/5beaeee1e27007bd12967480bb9c379cebd15e6e)\]. However, existing GR models are often evaluated on static corpora, limiting their applicability in real-world scenarios where document sets are continuously updated \[[1](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/5beaeee1e27007bd12967480bb9c379cebd15e6e)\]. Recent studies have highlighted the challenges of adapting GR to dynamic environments, such as the inefficiency of numeric-based docids and the risk of overfitting to initial training data \[[1](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/5beaeee1e27007bd12967480bb9c379cebd15e6e)\]. To address these limitations, researchers have proposed alternative indexing strategies, such as few-shot indexing-based GR frameworks, which eliminate the need for extensive training by prompting large language models to generate docids directly \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/2305.18952)\]. Additionally, the design of docids plays a crucial role in GR performance, with text-based docids demonstrating superior generalization and adaptability due to their fine-grained representation and semantic alignment with pre-trained language models \[[1](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/5beaeee1e27007bd12967480bb9c379cebd15e6e)\]. This has led to the development of novel approaches like the multi-docid design, which combines the efficiency of numeric-based docids with the effectiveness of text-based ones \[[1](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/5beaeee1e27007bd12967480bb9c379cebd15e6e)\]. Furthermore, the use of listwise optimization and positional conditional probability has been introduced to improve the ranking of generated docid lists, enhancing retrieval performance \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/2308.14968)\]. These advancements underscore the ongoing efforts to enhance GR's adaptability, efficiency, and robustness in dynamic settings \[[1](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/5beaeee1e27007bd12967480bb9c379cebd15e6e)\].

### <a id='category-impact-of-docid-design-on-gr-performance'></a>Impact of DocID Design on GR Performance: 3 papers found.
The impact of DocID design on generative retrieval (GR) performance is a critical area of research, with several approaches proposed to optimize document identifiers for better retrieval effectiveness. Traditional methods rely on static, pre-defined DocIDs, such as document titles or clustered BERT embeddings, which often fail to capture the full semantic information of a document \[[14](http://arxiv.org/abs/2304.04171)\]. In contrast, model-based IR approaches, such as NOVO, introduce learnable DocIDs that are optimized through training, enabling better semantic alignment and relationship learning between documents \[[1](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/5beaeee1e27007bd12967480bb9c379cebd15e6e)\]. Similarly, GenRet proposes a discrete auto-encoding framework to learn semantic DocIDs in an end-to-end manner, improving retrieval performance on benchmark datasets \[[14](http://arxiv.org/abs/2304.04171)\]. TSGen further advances this by using term-sets as DocIDs, allowing for permutation-invariant decoding and more reliable retrieval \[[16](http://arxiv.org/abs/2305.13859)\]. These methods highlight the importance of DocID design in GR, as static or poorly optimized identifiers can lead to suboptimal performance, especially in dynamic corpora where document collections evolve continuously \[[1](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/5beaeee1e27007bd12967480bb9c379cebd15e6e)\]. The effectiveness of text-based DocIDs, which align better with language models' pretrained knowledge and offer fine-grained design and lexical diversity, has been demonstrated in various studies \[[1](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/5beaeee1e27007bd12967480bb9c379cebd15e6e)\]. Overall, the design of DocIDs plays a pivotal role in determining the success of generative retrieval systems, influencing both their generalization to unseen documents and their efficiency in dynamic environments \[[14](http://arxiv.org/abs/2304.04171)\].
