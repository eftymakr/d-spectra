## Related Works

Anomaly detection in industrial settings has garnered significant attention due to its critical role in quality control and fault prevention in modern manufacturing processes. Existing approaches have primarily focused on improving accuracy, but recent research has also emphasized the need for models that are lightweight and suitable for deployment on resource-constrained embedded devices \[[10](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/e4d713a97daf5acc250432246b45c9558a5e8b87)\]. For instance, the deployment of deep learning models on embedded systems remains a challenge due to their high computational demands and memory usage \[[8](http://arxiv.org/abs/2403.04932)\]. This has led to the development of memory-efficient architectures, such as the tiled ensemble approach, which enables high-resolution anomaly detection while keeping memory consumption low \[[8](http://arxiv.org/abs/2403.04932)\]. 

Several datasets have been introduced to benchmark industrial anomaly detection models. The MVTec dataset, for example, has been widely used as a benchmark, but recent studies have highlighted its limitations in capturing real-world scenarios \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2403.12580)\]. To address this, the Real-IAD dataset was proposed, offering a larger and more challenging set of images with a broader range of defect types and proportions \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2403.12580)\]. Similarly, the ISP-AD dataset was introduced to address the limitations of existing datasets by including both synthetic and real-world defects, enhancing the generalization of models \[[4](http://arxiv.org/abs/2503.04997)\]. 

In addition to dataset improvements, there has been a growing interest in lightweight models for anomaly detection. The TinyAD framework, for instance, focuses on reducing memory consumption by employing depthwise separable convolutions and memory-efficient inference strategies \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/2303.03611)\]. This is particularly relevant for industrial IoT (IIoT) applications, where models must be deployed on microcontroller units (MCUs) with limited resources \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/2303.03611)\]. Another approach, the P3 AD model, addresses privacy concerns in IIoT by using a federated learning framework to train anomaly detection models without directly sharing sensitive data \[[9](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/fb477df2cfe0ef4c39b4e0bb75bf751e5afcc922)\]. 

Active learning has also been explored as a means to reduce the reliance on large labeled datasets. The P3 AD model, for example, integrates active learning strategies to optimize the use of expert feedback, improving detection performance with fewer labeled samples \[[10](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/e4d713a97daf5acc250432246b45c9558a5e8b87)\]. This is particularly useful in industrial settings where labeled data is scarce and expensive to obtain \[[10](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/e4d713a97daf5acc250432246b45c9558a5e8b87)\]. 

Furthermore, the use of multi-modal data has been explored to improve the robustness of anomaly detection models. The CMDIAD framework, for instance, leverages cross-modal distillation to handle incomplete modalities during inference, allowing models to effectively utilize multimodal information \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/2405.13571)\]. This is especially relevant in industrial applications where only a subset of modalities may be available for each sample \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/2405.13571)\].
