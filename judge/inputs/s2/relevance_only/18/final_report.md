## Related Works

Anomaly detection in industrial settings has garnered significant attention due to its critical role in quality control and fault prevention in modern manufacturing processes. Existing approaches have primarily focused on improving accuracy, but recent research has also emphasized the need for models that are lightweight and suitable for deployment on resource-constrained embedded devices \[[10](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/e4d713a97daf5acc250432246b45c9558a5e8b87)\]. For instance, the deployment of deep learning models on embedded systems remains a challenge due to their high computational demands and memory usage \[[8](http://arxiv.org/abs/2403.04932)\]. This has led to the development of memory-efficient architectures, such as the tiled ensemble approach, which enables high-resolution anomaly detection while keeping memory consumption low \[[8](http://arxiv.org/abs/2403.04932)\]. 

Several datasets have been introduced to benchmark industrial anomaly detection models. The MVTec dataset, for example, has been widely used as a benchmark, but recent studies have highlighted its limitations in capturing real-world scenarios \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2403.12580)\]. To address this, the Real-IAD dataset was proposed, offering a larger and more challenging set of images with a broader range of defect types and proportions \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2403.12580)\]. Similarly, the ISP-AD dataset was introduced to address the limitations of existing datasets by including both synthetic and real-world defects, enhancing the generalization of models \[[4](http://arxiv.org/abs/2503.04997)\]. 

In addition to dataset improvements, there has been a growing interest in lightweight models for anomaly detection. The TinyAD framework, for instance, focuses on reducing memory consumption by employing depthwise separable convolutions and memory-efficient inference strategies \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/2303.03611)\]. This is particularly relevant for industrial IoT (IIoT) applications, where models must be deployed on microcontroller units (MCUs) with limited resources \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/2303.03611)\]. Another approach, the P3 AD model, addresses privacy concerns in IIoT by using a federated learning framework to train anomaly detection models without directly sharing sensitive data \[[9](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/fb477df2cfe0ef4c39b4e0bb75bf751e5afcc922)\]. 

Active learning has also been explored as a means to reduce the reliance on large labeled datasets. The P3 AD model, for example, integrates active learning strategies to optimize the use of expert feedback, improving detection performance with fewer labeled samples \[[10](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/e4d713a97daf5acc250432246b45c9558a5e8b87)\]. This is particularly useful in industrial settings where labeled data is scarce and expensive to obtain \[[10](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/e4d713a97daf5acc250432246b45c9558a5e8b87)\]. 

Furthermore, the use of multi-modal data has been explored to improve the robustness of anomaly detection models. The CMDIAD framework, for instance, leverages cross-modal distillation to handle incomplete modalities during inference, allowing models to effectively utilize multimodal information \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/2405.13571)\]. This is especially relevant in industrial applications where only a subset of modalities may be available for each sample \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/2405.13571)\].

### <a id='category-industrial-anomaly-detection-datasets'></a>Industrial Anomaly Detection Datasets: 4 papers found.
Industrial anomaly detection datasets play a crucial role in advancing research and practical applications in quality control. Existing datasets, such as MVTec and VisA, have been widely used to benchmark anomaly detection methods, but they often suffer from limitations in scale, real-world applicability, and diversity of defect types \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2403.12580)\]. To address these challenges, recent works have introduced larger and more realistic datasets. For instance, the Real-IAD dataset contains 150K high-resolution images, significantly expanding the scope of previous datasets and offering more challenging defect variations \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2403.12580)\]. Similarly, the Industrial Screen Printing Anomaly Detection Dataset (ISP-AD) includes both synthetic and real defects collected directly from factory floors, enhancing the generalization of models in real-world scenarios \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/2303.03611)\]. Another dataset, PCAD, leverages multi-component CAD models to provide high-quality 3D information, enabling more accurate defect detection by considering geometric surface variations \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/2405.13571)\]. These datasets emphasize the need for more realistic and diverse data to improve the performance of anomaly detection models in industrial settings. Additionally, the tiled ensemble approach has been proposed to address the memory constraints associated with high-resolution anomaly detection, allowing for efficient deployment on resource-limited devices \[[4](http://arxiv.org/abs/2503.04997)\]. These advancements highlight the ongoing efforts to create more effective and practical datasets that align with the demands of real-world industrial applications.

[Snippet]: «Monitoring and detecting abnormal events in cyber-physical systems is crucial to industrial production. With the prevalent deployment of the industrial Internet of Things (IIoTs), an enormous amount of time-series data is collected to facilitate machine learning models for anomaly detection, and it is of the utmost importance to directly deploy the trained models on the IIoT devices. However, it is most challenging to deploy complex deep learning models such as convolutional neural networks (CNNs) on these memory-constrained IIoT devices embedded with microcontrollers (MCUs). To alleviate the memory constraints of MCUs, we propose a novel framework named Tiny Anomaly Detection (TinyAD) to efficiently facilitate onboard inference of CNNs for real-time anomaly detection. First, we conduct a comprehensive analysis of depthwise separable CNNs and regular CNNs for anomaly detection and find that the depthwise separable convolution operation can reduce the model size by 50%–90% compared with the traditional CNNs. Then, to reduce the peak memory consumption of CNNs, we explore two complementary strategies, 1) in-place; and 2) patch-by-patch memory rescheduling, and integrate them into a unified framework. The in-place method decreases the peak memory of the depthwise convolution by sparing a temporary buffer to transfer the activation results, while the patch-by-patch method further reduces the peak memory of layer-wise execution by slicing the input data into corresponding receptive fields and executing in order. Furthermore, by adjusting the dimension of convolution filters, these strategies apply to both univariate time series and multidomain time series features. Extensive experiments on real-world industrial datasets show that our framework can reduce peak memory consumption by 2–5× with negligible computation overhead.»

Existing quality control processes combine rapid in-line inspections, such as optical and infrared imaging with high-resolution but time-consuming near-line characterization techniques, including industrial CT and electron microscopy to manually or semi-automatically locate and analyze defects in the production of Li-ion batteries and composite materials. Our findings show that the MTFI pipeline can more effectively utilize incomplete multimodal information compared to applying only a single modality for training and inference. Moreover, we investigate the reasons behind the asymmetric performance improvement using point clouds or RGB images as the main modality of inference. This provides a foundation for our future multimodal dataset construction with additional modalities from manufacturing scenarios.»

[Snippet]: «Payload-based anomaly detection (PAD) model is commonly built upon a big data of normal payload samples, and hence is able to discover zero-day attacks and unknown faults without the need of any negative samples in training phase. But such detection model encounters new challenges to adapt well to the emerging Industrial Internet of Things (IIoT). That is, the modern industrial processes are usually running in a very high complexity, resulting the payloads much more complex and diverse. Further, the industrial data is likely too sensitive to be shared in public, and thus induces a new privacy concern. To tackle these challenges, we propose $(\mathrm( P))^(3)(\mathrm( AD))$ , a novel privacy-preserved payload-based anomaly detection model for IIoT. The basic idea is to train a two-dimensional convolutional neural network (2D-CNN) based auto-encoder using normal payloads over a federated GAN (Generative Adversarial Network) architecture, and then to detect anomalies by an unexpected dissimilarity between the original payloads and the payloads reconstructed by the auto-encoder. By the 2D-CNN, we can model the normal payloads from both the request and response directions simultaneously, and thus have more opportunities to capture the complex and dynamic industrial behaviors that are possibly reflected in the bi-directional network communications. By the GAN, we can train a more generalized auto-encoder that is able to reconstruct more general payload samples without the need to have them in advance for model training. By the federated architecture, we can remove the need of direct sharing of normal payloads, and learn them indirectly by aggregating local models across different industrial data owners, hence ensuring the payload privacy. We have evaluated $(\mathrm( P))^(3)(\mathrm( AD))$ using four public industrial payload datasets as well as considering four typical IIoT PAD scenarios. The detection results achieve more than 0.966 in terms of F1 score for global condition and at least 0.753 for all kinds of federated settings, proving the effectiveness of our $(\mathrm( P))^(3)(\mathrm( AD))$ with privacy preserved.»

[Snippet]: «Anomaly detection is becoming increasingly important and has found its way into manufacturing applications. The potential is seen in use cases such as maintenance cost reduction, machine fault reduction, or increased overall production based on industrial time series data. However, obstacles arise in practice. Supervised algorithms lack limited and expensive labeled training data, and unsupervised algorithms do not have the capabilities for evaluation and tracking. We propose a data-efficient architecture for anomaly detection using energy consumption time series data to address these limitations. To do so, we design an active learning model that optimizes an uns supervised model by integrating budgeted expert feedback. Our solution builds on an autoencoder to leverage latent space representations for an additional supervised feedforward network trained with expert knowledge labels to distinguish between normal data and anomalies. Four different strategies for querying the still-unlabeled data are compared so that the expert’s resources are used efficiently. We validate our concept in an industrial robotic screwdriving application based on energy data for condition monitoring. Findings for the application tested indicate that anomaly detection performance can be significantly increased by 59% for the F1 score with active learning compared to unsupervised models. Furthermore, models trained only on energy consumption data exhibit the same performance as models trained on difficult-to-obtain mechanical process data, thus confirming the practicality of our proposed approach and data efficiency for the use of easily accessible energy data in manufacturing applications. While our approach enables an active learning model to be added to an existing unsupervised model, it allows for straightforward benchmarking and extension to other manufacturing applications.»
