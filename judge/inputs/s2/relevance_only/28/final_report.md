## Related Works

Federated Learning (FL) has emerged as a promising paradigm for collaborative model training while preserving data privacy \[[15](http://arxiv.org/abs/2210.04505)\]. However, existing FL approaches often assume homogeneous data distributions across clients, which is rarely the case in real-world scenarios \[[15](http://arxiv.org/abs/2210.04505)\]. This heterogeneity, both in data distribution and client resources, poses significant challenges to model convergence and accuracy \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2504.01921)\]. To address these issues, several studies have proposed client selection strategies that aim to improve the efficiency and effectiveness of FL training \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2504.01921)\]\[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/2310.00198)\]\[[8](http://arxiv.org/abs/1804.08333)\]. For instance, \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/2310.00198)\] introduces a method that clusters clients based on their data heterogeneity to improve convergence, while \[[8](http://arxiv.org/abs/1804.08333)\] proposes a client selection algorithm that prioritizes clients with better computational capabilities to accelerate training. These methods focus on either data or system heterogeneity but not both simultaneously \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2504.01921)\].

Active Learning (AL) has also been explored in the context of FL, leading to the development of Federated Active Learning (FAL) \[[18](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/6b0d47501ad9efd386837959ea31cd3f58199797)\]. FAL aims to reduce annotation costs by strategically selecting the most informative unlabeled data for annotation, while preserving data privacy. However, existing FAL methods often fail to account for the heterogeneity of data distribution across clients, which can lead to suboptimal model performance \[[18](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/6b0d47501ad9efd386837959ea31cd3f58199797)\]. To address this, \[[18](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/6b0d47501ad9efd386837959ea31cd3f58199797)\] proposes a clustering-based method for labeling unlabeled data in FL, which is particularly useful in scenarios where labeled data is scarce. This approach, however, relies on assumptions about class-prior probabilities, which may not be feasible in all settings \[[18](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/6b0d47501ad9efd386837959ea31cd3f58199797)\].

To further enhance FAL, \[[16](http://arxiv.org/abs/2411.01240)\] introduces a method that selects clients based on the entropy of the aggregated label distribution, ensuring that the global model is exposed to data from all available classes. This approach improves classification accuracy by up to 6% in standard settings and achieves gains of over 30% in scenarios with low participation rates and client dropout \[[16](http://arxiv.org/abs/2411.01240)\]. Additionally, \[[16](http://arxiv.org/abs/2411.01240)\] highlights the flexibility of its method to be combined with existing algorithms, enhancing their performance by more than 40%. This demonstrates the potential of entropy-based client selection in improving model generalization in heterogeneous settings.

WAFFLE uses the Euclidean distance between clients' updates to weigh their individual contributions, minimizing the personalized model loss on the specific agent of interest \[[13](http://arxiv.org/abs/2110.06978)\]. ### <a id='category-client-selection-strategies-in-fal'></a>Client Selection Strategies in FAL: 4 papers found.
Client selection strategies in federated learning (FL) are critical for addressing the challenges posed by data and communication heterogeneity across clients. Existing methods often focus on mitigating either data heterogeneity or communication latency individually, but few address both simultaneously \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2504.01921)\]. For instance, some approaches prioritize selecting clients with more informative updates to improve model convergence, yet they may introduce significant computational overhead or fail in scenarios with diverse data distributions \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/2504.11216)\]. Recent work has introduced hierarchical clustering techniques to estimate client data heterogeneity using model output updates, enabling more adaptive and efficient client sampling \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/2310.00198)\]. Additionally, methods like Power-of-Choice have been proposed to bias client selection toward those with higher local loss, thereby accelerating convergence while maintaining a balance between speed and solution bias \[[4](http://arxiv.org/abs/2206.10032)\]. These strategies highlight the evolving focus on developing client selection mechanisms that are both computationally efficient and robust to real-world FL settings characterized by non-IID data and variable communication delays \[[5](http://arxiv.org/abs/2010.01264)\].

### <a id='category-client-heterogeneity-in-fl-and-its-challenges'></a>Client Heterogeneity in FL and Its Challenges: 8 papers found.
Client heterogeneity in Federated Learning (FL) presents significant challenges due to variations in data distribution, computational capabilities, and model architectures across clients \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2504.01921)\]. These differences can lead to statistical data heterogeneity, where client data is non-identically distributed and imbalanced, thereby affecting the generalization of the global model \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/2504.11216)\]. To address this, several approaches have been proposed. For instance, FedDiverse introduces a client selection algorithm that promotes collaboration between clients with complementary data distributions, enhancing model performance while maintaining low communication overhead \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2504.01921)\]. Similarly, HeteroFL enables the training of heterogeneous local models with varying computation complexities, challenging the assumption that local models must share the same architecture as the global model \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/2504.11216)\]. Additionally, FedCS optimizes client selection based on resource constraints, improving training efficiency in mobile edge computing environments \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/2310.00198)\]. In the context of model heterogeneity, RHFL addresses noisy and heterogeneous clients by aligning models using public data and applying robust loss functions to mitigate label noise \[[4](http://arxiv.org/abs/2206.10032)\]. Furthermore, Hetero-SSFL enables self-supervised learning across heterogeneous clients without requiring labeled data or architectural constraints, allowing for collaborative representation learning \[[5](http://arxiv.org/abs/2010.01264)\]. These works collectively highlight the need for adaptive and efficient strategies to manage client heterogeneity in FL, ensuring robust and scalable model training in decentralized settings \[[6](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/514b57ac2810be1242395b15d5e57635c93635de)\].

Recent works have addressed these issues by introducing variants of the federated averaging (FedAvg) algorithm that incorporate communication compression and partial client participation to improve efficiency and convergence \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/2504.11216)\]. ### <a id='category-personalized-model-training-in-fl'></a>Personalized Model Training in FL: 3 papers found.
The category "Personalized Model Training in FL" explores methods to enhance model performance in federated learning by addressing data heterogeneity and improving personalization. Existing approaches, such as traditional FL frameworks, often struggle with non-i.i.d. data and disjoint classes, leading to suboptimal global model performance \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2504.01921)\]. To overcome these limitations, researchers have proposed personalized FL methods that allow for both global model aggregation and client-specific parameter adaptation. For instance, one method aggregates generic model parameters on the server while retaining task-specific layers on individual clients, enabling better adaptation to local data distributions \[[7](http://arxiv.org/abs/2011.06393)\]. Another approach, WAFFLE, introduces a personalized collaborative learning algorithm that uses stochastic control variates and Euclidean distances to weigh client contributions, achieving faster convergence and improved accuracy on non-identical data distributions \[[13](http://arxiv.org/abs/2110.06978)\]. Additionally, the connection between federated learning and Model Agnostic Meta Learning (MAML) has been explored, highlighting how FL can be viewed as a meta-learning problem where the goal is to adapt models to heterogeneous client data \[[17](http://arxiv.org/abs/1909.12488)\]. These methods collectively emphasize the importance of balancing global model consistency with local personalization, offering promising directions for improving the effectiveness of federated learning in real-world applications.
