## Related Works

De novo genome assembly has been a focal point of computational biology for decades, driven by the need to reconstruct genomes from short, overlapping DNA sequences without a reference. Early approaches relied on overlap-layout-consensus (OLC) methods, which involve comparing all reads pairwise, leading to quadratic computational complexity \[[18](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/7279acd9c61a8de019ea2d55d23124dfbb6b5269)\]. This limitation has spurred the development of de Bruijn graph-based algorithms, which offer more efficient handling of short reads by reducing the problem to graph traversal \[[11](http://arxiv.org/abs/1106.2603)\]. However, these methods still face challenges in terms of memory usage and scalability, particularly for large genomes \[[14](http://arxiv.org/abs/1008.2555)\].

Several studies have addressed the computational and memory bottlenecks in de novo assembly. For instance, SparseAssembler and its successor, SparseAssembler2, introduced sparse k-mer graphs to reduce memory consumption by up to 90% compared to traditional de Bruijn graph-based assemblers \[[11](http://arxiv.org/abs/1106.2603)\]\[[12](http://arxiv.org/abs/1108.3556)\]. These methods skip intermediate k-mers, thereby significantly lowering memory requirements while maintaining assembly quality. Additionally, they incorporate denoising algorithms to mitigate the impact of sequencing errors \[[11](http://arxiv.org/abs/1106.2603)\]. Similarly, SOAPdenovo2 improved upon earlier versions by optimizing memory usage and increasing contig and scaffold lengths, achieving better performance in terms of accuracy and coverage \[[9](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/f8d1052e2c9d1cdd63093427ccc9169e848d777d)\].

Distributed computing frameworks have also played a crucial role in enabling the assembly of large genomes. PPA-assembler, for example, leverages the Pregel framework to perform distributed de Bruijn graph-based assembly, demonstrating superior performance compared to existing assemblers \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/1801.04453)\]. Similarly, HipMER utilizes efficient parallelization of the Meraculous code to handle extreme-scale genome assembly on supercomputers with thousands of cores \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/1705.11147)\]. These approaches highlight the importance of parallelism and distributed memory systems in overcoming the computational demands of de novo assembly.

Recent advancements in hardware-software co-design have further enhanced the efficiency of genome assembly. For example, the diBELLA 2D pipeline introduced distributed-memory algorithms for overlap detection and layout simplification, achieving near-linear scaling with over 80% parallel efficiency \[[6](http://arxiv.org/abs/2010.10055)\]. Similarly, the development of FPGA-based accelerators for genome analysis has shown promise in reducing data movement bottlenecks and improving energy efficiency \[[25](http://arxiv.org/abs/2106.06433)\]. These innovations underscore the growing trend of integrating hardware and software optimizations to address the challenges of de novo assembly.

### <a id='category-comparative-analysis-of-assemblers'></a>Comparative Analysis of Assemblers: 7 papers found.
The category "Comparative Analysis of Assemblers" highlights the ongoing challenges and advancements in de novo genome assembly, particularly in the context of high-throughput sequencing technologies. The integration of genetic linkage maps with de novo assemblies has been proposed as a method to resolve misassemblies and anchor chromosome-scale sequences \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/1705.11147)\]. The development of assemblers like SOAPdenovo2 has significantly improved assembly continuity, accuracy, and coverage, especially in repeat regions, with benchmarking showing its superiority over previous versions and competitiveness with other assemblers \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/1801.04453)\]. Theoretical studies have also explored the parametric complexity of the assembly problem, emphasizing the need for better understanding of repeat complexity, read lengths, and coverage to improve existing assemblers \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/2207.04350)\]. The choice of assembler depends on factors such as read length, genome size, and computational resources, with string-based and overlap-layout-consensus (OLC) assemblers being suitable for short reads and small genomes, while de Bruijn graph-based assemblers are more appropriate for large datasets \[[4](http://arxiv.org/abs/2011.05013)\]. The challenges of generating complete de novo assemblies remain, as short-read technologies are insufficient for resolving structural variations and achieving high-quality genome sequences \[[5](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/c6e319a023f932d6d2ff8897c2c91e56e872db8e)\]. Recent advances in long-read sequencing have enabled near-complete chromosome-level assemblies, but algorithmic and computational challenges persist, necessitating new approaches such as hardware-software co-designs to address memory bottlenecks and improve efficiency \[[6](http://arxiv.org/abs/2010.10055)\].

The category "De Bruijn Graph-Based Algorithms" encompasses a range of approaches aimed at improving the efficiency and accuracy of de novo genome assembly. Recent advancements have focused on reducing computational complexity and memory usage, particularly in the face of growing data volumes and algorithmic challenges \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/2207.04350)\]. For instance, entropy-compressed data structures have been employed to create compact representations of de Bruijn graphs, requiring up to 10 times less storage than traditional methods and offering better scalability in the presence of sequencing errors \[[14](http://arxiv.org/abs/1008.2555)\]. Additionally, quantum-inspired optimization techniques have been explored to address the NP-hard nature of de novo assembly, with experimental results demonstrating potential improvements in efficiency using quantum annealers for both simulated and real data \[[13](http://arxiv.org/abs/2004.06719)\]. Furthermore, tools like ntLink utilize minimizer-based mappings to scaffold genomes efficiently, offering flexibility and computational efficiency by avoiding reliance on read alignments \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/1705.11147)\]. These innovations highlight the ongoing efforts to enhance de Bruijn graph-based algorithms, making them more suitable for large-scale and complex genome assembly tasks.
