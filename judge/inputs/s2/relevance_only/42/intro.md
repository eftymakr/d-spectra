## Related Works

The growing adoption of cloud-native applications has led to increased interest in optimizing costs and resource usage, particularly in containerized environments. Prior research has explored system-level optimization strategies for cost and resource efficiency in containerized systems \[[3](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/2ce38cfb0bf3be6e875f5feb8c4b5e140587bb10)\]. For instance, studies have focused on resource allocation strategies such as auto-scaling, right-sizing, and workload distribution to avoid unnecessary overhead and prevent resource wastage \[[1](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/8335555ae08140dc2fe0da4807040fc88b0cace1)\]. Additionally, cloud management tools and services have been examined for their ability to monitor, analyze, and predict cloud spending \[[1](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/8335555ae08140dc2fe0da4807040fc88b0cace1)\]. These tools include cost management platforms, cost monitoring dashboards, and predictive analytics, which are essential for maintaining cost efficiency in cloud environments \[[1](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/8335555ae08140dc2fe0da4807040fc88b0cace1)\].

FinOps, an emerging discipline that combines financial management and cloud operations, has gained attention for its role in optimizing costs in cloud-based applications \[[17](http://arxiv.org/abs/2307.12479)\]. ABACUS, an automated budget analysis and cloud usage surveillance tool, exemplifies this approach by setting budgets, enforcing them through blocking new deployments, and alerting teams when spending breaches a threshold \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/2501.14753)\]. This tool also leverages Infrastructure-as-Code to provide cost estimates before deployment, enhancing budgetary control \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/2501.14753)\].

In Kubernetes environments, cost optimization remains underexplored, particularly concerning network costs. While native Kubernetes autoscaling mechanisms such as Horizontal Pod Autoscaler (HPA), Vertical Pod Autoscaler (VPA), and Cluster Autoscaler (CA) are widely used, they often fail to strike an optimal balance between resource utilization and application performance \[[3](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/2ce38cfb0bf3be6e875f5feb8c4b5e140587bb10)\]. Recent research has focused on leveraging AI and machine learning techniques to enhance Kubernetes’ resource allocation efficiency \[[3](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/2ce38cfb0bf3be6e875f5feb8c4b5e140587bb10)\]. Predictive scaling algorithms, reinforcement learning-based orchestration, and time-series forecasting models have shown promise in improving resource utilization and reducing operational expenses \[[3](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/2ce38cfb0bf3be6e875f5feb8c4b5e140587bb10)\].

Kubernetes has also been the subject of performance optimization studies. For example, research has aimed to reduce data distribution latency, improve cluster backup and restore strategies, and optimize autoscaling and scheduling strategies to enhance performance and reduce resource overhead \[[4](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/e7d7f1366e96c3970cfdabb69372b774fe9c5e26)\]. Experimental results have demonstrated significant improvements in handling concurrent requests, reducing CPU and memory overhead, and decreasing request failure rates \[[4](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/e7d7f1366e96c3970cfdabb69372b774fe9c5e26)\].

In the context of security, Kubernetes-based multi-cloud architectures have raised concerns about configuration management, network security, and compliance \[[5](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/c60cd2fe9e05152ae366a070ba5d8798f7e64151)\]. CNAPP platforms that integrate cloud security management (CSPM) and Kubernetes security management (KSPM) have been proposed to address these challenges \[[5](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/c60cd2fe9e05152ae366a070ba5d8798f7e64151)\].
