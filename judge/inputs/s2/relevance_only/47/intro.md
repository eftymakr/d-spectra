## Related Works

Multi-Domain Recommendation (MDR) has emerged as a promising approach to improve recommendation performance by leveraging knowledge transfer across different domains \[[10](http://arxiv.org/abs/2202.12524)\]. Traditional MDR methods often assume a shared structure for transferring knowledge, but this can lead to the Negative Transfer Problem (NTP) when there are conflicts between domains or when a domain has poor quality \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2201.05973)\]. The NTP occurs when information from one domain negatively affects the performance in another domain, especially when the domains are dissimilar \[[13](http://arxiv.org/abs/2403.17374)\]. 

Several approaches have been proposed to address the NTP. For instance, \[[7](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/2b031c7170c9dccbe506f3d3a1d6104f65e835ae)\] introduces a framework that uses hierarchical hypergraph networks to model correlative preference transfer, aiming to reduce domain discrepancy in item representations. This method incorporates dynamic item transfer and adaptive user aggregation modules to enhance user representations by capturing high-order connections \[[7](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/2b031c7170c9dccbe506f3d3a1d6104f65e835ae)\]. Similarly, \[[11](http://arxiv.org/abs/1910.05189)\] proposes a dual learning-based approach that iteratively transfers information between two domains to stabilize the learning process and preserve user-item relations across latent spaces. This model, called DDTCDR, demonstrates significant improvements over classical transfer learning approaches \[[11](http://arxiv.org/abs/1910.05189)\].

Another line of research focuses on domain alignment and knowledge disentanglement. \[[5](http://arxiv.org/abs/2308.05508)\] introduces EDDA, a method that separates embedding representations into inter-domain and intra-domain components, enabling more accurate knowledge transfer. Additionally, \[[12](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/cdc38867ade499e320e4ed3f93473a7cd67ecad4)\] proposes AMLCDR, which aligns user preference distributions across domains by incorporating a domain adaptation network and a meta-learning network to reduce user preference loss. This approach addresses the issue of misalignment between user and item embeddings in the target domain \[[12](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/cdc38867ade499e320e4ed3f93473a7cd67ecad4)\].

In the context of cross-domain sequential recommendation, \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/2311.13188)\] presents a framework that uses cooperative game theory to assess the extent of negative transfer between domains and adaptively assigns weights to prediction losses. This method also employs a hierarchical contrastive learning approach to improve domain alignment at different levels of abstraction \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/2311.13188)\]. Similarly, \[[3](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/756e3e6c0956d1daa19367eb13115151b20eeb7f)\] introduces MSDCR, which treats multiple domains as sparse and uses adversarial training and a gated aspect preference enhancement module to preserve domain-specific preferences while leveraging complementary information from other domains \[[3](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/756e3e6c0956d1daa19367eb13115151b20eeb7f)\].

To further address the challenges of sparse overlapping users and items, \[[4](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/161f9d8c3694ed32e07a716c63c109d60bf2d4ee)\] proposes a tag tree-guided multi-grained alignment method that exploits the structure of shared tags to enable more accurate domain alignment. This approach integrates publisher and tag nodes into a user-video bipartite graph, facilitating cross-domain alignment through graph propagation \[[4](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/161f9d8c3694ed32e07a716c63c109d60bf2d4ee)\].

In summary, existing methods for MDR and CDR have explored various strategies to mitigate NTP
