## Related Works

3D human pose estimation has seen significant advancements with the integration of deep learning and transformer-based architectures. Early approaches primarily relied on convolutional neural networks (CNNs) to directly regress from image to 3D pose, often ignoring the dependencies between human joints \[[23](http://arxiv.org/abs/1605.05180)\]. More recent methods have incorporated graph convolutional networks (GCNs) to model spatial relationships between body parts, while others have adopted self-attention mechanisms to capture long-range dependencies \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2203.13387)\]. However, these methods often struggle with modeling both local and global interactions effectively, especially in complex and occluded scenarios \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/2505.01003)\].

Transformers have been increasingly adopted due to their ability to model long-range dependencies in the spatial and temporal domains \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2203.13387)\]. For instance, CrossFormer introduces two interaction modules—Cross-Joint Interaction and Cross-Frame Interaction—to explicitly encode local and global dependencies between body joints, achieving state-of-the-art performance on Human3.6M and MPI-INF-3DHP datasets \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2203.13387)\]. Similarly, HDFormer leverages high-order attention mechanisms to capture "joint-joint", "bone-joint", and "hyperbone-joint" interactions, improving performance in complex and occlusion-heavy situations \[[5](http://arxiv.org/abs/2302.01825)\]. These methods demonstrate the importance of modeling both spatial and temporal dependencies for accurate 3D pose estimation.

Despite these advancements, existing methods often face challenges in capturing long-range dependencies without introducing uncorrelated noise or increasing model size \[[30](http://arxiv.org/abs/1711.08585)\]. For example, some approaches rely on deepening networks to learn dependencies between non-linked parts, which can lead to computational inefficiencies and reduced generalization \[[30](http://arxiv.org/abs/1711.08585)\]. To address these issues, the proposed PGFormer utilizes a pyramid structure to better learn potential long-range dependencies by capturing correlations across joints and groups in a cross-scale manner \[[30](http://arxiv.org/abs/1711.08585)\]. This approach complements the context of human sub-structures and enables efficient modeling of long-range dependencies without excessive parameter usage.

Additionally, several works have explored the integration of spatial and temporal modeling capacities to improve efficiency and accuracy. For example, the Graph and Skipped Transformer (G-SFormer) architecture exploits spatio-temporal information through a global approach, achieving superior performance with significantly reduced computational complexity \[[18](http://arxiv.org/abs/2407.02990)\]. Similarly, the Dual-Path Transformer (DualFormer) models long-range dependencies of both joint sequences and limb sequences by incorporating motion information and dual-path interactions \[[17](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/bee348fbb077d48feab495c4d1e47cde7463f61b)\]. These methods highlight the importance of combining spatial and temporal modeling to enhance performance in 3D human pose estimation.
