## Related Works

3D human pose estimation has seen significant advancements with the integration of deep learning and transformer-based architectures. Early approaches primarily relied on convolutional neural networks (CNNs) to directly regress from image to 3D pose, often ignoring the dependencies between human joints \[[23](http://arxiv.org/abs/1605.05180)\]. More recent methods have incorporated graph convolutional networks (GCNs) to model spatial relationships between body parts, while others have adopted self-attention mechanisms to capture long-range dependencies \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2203.13387)\]. However, these methods often struggle with modeling both local and global interactions effectively, especially in complex and occluded scenarios \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/2505.01003)\].

Transformers have been increasingly adopted due to their ability to model long-range dependencies in the spatial and temporal domains \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2203.13387)\]. For instance, CrossFormer introduces two interaction modules—Cross-Joint Interaction and Cross-Frame Interaction—to explicitly encode local and global dependencies between body joints, achieving state-of-the-art performance on Human3.6M and MPI-INF-3DHP datasets \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2203.13387)\]. Similarly, HDFormer leverages high-order attention mechanisms to capture "joint-joint", "bone-joint", and "hyperbone-joint" interactions, improving performance in complex and occlusion-heavy situations \[[5](http://arxiv.org/abs/2302.01825)\]. These methods demonstrate the importance of modeling both spatial and temporal dependencies for accurate 3D pose estimation.

Despite these advancements, existing methods often face challenges in capturing long-range dependencies without introducing uncorrelated noise or increasing model size \[[30](http://arxiv.org/abs/1711.08585)\]. For example, some approaches rely on deepening networks to learn dependencies between non-linked parts, which can lead to computational inefficiencies and reduced generalization \[[30](http://arxiv.org/abs/1711.08585)\]. To address these issues, the proposed PGFormer utilizes a pyramid structure to better learn potential long-range dependencies by capturing correlations across joints and groups in a cross-scale manner \[[30](http://arxiv.org/abs/1711.08585)\]. This approach complements the context of human sub-structures and enables efficient modeling of long-range dependencies without excessive parameter usage.

Additionally, several works have explored the integration of spatial and temporal modeling capacities to improve efficiency and accuracy. For example, the Graph and Skipped Transformer (G-SFormer) architecture exploits spatio-temporal information through a global approach, achieving superior performance with significantly reduced computational complexity \[[18](http://arxiv.org/abs/2407.02990)\]. Similarly, the Dual-Path Transformer (DualFormer) models long-range dependencies of both joint sequences and limb sequences by incorporating motion information and dual-path interactions \[[17](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/bee348fbb077d48feab495c4d1e47cde7463f61b)\]. These methods highlight the importance of combining spatial and temporal modeling to enhance performance in 3D human pose estimation.

### <a id='category-transformer-based-approaches-for-pose-estimation'></a>Transformer-Based Approaches for Pose Estimation: 5 papers found.
Recent advancements in transformer-based approaches for 3D human pose estimation have focused on capturing both spatial and temporal dependencies through novel architectural designs. These methods leverage the self-attention mechanism of transformers to model long-range interactions between joints, addressing challenges such as the need for precise inter-feature representation and the integration of multi-view data \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2203.13387)\]. For instance, CrossFormer introduces two interaction modules—Cross-Joint Interaction and Cross-Frame Interaction—to explicitly encode local and global dependencies between body joints, achieving state-of-the-art performance on datasets like Human3.6 and MPI-INF-3DHP \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2203.13387)\]. Similarly, a multi-stage framework has been proposed to handle 3D sequence-to-sequence pose detection by combining spatial and temporal modules, utilizing self-attention to reduce interference from non-human elements and improve efficiency \[[10](http://arxiv.org/abs/2401.16700)\]. The DualFormer network further enhances performance by incorporating multi-level human knowledge and motion information through a dual-path transformer framework, which models both joint and limb sequences while improving feature robustness \[[17](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/bee348fbb077d48feab495c4d1e47cde7463f61b)\]. Additionally, the LPFormer framework introduces an end-to-end approach using LiDAR data, eliminating the need for 2D annotations and enabling direct 3D pose estimation \[[20](http://arxiv.org/abs/2306.12525)\]. Another notable work, KTPFormer, enhances transformer-based methods by integrating kinematic and trajectory prior knowledge through specialized attention modules, enabling simultaneous modeling of spatial and temporal correlations \[[24](http://arxiv.org/abs/2404.00658)\]. These approaches collectively highlight the growing trend of using transformers to address the complexities of 3D human pose estimation, emphasizing the importance of both structural and motion-aware modeling.

### <a id='category-3d-human-pose-estimation-methods'></a>3D Human Pose Estimation Methods: 21 papers found.
The related works in 3D human pose estimation focus on addressing challenges such as modeling long-range dependencies, capturing spatial and temporal relationships, and improving accuracy while reducing computational costs. Existing methods often rely on Transformers or Graph Convolutional Networks (GCNs) to model these dependencies, but they face limitations such as ignoring spatial relationships or failing to capture local temporal patterns \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2203.13387)\]. To overcome these issues, recent approaches have introduced novel architectures that integrate graph modeling with attention mechanisms, such as the Graph Order Attention module \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2203.13387)\], multi-hop graph convolutional networks \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/2505.01003)\], and high-order attention modules \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/2405.03055)\]. These methods aim to better capture complex interactions between joints and body parts by incorporating multi-scale and multi-order dependencies. Additionally, some works have focused on improving efficiency by combining CNN techniques with Transformers \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/2405.03055)\], or by leveraging spatiotemporal graph structures \[[4](http://arxiv.org/abs/1708.01101)\]. Others have emphasized the importance of self-attention mechanisms that are aware of the central pose \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2203.13387)\], or have introduced dual-stream models to integrate local and global features \[[5](http://arxiv.org/abs/2302.01825)\]. Furthermore, methods like the Spatio-Temporal GraphFormer \[[6](http://arxiv.org/abs/2308.05298)\] and the multi-view 3D HPE framework \[[7](http://arxiv.org/abs/2412.19676)\] have been proposed to enhance performance by exploiting spatiotemporal information and multi-view semantic features. These advancements highlight the ongoing efforts to improve accuracy, efficiency, and robustness in 3D human pose estimation.
