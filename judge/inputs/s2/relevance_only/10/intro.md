## Related Works

Multi-token prediction (MTP) has emerged as a promising pre-training objective for language models, offering potential improvements in downstream performance, inference speed, and training efficiency compared to traditional next-token prediction (NTP) \[[5](http://arxiv.org/abs/2404.19737)\]. While MTP involves predicting multiple future tokens at each step using multiple prediction heads, its effectiveness varies across model sizes. Smaller language models (SLMs) have been shown to struggle with the MTP objective, prompting the development of curriculum learning strategies to address this limitation \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2505.17505)\].

Curriculum learning has been widely explored in various domains, including natural language understanding and code language models. For instance, curriculum learning has been shown to significantly improve the accuracy of small decoder-only code language models on code execution tasks, although its impact on code completion is less pronounced \[[8](http://arxiv.org/abs/2407.10194)\]. Similarly, in natural language understanding, curriculum learning has been proposed as a method to arrange training examples in an easy-to-difficult order, leading to significant performance improvements across a wide range of tasks without requiring manual model architecture design or external data \[[9](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/2d315208dd88eed245dc4ae1ae398af75aa291f8)\]. These findings suggest that curriculum learning can be a valuable strategy for improving the effectiveness of MTP training.

The concept of curriculum learning for MTP training has been explored in two variants: a forward curriculum, which gradually increases the complexity of the pre-training objective from NTP to MTP, and a reverse curriculum, which does the opposite \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2505.17505)\]. The forward curriculum enables SLMs to better leverage the MTP objective during pre-training, improving downstream NTP performance and generative output quality while retaining the benefits of self-speculative decoding. In contrast, the reverse curriculum achieves stronger NTP performance and output quality but fails to provide any self-speculative decoding benefits \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2505.17505)\].

The benefits of MTP have also been studied in the context of speech LLMs, where multi-token prediction has been applied to improve generation speed and quality. VocalNet, a series of high-performance, low-latency speech LLMs, demonstrates that MTP can lead to significant improvements in both generation speed and quality compared to standard NTP \[[6](http://arxiv.org/abs/2504.04060)\]. Additionally, research on multi-token prediction in multimodal settings has shown that NTP can be extended to encapsulate information from different modalities, enabling the development of multimodal models that can understand and generate content across multiple modalities \[[4](http://arxiv.org/abs/2412.18619)\].
