## Related Works

The field of large language model (LLM) training and optimization has seen significant advancements, particularly in the areas of distributed training, performance modeling, and efficient deployment. Early work on distributed training focused on improving scalability and reducing communication overhead, with notable contributions from frameworks like Megatron-LM \[[17](http://arxiv.org/abs/1909.08053)\], which introduced model parallelism to train multi-billion parameter models efficiently. This work laid the foundation for subsequent research on distributed training, emphasizing the importance of memory management and computational efficiency.

Recent studies have also explored the challenges of training LLMs in complex environments, such as the need for accurate performance models to guide optimization efforts. For instance, the paper on LLM inference \[[8](http://arxiv.org/abs/2402.16363)\] provides a comprehensive survey of efficient inference techniques, highlighting the importance of understanding memory access patterns and computational bottlenecks. This work emphasizes the need for hardware-aware methods to improve inference speed and reduce memory usage, which is critical for deploying LLMs on devices with limited resources.

The importance of performance modeling in LLM training is further underscored by the work on LLM-ProS \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/2502.04355)\], which evaluates the performance of LLMs on competitive programming tasks. This study highlights the variability in model performance across different tasks and the impact of training methodologies on generalization. Similarly, the paper on instruction-tuned LLMs \[[14](http://arxiv.org/abs/2408.16440)\] demonstrates how fine-tuning on domain-specific instructions can significantly improve performance in specialized areas such as medical translation, emphasizing the role of task-specific training in enhancing model capabilities.

In addition, the paper on visual instruction tuning \[[9](http://arxiv.org/abs/2304.08485)\] introduces a method for generating multimodal instruction-following data, enabling LLMs to handle visual inputs effectively. This work highlights the potential of multimodal models in tasks requiring both language and visual understanding. The study on teaching-assistant-in-the-loop \[[12](http://arxiv.org/abs/2406.05322)\] further explores the challenges of knowledge distillation, proposing a framework to improve sample efficiency and reduce the impact of imperfect teacher models.
