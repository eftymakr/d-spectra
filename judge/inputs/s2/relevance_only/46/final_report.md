## Related Works

Cross-domain sequential recommendation (CDSR) has gained significant attention in recent years due to its ability to alleviate data sparsity and cold-start problems by leveraging user interactions across multiple domains \[[1](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/27670ed18e96550c674e022e284fc5356a7458c2)\]. Traditional approaches to CDSR often rely on self-attention mechanisms within transformer-based models, such as SASRec and Bert4Rec, to capture sequential dependencies \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/2407.11245)\]. However, these methods typically inject domain-specific components as auxiliary modules without thoroughly exploring the potential of the core self-attention mechanism \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/2401.04971)\]. 

Several works have attempted to improve CDSR by incorporating domain-aware modules. For example, \[[4](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/96bfe33a2503e960153a934e10be91da4d8b13a6)\] introduces a shared proxy item encoder to generate universal representations for items across domains, while \[[5](http://arxiv.org/abs/2405.12473)\] proposes a time-interval-aware attention encoder to better model sequential behavior. Additionally, \[[6](http://arxiv.org/abs/2311.04760)\] develops a contrastive learning auxiliary task to enhance cross-domain sequence representation. These methods primarily focus on domain alignment and transfer learning, but they often overlook the importance of dynamically adjusting attention scores to mitigate negative transfer \[[7](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/10715bc315c7c1075b5bf35e603fc08aaf5dff36)\].

Another line of research explores cooperative learning between single- and cross-domain sequential recommendation tasks. For instance, \[[8](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/6dd0605f7de7581c69b4acf0ab78000748a09f3e)\] introduces a cooperative learning framework that compares the performance of models trained on multiple domains with those trained on specific domains to estimate the degree of negative transfer. Similarly, \[[9](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/11da511e9baafc1dfdf58a287c1ead787c9345bc)\] proposes an auxiliary loss that maximizes mutual information between representation pairs from different tasks. These approaches aim to improve knowledge transfer but may not fully address the challenges of aligning item representations and capturing inter-sequence relationships \[[10](http://arxiv.org/abs/2304.03891)\].

This work highlights the importance of aligning item representations to enhance sequential modeling and user preference alignment. Furthermore, \[[12](http://arxiv.org/abs/2311.04590)\] introduces a contrastive cross-domain sequential recommendation technique with an attention-aware mechanism to better capture user preferences across domains. These methods emphasize the need for more sophisticated attention mechanisms and better modeling of inter-sequence relationships \[[13](http://arxiv.org/abs/2410.13588)\].

### <a id='category-recent-advances-in-cross-domain-sequential-recommendation'></a>Recent Advances in Cross-Domain Sequential Recommendation: 25 papers found.
Cross-domain sequential recommendation (CDSR) has emerged as a critical research direction in recommendation systems, aiming to leverage sequential interactions across multiple domains to improve prediction accuracy \[[1](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/27670ed18e96550c674e022e284fc5356a7458c2)\]. Existing approaches often rely on domain-specific modules or self-attention mechanisms to capture cross-domain correlations, but they typically overlook the inherent power of self-attention in learning behavioral correlations \[[1](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/27670ed18e96550c674e022e284fc5356a7458c2)\]. For instance, some methods focus on aligning user and item representations across domains using attention mechanisms \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/2401.04971)\], while others incorporate time-aware attention to model temporal dynamics \[[4](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/96bfe33a2503e960153a934e10be91da4d8b13a6)\]. Additionally, contrastive learning has been utilized to enhance cross-domain knowledge transfer by emphasizing the importance of items in auxiliary domains \[[1](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/27670ed18e96550c674e022e284fc5356a7458c2)\]. However, these methods often struggle with data sparsity and cold-start issues, especially when dealing with non-overlapping users or sparse interaction sequences \[[5](http://arxiv.org/abs/2405.12473)\]. To address these limitations, recent works have proposed frameworks that integrate multi-domain information more effectively, such as the Decoupled Representation via Extraction Attention Module (DREAM), which simultaneously learns single- and cross-domain preferences \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/2401.04971)\]. Moreover, graph-based models have been introduced to capture collaborative relationships and temporal context, such as the time-interval-aware attention encoder \[[1](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/27670ed18e96550c674e022e284fc5356a7458c2)\]. Despite these advancements, challenges remain in aligning item representations across domains and capturing complex sequential patterns \[[4](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/96bfe33a2503e960153a934e10be91da4d8b13a6)\]. The proposed AutoCDSR framework addresses these issues by enhancing the self-attention mechanism to dynamically minimize cross-domain attention scores, thereby improving knowledge transfer efficiency \[[1](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/27670ed18e96550c674e022e284fc5356a7458c2)\].
