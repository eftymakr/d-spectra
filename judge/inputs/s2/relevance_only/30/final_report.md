## Related Works

Modern databases often trade strong transaction isolation for weak isolation guarantees to achieve low latency, high throughput, and partition tolerance \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2504.02344)\]. This trade-off has led to the emergence of isolation bugs in production databases, which can break data-consistency contracts \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2504.02344)\]. Black-box testing has become a prominent technique for detecting such bugs by verifying whether transaction histories adhere to prescribed isolation levels \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2504.02344)\]. However, testing large-scale databases remains challenging due to the computational complexity of existing isolation checkers \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2504.02344)\].

Several works have explored the complexity of checking transactional consistency. It has been shown that strong isolation levels such as serializability and snapshot isolation are NP-complete in general \[[8](http://arxiv.org/abs/1908.04509)\]. In contrast, weak isolation levels like read committed, read atomic, and causal consistency are polynomial-time checkable \[[8](http://arxiv.org/abs/1908.04509)\]. This distinction has enabled the development of scalable testing tools for weak isolation levels \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2504.02344)\]. However, existing testers often have large polynomial complexity, which limits their applicability to moderate-sized workloads \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2504.02344)\].

To address these limitations, researchers have proposed various approaches. For instance, MTC introduces Mini-Transactions (MTs) to reduce end-to-end checking overhead during history generation and verification \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2504.02344)\]. MTs are compact transactions that execute faster than general workloads, enabling efficient verification of strong isolation levels in linear or quadratic time \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2504.02344)\]. Similarly, Viper presents a fast snapshot isolation checker that is sound, complete, and efficient, leveraging BC-polygraphs and heuristic pruning to accelerate checking \[[6](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/9f96a48d2bc0e77004b32960495685be6c301c63)\]. 

Plume, on the other hand, focuses on weak isolation levels and introduces a novel combination of vectors and tree clocks to accelerate black-box checking \[[2](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/cbe191bda20907994d45bc835d20446968370add)\]. It is the first efficient and complete black-box checker for weak isolation levels, capable of detecting new isolation bugs in production databases \[[2](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/cbe191bda20907994d45bc835d20446968370add)\]. Additionally, MonkeyDB provides a mock storage system for testing correctness under weak isolation levels by using a logical specification of the isolation level to compute possible return values for read operations \[[4](http://arxiv.org/abs/2103.02830)\]. This approach ensures good coverage of weak behaviors, which is complete in the limit \[[4](http://arxiv.org/abs/2103.02830)\].

Efforts to formalize weak isolation levels have also been pursued. For example, a paper formalizes three weak isolation levels—read uncommitted, read committed, and snapshot isolation—in separation logic \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/2501.14421)\]. This formalization enables modular and implementation-independent specifications that correctly prohibit certain execution patterns \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/2501.14421)\]. Furthermore, the paper demonstrates that an executable implementation of a key-value database satisfies snapshot isolation specifications \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/2501.14421)\].

[Snippet]: «Transactional isolation guarantees are crucial for database correctness. However, recent studies have uncovered numerous isolation bugs in production databases. The common black-box approach to isolation checking stresses databases with large, concurrent, randomized transaction workloads and verifies whether the resulting execution histories satisfy specified isolation levels. For strong isolation levels such as strict serializability, serializability, and snapshot isolation, this approach often incurs significant end-to-end checking overhead during both history generation and verification. We address these inefficiencies through the novel design of Mini-Transactions (MTs). MTs are compact, short transactions that execute much faster than general workloads, reducing overhead during history generation by minimizing database blocking and transaction retries. By leveraging MTs' read-modify-write pattern, we develop highly efficient algorithms to verify strong isolation levels in linear or quadratic time. Despite their simplicity, MTs are semantically rich and effectively capture common isolation anomalies described in the literature. We implement our verification algorithms and an MT workload generator in a tool called MTC. Experimental results show that MTC outperforms state-of-the-art tools in both history generation and verification. Moreover, MTC can detect bugs across various isolation levels in production databases while maintaining the effectiveness of randomized testing with general workloads, making it a cost-effective solution for black-box isolation checking.»

[Snippet]: «Snapshot isolation (SI) is supported by most commercial databases and is widely used by applications. However, checking SI today---given a set of transactions, checking if they obey SI---is either slow or gives up soundness. We present viper, an SI checker that is sound, complete, and fast. Viper checks black-box databases and hence is transparent to both users and databases. To be fast, viper introduces BC-polygraphs, a new representation of transaction dependencies. Viper also introduces heuristic pruning, an optimization to accelerate checking SI by leveraging common knowledge of real-world database implementations. Besides vanilla SI, viper supports major SI variants including Strong SI, Generalized SI, and Strong Session SI. Our experiments show that given the same time budget, viper improves over baselines by 15× in the workload sizes being checked.»
