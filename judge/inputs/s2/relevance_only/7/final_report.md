## Related Works

Recent advancements in large language models (LLMs) have focused on aligning model behavior with human preferences and improving the robustness of model outputs. Traditional approaches to identifying refusal behaviors in LLMs often rely on predefined templates detectable in output tokens or require manual analysis \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2309.07915)\]. These methods are limited in their ability to generalize across different alignment conditions and may fail in adversarial settings \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/2505.20977)\]. 

To address these limitations, several works have explored the use of in-context learning (ICL) to improve the interpretability and controllability of LLMs. For instance, MMICL introduces a novel approach to enable vision-language models (VLMs) to handle complex multi-modal prompts more effectively by enhancing their in-context learning capabilities \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/2505.15436)\]. Similarly, the MC² benchmark evaluates modality preferences in MLLMs, revealing that all tested models exhibit clear modality preferences that can be controlled through instruction guidance \[[4](http://arxiv.org/abs/2311.04589)\]. These findings suggest that LLMs can be steered toward desired behaviors by leveraging their internal representations and modality-specific biases.

Another line of research focuses on improving the reasoning capabilities of LLMs through multimodal reasoning frameworks. Multimodal-CoT incorporates both text and vision modalities into a two-stage framework, separating rationale generation from answer inference, thereby improving the accuracy of complex reasoning tasks \[[5](http://arxiv.org/abs/2411.16260)\]. Similarly, the LXMERT framework learns cross-modality encoder representations from Transformers, enabling models to understand and align visual and language semantics \[[6](http://arxiv.org/abs/2407.00902)\]. These approaches demonstrate the potential of multimodal reasoning to enhance the performance of LLMs on complex tasks.

In addition to improving reasoning, recent work has also focused on enhancing the interpretability and alignment of LLMs. The RDA-Driver model integrates reasoning and decision-making processes, ensuring that the generated outputs are consistent with the underlying reasoning steps \[[7](http://arxiv.org/abs/2408.13890)\]. Furthermore, the SciInstruct dataset introduces a self-reflective instruction framework to improve the scientific reasoning capabilities of LLMs, enabling them to derive symbolic equations and solve numerical calculations \[[8](http://arxiv.org/abs/2302.00923)\]. These methods highlight the importance of structured instruction and self-reflection in improving the reliability and performance of LLMs.

The challenge of aligning LLMs with human preferences has also been addressed through preference learning frameworks. A survey on preference learning for LLMs outlines various strategies for aligning model outputs with human preferences, decomposing them into model, data, feedback, and algorithm components \[[9](http://arxiv.org/abs/2401.07950)\]. This unified view provides a structured approach to understanding and improving the alignment of LLMs with human values.
