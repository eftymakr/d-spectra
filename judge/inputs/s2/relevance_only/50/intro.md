## Related Works

The rapid growth of open-source machine learning (ML) resources has significantly impacted information retrieval (IR) research, as noted in the paper’s abstract \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2502.04484)\]. However, existing platforms like Hugging Face lack structured representations, which limits advanced querying and analysis \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2502.04484)\]. This section reviews related works that address similar challenges, focusing on dataset documentation, model evolution, and the use of knowledge graphs for resource management.

Several studies have examined the documentation and structure of datasets on Hugging Face. For instance, \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/2401.13822)\] conducted a large-scale analysis of 7,433 dataset cards and found that while dataset descriptions and structures are well-documented, sections like "Considerations for Using the Data" are often underdeveloped. This highlights a gap in comprehensive documentation that could hinder effective resource management and retrieval. Similarly, \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/2311.13380)\] analyzed the evolution of ML models on Hugging Face and found that maintenance practices vary significantly, with some models receiving frequent updates while others remain static. This variability underscores the need for structured representations to track changes and improve traceability.

The importance of structured data for advanced querying has also been emphasized in other works. For example, \[[4](http://arxiv.org/abs/2402.07323)\] discussed the challenges of carbon emissions reporting for ML models on Hugging Face, noting that only a small proportion of models provide detailed emissions data. This lack of structured information limits the ability to perform comparative analyses or recommend models based on environmental impact. Additionally, \[[5](http://arxiv.org/abs/2305.11164)\] explored the carbon footprint of 1,417 ML models and found correlations between model size, dataset size, and emissions, reinforcing the need for standardized metrics and structured representations.

The use of knowledge graphs for managing ML resources has been explored in a few studies. For example, \[[6](http://arxiv.org/abs/2005.00687)\] introduced the Open Graph Benchmark (OGB), a set of benchmark datasets for graph-based ML tasks. While OGB focuses on graph data, it shares similarities with HuggingKG in its goal of providing structured representations for scalable and reproducible research. Similarly, \[[7](http://arxiv.org/abs/2307.14841)\] discussed the challenges of empirical studies on Hugging Face, emphasizing the need for better data organization and standardized reporting practices. These works align with the goals of HuggingKG and HuggingBench by advocating for more structured and accessible ML resources.
