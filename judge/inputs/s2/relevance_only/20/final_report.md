## Related Works

Deep-unrolling and plug-and-play (PnP) approaches have emerged as dominant methods for solving inverse problems in imaging, particularly in single-pixel imaging (SPI) \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/1809.04693)\]. PnP algorithms leverage off-the-shelf deep denoisers to implicitly perform regularization within iterative frameworks such as ISTA or ADMM \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/2411.01955)\]. These methods are flexible for varying compression ratios (CRs) but often suffer from limited reconstruction accuracy and speed \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/2407.01469)\]. In contrast, unrolling approaches transform truncated iterative optimization processes into end-to-end trainable neural networks, typically achieving better accuracy and faster inference \[[4](http://arxiv.org/abs/2107.11007)\]. However, they require fine-tuning or retraining when CR changes, limiting their adaptability \[[5](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/c8cd06d5d37c8d096acbead74ff1085091e1bc24)\].

Several works have explored the integration of deep learning with traditional optimization algorithms to improve image restoration. For instance, the work by \[[6](http://arxiv.org/abs/2012.05703)\] proposed an algorithm unrolling approach to deep image deblurring, connecting iterative algorithms to neural network architectures. Similarly, \[[7](http://arxiv.org/abs/2002.09611)\] introduced a dynamic proximal unrolling network (DPUNet) that can handle multiple compressive imaging modalities without retraining, demonstrating adaptability to varying sampling ratios and noise levels \[[8](http://arxiv.org/abs/2104.10348)\]. The use of deep neural networks for image restoration has also been studied in the context of hyperspectral unmixing, where unrolling plug-and-play algorithms was shown to improve performance while maintaining interpretability \[[9](http://arxiv.org/abs/1704.03488)\].

The convergence properties of PnP algorithms have been extensively studied. For example, \[[10](http://arxiv.org/abs/2312.16024)\] established both fixed-point and objective convergence for PnP algorithms using linear denoisers, showing that these methods can guarantee convergence under certain conditions \[[11](http://arxiv.org/abs/2311.13682)\]. Additionally, \[[12](http://arxiv.org/abs/1902.05399)\] proposed a tuning-free PnP proximal algorithm that automatically determines internal parameters, improving the robustness of the method across varying imaging conditions \[[13](http://arxiv.org/abs/2501.03780)\]. This approach was further extended to nonlinear inverse problems, demonstrating its versatility in applications such as compressed sensing MRI and phase retrieval \[[14](http://arxiv.org/abs/2402.12872)\].

In the context of algorithm unrolling, \[[15](http://arxiv.org/abs/2505.15318)\] introduced a framework for deep unrolling of the Half-Quadratic Splitting (HQS) algorithm, ensuring convergence as the number of layers increases \[[16](http://arxiv.org/abs/1902.03493)\]. This work demonstrated that unrolled networks can achieve competitive performance while maintaining the convergence guarantees of their underlying algorithms \[[17](http://arxiv.org/abs/2312.15788)\]. Similarly, \[[18](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/b954a94af132294a4a4e2efce15027758a535742)\] proposed a convergent unrolled network for image deconvolution, showing that the method outperforms traditional HQS algorithms and achieves linear convergence \[[19](http://arxiv.org/abs/2409.04719)\].

Additionally, the integration of attention mechanisms and dynamic convolutions in unrolled architectures has further improved the ability to model multiscale information and learn pairwise similarity structures from data \[[5](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/c8cd06d5d37c8d096acbead74ff1085091e1bc24)\]. [Snippet]: «Deep unrolling, or unfolding, is an emerging learning-to-optimize method that unrolls a truncated iterative algorithm in the layers of a trainable neural network. However, the convergence guarantees and generalizability of the unrolled networks are still open theoretical problems. To tackle these problems, we provide deep unrolled architectures with a stochastic descent nature by imposing descending constraints during training. The descending constraints are forced layer by layer to ensure that each unrolled layer takes, on average, a descent step toward the optimum during training. We theoretically prove that the sequence constructed by the outputs of the unrolled layers is then guaranteed to converge for in-distribution problems. We then analyze the generalizability to certain out-of-distribution (OOD) shifts in the optimization problems being solved. Our analysis shows that the descending nature imposed by the proposed constraints is transferable under these distribution shifts, subject to a generalization error, thereby providing the unrolled networks with OOD robustness. We numerically assess unrolled architectures trained with the proposed constraints in two different applications, including the sparse coding using learnable iterative shrinkage and thresholding algorithm (LISTA) and image inpainting using proximal generative flow (GLOW-Prox), and demonstrate the performance and robustness advantages of the proposed method.»
