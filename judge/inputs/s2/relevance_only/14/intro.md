## Related Works

Video super-resolution (VSR) has been extensively studied, with a growing emphasis on handling real-world degradations and artifacts. Early approaches relied on traditional image super-resolution techniques, often applied frame-by-frame, which limited the ability to exploit temporal consistency \[[18](http://arxiv.org/abs/1801.04590)\]. More recent methods have introduced recurrent architectures, such as recurrent neural networks (RNNs), to model temporal dependencies between frames \[[13](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/a2c50f93f6d0892918e0ff653750d198ccab48d0)\]. These methods typically incorporate motion compensation strategies to align frames, but such approaches can be computationally intensive and prone to error propagation \[[18](http://arxiv.org/abs/1801.04590)\].

Diffusion models have emerged as a promising alternative for VSR due to their ability to generate high-quality, realistic images \[[14](http://arxiv.org/abs/2503.03355)\]. However, diffusion-based VSR methods often suffer from introducing artifacts and inconsistencies, particularly in real-world scenarios where degradation patterns are complex and unknown \[[14](http://arxiv.org/abs/2503.03355)\]. To address this, several works have explored ways to enhance the robustness of diffusion models in VSR tasks. For instance, \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2502.07381)\] introduces a spatial degradation-aware and temporal consistent diffusion model that incorporates a distortion control module to minimize the impact of noise from low-quality frames. This model also employs a spatio-temporal attention module to capture temporal correlations, which helps in generating temporally consistent results.

Another approach to improving diffusion-based VSR is through the integration of attention mechanisms. \[[16](http://arxiv.org/abs/2207.06345)\] proposes a Hidden State Attention (HSA) module that mitigates artifacts by replacing the hidden state with a cleaner counterpart. This reduces the accumulation of artifacts during the propagation process. Similarly, \[[20](http://arxiv.org/abs/2312.09909)\] introduces a Temporal Motion Propagation (TMP) method that leverages the continuity of motion fields to achieve fast pixel-level alignment among consecutive frames, improving both accuracy and efficiency.

In addition to attention mechanisms, some works have focused on improving the efficiency of VSR algorithms. \[[24](http://arxiv.org/abs/2202.01731)\] proposes a recurrent VSR architecture based on a deformable attention pyramid (DAP) that reduces computational complexity while maintaining high performance. This approach aligns and integrates information from the recurrent state into the current frame prediction, significantly reducing processing time compared to state-of-the-art methods.
