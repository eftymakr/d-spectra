## Related Works

Video super-resolution (VSR) has been extensively studied, with a growing emphasis on handling real-world degradations and artifacts. Early approaches relied on traditional image super-resolution techniques, often applied frame-by-frame, which limited the ability to exploit temporal consistency \[[18](http://arxiv.org/abs/1801.04590)\]. More recent methods have introduced recurrent architectures, such as recurrent neural networks (RNNs), to model temporal dependencies between frames \[[13](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/a2c50f93f6d0892918e0ff653750d198ccab48d0)\]. These methods typically incorporate motion compensation strategies to align frames, but such approaches can be computationally intensive and prone to error propagation \[[18](http://arxiv.org/abs/1801.04590)\].

Diffusion models have emerged as a promising alternative for VSR due to their ability to generate high-quality, realistic images \[[14](http://arxiv.org/abs/2503.03355)\]. However, diffusion-based VSR methods often suffer from introducing artifacts and inconsistencies, particularly in real-world scenarios where degradation patterns are complex and unknown \[[14](http://arxiv.org/abs/2503.03355)\]. To address this, several works have explored ways to enhance the robustness of diffusion models in VSR tasks. For instance, \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2502.07381)\] introduces a spatial degradation-aware and temporal consistent diffusion model that incorporates a distortion control module to minimize the impact of noise from low-quality frames. This model also employs a spatio-temporal attention module to capture temporal correlations, which helps in generating temporally consistent results.

Another approach to improving diffusion-based VSR is through the integration of attention mechanisms. \[[16](http://arxiv.org/abs/2207.06345)\] proposes a Hidden State Attention (HSA) module that mitigates artifacts by replacing the hidden state with a cleaner counterpart. This reduces the accumulation of artifacts during the propagation process. Similarly, \[[20](http://arxiv.org/abs/2312.09909)\] introduces a Temporal Motion Propagation (TMP) method that leverages the continuity of motion fields to achieve fast pixel-level alignment among consecutive frames, improving both accuracy and efficiency.

These methods collectively highlight the ongoing efforts to address the challenges of diffusion-based VSR, including artifact reduction, temporal consistency, and efficient alignment strategies.

### <a id='category-recurrent-neural-network-(rnn)-based-vsr'></a>Recurrent Neural Network (RNN) Based VSR: 5 papers found.
Video super-resolution (VSR) has been extensively studied, with recent efforts focusing on temporal modeling and computational efficiency. Several approaches leverage recurrent neural networks (RNNs) to capture long-term dependencies in video sequences, improving the temporal consistency of super-resolved outputs \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2502.07381)\]. For instance, the Recurrent Residual Network (RRN) utilizes residual learning to stabilize RNN training and enhance performance, achieving high computational efficiency and producing temporally consistent results with finer details \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/2008.05765)\]. Similarly, the Sliding Window based Recurrent Network (SWRN) enables real-time inference on mobile devices by recurrently storing and updating temporal information from neighboring frames \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/2104.10642)\]. Additionally, a fully convolutional RNN, the bidirectional recurrent convolutional network, reduces network parameters and models temporal dependencies at a finer level, enabling faster multi-frame SR \[[4](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/c6a8ff4d9dcdf9e5d82310b847af999ed6655f65)\]. In contrast, spatial-temporal video super-resolution (ST-VSR) methods aim to generate high-quality videos with higher resolution and frame rates by integrating spatial and temporal modeling, though they often require multiple alignments and fusions \[[5](http://arxiv.org/abs/2410.18137)\]. To address these challenges, a novel recurrent network with bidirectional interaction has been proposed, requiring only one alignment and fusion, and incorporating a Hybrid Fusion Module (HFM) to refine spatial information and reconstruct high-quality frames \[[6](http://arxiv.org/abs/2407.13987)\]. Furthermore, recent work has focused on end-to-end trainable frameworks that use previously inferred high-resolution estimates to super-resolve subsequent frames, promoting temporal consistency while reducing computational costs \[[7](http://arxiv.org/abs/2208.11608)\]. These methods highlight the ongoing efforts to balance performance, efficiency, and temporal coherence in VSR.
