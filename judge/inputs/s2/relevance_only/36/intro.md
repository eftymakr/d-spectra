## Related Works

Deploying large language models (LLMs) on edge devices has garnered significant attention due to its potential to enable real-time, privacy-preserving AI services \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2505.03745)\]. However, the computational and memory demands of LLMs pose substantial challenges for resource-constrained edge hardware \[[2](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/007e7d6cb64a7dfcdcad9fc6f4f0ba69fdc88203)\]. Several approaches have been proposed to address these challenges, focusing on algorithmic optimizations, hardware accelerators, and collaborative edge-cloud computing.

Early efforts in edge deployment focused on model compression techniques such as quantization and pruning to reduce the size and computational load of LLMs \[[3](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/1206ccdce4f721462b5e185c9b2414b5f8f13116)\]. For instance, \[[4](http://arxiv.org/abs/2410.11845)\] introduced a W2A8KV4 quantization scheme that reduces memory and bandwidth requirements while maintaining model accuracy. Similarly, \[[5](http://arxiv.org/abs/2504.03360)\] explored the use of low-bit quantization to enable efficient inference on edge devices with limited resources. These methods, however, often result in significant accuracy degradation, limiting their applicability in critical applications \[[6](http://arxiv.org/abs/2312.11514)\].

To further improve efficiency, researchers have turned to algorithm-hardware co-design, leveraging specialized hardware accelerators tailored for LLM inference \[[7](http://arxiv.org/abs/1806.07846)\]. FPGA-based accelerators, in particular, have shown promise due to their reconfigurability and ability to support model-specific optimizations \[[8](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/ec93fc7ec82c65a7bf7213488327d391014539ed)\]. The AccLLM framework \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2505.03745)\] exemplifies this approach by integrating pruning, $\Lambda$-shaped attention, and W2A8KV4 quantization with an FPGA-based accelerator, achieving up to 4.07× energy efficiency improvement over state-of-the-art methods. Similarly, \[[9](http://arxiv.org/abs/2504.00002)\] proposed SECDA-LLM, a design platform that streamlines the development of FPGA-based LLM accelerators, demonstrating a 11× latency reduction for the TinyLlama model.

Collaborative edge computing has also emerged as a promising direction for deploying LLMs on resource-constrained devices \[[10](http://arxiv.org/abs/2408.00462)\]. The EdgeShard framework \[[2](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/007e7d6cb64a7dfcdcad9fc6f4f0ba69fdc88203)\] partitions LLMs into shards and distributes them across edge devices and cloud servers, achieving up to 50% latency reduction and 2× throughput improvement. Similarly, \[[11](http://arxiv.org/abs/2410.00531)\] introduced Galaxy, a collaborative edge AI system that leverages heterogeneous edge devices to accelerate Transformer inference, achieving up to 2.5× end-to-end latency reduction.
