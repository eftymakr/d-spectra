## Related Works

The integration of knowledge graphs (KGs) with large language models (LLMs) to enhance reasoning capabilities has gained significant attention in recent research. While LLMs have demonstrated strong reasoning abilities, they often struggle with multi-step reasoning and accurate knowledge retrieval, particularly when dealing with structured data such as KGs \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2305.13168)\]. KGs, on the other hand, provide semantically rich, structured representations that are ideal for reasoning tasks, yet most existing claim verification methods rely on unstructured text corpora, limiting their ability to effectively leverage KGs \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2305.13168)\]. 

Several studies have explored the synergy between LLMs and KGs to address these limitations. For instance, \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/2305.01157)\] proposed a decoupled approach called Language-guided Abstract Reasoning over Knowledge graphs (LARK), which combines contextual KG search with logical query reasoning to improve performance on complex queries. This method leverages the strengths of graph extraction algorithms and LLMs, achieving significant improvements on benchmark datasets \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/2305.01157)\]. Similarly, \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/2402.04978)\] introduced a collaborative training-free reasoning scheme that integrates KGs and LLMs to enable more reliable and interpretable reasoning. This approach iteratively explores KGs to retrieve task-relevant subgraphs, allowing LLMs to perform reasoning while explicitly explaining the process \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/2402.04978)\].

Another notable work is \[[4](http://arxiv.org/abs/2410.20724)\], which introduces SubgraphRAG, an extension of KG-based Retrieval-Augmented Generation (RAG) that retrieves subgraphs and leverages LLMs for reasoning and answer prediction. This approach integrates a lightweight multilayer perceptron with a parallel triple-scoring mechanism to optimize retrieval effectiveness and efficiency, enabling scalable and generalizable retrieval processes \[[4](http://arxiv.org/abs/2410.20724)\]. Additionally, \[[5](http://arxiv.org/abs/2310.01061)\] proposed a novel method called reasoning on graphs (RoG), which synergizes LLMs with KGs to enable faithful and interpretable reasoning. RoG employs a planning-retrieval-reasoning framework, where LLMs generate relation paths grounded in KGs to guide the reasoning process \[[5](http://arxiv.org/abs/2310.01061)\].

Moreover, \[[6](http://arxiv.org/abs/2406.06621)\] introduced LinkQ, a system that leverages LLMs to facilitate KG query construction through natural language question-answering. This system simplifies the process of querying KGs by interpreting user questions and converting them into well-formed queries, while ensuring answers are grounded in ground truth KG data \[[6](http://arxiv.org/abs/2406.06621)\].
