## Related Works

The integration of knowledge graphs (KGs) with large language models (LLMs) to enhance reasoning capabilities has gained significant attention in recent research. While LLMs have demonstrated strong reasoning abilities, they often struggle with multi-step reasoning and accurate knowledge retrieval, particularly when dealing with structured data such as KGs \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2305.13168)\]. KGs, on the other hand, provide semantically rich, structured representations that are ideal for reasoning tasks, yet most existing claim verification methods rely on unstructured text corpora, limiting their ability to effectively leverage KGs \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2305.13168)\]. 

Several studies have explored the synergy between LLMs and KGs to address these limitations. For instance, \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/2305.01157)\] proposed a decoupled approach called Language-guided Abstract Reasoning over Knowledge graphs (LARK), which combines contextual KG search with logical query reasoning to improve performance on complex queries. This method leverages the strengths of graph extraction algorithms and LLMs, achieving significant improvements on benchmark datasets \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/2305.01157)\]. Similarly, \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/2402.04978)\] introduced a collaborative training-free reasoning scheme that integrates KGs and LLMs to enable more reliable and interpretable reasoning. ### <a id='category-kg-integration-with-llms-for-reasoning'></a>KG Integration with LLMs for Reasoning: 10 papers found.
The integration of knowledge graphs (KGs) with large language models (LLMs) to enhance reasoning capabilities has been a growing area of research, addressing limitations such as hallucinations, knowledge gaps, and the inability to effectively leverage structured data \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/2402.04978)\]. Existing approaches often treat KGs as mere knowledge bases, neglecting their structural properties that are crucial for accurate reasoning \[[5](http://arxiv.org/abs/2310.01061)\]. To overcome these challenges, several studies have proposed frameworks that combine the strengths of LLMs and KGs, such as LARK \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2305.13168)\], which decouples reasoning into contextual KG search and logical query reasoning, and GCR \[[5](http://arxiv.org/abs/2310.01061)\], which integrates KG structure into the LLM decoding process using a trie-based index to ensure faithful reasoning. Furthermore, dual-agent frameworks such as R2-KG \[[6](http://arxiv.org/abs/2406.06621)\] have been developed to separate reasoning roles between low- and high-capacity LLMs, enhancing reliability and reducing dependency on single, high-capacity models. These approaches collectively highlight the importance of leveraging both the structural and semantic information from KGs to improve the accuracy and robustness of LLM-based reasoning systems.
