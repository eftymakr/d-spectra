## Related Works

Recent advancements in Text-to-Speech (TTS) systems have significantly enhanced speech naturalness, but challenges remain in achieving precise prosody control and mispronunciation correction \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/1909.10302)\]. Traditional approaches for prosody manipulation often rely on specialized modules or additional training, which limits their capacity for post-hoc adjustments \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/1909.10302)\]. Similarly, conventional mispronunciation correction methods depend on grapheme-to-phoneme dictionaries, making them less practical in low-resource settings \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/1909.10302)\]. This section reviews related works that address these challenges.

Several studies have explored prosody modeling and control in TTS systems. For example, \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/2409.12176)\] introduces a fully automatic method that enables sentence-wise speaking pace and expressiveness control on a continuous scale. This method improves the overall quality and expressiveness of synthesized speech by explicitly modeling prosody. Similarly, \[[3](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/ffe8bc7ea78838e8f35b2bc518f33037374ae0ef)\] proposes a novel augmented attention mechanism that facilitates better pace control sensitivity and faster attention convergence. These works highlight the importance of explicit prosody modeling for controllable TTS systems.

In addition to prosody control, several studies have focused on improving the naturalness and expressiveness of TTS systems by aligning generated speech with human speech patterns. \[[4](http://arxiv.org/abs/2102.08328)\] explores the manipulation of prosodic parameters in TTS systems to achieve controlled speech generation, comparing TTS-generated audio with human-recorded speech to analyze differences in pitch, duration, and energy. The findings demonstrate the feasibility and effectiveness of prosodic parameter manipulation for controlled speech generation \[[4](http://arxiv.org/abs/2102.08328)\]. Similarly, \[[5](http://arxiv.org/abs/2309.05423)\] proposes a two-stage automatic annotation pipeline to enhance prosodic information in latent representations, achieving state-of-the-art performance in prosodic boundary detection \[[5](http://arxiv.org/abs/2309.05423)\].

The ability to control prosody in TTS systems has also been explored in the context of spontaneous speech. \[[6](http://arxiv.org/abs/2011.06465)\] proposes a TTS architecture that can rapidly learn to speak from small and irregular datasets while reproducing the diversity of expressive phenomena present in spontaneous speech. This work adds utterance-level prosody control to an existing neural HMM-based TTS system, demonstrating that prosody control does not degrade synthesis quality \[[6](http://arxiv.org/abs/2011.06465)\]. Similarly, \[[7](http://arxiv.org/abs/2211.13533)\] introduces a hierarchical architecture for non-autoregressive TTS models, where phoneme-level prosody features are conditioned on word-level prosody features, leading to improved audio quality and prosody naturalness \[[7](http://arxiv.org/abs/2211.13533)\].
