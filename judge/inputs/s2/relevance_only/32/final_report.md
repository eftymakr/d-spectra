## Related Works

Recent research has focused on understanding how feature representations evolve across layers in large language models (LLMs), aiming to improve interpretability and robustness \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2411.09539)\]. While several studies have identified critical layers linked to specific functions or behaviors, these efforts typically rely on data-dependent analyses of fine-tuned models, limiting their use to post-hoc settings \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2411.09539)\]. In contrast, our work introduces a data-oblivious approach to identify intrinsic critical layers in pre-fine-tuned LLMs by analyzing representation dynamics via Centered Kernel Alignment (CKA) \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2411.09539)\]. This method enables the identification of layers that undergo significant shifts in representation space, which are also most affected during fine-tuning \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2411.09539)\]. 

The pattern of layer sensitivity during fine-tuning is consistent across tasks for a given model, suggesting a generalizable principle in how LLMs adapt to downstream tasks \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2411.09539)\]. Our spectral analysis further reveals that these shifts are driven by changes in the top principal components, which encode semantic transitions from rationales to conclusions \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2411.09539)\]. This insight aligns with findings from studies that examine the impact of model complexity on fine-tuning performance, where the risk initially increases and then decreases with rising model complexity, showcasing a "double descent" phenomenon \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/2410.00699)\].

LoRA has gained significant attention due to its advantages in cross-task generalization and privacy-preserving \[[9](http://arxiv.org/abs/2407.11046)\]. However, its behavior under training-time attacks remains underexplored, posing significant security risks \[[13](http://arxiv.org/abs/2505.12871)\]. Our findings on critical layer identification and their impact on fine-tuning performance complement these efforts by offering a new perspective on how to optimize fine-tuning strategies \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2411.09539)\].

In the context of domain adaptation, our results show that fine-tuning critical layers leads to greater loss reduction compared to non-critical layers \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2411.09539)\]. ### <a id='category-parameter-efficient-fine-tuning-techniques'></a>Parameter-Efficient Fine-Tuning Techniques: 11 papers found.
The study of parameter-efficient fine-tuning techniques has gained significant traction due to the challenges of fine-tuning large language models (LLMs) with limited data, computational resources, and the need for domain adaptation \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2411.09539)\]. Researchers have explored methods such as Low-Rank Adaptation (LoRA), which updates dense neural network layers with low-rank matrices, offering efficiency gains and cross-task generalization \[[6](http://arxiv.org/abs/2501.05032)\]. LoRA has been extensively studied for its performance and structural properties, although its vulnerability to training-time attacks, such as data poisoning and backdoor attacks, remains an open research question \[[8](http://arxiv.org/abs/2408.16440)\]. Additionally, there is growing interest in understanding how fine-tuning affects model behavior, with some studies investigating the Neural Tangent Kernel (NTK) to explain the success of parameter-efficient methods in avoiding overfitting \[[4](http://arxiv.org/abs/2402.16363)\]. The field also includes work on continual learning with pre-training, where sequential fine-tuning (Seq FT) has been revisited as a simpler baseline for learning sequential downstream tasks while preserving pre-trained knowledge \[[10](http://arxiv.org/abs/2412.19512)\]. Furthermore, recent surveys have categorized and reviewed the progress of parameter-efficient fine-tuning from multiple perspectives, including downstream adaptation, cross-task generalization, and efficiency improvements \[[6](http://arxiv.org/abs/2501.05032)\]. These efforts highlight the importance of understanding the dynamics of representation shifts during fine-tuning, as well as the trade-offs between computational efficiency and model performance \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2411.09539)\].
