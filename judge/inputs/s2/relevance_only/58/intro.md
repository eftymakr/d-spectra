## Related Works

Table discovery and understanding have garnered significant attention in recent years due to the increasing availability of tabular data in various domains. Traditional methods often rely on query tables or vague keywords to retrieve relevant tables, which can lead to large result sets requiring manual filtering \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/1208.4016)\]. This limitation highlights the need for more sophisticated approaches that can better align user intent with table content. 

Recent advances in deep learning have improved the performance of table detection and recognition tasks. For instance, TableBank introduces a large-scale dataset with 417K high-quality labeled tables, enabling the development of strong baselines using state-of-the-art models \[[4](http://arxiv.org/abs/1903.01949)\]. Similarly, the introduction of a diverse table detection dataset with over 7,000 samples has enabled the evaluation of convolutional neural network-based methods, demonstrating their superiority over classical computer vision approaches \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/2209.09207)\]. These datasets are crucial for advancing research in table understanding and processing.

The semantic interpretation of tables remains a challenging task, as many tables lack explicit semantics that could aid in automatic interpretation. Table understanding (TU) encompasses a wide range of issues, from detecting tables in document images to interpreting their semantic content with the help of external knowledge bases \[[5](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/cf5f42b60003f9fc11d14a6eb126130411f861a4)\]. Despite ongoing research for over three decades, there is still no consensus on the scope and terminology of TU, underscoring the need for further exploration \[[5](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/cf5f42b60003f9fc11d14a6eb126130411f861a4)\].

To address the limitations of existing methods, researchers have proposed concept-driven frameworks that enhance the semantic capabilities of database systems by semantically connecting unrelated tables \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/1208.4016)\]. These frameworks aim to provide more meaningful and customized information to end users, which is essential for natural language query interfaces \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/1208.4016)\]. Additionally, the development of TaBERT, a pretrained model that jointly learns representations for natural language sentences and structured tables, has shown promising results in semantic parsing tasks \[[6](http://arxiv.org/abs/2005.08314)\]. TaBERT is trained on a large corpus of 26 million tables and their English contexts, enabling better performance on tasks like semantic parsing and text-to-SQL \[[6](http://arxiv.org/abs/2005.08314)\].

In the context of natural language-conditional table discovery (nlcTD), recent work has focused on improving the alignment between user intent and table content. The nlcTables dataset, which includes 627 diverse queries and 22,200 candidate tables, provides a comprehensive benchmark for evaluating the performance of table discovery methods under various conditions \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/1208.4016)\]. The results of evaluating six state-of-the-art methods on this dataset reveal substantial performance gaps, emphasizing the need for advanced techniques to tackle this challenging task \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/1208.4016)\].
