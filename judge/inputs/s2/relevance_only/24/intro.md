## Related Works

Diffusion models have become a cornerstone in generative AI, particularly in image and video generation tasks, due to their ability to produce high-quality, diverse outputs \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2406.02540)\]. However, their computational and memory demands hinder deployment on edge devices, prompting research into model compression techniques such as quantization \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/2406.17343)\]. While quantization has been extensively studied for image generation models, its application to video generation remains challenging due to the unique temporal dynamics and multi-frame processing required \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/2505.18663)\]. 

Several works have explored quantization for diffusion models. For instance, Q-DiT \[[4](http://arxiv.org/abs/2411.14172)\] addresses the spatial and temporal variance in DiT models by introducing automatic quantization granularity allocation and sample-wise dynamic activation quantization. This approach improves fidelity in image and video generation, achieving a notable reduction in FID when quantizing DiT-XL/2 to W6A8 on ImageNet \[[4](http://arxiv.org/abs/2411.14172)\]. Similarly, PTQ4DiT \[[5](http://arxiv.org/abs/2306.02316)\] identifies salient channels with extreme magnitudes and temporal variability in activation distributions as key challenges, proposing Channel-wise Salience Balancing (CSB) and Spearmen's ρ-guided Salience Calibration (SSC) to mitigate quantization errors. These methods demonstrate that careful calibration is essential for maintaining performance in quantized DiT models \[[5](http://arxiv.org/abs/2306.02316)\].

In the context of video diffusion models, QVD \[[6](http://arxiv.org/abs/2405.16005)\] introduces a novel PTQ strategy tailored for video diffusion models, addressing the skewness of temporal features and inter-channel disparities in activation. The High Temporal Discriminability Quantization (HTDQ) method retains high discriminability of quantized features, providing precise temporal guidance across video frames \[[6](http://arxiv.org/abs/2405.16005)\]. Additionally, DVD-Quant \[[7](http://arxiv.org/abs/2407.11585)\] proposes a data-free quantization framework for Video DiTs, incorporating Bounded-init Grid Refinement (BGR), Auto-scaling Rotated Quantization (ARQ), and δ-Guided Bit Switching (δ-GBS) to achieve a 2× speedup over full-precision baselines while maintaining visual fidelity \[[7](http://arxiv.org/abs/2407.11585)\].

The challenges of quantization in diffusion models are further compounded by the need to handle time-step variation and activation sensitivity.
