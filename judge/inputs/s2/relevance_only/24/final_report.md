## Related Works

Diffusion models have become a cornerstone in generative AI, particularly in image and video generation tasks, due to their ability to produce high-quality, diverse outputs \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2406.02540)\]. However, their computational and memory demands hinder deployment on edge devices, prompting research into model compression techniques such as quantization \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/2406.17343)\]. While quantization has been extensively studied for image generation models, its application to video generation remains challenging due to the unique temporal dynamics and multi-frame processing required \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/2505.18663)\]. 

Several works have explored quantization for diffusion models. For instance, Q-DiT \[[4](http://arxiv.org/abs/2411.14172)\] addresses the spatial and temporal variance in DiT models by introducing automatic quantization granularity allocation and sample-wise dynamic activation quantization. This approach improves fidelity in image and video generation, achieving a notable reduction in FID when quantizing DiT-XL/2 to W6A8 on ImageNet \[[4](http://arxiv.org/abs/2411.14172)\]. Similarly, PTQ4DiT \[[5](http://arxiv.org/abs/2306.02316)\] identifies salient channels with extreme magnitudes and temporal variability in activation distributions as key challenges, proposing Channel-wise Salience Balancing (CSB) and Spearmen's ρ-guided Salience Calibration (SSC) to mitigate quantization errors. These methods demonstrate that careful calibration is essential for maintaining performance in quantized DiT models \[[5](http://arxiv.org/abs/2306.02316)\].

In the context of video diffusion models, QVD \[[6](http://arxiv.org/abs/2405.16005)\] introduces a novel PTQ strategy tailored for video diffusion models, addressing the skewness of temporal features and inter-channel disparities in activation. The High Temporal Discriminability Quantization (HTDQ) method retains high discriminability of quantized features, providing precise temporal guidance across video frames \[[6](http://arxiv.org/abs/2405.16005)\]. Additionally, DVD-Quant \[[7](http://arxiv.org/abs/2407.11585)\] proposes a data-free quantization framework for Video DiTs, incorporating Bounded-init Grid Refinement (BGR), Auto-scaling Rotated Quantization (ARQ), and δ-Guided Bit Switching (δ-GBS) to achieve a 2× speedup over full-precision baselines while maintaining visual fidelity \[[7](http://arxiv.org/abs/2407.11585)\].

### <a id='category-quantization-methods-for-diffusion-models'></a>Quantization Methods for Diffusion Models: 15 papers found.
Diffusion transformers (DiT) have shown remarkable performance in video generation, but their large parameter count and computational complexity hinder deployment on edge devices \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2406.02540)\]. Quantization techniques, which reduce bit-widths of model parameters to lower storage and accelerate inference, have been explored for image generation models but struggle to generalize to video tasks \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/2406.17343)\]. This is due to two key challenges: information loss during quantization and misalignment between optimization objectives and video-specific requirements \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/2505.18663)\]. To address these, recent works have proposed specialized quantization methods tailored for DiT models. For instance, ViDiT-Q was designed to handle text-to-image and video tasks by addressing quantization errors through an improved quantization scheme, achieving W8A8 with minimal quality degradation \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2406.02540)\]. Similarly, Q-DiT introduced automatic quantization granularity allocation and sample-wise dynamic activation quantization to manage spatial and temporal variances in DiT models, achieving significant FID improvements \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/2406.17343)\]. The TaQ-DiT method further enhanced quantization by introducing joint reconstruction and time-variance-aware transformations, surpassing previous methods when quantizing to W4A8 \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/2505.18663)\]. Additionally, PTQ4DiT tackled salient channel and temporal activation distribution issues through channel-wise salience balancing and dynamic calibration, enabling W4A8 quantization for the first time \[[5](http://arxiv.org/abs/2306.02316)\]. For video diffusion models, QVD was introduced to handle temporal features and inter-channel disparities, achieving near-lossless performance on W8A8 \[[6](http://arxiv.org/abs/2405.16005)\]. These methods collectively highlight the need for specialized quantization strategies that account for the unique properties of DiT models, such as temporal variability and spatial activation differences \[[7](http://arxiv.org/abs/2407.11585)\].

### <a id='category-model-compression-techniques'></a>Model Compression Techniques: 2 papers found.
Document 1 discusses the challenges of quantizing Video Diffusion Transformers (DiTs), highlighting the limitations of post-training quantization (PTQ) methods, such as their reliance on computationally intensive calibration and performance degradation after quantization. To address these issues, the authors introduce DVD-Quant, a data-free quantization framework that incorporates Bounded-init Grid Refinement (BGR), Auto-scaling Rotated Quantization (ARQ), and $\delta$-Guided Bit Switching ($\delta$-GBS) to reduce quantization errors and enable efficient bit-width allocation \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2406.02540)\]. This work is significant as it is the first to achieve W4A4 PTQ for Video DiTs without compromising video quality \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2406.02540)\].

Document 2 focuses on the quantization of vision transformers, specifically addressing the performance degradation caused by inter-channel variation in LayerNorm inputs and the non-uniform distribution in attention maps. The authors propose Power-of-Two Factor (PTF) and Log-Int-Softmax (LIS) to mitigate these issues, enabling efficient 4-bit quantization while maintaining high accuracy. Their Fully Quantized Vision Transformer (FQ-ViT) achieves state-of-the-art results, including 84.89% top-1 accuracy on ImageNet with ViT-L and 50.8 mAP on COCO with Cascade Mask R-CNN (Swin-S) \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/2406.17343)\]. This work is notable for achieving lossless accuracy degradation (~1%) on fully quantized vision transformers \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/2406.17343)\].

Both documents highlight the importance of addressing quantization challenges in transformer-based models, particularly in vision tasks. While Document 1 focuses on video generation with DiTs, Document 2 addresses vision transformers in general, demonstrating that specialized quantization techniques are necessary to maintain performance and efficiency. These works contribute to the broader field of model compression by offering novel approaches that balance accuracy and computational efficiency \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2406.02540)\]\[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/2406.17343)\].

### <a id='category-quantization-aware-training-frameworks'></a>Quantization-aware Training Frameworks: 2 papers found.
QVGen addresses the challenges of applying quantization to video diffusion models by introducing a quantization-aware training (QAT) framework tailored for low-bit settings \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2406.02540)\]. Unlike previous approaches that struggled with the unique characteristics of video models, QVGen performs a theoretical analysis showing that reducing gradient norm is essential for convergence under quantization \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2406.02540)\]. To mitigate quantization errors, the framework introduces auxiliary modules ($\Phi$) that enhance convergence, followed by a rank-decay strategy to eliminate inference overhead by progressively removing low-contributing components through SVD and rank-based regularization $\mathbf(\gamma)$ \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2406.02540)\]. This approach enables QVGen to achieve full-precision quality under 4-bit quantization, outperforming existing methods on benchmarks like VBench \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2406.02540)\]. Similarly, research on transformer quantization highlights the unique challenges posed by low-bit quantization, including module sensitivity, activation distribution outliers, and dynamic parameter oscillations \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/2406.17343)\]. These variations hinder stable QAT, prompting the development of variation-aware quantization schemes that improve performance across vision and language transformers \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/2406.17343)\]. Both QVGen and these transformer-specific methods underscore the need for tailored quantization strategies to maintain performance while reducing computational costs.

### <a id='category-diffusion-models-in-generative-ai'></a>Diffusion Models in Generative AI: 4 papers found.
Document 1 introduces the VQ-Diffusion model, which combines vector quantized variational autoencoders (VQ-VAEs) with diffusion models to improve text-to-image generation. This approach addresses issues like unidirectional bias and error accumulation in existing methods \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2406.02540)\]. The model also demonstrates a significant improvement in both quality and efficiency compared to traditional autoregressive models \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2406.02540)\]. 

Document 2 discusses MALT Diffusion, a model designed for long video generation. It uses memory-augmented latent transformers to handle extended video sequences by segmenting them and maintaining a memory vector across segments \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/2406.17343)\]. This enables consistent quality over long durations, as evidenced by its performance on benchmarks like UCF-101 \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/2406.17343)\].

Document 3 presents Latte, a latent diffusion transformer for video generation that extracts spatio-temporal tokens and employs Transformer blocks to model video distribution. It introduces efficient variants for handling large token sets and achieves state-of-the-art results on multiple video generation datasets \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/2505.18663)\]. Additionally, Latte is extended to text-to-video generation, showing competitive performance against recent models \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/2505.18663)\].

Document 4 describes Video LDM, which applies the latent diffusion model paradigm to high-resolution video generation. It leverages pretrained image LDMs and introduces temporal alignment to enable video super resolution and text-to-video generation. This approach achieves state-of-the-art performance on real-world driving data and enables personalized text-to-video generation \[[4](http://arxiv.org/abs/2411.14172)\]. 

These models collectively highlight the advancements in diffusion models for video generation, addressing challenges such as long sequence handling, computational efficiency, and quality preservation across frames \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2406.02540)\]\[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/2406.17343)\]\[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/2505.18663)\]\[[4](http://arxiv.org/abs/2411.14172)\].

### <a id='category-temporal-dynamics-in-video-diffusion'></a>Temporal Dynamics in Video Diffusion: 2 papers found.
The category "Temporal Dynamics in Video Diffusion" focuses on advancements in video generation models that incorporate temporal coherence and motion consistency. Recent research has emphasized the challenge of generating long, seamless videos while maintaining temporal relationships between frames. For instance, Anchored Diffusion introduces a sequence-DiT (sDiT) model that extends Diffusion Transformers (DiTs) to handle temporal information, enabling the generation of short video segments with flexible temporal spacing \[[16](http://arxiv.org/abs/2407.15153)\]. This approach leverages transformer architecture during inference to anchor sequences to a common frame, ensuring consistency across varying temporal distances. Similarly, the Video Motion Customization (VMC) framework addresses the challenge of reproducing specific motions from target videos by introducing a motion distillation objective that preserves low-frequency motion trajectories while reducing high-frequency noise \[[25](http://arxiv.org/abs/2312.00845)\]. These methods highlight the importance of temporal alignment and motion preservation in video diffusion models, which are critical for tasks like face reenactment and custom motion generation. Both approaches demonstrate how temporal dynamics can be effectively modeled to enhance video quality and realism.
