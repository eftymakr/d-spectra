## Related Works

The problem of learning from noisy labels has been extensively studied in recent years, with a growing body of research focused on mitigating the negative impact of label noise on model performance. Early work on this topic primarily relied on synthetic noise models, such as class-dependent label noise, where the probability of a label error is uniform across all instances \[[4](http://arxiv.org/abs/2110.12088)\]. However, real-world label noise often exhibits an instance-dependent pattern, where the likelihood of a label error varies based on the difficulty of the task or the characteristics of the instance \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2007.00151)\]. This distinction is crucial, as it highlights the limitations of methods designed for synthetic noise when applied to real-world datasets \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2007.00151)\].

Several approaches have been proposed to address noisy labels by leveraging semi-supervised learning techniques. For instance, DivideMix \[[5](http://arxiv.org/abs/2002.07394)\] introduces a framework that dynamically divides the training data into clean and noisy subsets based on per-sample loss distributions, enabling semi-supervised training that reduces the impact of label noise. Similarly, NICEST \[[7](http://arxiv.org/abs/2207.13316)\] focuses on scene graph generation, where it corrects noisy labels by detecting and reassigning more robust soft labels to problematic instances. This method employs clustering and KNN techniques to refine the labels, improving the overall quality of the model's predictions.

Another line of research explores regularization-based strategies to prevent the memorization of noisy labels. Early-Learning Regularization \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2007.00151)\] proposes a framework that exploits the early learning phase, where models first fit clean samples before memorizing noisy ones. By designing a regularization term that steers the model toward the targets of semi-supervised learning, this approach enhances robustness to noisy annotations. Similarly, the work on Hierarchical Noise-Tolerant Meta-Learning \[[6](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/023f36b310b82df7163e0b7c9ef9ff6a9cb307ee)\] introduces a meta-learning approach that leverages consistency loss at different prediction hierarchies to improve model adaptation to label noise.

In addition to regularization and semi-supervised learning, some methods focus on improving the quality of the training data. For example, CleanNet \[[12](http://arxiv.org/abs/1711.07131)\] introduces a joint neural embedding network that allows for the transfer of label noise knowledge across classes, reducing the need for extensive human supervision. This approach is particularly useful in large-scale datasets where manual label verification is impractical. Similarly, the work on Learning from Long-Tailed Noisy Data \[[9](http://arxiv.org/abs/2211.10906)\] proposes a method that combines sample selection with a balanced loss function to address both class imbalance and label noise, achieving significant improvements over existing methods.
