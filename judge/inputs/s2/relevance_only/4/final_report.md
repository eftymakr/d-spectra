## Related Works

The problem of learning from noisy labels has been extensively studied in recent years, with a growing body of research focused on mitigating the negative impact of label noise on model performance. Early work on this topic primarily relied on synthetic noise models, such as class-dependent label noise, where the probability of a label error is uniform across all instances \[[4](http://arxiv.org/abs/2110.12088)\]. However, real-world label noise often exhibits an instance-dependent pattern, where the likelihood of a label error varies based on the difficulty of the task or the characteristics of the instance \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2007.00151)\]. This distinction is crucial, as it highlights the limitations of methods designed for synthetic noise when applied to real-world datasets \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2007.00151)\].

Several approaches have been proposed to address noisy labels by leveraging semi-supervised learning techniques. For instance, DivideMix \[[5](http://arxiv.org/abs/2002.07394)\] introduces a framework that dynamically divides the training data into clean and noisy subsets based on per-sample loss distributions, enabling semi-supervised training that reduces the impact of label noise. Similarly, NICEST \[[7](http://arxiv.org/abs/2207.13316)\] focuses on scene graph generation, where it corrects noisy labels by detecting and reassigning more robust soft labels to problematic instances. This method employs clustering and KNN techniques to refine the labels, improving the overall quality of the model's predictions.

Another line of research explores regularization-based strategies to prevent the memorization of noisy labels. Early-Learning Regularization \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2007.00151)\] proposes a framework that exploits the early learning phase, where models first fit clean samples before memorizing noisy ones. By designing a regularization term that steers the model toward the targets of semi-supervised learning, this approach enhances robustness to noisy annotations. Similarly, the work on Hierarchical Noise-Tolerant Meta-Learning \[[6](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/023f36b310b82df7163e0b7c9ef9ff6a9cb307ee)\] introduces a meta-learning approach that leverages consistency loss at different prediction hierarchies to improve model adaptation to label noise.

In addition to regularization and semi-supervised learning, some methods focus on improving the quality of the training data. For example, CleanNet \[[12](http://arxiv.org/abs/1711.07131)\] introduces a joint neural embedding network that allows for the transfer of label noise knowledge across classes, reducing the need for extensive human supervision. This approach is particularly useful in large-scale datasets where manual label verification is impractical. Similarly, the work on Learning from Long-Tailed Noisy Data \[[9](http://arxiv.org/abs/2211.10906)\] proposes a method that combines sample selection with a balanced loss function to address both class imbalance and label noise, achieving significant improvements over existing methods.

[Snippet]: «We propose a novel framework to perform classification via deep learning in the presence of noisy annotations. When trained on noisy labels, deep neural networks have been observed to first fit the training data with clean labels during an "early learning" phase, before eventually memorizing the examples with false labels. We prove that early learning and memorization are fundamental phenomena in high-dimensional classification tasks, even in simple linear models, and give a theoretical explanation in this setting. Motivated by these findings, we develop a new technique for noisy classification tasks, which exploits the progress of the early learning phase. In contrast with existing approaches, which use the model output during early learning to detect the examples with clean labels, and either ignore or attempt to correct the false labels, we take a different route and instead capitalize on early learning via regularization. There are two key elements to our approach. First, we leverage semi-supervised learning techniques to produce target probabilities based on the model outputs. Second, we design a regularization term that steers the model towards these targets, implicitly preventing memorization of the false labels. The resulting framework is shown to provide robustness to noisy annotations on several standard benchmarks and real-world datasets, where it achieves results comparable to the state of the art.»

### <a id='category-label-noise-models'></a>Label Noise Models: 8 papers found.
The category "Label Noise Models" encompasses a range of approaches aimed at addressing the challenges posed by noisy labels in machine learning tasks. Existing research has primarily focused on synthetic label noise, which, while enabling statistical analysis, often fails to capture the complexities of real-world noise patterns \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/2012.11854)\]. Recent efforts have sought to create controllable and moderate-sized real-world noisy datasets with ground-truth labels, such as CIFAR-10N and CIFAR-100N, which exhibit instance-dependent label noise rather than class-dependent noise \[[4](http://arxiv.org/abs/2110.12088)\]. This insight has led to the development of methods that account for the variability in noise rates across instances, such as the peer loss framework, which leverages second-order statistics to model the relationship between noise rates and Bayes optimal labels \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2007.00151)\]. Additionally, methods like NICEST have been proposed to address noisy labels in scene graph generation by detecting and correcting noisy samples through techniques such as density-based clustering and weighted KNN \[[7](http://arxiv.org/abs/2207.13316)\]. In other domains, such as semantic segmentation, Compensation Learning has been introduced to identify and mitigate label noise and ambiguities by incorporating a bias correction mechanism and an uncertainty branch \[[10](http://arxiv.org/abs/2304.13428)\]. Similarly, in domain adaptive object detection, the NLTE framework has been developed to handle noisy source annotations by employing techniques like Potential Instance Mining and Morphable Graph Relation Module \[[11](http://arxiv.org/abs/2204.02620)\]. These approaches highlight the growing recognition of instance-dependent label noise and the need for more robust and adaptable solutions to handle such challenges in real-world settings.

[Snippet]: «Noisy labels are ubiquitous in real-world datasets, especially in the large-scale ones derived from crowdsourcing and web searching. It is challenging to train deep neural networks with noisy datasets since the networks are prone to overfitting the noisy labels during training, resulting in poor generalization performance. During an early learning phase, deep neural networks have been observed to fit the clean samples before memorizing the mislabeled samples. In this paper, we dig deeper into the representation distributions in the early learning phase and find that, regardless of their noisy labels, learned representations of images from the same category still congregate together. Inspired by it, we propose a framework that trains the model with new synthetic samples to mitigate the impact of noisy labels. Specifically, we propose a mixing strategy to create the synthetic samples by aggregating original samples with their top-K nearest neighbours, wherein the weights are calculated using a mixture model learning from the per-sample loss distribution. Furthermore, we demonstrate that the estimated soft targets yield a more accurate approximation to ground truth labels and the proposed method produces a superior quality of learned representations with more separated and clearly bounded clusters. The extensive experiments in two benchmarks (CIFAR-10 and CIFAR-100) and two larg-scale real-world datasets (Clothing1M and Webvision) demonstrate that our approach outperforms the state-of-the-art methods and robustness of the learned representation.»

[Snippet]: «Due to the detrimental impact of noisy labels on the generalization of deep neural networks, learning with noisy labels has become an important task in modern deep learning applications. Many previous efforts have mitigated this problem by either removing noisy samples or correcting labels. In this letter, we address this issue from a new perspective and empirically find that models trained with both clean and mislabeled samples exhibit distinguishable activation feature distributions. Building on this observation, we propose a novel meta-learning approach called the Hierarchical Noise-tolerant Meta-Learning (HNML) method, which involves a bi-level optimization comprising meta-training and meta-testing. In the meta-training stage, we incorporate consistency loss at the output prediction hierarchy to facilitate model adaptation to dynamically changing label noise. In the meta-testing stage, we extract activation feature distributions using class activation maps and propose a new mask-guided self-learning method to correct biases in the foreground regions. Through the bi-level optimization of HNML, we ensure that the model generates discriminative feature representations that are insensitive to noisy labels. When evaluated on both synthetic and real-world noisy datasets, our HNML method achieves significant improvements over previous state-of-the-art methods.»
