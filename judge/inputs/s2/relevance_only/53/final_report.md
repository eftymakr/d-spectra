## Related Works

Sequential recommendation has emerged as a critical component of modern recommender systems, aiming to model user preferences based on historical interaction sequences. Early methods, such as Markov Chains (MCs), assumed that a user's next action could be predicted based solely on their last few actions \[[13](http://arxiv.org/abs/1808.09781)\]. However, these methods are limited in capturing long-term dependencies. Recurrent Neural Networks (RNNs) and more recently, Transformer-based models, have been widely adopted for their ability to model sequential dependencies \[[13](http://arxiv.org/abs/1808.09781)\]. Nevertheless, these models often suffer from high computational complexity, particularly with long sequences, due to the quadratic cost of attention mechanisms \[[19](http://arxiv.org/abs/2411.01537)\]. 

To address these limitations, several works have explored alternative architectures. For instance, Mamba4Rec leverages selective state space models (SSMs) to achieve efficient sequential modeling by reducing computational overhead while maintaining model expressiveness \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/2403.03900)\]. Similarly, LinRec introduces a linear attention mechanism, enabling linear complexity in sequence length while preserving the learning capabilities of traditional attention mechanisms \[[19](http://arxiv.org/abs/2411.01537)\]. These models demonstrate that SSMs can be effectively applied to sequential recommendation, offering both efficiency and performance.

Another line of research focuses on modeling user preferences through attention mechanisms that capture both similar and diverse item relationships. SASRec, for example, uses self-attention to identify relevant items from a user's action history, allowing for efficient prediction of the next item \[[13](http://arxiv.org/abs/1808.09781)\]. However, these models often neglect the temporal dynamics of user behavior, which is crucial for capturing evolving preferences. 

To address this, models such as TiSASRec explicitly model time intervals between interactions, enhancing the ability to predict next items based on temporal patterns \[[12](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/9c7a455b1e48d01a99e58e884a6c1acb75074ad0)\]. Similarly, TLSRec introduces a time-aware Transformer model that combines long-term and short-term temporal information, adjusting personalized trade-offs using adaptive fusion layers \[[5](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/417f89b6992377e3e5d706672bfa4da837f011df)\]. These models highlight the importance of incorporating temporal information to improve recommendation accuracy.

Moreover, recent works have explored the integration of social and contextual information to enhance recommendation quality. STRec, for instance, incorporates social influence and temporal information to provide a more holistic understanding of user preferences \[[4](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/dd27b3098dd3974068bed4e6291612c1c5bc3550)\]. Similarly, KATRec leverages knowledge graphs to model both temporal and side information, improving item representation learning by considering higher-order connections \[[20](http://arxiv.org/abs/2012.03323)\]. These approaches demonstrate the value of incorporating additional information to refine recommendations.

The integration of temporal information into item representations also plays a critical role in capturing dynamic user behavior \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/2409.01192)\]. These advancements highlight the growing importance of preference-aware attention in improving the adaptability and personalization of recommendation systems.
