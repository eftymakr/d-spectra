## Related Works

Maximum Inner Product Search (MIPS) has been extensively studied in recent years due to its critical role in recommendation systems, information retrieval, and high-dimensional data applications \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2503.06882)\]. Traditional approaches to MIPS either rely solely on inner product similarity, which can lead to local optima and redundant computations \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2503.06882)\], or reduce the problem to nearest neighbor search (NNS) under the Euclidean metric through space projection, which may cause topology distortion and information loss \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2503.06882)\]. These two paradigms have been considered as mutually exclusive, but recent work suggests that they are not inherently opposing \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2503.06882)\].

Several studies have explored graph-based methods for efficient MIPS. For instance, the work by \[[2](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/25454f456c514219c9d6eeb42200ebbf378db9ca)\] introduces a norm-adjusted proximity graph (NAPG) to enhance inner product search by selecting more meaningful data points during graph construction. Similarly, \[[3](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/386854741f828d30e25a5e01d75e3363d49cad1e)\] proposes a novel indexable probabilistic matrix factorization (IPMF) that integrates indexing into the training phase, achieving faster retrieval times compared to post-processing methods. The use of graph-based indexing has also been explored in \[[4](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/92fb60fde7aa7c94a22571dec416d4a795548f67)\], where a proximity graph is used to convert MIPS into a graph traversal problem, enabling efficient search.

Another line of research focuses on improving search efficiency through adaptive pruning and early termination. For example, \[[5](http://arxiv.org/abs/2201.09290)\] introduces the Adaptive Early Termination (AET) strategy to curtail excessive exploration once an accuracy bottleneck is reached. Additionally, \[[6](http://arxiv.org/abs/1610.03317)\] presents a greedy approach for budgeted MIPS, which allows for a trade-off between search efficiency and quality. This approach is particularly useful in scenarios where computational resources are constrained.

The importance of data distribution and norm bias in MIPS has also been highlighted. \[[7](http://arxiv.org/abs/1909.13459)\] shows that the inner-product navigable small world graph (ip-NSW) achieves significant speedups by leveraging the norm bias, where large norm items dominate the search process. To address this, \[[8](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/0a27db33fcdb1117c971263ada20718828a250d1)\] proposes ip-NSW+, which combines angular and inner-product proximity graphs to improve robustness across different data distributions.

Efforts to incorporate diversity into MIPS have also gained traction. \[[9](http://arxiv.org/abs/2212.07551)\] introduces a diversity-aware MIPS problem, where users can control the degree of diversity in their recommendations. This is further refined in \[[10](http://arxiv.org/abs/2309.09013)\], which proposes a greedy algorithm with early termination and skipping techniques to balance relevance and diversity.

### <a id='category-graph-based-mips-methods'></a>Graph-Based MIPS Methods: 4 papers found.
Graph-based methods have emerged as a powerful approach for solving the Maximum Inner Product Search (MIPS) problem, which is critical in recommendation systems and information retrieval \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2503.06882)\]. Unlike traditional Nearest Neighbor Search (NNS) methods that rely on metric spaces, MIPS involves non-metric functions like inner products, which lack the triangle inequality but offer advantages such as avoiding vanishing and exploding gradients \[[2](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/25454f456c514219c9d6eeb42200ebbf378db9ca)\]. This has motivated the development of graph-based indexing strategies that map data points to nodes in a proximity graph, enabling efficient search by traversing the graph \[[3](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/386854741f828d30e25a5e01d75e3363d49cad1e)\]. For instance, the norm adjusted proximity graph (NAPG) introduces adjusting factors to select more meaningful connections during graph construction, improving search efficiency \[[4](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/92fb60fde7aa7c94a22571dec416d4a795548f67)\]. Similarly, the inner-product navigable small world graph (ip-NSW) achieves significant speedups by leveraging the norm bias inherent in MIPS, where large norm items are more likely to be relevant \[[5](http://arxiv.org/abs/2201.09290)\]. To further enhance performance, ip-NSW+ integrates an angular proximity graph to refine search by first identifying angular neighbors before conducting inner product-based searches \[[6](http://arxiv.org/abs/1610.03317)\]. Additionally, the Inner Product Delaunay Graph (IPDG) transforms MIPS into a graph search problem, offering efficiency gains in high-dimensional settings \[[7](http://arxiv.org/abs/1909.13459)\]. These methods highlight the growing interest in graph-based indexing for MIPS, demonstrating their empirical superiority over hash-based or quantization-based approaches \[[8](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/0a27db33fcdb1117c971263ada20718828a250d1)\].

### <a id='category-diversity-aware-mips-approaches'></a>Diversity-Aware MIPS Approaches: 4 papers found.
The category "Diversity-Aware MIPS Approaches" addresses the limitations of traditional Maximum Inner Product Search (MIPS) methods, which prioritize relevance but neglect diversity in recommendation results. While existing MIPS techniques, such as those based on nearest neighbor search or space projection, often lead to skewed or redundant outputs \[[15](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/87bfc511e4ef4a8aeddad903b06036dbe7177625)\], recent work has focused on enhancing diversity in search results to improve user satisfaction \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2503.06882)\]. For instance, the categorical diversity-aware IPS problem introduces a framework where users can specify preferred categories, and an approximation algorithm is proposed to achieve sub-linear time complexity \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2503.06882)\]. Similarly, the fair and independent IPS problem ensures that each item has an equal probability of being selected, promoting diversity and novelty while maintaining a computational efficiency of O(log n + k) \[[8](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/0a27db33fcdb1117c971263ada20718828a250d1)\]. Additionally, the diversity-aware k-MIPS problem allows users to control the degree of diversity in recommendation lists through a parameter, though exact solutions are NP-hard, prompting the development of heuristic methods like IP-Greedy \[[15](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/87bfc511e4ef4a8aeddad903b06036dbe7177625)\]. Another approach, D$k$MIPS, integrates diversity objectives such as minimizing pairwise item similarities with relevance, offering a controllable trade-off between the two \[[17](http://arxiv.org/abs/2402.13858)\]. These methods collectively highlight the growing interest in diversifying MIPS results, addressing the limitations of traditional approaches and enhancing the practicality of recommendation systems.
