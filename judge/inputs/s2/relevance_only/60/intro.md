## Related Works

Calibration of deep neural networks has been a critical area of research, particularly due to the overconfidence exhibited by modern models in their predictions \[[21](http://arxiv.org/abs/1706.04599)\]. Traditional calibration approaches often rely on predefined transformation functions, such as piece-wise linear functions, which are order-preserving but lack flexibility and expressiveness \[[20](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/7a2a548f12e1be2300a34dc467e97b8abe4d0cbd)\]. These methods are constrained in their ability to adapt to complex calibration scenarios, limiting their effectiveness \[[20](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/7a2a548f12e1be2300a34dc467e97b8abe4d0cbd)\]. Furthermore, existing methods often fail to address the need for accurate absolute values in downstream tasks, which are essential for applications like ads ranking and other real-world systems \[[3](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/7f6bb146b9c1504ec77100e640d624bdc204f0ac)\].

Recent work has focused on improving calibration by leveraging more flexible and expressive models. For instance, the use of focal loss has shown promise in achieving well-calibrated models without compromising accuracy \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/2002.09437)\]. This approach modifies the standard cross-entropy loss to better align predicted probabilities with actual correctness, leading to improved calibration performance \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/2002.09437)\]. Similarly, the introduction of temperature scaling has been shown to effectively calibrate predictions by adjusting the confidence scores of a model \[[21](http://arxiv.org/abs/1706.04599)\].

In the context of ranking models, the importance of both relative order and absolute value calibration has been increasingly recognized. The paper \[[3](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/7f6bb146b9c1504ec77100e640d624bdc204f0ac)\] highlights that existing ranking models often neglect the significance of absolute values, which are crucial for downstream tasks such as ads ranking. This paper proposes a method that addresses this gap by integrating calibration into the ranking process. Another related work \[[8](http://arxiv.org/abs/2406.08010)\] introduces a Self-Boosted framework for calibrated ranking, which combines pointwise and ranking losses to achieve both accurate ranking and calibrated probabilistic predictions. This approach, however, still relies on predefined loss functions and may not offer the same level of flexibility as neural network-based calibrators.

The use of neural networks for calibration has also been explored in other domains. For example, the Unconstrained Monotonic Neural Network (UMNN) \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/1908.05164)\] has been proposed as a flexible and expressive model for learning monotonic functions, which is essential for maintaining the order of predictions while allowing for calibration. This architecture enables the modeling of complex, continuous probability distributions and has been applied in autoregressive flows \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/1908.05164)\]. The UMNN's ability to learn arbitrary monotonic functions makes it a strong candidate for calibration tasks, as it can adapt to different calibration requirements without imposing strict constraints on the form of the transformation \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/1908.05164)\].
