## Related Works

Calibration of deep neural networks has been a critical area of research, particularly due to the overconfidence exhibited by modern models in their predictions \[[21](http://arxiv.org/abs/1706.04599)\]. Traditional calibration approaches often rely on predefined transformation functions, such as piece-wise linear functions, which are order-preserving but lack flexibility and expressiveness \[[20](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/7a2a548f12e1be2300a34dc467e97b8abe4d0cbd)\]. These methods are constrained in their ability to adapt to complex calibration scenarios, limiting their effectiveness \[[20](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/7a2a548f12e1be2300a34dc467e97b8abe4d0cbd)\]. Furthermore, existing methods often fail to address the need for accurate absolute values in downstream tasks, which are essential for applications like ads ranking and other real-world systems \[[3](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/7f6bb146b9c1504ec77100e640d624bdc204f0ac)\].

Recent work has focused on improving calibration by leveraging more flexible and expressive models. For instance, the use of focal loss has shown promise in achieving well-calibrated models without compromising accuracy \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/2002.09437)\]. This approach modifies the standard cross-entropy loss to better align predicted probabilities with actual correctness, leading to improved calibration performance \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/2002.09437)\]. Similarly, the introduction of temperature scaling has been shown to effectively calibrate predictions by adjusting the confidence scores of a model \[[21](http://arxiv.org/abs/1706.04599)\].

In the context of ranking models, the importance of both relative order and absolute value calibration has been increasingly recognized. The paper \[[3](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/7f6bb146b9c1504ec77100e640d624bdc204f0ac)\] highlights that existing ranking models often neglect the significance of absolute values, which are crucial for downstream tasks such as ads ranking. This paper proposes a method that addresses this gap by integrating calibration into the ranking process. Another related work \[[8](http://arxiv.org/abs/2406.08010)\] introduces a Self-Boosted framework for calibrated ranking, which combines pointwise and ranking losses to achieve both accurate ranking and calibrated probabilistic predictions. This approach, however, still relies on predefined loss functions and may not offer the same level of flexibility as neural network-based calibrators.

The use of neural networks for calibration has also been explored in other domains. For example, the Unconstrained Monotonic Neural Network (UMNN) \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/1908.05164)\] has been proposed as a flexible and expressive model for learning monotonic functions, which is essential for maintaining the order of predictions while allowing for calibration. This architecture enables the modeling of complex, continuous probability distributions and has been applied in autoregressive flows \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/1908.05164)\]. The UMNN's ability to learn arbitrary monotonic functions makes it a strong candidate for calibration tasks, as it can adapt to different calibration requirements without imposing strict constraints on the form of the transformation \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/1908.05164)\].

### <a id='category-calibration-of-deep-neural-networks'></a>Calibration of Deep Neural Networks: 18 papers found.
The category "Calibration of Deep Neural Networks" encompasses a wide range of research aimed at improving the reliability of model predictions by ensuring that predicted probabilities accurately reflect the true likelihood of correct classifications. Existing studies highlight that deep neural networks often suffer from miscalibration, where confidence scores do not align with actual prediction accuracy \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/1908.05164)\]. This issue is particularly critical in safety-critical applications such as healthcare and autonomous driving, where accurate confidence estimates are essential for decision-making \[[3](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/7f6bb146b9c1504ec77100e640d624bdc204f0ac)\]. To address this, various calibration techniques have been proposed, including temperature scaling, which is a simple yet effective post-hoc method \[[16](http://arxiv.org/abs/2410.19796)\], and more advanced approaches like focal loss, which integrates into the training process to improve calibration without sacrificing accuracy \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/1908.05164)\]. Additionally, methods such as mixup training have been shown to enhance calibration by improving the model's ability to generalize and reduce overconfidence in predictions \[[9](http://arxiv.org/abs/1905.11001)\]. Recent work has also introduced novel calibration frameworks, such as Consistency Calibration (CC), which leverages model consistency across perturbed inputs to adjust confidence levels \[[3](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/7f6bb146b9c1504ec77100e640d624bdc204f0ac)\]. Furthermore, research has explored the use of kernel-based methods, such as KCal, which provides a full calibration guarantee by operating on latent embeddings rather than directly on logits or softmax outputs \[[9](http://arxiv.org/abs/1905.11001)\]. These approaches reflect a growing interest in developing flexible and effective calibration strategies that can be applied across diverse architectures and datasets.

Unlike existing post-processing methods, our calibration is performed during training, which can resolve the training instability issue without any additional processing. Our results show that our proposed calibrated ranking losses can achieve nearly optimal results in terms of both ranking quality and score scale calibration.»

[Snippet]: «Scale-calibrated ranking systems are ubiquitous in real-world applications nowadays, which pursue accurate ranking quality and calibrated probabilistic predictions simultaneously. For instance, in the advertising ranking system, the predicted click-through rate (CTR) is utilized for ranking and required to be calibrated for the downstream cost-per-click ads bidding. Recently, multi-objective based methods have been wildly adopted as a standard approach for Calibrated Ranking, which incorporates the combination of two loss functions: a pointwise loss that focuses on calibrated absolute values and a ranking loss that emphasizes relative orderings. However, when applied to industrial online applications, existing multi, objective CR approaches still suffer from two crucial limitations First, previous methods need to aggregate the full candidate list within a single mini-batch to compute the ranking loss. Such aggregation strategy violates extensive data shuffling which has long been proven beneficial for preventing overfitting, and thus degrades the training effectiveness. Second, existing multi-objective methods apply the two inherently conflicting loss functions on a single probabilistic prediction, which results in a sub-optimal trade-off between calibration and ranking. To tackle the two limitations, we propose a Self-Boosted framework for Calibrated Ranking (SBCR). In SBCR, the predicted ranking scores by the online deployed model are dumped into context features. With these additional context features, each single item can perceive the overall distribution of scores in the whole ranking list, so that the ranking loss can be constructed without the need for sample aggregation. As the deployed model is a few versions older than the training model, the dumped predictions reveal what was failed to learn and keep boosting the model to correct previously mis-predicted items. Moreover, a calibration module is introduced to decouple the point loss and ranking loss. The two losses are applied before and after the calibration module separately, which elegantly addresses the sub-optimal trade-off problem. We conduct comprehensive experiments on industrial scale datasets and online A/B tests, demonstrating that SBCR can achieve advanced performance on both calibration and ranking. Our method has been deployed on the video search system of Kuaishou, and results in significant performance improvements on CTR and the total amount of time users spend on Kuaishou.»
