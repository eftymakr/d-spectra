## Related Works

Semi-supervised learning (SSL) has been widely studied for its ability to leverage unlabeled data when labeled data is scarce. However, conventional SSL methods assume that labeled and unlabeled data share an identical class distribution, which is often violated in practice due to the presence of out-of-distribution (OOD) samples, i.e., outliers \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2311.10572)\]. These outliers are typically treated as noise, leading to significant performance degradation \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/2303.12091)\]. To address this challenge, various open-set SSL approaches have been proposed, aiming to detect and mitigate the adverse effects of outliers \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/2308.13168)\].

Early methods primarily focused on outlier detection using binary classifiers, but they suffered from limited scalability and inability to distinguish different types of uncertainty \[[4](http://arxiv.org/abs/2412.18256)\]. More recent approaches, such as Adaptive Negative Evidential Deep Learning (ANEDL), have introduced evidential deep learning (EDL) to quantify uncertainty and design adaptive strategies for self-training and inference \[[5](http://arxiv.org/abs/2105.14148)\]. Similarly, IOMatch has been proposed to jointly utilize inliers and outliers, even when distinguishing between them is difficult, by employing a multi-binary classifier and standard closed-set classifier to produce unified open-set classification targets \[[6](http://arxiv.org/abs/2109.00650)\].

Another line of research, such as OpenMatch, has focused on integrating novelty detection with SSL frameworks, using one-vs-all (OVA) classifiers to detect outliers and introducing an open-set soft-consistency regularization loss to improve outlier detection \[[7](http://arxiv.org/abs/2107.08943)\]. Additionally, methods like Distribution Aligning Refinery of Pseudo-label (DARP) have been developed to address class distribution mismatch by refining pseudo-labels generated from biased models \[[8](http://arxiv.org/abs/2007.08844)\]. The work in \[[9](http://arxiv.org/abs/2007.11330)\] further explores open-set SSL by introducing a multi-task curriculum learning framework that detects OOD samples and trains a classifier on in-distribution data \[[10](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/f3df804a7eef204b0c4fc4b81166dec9b4abc073)\].

Recent approaches have also focused on robust SSL in open environments, where labeled and unlabeled data may come from inconsistent distributions. For example, the work in \[[11](http://arxiv.org/abs/2308.11874)\] proposes a framework called Weight-Aware Distillation (WAD) that selectively transfers knowledge from unsupervised contrastive representations to the target classifier, improving SSL performance under class distribution mismatch \[[12](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/cfcdf167b3756735f230e72e4d2713bb063587b2)\]. Similarly, the Triplet Adaptation Framework (TAF) has been introduced to reduce distribution divergence and improve generalization by aligning the class and representation distributions of labeled and unlabeled data \[[13](http://arxiv.org/abs/2308.14119)\].

### <a id='category-open-set-ssl-approaches'></a>Open-Set SSL Approaches: 15 papers found.
Open-set semi-supervised learning (OSSL) addresses the challenge where unlabeled data contains out-of-distribution (OOD) samples, which can degrade SSL performance \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2311.10572)\]. Existing methods often assume that OOD samples are harmful and aim to exclude them during training \[[10](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/f3df804a7eef204b0c4fc4b81166dec9b4abc073)\], but this can lead to performance degradation when labeled data is scarce \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/2308.13168)\]. For instance, methods like FixMatch assume that labeled and unlabeled data share the same label space, which is not valid in open-world scenarios \[[4](http://arxiv.org/abs/2412.18256)\]. To handle this, researchers have proposed frameworks such as OpenMatch, which integrates novelty detection with SSL by using one-vs-all classifiers to detect inliers and outliers \[[4](http://arxiv.org/abs/2412.18256)\]. Similarly, IOMatch jointly utilizes inliers and outliers by introducing a multi-binary classifier and treating all outliers as a single class \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/2308.13168)\]. Other approaches, such as ANEDL, use evidential deep learning to quantify uncertainty and design adaptive negative optimization strategies for better outlier detection \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/2303.12091)\]. Additionally, methods like GlocalMatch exploit the glocal cluster structure of unlabeled data to improve pseudo-label reliability \[[12](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/cfcdf167b3756735f230e72e4d2713bb063587b2)\], while SAFE-R-STUDENT introduces a scoring framework and calibration loss to enhance seen-class classification \[[7](http://arxiv.org/abs/2107.08943)\]. These approaches highlight the importance of addressing OOD samples directly within the SSL framework, rather than treating them as noise \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2311.10572)\].

### <a id='category-robust-ssl-frameworks'></a>Robust SSL Frameworks: 6 papers found.
The category "Robust SSL Frameworks" addresses the limitations of conventional semi-supervised learning (SSL) methods that assume labeled and unlabeled data share the same class distribution, which is often violated in real-world scenarios where unlabeled data may contain out-of-distribution (OOD) or unknown classes \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2311.10572)\]. This assumption leads to severe performance degradation due to error propagation and the inclusion of harmful outliers \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/2303.12091)\]. To mitigate these issues, several frameworks have been proposed. For instance, Uncertainty-Aware Self-Distillation (UASD) introduces a novel algorithm that produces soft targets to avoid catastrophic error propagation and effectively learns from unconstrained unlabeled data with OOD samples \[[10](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/f3df804a7eef204b0c4fc4b81166dec9b4abc073)\]. Similarly, Weight-Aware Distillation (WAD) addresses class distribution mismatch by selectively transferring knowledge from unsupervised contrastive representations to the target classifier, using adaptive weights and high-quality pseudo-labels \[[11](http://arxiv.org/abs/2308.11874)\]. The Triplet Adaptation Framework (TAF) further reduces distribution divergence by incorporating three adapters—Balanced Residual Adapter, Representation Adapter, and Pseudo-Label Adapter—to align the model's predictions with the true class distribution \[[12](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/cfcdf167b3756735f230e72e4d2713bb063587b2)\]. Additionally, methods like the proposed Diversify and Conquer (DAC) framework leverage prediction disagreements among multiple differently biased models trained on unlabeled data to robustly detect outliers, even with underspecified labeled data \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2311.10572)\]. These approaches collectively highlight the importance of handling class distribution mismatch and OOD data in SSL, with a focus on improving robustness and generalization in open-set scenarios \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2311.10572)\]\[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/2303.12091)\]\[[10](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/f3df804a7eef204b0c4fc4b81166dec9b4abc073)\]\[[11](http://arxiv.org/abs/2308.11874)\]\[[12](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/cfcdf167b3756735f230e72e4d2713bb063587b2)\].

### <a id='category-pseudo-label-refinement-techniques'></a>Pseudo-Label Refinement Techniques: 8 papers found.
In the context of pseudo-label refinement techniques, several approaches have been proposed to improve the quality of pseudo-labels generated from unlabeled data. These methods aim to address issues such as biased pseudo-labels in imbalanced class distributions \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/2303.12091)\], overconfidence in predictions leading to miscalibration \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/2308.13168)\], and the reliance on uncertain pseudo-labels without considering their correctness \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2311.10572)\]. For instance, DARP introduces a convex optimization framework to refine pseudo-labels by aligning their distribution with the true data distribution, thereby mitigating bias toward majority classes \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/2303.12091)\]. Similarly, the UPS framework enhances pseudo-labeling accuracy by reducing noise through uncertainty-aware selection and enabling the creation of negative pseudo-labels for multi-label tasks \[[7](http://arxiv.org/abs/2107.08943)\]. Additionally, methods like Dash dynamically adjust pseudo-label selection based on loss thresholds, ensuring only high-quality examples are used for training \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2311.10572)\]. These techniques collectively highlight the importance of refining pseudo-labels to improve model performance in semi-supervised learning settings.

### <a id='category-distribution-alignment-strategies'></a>Distribution Alignment Strategies: 1 papers found.
In the domain of semi-supervised learning (SSL), traditional approaches assume that labeled and unlabeled data share the same underlying distribution, but this assumption is often violated in practice due to the presence of outliers in the unlabeled data \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2311.10572)\]. These outliers, treated as noise, can significantly degrade model performance \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2311.10572)\]. To address this, recent work has focused on improving robustness in open-set SSL scenarios, where the presence of unknown classes is explicitly considered \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2311.10572)\]. One notable approach, PruneSSL, proposes a method to enhance the separability of the unlabeled dataset by selectively removing examples, leading to improved performance across various SSL algorithms \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2311.10572)\]. This aligns with broader efforts to refine SSL strategies by addressing distribution mismatches and noise \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2311.10572)\]. Additionally, methods like Diversify and Conquer (DAC) leverage prediction disagreements among multiple models with different biases toward the unlabeled distribution, offering a more robust approach to outlier detection, particularly when labeled data is limited \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2311.10572)\]. These strategies highlight the ongoing evolution of SSL techniques to better handle real-world data challenges.
