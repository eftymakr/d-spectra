## Related Works

Neural retrieval models have become a cornerstone of modern information retrieval systems, particularly in web search applications \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2402.10571)\]. These models excel at ranking documents based on their relevance to a given query, but their training typically requires substantial amounts of labeled query-document pairs, which are costly to obtain \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2402.10571)\]. With the availability of large document collections like ClueWeb22, researchers have explored synthetic query generation as a scalable alternative \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2402.10571)\]. Large language models (LLMs) can generate synthetic queries, but these often vary in quality, leading to suboptimal downstream retrieval performance \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2402.10571)\]. 

Existing methods typically filter out noisy query-document pairs using external re-rankers, which can be computationally expensive and may not always yield optimal results \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2402.10571)\]. In contrast, this work proposes a framework that integrates ranking signals directly into the query generation process using Direct Preference Optimization (DPO) \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2402.10571)\]. DPO has been widely adopted for preference alignment of LLMs due to its simplicity and effectiveness \[[10](http://arxiv.org/abs/2410.04350)\]. It directly optimizes the model to increase the likelihood of preferred responses over dispreferred ones without requiring a reward model or reinforcement learning \[[10](http://arxiv.org/abs/2410.04350)\]. 

DPO has been shown to outperform traditional reinforcement learning from human feedback (RLHF) methods in several ways \[[10](http://arxiv.org/abs/2410.04350)\]. For instance, DPO eliminates the need for sampling from the LM during fine-tuning and avoids significant hyperparameter tuning \[[10](http://arxiv.org/abs/2410.04350)\]. Moreover, DPO has been extended to handle more complex scenarios, such as when the preferred response is only slightly better than the dispreferred one \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2402.10571)\]. This is achieved by introducing an offset value that ensures the difference in likelihood between preferred and dispreferred responses is greater than a certain threshold \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2402.10571)\]. 

In addition to DPO, several other approaches have been proposed to improve the quality of synthetic queries and enhance retrieval performance. For example, filtered direct preference optimization (fDPO) uses a trained reward model to monitor the quality of texts within the preference dataset during DPO training, discarding lower-quality samples \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/2404.13846)\]. Similarly, TIS-DPO introduces token-level importance sampling to assign importance weights to each token based on its reward, improving the efficiency and effectiveness of the optimization process \[[10](http://arxiv.org/abs/2410.04350)\]. 

Furthermore, the use of synthetic queries for training retrieval models has been explored in the context of privacy-preserving systems, where differentially private language models are used to generate private synthetic queries \[[17](http://arxiv.org/abs/2305.05973)\]. This approach ensures that the training data remains private while still allowing for high-quality retrieval performance.
