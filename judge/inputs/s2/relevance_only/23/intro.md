## Related Works

Radar and camera fusion has gained significant attention in autonomous driving due to their complementary sensing capabilities and robustness in adverse weather conditions \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2307.15264)\]. However, accurate calibration between these sensors remains a critical challenge. Traditional calibration methods often require specialized targets or controlled environments, which limits their applicability in real-world scenarios \[[2](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/0e88fc24ae7694d0b1a2fe78322936fc568bc7d2)\]. 

Several approaches have been proposed to address these limitations. For instance, \[[3](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/31cddde4f300fe043e0de0d0373affedd0539685)\] introduced a flexible and accurate method for extrinsic calibration of 3D radar and camera, using a single corner reflector and leveraging ROS to collect data. This method demonstrated high accuracy with an AED error of 15.31 pixels and an accuracy of up to 89%. Similarly, \[[4](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/82a15430a8066ea39bc45ae339ed645660c98e32)\] proposed an automatic targetless calibration method for multiple 3D LiDAR and radar sensors, requiring only a short drive distance. This method estimated the 6-DoF pose of each sensor by minimizing point-to-plane distances and building a 3D map of the environment. 

In the context of radar-camera fusion, \[[5](http://arxiv.org/abs/2309.00787)\] introduced an online targetless calibration method using deep learning to extract common features from raw radar data and camera images. This approach leveraged these common features to match identical objects from both data sources and applied RANSAC and Levenberg-Marquardt optimization to improve accuracy. \[[6](http://arxiv.org/abs/2211.01871)\] further extended this idea by proposing a continuous-time 3D radar-to-camera extrinsic calibration algorithm that utilized radar velocity measurements and did not require specialized radar retroreflectors. 

Another notable work is \[[7](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/75f57abbdcf8740c40575bb5bcab25d98a1925d3)\], which presented an automatic calibration framework for roadside radar-camera fusion. This framework leveraged trajectory events from sensor measurements to eliminate modality disparity and achieve stable target representation. It demonstrated feasibility and accuracy in real-world scenarios, surpassing other methods in calibration performance. 

Additionally, \[[8](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/eb9938c7c7d95c70952b2a4b4fcf1796b2ba3fb2)\] introduced a novel framework for integrating camera and radar inputs to enhance perception robustness. This approach fused 2D detection proposals from camera imagery with radar points, enabling reliable object detection. The paper also presented a calibration algorithm and evaluated its performance on real-world datasets.
