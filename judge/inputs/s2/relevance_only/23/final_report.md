## Related Works

Radar and camera fusion has gained significant attention in autonomous driving due to their complementary sensing capabilities and robustness in adverse weather conditions \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2307.15264)\]. However, accurate calibration between these sensors remains a critical challenge. Traditional calibration methods often require specialized targets or controlled environments, which limits their applicability in real-world scenarios \[[2](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/0e88fc24ae7694d0b1a2fe78322936fc568bc7d2)\]. 

Several approaches have been proposed to address these limitations. For instance, \[[3](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/31cddde4f300fe043e0de0d0373affedd0539685)\] introduced a flexible and accurate method for extrinsic calibration of 3D radar and camera, using a single corner reflector and leveraging ROS to collect data. This method demonstrated high accuracy with an AED error of 15.31 pixels and an accuracy of up to 89%. Similarly, \[[4](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/82a15430a8066ea39bc45ae339ed645660c98e32)\] proposed an automatic targetless calibration method for multiple 3D LiDAR and radar sensors, requiring only a short drive distance. This method estimated the 6-DoF pose of each sensor by minimizing point-to-plane distances and building a 3D map of the environment. 

In the context of radar-camera fusion, \[[5](http://arxiv.org/abs/2309.00787)\] introduced an online targetless calibration method using deep learning to extract common features from raw radar data and camera images. The Related Works section for the paper's category on Calibration Methodologies highlights recent advancements in automatic radar-camera calibration. Existing methods often rely on target-based approaches, which require specialized calibration environments and are cumbersome to implement \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2307.15264)\]. In contrast, targetless methods have gained traction, as they offer flexibility and ease of use in real-world settings \[[2](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/0e88fc24ae7694d0b1a2fe78322936fc568bc7d2)\]. Another approach leverages the vehicle's movement to estimate 6-DoF poses for LiDAR and radar sensors, building a 3D map without requiring overlapping fields of view \[[2](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/0e88fc24ae7694d0b1a2fe78322936fc568bc7d2)\]. Similarly, a method using object contours and velocity differences for RGBD camera and millimeter-wave radar calibration eliminates the need for calibration boards \[[3](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/31cddde4f300fe043e0de0d0373affedd0539685)\]. Deep learning has also been applied to extract common features from radar and camera data, enabling online targetless calibration through implicit feature matching \[[4](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/82a15430a8066ea39bc45ae339ed645660c98e32)\]. Additionally, a continuous-time 3D radar-to-camera calibration algorithm utilizes radar velocity measurements, avoiding the need for specialized retroreflectors \[[8](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/eb9938c7c7d95c70952b2a4b4fcf1796b2ba3fb2)\]. These methods demonstrate a shift towards more robust, automated calibration techniques, especially in challenging environments with adverse weather conditions \[[5](http://arxiv.org/abs/2309.00787)\]. Furthermore, some approaches focus on improving the accuracy and robustness of calibration through optimization algorithms like RANSAC and Levenberg-Marquardt \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2307.15264)\]\[[4](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/82a15430a8066ea39bc45ae339ed645660c98e32)\]. ### <a id='category-sensor-fusion-techniques'></a>Sensor Fusion Techniques: 14 papers found.
The related works in sensor fusion for autonomous driving focus on improving the robustness and accuracy of 3D object detection by integrating radar and camera data. Early approaches primarily relied on decision-level fusion, which combined independently processed radar and image detection results, often leading to suboptimal performance due to the lack of full exploitation of semantic and spatial information \[[17](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/65db002b2c185f5366c2ee7fa855ecfecb6e56cb)\]. More recent methods have shifted toward feature-level fusion, aiming to align and integrate features from both modalities more effectively. For instance, RCFusion \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2307.15264)\] proposes a unified bird’s-eye view (BEV) space for multimodal feature fusion, leveraging 4-D radar data to enhance point cloud density and improve detection accuracy. Similarly, CRAB \[[3](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/31cddde4f300fe043e0de0d0373affedd0539685)\] introduces a backward projection method that uses radar to mitigate depth ambiguity, enhancing the reliability of 3D object detection in challenging conditions. CramNet \[[4](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/82a15430a8066ea39bc45ae339ed645660c98e32)\] further advances this by employing a ray-constrained cross-attention mechanism to resolve geometric ambiguities between camera and radar features, ensuring robust performance even when sensors malfunction. RAFDet \[[5](http://arxiv.org/abs/2309.00787)\] integrates radar RF images and point clouds to refine depth estimation and detection accuracy, while also introducing an Edge-Awareness Feature Enhancement mechanism to address radar sparsity. The use of BEV representations has become increasingly prevalent, as seen in RC-BEVFusion \[[9](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/fa7f4199640500d48c0671181ea6a8dce3141123)\], which presents a modular fusion network that leverages BEV features for better alignment and integration of radar and camera data. Additionally, methods like RVIFNet \[[10](http://arxiv.org/abs/2103.07505)\] focus on multilevel fusion strategies to exploit the complementary attributes of radar and camera data across different semantic and spatial levels. These approaches collectively highlight the growing trend toward more sophisticated and robust sensor fusion techniques that address the inherent challenges of integrating sparse radar data with dense visual information.
