## Related Works

Brain tumor segmentation remains a critical challenge in medical imaging due to the complexity of tumor morphology, limited data availability, and the need for high accuracy \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2011.11052)\]. Several studies have explored deep learning-based approaches to address these challenges, with a focus on improving segmentation performance while reducing computational costs \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/1812.01049)\]. The U-Net architecture has been widely adopted for its encoder-decoder structure, which enables effective feature extraction and spatial reconstruction \[[3](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/ea1672b029da4bcb3648ec5c7db616b303756db6)\]. However, training 3D U-Net models often requires substantial computational resources and extensive data \[[4](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/6c9c65db8099038733cc0644265764c0570d4fbe)\]. 

To mitigate these issues, researchers have proposed various modifications to the U-Net framework. For instance, the EfficientNet-based asymmetric U-Net was introduced to reduce the third dimension of 3D inputs, allowing the use of 2D pre-trained models \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2011.11052)\]. Similarly, the 3D-Znet model leverages variational autoencoder–autodecoder architecture and dense connections to improve feature reuse and model performance \[[7](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/bccbc57c2b9a995a1cd387c45fc21f12af3182ba)\]. The nnU-Net framework further enhances segmentation by incorporating depthwise separable convolutions and shuffle attention mechanisms, achieving competitive performance with lower computational costs \[[8](http://arxiv.org/abs/2012.06760)\]. 

Another approach to improving efficiency is the use of transfer learning, where pre-trained models from other domains are fine-tuned for brain tumor segmentation \[[9](http://arxiv.org/abs/2411.01896)\]. This strategy has been successfully applied to reduce training time and improve generalization, especially in scenarios with limited annotated data \[[10](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/22c118eb6a0d432501df0a437084be700653975c)\]. For example, the RMU-Net model integrates MobileNetV2 with residual blocks to create a lightweight yet effective architecture for brain tumor segmentation \[[18](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/49a5815225e9016b3ac587f0e03bb6acbeeb7d4e)\]. Similarly, the SD-UNet model employs depthwise separable convolutions and weight standardization to achieve high accuracy with minimal computational overhead \[[17](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/965f780b5627ed3ee5a6175f7de77996dfe66c32)\]. 

In addition to architectural improvements, attention mechanisms have been integrated into segmentation models to enhance feature representation and reduce noise. The Attention Residual U-Net 3D (ARU-Net 3D) and LinkNet 3D architectures incorporate soft attention and pyramid pooling to improve segmentation accuracy \[[19](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/a06ad56eec4c9a167e59db71bc4307fdc56f2d9a)\]. Furthermore, the use of 3D convolutional neural networks (CNNs) with uncertainty estimation has been explored to provide reliable segmentation results and quantify model confidence \[[28](http://arxiv.org/abs/2009.12188)\]. 

Recent advancements have also focused on combining segmentation with classification tasks to improve performance. The joint classification and segmentation model leverages pre-trained encoders from a brain glioma grade classification proxy task, leading to enhanced segmentation performance, especially for challenging tumor labels \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2011.11052)\].
