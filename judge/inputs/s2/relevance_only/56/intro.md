## Related Works

Cross-domain Sequential Recommendation (CDSR) has gained significant attention in recent years due to its ability to alleviate the data sparsity and cold-start problems that plague single-domain recommendation systems \[[1](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/e33f51c13728bedeeb54a76b398949a381d6152f)\]. Traditional CDSR methods primarily rely on overlapping users to transfer knowledge between domains, which limits their applicability in scenarios with non-overlapping users \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/2406.03085)\]. Moreover, existing approaches often fail to capture the complex transition patterns inherent in mixed behavior sequences, further hindering performance \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/2401.04971)\].

Several works have explored the integration of Large Language Models (LLMs) into CDSR to enhance semantic understanding and cross-domain alignment. For instance, \[[4](http://arxiv.org/abs/2501.19232)\] introduces LLMCDSR, which leverages LLMs to predict pseudo items within single-domain interactions and employs a Collaborative-Textual Contrastive Pre-Training strategy to infuse collaborative information into textual features. Similarly, \[[5](http://arxiv.org/abs/2304.03891)\] proposes URLLM, which integrates user retrieval and domain grounding with LLMs to improve cross-domain knowledge transfer. These methods demonstrate the potential of LLMs in capturing semantic information, but they often struggle with domain-specific generation and information integration \[[6](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/27670ed18e96550c674e022e284fc5356a7458c2)\].

To address the limitations of existing methods, \[[7](http://arxiv.org/abs/2411.19862)\] introduces a framework that uses LLMs to generate pseudo items and aligns them with real items through a contrastive learning strategy. This approach improves the accuracy of cross-domain recommendations by leveraging the semantic reasoning capabilities of LLMs. Additionally, \[[8](http://arxiv.org/abs/2503.07761)\] proposes a semantic bias-aware framework for zero-shot sequential recommendation, which aligns item embeddings across domains while preserving intra-domain diversity. This is crucial for ensuring effective knowledge transfer without collapsing into overly generic representations \[[9](http://arxiv.org/abs/2405.12473)\].

Another line of research focuses on the challenges of non-overlapping cross-domain sequential recommendation. \[[10](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/96bfe33a2503e960153a934e10be91da4d8b13a6)\] introduces a framework that uses automated prompting to address the lack of overlapping entities between domains, enabling the system to learn from implicit feedback without relying on content information. This approach is particularly useful in scenarios where users interact with only one domain \[[11](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/11da511e9baafc1dfdf58a287c1ead787c9345bc)\].

Furthermore, \[[12](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/756e3e6c0956d1daa19367eb13115151b20eeb7f)\] proposes a method that bridges domains through diverse interests, mitigating the issue of negative transfer by extracting multi-interest representations and using them as a bridge for inter-domain information transfer. This ensures that recommendations are more aligned with user preferences across domains \[[13](http://arxiv.org/abs/2408.08208)\].

In addition to semantic alignment, \[[14](http://arxiv.org/abs/2209.10163)\] explores the use of hierarchical gating networks to capture both single-domain and cross-domain user preferences, enabling more accurate prediction of the user’s next interaction.
