Traditional CDSR methods primarily rely on overlapping users to transfer knowledge between domains, which limits their applicability in scenarios with non-overlapping users \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/2406.03085)\]. Moreover, existing approaches often fail to capture the complex transition patterns inherent in mixed behavior sequences, further hindering performance \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/2401.04971)\].

Several works have explored the integration of Large Language Models (LLMs) into CDSR to enhance semantic understanding and cross-domain alignment. These methods demonstrate the potential of LLMs in capturing semantic information, but they often struggle with domain-specific generation and information integration \[[6](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/27670ed18e96550c674e022e284fc5356a7458c2)\].
