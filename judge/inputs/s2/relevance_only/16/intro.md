## Related Works

Open-vocabulary object detection (OVD) has gained significant attention in recent years as a way to extend object detection models to recognize objects from an unbounded vocabulary, defined by natural language descriptions \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2306.05493)\]. This paradigm allows users to specify categories of interest at inference time without requiring model retraining \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2306.05493)\]. Prior work has explored various approaches to achieve this goal, including leveraging large language models (LLMs) for generating language descriptions \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2306.05493)\], using vision-language models (VLMs) for cross-modal alignment \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/2211.14843)\], and employing pseudo-labeling techniques to generate bounding box annotations for novel objects \[[7](http://arxiv.org/abs/2111.09452)\].

Several studies have focused on improving the alignment between visual and textual modalities to enable accurate detection of novel object categories. For instance, \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/2211.14843)\] proposed a framework that directly learns from image-text pairs, formulating object-language alignment as a set matching problem between image region features and word embeddings. This approach enables training on image-text pairs without the need for expensive grounding annotations. Similarly, \[[14](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/1e651d05e2f6c71081a33cfbc8375093c3c8b043)\] introduced a method that uses scene-adaptive and region-aware multi-modal prompts to enhance the adaptability of vision-language models to diverse classes and align image-level pre-training with region-level detection. Their approach significantly outperformed state-of-the-art models on the OV-COCO and OV-LVIS datasets.

Another line of research has focused on improving the generalization ability of OVD models by incorporating pseudo-labeling techniques. For example, \[[7](http://arxiv.org/abs/2111.09452)\] proposed a method to generate pseudo bounding-box annotations from large-scale image-caption pairs, which are then used to train object detectors. This approach outperformed the state-of-the-art open vocabulary detector by a notable margin on several benchmarks. Similarly, \[[16](http://arxiv.org/abs/2310.01393)\] introduced a dynamic self-training strategy that utilizes pre-trained vision-language models to identify potential novel classes through zero-shot classification. This method improved recall and accuracy for novel classes without requiring additional annotations or retraining.

The challenge of aligning visual and textual representations has also been addressed by methods that leverage contrastive learning. This approach aligned region embeddings of detected boxes with text and image embeddings inferred by the teacher, leading to significant improvements in detection accuracy.
