## Related Works

Open-vocabulary object detection (OVD) has gained significant attention in recent years as a way to extend object detection models to recognize objects from an unbounded vocabulary, defined by natural language descriptions \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2306.05493)\]. This paradigm allows users to specify categories of interest at inference time without requiring model retraining \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2306.05493)\]. Prior work has explored various approaches to achieve this goal, including leveraging large language models (LLMs) for generating language descriptions \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2306.05493)\], using vision-language models (VLMs) for cross-modal alignment \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/2211.14843)\], and employing pseudo-labeling techniques to generate bounding box annotations for novel objects \[[7](http://arxiv.org/abs/2111.09452)\].

Several studies have focused on improving the alignment between visual and textual modalities to enable accurate detection of novel object categories. For instance, \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/2211.14843)\] proposed a framework that directly learns from image-text pairs, formulating object-language alignment as a set matching problem between image region features and word embeddings. This approach enables training on image-text pairs without the need for expensive grounding annotations. Similarly, \[[14](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/1e651d05e2f6c71081a33cfbc8375093c3c8b043)\] introduced a method that uses scene-adaptive and region-aware multi-modal prompts to enhance the adaptability of vision-language models to diverse classes and align image-level pre-training with region-level detection. Their approach significantly outperformed state-of-the-art models on the OV-COCO and OV-LVIS datasets.

Another line of research has focused on improving the generalization ability of OVD models by incorporating pseudo-labeling techniques. For example, \[[7](http://arxiv.org/abs/2111.09452)\] proposed a method to generate pseudo bounding-box annotations from large-scale image-caption pairs, which are then used to train object detectors. This approach outperformed the state-of-the-art open vocabulary detector by a notable margin on several benchmarks. Similarly, \[[16](http://arxiv.org/abs/2310.01393)\] introduced a dynamic self-training strategy that utilizes pre-trained vision-language models to identify potential novel classes through zero-shot classification. This method improved recall and accuracy for novel classes without requiring additional annotations or retraining.

### <a id='category-open-vocabulary-object-detection-frameworks'></a>Open-vocabulary object detection frameworks: 16 papers found.
Open-vocabulary object detection (OVD) has emerged as a critical research direction, enabling detectors to identify objects from an unbounded vocabulary, including novel categories not seen during training \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2306.05493)\]. This paradigm addresses the limitations of traditional closed-set detectors, which are restricted to predefined classes \[[15](http://arxiv.org/abs/2312.02103)\]. Several approaches have been proposed to enhance OVD, such as leveraging pre-trained vision-language models (VLMs) like CLIP for cross-modal alignment \[[6](http://arxiv.org/abs/2309.00227)\], and integrating language supervision to reduce reliance on costly labeled data \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/2211.14843)\]. Methods like ViLD and VLDet utilize knowledge distillation to transfer knowledge from open-vocabulary classifiers to detectors, improving performance on novel categories \[[10](http://arxiv.org/abs/2411.16833)\]. Additionally, frameworks such as PromptDet and OV-DETR introduce novel training strategies, such as self-training and region prompting, to enhance detection accuracy and adaptability \[[5](http://arxiv.org/abs/2011.10678)\]\[[6](http://arxiv.org/abs/2309.00227)\]. The challenge of aligning visual and textual features remains central, with approaches like CoDet and LP-OVOD focusing on improving region-word alignment and filtering low-quality box proposals \[[13](http://arxiv.org/abs/2301.09506)\]\[[9](http://arxiv.org/abs/2503.14862)\]. These works collectively highlight the importance of multimodal alignment, efficient training strategies, and robustness to unseen categories, setting the stage for more versatile and scalable OVD solutions.

### <a id='category-evaluation-benchmarks-for-open-vocabulary-detection'></a>Evaluation benchmarks for open-vocabulary detection: 3 papers found.
The category "Evaluation benchmarks for open-vocabulary detection" encompasses efforts to develop and refine benchmarks for assessing open-vocabulary object detection models. These benchmarks aim to evaluate models' ability to detect objects in novel classes specified via natural language prompts, often focusing on object attributes, fine-grained properties, and the reliability of predictions in the absence of pre-defined class labels \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2306.05493)\]. One key contribution is the introduction of the Open-Vocabulary Attribute Detection (OVAD) benchmark, which includes a densely annotated test set covering 117 attribute classes on 80 object classes from MS COCO, enabling open-vocabulary evaluation with positive and negative annotations \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2306.05493)\]. Another effort, 3F-OVD, extends supervised fine-grained object detection to the open-vocabulary setting by requiring detectors to understand fine-grained captions and image details, while also addressing the challenge of limited fine-grained object detection datasets by introducing the NEU-171K dataset \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/2211.14843)\]. Additionally, research has focused on evaluating models' ability to discern fine-grained properties such as color, pattern, and material, using dynamic vocabulary generation to test models' capacity to detect and assign correct descriptions to objects in the presence of hard-negative classes \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/2205.06160)\]. These benchmarks highlight the importance of accurate and reliable evaluation protocols in open-vocabulary detection, emphasizing the need for models to understand both object classes and their attributes in a flexible and robust manner.
