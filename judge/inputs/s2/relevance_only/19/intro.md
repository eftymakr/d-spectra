## Related Works

Speech-driven 3D facial animation has been a focal point in recent research, with numerous approaches aiming to generate realistic and synchronized facial animations from audio inputs. Early methods primarily relied on phoneme-based models, where phoneme sequences were used to drive facial expressions \[[25](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/0e15c827494535ff7d39000187ccbabcfb5666c4)\]. However, these approaches often struggled with capturing the nuanced variations in facial expressions and lip movements due to the limited context and the lack of high-quality 3D audio-visual data \[[15](http://arxiv.org/abs/1807.07860)\].

Recent advances have focused on leveraging deep learning techniques to improve the accuracy and realism of facial animations. For instance, the Voice2Face model introduced a conditional Variational Autoencoder (cVAE) to generate mesh animations directly from speech, while also mapping animations to rig controller space \[[1](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/435b15745fb279db4ff8daf9d5facb9b21d58f3b)\]. This approach demonstrated significant improvements in perceived animation quality and lip closure accuracy \[[1](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/435b15745fb279db4ff8daf9d5facb9b21d58f3b)\]. Similarly, PMMTalk utilized complementary pseudo multi-modal features to enhance the accuracy of facial animation by aligning audio, image, and text features at both temporal and semantic levels \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/2312.02781)\].

The Learning Audio-Driven Viseme Dynamics approach further advanced the field by learning viseme dynamics from speech videos and producing animator-friendly viseme curves, which support multilingual speech inputs and generalize to unseen voices \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/2301.06059)\]. This method also introduced a viseme scanning approach to acquire high-fidelity viseme assets for efficient speech animation production \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/2301.06059)\].

Another notable contribution is the Content and Style Aware Audio-Driven Facial Animation method, which enables precise control over both content and style of the final performance by extracting temporally-aligned content and disentangled style representations \[[5](http://arxiv.org/abs/2408.07005)\]. This method was trained in two stages, evolving from audio prominent styles to visual prominent styles, and provided flexibility in combining reference audio styles with source audio content \[[5](http://arxiv.org/abs/2408.07005)\].

The Learn2Talk framework addressed the gap between 2D and 3D talking face research by integrating insights from 2D methods into 3D animation. It introduced a 3D sync-lip expert model and a teacher model from 2D methods to guide the training of the audio-to-3D motions regression network, significantly improving lip-sync and vertex accuracy \[[6](http://arxiv.org/abs/2404.12888)\].

The StyleSpeaker framework explicitly extracted speaking styles based on speaker characteristics and accounted for style biases across varying speech conditions, leading to more accurate style modeling and faster adaptation to unseen speakers \[[7](http://arxiv.org/abs/2503.09852)\]. This approach also introduced a trend loss and a local contrastive loss to improve synchronization between synthesized motions and speeches \[[7](http://arxiv.org/abs/2503.09852)\].
