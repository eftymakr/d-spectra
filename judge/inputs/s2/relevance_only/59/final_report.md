Prior research has primarily focused on the trade-off between ranking accuracy and fairness, often treating these as opposing objectives \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2404.17826)\]. This tension has been explored in various contexts, including recommendation systems, where fairness considerations have been primarily directed toward user-side outcomes \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/2005.12974)\]. However, recent work has begun to shift focus toward item-side fairness, recognizing that ranking algorithms can also introduce biases in how items are exposed to users \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/1809.02921)\].
