## Related Works

Nonverbal communication plays a crucial role in human interaction, encompassing gestures, facial expressions, and body language that convey intent and emotion \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/1811.09362)\]. Existing large language models (LLMs) have primarily focused on textual inputs, often neglecting the integration of nonverbal cues, which limits their ability to generate fully immersive conversational experiences \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/1811.09362)\]. This gap has motivated recent research efforts to enhance LLMs with multimodal capabilities.

Several studies have explored the integration of nonverbal behaviors into language models. For instance, \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/1811.09362)\] discusses the importance of modeling nonverbal contexts during face-to-face communication, emphasizing the dynamic nature of speaker intentions based on vocal patterns and facial expressions. The authors propose the Recurrent Attended Variation Embedding Network (RAVEN) to dynamically shift word representations based on nonverbal cues, achieving competitive performance in multimodal sentiment analysis and emotion recognition \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/1811.09362)\]. Similarly, \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/1712.04314)\] introduces the concept of generating and estimating nonverbal alphabets for situated communication, leveraging multimodal data sources such as wearable sensors and brain-computer interfaces to improve the effectiveness of nonverbal communication systems \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/1712.04314)\].

The challenge of modeling human communication has also been addressed by \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/1802.00923)\], which presents the Multi-attention Recurrent Network (MARN) for understanding human communication. This model incorporates multiple modalities—language, gestures, and tone—through a novel architecture that captures interactions between modalities over time \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/1802.00923)\]. MARN achieves state-of-the-art results on various datasets for multimodal sentiment analysis and emotion recognition \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/1802.00923)\].

Recent advancements in vision-language models (VLMs) have enabled more sophisticated handling of multimodal data. \[[4](http://arxiv.org/abs/2404.07214)\] provides a comprehensive survey of VLMs, categorizing them based on their capabilities in processing and generating multimodal data \[[4](http://arxiv.org/abs/2404.07214)\]. These models have been applied to tasks such as image captioning and visual question answering, demonstrating their potential for more complex interactions \[[4](http://arxiv.org/abs/2404.07214)\].

In the context of video-based communication, \[[5](http://arxiv.org/abs/2306.05424)\] introduces Video-ChatGPT, a multimodal model that merges a video-adapted visual encoder with a large language model (LLM) to enable detailed conversations about videos \[[5](http://arxiv.org/abs/2306.05424)\].
