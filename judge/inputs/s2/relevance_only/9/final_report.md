## Related Works

The detection and segmentation of paramagnetic rim lesions (PRLs) in multiple sclerosis (MS) have been the focus of significant research, driven by their association with disease progression and severity. Early efforts relied on manual identification, which is time-consuming and prone to inter-observer variability \[[1](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/09b2a6040c15e8672169531fc76bba0b5a04296b)\]. Recent studies have introduced automated methods to address these challenges. For instance, a fully automated technique was developed to detect PRLs using standard T1 and FLAIR sequences alongside T2*-phase images, achieving a high correlation with gold-standard counts \[[1](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/09b2a6040c15e8672169531fc76bba0b5a04296b)\]. Another study proposed QSM-RimNet, a deep learning-based method for PRL detection, though it lacked rim segmentation capabilities \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/2412.10492)\]. To overcome this limitation, QSM-RimDS was introduced, offering joint PRL detection and rim segmentation with improved performance over QSM-RimNet \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/2412.10492)\]. 

The challenge of class imbalance between PRLs and non-PRLs has also been addressed. QSMRim-Net, a data imbalance-aware deep neural network, was developed to fuse lesion-level radiomic and convolutional image features, achieving a high correlation with expert annotations \[[4](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/45bf918158d3f779cb41b982fa9237682c11dd96)\]. Similarly, RimSet, which combines unsupervised segmentation with radiomic measurements, demonstrated superior performance in detecting PRLs on quantitative susceptibility maps (QSM) \[[9](http://arxiv.org/abs/2312.16835)\]. These methods highlight the importance of integrating both image and radiomic features for accurate classification.

In addition to lesion detection, the segmentation of MS lesions remains a critical task. Various deep learning architectures have been proposed to improve segmentation accuracy. For example, a fully convolutional neural network (CNN) was used to segment white matter lesions, showing significant improvements over existing methods \[[12](http://arxiv.org/abs/1803.09172)\]. The U-Net architecture has also been widely adopted, with modifications such as the modified U-Net model achieving high Dice scores on benchmark datasets \[[15](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/0cdbf68d1adbbc4c4548ce1079737cf7a6a0e8a5)\]. Attention mechanisms have further enhanced segmentation performance by focusing on relevant regions during the learning process \[[28](http://arxiv.org/abs/2304.10790)\]. 

Data augmentation techniques have also been explored to improve model generalization. A study demonstrated that imaging and lesion-aware augmentation methods, such as axial subsampling and CarveMix, can generate diverse samples and improve segmentation performance \[[6](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/b1d6aa6e4f34601f21fdc459d2aed99a464726fa)\]. Similarly, a self-ensembled lesion fusion strategy was proposed to enhance the generalizability of MS lesion segmentation models across different scanners and contrast variations \[[20](http://arxiv.org/abs/2312.01460)\].
