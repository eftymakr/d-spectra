## Related Works

Protein dynamics are essential for understanding biological functions and properties, yet traditional methods such as molecular dynamics (MD) simulations are computationally expensive and limited in their ability to sample rare conformational states \[[22](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/934892bbb74d9cac69061977f5937eba6fd8da52)\]. Recent advances in generative modeling, particularly denoising diffusion models, have enabled more efficient and accurate protein structure prediction and conformation sampling by learning distributions over crystallographic structures \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2402.04845)\]. However, integrating physical supervision into these data-driven approaches remains a challenge, as standard energy-based objectives often lead to intractable optimization \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2402.04845)\]. 

Several studies have explored alternative methods for generating protein conformations. For instance, AlphaFold and ESMFold, which are highly accurate single-state predictors, have been repurposed and fine-tuned under a custom flow matching framework to obtain sequence-conditioned generative models of protein structure \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2402.04845)\]. These models, called AlphaFlow and ESMFlow, provide a superior combination of precision and diversity compared to AlphaFold with MSA subsampling. Additionally, they can diversify a static PDB structure with faster wall-clock convergence to certain equilibrium properties than replicate MD trajectories \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2402.04845)\].

Energy-based models (EBMs) have also been proposed for atomic-resolution protein conformations. These models operate at the atomic scale and are trained solely on crystallized protein data, achieving comparable performance to the Rosetta energy function, a state-of-the-art method widely used in protein structure prediction and design \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/2004.13167)\]. Furthermore, deep learning models based on the transformer architecture have been used to explore the conformational ensembles of protein-protein complexes through MD simulations, demonstrating the ability to generate unsampled conformations for use as an exploratory tool \[[6](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/f1748ef585758622ea82d64e4ba5674ec0636d2b)\].

In addition, the use of diffusion models has been explored for efficient protein conformational ensemble generation. The Angular Deviation Diffuser, a transformer-based diffusion model, generates diverse conformational ensembles of protein backbones by utilizing angular deviations as data flow. This model combines a cutting-edge diffusion model with the principles of SE(3) symmetry to enhance both the accuracy and efficiency of conformational sampling \[[4](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/5d2b69b7ba59017fbf7fed60ffe85165b3207e57)\]. Similarly, the ConfDiff model, a force-guided SE(3) diffusion model, has been proposed to address the limitations of existing score-based diffusion methods by incorporating physical prior knowledge to guide the generation process \[[18](http://arxiv.org/abs/2403.14088)\].
