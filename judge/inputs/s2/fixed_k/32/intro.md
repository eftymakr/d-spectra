## Related Works

Understanding the evolution of feature representations across layers in large language models (LLMs) is crucial for improving their interpretability and robustness \[[12](http://arxiv.org/abs/2207.09225)\]. Recent studies have identified critical layers linked to specific functions or behaviors, but these efforts typically rely on data-dependent analyses of fine-tuned models, limiting their use to post-hoc settings \[[12](http://arxiv.org/abs/2207.09225)\]. In contrast, our work introduces a data-oblivious approach to identify intrinsic critical layers in pre-fine-tuned LLMs by analyzing representation dynamics via Centered Kernel Alignment (CKA) \[[15](http://arxiv.org/abs/2401.11824)\]. This method enables the identification of critical layers without requiring fine-tuning, offering a more generalizable approach \[[15](http://arxiv.org/abs/2401.11824)\].

The concept of representation dynamics in LLMs has been explored in several studies. For instance, \[[12](http://arxiv.org/abs/2207.09225)\] analyzed how representations change during transfer learning and found that pre-trained structures are unlearned if not usable. This aligns with our findings that layers with significant shifts in representation space are also those most affected during fine-tuning \[[12](http://arxiv.org/abs/2207.09225)\]. Furthermore, \[[15](http://arxiv.org/abs/2401.11824)\] introduced Centered Kernel Alignment (CKA) as a method to measure representation similarity, which has been applied in knowledge distillation to bridge the representation gap between large and lightweight models \[[15](http://arxiv.org/abs/2401.11824)\]. Our work builds upon this by demonstrating that CKA can be used to identify critical layers in pre-fine-tuned models, offering a new perspective on how representations evolve during training \[[15](http://arxiv.org/abs/2401.11824)\].

Our spectral analysis reveals that shifts in representation space are driven by changes in the top principal components, which encode semantic transitions from rationales to conclusions \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2411.09539)\]. This finding is supported by prior work on how early layers in LLMs can benefit from cross-domain adaptation, as they capture more generalizable features \[[12](http://arxiv.org/abs/2207.09225)\]. Additionally, \[[13](http://arxiv.org/abs/2411.16260)\] explored how LLMs learn arithmetic by capturing algebraic structures, such as commutativity and identity properties, which may be related to the semantic transitions observed in our analysis \[[13](http://arxiv.org/abs/2411.16260)\].

In practical applications, our findings have implications for efficient domain adaptation and backdoor defense. For domain adaptation, we show that fine-tuning critical layers leads to greater loss reduction compared to non-critical layers \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2411.09539)\]. This aligns with prior work on how parameter-efficient fine-tuning methods can improve performance with limited data \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2411.09539)\]. For backdoor defense, freezing critical layers reduces attack success rates by up to 40% \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2411.09539)\], a result that echoes findings from \[[14](http://arxiv.org/abs/2503.14504)\], which explored the alignment of multimodal LLMs
