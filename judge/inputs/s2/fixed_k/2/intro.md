## Related Works

Nonverbal communication plays a crucial role in human interaction, encompassing gestures, facial expressions, and body language that convey intent and emotion \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/1811.09362)\]. Existing large language models (LLMs) often lack the ability to effectively integrate these nonverbal elements, limiting their capacity to create immersive conversational experiences \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/1811.09362)\]. This section reviews relevant works that address nonverbal communication in AI systems, focusing on multimodal models, datasets, and methods for incorporating nonverbal cues into language understanding and generation.

Several studies have explored the integration of nonverbal cues into AI systems. For instance, \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/1811.09362)\] discusses the importance of modeling nonverbal behaviors, such as vocal patterns and facial expressions, to better capture speaker intentions. This work proposes the RAVEN model, which dynamically shifts word representations based on nonverbal cues, achieving competitive performance in multimodal sentiment analysis and emotion recognition \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/1811.09362)\]. Similarly, \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/1712.04314)\] introduces the concept of nonverbal alphabets, which can be used in situated and multimodal communication, emphasizing the use of wearable sensors and machine learning to estimate the effectiveness of such systems \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/1712.04314)\].

The integration of nonverbal cues into language models has also been explored in the context of human-robot interaction. \[[8](http://arxiv.org/abs/2304.11293)\] discusses the importance of nonverbal cues in human-robot communication, proposing a set of nonverbal codes that can be used to enhance the social agency of robots \[[8](http://arxiv.org/abs/2304.11293)\]. This work highlights the potential of nonverbal cues to make robots feel more "alive" and engaging to users.

In addition, \[[19](http://arxiv.org/abs/2008.13369)\] presents a study on the use of facial affect in automated deception detection, demonstrating that facial valence and arousal can be used to distinguish between truthful and deceptive speech \[[19](http://arxiv.org/abs/2008.13369)\]. This work highlights the importance of nonverbal cues in understanding human behavior and emotions, which is a key aspect of conversational AI.

Recent advances in multimodal models have also focused on the integration of nonverbal cues. \[[11](http://arxiv.org/abs/2501.10857)\] introduces a model that uses implicit behavior cloning to replicate nonverbal cues such as gaze behaviors, demonstrating the effectiveness of this approach in social interaction tasks \[[11](http://arxiv.org/abs/2501.10857)\]. Similarly, \[[21](http://arxiv.org/abs/2307.00841)\] evaluates the advantages of multimodal versus verbal-only communication in robot-to-human interaction, showing that multimodal approaches lead to more natural and effective communication \[[21](http://arxiv.org/abs/2307.00841)\].
