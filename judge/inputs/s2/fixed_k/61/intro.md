## Related Works

Personalized outfit generation has been a focal point in recent fashion AI research, with a growing emphasis on generating compatible and personalized fashion items. Early approaches to outfit recommendation were constrained by pre-defined outfit pools or simple compatibility rules, which limited their ability to adapt to user preferences \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/2211.16353)\]. More recent work has shifted toward generative models that can create new outfits based on user input or preferences \[[22](http://arxiv.org/abs/2203.16161)\]. However, these models often suffer from a lack of diversity and rely on supervised learning paradigms that may not generalize well to real-world scenarios \[[18](http://arxiv.org/abs/2502.08674)\].

Several studies have explored the use of deep learning for outfit generation and recommendation. For instance, \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/1905.01866)\] introduced the Personalized Outfit Generation (POG) model, which leverages Transformer architecture to connect user preferences with outfit compatibility. This work demonstrated that personalization and compatibility could be achieved through large-scale data analysis and user behavior modeling. Similarly, \[[16](http://arxiv.org/abs/2204.04812)\] proposed the OutfitTransformer framework, which uses self-attention mechanisms to learn outfit-level representations that encode compatibility relations between items. This approach improved compatibility prediction and complementary item retrieval tasks.

Another line of research focuses on the integration of user preferences and style into outfit generation. \[[6](http://arxiv.org/abs/1906.07273)\] introduced a method that generates outfits based on a text query, using text and image embeddings to measure item-query coherence. This work emphasized the importance of aligning generated items with user-specified themes or styles. \[[22](http://arxiv.org/abs/2203.16161)\] further expanded on this idea by proposing a style encoder network that maps items to a latent space, enabling the generation of outfits conditioned on high-level style categories such as "formal" or "outdoor."

To enhance the diversity and quality of generated outfits, \[[9](http://arxiv.org/abs/2502.00992)\] introduced FCBoost-Net, a framework that uses a fashion compatibility booster to iteratively improve the compatibility of randomly synthesized outfits. This approach was inspired by boosting algorithms and allowed for the generation of multiple collocated and diversified outfits. \[[18](http://arxiv.org/abs/2502.08674)\] also proposed COutfitGAN, which uses silhouette masks and fashion styles to synthesize photo-realistic images of complementary items. This work demonstrated that compatibility could be improved through semantic alignment and collocation classification.

In addition to generating outfits, some studies have focused on evaluating and improving the quality of generated recommendations. \[[19](http://arxiv.org/abs/1804.09979)\] introduced a system for grading fashion outfits based on visual features and user preferences, achieving high accuracy in predicting outfit quality. \[[17](http://arxiv.org/abs/1806.08977)\] proposed a framework that simultaneously provides outfit recommendations and generates abstractive comments, using a multi-task learning approach to improve both recommendation quality
