## Related Works

Radar and camera fusion has become a critical component in autonomous driving systems due to the complementary sensing capabilities of these modalities \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2307.15264)\]. Early approaches to radar-camera calibration relied on target-based methods, which required complex setups and were difficult to reproduce \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2307.15264)\]. These methods often involved placing calibration targets in the environment and using them to estimate the extrinsic calibration matrix \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2307.15264)\]. However, such approaches are not always feasible in real-world scenarios due to the lack of suitable calibration targets \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2307.15264)\].

Recent research has shifted towards targetless calibration methods, which aim to calibrate radar and camera systems without the need for dedicated calibration targets \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2307.15264)\]. For instance, \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/2309.00787)\] introduced an online targetless calibration method that leverages common features extracted from raw radar data and camera images to match identical objects. This approach uses RANSAC and Levenberg-Marquardt (LM) optimization to improve accuracy and robustness \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/2309.00787)\]. Similarly, \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/2211.01871)\] proposed a method that uses the radar's ability to measure its own ego-velocity to perform spatiotemporal calibration without the need for specialized infrastructure \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/2211.01871)\].

Another approach to radar-camera calibration is based on deep learning techniques that extract common features from both modalities \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/2309.00787)\]. This method implicitly uses these features to match objects from both data sources, enabling online calibration \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/2309.00787)\]. Additionally, \[[4](http://arxiv.org/abs/2103.07505)\] introduced a continuous-time approach for 3D radar-to-camera extrinsic calibration that does not require specialized radar retroreflectors \[[4](http://arxiv.org/abs/2103.07505)\]. This method leverages radar velocity measurements to estimate the extrinsic transformation matrix \[[4](http://arxiv.org/abs/2103.07505)\].

In the context of object detection, several works have explored the fusion of radar and camera data to improve performance in adverse weather conditions \[[5](http://arxiv.org/abs/2210.09267)\]. For example, \[[6](http://arxiv.org/abs/1904.08743)\] proposed a camera-radar matching network called CramNet, which uses a ray-constrained cross-attention mechanism to resolve geometric ambiguities between camera and radar features \[[6](http://arxiv.org/abs/1904.08743)\]. This method supports training with sensor modality dropout, leading to robust 3D object detection even when a sensor malfunctions \[[6](http://arxiv.org/abs/1904.08743)\].

Moreover, \[[7](http://arxiv.org/abs/2307.08339)\] introduced a multi-task cross-modality attention-fusion network (MCAF-Net) that enhances object detection by exploiting information from both radar and camera feature maps \[[7](http://arxiv.org/abs/2307.08339)\]. This method jointly detects objects and segments free space, guiding the model to focus on the more relevant parts of the scene \[[7](http://arxiv.org/abs/2307.08339)\]. Similarly, \[[8](http://arxiv.org/abs/2307.10249)\] proposed RCM-Fusion, a multi-level fusion approach that integrates radar and camera data at both feature and instance levels \[[8](http://arxiv.org/abs/2307.10249)\].

Traditional extrinsic calibration methods for 2D radar sensors have been widely studied, but the emergence of low-cost 3D millimetre-wavelength radars has shifted focus toward more advanced calibration approaches \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2307.15264)\]. Recent work has addressed this by proposing continuous-time calibration algorithms that leverage radar velocity measurements, eliminating the dependency on specialized retroreflectors \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2307.15264)\]. Additionally, the observability properties of these formulations have been analyzed to ensure reliable performance under varying conditions \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2307.15264)\]. ### <a id='category-sensor-fusion-for-object-detection'></a>Sensor Fusion for Object Detection: 17 papers found.
Sensor fusion for object detection in autonomous driving has seen significant advancements, with a focus on integrating radar and camera data to enhance robustness and accuracy. Early methods often relied on late fusion strategies, which fused outputs at the result level, limiting the exploitation of complementary sensor properties \[[9](http://arxiv.org/abs/2410.14505)\]. More recent approaches have shifted toward early fusion, leveraging feature-level interactions to better capture spatial and contextual information \[[16](http://arxiv.org/abs/2209.06535)\]. Techniques such as cross-attention mechanisms and deformable attention have been introduced to align radar and camera features effectively, addressing challenges like depth ambiguity and sparse radar data \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2307.15264)\]. For instance, the CramNet framework proposed a ray-constrained cross-attention mechanism to resolve geometric correspondences between camera and radar features \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2307.15264)\]. Similarly, the MCAF-Net introduced two new fusion blocks to enhance cross-modal information interaction, improving performance in adverse weather and nighttime scenarios \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/2309.00787)\]. The SpaRC model further advanced sparse fusion transformers, integrating multi-view image semantics with radar and camera point features, achieving state-of-the-art results on nuScenes and TruckScenes benchmarks \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/2211.01871)\]. Additionally, the MVFusion method emphasized semantic alignment and cross-modal correlation through a semantic-aligned radar encoder and radar-guided fusion transformer \[[4](http://arxiv.org/abs/2103.07505)\]. The RCBEVDet framework, which operates directly on encoded point features, demonstrated superior efficiency and accuracy compared to dense BEV-based approaches \[[5](http://arxiv.org/abs/2210.09267)\]. These developments highlight the growing trend toward sophisticated fusion strategies that address the inherent challenges of radar-camera integration, paving the way for more reliable and robust perception systems.
