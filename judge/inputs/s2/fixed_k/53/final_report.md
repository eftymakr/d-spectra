## Related Works

Deep sequential recommendation models have seen significant advancements, driven by the need to effectively model user behavior sequences. Traditional approaches, such as RNNs and Transformers, have been widely used to capture sequential dependencies \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2502.08132)\]. However, these models often struggle with computational efficiency and the ability to handle variable-length sequences \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/2409.01192)\]. For instance, Transformer-based models suffer from quadratic complexity due to self-attention mechanisms, which limits their scalability for long sequences \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/2403.03900)\]. This challenge has motivated the exploration of alternative architectures, such as state space models (SSMs), which offer linear complexity and efficient processing of variable-length sequences \[[4](http://arxiv.org/abs/2105.14060)\].

Several works have attempted to address the limitations of attention-based models. Mamba, a selective SSM, has shown promise in efficient sequence modeling due to its hardware-aware design and linear complexity \[[5](http://arxiv.org/abs/2406.02638)\]. Similarly, the SS4Rec model integrates time-aware and relation-aware SSMs to better handle irregular time intervals and contextual dependencies \[[6](http://arxiv.org/abs/2504.05323)\]. These models demonstrate that SSMs can effectively capture temporal dynamics and user preferences while maintaining computational efficiency \[[7](http://arxiv.org/abs/2409.05022)\].

Efforts to improve attention mechanisms have also been made. For example, the Mamba4Rec model introduces selective SSMs to enhance inference efficiency while maintaining model effectiveness \[[8](http://arxiv.org/abs/2411.01537)\]. Additionally, the EchoMamba4Rec model combines bidirectional processing with spectral filtering to capture complex patterns in user interactions \[[9](http://arxiv.org/abs/2012.03323)\]. These approaches highlight the potential of combining SSMs with attention mechanisms to achieve both efficiency and accuracy in sequential recommendation \[[10](http://arxiv.org/abs/2501.01242)\].

Another line of research focuses on multi-head attention and mixture-of-experts (MoE) structures to better capture diverse user preferences. The FAME model introduces a facet-aware MoE structure that disentangles user preferences within different item facets, such as genres or actors \[[11](http://arxiv.org/abs/2311.08272)\]. Similarly, the MAN model employs local and global attention modules to extract both domain-specific and cross-domain information, improving performance in data-scarce scenarios \[[12](http://arxiv.org/abs/2109.05261)\]. These models demonstrate the value of incorporating multiple attention mechanisms to better model complex user behaviors \[[13](http://arxiv.org/abs/2008.08273)\].

Moreover, the use of multi-modal data has been explored to enhance recommendation accuracy. The attention-based sequential recommendation system using multimodal data integrates image, text, and category features to provide more comprehensive item representations \[[14](http://arxiv.org/abs/2209.07997)\]. This approach highlights the importance of leveraging diverse data sources to improve recommendation quality \[[15](http://arxiv.org/abs/2405.17959)\].

### <a id='category-state-space-models-(ssms)-in-recommendation'></a>State Space Models (SSMs) in Recommendation: 5 papers found.
State space models (SSMs) have emerged as a promising approach for sequential recommendation, addressing limitations of traditional methods such as recurrent neural networks (RNNs) and attention-based models \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2502.08132)\]. These models are particularly effective in capturing the dynamic evolution of user interests over time, especially in scenarios with irregular time intervals between interactions \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2502.08132)\]. For instance, SS4Rec introduces a hybrid SSM-based model that integrates a time-aware SSM to handle irregular intervals and a relation-aware SSM to model contextual dependencies, enabling more accurate and personalized recommendations \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2502.08132)\]. Similarly, Mamba, an SSM-based architecture with efficient hardware-aware designs, has shown significant potential for sequence modeling, leading to the development of SSD4Rec, which adapts Mamba for recommendation tasks by incorporating structured state space duality and hardware-aware matrix multiplication \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/2409.01192)\]. Additionally, Mamba4Rec leverages selective SSMs to achieve both high effectiveness and inference efficiency, outperforming RNN- and attention-based baselines \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/2403.03900)\]. EchoMamba4Rec further enhances SSMs by integrating bi-directional processing and frequency-domain filtering, allowing for more effective modeling of complex user interaction patterns \[[4](http://arxiv.org/abs/2105.14060)\]. Furthermore, the application of Mamba to lifelong sequential recommendation has demonstrated its ability to handle long sequences efficiently, with RecMamba achieving comparable performance to existing models while significantly reducing training time and memory costs \[[5](http://arxiv.org/abs/2406.02638)\]. These works collectively highlight the growing interest in SSMs for sequential recommendation, emphasizing their ability to model long-range dependencies and variable-length sequences with linear complexity \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2502.08132)\]\[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/2409.01192)\]\[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/2403.03900)\]\[[4](http://arxiv.org/abs/2105.14060)\]\[[5](http://arxiv.org/abs/2406.02638)\].

### <a id='category-temporal-dynamics-and-user-preference-modeling'></a>Temporal Dynamics and User Preference Modeling: 6 papers found.

Another line of research focuses on improving robustness and generalization by introducing mix-attention mechanisms with regularization techniques \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/2403.03900)\]. These approaches collectively highlight the ongoing efforts to better model temporal dynamics and user preferences in sequential recommendation systems.

### <a id='category-efficiency-and-scalability-in-sequence-modeling'></a>Efficiency and Scalability in Sequence Modeling: 4 papers found.
Efficiency and scalability in sequence modeling have been critical areas of research, particularly in the context of sequential recommendation systems (SRSs), where traditional Transformer models face challenges due to quadratic computational complexity from dot-product attention mechanisms \[[10](http://arxiv.org/abs/2501.01242)\]. To address these limitations, several works have proposed linear attention mechanisms that reduce complexity to linear in sequence length while preserving attention properties. For instance, LinRec introduces an L2-normalized linear attention mechanism that maintains the learning capabilities of dot-product attention while achieving linear complexity \[[8](http://arxiv.org/abs/2411.01537)\]. Similarly, HydraRec builds on Hydra attention, which reduces attention complexity for both token and embedding dimensions, offering improved efficiency for longer sequences and larger datasets \[[10](http://arxiv.org/abs/2501.01242)\]. These approaches aim to balance efficiency with performance, ensuring that models can handle dynamic user behavior and real-time recommendations. Additionally, other works have explored alternative architectures, such as causal convolutions for capturing local item context \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/2403.03900)\] and linear recurrent units (LRURec) that enable fast inference and parallelizable training \[[25](http://arxiv.org/abs/2310.02367)\]. These methods collectively highlight the ongoing efforts to enhance the scalability of sequence modeling in recommendation systems, addressing both computational constraints and the need for capturing diverse user interactions.

### <a id='category-hybrid-attention-and-ssm-approaches'></a>Hybrid Attention and SSM Approaches: 3 papers found.
In the context of hybrid attention and SSM approaches, recent research has focused on improving sequential recommendation by combining the strengths of attention mechanisms with state-space models (SSMs). For instance, cross-domain sequential recommendation models like PiNet and DASL have been proposed to address data sparsity issues, but they often rely on overlapping users across domains, limiting their practicality \[[11](http://arxiv.org/abs/2311.08272)\]. To overcome this, the Mixed Attention Network (MAN) introduces local and global attention modules to capture both domain-specific and cross-domain information, enhancing the model's ability to generalize across different domains \[[11](http://arxiv.org/abs/2311.08272)\]. Similarly, the Frequency Enhanced Hybrid Attention Network (FEARec) shifts the focus from the time domain to the frequency domain, incorporating a ramp structure to better capture both low- and high-frequency information, along with a similar attention mechanism to identify periodic patterns \[[18](http://arxiv.org/abs/2304.09184)\]. Meanwhile, the MLSA4Rec framework integrates Mamba, an SSM with linear complexity, with low-rank decomposed self-attention (LSA) to achieve efficient processing of variable-length sequences while preserving structural bias \[[24](http://arxiv.org/abs/2407.13135)\]. These approaches highlight the growing trend of combining attention-based and SSM-based methods to enhance the modeling of complex user behaviors in sequential recommendation tasks.

### <a id='category-over-smoothing-and-model-complexity-challenges'></a>Over-Smoothing and Model Complexity Challenges: 2 papers found.
Over-Smoothing and Model Complexity Challenges in sequential recommendation have been identified as critical issues in self-attention-based methods, where item embeddings tend to become overly similar across attention blocks, leading to information loss and degraded performance \[[14](http://arxiv.org/abs/2209.07997)\]. This problem is exacerbated in long sequences, as the repeated application of self-attention can cause item representations to diverge from their original form, impairing subsequent learning \[[14](http://arxiv.org/abs/2209.07997)\]. To address this, researchers have proposed alternative architectures that mitigate over-smoothing by introducing mechanisms to preserve item-specific information. For instance, one approach involves modeling item interactions as a star graph, where an internal node captures global sequence information without requiring information propagation among items, thereby preventing over-smoothing and enabling linear time complexity \[[20](http://arxiv.org/abs/2311.07742)\]. Another solution introduces a recursive attentive method with reused item representations, which stabilizes the learning process by maintaining consistent item representations across blocks \[[14](http://arxiv.org/abs/2209.07997)\]. These approaches highlight the broader challenge of balancing model complexity with the need to preserve item distinctiveness in sequential recommendation, particularly in scenarios with sparse or long-tailed data \[[20](http://arxiv.org/abs/2311.07742)\].

### <a id='category-attention-mechanism-limitations'></a>Attention Mechanism Limitations: 2 papers found.
Attention mechanism limitations in Transformer-based sequential recommendation models have been identified as a critical challenge, with empirical studies revealing that large attention weights are sometimes assigned to less relevant items, leading to inaccurate recommendations \[[21](http://arxiv.org/abs/2308.09419)\]. This issue is attributed to factors such as sub-optimal position encoding and noisy input \[[21](http://arxiv.org/abs/2308.09419)\]. Similarly, traditional self-attention models may fail to capture intricate dependencies within item sequences due to the lack of explicit emphasis on attention weights, which are crucial for understanding item-to-item correlations \[[23](http://arxiv.org/abs/2410.21048)\]. To address these limitations, researchers have proposed approaches like Attention Calibration for Transformer-based Sequential Recommendation (AC-TSR), which introduces spatial and adversarial calibrators to refine attention weights \[[21](http://arxiv.org/abs/2308.09419)\], and Attention Weight Refinement for Sequential Recommendation (AWRSR), which enhances self-attention by refining attention distributions through explicit attention weight modeling \[[23](http://arxiv.org/abs/2410.21048)\]. These studies highlight the need for more sophisticated mechanisms to improve the accuracy and effectiveness of attention-based sequential recommendation models.
