## Related Works

Deep-unrolling and plug-and-play (PnP) approaches have emerged as dominant methods for solving inverse problems in imaging, particularly in single-pixel imaging (SPI) \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2107.11007)\]. PnP methods are iterative algorithms where the regularization is implicitly performed by an off-the-shelf deep denoiser \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/2104.10348)\]. These methods are flexible for varying compression ratios (CRs) but often suffer from limitations in reconstruction accuracy and speed \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/2402.12872)\]. On the other hand, unrolling approaches involve transforming a truncated iterative optimization process into an end-to-end trainable neural network \[[4](http://arxiv.org/abs/1902.03493)\]. These methods typically achieve better accuracy and faster inference but require fine-tuning or retraining when CR changes \[[5](http://arxiv.org/abs/2312.15788)\].

Several works have explored the use of unrolling techniques for image reconstruction. For instance, DPUNet was proposed to handle a variety of measurement matrices via one single model without retraining \[[6](http://arxiv.org/abs/2409.04719)\]. Similarly, the Deep Unrolling for Blind Deblurring (DUBLID) architecture was introduced to connect iterative algorithms to neural networks, enabling interpretable and efficient image deblurring \[[7](http://arxiv.org/abs/1912.10557)\]. The convergence properties of unrolled networks have also been studied, with some works establishing theoretical guarantees for their performance \[[8](http://arxiv.org/abs/2211.16881)\].

The plug-and-play (PnP) strategy has been widely adopted in image restoration tasks, where a powerful denoiser is used as an implicit image prior \[[9](http://arxiv.org/abs/1711.07653)\]. However, PnP methods often face challenges in terms of stability and convergence, especially when dealing with complex noise models \[[10](http://arxiv.org/abs/2203.11156)\]. To address these issues, some works have proposed enforcing equivariance on the denoiser to improve the stability and quality of the reconstructed images \[[11](http://arxiv.org/abs/2109.14025)\]. Additionally, the use of stochastic unrolling and operator sketching has been explored to enhance the efficiency of unrolled networks for high-dimensional imaging tasks \[[12](http://arxiv.org/abs/2307.09818)\].

Recent advancements in unrolling techniques have focused on integrating both the flexibility of PnP methods and the performance of unrolled networks.
