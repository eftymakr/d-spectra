## Related Works

Generative retrieval (GR) has gained significant attention as a promising paradigm in information retrieval (IR), offering end-to-end optimization of retrieval tasks by generating document identifiers (docids) directly from queries \[[4](http://arxiv.org/abs/2304.04171)\]. However, most existing GR models are primarily evaluated on static document collections, with limited studies on their performance in dynamic corpora where document sets evolve continuously \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2305.18952)\]. This section reviews related works that address the challenges of GR in dynamic environments, focusing on docid design, continual learning, and efficiency.

Several works have explored the use of text-based docids in GR, which align with the semantic knowledge of large language models (LLMs), enabling better generalization to unseen documents \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2305.18952)\]. For instance, \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/2408.02152)\] proposes a few-shot indexing-based GR framework that generates docids without training, leveraging pre-trained LLMs to create a docid bank for retrieval. This approach improves efficiency and adaptability to dynamic corpora. Similarly, \[[6](http://arxiv.org/abs/2305.13859)\] introduces a framework where docids are generated as sets of terms, allowing for permutation-invariant decoding and robustness against pruning errors. These methods demonstrate that fine-grained docid design can enhance performance in dynamic settings \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2305.18952)\].

In contrast, numeric-based docids, while efficient, suffer from poor performance in dynamic corpora due to their tendency to overfit to the initial document set \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2305.18952)\]. This issue is attributed to the lack of semantic alignment with LLMs and limited lexical diversity \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2305.18952)\]. For example, \[[7](http://arxiv.org/abs/2504.09935)\] highlights that constrained auto-regressive decoding in GR models can lead to suboptimal generalization on out-of-distribution corpora due to the unawareness of future constraints during generation. These findings align with the observation that numeric-based docids exhibit significant performance drops in dynamic settings \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2305.18952)\].

To address these limitations, recent works have proposed hybrid approaches that combine the strengths of text-based and numeric-based docids. For example, \[[9](http://arxiv.org/abs/2404.08137)\] introduces a method that leverages the efficiency of numeric-based docids while incorporating the semantic richness of text-based docids through a multi-docid design. This approach aims to balance performance and efficiency without requiring additional retraining \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2305.18952)\].

This work emphasizes the importance of continual learning in dynamic environments \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/2308.14968)\].

### <a id='category-efficiency-vs.-performance-trade-offs'></a>Efficiency vs. Performance Trade-offs: 4 papers found.
In the context of efficiency versus performance trade-offs in information retrieval, recent studies have highlighted the importance of adaptability and computational efficiency in dynamic environments. For instance, Generative Retrievals (GR) have shown superior adaptability to evolving document collections, with improvements in inference FLOPs, indexing time, and storage footprint compared to traditional methods like Dual Encoders \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2305.18952)\]. Additionally, the design of document identifiers (docids) plays a crucial role in performance over dynamic corpora, with text-based docids demonstrating better generalization and performance due to their semantic alignment with language models' pretrained knowledge and fine-grained design \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2305.18952)\]. In contrast, numeric-based docids, while efficient, suffer from overfitting on the training set, leading to significant performance drops in dynamic settings \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2305.18952)\]. This trade-off between efficiency and performance is also evident in other areas, such as text-to-image diffusion models, where the balance between model complexity and generation quality is a key consideration \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/2308.14968)\]. Similarly, in Scene Text Recognition (STR) tasks, the use of customized, efficient models is essential for practical deployment, especially in scenarios with regular scene text \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/2408.02152)\]. These findings underscore the broader challenge of optimizing retrieval systems for both performance and efficiency, particularly in real-world applications where data is constantly evolving \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2305.18952)\]\[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/2308.14968)\]\[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/2408.02152)\].

### <a id='category-generative-retrieval-(gr)-frameworks'></a>Generative Retrieval (GR) Frameworks: 8 papers found.
Generative retrieval (GR) has gained significant attention as an alternative to traditional retrieval paradigms, with recent works focusing on improving performance over dynamic corpora \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2305.18952)\]. Existing GR methods often rely on training-based indexing, which involves fine-tuning models to memorize associations between queries and document identifiers (docids), but this approach is computationally expensive and limits adaptability to evolving document collections \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/2408.02152)\]. To address these limitations, few-shot indexing-based GR frameworks have been proposed, such as Few-Shot GR, which uses prompt-based generation to create a docid bank without requiring training \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/2408.02152)\]. This approach enables efficient retrieval by constraining the LLM to generate docids within the pre-established bank \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/2408.02152)\]. However, token-level misalignment remains a challenge in generative information retrieval (GenIR), where models trained to predict next tokens struggle to capture document-level relevance \[[5](http://arxiv.org/abs/2504.05181)\]. To resolve this, direct document relevance optimization (DDRO) has been introduced, aligning token-level docid generation with document-level relevance estimation through pairwise ranking, eliminating the need for reinforcement learning \[[5](http://arxiv.org/abs/2504.05181)\]. Additionally, Term-Set Generation (TSGen) has been proposed to overcome issues with sequence-based DocID generation by using a set of terms instead, allowing for permutation-invariant decoding and improved reliability \[[6](http://arxiv.org/abs/2305.13859)\]. These advancements highlight the ongoing efforts to enhance GR models, particularly in dynamic environments, by leveraging pre-trained knowledge and optimizing retrieval effectiveness \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/2408.02152)\]\[[5](http://arxiv.org/abs/2504.05181)\]\[[6](http://arxiv.org/abs/2305.13859)\].

[Snippet]: «Conventional document retrieval techniques are mainly based on the index-retrieve paradigm. It is challenging to optimize pipelines based on this paradigm in an end-to-end manner.
