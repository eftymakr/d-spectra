## Related Works

Maximum Inner Product Search (MIPS) has been extensively studied in recent years due to its critical role in machine learning and information retrieval, especially in high-dimensional data applications \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2503.06882)\]. Early approaches to MIPS primarily relied on inner product (IP) similarity, which, while intuitive, often led to issues with local optima and redundant computations \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2503.06882)\]. Alternatively, some methods attempted to reduce the MIPS problem to nearest neighbor search (NNS) under the Euclidean metric through space projection, but this approach frequently resulted in topology destruction and information loss \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2503.06882)\]. 

Despite these divergent paradigms, recent work has argued that there is no inherent binary opposition between IP and Euclidean metrics. Instead, integrating both metrics in indexing and search algorithms can significantly enhance MIPS performance \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2503.06882)\]. This idea has been explored in several studies, such as the work by \[[11](http://arxiv.org/abs/1405.5869)\], which introduced the first provably sublinear time algorithm for approximate MIPS using asymmetric hashing. This method leverages mathematical insights to convert inner products into near neighbor search problems, enabling efficient sublinear time search \[[11](http://arxiv.org/abs/1405.5869)\]. Similarly, \[[12](http://arxiv.org/abs/1812.06360)\] proposed a bandit-based approach that allows users to control the suboptimality of approximate results without requiring preprocessing, offering a flexible trade-off between accuracy and runtime \[[12](http://arxiv.org/abs/1812.06360)\].

Efficiency and Scalability Improvements in Maximum Inner Product Search (MIPS) have been a focal point in recent research, addressing the computational challenges inherent in high-dimensional data. Existing methods often reduce MIPS to Nearest Neighbor Search (NNS) under the Euclidean metric, leading to topology destruction and information loss \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2503.06882)\], or attempt direct MIPS with suboptimal computation due to the absence of the triangle inequality \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2503.06882)\]. To mitigate these issues, several approaches have been proposed, such as Greedy-MIPS, which introduces a computational budget to balance search efficiency and quality, achieving 200x speedup on large-scale datasets \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/1610.03317)\]. BanditMIPS further improves efficiency by using adaptive sampling strategies inspired by multi-armed bandits, achieving $O(1)$ complexity independent of dimensionality \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/1909.13459)\]. Additionally, Simpfer addresses reverse MIPS by building an index that maintains lower bounds of maximum inner products, enabling fast filtering of user vectors \[[4](http://arxiv.org/abs/2212.07551)\]. CEOs introduces a dimensionality reduction method based on extreme order statistics, achieving sublinear time complexity and high accuracy \[[5](http://arxiv.org/abs/2309.09013)\]. NORM-RANGING LSH tackles normalization issues in real datasets by partitioning data into sub-datasets, improving query efficiency \[[6](http://arxiv.org/abs/1908.08656)\]. Finally, SAH proposes a subquadratic-time algorithm for Reverse k-Maximum Inner Product Search, combining asymmetric hashing and blocking strategies for improved performance \[[7](http://arxiv.org/abs/1509.01469)\]. These approaches collectively highlight the ongoing efforts to enhance MIPS efficiency and scalability through innovative indexing, adaptive sampling, and hybrid metric strategies.

### <a id='category-approximate-mips-algorithms'></a>Approximate MIPS Algorithms: 10 papers found.
The category "Approximate MIPS Algorithms" encompasses a range of methods aimed at efficiently solving the Maximum Inner Product Search (MIPS) problem, which is critical in machine learning and information retrieval. Existing approaches often rely on inner product similarity, which can suffer from local optima and redundant computations \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/1909.13459)\], or transform the problem into Euclidean nearest neighbor search, which may distort data topology and lead to information loss \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/1909.13459)\]. However, recent work argues that IP and Euclidean metrics are not inherently opposed, and integrating both can enhance MIPS performance \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/1909.13459)\]. For instance, the ip-NSW algorithm leverages a norm bias in MIPS, where items with larger norms are more likely to be top results, leading to significant speedups by focusing computation on high-norm items \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2503.06882)\]. Similarly, the ip-NSW+ algorithm improves performance by combining angular and inner-product proximity graphs, enabling more robust search under varying data distributions \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2503.06882)\]. Other methods, such as wedge sampling, have been adapted for budgeted settings, offering efficient and accurate approximations while maintaining high precision \[[6](http://arxiv.org/abs/1908.08656)\]. Additionally, diversity-aware approaches like D$k$MIPS aim to balance relevance and diversity in search results, using techniques inspired by Maximal Marginal Relevance (MMR) \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/1909.13459)\]. Furthermore, hashing-based methods, such as the asymmetric LSH (ALSH) framework, have been developed to enable sublinear time MIPS by converting the problem into near neighbor search \[[5](http://arxiv.org/abs/2309.09013)\]. These approaches highlight the ongoing efforts to improve both the efficiency and effectiveness of approximate MIPS algorithms, addressing challenges such as scalability, accuracy, and adaptability to different data distributions \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/1909.13459)\]\[[6](http://arxiv.org/abs/1908.08656)\]\[[8](http://arxiv.org/abs/2402.13858)\].

### <a id='category-quantization-and-clustering-techniques'></a>Quantization and Clustering Techniques: 5 papers found.
Clustering-based approaches have emerged as a promising direction for addressing Maximum Inner Product Search (MIPS), particularly for sparse vectors, by leveraging efficient partitioning strategies and dynamic pruning mechanisms \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2503.06882)\]. These methods involve partitioning vectors into clusters, allowing only a subset of clusters to be searched during retrieval, thereby reducing computational overhead while maintaining accuracy \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2503.06882)\]. This approach contrasts with traditional methods that often rely on dense vector assumptions or higher-dimensional feature space augmentations \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/1610.03317)\]. Recent work has also explored quantization techniques, where vectors are quantized into subspaces using learned codebooks, enabling fast approximate MIPS without the need for dimensionality expansion \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/1610.03317)\]. Additionally, spherical $k$-means has been adapted for MIPS, offering a more accurate candidate selection compared to Locality Sensitive Hashing (LSH) methods \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/1909.13459)\]. Furthermore, the analysis of quantization error has led to the development of norm-explicit quantization (NEQ), which explicitly minimizes norm errors that significantly impact inner product accuracy \[[4](http://arxiv.org/abs/2212.07551)\]. These advancements highlight the growing interest in hybrid and clustering-based strategies that combine the strengths of different paradigms to improve MIPS performance across diverse applications \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2503.06882)\]\[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/1610.03317)\]\[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/1909.13459)\]\[[4](http://arxiv.org/abs/2212.07551)\].
