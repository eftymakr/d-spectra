## Related Works

Maximum Inner Product Search (MIPS) has been extensively studied in recent years due to its critical role in machine learning and information retrieval, especially in high-dimensional data applications \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2503.06882)\]. Early approaches to MIPS primarily relied on inner product (IP) similarity, which, while intuitive, often led to issues with local optima and redundant computations \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2503.06882)\]. Alternatively, some methods attempted to reduce the MIPS problem to nearest neighbor search (NNS) under the Euclidean metric through space projection, but this approach frequently resulted in topology destruction and information loss \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2503.06882)\]. 

Despite these divergent paradigms, recent work has argued that there is no inherent binary opposition between IP and Euclidean metrics. Instead, integrating both metrics in indexing and search algorithms can significantly enhance MIPS performance \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2503.06882)\]. This idea has been explored in several studies, such as the work by \[[11](http://arxiv.org/abs/1405.5869)\], which introduced the first provably sublinear time algorithm for approximate MIPS using asymmetric hashing. This method leverages mathematical insights to convert inner products into near neighbor search problems, enabling efficient sublinear time search \[[11](http://arxiv.org/abs/1405.5869)\]. Similarly, \[[12](http://arxiv.org/abs/1812.06360)\] proposed a bandit-based approach that allows users to control the suboptimality of approximate results without requiring preprocessing, offering a flexible trade-off between accuracy and runtime \[[12](http://arxiv.org/abs/1812.06360)\].

The importance of graph-based methods for MIPS has also been highlighted in multiple studies. For instance, \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2503.06882)\] proposed a novel theoretical framework that equates MIPS with NNS without requiring space transformation, allowing the use of advanced graph-based indices and edge pruning strategies to reduce unnecessary computations. This approach was further refined with the introduction of the Proximity Graph with Spherical Pathway (PSP) and Adaptive Early Termination (AET) \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2503.06882)\]. Additionally, \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/1909.13459)\] developed the ip-NSW algorithm, which achieves an order of magnitude speedup over the fastest baseline by exploiting the norm bias in the MIPS problem \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/1909.13459)\]. This work was later improved with the ip-NSW+ algorithm, which introduces an angular proximity graph to enhance search efficiency and robustness across different data distributions \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/1909.13459)\].

Efforts to improve the efficiency and accuracy of MIPS have also included the use of quantization and clustering techniques.
