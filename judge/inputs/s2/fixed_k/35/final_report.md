## Related Works

Protein conformational dynamics are essential for understanding protein function and have traditionally been studied using molecular dynamics (MD) simulations, which are computationally intensive \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2004.13167)\]. Recent advances in generative modeling have introduced alternative approaches for protein structure prediction and conformation sampling, particularly through denoising diffusion models \[[21](http://arxiv.org/abs/2209.15611)\]. These models learn distributions over crystallographic structures and enable efficient and accurate prediction of protein conformations \[[21](http://arxiv.org/abs/2209.15611)\]. However, integrating physical supervision into data-driven generative models remains challenging, as standard energy-based objectives often lead to intractable optimization \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2004.13167)\].

Several studies have explored energy-based models for protein conformations, aiming to capture physicochemical properties relevant to protein energy. For instance, an energy-based model (EBM) was proposed that operates at atomic scale and achieves comparable performance to the Rosetta energy function, a state-of-the-art method in protein structure prediction and design \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2004.13167)\]. This model is trained solely on crystallized protein data and evaluates performance on the rotamer recovery task, a restricted problem setting used to evaluate energy functions for protein design \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2004.13167)\].

The use of generative models for conformational ensemble generation has also been explored, particularly through backbone-to-side-chain transformers that maintain conformational diversity and couple side-chain fluctuations using global information about the protein conformation \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/2311.11459)\]. These models combine statistical models of side-chain conformations with transformer architectures to sample with atomistic accuracy \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/2311.11459)\].

Another approach, ConfDiff, introduces a force-guided SE(3) diffusion model that incorporates a force-guided network with a mixture of data-based score models to generate protein conformations with rich diversity while preserving high fidelity \[[8](http://arxiv.org/abs/2403.14088)\]. This model outperforms state-of-the-art methods in conformation prediction tasks, including fast-folding proteins and the Bovine Pancreatic Trypsin Inhibitor (BPTI) \[[8](http://arxiv.org/abs/2403.14088)\].

Structure alignment and protein sequence alignment have also been addressed through Bayesian methods and probabilistic frameworks, enabling the comparison of protein structures and the estimation of evolutionary relationships \[[7](http://arxiv.org/abs/1501.03971)\]\[[15](http://arxiv.org/abs/1404.1556)\]. These methods often incorporate gap penalties and allow for the exploration of multiple competing alignments \[[15](http://arxiv.org/abs/1404.1556)\].

Additionally, structure language models (SLM) have been introduced as a novel framework for efficient protein conformation generation, using discrete variational auto-encoders and conditional language modeling to capture sequence-specific conformation distributions \[[16](http://arxiv.org/abs/2410.18403)\]. This approach provides a 20-100x speedup compared to existing methods, offering significant improvements in generating diverse conformations \[[16](http://arxiv.org/abs/2410.18403)\].

### <a id='category-generative-models-for-protein-structure-prediction'></a>Generative Models for Protein Structure Prediction: 5 papers found.
Recent advances in generative models for protein structure prediction have focused on improving the efficiency and accuracy of conformational sampling and structure generation. Molecular dynamics (MD) simulations, while principled, are computationally expensive and challenging for generating large ensembles of structures or characterizing transitions between metastable conformations \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2004.13167)\]. To address these limitations, researchers have developed generative frameworks that leverage deep learning to learn latent representations of protein conformational landscapes. For instance, ProtScape introduces a geometric deep learning approach that combines equivariant graph neural networks with multiscale wavelet transforms to capture both local geometric interactions and nonlocal collective motions, enabling structured latent spaces for meaningful generation and exploration of protein conformations \[[5](http://arxiv.org/abs/2410.20317)\]. Similarly, ConfRover is an autoregressive model that simultaneously learns protein conformation and dynamics from MD trajectories, supporting both time-dependent and time-independent sampling through a modular architecture that includes an encoding layer, a temporal module, and an SE(3) diffusion model \[[17](http://arxiv.org/abs/2505.17478)\]. Additionally, diffusion-based models have been proposed to generate protein backbone structures by denoising from a random, unfolded state toward a stable folded structure, mimicking the natural folding process \[[21](http://arxiv.org/abs/2209.15611)\]. These models often rely on equivariant architectures to handle the rotational and translational invariance of protein structures, reducing the need for complex equivariant networks \[[21](http://arxiv.org/abs/2209.15611)\]. Furthermore, latent diffusion models have been introduced to reduce the complexity of protein modeling by embedding proteins into a latent space and using diffusion models to learn the distribution of latent representations, enabling efficient generation of novel protein structures \[[22](http://arxiv.org/abs/2305.04120)\]. These approaches collectively highlight the growing trend of integrating physical supervision and deep learning to improve the accuracy and efficiency of protein structure prediction and conformational sampling.
