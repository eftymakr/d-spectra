## Related Works

Diffusion transformers (DiT) have emerged as a powerful architecture for video generation, offering state-of-the-art performance in generating high-quality, coherent video content \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2406.02540)\]. However, their large parameter count and computational complexity hinder deployment on resource-constrained devices \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2406.02540)\]. To address these challenges, model compression techniques such as quantization have been explored to reduce storage and accelerate inference \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2406.02540)\]. Quantization involves reducing the bit-width of model parameters, which can significantly lower memory usage and improve computational efficiency \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2406.02540)\]. However, existing quantization methods for image generation models often fail to generalize to video generation tasks due to the unique challenges posed by temporal dependencies and multi-frame processing \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2406.02540)\].

Several recent works have focused on quantization for diffusion models. ViDiT-Q introduces an improved quantization scheme tailored for DiT models, achieving W8A8 quantization with minimal degradation in visual quality \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/2505.18663)\]. DVD-Quant proposes a data-free quantization framework for video DiTs, achieving a 2× speedup while maintaining visual fidelity \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/2411.14172)\]. TaQ-DiT introduces time-aware quantization for DiTs, addressing issues related to reconstruction and varying quantization sensitivities across layers \[[4](http://arxiv.org/abs/2407.11585)\]. QVD presents the first post-training quantization strategy specifically for video diffusion models, achieving near-lossless performance degradation on W8A8 \[[5](http://arxiv.org/abs/2302.04304)\]. Q-Diffusion proposes a novel PTQ method tailored for diffusion models, achieving 4-bit quantization with minimal performance loss \[[6](http://arxiv.org/abs/2502.15077)\]. The Hardware-Friendly Static Quantization Method for Video Diffusion Transformers demonstrates that static quantization can be a viable alternative to dynamic quantization for video diffusion transformers \[[7](http://arxiv.org/abs/2307.00331)\]. Quantization Variation presents a new perspective on training transformers with low-bit precision, identifying challenges in transformer low-bit quantization-aware training \[[8](http://arxiv.org/abs/2312.00845)\]. These works highlight the growing interest in quantization for diffusion models, but they often focus on image generation or do not address the unique challenges of video generation.

### <a id='category-model-compression-techniques'></a>Model Compression Techniques: 4 papers found.
Model compression techniques have gained significant attention for reducing the computational and storage demands of large models like diffusion transformers (DiT) and video diffusion models (VDMs). Existing methods often overlook critical factors such as the impact of reconstruction during quantization and the varying sensitivity of different model layers to quantization errors \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2406.02540)\]. For instance, DiT models face challenges in achieving effective quantization due to non-convergence issues during weight and activation reconstruction, as well as the sensitivity of Post-GELU activations to quantization \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/2505.18663)\]. Similarly, video diffusion models encounter unique challenges due to the integration of temporal features and inter-channel disparities in activation distributions, making standard quantization strategies less effective \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/2411.14172)\]. To address these issues, researchers have proposed specialized quantization strategies, such as time-aware quantization for DiTs (TaQ-DiT) and the first post-training quantization (PTQ) strategy tailored for video diffusion models (QVD) \[[4](http://arxiv.org/abs/2407.11585)\]. These methods introduce innovations like joint reconstruction for DiTs and temporal discriminability quantization for VDMs, enhancing quantization accuracy and preserving model performance \[[5](http://arxiv.org/abs/2302.04304)\]. Additionally, transformer-based models face unique challenges in low-bit quantization due to their distinct variation behaviors compared to ConvNets, leading to instability in quantization-aware training (QAT) \[[6](http://arxiv.org/abs/2502.15077)\]. To mitigate these issues, variation-aware quantization schemes have been proposed, demonstrating improvements in both vision and language transformers \[[7](http://arxiv.org/abs/2307.00331)\]. Overall, these studies highlight the need for tailored quantization strategies that account for the specific characteristics of different model architectures and tasks.
