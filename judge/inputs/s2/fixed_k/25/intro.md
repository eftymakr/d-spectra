## Related Works

The integration of knowledge graphs (KGs) into large language models (LLMs) for claim verification has gained significant attention in recent years. KGs provide structured, semantically rich representations that are well-suited for reasoning tasks, while LLMs offer strong natural language understanding and generation capabilities \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2305.01157)\]. However, most existing claim verification methods rely on unstructured text corpora, limiting their ability to effectively leverage KGs \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2305.01157)\]. This gap has motivated several studies that explore the synergy between LLMs and KGs for reasoning and verification tasks.

Several approaches have been proposed to enhance LLMs with KGs. For instance, LARK \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2305.01157)\] introduces a decoupled approach that combines contextual KG search with logical query reasoning, leveraging the strengths of graph extraction algorithms and LLMs. This method has shown promising results on standard benchmark datasets, particularly for complex queries \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2305.01157)\]. Similarly, KGLens \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/2312.11539)\] presents a framework for measuring the alignment between KGs and LLMs, using a graph-guided question generator and importance sampling strategy to improve probing efficiency. This work demonstrates that KGLens can assess LLMs with high accuracy, nearly equivalent to human annotators \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/2312.11539)\].

In the context of claim verification, several studies have explored the use of LLMs with external knowledge. For example, Reasoning-CV \[[4](http://arxiv.org/abs/2505.12348)\] proposes a fine-tuning method called CoT-Verify, which generates reasoning paths for complex claims without requiring decomposition into sub-claims. This approach outperforms existing Decompose-Then-Verify methods and achieves competitive results on scientific claim verification datasets \[[4](http://arxiv.org/abs/2505.12348)\]. Additionally, CRAVE \[[14](http://arxiv.org/abs/2504.14905)\] introduces a framework that leverages conflicting rationales generated by LLMs for explainable and accurate claim verification. The framework includes modules for evidence retrieval, conflicting perspective reasoning, and a small language model-based judge to assess the confidence of rationales \[[14](http://arxiv.org/abs/2504.14905)\].

The use of KGs for reasoning and verification has also been explored in other domains. For instance, StructGPT \[[19](http://arxiv.org/abs/2305.09645)\] presents a general framework for LLMs to reason over structured data, using an iterative reading-then-reasoning approach. This method has shown effectiveness in improving the zero-shot reasoning ability of LLMs over structured data \[[19](http://arxiv.org/abs/2305.09645)\]. Similarly, HRGraph \[[12](http://arxiv.org/abs/2408.13521)\] presents a framework for developing HR knowledge graphs using LLMs, demonstrating the utility of KGs in tasks such as job matching and employee skill gap identification \[[12](http://arxiv.org/abs/2408.13521)\].
