## Related Works

Zero-Knowledge Proofs (ZKPs) have emerged as a critical tool in privacy-preserving and verifiable computing, enabling a prover to demonstrate the validity of a statement without revealing additional information \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2408.05890)\]. Recent advancements in ZKP protocols have focused on improving efficiency and reducing computational overhead, particularly in the context of blockchain and machine learning \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/2412.12481)\]. However, generating ZKPs remains computationally intensive, with the proving process often being the primary bottleneck \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/2206.13350)\].

Several studies have explored hardware acceleration for ZKP generation, aiming to reduce the time required for proof generation. For instance, SZKP presents an ASIC-based accelerator that leverages structured dataflows for Number Theoretic Transform (NTT) and Multi-scalar Multiplication (MSM) operations, achieving speedups of over 400× compared to CPU implementations \[[4](http://arxiv.org/abs/2403.15676)\]. Similarly, if-ZKP introduces an FPGA-based architecture for accelerating the MSM operation in zk-SNARKs, achieving 110×-150× speedups over software implementations for specific elliptic curves \[[5](http://arxiv.org/abs/2307.00521)\]. These works highlight the potential of hardware acceleration in improving ZKP performance, though they often require specialized hardware or may introduce trade-offs in terms of proof size or setup requirements \[[6](http://arxiv.org/abs/1911.02459)\].

In the context of ZKP protocols, trusted setup is a common requirement, which limits their applicability in certain scenarios \[[7](http://arxiv.org/abs/2504.01198)\]. For example, many ZKP systems require a one-time setup phase that is typically performed off-chain, which can be a barrier to widespread adoption \[[8](http://arxiv.org/abs/2012.10452)\]. However, HyperPlonk, the protocol underpinning this work, supports a one-time, universal setup, allowing for greater flexibility and scalability \[[9](http://arxiv.org/abs/2408.00395)\]. This is a significant advantage over other ZKP protocols that require application-specific trusted setups \[[10](http://arxiv.org/abs/2307.16273)\].

Another challenge in ZKP systems is the generation of large proof sizes, which can increase verification costs and limit their use in environments with strict time constraints or a large number of verifiers \[[11](http://arxiv.org/abs/2404.06495)\]. To address this, recent works have focused on optimizing the core primitives of ZKP protocols, such as the SumCheck and MSM operations, which are critical to the proof generation process \[[12](http://arxiv.org/abs/2301.02161)\]. For instance, zkSpeed accelerates both of these primitives, achieving geometric mean speedups of 801× over CPU baselines \[[13](http://arxiv.org/abs/2411.06350)\].

Hardware acceleration for Zero-Knowledge Proof (ZKP) generation has emerged as a critical area of research to address the computational inefficiencies inherent in ZKP protocols. Recent works have focused on optimizing key primitives such as Number Theoretic Transform (NTT) and Multi-scalar Multiplication (MSM), which are computationally intensive during proof generation \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2408.05890)\]. For instance, SZKP introduces an ASIC-based accelerator that leverages structured dataflows for both NTT and MSM, achieving speedups of over 400×, 3×, and 12× compared to CPU, ASIC, and GPU implementations, respectively \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2408.05890)\]. Similarly, an FPGA-based architecture has been proposed to accelerate the MSM operation in zk-SNARKs, utilizing optimized modular arithmetic and achieving 110x–150x speedups over software for elliptic curves like BLS12-381 and BN128 \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/2412.12481)\]. Additionally, ZK-friendly hash functions such as MiMC have been optimized for hardware to improve the efficiency of cryptographic hashing within ZKP protocols, with AMAZE demonstrating over 13× performance improvements over CPU implementations on resource-constrained devices \[[13](http://arxiv.org/abs/2411.06350)\]. These advancements highlight the growing interest in hardware acceleration to enable practical ZKP applications, particularly in scenarios requiring high throughput and low latency.

### <a id='category-zkp-protocol-overview-and-importance'></a>ZKP Protocol Overview and Importance: 11 papers found.
Zero-knowledge proofs (ZKPs) have emerged as a critical tool in privacy-preserving systems, enabling parties to verify the truth of a statement without revealing additional information \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/2206.13350)\]. Recent advancements have focused on improving the efficiency and practicality of ZKP protocols, addressing challenges such as computational complexity and scalability \[[10](http://arxiv.org/abs/2307.16273)\]. For instance, the development of zksk, a Python library for defining sigma protocols, has simplified the implementation of ZKPs by providing a domain-specific language that integrates with Python's ecosystem \[[6](http://arxiv.org/abs/1911.02459)\]. Similarly, the introduction of Crêpe has enabled the verification of regular expression equivalence, a PSPACE-complete problem, through a novel ZKP approach that leverages coinduction and regular expression derivatives \[[7](http://arxiv.org/abs/2504.01198)\]. In the realm of hardware acceleration, efforts such as zkSpeed have aimed to enhance the performance of state-of-the-art ZKP protocols like HyperPlonk, achieving significant speedups through optimized architectures for primitives like SumCheck and Multi-scalar Multiplications (MSMs) \[[10](http://arxiv.org/abs/2307.16273)\]. Additionally, research has explored the use of quantum computational models to generalize ZKP systems, demonstrating that problems in the complexity class QMA can be addressed with quantum zero-knowledge proof systems under certain cryptographic assumptions \[[10](http://arxiv.org/abs/2307.16273)\]. Furthermore, practical implementations of ZKPs have been developed for logic puzzles such as Sudoku and Makaro, using physical card-based protocols that allow for secure verification without revealing the solution \[[11](http://arxiv.org/abs/2404.06495)\]\[[16](http://arxiv.org/abs/0705.1129)\]. These developments highlight the growing importance of ZKPs in both theoretical and applied contexts, with ongoing efforts to enhance their efficiency, security, and usability in real-world applications.

### <a id='category-zkp-applications-in-blockchain-and-machine-learning'></a>ZKP Applications in Blockchain and Machine Learning: 4 papers found.
Zero-knowledge proofs (ZKPs) are increasingly being applied across various domains, including blockchain and machine learning, to ensure privacy and verifiability without compromising security \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2408.05890)\]. In blockchain, ZKPs are used to enable transactions that are both private and verifiable, such as in zkFi protocols that allow for compliance with regulations while preserving consumer privacy \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2408.05890)\]. These protocols are designed to be modular and plug-and-play, reducing the need for specialized expertise in ZKPs \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2408.05890)\]. Additionally, ZKPs are being integrated into decentralized applications like NuLink, where they are used for proof of storage, proof of correctness, and secure data trading, ensuring that sensitive parameters remain confidential \[[4](http://arxiv.org/abs/2403.15676)\]. In the realm of machine learning, ZKPs are being applied to verify the training process of deep neural networks without exposing model parameters or training data \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/2206.13350)\]. This has led to the development of zero-knowledge deep learning (zkDL), which enables efficient and secure verification of non-linear computations in deep learning models \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/2206.13350)\]. These applications demonstrate the versatility of ZKPs in addressing privacy and verification challenges across different domains.
