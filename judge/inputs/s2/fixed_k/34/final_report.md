## Related Works

Fund allocation in financial domains has attracted significant research interest, with a growing emphasis on integrating forecasting and optimization in a unified framework. Traditional approaches often separate these tasks, leading to goal mismatch and suboptimal results \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2503.03165)\]. For instance, prediction-only methods neglect the optimization constraints, while Predict-Then-Optimize (PTO) approaches may fail to align the forecasting and optimization objectives effectively \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/2206.03246)\]. This issue is further compounded by the uncertainty introduced by state-of-the-art time series forecasting models, which can affect the reliability of the allocation process \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/2503.04662)\].

To address these challenges, several studies have proposed frameworks that integrate forecasting and optimization. The PTOFA framework \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2503.03165)\] models fund matching under an allocation formulation, combining prediction and optimization stages to maximize revenue while respecting constraints. Similarly, the Portfolio Transformer (PT) \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/2206.03246)\] directly optimizes the Sharpe ratio without explicitly predicting asset returns, leveraging attention mechanisms for better performance in financial asset allocation. These approaches highlight the importance of aligning forecasting and optimization objectives.

Another line of research focuses on risk-aware portfolio optimization, such as the Risk-Aware Trading Portfolio Optimization (RATPO) \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/2503.04662)\], which incorporates market risk and profit&loss measures into the optimization process. The RATS algorithm \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/2503.04662)\] is a specialized Particle Swarm Optimization method that enables efficient portfolio optimization while respecting risk limits. These methods demonstrate the value of incorporating risk considerations into the allocation process.

In the context of time series forecasting, recent advances have focused on improving accuracy and uncertainty quantification. For example, the FinTSBridge suite \[[4](http://arxiv.org/abs/2503.06928)\] evaluates advanced forecasting models in financial contexts, introducing new metrics like msIC and msIR to assess time series correlation. Additionally, the use of multi-source transfer learning \[[5](http://arxiv.org/abs/2503.12648)\] has shown promise in forecasting financial assets with limited historical data, such as new issues or spin-offs. These methods emphasize the importance of leveraging complementary data sources to improve forecasting performance.

Deep learning models, including CNNs and Transformers, have also been applied to financial time series forecasting \[[6](http://arxiv.org/abs/2304.04912)\]. These models are capable of capturing both short-term and long-term dependencies, which is crucial for accurate price prediction.
