## Related Works

Recent advancements in Text-to-Speech (TTS) systems have significantly enhanced speech naturalness, but challenges remain in achieving precise prosody control and effective mispronunciation correction \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2409.12176)\]. Traditional approaches for prosody manipulation often rely on specialized modules or additional training, which limits their ability to perform post-hoc adjustments \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/2102.08328)\]. Similarly, conventional mispronunciation correction methods depend on grapheme-to-phoneme (G2P) dictionaries, which are impractical in low-resource settings \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/2211.13533)\]. This section reviews related works that address these challenges, focusing on prosody modeling, mispronunciation correction, and post-hoc TTS modifications.

Prosody modeling in TTS has been extensively studied, with methods ranging from rule-based systems to deep learning approaches \[[4](http://arxiv.org/abs/2202.07816)\]. For instance, \[[5](http://arxiv.org/abs/2110.02854)\] explores the manipulation of prosodic parameters in TTS to achieve controlled speech generation, comparing TTS-generated audio with human-recorded speech to analyze differences in pitch, duration, and energy. They use tools like PyWorld and Librosa to extract and adjust features, aiming to improve the naturalness of TTS outputs. Similarly, \[[6](http://arxiv.org/abs/2206.14643)\] proposes a context-aware method for more natural-sounding text-based speech editing, addressing prosody mismatches around edited regions by generating salient prosody features and applying denoising neural networks. These approaches highlight the importance of context-aware prosody modeling in improving the quality of edited speech.

Mispronunciation correction in TTS has also been a focus of research, particularly in multilingual and low-resource settings \[[7](http://arxiv.org/abs/2110.02952)\]. \[[8](http://arxiv.org/abs/2309.11487)\] introduces a framework for TTS mispronunciation detection and correction, using a Siamese architecture with Dynamic Time Warping (DTW) and triplet loss to improve pronunciation accuracy. They demonstrate that their approach outperforms phoneme-based and audio-based baselines by ~6% in average pronunciation accuracy. Additionally, \[[9](http://arxiv.org/abs/2412.20155)\] proposes a method for ASR+TTS based voice conversion, emphasizing the importance of prosody modeling in speech naturalness and conversion similarity. They directly predict prosody from linguistic representations in a target-speaker-dependent manner, improving both objective and subjective evaluations.
