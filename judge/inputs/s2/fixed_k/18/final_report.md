## Related Works

Anomaly detection has gained significant attention in recent years due to its critical role in quality control and fault diagnosis in industrial settings. Several approaches have been proposed to address the challenges of deploying anomaly detection models on resource-constrained embedded devices, particularly in small and medium enterprises (SMEs) \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2303.03611)\]. Traditional methods often rely on deep learning models that are computationally intensive and require substantial memory, making them unsuitable for deployment on embedded systems \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/2405.13571)\]. To mitigate these issues, researchers have explored lightweight architectures and efficient inference strategies.

One notable approach is TinyAD, which focuses on reducing memory consumption for CNN-based anomaly detection on microcontroller units (MCUs) by employing depthwise separable convolutions and memory rescheduling techniques \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/2504.08115)\]. This framework achieves up to a 5× reduction in peak memory usage while maintaining negligible computation overhead. Similarly, the work in \[[4](http://arxiv.org/abs/2403.04932)\] introduces a tiled ensemble approach that divides images into tiles to reduce memory consumption during inference, allowing for high-resolution anomaly detection without exceeding GPU memory constraints.

In addition to memory efficiency, the ability to handle incomplete or multimodal data is crucial in real-world industrial scenarios. CMDIAD \[[5](http://arxiv.org/abs/1607.00148)\] proposes a cross-modal distillation framework that enables models to leverage incomplete multimodal information during inference, supporting the Multi-modal Training, Few-modal Inference (MTFI) pipeline. This approach is particularly relevant in settings where only a subset of modalities is available for inspection.

For time-series data, LSTM-based models have been used to detect anomalies by learning to reconstruct normal behavior and using reconstruction error as a detection metric \[[6](http://arxiv.org/abs/2012.03586)\]. This method is effective for both predictable and unpredictable time-series, making it suitable for a wide range of industrial applications. Furthermore, the use of lightweight models, such as MobileSAM, has been explored for image-based anomaly detection in embedded systems \[[7](http://arxiv.org/abs/2505.17551)\].

Another important consideration is the resilience of anomaly detection models against adversarial attacks. A study \[[8](http://arxiv.org/abs/2006.10587)\] highlights the vulnerabilities of process-based anomaly detection systems to synthetic sensor spoofing attacks, emphasizing the need for robust models that can learn and maintain physical properties of the system. This is particularly relevant for industrial control systems where security is a critical concern.

To improve detection accuracy in multiclass settings, CRAS \[[9](http://arxiv.org/abs/2505.07998)\] introduces a center-aware residual anomaly synthesis method that reduces interclass interference and intraclass overlap, leading to better detection performance. Additionally, the use of blockchain for collaborative anomaly detection in IoT environments has been explored, offering a decentralized and secure approach to model training and updates \[[10](http://arxiv.org/abs/2011.06296)\].

[Snippet]: «Monitoring and detecting abnormal events in cyber-physical systems is crucial to industrial production. With the prevalent deployment of the industrial Internet of Things (IIoTs), an enormous amount of time-series data is collected to facilitate machine learning models for anomaly detection, and it is of the utmost importance to directly deploy the trained models on the IIoT devices. However, it is most challenging to deploy complex deep learning models such as convolutional neural networks (CNNs) on these memory-constrained IIoT devices embedded with microcontrollers (MCUs). To alleviate the memory constraints of MCUs, we propose a novel framework named Tiny Anomaly Detection (TinyAD) to efficiently facilitate onboard inference of CNNs for real-time anomaly detection. First, we conduct a comprehensive analysis of depthwise separable CNNs and regular CNNs for anomaly detection and find that the depthwise separable convolution operation can reduce the model size by 50%–90% compared with the traditional CNNs. Then, to reduce the peak memory consumption of CNNs, we explore two complementary strategies, 1) in-place; and 2) patch-by-patch memory rescheduling, and integrate them into a unified framework. The in-place method decreases the peak memory of the depthwise convolution by sparing a temporary buffer to transfer the activation results, while the patch-by-patch method further reduces the peak memory of layer-wise execution by slicing the input data into corresponding receptive fields and executing in order. Furthermore, by adjusting the dimension of convolution filters, these strategies apply to both univariate time series and multidomain time series features. Extensive experiments on real-world industrial datasets show that our framework can reduce peak memory consumption by 2–5× with negligible computation overhead.»

[Snippet]: «Anomaly detection plays a vital role in the inspection of industrial images. Most existing methods require separate models for each category, resulting in multiplied deployment costs. This highlights the challenge of developing a unified model for multiclass anomaly detection. However, the significant increase in interclass interference leads to severe missed detections. To tackle these issues, we propose a novel center-aware residual anomaly synthesis (CRAS) method for multiclass anomaly detection. CRAS leverages center-aware residual learning to couple samples from different categories into a unified center, mitigating the effects of interclass interference. Experimental results on diverse datasets and real-world industrial applications demonstrate the superior detection accuracy and competitive inference speed of CRAS.»
