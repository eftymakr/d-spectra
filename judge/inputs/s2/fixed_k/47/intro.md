## Related Works

Multi-Domain Recommendation (MDR) has emerged as a critical research area in recommender systems, aiming to improve recommendation performance across multiple domains by leveraging cross-domain knowledge transfer \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2201.05973)\]. Traditional MDR approaches often assume a shared structure for transferring knowledge, which may not account for domain-specific variations or lead to negative transfer when domain conflicts arise \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2201.05973)\]. This issue is particularly pronounced in scenarios where domains exhibit significant differences in user preferences or item characteristics \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2201.05973)\]. 

Several studies have explored the challenges of cross-domain knowledge transfer. For example, \[[8](http://arxiv.org/abs/1210.7056)\] proposed a selective transfer learning framework that uses empirical prediction error and its variance to better capture domain consistency in collaborative filtering settings. This approach helps in filtering out inconsistent knowledge from source domains, thereby mitigating the negative transfer problem. Similarly, \[[12](http://arxiv.org/abs/1905.10756)\] introduced a reinforced transfer network (RTNet) that utilizes both high-level and pixel-level information for partial domain adaptation, enabling more accurate selection of relevant source samples. This method is particularly useful in scenarios where domain shifts are significant \[[12](http://arxiv.org/abs/1905.10756)\].

In the context of MDR, \[[7](http://arxiv.org/abs/2309.06217)\] proposed a hierarchical hypergraph network based framework that models correlative preference transfer by representing multi-domain user-item interactions into a unified graph. This allows for the extraction of correlative information from multi-domain user-item feedbacks and the aggregation of users’ scattered preferences across domains \[[7](http://arxiv.org/abs/2309.06217)\]. Another approach, \[[11](http://arxiv.org/abs/1812.06229)\], introduced a domain-to-domain translation model (D2D-TM) based on generative adversarial networks (GANs), Variational Autoencoders (VAEs), and Cycle-Consistency (CC) for weight-sharing. This model is designed to extract both homogeneous and divergent features across domains, enhancing the ability to support cross-domain recommendations \[[11](http://arxiv.org/abs/1812.06229)\].

Recent works have also focused on the challenges of modeling domain-specific preferences and improving knowledge transfer. \[[6](http://arxiv.org/abs/2403.17374)\] introduced a framework that models users’ preferences at both the domain and item levels, learning various seen-unseen domain mappings in a unified way with masked domain modeling. This approach effectively captures domain-level preferences and improves recommendation accuracy for unseen domains \[[6](http://arxiv.org/abs/2403.17374)\]. Additionally, \[[9](http://arxiv.org/abs/2405.12892)\] proposed a domain-sensitive feature attribution method to identify features that best reflect domain distinctions, enabling more accurate modeling of domain-specific characteristics \[[9](http://arxiv.org/abs/2405.12892)\].

To address the limitations of existing MDR methods, \[[10](http://arxiv.org/abs/2302.05990)\] introduced MAGRec, a multi-domain graph-based recommender that learns sequential representations of user interactions using graph neural networks.
