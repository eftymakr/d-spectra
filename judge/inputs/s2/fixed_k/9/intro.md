## Related Works

The detection and segmentation of paramagnetic rim lesions (PRLs) in multiple sclerosis (MS) have been the focus of several recent studies, particularly due to their significance as emerging biomarkers. Traditional methods for identifying PRLs rely on manual segmentation and expert interpretation, which are time-consuming and prone to inter-rater variability \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2412.10492)\]. To address these limitations, deep learning approaches have been proposed to automate the detection and segmentation of PRLs. For instance, QSM-RimNet was introduced as a deep learning-based method for PRL detection, but it lacks the capability to provide accurate rim segmentation for microglial density quantification \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2412.10492)\]. In contrast, QSM-RimDS was developed as a U-Net-based method that not only detects PRLs but also provides reasonable rim segmentation, achieving a mean Dice similarity coefficient (DSC) of 0.57 with moderate to high agreement \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2412.10492)\]. This represents a significant improvement over manual segmentation and other automated methods.

The challenge of class imbalance between PRLs and non-PRLs has also been addressed in the literature. For example, the work by Dogabasaran et al. introduced a novel foreground-based generative method for modeling local lesion characteristics, which can generate synthetic lesions on healthy images and synthesize subject-specific pseudo-healthy images from pathological ones \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/2208.02135)\]. This approach has been shown to improve segmentation performance when used as a data augmentation module for training brain image segmentation networks \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/2208.02135)\]. Similarly, the use of generative adversarial networks (GANs) for synthesizing realistic skin lesions has been explored, demonstrating that high-resolution synthetic images can be generated to augment training data and improve segmentation accuracy \[[8](http://arxiv.org/abs/1809.01410)\]. These methods are particularly relevant for addressing the class imbalance issue in PRL detection.

In addition to lesion detection, several studies have focused on lesion segmentation using deep learning techniques. For example, the work by Zhang et al. proposed a fully convolutional neural network (CNN) for segmenting white matter lesions from multi-contrast MR images, achieving significant improvements in segmentation quality over existing methods \[[6](http://arxiv.org/abs/1803.09172)\]. Another study introduced a multi-branch CNN for MS lesion segmentation, incorporating multi-scale feature fusion blocks to combine information from different modalities and improve segmentation accuracy \[[29](http://arxiv.org/abs/1811.02942)\]. Furthermore, the use of attention mechanisms has been explored to enhance lesion segmentation performance, with methods such as the attention-guided two-path CNNs showing promising results in lesion activity segmentation \[[5](http://arxiv.org/abs/2008.02001)\].

The importance of quantitative analysis of PRLs has also been highlighted in the literature.
