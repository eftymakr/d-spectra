## Related Works

The rapid growth of open-source machine learning (ML) resources has significantly impacted information retrieval (IR) research, as these resources are increasingly used for tasks such as model evolution tracing and dataset recommendation \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2502.04484)\]. However, existing platforms like Hugging Face have limitations in utilizing structured representations, which restricts advanced queries and analyses \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2502.04484)\]. This section reviews related works that address these challenges, focusing on dataset and model documentation, supply chain analysis, and knowledge graph construction for ML resources.

Several studies have examined the documentation and supply chain of ML models and datasets on Hugging Face. For instance, an empirical analysis of 760,460 models and 175,000 datasets revealed significant gaps in documentation and licensing practices \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/2401.13822)\]. Similarly, a large-scale analysis of dataset cards on Hugging Face highlighted inconsistencies in documentation completeness, with certain sections receiving less attention than others \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/2311.13380)\]. These findings underscore the need for better documentation practices and structured representations to support advanced querying and analysis \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/2311.13380)\].

The lack of structured representations in Hugging Face has also been a focus of other studies. A repository mining study on the carbon footprint of ML models found that only a small proportion of models report carbon emissions, indicating a need for more transparent and standardized reporting practices \[[4](http://arxiv.org/abs/2402.07323)\]. Another study on the evolution and maintenance of ML models on Hugging Face emphasized the importance of tracking model updates and maintenance activities, which could be facilitated by structured data formats \[[5](http://arxiv.org/abs/2305.11164)\]. These studies highlight the limitations of current platforms in supporting advanced analyses of ML resources.

To address these limitations, several works have proposed knowledge graphs for organizing and managing ML resources. A comprehensive survey on zero-shot and few-shot learning with knowledge graphs explored how auxiliary information, such as knowledge graph structures, can be used to reduce reliance on labeled data \[[6](http://arxiv.org/abs/2307.14841)\]. Additionally, a study on knowledge graph curation proposed a framework for improving the quality of knowledge graphs by defining quality metrics and implementing cleaning and enrichment strategies \[[7](http://arxiv.org/abs/2303.17580)\]. These works demonstrate the potential of knowledge graphs in organizing and analyzing ML resources.

The construction of HuggingKG, a large-scale knowledge graph built from the Hugging Face community, addresses these gaps by capturing domain-specific relations and rich textual attributes \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2502.04484)\]. This work builds on prior studies that have highlighted the need for structured representations in ML resource management \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/2401.13822)\]\[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/2311.13380)\]\[[5](http://arxiv.org/abs/2305.11164)\]. By leveraging the Hugging Face ecosystem, HuggingKG enables advanced queries and analyses, such as model evolution tracing and dataset recommendation \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2502.04484)\].

### <a id='category-zero-shot-and-few-shot-learning-with-knowledge-graphs'></a>Zero-Shot and Few-Shot Learning with Knowledge Graphs: 1 papers found.
The category "Zero-Shot and Few-Shot Learning with Knowledge Graphs" focuses on addressing the challenges of sample scarcity in machine learning by leveraging knowledge graphs (KGs) as a source of auxiliary information. This approach is particularly relevant in scenarios where labeled data is limited or unavailable, such as in zero-shot learning (ZSL), where the model must predict classes with no prior examples, and few-shot learning (FSL), where only a small number of labeled samples are available \[[14](http://arxiv.org/abs/2112.10006)\]. KGs provide structured, semantic relationships that can enhance model performance by capturing domain knowledge and enabling more effective generalization. The integration of KGs into ZSL and FSL has led to the development of various paradigms, including mapping-based methods that align KG entities with model representations, data augmentation techniques that generate synthetic data using KG information, propagation-based approaches that leverage relational patterns, and optimization-based strategies that incorporate KG constraints into training objectives \[[14](http://arxiv.org/abs/2112.10006)\]. These methods have been applied to a range of tasks, including image classification, question answering, text classification, and knowledge extraction, as well as KG completion tasks, demonstrating the versatility and effectiveness of KG-aware learning in resource-constrained settings \[[14](http://arxiv.org/abs/2112.10006)\]. The use of KGs in these contexts not only reduces the reliance on labeled data but also improves the interpretability and robustness of ML models by grounding predictions in structured knowledge.
