## Related Works

Rehearsal-based methods have been widely studied in continual learning (CL) to mitigate catastrophic forgetting, where models forget previously learned knowledge when trained on new tasks. These methods typically involve storing a subset of past data in a memory buffer and reusing it during training on new data \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2104.07446)\]. However, the optimal strategy for rehearsal remains an open question. While concurrent rehearsal, where past and new data are trained together, is commonly used, recent work suggests that sequential rehearsal, where new data is trained first and past data is revisited later, may offer superior performance in certain scenarios \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/2302.05836)\]. 

The effectiveness of rehearsal strategies has been explored in various contexts. For instance, \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/2306.17091)\] highlights that models trained sequentially with rehearsal tend to stay in a low-loss region but risk overfitting on the sample memory, potentially harming generalization. This aligns with findings that sequential rehearsal can improve performance when tasks are less similar \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/2302.05836)\]. Furthermore, \[[4](http://arxiv.org/abs/2203.14544)\] introduces a coreset selection method for rehearsal-based CL, emphasizing the importance of selecting informative samples to maintain performance without excessive memory usage. 

Several studies have investigated the role of task similarity in rehearsal strategies. \[[5](http://arxiv.org/abs/1812.02464)\] shows that catastrophic forgetting is most severe when tasks are dissimilar, suggesting that rehearsal strategies should be adapted based on task relationships. This idea is further supported by \[[6](http://arxiv.org/abs/2103.07492)\], which proposes a hybrid rehearsal method that trains similar tasks concurrently and revisits dissimilar tasks sequentially, leading to improved performance. The hybrid approach is motivated by the observation that sequential rehearsal is more effective when tasks are less similar \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/2302.05836)\].

The theoretical underpinnings of rehearsal-based CL have also been explored. \[[7](http://arxiv.org/abs/2312.00276)\] provides a theoretical analysis of forgetting and generalization error in overparameterized linear models, showing that sequential rehearsal outperforms concurrent rehearsal when tasks are less similar. This theoretical insight is corroborated by empirical results in deep neural networks \[[8](http://arxiv.org/abs/2405.04101)\]. Additionally, \[[9](http://arxiv.org/abs/2306.10181)\] investigates the role of robust features in mitigating catastrophic forgetting, demonstrating that models trained on robust features experience less forgetting than those trained on standard datasets.

Recent work has also explored alternative approaches to rehearsal, such as pseudo-rehearsal and self-synthesized rehearsal. \[[10](http://arxiv.org/abs/2004.13414)\] introduces pseudo-rehearsal, where synthetic data is generated for rehearsal, reducing memory and computational costs. Similarly, \[[11](http://arxiv.org/abs/2403.01244)\] proposes self-synthesized rehearsal for large language models, generating synthetic instances to preserve past knowledge without requiring access to original training data. These methods highlight the growing interest in data-efficient rehearsal strategies.

### <a id='category-theoretical-analysis-of-forgetting'></a>Theoretical Analysis of Forgetting: 7 papers found.
The theoretical analysis of forgetting in continual learning (CL) has been a focal point in recent research, with multiple studies addressing the challenges of catastrophic forgetting and generalization performance. Researchers have explored the impact of overparameterization, task similarity, and task ordering on forgetting, with some studies providing explicit forms of expected forgetting and generalization error under linear models \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2104.07446)\]. Additionally, the role of task sequence and algorithmic parameters has been examined, revealing that tasks with larger eigenvalues in their population data covariance matrices, when trained later, can lead to increased forgetting \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/2306.17091)\]. Theoretical frameworks have also been developed to understand how buffer size and model capacity influence the forgetting rate in replay-based CL models \[[4](http://arxiv.org/abs/2203.14544)\]. Furthermore, the use of consistency regularization in Experience Replay (ER) has been investigated, showing that stricter consistency constraints can preserve previous task information more effectively \[[7](http://arxiv.org/abs/2312.00276)\]. These findings highlight the importance of both theoretical and empirical approaches in designing more effective CL algorithms.

### <a id='category-data-efficiency-in-rehearsal'></a>Data Efficiency in Rehearsal: 3 papers found.
In the context of data efficiency in rehearsal, several approaches have been proposed to mitigate catastrophic forgetting in continual learning. Traditional rehearsal methods rely on storing previous task data to maintain model performance, but this can be computationally expensive \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2104.07446)\]. To address this, pseudo rehearsal was introduced, where synthetic data is generated to replace the need for storing entire previous datasets, leading to reduced computational and memory resource consumption \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2104.07446)\]. More recently, Self-Synthesized Rehearsal (SSR) has been proposed, leveraging large language models to generate and refine synthetic instances for rehearsal, which improves data efficiency while preserving model generalization capabilities \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/2302.05836)\]. Additionally, the mix-cd approach focuses on prioritizing rehearsal of "collateral damage" samples—those correctly predicted by the prior model but forgotten by the incrementally tuned one—without requiring additional model inferences, thereby enhancing computational efficiency \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/2306.17091)\]. These methods collectively highlight the importance of data efficiency in rehearsal strategies, emphasizing the trade-offs between data storage, computational cost, and model performance in continual learning scenarios.

### <a id='category-alternative-rehearsal-techniques'></a>Alternative Rehearsal Techniques: 3 papers found.
The category "Alternative Rehearsal Techniques" explores methods to mitigate catastrophic forgetting in continual learning (CL) without relying on traditional memory replay buffers. These techniques aim to improve performance while reducing computational and memory overhead. For instance, ensemble models have been proposed as a simple yet effective approach to enhance continual performance by leveraging multiple models, though they come with increased training and inference costs \[[18](http://arxiv.org/abs/2202.09826)\]. To address this limitation, researchers have investigated scalable alternatives such as SPARC, which eliminates the need for experience rehearsal and full-model surrogates by combining task-specific working memories with task-agnostic semantic memory, achieving parameter efficiency and competitive performance \[[21](http://arxiv.org/abs/2505.21942)\]. Additionally, memoryless methods have been introduced that use self-organizing maps to adaptively cluster inputs into task contexts without explicit labels, maintaining past learning while enabling efficient resource allocation \[[22](http://arxiv.org/abs/1904.09330)\]. These approaches highlight the growing interest in alternative rehearsal strategies that balance performance, efficiency, and scalability in CL.
