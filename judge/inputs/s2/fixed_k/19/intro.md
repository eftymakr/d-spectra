## Related Works

Speech-driven 3D facial animation has been a focal point in computer vision and machine learning research, with numerous approaches aiming to synthesize realistic facial expressions that synchronize with speech. Early methods primarily relied on handcrafted features and rule-based systems, which lacked the flexibility and accuracy required for complex facial animations \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2312.02781)\]. With the advent of deep learning, researchers began exploring data-driven models that could learn mappings from audio signals to facial motions \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/2503.09852)\].

A significant body of work focuses on improving lip synchronization by leveraging audio-visual data. For instance, \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/2112.02214)\] introduced a system that generates talking head videos directly from raw audio, eliminating the need for handcrafted intermediate features. This method uses a temporal GAN with two discriminators to capture different aspects of the video, resulting in more natural sequences than static GAN-based approaches. Similarly, \[[4](http://arxiv.org/abs/2312.10877)\] proposed a diffusion model (SAiD) that enhances lip synchronization by incorporating a cross-modality alignment bias between audio and visual features, leading to more diverse and accurate lip movements.

Several studies have also emphasized the importance of incorporating visual and textual cues to improve the accuracy and coherence of facial animations. \[[5](http://arxiv.org/abs/2407.12501)\] argued that visual and textual information is crucial for achieving realistic facial expressions and proposed a framework that integrates these modalities to enhance the quality of generated animations. This approach aligns with \[[6](http://arxiv.org/abs/2407.01034)\], which introduced a joint audio-text model to capture contextual information for expressive speech-driven 3D facial animation, showing that combining acoustic and textual modalities can synthesize more realistic facial expressions while maintaining audio-lip synchronization.

The challenge of modeling diverse speaking styles and emotional expressions has also been addressed in recent research. \[[7](http://arxiv.org/abs/2409.01113)\] proposed a method that disentangles speaking styles from semantic content, enabling the synthesis of realistic facial animations that match the unique speaking style of a subject. This work builds on earlier efforts such as \[[8](http://arxiv.org/abs/2409.19143)\], which introduced a framework for disentangling speaking style and content from facial motions, leading to more realistic and controllable facial animations.

In addition to style and emotion modeling, researchers have explored the use of pre-trained models and self-supervised learning to improve the quality of speech-driven facial animation. \[[9](http://arxiv.org/abs/2303.11089)\] proposed a method that leverages self-supervised speech representations to enhance the realism of generated facial expressions. This approach is similar to \[[10](http://arxiv.org/abs/2310.17011)\], which introduced a system for real-time speech-driven 3D facial animation from raw waveforms, directly mapping speech audio to facial motion activations.
