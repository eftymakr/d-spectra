## Related Works

Narrative summarization, particularly for long-form texts such as books, movies, and TV scripts, remains a challenging task due to the complexity of plotlines, character interactions, and thematic coherence \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2004.12727)\]. Existing approaches often rely on general-purpose extractive summarization models trained on news articles, which are short and present information upfront, leading to biases in sentence selection based on position \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2004.12727)\]. These models struggle with long narratives that present information piecemeal and require deeper structural understanding \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2004.12727)\]. 

Several works have explored plan-guided summarization for narrative texts, aiming to reduce hallucinations in small language models (SLMs) by grounding summaries to the source text \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/2504.09071)\]. However, these approaches have not significantly improved summary quality or faithfulness, as plans are equally likely to contain hallucinations as the summaries themselves \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/2504.09071)\]. Similarly, while some studies have investigated the use of multi-LLM agents for complex tasks, they often focus on specific components like task planning or tool invocation without addressing the broader challenge of long-form narrative summarization \[[4](http://arxiv.org/abs/2401.07324)\]. 

The use of multi-agent frameworks for long-form text generation has also been explored, with frameworks such as CogWriter introducing cognitive writing modules that decompose tasks into planning, generation, and review stages \[[6](http://arxiv.org/abs/2502.12568)\]. These systems aim to mimic human cognitive processes, enabling more structured and coherent output. However, they typically focus on constrained long-form generation rather than the specific challenges of narrative summarization \[[6](http://arxiv.org/abs/2502.12568)\]. 

In contrast, the proposed NexusSum framework introduces a structured, sequential pipeline that does not require fine-tuning, leveraging multi-agent LLMs to process long-form narratives effectively \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2004.12727)\]. The Dialogue-to-Description Transformation preprocessing method standardizes character dialogue and descriptive text, improving coherence by aligning narrative elements \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2004.12727)\]. This is similar to the Dial2Desc task, which focuses on generating concise descriptions of objects or actions from dialogue \[[10](http://arxiv.org/abs/1811.00185)\], but NexusSum extends this to full narrative summarization. 

The Hierarchical Multi-LLM Summarization approach in NexusSum optimizes chunk processing and controls output length, ensuring accurate and high-quality summaries \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2004.12727)\]. This is akin to the SEAL model, which dynamically extracts input snippets for each output segment, enabling effective long-form abstractive summarization \[[5](http://arxiv.org/abs/2006.10213)\]. However, NexusSum introduces a structured pipeline that avoids the need for fine-tuning, making it more scalable for diverse storytelling domains \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2004.12727)\].
