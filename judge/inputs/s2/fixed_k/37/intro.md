## Related Works

Vision Transformers (ViTs) have emerged as a powerful alternative to convolutional neural networks (CNNs) in computer vision tasks, leveraging self-attention mechanisms to capture global context \[[7](http://arxiv.org/abs/2503.02891)\]. However, their deployment on resource-constrained hardware remains challenging due to their high computational and memory demands \[[7](http://arxiv.org/abs/2503.02891)\]. Existing methods often rely on CPU for complex operations such as Softmax and LayerNorm, leading to suboptimal performance due to communication overhead between CPU and accelerator \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2211.08110)\]. This section reviews related works that address ViT hardware acceleration, model compression, and efficient inference strategies.

Several studies have focused on reducing the computational cost of ViTs through token pruning and attention optimization. HeatViT introduces an adaptive token pruning framework that dynamically identifies and consolidates non-informative tokens, achieving significant computation reduction while maintaining accuracy \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2211.08110)\]. Similarly, Evo-ViT proposes a slow-fast token evolution approach that maintains spatial structure and information flow, enabling efficient training and inference \[[9](http://arxiv.org/abs/2108.01390)\]. ViTCoD further advances this by combining algorithmic and hardware co-design, pruning attention maps and integrating lightweight auto-encoder modules to reduce data movement \[[5](http://arxiv.org/abs/2210.09573)\]. These methods demonstrate that reducing attention computations can significantly improve hardware efficiency.

Model quantization has also been widely explored to reduce the computational demands of ViTs. ViTA employs a head-level pipeline and inter-layer MLP optimizations, achieving nearly 90% hardware utilization efficiency and low power consumption \[[4](http://arxiv.org/abs/2302.09108)\]. SuperViT introduces a novel training paradigm that reduces computational costs while improving performance, achieving up to 2× FLOPs reduction with minimal accuracy loss \[[6](http://arxiv.org/abs/2205.11397)\]. These quantization techniques enable ViTs to be deployed on edge devices with limited resources.

Hardware acceleration for ViTs has seen significant progress, with dedicated accelerators designed for FPGAs and ASICs. CHOSEN introduces a software-hardware co-design framework that maximizes bandwidth and efficiently uses FPGA resources, achieving throughput improvements of 1.5× and 1.42× on DeiT-S and DeiT-B models \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/2407.12736)\]. ViTALiTy unifies low-rank and sparse approximations for attention, improving inference efficiency with a linear Taylor attention mechanism \[[8](http://arxiv.org/abs/2211.05109)\]. These accelerators demonstrate that tailored hardware designs can significantly enhance the performance of ViT models.

In addition to algorithmic and hardware optimizations, recent work has explored the use of sparse attention and efficient data formats to improve ViT inference.
