## Related Works

The growing adoption of cloud-native applications has led to an increased reliance on managed cloud services and containerization technologies like Kubernetes, which enable rapid deployment and scalability \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2501.14753)\]. However, the financial implications of hosting containerized applications on cloud platforms have become a critical concern for organizations \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2501.14753)\]. This has given rise to the FinOps discipline, which integrates financial management with cloud operations to optimize costs \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2501.14753)\]. While prior research has explored system-level optimization strategies for cost and resource efficiency in containerized systems \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2501.14753)\], the analysis of network costs in Kubernetes clusters remains underexplored \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2501.14753)\].

Several studies have focused on optimizing cloud costs through automated budgeting and resource allocation. ABACUS is a FinOps solution that sets budgets, enforces them through blocking new deployments, and alerts teams when spending exceeds thresholds \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/2307.12479)\]. It also leverages Infrastructure-as-Code to estimate deployment costs before resource allocation \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/2307.12479)\]. Similarly, SAGE introduces a tool for optimal deployment plans in Kubernetes clusters, minimizing infrastructure rental costs by analyzing application demand and cluster resources \[[5](http://arxiv.org/abs/2307.06318)\]. These approaches highlight the importance of integrating financial considerations into deployment strategies.

In the context of microservices, SpotKube offers a Kubernetes-based solution that uses a genetic algorithm to optimize deployment costs by dynamically scaling clusters and leveraging spot pricing \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/2405.12311)\]. This aligns with broader efforts to reduce cloud expenses through cost-effective resource allocation. Additionally, KubeAdaptor addresses the challenge of maintaining task scheduling consistency between workflow systems and Kubernetes, ensuring efficient execution of containerized workflows \[[6](http://arxiv.org/abs/2207.01222)\]. This is particularly relevant for applications requiring strict scheduling constraints.

Cloud and AI infrastructure cost optimization has also been extensively studied, with a focus on reducing GPU compute costs for AI/ML workloads \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/2307.12479)\]. Techniques such as model quantization, GPU instance selection, and inference optimization have shown significant cost reductions, with some studies reporting up to 10x improvements since 2021 \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/2307.12479)\]. These strategies are crucial for organizations relying heavily on AI-driven applications.

Network cost analysis in Kubernetes clusters is a relatively new area, but several studies have explored the financial and operational aspects of managing network costs in cloud-native environments. The paper under review contributes to this field by combining measurement analysis, experimentation, and cost modeling to provide actionable insights into network cost optimization \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2501.14753)\]. This aligns with broader efforts to integrate financial considerations into cloud operations, as seen in the FinOps framework \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2501.14753)\].

### <a id='category-task-scheduling-and-workflow-management-in-kubernetes'></a>Task Scheduling and Workflow Management in Kubernetes: 1 papers found.
The category "Task Scheduling and Workflow Management in Kubernetes" focuses on the challenges and solutions related to integrating workflow systems with Kubernetes. Workflow systems typically use scheduling algorithms to optimize task execution order, enhancing performance and efficiency \[[6](http://arxiv.org/abs/2207.01222)\]. However, Kubernetes' inherent scheduling mechanism does not align with the optimized task execution order required by workflow systems, leading to inefficiencies in workflow execution and challenges in containerizing workflows on Kubernetes \[[6](http://arxiv.org/abs/2207.01222)\]. To address this, researchers have proposed solutions such as KubeAdaptor, a cloud-native workflow engine that acts as a docking framework to ensure consistency in task scheduling order between workflow systems and Kubernetes \[[6](http://arxiv.org/abs/2207.01222)\]. This approach allows for the integration of workflow systems with Kubernetes, improving execution efficiency and resource utilization \[[6](http://arxiv.org/abs/2207.01222)\]. Experimental results demonstrate that KubeAdaptor outperforms existing solutions like Argo workflow engine in terms of average execution time, workflow lifecycle, and resource usage rate \[[6](http://arxiv.org/abs/2207.01222)\]. These findings highlight the importance of aligning workflow scheduling with Kubernetes' capabilities to achieve optimal performance in cloud-native environments.
