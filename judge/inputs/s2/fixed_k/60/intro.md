## Related Works

Ranking models traditionally focus on modeling the relative order of predictions, often at the expense of the accuracy of absolute values \[[22](http://arxiv.org/abs/1710.10814)\]. This limitation has led to a growing interest in calibrating ranking models to ensure that the predicted scores reflect the true likelihood of preference \[[11](http://arxiv.org/abs/2101.04356)\]. Calibration of ranking models is particularly important in applications such as advertising, where the predicted click-through rate (CTR) must be calibrated for downstream cost-per-click bidding \[[4](http://arxiv.org/abs/2406.08010)\]. 

Several approaches have been proposed to calibrate ranking models. One common strategy is to use predefined transformation functions with order-preserving properties to adjust the original predictions \[[11](http://arxiv.org/abs/2101.04356)\]. However, these functions, such as piece-wise linear functions, are often limited in their expressiveness and flexibility, making them less effective in complex calibration scenarios \[[22](http://arxiv.org/abs/1710.10814)\]. 

To address this limitation, recent work has explored the use of neural networks for calibration. For example, the Unconstrained Monotonic Neural Network (UMNN) has been proposed as a flexible and powerful tool for modeling monotonic functions, which is essential for maintaining the order of predictions while improving calibration \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/1908.05164)\]. UMNNs are designed to enforce monotonicity by ensuring that the output of the network is strictly positive, allowing for the learning of arbitrary monotonic functions \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/1908.05164)\]. This approach has been applied to distributional reinforcement learning, where it enables the efficient decoupling of the function approximator class from the probability metric \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/2106.03228)\]. 

In addition to UMNNs, other methods have focused on improving the calibration of ranking models through novel loss functions. For instance, the Smooth Calibration Loss (SCLoss) has been introduced to optimize the calibration of highly flexible networks by fulfilling a necessary condition for achieving ideal calibration \[[20](http://arxiv.org/abs/2104.09286)\]. This loss function has been shown to improve the calibration performance of ranking models in both offline experiments and large-scale online deployments \[[20](http://arxiv.org/abs/2104.09286)\]. 

Furthermore, the Self-Boosted framework for Calibrated Ranking (SBCR) has been proposed to address the limitations of existing multi-objective approaches. SBCR introduces a calibration module that decouples the point loss and ranking loss, allowing for a more optimal trade-off between calibration and ranking \[[4](http://arxiv.org/abs/2406.08010)\]. This approach has been successfully deployed in industrial applications, demonstrating significant improvements in both calibration and ranking performance \[[4](http://arxiv.org/abs/2406.08010)\].
