## Related Works

Ranking models traditionally focus on modeling the relative order of predictions, often at the expense of the accuracy of absolute values \[[22](http://arxiv.org/abs/1710.10814)\]. This limitation has led to a growing interest in calibrating ranking models to ensure that the predicted scores reflect the true likelihood of preference \[[11](http://arxiv.org/abs/2101.04356)\]. Calibration of ranking models is particularly important in applications such as advertising, where the predicted click-through rate (CTR) must be calibrated for downstream cost-per-click bidding \[[4](http://arxiv.org/abs/2406.08010)\]. 

Several approaches have been proposed to calibrate ranking models. One common strategy is to use predefined transformation functions with order-preserving properties to adjust the original predictions \[[11](http://arxiv.org/abs/2101.04356)\]. However, these functions, such as piece-wise linear functions, are often limited in their expressiveness and flexibility, making them less effective in complex calibration scenarios \[[22](http://arxiv.org/abs/1710.10814)\]. 

To address this limitation, recent work has explored the use of neural networks for calibration. For example, the Unconstrained Monotonic Neural Network (UMNN) has been proposed as a flexible and powerful tool for modeling monotonic functions, which is essential for maintaining the order of predictions while improving calibration \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/1908.05164)\]. UMNNs are designed to enforce monotonicity by ensuring that the output of the network is strictly positive, allowing for the learning of arbitrary monotonic functions \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/1908.05164)\]. This approach has been applied to distributional reinforcement learning, where it enables the efficient decoupling of the function approximator class from the probability metric \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/2106.03228)\]. 

In addition to UMNNs, other methods have focused on improving the calibration of ranking models through novel loss functions. For instance, the Smooth Calibration Loss (SCLoss) has been introduced to optimize the calibration of highly flexible networks by fulfilling a necessary condition for achieving ideal calibration \[[20](http://arxiv.org/abs/2104.09286)\]. This loss function has been shown to improve the calibration performance of ranking models in both offline experiments and large-scale online deployments \[[20](http://arxiv.org/abs/2104.09286)\]. 

Furthermore, the Self-Boosted framework for Calibrated Ranking (SBCR) has been proposed to address the limitations of existing multi-objective approaches. SBCR introduces a calibration module that decouples the point loss and ranking loss, allowing for a more optimal trade-off between calibration and ranking \[[4](http://arxiv.org/abs/2406.08010)\]. This approach has been successfully deployed in industrial applications, demonstrating significant improvements in both calibration and ranking performance \[[4](http://arxiv.org/abs/2406.08010)\].

### <a id='category-monotonic-neural-networks'></a>Monotonic Neural Networks: 2 papers found.
The category of Monotonic Neural Networks (MNNs) has gained attention for their ability to model invertible transformations and their applicability in various probabilistic and reinforcement learning tasks. These networks enforce monotonicity through constraints on weights and activation functions, ensuring invertibility but limiting expressiveness \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/1908.05164)\]. Recent work has introduced the Unconstrained Monotonic Neural Network (UMNN) architecture, which relaxes these constraints by requiring only that the output of the network remains positive, thereby allowing for greater flexibility while maintaining monotonicity \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/1908.05164)\]. This approach has been applied in distributional reinforcement learning, where UMNNs are used to model different representations of the random return distribution, such as probability density functions (PDF), cumulative distribution functions (CDF), and quantile functions (QF) \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/2106.03228)\]. The UMNN architecture enables the efficient decoupling of the function approximator from the probability metric, leading to more versatile and adaptable models. Additionally, the UMNN has been integrated into autoregressive flows, such as the UMNN-MAF, demonstrating its effectiveness in density estimation tasks \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/1908.05164)\]. The flexibility of UMNNs also extends to calibration tasks, where they are used to improve the accuracy of absolute predictions while preserving the relative order, addressing limitations of traditional calibration methods that rely on fixed transformation functions \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/1908.05164)\]. This versatility highlights the broader applicability of MNNs in modeling monotonic relationships across diverse domains.

### <a id='category-loss-function-design-for-calibration'></a>Loss Function Design for Calibration: 7 papers found.
The category "Loss Function Design for Calibration" encompasses a range of approaches aimed at improving the reliability and accuracy of predictive models by refining their calibration. Existing methods often rely on predefined transformation functions with order-preserving properties to adjust predictions, but these are limited in expressiveness and flexibility, especially in complex scenarios \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/2301.04378)\]. To address this, recent work has explored more adaptable solutions, such as the use of monotonic neural networks, which can learn arbitrary monotonic functions while preserving the necessary constraints for calibration \[[7](http://arxiv.org/abs/2308.11990)\]. Additionally, novel loss functions have been introduced to further enhance calibration performance, such as the Smooth Calibration Loss (SCLoss), which ensures the model meets the necessary conditions for ideal calibration \[[19](http://arxiv.org/abs/1408.2764)\]. These efforts align with broader goals of uncertainty calibration, where models are trained to provide accurate confidence estimates alongside high predictive accuracy \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/2106.03228)\]. For instance, methods like the AvUC loss function have been developed to directly link accuracy and uncertainty during training, leading to better-calibrated models in tasks like image classification \[[5](http://arxiv.org/abs/2012.07923)\]. Similarly, approaches such as RankMixup have been proposed to improve calibration by leveraging ordinal ranking relationships between raw and augmented samples, ensuring that confidence levels are appropriately aligned with prediction reliability \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/2301.04378)\]. Furthermore, the importance of calibration in real-world applications has led to the development of methods tailored for specific use cases, such as cascade inference systems, where confidence scores directly impact system performance \[[7](http://arxiv.org/abs/2308.11990)\]. These contributions highlight the ongoing evolution in loss function design for calibration, emphasizing the need for flexible, expressive, and task-specific solutions to achieve accurate and reliable predictions.

### <a id='category-online-deployment-and-business-impact'></a>Online Deployment and Business Impact: 1 papers found.
Document 1 discusses the challenges and limitations of existing multi-objective approaches in calibrated ranking, particularly in industrial online applications. These methods often require aggregating the full candidate list within a single mini-batch to compute ranking loss, which can hinder extensive data shuffling and degrade training effectiveness \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/1908.05164)\]. Additionally, they apply inherently conflicting loss functions on a single probabilistic prediction, leading to sub-optimal trade-offs between calibration and ranking \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/1908.05164)\]. To address these issues, the Self-Boosted framework for Calibrated Ranking (SBCR) is proposed. This framework utilizes dumped predicted ranking scores from the online deployed model as context features, allowing each item to perceive the overall score distribution without requiring sample aggregation \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/1908.05164)\]. Furthermore, a calibration module is introduced to decouple pointwise and ranking losses, enabling a more balanced optimization process \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/1908.05164)\]. The effectiveness of SBCR is validated through comprehensive experiments on industrial-scale datasets and online A/B tests, demonstrating significant improvements in both calibration and ranking performance \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/1908.05164)\]. The method has been successfully deployed in Kuaishou's video search system, resulting in notable enhancements in click-through rate (CTR) and user engagement \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/1908.05164)\].

For personalized ranking models, the calibration of preference probabilities is crucial, and recent work has introduced parametric calibration methods such as Gaussian and Gamma calibration to improve the accuracy of user-item preference predictions \[[14](http://arxiv.org/abs/1710.08901)\].
