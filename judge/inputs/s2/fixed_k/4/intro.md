## Related Works

The problem of handling noisy annotations in machine learning has been extensively studied, with a growing body of literature focusing on the challenges posed by label noise in various learning settings. Early approaches to label noise typically assumed class-dependent noise, where the noise rate was uniform across all instances of a given class \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2012.11854)\]. However, recent studies have highlighted that real-world label noise is often instance-dependent, meaning that the likelihood of a label error varies with the difficulty of the instance \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/2406.16966)\]. This distinction is crucial, as it affects the design of noise mitigation strategies and the evaluation of model performance.

Several methods have been proposed to address label noise in deep learning. One line of work focuses on estimating the noise rates and adjusting the loss function accordingly. For example, \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/2207.13316)\] introduces a second-order approach that leverages covariance terms between noise rates and Bayes optimal labels to correct for imbalances caused by instance-dependent noise. This method does not require access to ground truth labels or prior knowledge of noise rates, making it particularly useful in real-world scenarios where such information is unavailable. Similarly, \[[4](http://arxiv.org/abs/2211.10906)\] proposes a method that separates noisy training data into clean and unlabeled sets and uses a balanced loss based on model bias to improve performance on long-tailed noisy data.

Another approach involves generating synthetic samples to mitigate the impact of noisy labels. \[[5](http://arxiv.org/abs/2204.02620)\] introduces a framework that creates synthetic samples by mixing original samples with their top-K nearest neighbors, using a mixture model to compute weights. This method helps the model learn more robust representations by reducing the influence of noisy labels. Additionally, \[[6](http://arxiv.org/abs/2312.12275)\] proposes a method that estimates soft targets by gradually correcting noisy labels, leading to more accurate approximations of ground truth labels.

In the context of scene graph generation, \[[7](http://arxiv.org/abs/2006.05142)\] addresses the issue of noisy labels by introducing NICEST, a novel strategy that detects and corrects noisy samples. This method includes three main steps: detecting noisy samples, reassigning soft labels, and designing an effective training strategy. NICEST is particularly useful in scenarios where ground-truth annotations are unreliable, as it enables the model to learn unbiased fusion knowledge through multi-teacher knowledge distillation.

In the field of metric learning, \[[8](http://arxiv.org/abs/2212.01189)\] proposes a Smooth Proxy-Anchor Loss that is robust to noisy labels. This method introduces a confidence module that computes sample class confidences and uses them to weight the influence of each sample during training. This approach leads to more robust sample embeddings, improving performance in noisy labeling conditions.
