## Related Works

Brain tumor segmentation remains a critical yet challenging task in medical image analysis, particularly due to the complexity of tumor morphology, limited annotated data, and the need for high accuracy in clinical settings \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2011.11052)\]. Traditional methods, such as threshold-based segmentation, have been widely studied but often struggle with capturing the intricate details of brain tumors \[[19](http://arxiv.org/abs/1503.02466)\]. Recent advances in deep learning have significantly improved the performance of automated segmentation, with convolutional neural networks (CNNs) and fully convolutional networks (FCNs) leading the way \[[22](http://arxiv.org/abs/1803.08691)\]. Among these, the U-Net architecture has become a foundational model for 3D medical image segmentation, particularly in brain tumor segmentation tasks \[[13](http://arxiv.org/abs/2010.13082)\].

Several studies have explored the use of pre-trained models to address the data scarcity issue in medical imaging. For instance, the work by \[[6](http://arxiv.org/abs/1912.12452)\] demonstrated that pre-trained models, such as V-Net, can be effectively used in 3D brain tumor segmentation by reducing memory consumption through patching techniques. Similarly, \[[25](http://arxiv.org/abs/2308.06821)\] introduced a transfer learning approach, leveraging pre-trained models for brain tumor classification, which showed promising results in handling class imbalance and improving classification accuracy. These approaches highlight the importance of transfer learning in overcoming the limitations of small annotated datasets \[[25](http://arxiv.org/abs/2308.06821)\].

The nnU-Net framework has emerged as a state-of-the-art solution for brain tumor segmentation, offering automated and efficient segmentation through an adaptive architecture \[[17](http://arxiv.org/abs/2011.00848)\]. However, it still requires extensive training and may not fully utilize pre-trained weights. To address these limitations, several studies have investigated the integration of pre-trained weights from ImageNet into medical segmentation models. For example, \[[23](http://arxiv.org/abs/2403.06396)\] proposed a large-scale Tumor Segmentation Foundation Model (TSFM) that leverages pre-trained weights and achieves superior performance across various tumor types. Additionally, \[[28](http://arxiv.org/abs/2111.12991)\] introduced non-parametric data augmentation techniques that enhance the performance of segmentation models without requiring large labeled datasets.

In the context of 3D segmentation, the use of 3D convolutions and multi-scale feature extraction has been explored to capture spatial dependencies in MRI data \[[13](http://arxiv.org/abs/2010.13082)\].
