## Related Works

Representation learning is a cornerstone of modern recommender systems, enabling efficient user-item matching through vectorized embeddings \[[19](http://arxiv.org/abs/2102.02549)\]. However, the computational and memory costs of these embeddings pose challenges for real-time inference. To address this, binarization techniques have emerged, converting dense embeddings into compact binary representations to reduce overhead \[[21](http://arxiv.org/abs/1611.01600)\]. While early approaches focused on numerical quantization \[[21](http://arxiv.org/abs/1611.01600)\], they often introduced significant information loss, degrading model performance \[[21](http://arxiv.org/abs/1611.01600)\]. Recent work has explored graph-based methods to enhance collaborative filtering, leveraging the user-item interaction graph for more expressive modeling \[[6](http://arxiv.org/abs/2001.00267)\]. For instance, Neural Graph Collaborative Filtering (NGCF) integrates user-item interactions into the embedding process, enabling high-order connectivity modeling \[[6](http://arxiv.org/abs/2001.00267)\]. Similarly, Multi-Graph Convolution Collaborative Filtering (Multi-GCCF) explicitly incorporates multiple graphs, including user-user and item-item relations, to capture proximal information \[[6](http://arxiv.org/abs/2001.00267)\]. These methods demonstrate the value of graph structure in collaborative filtering, but they often rely on dense embeddings, limiting scalability.

To further optimize performance, several studies have explored binary representations for graph-based collaborative filtering. Binarized Collaborative Filtering with Distilling Graph Convolutional Networks (Binarized GCN) introduces a framework to convert binary constrained optimization into a continuous problem, enabling efficient optimization with SGD or Adam \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/1906.01829)\]. This approach addresses the challenge of binary code optimization and information loss during training \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/1906.01829)\]. Similarly, the work on Light-weight Embeddings for Graph Collaborative Filtering (LEGCF) proposes a parameter-efficient embedding framework, reducing the storage overhead while maintaining recommendation accuracy \[[10](http://arxiv.org/abs/2403.18479)\]. These methods highlight the trade-off between model efficiency and performance, with binarization offering promising gains in memory and computation.

Another line of research focuses on improving the quality of embeddings through attention mechanisms and graph propagation. Graph Attention Collaborative Similarity Embedding (GACSE) learns explicit and implicit collaborative signals via attention-based propagation and auxiliary loss functions \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/2102.03135)\]. This approach enhances the modeling of user-item associations and similarities, leading to better recommendation performance \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/2102.03135)\]. Additionally, the work on Graph Attention Collaborative Similarity Embedding (GACSE) demonstrates the importance of attention mechanisms in capturing nuanced relationships in the user-item graph \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/2102.03135)\]. These methods, while effective, often rely on dense embeddings, which may not be suitable for resource-constrained environments.

### <a id='category-graph-based-collaborative-filtering-methods'></a>Graph-Based Collaborative Filtering Methods: 13 papers found.
Graph-based collaborative filtering methods have gained significant attention in recommender systems due to their ability to effectively model complex user-item interactions through graph structures \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/1905.08108)\]. These methods often focus on learning vector representations of users and items by integrating collaborative signals from the user-item bipartite graph \[[5](http://arxiv.org/abs/2003.13345)\]. For instance, Neural Graph Collaborative Filtering (NGCF) explicitly incorporates user-item interactions into the embedding process by propagating embeddings on the graph, leading to more expressive modeling of high-order connectivity \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/1905.08108)\]. Similarly, the Multi-Graph Convolution Collaborative Filtering (Multi-GCCF) framework integrates multiple graphs, including user-user and item-item graphs, to capture proximal information and model high-order interactions more comprehensively \[[5](http://arxiv.org/abs/2003.13345)\]. Additionally, methods like Graph Attention Collaborative Similarity Embedding (GACSE) utilize attention mechanisms and auxiliary losses to learn both explicit and implicit collaborative signals, enhancing the quality of user and item representations \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/2102.03135)\]. Addressing data sparsity, some approaches leverage side information, such as textual data, using advanced NLP models and relational graph convolutional networks (RGCN) to enrich embeddings with semantic knowledge \[[7](http://arxiv.org/abs/2304.13195)\]. Furthermore, techniques such as spectral graph wavelets have been introduced to capture both local and global structures in the graph, improving the discriminative power of user and item representations \[[15](http://arxiv.org/abs/2312.03167)\]. These advancements highlight the growing importance of graph-based methods in collaborative filtering, emphasizing the need for efficient and accurate representation learning to enhance recommendation performance \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/1905.08108)\]\[[5](http://arxiv.org/abs/2003.13345)\]\[[7](http://arxiv.org/abs/2304.13195)\]\[[15](http://arxiv.org/abs/2312.03167)\].

Parameter-efficient embedding frameworks have gained significant attention in the field of graph representation learning and collaborative filtering, aiming to reduce computational overhead while maintaining high performance. These frameworks address the traditional inefficiencies of embedding models by introducing mechanisms such as parameter sharing and learnable assignment matrices \[[10](http://arxiv.org/abs/2403.18479)\]. For instance, the LEGCF framework introduces an assignment matrix to capture semantic correlations between entities in the user-item interaction graph, improving recommendation performance through joint optimization of meta-embeddings and assignment matrices \[[10](http://arxiv.org/abs/2403.18479)\]. Similarly, the DNCF framework enhances user and item representations by incorporating dual embeddings derived from historical interactions, thereby improving the quality of latent representations \[[14](http://arxiv.org/abs/1502.04163)\]. Additionally, the Connector framework provides a comprehensive overview of graph embedding models, ranging from shallow to deep architectures, emphasizing the need for efficient and scalable solutions \[[7](http://arxiv.org/abs/2304.13195)\]. These approaches collectively highlight the importance of balancing parameter efficiency with model performance, particularly in large-scale recommendation systems where computational resources are constrained \[[10](http://arxiv.org/abs/2403.18479)\].
