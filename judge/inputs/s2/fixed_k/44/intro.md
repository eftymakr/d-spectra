## Related Works

Representation learning is a cornerstone of modern recommender systems, enabling efficient user-item matching through vectorized embeddings \[[19](http://arxiv.org/abs/2102.02549)\]. However, the computational and memory costs of these embeddings pose challenges for real-time inference. To address this, binarization techniques have emerged, converting dense embeddings into compact binary representations to reduce overhead \[[21](http://arxiv.org/abs/1611.01600)\]. While early approaches focused on numerical quantization \[[21](http://arxiv.org/abs/1611.01600)\], they often introduced significant information loss, degrading model performance \[[21](http://arxiv.org/abs/1611.01600)\]. Recent work has explored graph-based methods to enhance collaborative filtering, leveraging the user-item interaction graph for more expressive modeling \[[6](http://arxiv.org/abs/2001.00267)\]. For instance, Neural Graph Collaborative Filtering (NGCF) integrates user-item interactions into the embedding process, enabling high-order connectivity modeling \[[6](http://arxiv.org/abs/2001.00267)\]. Similarly, Multi-Graph Convolution Collaborative Filtering (Multi-GCCF) explicitly incorporates multiple graphs, including user-user and item-item relations, to capture proximal information \[[6](http://arxiv.org/abs/2001.00267)\]. These methods demonstrate the value of graph structure in collaborative filtering, but they often rely on dense embeddings, limiting scalability.

To further optimize performance, several studies have explored binary representations for graph-based collaborative filtering. Binarized Collaborative Filtering with Distilling Graph Convolutional Networks (Binarized GCN) introduces a framework to convert binary constrained optimization into a continuous problem, enabling efficient optimization with SGD or Adam \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/1906.01829)\]. This approach addresses the challenge of binary code optimization and information loss during training \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/1906.01829)\]. Similarly, the work on Light-weight Embeddings for Graph Collaborative Filtering (LEGCF) proposes a parameter-efficient embedding framework, reducing the storage overhead while maintaining recommendation accuracy \[[10](http://arxiv.org/abs/2403.18479)\]. These methods highlight the trade-off between model efficiency and performance, with binarization offering promising gains in memory and computation.

Another line of research focuses on improving the quality of embeddings through attention mechanisms and graph propagation. Graph Attention Collaborative Similarity Embedding (GACSE) learns explicit and implicit collaborative signals via attention-based propagation and auxiliary loss functions \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/2102.03135)\]. This approach enhances the modeling of user-item associations and similarities, leading to better recommendation performance \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/2102.03135)\]. Additionally, the work on Graph Attention Collaborative Similarity Embedding (GACSE) demonstrates the importance of attention mechanisms in capturing nuanced relationships in the user-item graph \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/2102.03135)\]. These methods, while effective, often rely on dense embeddings, which may not be suitable for resource-constrained environments.
