## Related Works

Multi-token prediction (MTP) has emerged as a promising alternative to the traditional next-token prediction (NTP) objective in language model pretraining. While NTP focuses on predicting one token at a time, MTP extends this by predicting multiple future tokens simultaneously, often using multiple prediction heads \[[5](http://arxiv.org/abs/2404.19737)\]. This shift has been motivated by the potential to improve downstream performance, inference speed, and training efficiency, particularly for large models \[[5](http://arxiv.org/abs/2404.19737)\]. However, prior work has demonstrated that smaller language models (SLMs) struggle with the MTP objective, often due to the increased complexity and computational demands \[[5](http://arxiv.org/abs/2404.19737)\]. 

Several studies have explored the implications of MTP in different contexts. For instance, \[[5](http://arxiv.org/abs/2404.19737)\] highlights that MTP can lead to significant gains in inference speed, with models trained using 4-token prediction being up to 3 times faster at inference, even with large batch sizes. Additionally, \[[6](http://arxiv.org/abs/2504.04060)\] introduces VocalNet, a speech LLM that leverages MTP to achieve faster and higher-quality generation, demonstrating the versatility of MTP across modalities. 

The challenges of integrating MTP into existing models have also been investigated. \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/2502.09419)\] shows that while MTP capabilities are inherent in models pre-trained for NTP, performance is data-dependent and improves with model scale. Moreover, integrating MTP heads into frozen LLMs is non-trivial due to the strong specialization of hidden layers for NTP. \[[7](http://arxiv.org/abs/2505.10518)\] further explores this by proposing MuToR, a method that introduces learnable register tokens to facilitate MTP without significant architectural changes, ensuring compatibility with existing models.

Curriculum learning has also been proposed as a strategy to address the difficulties of MTP for SLMs. \[[8](http://arxiv.org/abs/2407.10194)\] explores curriculum learning for code language models, showing that a well-designed curriculum can significantly improve performance on code execution tasks. This aligns with the current work, which proposes a curriculum learning strategy for MTP training, including both forward and reverse variants. The forward curriculum gradually increases complexity from NTP to MTP, while the reverse curriculum does the opposite. 

The effectiveness of curriculum learning in MTP training has been validated through experiments. \[[5](http://arxiv.org/abs/2404.19737)\] demonstrates that the forward curriculum enables SLMs to better leverage the MTP objective during pretraining, improving downstream NTP performance and generative output quality. In contrast, the reverse curriculum achieves stronger NTP performance but fails to provide the benefits of self-speculative decoding.
