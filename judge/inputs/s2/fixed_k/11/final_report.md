## Related Works

3D human pose estimation has seen significant advancements with the integration of deep learning techniques, particularly leveraging transformers and graph-based architectures to model spatial and temporal dependencies \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2203.13387)\]. Early approaches relied on convolutional neural networks (CNNs), which, while effective for local feature extraction, struggled with capturing long-range dependencies crucial for accurate pose estimation \[[14](http://arxiv.org/abs/2201.07384)\]. This limitation led to the adoption of transformers, which excel at modeling long-range interactions through self-attention mechanisms \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2203.13387)\]. However, these models often suffer from computational inefficiency and lack of local detail capture \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2203.13387)\].

Several works have attempted to address these challenges. For instance, \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2203.13387)\] proposed a novel pose estimation Transformer, CrossFormer, which explicitly encodes both local and global dependencies between body joints through two interaction modules. This approach improved performance on standard benchmarks by 0.9% and 0.3% compared to PoseFormer \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2203.13387)\]. Similarly, \[[12](http://arxiv.org/abs/2304.14045)\] introduced an Interweaved Graph and Attention Network (IGANet), allowing bidirectional communication between graph convolutional networks (GCNs) and attention mechanisms to better capture both local and global correlations. These methods demonstrate the importance of integrating spatial and temporal modeling in 3D human pose estimation.

Another key contribution is the use of graph-based architectures to model human skeletons. \[[13](http://arxiv.org/abs/2305.05785)\] introduced a higher-order regular splitting graph network (RS-Net) to capture long-range dependencies by leveraging multi-hop neighborhoods and learnable modulation matrices. This approach improved performance over recent state-of-the-art methods by effectively modeling complex joint relationships. Additionally, \[[27](http://arxiv.org/abs/2107.07797)\] proposed a conditional directed graph convolution to explicitly represent hierarchical joint relationships, leading to improved performance on large-scale benchmarks like Human3.6M and MPI-INF-3DHP \[[27](http://arxiv.org/abs/2107.07797)\].

Transformers have also been adapted for efficient 3D human pose estimation. \[[21](http://arxiv.org/abs/2103.10455)\] introduced PoseFormer, a purely transformer-based approach for 3D human pose estimation in videos, which effectively models spatial-temporal dependencies.
