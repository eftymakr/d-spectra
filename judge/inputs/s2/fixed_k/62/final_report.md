## Related Works

Generative retrieval has emerged as a promising paradigm that replaces traditional search index data structures with a single large-scale neural network, enabling improved efficiency and seamless integration with generative large language models \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2501.11034)\]. This approach directly generates document identifiers through corpus-specific constrained decoding, offering a differentiable search index \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2501.11034)\]. The generalization capabilities of generative retrieval on out-of-distribution corpora have attracted significant attention \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2501.11034)\].

Several works have explored the challenges of generative retrieval, particularly in terms of constraint satisfaction and decoding strategies. For instance, \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/2407.19829)\] introduces *Discrete Auto-regressive Biasing*, a controlled decoding algorithm that operates entirely in the discrete text domain, improving constraint satisfaction while maintaining fluency. This method leverages gradients to define a joint distribution over the generated sequence and an auxiliary bias sequence, using a Langevin-within-Gibbs sampling algorithm for efficient sampling \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/2407.19829)\]. Similarly, \[[11](http://arxiv.org/abs/1610.02424)\] proposes *Diverse Beam Search (DBS)*, which optimizes for a diversity-augmented objective to decode a list of diverse outputs, showing improvements in tasks like image captioning and machine translation \[[11](http://arxiv.org/abs/1610.02424)\].

In the context of E-commerce search, \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/2407.19829)\] develops a framework for generative retrieval with preference optimization, addressing challenges such as generating detailed item titles from brief queries and improving interpretability through constraint-based beam search and click data alignment \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/2407.19829)\]. This work highlights the importance of aligning generated outputs with human preferences to enhance result relevance and usability \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/2407.19829)\].

Another line of research focuses on improving the efficiency and adaptability of generative retrieval models. \[[4](http://arxiv.org/abs/2408.02152)\] proposes *Few-Shot Generative Retrieval (Few-Shot GR)*, which eliminates the need for extensive training by prompting an LLM to generate document identifiers for all documents in a corpus, creating a docid bank for retrieval \[[4](http://arxiv.org/abs/2408.02152)\]. This method significantly reduces training costs and leverages pre-trained knowledge in large language models \[[4](http://arxiv.org/abs/2408.02152)\].

[Snippet]: «Controlled text generation allows for enforcing user-defined constraints on large language model outputs, an increasingly important field as LLMs become more prevalent in everyday life. One common approach uses energy-based decoding, which defines a target distribution through an energy function that combines multiple constraints into a weighted average. However, these methods often struggle to balance fluency with constraint satisfaction, even with extensive tuning of the energy function's coefficients. In this paper, we identify that this suboptimal balance arises from sampling in continuous space rather than the natural discrete space of text tokens. To address this, we propose Discrete Auto-regressive Biasing, a controlled decoding algorithm that leverages gradients while operating entirely in the discrete text domain. Specifically, we introduce a new formulation for controlled text generation by defining a joint distribution over the generated sequence and an auxiliary bias sequence. To efficiently sample from this joint distribution, we propose a Langevin-within-Gibbs sampling algorithm using gradient-based discrete MCMC. Our method significantly improves constraint satisfaction while maintaining comparable or better fluency, all with even lower computational costs. We demonstrate the advantages of our controlled decoding method on sentiment control, language detoxification, and keyword-guided generation.»

### <a id='category-beam-search-optimization-techniques'></a>Beam Search Optimization Techniques: 2 papers found.
Beam search, a widely adopted method for decoding auto-regressive models in machine translation and other sequence generation tasks, has been extensively studied for its limitations and potential improvements \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2501.11034)\]. While beam search retains only the top-B candidates during decoding, it often results in sequences that are highly similar, limiting diversity and failing to capture the full ambiguity of complex tasks \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/2502.03685)\]. This has led to the development of alternative decoding strategies, such as Diverse Beam Search (DBS), which introduces a diversity-augmented objective to generate more varied outputs while maintaining competitive performance \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/2502.03685)\]. DBS has been shown to improve top-1 results by better balancing exploration and exploitation in the search space, with minimal computational overhead compared to traditional beam search \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/2502.03685)\]. Additionally, recent work has explored metric-driven search techniques, such as Monte-Carlo Tree Search (MCTS), which may offer more flexibility in aligning with specific evaluation metrics \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2501.11034)\]. These approaches highlight the ongoing efforts to refine decoding algorithms beyond standard beam search, emphasizing the importance of tailoring search strategies to the characteristics of the target metric or application \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2501.11034)\].

### <a id='category-efficiency-and-adaptability-improvements'></a>Efficiency and Adaptability Improvements: 3 papers found.
Efficiency and adaptability improvements in generative retrieval systems have been a focal point of recent research, addressing key challenges such as computational overhead and suboptimal retrieval strategies. One approach, Retrieval-Augmented Generation (RAG), seeks to enhance the accuracy of large language models (LLMs) by integrating external knowledge through selective chunk retrieval \[[8](http://arxiv.org/abs/2411.00744)\]. However, existing RAG systems often suffer from issues like independent chunk retrieval without considering their contextual relationships, non-monotonic utility of additional chunks, and lack of adaptability to query-specific characteristics \[[8](http://arxiv.org/abs/2411.00744)\]. To overcome these limitations, researchers have proposed a cost-constrained retrieval optimization framework using Monte Carlo Tree Search (MCTS) to determine the optimal chunk combination order, while also introducing a utility computation strategy to avoid over-saturation \[[8](http://arxiv.org/abs/2411.00744)\]. Additionally, parallel auto-regressive generation methods have been developed to improve the efficiency of LLMs during text generation, enabling independent planning of the generation process and reducing the number of required steps \[[12](http://arxiv.org/abs/2401.06761)\]. These techniques significantly reduce key-value cache consumption and attention computation, leading to improved throughput and reduced latency in high-throughput scenarios \[[12](http://arxiv.org/abs/2401.06761)\]. Meanwhile, novel optimization approaches such as PAG (Parallel Auto-regressive Generation) have been introduced to guide the autoregressive generation of document identifiers through simultaneous decoding, achieving substantial improvements in query latency and retrieval performance \[[9](http://arxiv.org/abs/2404.14600)\]. These advancements collectively highlight the ongoing efforts to enhance both the efficiency and adaptability of generative retrieval systems, aligning with the broader goals of integrating large language models into more effective and scalable search paradigms.

### <a id='category-out-of-distribution-generalization'></a>Out-of-Distribution Generalization: 1 papers found.
Out-of-distribution (OOD) generalization has become a critical area of focus in the evolving field of generative retrieval, particularly as these models are increasingly deployed in real-world applications \[[13](http://arxiv.org/abs/2306.12756)\]. While much research has been dedicated to improving the performance of generative retrieval models, there has been relatively less attention on their robustness when faced with new or unseen distributions \[[13](http://arxiv.org/abs/2306.12756)\]. This is especially important because generative retrieval models, which directly generate document identifiers based on learned differentiable search indexes, may struggle with query variations, unforeseen query types, and novel tasks that differ from the training data \[[13](http://arxiv.org/abs/2306.12756)\]. Empirical studies have shown that current generative retrieval models exhibit limitations in their ability to generalize to out-of-distribution scenarios, highlighting the need for further investigation into their OOD robustness \[[13](http://arxiv.org/abs/2306.12756)\]. Understanding these challenges is essential for advancing the reliability and effectiveness of generative retrieval systems in practical settings.
