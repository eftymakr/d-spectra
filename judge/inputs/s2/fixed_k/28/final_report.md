## Related Works

Federated Learning (FL) has emerged as a promising paradigm for collaborative machine learning while preserving data privacy \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2504.01921)\]. However, existing approaches often fail to address the challenges posed by data and model heterogeneity across clients, which can significantly degrade model performance \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/2504.11216)\]. This section reviews related works that have contributed to the development of FL, particularly focusing on methods that address data heterogeneity, client selection, and efficient training strategies.

Early FL frameworks, such as FedAvg, assume homogeneous data distributions and synchronous communication \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/2206.10032)\]. However, in practice, data is often non-i.i.d., and clients may have varying computational capabilities \[[4](http://arxiv.org/abs/2011.06393)\]. To address data heterogeneity, several studies have proposed client selection strategies that prioritize clients with more informative or diverse data \[[5](http://arxiv.org/abs/2205.12493)\]. For instance, FedDiverse introduces a client selection algorithm that promotes collaboration between clients with complementary data distributions, enhancing model performance \[[6](http://arxiv.org/abs/2207.08187)\]. Similarly, WAFFLE leverages the Euclidean distance between clients' updates to weigh their contributions, improving convergence and accuracy \[[7](http://arxiv.org/abs/2110.06978)\].

To handle the issue of label skew, where the label distribution differs between clients, FedEntOpt selects clients to maximize the entropy of the aggregated label distribution, ensuring the global model is exposed to data from all classes \[[8](http://arxiv.org/abs/2411.01240)\]. This approach improves classification accuracy and is robust to low participation rates and client dropout \[[9](http://arxiv.org/abs/2206.05507)\]. Additionally, methods like SDA-FL use synthetic data generation to address non-IID data by sharing differentially private synthetic data across clients, improving data consistency and model performance \[[10](http://arxiv.org/abs/2309.14675)\].

Efforts to reduce communication overhead and improve convergence in FL have also been explored. For example, FedCompass introduces a computing power-aware scheduler that adaptively assigns training tasks to clients, reducing staleness and improving convergence speed \[[11](http://arxiv.org/abs/2408.09545)\]. Similarly, FedFN proposes feature normalization techniques to alleviate data heterogeneity, demonstrating improved performance even when applied to pretrained models \[[12](http://arxiv.org/abs/2209.05424)\].

In the context of semi-supervised learning, FedMatch introduces an inter-client consistency loss to improve federated semi-supervised learning, outperforming both local semi-supervised learning and naive federated approaches \[[13](http://arxiv.org/abs/2006.12097)\]. Furthermore, vertical FL methods like VAFL enable asynchronous training by allowing clients to run stochastic gradient algorithms independently, improving communication efficiency and privacy \[[14](http://arxiv.org/abs/2007.06081)\].

Recent work has also focused on interpretability and fairness in FL, with studies exploring how to fairly rank the contribution of different institutions using Shapley values \[[15](http://arxiv.org/abs/2002.11223)\]. These methods are particularly relevant in healthcare settings where data heterogeneity is common \[[16](http://arxiv.org/abs/2308.00155)\].

### <a id='category-client-selection-strategies'></a>Client Selection Strategies: 2 papers found.
Client selection strategies in federated learning (FL) are critical for addressing the challenges posed by data and latency heterogeneity across clients. Existing methods often focus on mitigating one type of heterogeneity, but real-world scenarios require handling both simultaneously. This has led to the development of novel client selection schemes that optimize for convergence runtime by solving simple optimization problems in each round \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2504.01921)\]. Additionally, methods like FedEntOpt have been introduced to address label skew by maximizing the entropy of the aggregated label distribution, ensuring the global model is exposed to data from all classes \[[8](http://arxiv.org/abs/2411.01240)\]. These approaches enhance model performance and robustness, particularly in settings with low participation rates or client dropout. Furthermore, the integration of active learning techniques, such as CHASe, which focuses on identifying high-epistemic variation samples, demonstrates the potential for combining client selection with data selection strategies to improve both effectiveness and efficiency in federated active learning \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2504.01921)\].

### <a id='category-federated-learning-frameworks'></a>Federated Learning Frameworks: 6 papers found.
Federated Learning (FL) has gained significant attention for its ability to enable collaborative model training while preserving data privacy, but it faces challenges such as non-IID data distribution, heterogeneous client resources, and the lack of labeled data \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2504.01921)\]. To address these issues, several frameworks have been proposed. For instance, Heterogeneous Self-supervised Federated Learning (Hetero-SSFL) enables self-supervised learning across clients without requiring labeled data or imposing architectural constraints, aligning lower-dimensional representations through a common dataset \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2504.01921)\]. Similarly, Synthetic Data Aided Federated Learning (SDA-FL) tackles non-IID data by generating synthetic data using GANs and pseudo labeling, leading to more consistent local models \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/2206.10032)\]. Additionally, FedCompass improves efficiency in cross-silo FL by introducing a computing power-aware scheduler, reducing staleness and improving convergence \[[4](http://arxiv.org/abs/2011.06393)\]. Personalization strategies, such as WAFFLE, use weighted averaging based on client update distances to enhance model performance on non-identical data distributions \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/2504.11216)\]. Furthermore, research on federated continual learning, like Freddie, integrates methodologies to handle evolving client tasks and data distributions \[[6](http://arxiv.org/abs/2207.08187)\]. These approaches collectively highlight the ongoing efforts to enhance FL's robustness, efficiency, and adaptability in heterogeneous and dynamic environments.

### <a id='category-interpretability-and-fairness-in-federated-learning'></a>Interpretability and Fairness in Federated Learning: 2 papers found.
In the context of interpretability and fairness in federated learning, recent research has focused on addressing the challenges of data heterogeneity and fair contribution assessment among participating institutions. Federated Learning (FL) enables collaborative model training without exposing private data, but the lack of transparency and fairness in attributing model performance to individual contributors remains a critical issue \[[20](http://arxiv.org/abs/2302.13473)\]. The Shapley value (SV) has emerged as a promising method for fairly ranking institutional contributions, particularly in healthcare settings where data diversity is high and accurate valuation is essential \[[12](http://arxiv.org/abs/2209.05424)\]. However, exact SV computation is computationally infeasible for large-scale FL systems, prompting the development of approximation techniques like SaFE (Shapley Value for Federated Learning using Ensembling), which balances accuracy and efficiency \[[12](http://arxiv.org/abs/2209.05424)\]. Additionally, interpretable federated learning (IFL) has gained traction as a field aiming to enhance model explainability, support debugging, and enable fair reward allocation to data contributors \[[20](http://arxiv.org/abs/2302.13473)\]. These efforts highlight the growing emphasis on balancing performance, privacy, and interpretability in FL, especially for mission-critical applications.
