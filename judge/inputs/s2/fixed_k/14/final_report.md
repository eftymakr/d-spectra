## Related Works

Video super-resolution (VSR) has been extensively studied, with a growing focus on diffusion-based approaches due to their ability to generate high-quality, realistic images \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2502.07381)\]. However, these methods often suffer from introducing artifacts and degradations into high-resolution outputs due to the inherent randomness in the diffusion process \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/2008.05765)\]. This issue has been addressed in several works that aim to improve temporal consistency and reduce artifacts in real-world scenarios \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/2410.18137)\].

Early VSR methods, such as those based on 2D CNNs and 3D CNNs, struggled with capturing temporal dependencies effectively \[[4](http://arxiv.org/abs/2208.11608)\]. Recurrent Neural Networks (RNNs) were later introduced to model temporal information more accurately, but they faced challenges with training stability and computational efficiency \[[5](http://arxiv.org/abs/2103.10081)\]. More recently, the introduction of the Recurrent Residual Network (RRN) has shown promising results in achieving efficient and temporally consistent VSR \[[6](http://arxiv.org/abs/2503.03355)\]. Similarly, the Sliding Window Recurrent Network (SWRN) has been proposed to enable real-time inference while maintaining high performance \[[7](http://arxiv.org/abs/2212.07339)\].

Diffusion models have emerged as a powerful tool for VSR, offering superior performance in generating high-resolution videos \[[8](http://arxiv.org/abs/2201.12288)\]. However, they often require careful alignment and motion estimation to ensure temporal consistency \[[9](http://arxiv.org/abs/2201.07422)\]. The work by \[[10](http://arxiv.org/abs/2104.07473)\] introduced a diffusion-based framework that eliminates the need for explicit optical flow estimation by leveraging a diffusion transformer in latent space, enabling alignment-free video super-resolution \[[11](http://arxiv.org/abs/2408.03540)\]. This approach has been further refined in \[[12](http://arxiv.org/abs/2312.06640)\], where a flow-guided recurrent latent propagation module is introduced to enhance overall video stability \[[13](http://arxiv.org/abs/2405.02171)\].

To address the issue of artifacts in diffusion-based VSR, several works have proposed methods to improve the quality of generated details. For example, the work by \[[14](http://arxiv.org/abs/2108.10696)\] introduced a self-supervised learning approach that leverages auxiliary paired data to simultaneously estimate blur kernels and high-resolution videos \[[15](http://arxiv.org/abs/2104.05778)\]. This method has been extended in \[[16](http://arxiv.org/abs/2501.02976)\], where a self-supervised learning framework is proposed for real-world RefSR from dual and multiple zoomed observations \[[17](http://arxiv.org/abs/1905.13388)\].

In addition to improving quality, recent works have focused on reducing computational costs and enhancing efficiency. ### <a id='category-diffusion-based-video-super-resolution-methods'></a>Diffusion-Based Video Super-Resolution Methods: 4 papers found.
Recent advancements in diffusion-based video super-resolution (VSR) have focused on addressing the challenges of temporal consistency and degradation artifacts in real-world videos \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2502.07381)\]. Existing methods often struggle with maintaining visual fidelity and temporal coherence due to the inherent stochastic nature of diffusion models \[[12](http://arxiv.org/abs/2312.06640)\]. To mitigate these issues, researchers have introduced various techniques, such as spatial degradation-aware modules and temporal consistency mechanisms, to enhance the quality of reconstructed videos \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2502.07381)\]. For instance, the SDATC diffusion model incorporates a distortion control module to minimize the impact of noise from low-quality frames, while also utilizing a spatio-temporal attention module to capture temporal correlations \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2502.07381)\]. Similarly, the Upscale-A-Video framework ensures temporal coherence through a flow-guided recurrent latent propagation module, which enhances video stability without requiring additional training \[[12](http://arxiv.org/abs/2312.06640)\]. Additionally, the STAR approach leverages text-to-video (T2V) models to improve temporal modeling, incorporating local information enhancement and a dynamic frequency loss to reinforce fidelity and reduce artifacts \[[16](http://arxiv.org/abs/2501.02976)\]. Another notable work, SATeCo, introduces spatial feature adaptation and temporal feature alignment modules to achieve high-resolution video synthesis while preserving temporal consistency \[[19](http://arxiv.org/abs/2403.17000)\]. These methods collectively highlight the ongoing efforts to improve the realism and temporal coherence of diffusion-based VSR techniques, addressing the limitations of earlier approaches.

### <a id='category-efficiency-and-computational-cost-in-vsr'></a>Efficiency and Computational Cost in VSR: 5 papers found.
Efficiency and computational cost in video super-resolution (VSR) have been extensively studied, with a focus on developing methods that balance performance and resource usage. Researchers have explored various temporal modeling approaches, such as 2D CNN with early fusion, 3D CNN with slow fusion, and recurrent neural networks (RNNs), to improve temporal consistency and computational efficiency \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2502.07381)\]. A novel Recurrent Residual Network (RRN) was proposed to stabilize RNN training and enhance super-resolution performance while maintaining computational efficiency \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/2008.05765)\]. Additionally, a Sliding Window based Recurrent Network (SWRN) was introduced to enable real-time inference on mobile devices by leveraging spatial and temporal relations among frames \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/2410.18137)\]. For space-time video super-resolution, a one-stage framework was developed to directly generate high-resolution slow-motion videos from low-resolution, low-frame-rate inputs, achieving better performance and faster inference compared to two-stage approaches \[[4](http://arxiv.org/abs/2208.11608)\]. Another study proposed a lightweight model that combines video super-resolution and frame interpolation, utilizing quadratic modeling and refinement networks to reduce artifacts and improve efficiency \[[5](http://arxiv.org/abs/2103.10081)\]. These efforts highlight the ongoing challenge of achieving high-quality VSR results while minimizing computational overhead and adapting to real-world constraints.
