## Related Works

Neural retrieval models have become a cornerstone of modern information retrieval systems, particularly in Web search, due to their ability to learn complex patterns from large-scale data \[[9](http://arxiv.org/abs/2102.11903)\]. However, training these models typically requires substantial amounts of labeled query-document pairs, which are both costly and labor-intensive to obtain \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2404.13846)\]. This challenge has led to the exploration of alternative methods for generating synthetic training data, such as leveraging large language models (LLMs) to create synthetic queries \[[17](http://arxiv.org/abs/2303.11648)\]. While these synthetic queries offer a scalable solution, they often vary in quality, which can lead to suboptimal retrieval performance \[[17](http://arxiv.org/abs/2303.11648)\]. 

Existing approaches typically address this issue by filtering out noisy query-document pairs using signals from an external re-ranker \[[17](http://arxiv.org/abs/2303.11648)\]. However, this method is limited in its ability to directly optimize the generation of high-quality queries that align with downstream retrieval objectives. In contrast, our work proposes a framework that integrates ranking signals into the query generation process using Direct Preference Optimization (DPO) \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2404.13846)\]. This approach allows the model to directly optimize for the generation of high-quality queries that maximize downstream retrieval effectiveness \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2404.13846)\].

DPO has been widely adopted for preference alignment of LLMs due to its simplicity and effectiveness \[[5](http://arxiv.org/abs/2410.04350)\]. It is particularly useful in scenarios where the goal is to align model outputs with human preferences, such as in counter-speech generation \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/2412.15453)\] or 3D content creation \[[4](http://arxiv.org/abs/2502.04370)\]. The core idea of DPO is to optimize the model by comparing responses from a model and a reference model, using pairwise comparisons to guide the optimization process \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2404.13846)\]. This method has shown promise in improving the quality of generated outputs, especially when aligned with human preferences \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2404.13846)\].

In addition to DPO, other approaches have been proposed to enhance retrieval performance. For instance, the use of synthetic queries for privacy-preserving deep retrieval systems has been explored, where differentially private language models generate synthetic queries to maintain privacy while improving retrieval quality \[[10](http://arxiv.org/abs/2305.05973)\]. Similarly, methods such as CtrlQGen have been introduced to improve content retrievability by generating diverse synthetic queries that better represent user intent \[[17](http://arxiv.org/abs/2303.11648)\]. These approaches highlight the importance of query generation in improving retrieval performance, particularly in the absence of extensive labeled data.

Furthermore, the integration of reinforcement learning techniques, such as reinforcement learning from relevance feedback (RLRF), has been explored to address token-level misalignment in generative information retrieval \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/2504.05181)\].
