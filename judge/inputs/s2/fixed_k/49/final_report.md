## Related Works

Neural retrieval models have become a cornerstone of modern information retrieval systems, particularly in Web search, due to their ability to learn complex patterns from large-scale data \[[9](http://arxiv.org/abs/2102.11903)\]. However, training these models typically requires substantial amounts of labeled query-document pairs, which are both costly and labor-intensive to obtain \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2404.13846)\]. This challenge has led to the exploration of alternative methods for generating synthetic training data, such as leveraging large language models (LLMs) to create synthetic queries \[[17](http://arxiv.org/abs/2303.11648)\]. While these synthetic queries offer a scalable solution, they often vary in quality, which can lead to suboptimal retrieval performance \[[17](http://arxiv.org/abs/2303.11648)\]. 

Existing approaches typically address this issue by filtering out noisy query-document pairs using signals from an external re-ranker \[[17](http://arxiv.org/abs/2303.11648)\]. However, this method is limited in its ability to directly optimize the generation of high-quality queries that align with downstream retrieval objectives. In contrast, our work proposes a framework that integrates ranking signals into the query generation process using Direct Preference Optimization (DPO) \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2404.13846)\]. This approach allows the model to directly optimize for the generation of high-quality queries that maximize downstream retrieval effectiveness \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2404.13846)\].

DPO has been widely adopted for preference alignment of LLMs due to its simplicity and effectiveness \[[5](http://arxiv.org/abs/2410.04350)\]. It is particularly useful in scenarios where the goal is to align model outputs with human preferences, such as in counter-speech generation \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/2412.15453)\] or 3D content creation \[[4](http://arxiv.org/abs/2502.04370)\]. The core idea of DPO is to optimize the model by comparing responses from a model and a reference model, using pairwise comparisons to guide the optimization process \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2404.13846)\]. This method has shown promise in improving the quality of generated outputs, especially when aligned with human preferences \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2404.13846)\].

In addition to DPO, other approaches have been proposed to enhance retrieval performance. For instance, the use of synthetic queries for privacy-preserving deep retrieval systems has been explored, where differentially private language models generate synthetic queries to maintain privacy while improving retrieval quality \[[10](http://arxiv.org/abs/2305.05973)\]. Similarly, methods such as CtrlQGen have been introduced to improve content retrievability by generating diverse synthetic queries that better represent user intent \[[17](http://arxiv.org/abs/2303.11648)\]. These approaches highlight the importance of query generation in improving retrieval performance, particularly in the absence of extensive labeled data.

### <a id='category-direct-preference-optimization-(dpo)-framework'></a>Direct Preference Optimization (DPO) Framework: 4 papers found.
Direct Preference Optimization (DPO) has emerged as a significant approach in aligning models with human preferences, particularly in scenarios where traditional reward modeling is either impractical or less effective. This framework is utilized to optimize models by directly incorporating preference signals into the training process, bypassing the need for explicit reward models \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2404.13846)\]. In the context of 3D content generation, DPO is adapted to incorporate human preferences through pairwise comparisons, allowing for more controllable and higher-quality outputs by leveraging large multimodal models for preference alignment \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/2412.15453)\]. Additionally, DPO has been extended to address token-level importance, introducing methods like TIS-DPO that assign importance weights to individual tokens based on their contribution to the overall response, enhancing optimization efficiency and model alignment with human values \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/2504.05181)\]. Furthermore, in tasks requiring multi-turn reasoning and integration with external tools, DPO is adapted to handle trajectory-level preferences, improving performance in mathematical problem-solving by incorporating feedback from code interpreters \[[4](http://arxiv.org/abs/2502.04370)\]. These advancements highlight the versatility and effectiveness of DPO in various applications, from language models to 3D generation and mathematical reasoning, demonstrating its potential to enhance model performance through preference-driven optimization.

### <a id='category-dpo-applications-in-preference-alignment'></a>DPO Applications in Preference Alignment: 7 papers found.
The category "DPO Applications in Preference Alignment" explores how Direct Preference Optimization (DPO) can be leveraged to align language models with human preferences across various tasks, including hate speech mitigation, document retrieval, and personalized generation. Several studies highlight the importance of integrating preference signals directly into model training to enhance alignment with user intent. For instance, one paper proposes using DPO to improve counter-speech generation by aligning Large Language Models (LLMs) with human preferences, ensuring contextually appropriate and linguistically adaptable responses \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2404.13846)\]. Another study demonstrates that incorporating rationales into preference data can improve learning efficiency and model performance, showing that rationale-augmented learning accelerates convergence and achieves higher final model performance \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/2412.15453)\]. In the realm of generative retrieval, a framework combining DPO with constraint-based beam search is introduced to enhance E-commerce search by aligning models with human preferences through click data, leading to improved interpretability and conversion gains \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/2504.05181)\]. Additionally, Group Preference Optimization (GPO) is introduced as a method to align models with the preferences of different demographic groups using few-shot learning, reducing the need for extensive group-specific data \[[4](http://arxiv.org/abs/2502.04370)\]. Similarly, Few-shot Preference Optimization (FSPO) reframes reward modeling as a meta-learning problem, enabling LLMs to quickly infer personalized reward functions based on a few labeled preferences, which is particularly useful for applications requiring nuanced subjective judgments \[[5](http://arxiv.org/abs/2410.04350)\]. These works collectively underscore the versatility and effectiveness of DPO in aligning models with human preferences, offering scalable and efficient solutions for diverse applications.

### <a id='category-neural-retrieval-models-and-their-training-challenges'></a>Neural Retrieval Models and Their Training Challenges: 5 papers found.
The category "Neural Retrieval Models and Their Training Challenges" encompasses a range of approaches aimed at improving the efficiency and effectiveness of neural retrieval systems. Existing models, such as those based on generative information retrieval (GenIR), face challenges like token-level misalignment, where models trained to predict next tokens struggle to capture document-level relevance \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/2504.05181)\]. To address this, methods like direct document relevance optimization (DDRO) have been proposed, which aligns token-level generation with document-level relevance through pairwise ranking, avoiding the need for reinforcement learning \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/2504.05181)\]. Additionally, the scarcity of high-quality annotated data has led to the development of self-training frameworks that utilize synthetic queries to improve retrieval performance without external models \[[6](http://arxiv.org/abs/2311.15563)\]. These frameworks demonstrate consistent improvements across both general-domain and out-of-domain benchmarks, even with limited labeled data \[[6](http://arxiv.org/abs/2311.15563)\]. Furthermore, the efficiency of dense retrieval (DR) systems has been enhanced through vector compression techniques like Product Quantization (PQ), though these often lead to performance degradation due to the separation of encoding and compression \[[11](http://arxiv.org/abs/2108.00644)\]. To mitigate this, joint optimization methods such as JPQ have been introduced, which train query encoders and PQ indices together to maintain retrieval performance while achieving significant compression \[[11](http://arxiv.org/abs/2108.00644)\]. Overall, these studies highlight the ongoing efforts to enhance neural retrieval models through better alignment, data efficiency, and computational optimization.
