## Related Works

Dense retrieval (DR) has emerged as a powerful paradigm in information retrieval, leveraging pre-trained language models (PLMs) to generate dense vector representations of text that can be compared using simple similarity operations \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2208.09846)\]. Recent studies have explored the effectiveness of fine-tuning versus pre-training in DR, with some arguing that retrieval knowledge is primarily acquired during pre-training \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/2108.05540)\]. This section reviews relevant works that have contributed to the understanding of pre-training and fine-tuning in dense retrievers.

Pre-trained models such as BERT and LLaMA have been widely used as backbones for dense retrieval, where they are fine-tuned using contrastive learning to align queries and documents in an embedding space \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/2306.03166)\]. The contrastive learning approach has been shown to improve retrieval performance by encouraging the model to distinguish between relevant and irrelevant documents \[[4](http://arxiv.org/abs/2104.08253)\]. However, the extent to which fine-tuning contributes to retrieval performance remains a topic of debate. Some studies suggest that fine-tuning primarily adjusts neuron activations rather than reorganizing the underlying knowledge learned during pre-training \[[5](http://arxiv.org/abs/2010.02616)\].

The role of pre-training in dense retrievers has been further explored through the use of different representation strategies, such as CLS token and mean pooling. For example, the Contriever model uses mean pooling to generate document representations, which has been shown to outperform CLS-based approaches in certain settings \[[6](http://arxiv.org/abs/2312.05934)\]. Similarly, the use of decoder-only architectures like LLaMA has also been investigated, with some studies suggesting that these models may benefit more from fine-tuning compared to encoder-only models \[[7](http://arxiv.org/abs/2204.10641)\].

The effectiveness of fine-tuning has also been studied in the context of different datasets. For instance, the MS MARCO and Natural Questions datasets have been used to evaluate how well fine-tuning can improve retrieval performance \[[8](http://arxiv.org/abs/2112.09118)\]. These studies have shown that while fine-tuning can enhance retrieval performance, its impact varies depending on the model architecture and the dataset used \[[9](http://arxiv.org/abs/2408.06663)\].

In addition to model architecture and dataset, the pre-training strategy has also been found to influence retrieval performance. ### <a id='category-contrastive-pre-training-approaches'></a>Contrastive Pre-training Approaches: 7 papers found.
Contrastive pre-training approaches have emerged as a powerful paradigm for improving dense retrieval systems by enhancing the discriminative power of text representations. These methods aim to address limitations in traditional autoencoder-based models, where weak decoders can lead to bypass effects and limited discriminative ability \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2208.09846)\]. By introducing contrastive learning strategies, researchers have sought to refine representations by pulling together similar word distributions while pushing apart dissimilar ones, thereby emphasizing representative words over common ones \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2208.09846)\]. This approach has been further refined with relevance-aware contrastive learning, which leverages an intermediate model to estimate the relevance of positive pairs, allowing for more adaptive and effective contrastive loss weighting \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/2306.03166)\]. Additionally, novel tasks such as contrastive span prediction have been proposed to pre-train encoders independently, ensuring discriminative representations without relying on decoders \[[7](http://arxiv.org/abs/2204.10641)\]. These strategies collectively aim to improve retrieval performance by enhancing the quality of text embeddings, as evidenced by empirical results showing significant improvements over existing pre-training methods \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2208.09846)\]\[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/2306.03166)\]\[[7](http://arxiv.org/abs/2204.10641)\].

### <a id='category-pre-trained-language-models-in-dense-retrieval'></a>Pre-trained Language Models in Dense Retrieval: 9 papers found.
Recent research has focused on improving dense retrievers by leveraging pre-trained language models (PLMs) and addressing challenges such as training data noise, batch size requirements, and robustness to misspelled queries \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2208.09846)\]\[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/2108.05540)\]. For instance, the Condenser architecture was introduced to pre-train models that condense information into dense vectors, enhancing retrieval performance \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/2108.05540)\]. Building on this, coCondenser adds an unsupervised contrastive loss to further refine passage embeddings without requiring large batches or heavy data engineering \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2208.09846)\]. Similarly, the MASTER model integrates multiple pre-training tasks into a bottlenecked masked autoencoder framework, achieving better performance by capturing both intra- and inter-passage relationships \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/2306.03166)\]. Additionally, the ROM masking strategy was proposed to prioritize important tokens during pre-training, improving retrieval effectiveness by focusing on term importance \[[4](http://arxiv.org/abs/2104.08253)\]. Meanwhile, the SimLM model employs a bottleneck architecture with a replaced language modeling objective, enhancing sample efficiency and reducing input distribution mismatch between pre-training and fine-tuning \[[7](http://arxiv.org/abs/2204.10641)\]. The ART approach introduces an autoencoding method that trains dense retrievers without labeled data, using only unpaired inputs and outputs \[[8](http://arxiv.org/abs/2112.09118)\]. Furthermore, ToRoDer was developed to improve robustness to misspelled queries by incorporating an encoder-decoder architecture that recovers masked tokens during pre-training, enhancing retrieval performance on out-of-distribution inputs \[[9](http://arxiv.org/abs/2408.06663)\]. These studies collectively highlight the importance of pre-training strategies in dense retrieval, emphasizing the need for architectures and techniques that enhance model robustness, efficiency, and generalization.

### <a id='category-role-of-fine-tuning-vs.-pre-training'></a>Role of Fine-tuning vs. Pre-training: 8 papers found.
The role of fine-tuning versus pre-training in natural language processing has been extensively explored, with studies highlighting how fine-tuning can alter the representations of pre-trained models and influence their linguistic knowledge \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2208.09846)\]. For instance, research has shown that fine-tuning can lead to substantial changes in probing task accuracy, suggesting it may introduce or remove linguistic knowledge \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2208.09846)\]. Additionally, the effectiveness of fine-tuning varies across different models and tasks, with some models showing minimal gains while others experience significant improvements \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2208.09846)\]. Furthermore, the impact of fine-tuning on model performance is not uniform, as it can sometimes lead to forgetting previously learned domain knowledge, especially when the model is not exposed to diverse training data \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/2306.03166)\]. In contrast, pre-training provides a foundational knowledge base that can be leveraged for various downstream tasks, and in some cases, the benefits of pre-training can be retained even after fine-tuning \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/2306.03166)\]. This interplay between pre-training and fine-tuning is further complicated by the computational costs associated with pre-training, which can be significantly reduced through techniques like sparse pre-training and dense fine-tuning \[[4](http://arxiv.org/abs/2104.08253)\]. Moreover, the choice between pre-training and fine-tuning can also depend on the specific task and dataset, with some studies suggesting that using pre-training data during fine-tuning can improve generalization \[[7](http://arxiv.org/abs/2204.10641)\]. Overall, the relationship between pre-training and fine-tuning remains a critical area of research, with ongoing efforts to better understand how these processes interact to enhance model performance \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2208.09846)\].

### <a id='category-contrastive-learning-in-retrieval-tasks'></a>Contrastive Learning in Retrieval Tasks: 4 papers found.
Contrastive learning has emerged as a pivotal approach in dense retrieval tasks, enabling the training of unsupervised dense retrievers that can achieve strong performance even in data-scarce scenarios \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2208.09846)\]. Unlike traditional term-frequency based methods like BM25, which dominate in low-resource settings, contrastive learning leverages neural networks to align query-document pairs in an embedding space, pushing negative examples away to improve retrieval accuracy \[[8](http://arxiv.org/abs/2112.09118)\]. This paradigm has been further refined with approaches like DANCE, which introduces a dual training objective inspired by query likelihood, leading to more uniform and discriminative embedding spaces that enhance ranking performance \[[12](http://arxiv.org/abs/2107.07773)\]. Additionally, contrastive learning has been adapted to handle challenges such as typo-robustness by employing multi-positive contrastive learning, which effectively utilizes multiple positive samples during training to improve retrieval robustness \[[19](http://arxiv.org/abs/2403.10939)\]. Furthermore, methods like ANCE address the limitations of traditional negative sampling by constructing negatives from an Approximate Nearest Neighbor (ANN) index, aligning training data distribution with testing conditions and significantly boosting retrieval performance \[[27](http://arxiv.org/abs/2007.00808)\]. These advancements highlight the versatility and effectiveness of contrastive learning in dense retrieval, offering improvements across various settings including cross-lingual and multi-lingual retrieval \[[8](http://arxiv.org/abs/2112.09118)\].
