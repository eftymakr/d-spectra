## Related Works

The field of large language model (LLM) training in distributed environments has seen significant advancements, driven by the need for efficient execution, deployment, and optimization. Early work focused on distributed training frameworks, such as the MEANet architecture \[[10](http://arxiv.org/abs/2109.06440)\], which enables adaptive training and inference by categorizing inputs into easy, hard, or complex classes. This approach allows for efficient resource allocation, with most inference tasks handled at the edge and only complex cases sent to the cloud. Similarly, the FlexModel framework \[[17](http://arxiv.org/abs/2312.03140)\] addresses the challenge of interpretability in distributed LLMs by providing a streamlined interface for interacting with models across multi-GPU and multi-node configurations. This enhances accessibility and supports responsible AI techniques.

Performance modeling in distributed systems has also been a key area of research. The paper "Online Collection and Forecasting of Resource Utilization in Large-Scale Distributed Systems" \[[5](http://arxiv.org/abs/1905.09219)\] introduces a scalable mechanism for monitoring and forecasting resource utilization in distributed computing systems. This work enables efficient data collection and forecasting, reducing the computational burden of maintaining detailed system metrics. Another relevant study, "Expediting Distributed DNN Training With Device Topology-Aware Graph Deployment" \[[18](http://arxiv.org/abs/2302.06126)\], presents TAG, an automatic system for optimizing DNN training graphs and their deployment across heterogeneous device topologies. TAG combines DNN computation graphs with device topology graphs to identify optimized training strategies, achieving up to 4.56x speed-up over existing methods.

Instruction tuning has also been explored as a means to improve LLM performance in specific domains. The paper "Instruction-tuned Large Language Models for Machine Translation in the Medical Domain" \[[4](http://arxiv.org/abs/2408.16440)\] demonstrates that instruction-tuned LLMs outperform baseline models in medical translation tasks, especially when specialized terminology is incorporated into the training data. Similarly, "Demystifying Instruction Mixing for Fine-tuning Large Language Models" \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/2312.10793)\] investigates the impact of different instruction types on LLM performance, showing that certain instruction mixtures are more effective for specific applications. These findings support the idea that instruction tuning can significantly enhance model capabilities.
