## Related Works

Fairness in re-ranking has garnered significant attention in recent years, with a growing body of research exploring the trade-offs between ranking accuracy and item fairness \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2404.17826)\]. Early works such as \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/2005.12974)\] highlighted the importance of fairness-aware recommendation systems, particularly in domains with high social impact, such as employment and housing. These studies emphasized the need to balance recommendation accuracy with fairness metrics, often focusing on single sensitive attributes like race or gender \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/2005.12974)\]. However, they largely ignored the role of individual preferences in limiting the ability of algorithms to produce fair recommendations \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/2005.12974)\].

The concept of fairness-aware re-ranking has been further advanced by works such as \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/1809.02921)\], which introduced the idea of personalized fairness-aware re-ranking. This approach incorporates user-specific preferences to enhance fairness while maintaining recommendation quality \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/1809.02921)\]. Similarly, \[[4](http://arxiv.org/abs/2102.01203)\] proposed a method that uses representations of demographic groups to incorporate fairness into the re-ranking process, demonstrating that even limited labeled data can be effective in achieving fairness \[[4](http://arxiv.org/abs/2102.01203)\].

A key challenge in fairness-aware re-ranking is the trade-off between accuracy and fairness, which has been explored from various perspectives. For instance, \[[5](http://arxiv.org/abs/2309.04250)\] introduced the notion of provider fairness, emphasizing the need to ensure that items from different providers have a fair chance of being recommended. This work also highlighted the importance of evaluating beyond-accuracy aspects of recommendation systems, such as diversity and serendipity \[[5](http://arxiv.org/abs/2309.04250)\]. Another study \[[6](http://arxiv.org/abs/2405.15443)\] introduced a causal perspective to analyze the fairness-accuracy trade-off, proposing a new metric called the causal fairness/utility ratio to quantify the trade-off between fairness and utility \[[6](http://arxiv.org/abs/2405.15443)\].

The concept of elasticity has also been applied to the fairness-accuracy trade-off in re-ranking. \[[7](http://arxiv.org/abs/1809.03577)\] drew an analogy between re-ranking and economic transactions, suggesting that the accuracy-fairness trade-off can be formalized using the notion of elasticity, similar to how commodity taxes transfer costs between suppliers and consumers \[[7](http://arxiv.org/abs/1809.03577)\]. This insight has led to the development of new evaluation frameworks, such as the Elastic Fairness Curve (EF-Curve) \[[8](http://arxiv.org/abs/2008.01132)\], which enables a comparative analysis of algorithm performance across different elasticity levels \[[8](http://arxiv.org/abs/2008.01132)\].

Several studies have proposed novel algorithms to address the fairness-accuracy trade-off in re-ranking. For example, \[[9](http://arxiv.org/abs/2204.08085)\] introduced Tax-rank, a method that levies taxes based on the utility difference between items, and demonstrated its effectiveness in achieving both continuity and controllability over accuracy loss \[[9](http://arxiv.org/abs/2204.08085)\].
