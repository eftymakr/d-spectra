## Related Works

Neural retrieval methods using transformer-based pre-trained language models have advanced multilingual and cross-lingual retrieval \[[23](http://arxiv.org/abs/2212.09651)\]. However, their effectiveness for low-resource, morphologically rich languages such as Amharic remains underexplored due to data scarcity and suboptimal tokenization \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2503.18570)\]. This gap is addressed by introducing Amharic-specific dense retrieval models based on pre-trained Amharic BERT and RoBERTa backbones \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/2503.18485)\]. Our proposed RoBERTa-Base-Amharic-Embed model (110M parameters) achieves a 17.6% relative improvement in MRR@10 and a 9.86% gain in Recall@10 over the strongest multilingual baseline, Arctic Embed 2.0 (568M parameters) \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/2503.18485)\]. More compact variants, such as RoBERTa-Medium-Amharic-Embed (42M), remain competitive while being over 13x smaller \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/2503.18485)\]. Additionally, we train a ColBERT-based late interaction retrieval model that achieves the highest MRR@10 score (0.843) among all evaluated models \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/2503.18485)\]. We benchmark our proposed models against both sparse and dense retrieval baselines to systematically assess retrieval effectiveness in Amharic \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/2503.18485)\].

The challenges of low-resource language retrieval have been extensively studied. For instance, the work on dense retrieval for low-resource languages highlights the importance of tailored training datasets and evaluation metrics \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2503.18570)\]. The study on Amharic LLaMA and LLaVA demonstrates the potential of training large language models on low-resource languages through data augmentation and translation \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/2403.06354)\]. Similarly, the paper on parameter-efficient zero-shot transfer for cross-language dense retrieval with adapters shows that adapter-based methods can improve cross-lingual retrieval performance \[[4](http://arxiv.org/abs/2212.10448)\]. The work on enabling low-resource language retrieval for Urdu also emphasizes the importance of zero-shot learning and multilingual IR methodologies \[[5](http://arxiv.org/abs/2412.12997)\].

Fine-tuning strategies for low-resource languages have also been explored. The study on fine-tuning Whisper for Amharic highlights the importance of combining existing datasets with new, unseen data to improve ASR performance \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/2503.18485)\]. The paper on cross-lingual dense passage retrieval for low-resource languages further investigates the limitations of mDPR for extremely low-resource languages like Amharic and Khmer \[[6](http://arxiv.org/abs/2408.11942)\]. The work on low-resource language retrieval for Amharic also highlights the need for language-specific adaptation and the challenges of aligning language representations \[[7](http://arxiv.org/abs/2408.13909)\].
