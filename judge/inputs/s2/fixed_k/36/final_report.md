## Related Works

Deploying Large Language Models (LLMs) on edge devices has garnered significant attention due to the increasing demand for privacy-preserving, low-latency AI services. However, the resource constraints of edge devices, including limited storage, power, and computational capabilities, pose substantial challenges to deploying LLM-powered applications \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2505.03745)\]. Prior work has focused on addressing these challenges through algorithmic optimizations, hardware accelerators, and efficient deployment strategies.

Several studies have explored the use of quantization to reduce the memory footprint and computational demands of LLMs. For instance, \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/2504.03360)\] investigates the trade-offs between energy efficiency, inference speed, and accuracy in different quantization settings, highlighting the importance of hardware-level energy profiling for sustainable AI deployment. Similarly, \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/1806.07846)\] introduces a lightweight method for any-precision quantization of LLMs, enabling the deployment of multiple, different-sized LLMs with reduced costs. These approaches demonstrate the potential of quantization in enabling efficient LLM inference on resource-constrained devices.

Hardware accelerators have also been proposed to enhance the performance of LLMs on edge devices. \[[4](http://arxiv.org/abs/2504.00002)\] presents a dedicated FPGA-based accelerator with a reconfigurable computing engine to accommodate diverse operations arising from compression algorithms, translating algorithmic innovations into tangible hardware efficiency. Additionally, \[[5](http://arxiv.org/abs/2408.00462)\] introduces a new design platform, SECDA-LLM, to streamline the process of designing and deploying efficient FPGA-based LLM accelerators, demonstrating significant latency reductions over CPU-based execution.

Collaborative inference strategies have emerged as a promising solution to address the limitations of individual edge devices. \[[6](http://arxiv.org/abs/2410.00531)\] proposes a collaborative edge AI system for generative LLM inference, introducing a flexible pipelined architecture to maximize resource efficiency during both prefill and decoding phases. \[[7](http://arxiv.org/abs/2501.02438)\] further enhances collaborative inference by introducing a sliding window memory scheduler to dynamically manage layer weights during inference, reducing disk I/O latency and improving performance.

Efficient deployment of LLMs on edge devices also involves addressing the challenges of model compression and fine-tuning. \[[8](http://arxiv.org/abs/2502.15816)\] introduces FedSpine, an FL framework that combines Parameter-Efficient Fine-Tuning (PEFT) with structured pruning to enable efficient deployment of LLMs on resource-constrained devices. \[[9](http://arxiv.org/abs/2502.08381)\] investigates the use of activation sparsity for compressing general LLMs, achieving significant memory and computational reductions with minimal accuracy degradation.

### <a id='category-algorithm-hardware-co-design'></a>Algorithm-Hardware Co-Design: 5 papers found.
Algorithm-hardware co-design has emerged as a critical approach to address the challenges of deploying large language models (LLMs) on edge devices, where resource constraints and performance requirements are stringent \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2505.03745)\]. Researchers have focused on optimizing both algorithmic and hardware components to achieve efficient inference, particularly for long-context generation and low-bit quantization \[[10](http://arxiv.org/abs/2411.16158)\]. For instance, AccLLM integrates pruning, $\Lambda$-shaped attention, and W2A8KV4 quantization to reduce memory and bandwidth usage, while an FPGA-based accelerator with a reconfigurable computing engine enables flexible and efficient execution \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2505.03745)\]. Similarly, MixPE introduces a specialized processing element for mixed-precision matrix multiplication, minimizing dequantization overhead and improving energy efficiency by up to 1.4x \[[10](http://arxiv.org/abs/2411.16158)\]. These efforts highlight the importance of co-design in enabling scalable and efficient LLM deployment on edge platforms. Additionally, co-design methodologies have been explored for various applications, including DNNs and scientific instruments, emphasizing the need for joint optimization of algorithms and hardware accelerators \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/1806.07846)\]. The success of such approaches, as demonstrated by significant improvements in throughput and energy efficiency, underscores the growing importance of algorithm-hardware co-design in advancing AI deployment on resource-constrained devices \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2505.03745)\]\[[10](http://arxiv.org/abs/2411.16158)\].

### <a id='category-model-compression-and-fine-tuning'></a>Model Compression and Fine-Tuning: 5 papers found.

### <a id='category-fpga-based-accelerator-design'></a>FPGA-Based Accelerator Design: 2 papers found.
FPGA-based accelerators have emerged as a promising solution for deploying large language models (LLMs) on edge devices, offering reconfigurability and performance per watt advantages \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2505.03745)\]. However, the design and integration of such accelerators remain challenging due to limited hardware design flows for LLMs in existing FPGA platforms \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/2504.03360)\]. To address these challenges, researchers have proposed new design methodologies and platforms, such as SECDA-LLM, which streamlines the process of designing, integrating, and deploying efficient FPGA-based LLM accelerators \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2505.03745)\]. This approach enables model-specific optimizations and supports block floating point quantized operations, significantly reducing inference latency compared to traditional CPU implementations \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2505.03745)\]. Additionally, simultaneous FPGA/DNN co-design methodologies have been introduced, combining bottom-up hardware-oriented DNN model search with top-down FPGA accelerator design to enhance accuracy, frames per second, and energy efficiency \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/2504.03360)\]. These advancements highlight the growing interest in leveraging FPGAs for efficient LLM inference on resource-constrained edge devices.

To obtain the benefits of activation sparsity, we provide a guideline for the system architect for LLM prediction and prefetching. [Snippet]: «The deployment of Large Language Models (LLMs) on edge devices is increasingly important to enhance on-device intelligence. Weight quantization is crucial for reducing the memory footprint of LLMs on devices. However, low-bit LLMs necessitate mixed precision matrix multiplication (mpGEMM) of low precision weights and high precision activations during inference. Existing systems, lacking native support for mpGEMM, resort to dequantize weights for high precision computation. Such an indirect way can lead to a significant inference overhead. In this paper, we introduce T-MAC, an innovative lookup table(LUT)-based method designed for efficient low-bit LLM (i.e., weight-quantized LLM) inference on CPUs. T-MAC directly supports mpGEMM without dequantization, while simultaneously eliminating multiplications and reducing additions required. Specifically, T-MAC transforms the traditional data-type-centric multiplication to bit-wise table lookup, and enables a unified and scalable mpGEMM solution. Our LUT-based kernels scale linearly to the weight bit-width. Evaluated on low-bit Llama and BitNet models, T-MAC demonstrates up to 4× increase in throughput and 70% reduction in energy consumption compared to llama.cpp. For BitNet-b1.58-3B, T-MAC delivers a token generation throughput of 30 tokens/s with a single core and 71 tokens/s with eight cores on M2-Ultra, and 11 tokens/s on Raspberry Pi 5. T-MAC with LUT-based computing paradigm, paves the way for the practical deployment of low-bit LLMs on resource-constrained edge devices without compromising computational efficiency. The system is open-sourced at https://github.com/microsoft/T-MAC.»
