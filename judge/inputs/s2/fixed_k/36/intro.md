## Related Works

Deploying Large Language Models (LLMs) on edge devices has garnered significant attention due to the increasing demand for privacy-preserving, low-latency AI services. However, the resource constraints of edge devices, including limited storage, power, and computational capabilities, pose substantial challenges to deploying LLM-powered applications \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2505.03745)\]. Prior work has focused on addressing these challenges through algorithmic optimizations, hardware accelerators, and efficient deployment strategies.

Several studies have explored the use of quantization to reduce the memory footprint and computational demands of LLMs. For instance, \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/2504.03360)\] investigates the trade-offs between energy efficiency, inference speed, and accuracy in different quantization settings, highlighting the importance of hardware-level energy profiling for sustainable AI deployment. Similarly, \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/1806.07846)\] introduces a lightweight method for any-precision quantization of LLMs, enabling the deployment of multiple, different-sized LLMs with reduced costs. These approaches demonstrate the potential of quantization in enabling efficient LLM inference on resource-constrained devices.

Hardware accelerators have also been proposed to enhance the performance of LLMs on edge devices. \[[4](http://arxiv.org/abs/2504.00002)\] presents a dedicated FPGA-based accelerator with a reconfigurable computing engine to accommodate diverse operations arising from compression algorithms, translating algorithmic innovations into tangible hardware efficiency. Additionally, \[[5](http://arxiv.org/abs/2408.00462)\] introduces a new design platform, SECDA-LLM, to streamline the process of designing and deploying efficient FPGA-based LLM accelerators, demonstrating significant latency reductions over CPU-based execution.

Collaborative inference strategies have emerged as a promising solution to address the limitations of individual edge devices. \[[6](http://arxiv.org/abs/2410.00531)\] proposes a collaborative edge AI system for generative LLM inference, introducing a flexible pipelined architecture to maximize resource efficiency during both prefill and decoding phases. \[[7](http://arxiv.org/abs/2501.02438)\] further enhances collaborative inference by introducing a sliding window memory scheduler to dynamically manage layer weights during inference, reducing disk I/O latency and improving performance.

Efficient deployment of LLMs on edge devices also involves addressing the challenges of model compression and fine-tuning. \[[8](http://arxiv.org/abs/2502.15816)\] introduces FedSpine, an FL framework that combines Parameter-Efficient Fine-Tuning (PEFT) with structured pruning to enable efficient deployment of LLMs on resource-constrained devices. \[[9](http://arxiv.org/abs/2502.08381)\] investigates the use of activation sparsity for compressing general LLMs, achieving significant memory and computational reductions with minimal accuracy degradation.
