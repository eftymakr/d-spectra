## Related Works

In the field of 3D speech-driven facial animation, several approaches have been proposed to align audio signals with facial movements. Early methods often relied on handcrafted features such as facial landmarks or 3D models to map audio to visual outputs \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/1807.07860)\]. These approaches, while effective for specific subjects, struggled with generalization and often required extensive preprocessing \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/2104.11116)\]. More recent works have explored deep learning techniques, particularly leveraging pre-trained self-supervised audio models as encoders to capture rich audio representations \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/1609.03499)\]. These models have shown promise in generating realistic facial animations, but they face challenges when dealing with phonetically similar syllables that exhibit distinct lip shapes \[[4](http://arxiv.org/abs/1805.09313)\]. This issue leads to an averaging effect in lip motion generation, where subtle differences in lip shapes are lost due to the coupling of audio features in the feature space \[[5](http://arxiv.org/abs/2107.09293)\].

To address this limitation, several studies have investigated the integration of semantic information to improve the disentanglement of audio features. For instance, \[[6](http://arxiv.org/abs/2104.08223)\] introduced a method that learns audio-visual representations by disentangling subject-related and speech-related information through an associative-and-adversarial training process. This approach enables the generation of talking faces on arbitrary subjects with clearer lip motion patterns. Similarly, \[[7](http://arxiv.org/abs/2008.10010)\] proposed a framework that generates pose-controllable talking faces by modularizing audio-visual representations using an implicit low-dimensional pose code. This method allows for more natural head motions while maintaining lip synchronization with the audio. Additionally, \[[8](http://arxiv.org/abs/2205.15278)\] explored the use of a categorical latent space for facial animation, disentangling audio-correlated and audio-uncorrelated information to achieve more realistic facial expressions, including eye blinks and eyebrow movements.

Another line of research focuses on improving the synchronization between audio and video by incorporating temporal coherence. For example, \[[9](http://arxiv.org/abs/2211.00924)\] introduced a temporal GAN with two discriminators to generate videos of talking heads that exhibit natural facial expressions and lip synchronization. This method avoids the need for handcrafted intermediate features, relying instead on a fully end-to-end approach. Similarly, \[[10](http://arxiv.org/abs/2303.11089)\] proposed a system that generates subject-independent realistic videos directly from raw audio, achieving accurate lip synchronization and natural facial expressions without requiring additional post-processing.
