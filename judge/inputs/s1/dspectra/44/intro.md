## Related Works

Representation learning is a cornerstone of modern recommender systems, enabling efficient user-item matching by learning vectorized embeddings \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/1905.08108)\]. Traditional methods often rely on matrix factorization or deep learning approaches to capture latent user and item representations \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/1905.08108)\]. However, these methods typically fail to fully exploit the collaborative signal inherent in user-item interactions \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/1905.08108)\]. To address this, several works have proposed graph-based approaches that explicitly model the bipartite user-item graph structure \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/1905.08108)\]. For instance, Neural Graph Collaborative Filtering (NGCF) integrates user-item interactions into the embedding process by propagating embeddings on the graph, thereby capturing high-order connectivity \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/1905.08108)\]. Similarly, LightGCN simplifies the design of Graph Convolution Networks (GCNs) for recommendation by focusing on neighborhood aggregation, achieving significant performance improvements over NGCF \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/2002.02126)\]. 

The use of graph neural networks (GNNs) in recommendation has also been extended to incorporate side information, such as knowledge graphs, to enhance the modeling of high-order relations \[[7](http://arxiv.org/abs/1905.07854)\]. Knowledge Graph Attention Network (KGAT) explicitly models high-order connectivities in the knowledge graph using an attention mechanism, improving recommendation accuracy \[[7](http://arxiv.org/abs/1905.07854)\]. Additionally, Multi-Graph Convolution Collaborative Filtering (Multi-GCCF) integrates multiple graphs—user-item, user-user, and item-item—into the embedding learning process, capturing both high-order and proximal information \[[8](http://arxiv.org/abs/2001.00267)\]. 

In the context of efficient inference, representation binarization has emerged as a promising approach to reduce memory usage and computational overhead \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/1905.08108)\]. Binarized Collaborative Filtering with Distilling Graph Convolutional Networks (Binarized GCN) introduces a framework that distills ranking information from GCN into binarized collaborative filtering, improving the efficiency of online recommendations \[[12](http://arxiv.org/abs/1906.01829)\]. However, binary representations can lead to information loss, which is mitigated by converting the binary constrained optimization problem into a continuous optimization problem with a stochastic penalty \[[12](http://arxiv.org/abs/1906.01829)\]. 

To further enhance the robustness and accuracy of graph-based recommendation models, self-supervised learning has been explored. Self-supervised Graph Learning (SGL) introduces auxiliary tasks to reinforce node representation learning, improving the accuracy and robustness of GCNs, particularly for long-tail items \[[16](http://arxiv.org/abs/2010.10783)\]. This approach generates multiple views of a node through operators such as node dropout, edge dropout, and random walk, maximizing agreement between different views \[[16](http://arxiv.org/abs/2010.10783)\].
