## Related Works

Neural retrieval models have shown significant promise in Web search tasks, particularly due to their ability to learn complex patterns from raw text inputs \[[14](http://arxiv.org/abs/1903.06902)\]. However, training these models typically requires a large amount of labeled query-document pairs, which are expensive to obtain \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2305.18290)\]. Recent work has explored the use of synthetic queries generated by large language models as a scalable alternative to labeled data \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2305.18290)\]. While this approach offers cost savings, it often leads to suboptimal performance due to the variability in quality of the generated queries \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2305.18290)\].

To address this issue, several methods have been proposed to filter out noisy query-document pairs. These methods typically rely on external re-rankers to identify high-quality pairs \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2305.18290)\]. However, this approach introduces additional complexity and computational overhead \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2305.18290)\]. In contrast, our work proposes a framework that integrates ranking signals directly into the query generation process using Direct Preference Optimization (DPO) \[[8](http://arxiv.org/abs/2402.13228)\]. This approach allows for direct optimization of the model towards generating high-quality queries that maximize downstream retrieval effectiveness \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2305.18290)\].

DPO has been shown to be effective in improving the performance of large language models on downstream tasks such as reasoning, summarization, and alignment \[[8](http://arxiv.org/abs/2402.13228)\]. It eliminates the need for complex reinforcement learning procedures and instead uses a simple classification loss to fine-tune the model \[[8](http://arxiv.org/abs/2402.13228)\]. This makes DPO computationally lightweight and easier to implement \[[8](http://arxiv.org/abs/2402.13228)\]. Additionally, DPO has been shown to outperform reinforcement learning-based methods in terms of both performance and simplicity \[[8](http://arxiv.org/abs/2402.13228)\].

Other works have also explored the use of neural ranking models for information retrieval. For example, K-NRM uses a kernel-based approach to extract multi-level soft match features from query-document pairs \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/1706.06613)\]. This model has been shown to outperform traditional feature-based and neural-based state-of-the-art models \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/1706.06613)\]. Similarly, the DRMM model employs a joint deep architecture at the query term level for relevance matching \[[7](http://arxiv.org/abs/1711.08611)\]. This model has been shown to significantly outperform several well-known retrieval models as well as state-of-the-art deep matching models \[[7](http://arxiv.org/abs/1711.08611)\].

In addition to these models, there have been efforts to improve the effectiveness of BERT-based query-by-document retrieval using multi-task optimization \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/2202.00373)\]. This approach uses an additional document-level representation learning objective alongside the ranking objective when fine-tuning the BERT re-ranker \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/2202.00373)\]. This has been shown to significantly improve the ranking effectiveness without changing the BERT re-ranker or using additional training samples \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/2202.00373)\].

### <a id='category-direct-preference-optimization-(dpo)-framework'></a>Direct Preference Optimization (DPO) Framework: 1 papers found.
The Direct Preference Optimization (DPO) framework represents a significant advancement in aligning large-scale language models with human preferences by simplifying the reinforcement learning from human feedback (RLHF) process. Unlike traditional RLHF, which requires complex steps such as training a reward model and then fine-tuning the language model using reinforcement learning, DPO introduces a novel parameterization of the reward model that allows for the extraction of the optimal policy in closed form \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2305.18290)\]. This eliminates the need for extensive hyperparameter tuning and sampling from the language model during fine-tuning, resulting in a more stable and computationally efficient method \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2305.18290)\]. The approach leverages a simple classification loss to directly optimize the model's behavior, leading to improved alignment with human preferences without compromising the model's original capabilities \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2305.18290)\]. Experimental results demonstrate that DPO achieves performance comparable to or better than existing methods, particularly in controlling the sentiment of generated text and improving response quality in tasks such as summarization and single-turn dialogue \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2305.18290)\]. This makes DPO a promising alternative to methods like Proximal Policy Optimization (PPO), which are often complex and less reliable \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2305.18290)\].

By using pairs of preferred and dispreferred data, DPO models the relative probability of selecting one response over another, enabling more effective fine-tuning \[[8](http://arxiv.org/abs/2402.13228)\]. However, the standard DPO loss can inadvertently reduce the likelihood of preferred examples, especially when the edit distance between completions is low \[[8](http://arxiv.org/abs/2402.13228)\]. To address this, researchers have proposed DPO-Positive (DPOP), a novel loss function and training procedure that avoids this failure mode and achieves superior performance across various datasets and tasks \[[8](http://arxiv.org/abs/2402.13228)\]. Additionally, DPOP has been shown to outperform other fine-tuning methods on independent benchmarks like MT-Bench, highlighting its robustness and effectiveness \[[8](http://arxiv.org/abs/2402.13228)\]. These advancements in DPO have also led to the development of high-performing open-source models such as Smaug-72B, which surpasses 80% average accuracy on the HuggingFace Open LLM Leaderboard \[[8](http://arxiv.org/abs/2402.13228)\]. The integration of DPO into query generation processes further enhances retrieval performance by directly optimizing for high-quality synthetic queries, as demonstrated in experiments on the MS MARCO benchmark \[[8](http://arxiv.org/abs/2402.13228)\].
