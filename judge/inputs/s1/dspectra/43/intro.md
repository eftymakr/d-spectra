## Related Works

The field of large language model (LLM) training has seen significant advancements in recent years, particularly in the areas of distributed training, performance modeling, and optimization techniques. Early work on training large-scale models focused on overcoming the limitations of GPU memory and computational efficiency \[[6](http://arxiv.org/abs/1909.08053)\]. For instance, Megatron-LM introduced a model-parallel approach that enabled the training of transformer models with billions of parameters using a combination of tensor, pipeline, and data parallelism \[[6](http://arxiv.org/abs/1909.08053)\]. This approach demonstrated the feasibility of training models with up to 8.3 billion parameters on 512 GPUs, achieving a throughput of 15.1 PetaFLOPs with 76% scaling efficiency \[[6](http://arxiv.org/abs/1909.08053)\].

The importance of performance modeling in distributed training has also been widely recognized. For example, the work on DLRM (Deep Learning Recommendation Model) highlighted the need for performance models that account for both active and idle time on GPUs, as well as the impact of operator overheads on training time \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/2201.07821)\]. This led to the development of a critical-path-based algorithm for predicting per-batch training time with a geometric mean average error (GMAE) of less than 10% \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/2201.07821)\]. Similarly, the work on Efficient Large-Scale Language Model Training on GPU Clusters Using Megatron-LM further emphasized the need for scalable training methods that can handle the computational demands of training models with up to 1 trillion parameters \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/2104.04473)\].

Recent studies have also focused on the practical challenges of deploying LLMs at scale, including the need for accurate performance models that can guide optimization efforts. For example, the work on LLM-Pilot introduced a system for characterizing and predicting the performance of LLM inference services across a variety of hardware configurations \[[5](http://arxiv.org/abs/2410.02425)\]. This system was able to deliver on performance requirements 33% more frequently while reducing costs by 60% on average \[[5](http://arxiv.org/abs/2410.02425)\]. Additionally, the development of GPT-4 demonstrated the importance of infrastructure and optimization methods that can scale across a wide range of model sizes \[[7](http://arxiv.org/abs/2303.08774)\]. This allowed for accurate predictions of GPT-4's performance based on models trained with significantly less compute \[[7](http://arxiv.org/abs/2303.08774)\].
