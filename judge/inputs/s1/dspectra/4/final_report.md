## Related Works

In the context of learning from noisy annotations, several approaches have been proposed to improve the robustness of deep learning models. Traditional methods often treat label noise as statistical outliers and employ techniques such as importance re-weighting and bootstrapping to mitigate its effects \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/1703.02391)\]. However, these methods typically assume that label noise is independent and identically distributed, which may not hold in real-world scenarios where noisy labels exhibit multimodal characteristics \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/1703.02391)\]. 

Recent work has focused on leveraging additional side information, such as small clean datasets or label relations in knowledge graphs, to hedge the risk of learning from noisy labels \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/1703.02391)\]. This approach has been validated across domains including Sports, Species, and Artifacts, demonstrating its effectiveness in practical settings \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/1703.02391)\]. Similarly, the use of trusted data with clean labels has been explored as a means to improve robustness against severe label noise \[[6](http://arxiv.org/abs/1802.05300)\]. By incorporating a set of trusted examples, the model can be trained to correct for label noise in a data-efficient manner, leading to improved performance across vision and natural language processing tasks \[[6](http://arxiv.org/abs/1802.05300)\].

Another line of research has addressed the problem of noisy labels in the context of image classification and object detection. For example, a framework has been proposed that formulates the problem using an undirected graphical model, representing the relationship between noisy and clean labels \[[5](http://arxiv.org/abs/1706.00038)\]. This approach enables the inference of latent clean labels during training, regularized by auxiliary sources of information. The model has been applied to image labeling and has shown effectiveness in reducing label noise on datasets such as CIFAR-10 and MS COCO \[[5](http://arxiv.org/abs/1706.00038)\]. 

In the realm of vision-language representation learning, noisy text supervision has been leveraged to scale up pre-trained models. A large-scale dataset of over one billion image-alt text pairs has been used to train a dual-encoder architecture, achieving state-of-the-art results in visual and language representation learning \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/2102.05918)\]. This approach demonstrates that the scale of the dataset can compensate for its noise, leading to strong performance on transfer tasks such as ImageNet and VTAB \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/2102.05918)\].

For text generation, the issue of label noise has also been addressed. A framework has been proposed that uses reinforcement learning (RL) to train text generation models, particularly in scenarios where direct supervision is not available \[[13](http://arxiv.org/abs/2106.07704)\]. This approach, known as soft Q-learning, enables the model to learn effectively from sparse rewards by combining on- and off-policy updates, leading to improved performance on tasks such as generating adversarial attacks and prompts \[[13](http://arxiv.org/abs/2106.07704)\].

### <a id='category-label-noise-mitigation-techniques'></a>Label Noise Mitigation Techniques: 9 papers found.
In the context of label noise mitigation techniques, several approaches have been proposed to address the challenges posed by noisy annotations in machine learning tasks. These methods often focus on identifying and correcting noisy labels to improve model performance. For instance, one study highlights the importance of distinguishing between noisy and clean samples by leveraging density-based clustering and pseudo labeling strategies to refine predictions \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2207.13316)\]. Another work introduces a multi-teacher knowledge distillation framework that utilizes side information, such as a small clean dataset and label relations from knowledge graphs, to hedge against label noise \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/1703.02391)\]. Additionally, there are methods that model the relationship between noisy and clean labels using undirected graphical models, enabling semi-supervised learning in the presence of label noise \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/2102.05918)\]. Some approaches also emphasize the use of trusted data with clean labels to correct label noise, demonstrating robustness against severe noise levels \[[4](http://arxiv.org/abs/1704.05426)\]. Furthermore, research has explored the structure of human-centric annotations, showing that despite their noise, they can be modeled to decouple human reporting bias from visually grounded labels \[[5](http://arxiv.org/abs/1706.00038)\]. A theoretical framework has also been proposed to handle class-dependent label noise, offering loss correction techniques that are agnostic to the application domain and network architecture \[[7](http://arxiv.org/abs/1512.06974)\]. These diverse strategies collectively contribute to the growing body of work on mitigating label noise in deep learning, emphasizing the need for robust and adaptable solutions.

### <a id='category-large-scale-noisy-dataset-utilization'></a>Large-Scale Noisy Dataset Utilization: 4 papers found.
The category "Large-Scale Noisy Dataset Utilization" explores how noisy datasets can be effectively leveraged for training robust machine learning models, particularly in NLP and vision tasks. Research in this area highlights that while curated datasets like ImageNet or Conceptual Captions are valuable, they are often expensive to create and limit model scalability \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2207.13316)\]. In contrast, large-scale noisy datasets, such as those containing over one billion image-alt text pairs, can be used without extensive filtering, offering a cost-effective alternative \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/2102.05918)\]. These datasets, though noisy, can still yield state-of-the-art results when trained with appropriate architectures like dual-encoder models and contrastive loss functions \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/2102.05918)\]. Additionally, noisy datasets are not limited to vision tasks; they also play a role in natural language inference, as seen in the MultiNLI corpus, which provides diverse and challenging examples for evaluating cross-genre domain adaptation \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/1703.02391)\]. Industry datasets, often derived from historical data, are also frequently noisy, yet they can still be useful for training models if the performance metrics are carefully evaluated against cleaner inputs \[[12](http://arxiv.org/abs/1809.04019)\]. Transfer learning further benefits from large unlabeled datasets, as seen in the Colossal Clean Crawled Corpus, which enables pre-training on diverse tasks and improves performance across multiple benchmarks \[[15](http://arxiv.org/abs/1910.10683)\]. These studies collectively demonstrate that noisy datasets, when properly utilized, can overcome the limitations of curated data and contribute to the development of robust and scalable models.

This paradigm relies on direct supervision examples, which is not applicable to many emerging applications, such as generating adversarial attacks or generating prompts to control language models.
