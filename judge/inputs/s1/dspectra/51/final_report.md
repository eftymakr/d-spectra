## Related Works

Federated unlearning has emerged as a critical research area to address the "right to be forgotten" in federated learning (FL) systems \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2310.20448)\]. This concept allows users to remove their data contributions from the global model while preserving the model's utility \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/2401.05146)\]. Early approaches to federated unlearning primarily relied on retraining the model from scratch, which is computationally expensive \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/1910.01991)\]. To improve efficiency, several methods have been proposed that leverage historical parameter updates or local unlearning techniques \[[4](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/39da2d48f2132b919405e3bb6df6ece44ac6e15a)\]. For instance, FedEraser enables efficient client-level data removal by reconstructing the unlearned model using historical parameter updates retained at the central server \[[5](http://arxiv.org/abs/2205.12709)\]. This approach significantly reduces the time required for unlearning compared to full retraining \[[6](http://arxiv.org/abs/2210.10958)\].

In addition to efficiency, verifiability has become an essential requirement for federated unlearning. Verifiable federated unlearning ensures that the unlearning effect can be independently verified, which is crucial for compliance with data protection regulations \[[7](http://arxiv.org/abs/2207.05521)\]. The VeriFi framework was introduced to systematically analyze federated unlearning and quantify its effect through marking and checking mechanisms \[[8](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/eadeffdec9fac8fd7f9aea732ca410eb082b7dcf)\]. This approach allows users to verify the unlearning effect immediately after notifying the server of their intention to leave \[[9](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/f2f8f7a2ec1b2ede48cbcd189b376ab9fa0735ef)\]. ### <a id='category-unlearning-in-federated-learning'></a>Unlearning in Federated Learning: 11 papers found.
The category "Unlearning in Federated Learning" explores the challenges and solutions for enabling data erasure in federated learning (FL) systems, driven by the "right to be forgotten" and the need for robustness against malicious clients. Federated learning, which trains models across decentralized data sources without sharing raw data, requires specialized unlearning techniques to remove specific clients' contributions or data samples from the global model \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2310.20448)\]. This is particularly important given the increasing regulatory pressure and privacy concerns, as FL systems must now support mechanisms that allow users to withdraw their data contributions \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/2401.05146)\]. Existing unlearning methods in centralized machine learning are not directly applicable to FL due to differences in learning protocols and the distributed nature of FL \[[6](http://arxiv.org/abs/2210.10958)\]. To address this, researchers have proposed various federated unlearning (FU) approaches, such as FedEraser, which leverages historical parameter updates stored at the central server to reconstruct unlearned models efficiently, reducing the need for full retraining \[[6](http://arxiv.org/abs/2210.10958)\]. Other methods, like those proposed in \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/1910.01991)\], focus on defining the problem of efficient federated unlearning, identifying challenges, and proposing a general pipeline for handling class, client, and sample unlearning. Additionally, methods such as the one introduced in \[[11](http://arxiv.org/abs/2209.02299)\] aim to eliminate a client's contribution by subtracting historical updates and using knowledge distillation to maintain model performance without client data. Furthermore, the need for evaluating unlearning effectiveness has led to the development of new metrics and frameworks, such as Ferrari, which minimizes feature sensitivity to ensure successful unlearning \[[10](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/a52c3ec7325e81bd87fdf8d74bf9acfd1e1ffe5f)\]. These approaches highlight the growing interest in balancing data privacy, model performance, and system efficiency in FL unlearning \[[7](http://arxiv.org/abs/2207.05521)\].

However, FL also introduces new privacy risks, such as information leakage through model updates, which have led to the development of security analyses and attack mitigation strategies \[[6](http://arxiv.org/abs/2210.10958)\]. Overall, these works collectively underscore the evolving landscape of FL, focusing on improving performance, privacy, and practical deployment across diverse applications \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/1910.01991)\]\[[6](http://arxiv.org/abs/2210.10958)\]\[[24](http://arxiv.org/abs/1711.10677)\].
