## Related Works

Federated unlearning has emerged as a critical research area to address the "right to be forgotten" in federated learning (FL) systems \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2310.20448)\]. This concept allows users to remove their data contributions from the global model while preserving the model's utility \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/2401.05146)\]. Early approaches to federated unlearning primarily relied on retraining the model from scratch, which is computationally expensive \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/1910.01991)\]. To improve efficiency, several methods have been proposed that leverage historical parameter updates or local unlearning techniques \[[4](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/39da2d48f2132b919405e3bb6df6ece44ac6e15a)\]. For instance, FedEraser enables efficient client-level data removal by reconstructing the unlearned model using historical parameter updates retained at the central server \[[5](http://arxiv.org/abs/2205.12709)\]. This approach significantly reduces the time required for unlearning compared to full retraining \[[6](http://arxiv.org/abs/2210.10958)\].

In addition to efficiency, verifiability has become an essential requirement for federated unlearning. Verifiable federated unlearning ensures that the unlearning effect can be independently verified, which is crucial for compliance with data protection regulations \[[7](http://arxiv.org/abs/2207.05521)\]. The VeriFi framework was introduced to systematically analyze federated unlearning and quantify its effect through marking and checking mechanisms \[[8](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/eadeffdec9fac8fd7f9aea732ca410eb082b7dcf)\]. This approach allows users to verify the unlearning effect immediately after notifying the server of their intention to leave \[[9](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/f2f8f7a2ec1b2ede48cbcd189b376ab9fa0735ef)\]. Furthermore, VeriFi incorporates various unlearning and verification methods, offering a comprehensive evaluation of their effectiveness \[[10](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/a52c3ec7325e81bd87fdf8d74bf9acfd1e1ffe5f)\].

The need for efficient and verifiable federated unlearning has also been addressed in the context of federated recommendation systems. The FRU method, inspired by transaction rollback mechanisms, enables efficient unlearning by rolling back and calibrating historical parameter updates \[[11](http://arxiv.org/abs/2209.02299)\]. This approach is particularly useful in recommendation systems where users may want to remove their contributions from the global model \[[12](http://arxiv.org/abs/2002.02730)\]. Similarly, the SISA training framework was introduced to reduce the computational overhead associated with unlearning by strategically limiting the influence of a data point during training \[[13](http://arxiv.org/abs/2203.07320)\]. This method has shown significant improvements in unlearning efficiency, especially for complex learning tasks \[[14](http://arxiv.org/abs/1912.03817)\].

Recent research has also focused on the privacy implications of federated unlearning. The Starfish framework was proposed to ensure privacy during the unlearning process by using Two-Party Computation (2PC) techniques and shared historical client data \[[15](http://arxiv.org/abs/2205.08096)\]. This approach minimizes the risk of information leakage, which is a critical concern in FL systems \[[16](http://arxiv.org/abs/2012.13891)\]. Additionally, the Ferrari framework was introduced to address feature unlearning by optimizing feature sensitivity \[[17](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/06edb1f6eadb6c141d720fcca4c854578dc5d670)\].
