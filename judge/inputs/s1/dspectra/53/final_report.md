## Related Works

Deep sequential recommendation models have seen significant advancements, with a focus on capturing the dynamic nature of user interactions. Early approaches such as Markov Chains (MCs) and Recurrent Neural Networks (RNNs) were widely used for sequential modeling \[[4](http://arxiv.org/abs/1808.09781)\]. However, these methods often struggle with capturing long-term dependencies and handling sparse data effectively \[[4](http://arxiv.org/abs/1808.09781)\]. More recently, self-attention based models, such as SASRec, have emerged as a powerful alternative, leveraging attention mechanisms to capture both short-term and long-term dependencies in user behavior \[[4](http://arxiv.org/abs/1808.09781)\]. Despite their success, these models typically assume a fixed sequence structure and do not explicitly model the temporal intervals between interactions \[[1](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/9c7a455b1e48d01a99e58e884a6c1acb75074ad0)\].

To address the limitations of traditional models, several works have introduced time-aware mechanisms. TiSASRec explicitly models the timestamps of interactions within a sequential modeling framework, incorporating both the absolute positions of items and the time intervals between them \[[1](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/9c7a455b1e48d01a99e58e884a6c1acb75074ad0)\]. Similarly, MEANTIME proposes a mixture of attention mechanisms with multi-temporal embeddings to better capture the temporal dynamics of user behavior \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/2008.08273)\]. These models aim to improve the modeling of temporal information, which is often overlooked in standard self-attention approaches \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/2008.08273)\].

Efficiency remains a critical concern for attention-based models, especially when dealing with long sequences. Mamba4Rec introduces a selective state space model (SSM) to achieve linear complexity, enabling efficient processing of long behavior sequences while maintaining model performance \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/2403.03900)\]. This approach is inspired by the success of SSMs in control theory, which are designed to model and control dynamic systems with high efficiency \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/2403.03900)\]. EchoMamba4Rec further enhances this by integrating bi-directional processing with spectral filtering, allowing for more accurate modeling of complex user interaction patterns \[[15](http://arxiv.org/abs/2406.02638)\].

Another challenge in sequential recommendation is the modeling of diverse user preferences and interaction patterns. The Hierarchical Attention Network (HAN) addresses this by incorporating both long-term and short-term preferences, allowing for more nuanced modeling of user behavior \[[6](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/0b42168f22713c48d8b0ce3bc8f680d347184d88)\]. Similarly, the Multi-Order Attentive Ranking Model (MARank) unifies individual- and union-level item interactions, providing a more comprehensive understanding of user preferences \[[23](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/0763d8f5aefd4a41d264a5392b4f4076761e2d2f)\]. These models demonstrate the importance of capturing both local and global patterns in user behavior.

Recent works have also focused on improving the robustness and generalization of sequential recommendation models. ### <a id='category-time-aware-mechanisms'></a>Time-Aware Mechanisms: 1 papers found.
Time-aware mechanisms in sequential recommendation systems focus on incorporating temporal dynamics into the modeling of user interactions. While traditional approaches such as Markov Chains (MCs), Recurrent Neural Networks (RNNs), and Self Attention (SA) have been widely used for capturing sequential patterns, they often overlook the importance of time intervals between interactions, treating sequences purely as ordered lists \[[1](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/9c7a455b1e48d01a99e58e884a6c1acb75074ad0)\]. Recent research has sought to address this limitation by explicitly modeling timestamps and time intervals, leading to the development of models like TiSASRec, which integrates both absolute item positions and time intervals within a self-attention framework \[[1](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/9c7a455b1e48d01a99e58e884a6c1acb75074ad0)\]. This advancement allows for a more nuanced understanding of user behavior, particularly in scenarios where the timing of interactions significantly influences future actions. The inclusion of time-aware features has been shown to improve performance across various datasets and evaluation metrics, highlighting the critical role of temporal information in sequential recommendation \[[1](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/9c7a455b1e48d01a99e58e884a6c1acb75074ad0)\].

### <a id='category-sequential-recommendation-models'></a>Sequential Recommendation Models: 11 papers found.
Recent advances in sequential recommendation models have focused on addressing challenges such as capturing diverse interaction patterns, handling variable-length sequences, and leveraging temporal information effectively. Traditional approaches like Markov Chains (MCs) and Recurrent Neural Networks (RNNs) have been widely used, with MCs excelling in sparse datasets due to their simplicity, while RNNs offer better modeling of long-term dependencies in denser data \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/2008.08273)\]. However, these methods often fail to fully exploit the rich temporal dynamics inherent in user behavior \[[1](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/9c7a455b1e48d01a99e58e884a6c1acb75074ad0)\]. To overcome these limitations, self-attention based models, inspired by the Transformer architecture, have gained traction, as they can capture both short- and long-term dependencies without explicit sequence modeling \[[10](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/455e11bde14967fc76d87d4649d821d2f27c3055)\]. These models, such as SASRec, utilize attention mechanisms to identify relevant items from a user’s history, enabling efficient prediction of the next item \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/2008.08273)\]. Nevertheless, they often lack personalization and struggle with long sequences \[[18](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/5aed438fecf702ead9c0cddfa9a80bd00cff0138)\]. To address these issues, researchers have proposed models like MEANTIME, which incorporate multi-temporal embeddings to better capture diverse temporal patterns \[[1](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/9c7a455b1e48d01a99e58e884a6c1acb75074ad0)\]. Additionally, models such as S3-Rec introduce self-supervised learning to enhance data representation and mitigate the sparsity problem \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/2403.03900)\]. Others, like LimaRec, focus on lifelong sequential modeling, enabling incremental updates for online inference while capturing diverse user interests \[[4](http://arxiv.org/abs/1808.09781)\]. Furthermore, graph-based models like SURGE and knowledge-aware models like KATRec have been developed to integrate additional information, such as item categories and side information, to improve recommendation accuracy \[[7](http://arxiv.org/abs/2109.05261)\]\[[9](http://arxiv.org/abs/2411.01537)\]. These approaches collectively highlight the ongoing efforts to enhance sequential recommendation by addressing temporal dynamics, sparsity, and diverse interaction patterns through advanced modeling techniques.

### <a id='category-state-space-modeling'></a>State-Space Modeling: 3 papers found.
State-space modeling has emerged as a promising approach to address the challenges of sequential recommendation, particularly in handling long behavior sequences and improving inference efficiency \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/2403.03900)\]. Traditional attention-based models, while effective in capturing sequential dependencies, suffer from quadratic computational complexity, which limits their scalability and efficiency, especially for long-range interactions \[[15](http://arxiv.org/abs/2406.02638)\]. In contrast, state space models (SSMs) offer a more efficient framework by leveraging structured state matrices and parallel computation, enabling linear complexity for sequence processing \[[17](http://arxiv.org/abs/2409.01192)\]. Recent works such as Mamba4Rec and EchoMamba4Rec have explored the potential of SSMs in recommendation systems, integrating hardware-aware designs and control theory principles to enhance both performance and efficiency \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/2403.03900)\]\[[15](http://arxiv.org/abs/2406.02638)\]. These models incorporate techniques like selective SSMs, bi-directional processing, and frequency-domain filtering to better capture complex user behavior patterns while maintaining computational efficiency \[[15](http://arxiv.org/abs/2406.02638)\]. Additionally, frameworks such as SSD4Rec further demonstrate the adaptability of SSMs by introducing novel mechanisms for handling variable-length sequences and improving scalability \[[17](http://arxiv.org/abs/2409.01192)\]. These advancements highlight the growing interest in state-space modeling as a viable alternative to traditional attention-based approaches in sequential recommendation tasks.

### <a id='category-contrastive-learning-for-recommendations'></a>Contrastive Learning for Recommendations: 3 papers found.
Contrastive learning has emerged as a powerful paradigm for improving user representation learning in sequential recommendation systems, addressing the limitations of traditional approaches that rely solely on observational behavior sequences \[[7](http://arxiv.org/abs/2109.05261)\]. These methods often struggle with noisy and sparse data, leading to brittle and unstable models \[[7](http://arxiv.org/abs/2109.05261)\]. To overcome these challenges, researchers have proposed frameworks that incorporate counterfactual data distributions to enhance the robustness of user representations \[[7](http://arxiv.org/abs/2109.05261)\]. For instance, the CauseRec framework identifies dispensable and indispensable concepts within user behavior sequences and conditionally samples counterfactual user concept sequences to perform contrastive learning \[[7](http://arxiv.org/abs/2109.05261)\]. Similarly, the DuoRec model introduces a contrastive regularization to reshape the distribution of item embeddings, mitigating representation degeneration and improving semantic consistency \[[11](http://arxiv.org/abs/2110.05730)\]. Additionally, the CL4SRec framework leverages contrastive learning to derive self-supervision signals from user behavior sequences, enabling the extraction of more meaningful user patterns and better user representation encoding \[[21](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/a8f2764a76772ab957b31f5d7bb88527d46fbdc8)\]. These approaches highlight the growing interest in contrastive learning for sequential recommendation, emphasizing its potential to enhance model robustness, semantic consistency, and performance on real-world datasets.

### <a id='category-efficiency-in-long-sequences'></a>Efficiency in Long Sequences: 1 papers found.
Efficiency in long sequences remains a critical challenge for Transformer-based sequential recommender systems (SRSs), as traditional dot-product attention mechanisms result in quadratic computational complexity with sequence length \[[1](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/9c7a455b1e48d01a99e58e884a6c1acb75074ad0)\]. This issue is particularly pronounced in real-world applications where user interaction sequences can be extremely long, leading to high memory and time costs \[[1](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/9c7a455b1e48d01a99e58e884a6c1acb75074ad0)\]. To address this, recent work has focused on developing efficient attention mechanisms that maintain the expressive power of traditional methods while reducing computational overhead. One such approach is the L2-Normalized Linear Attention (LinRec), which achieves linear complexity by leveraging the equivalence conditions of efficient attention mechanisms \[[1](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/9c7a455b1e48d01a99e58e884a6c1acb75074ad0)\]. This method preserves the core properties of attention while enabling scalable deployment on long sequences. Theoretical analysis further reveals that LinRec can be interpreted through a statistical lens, highlighting its latent efficiency properties \[[1](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/9c7a455b1e48d01a99e58e884a6c1acb75074ad0)\]. Experimental results on benchmark datasets demonstrate that LinRec-based models achieve comparable or superior performance compared to state-of-the-art Transformer-based SRS models, while significantly improving time and memory efficiency \[[1](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/9c7a455b1e48d01a99e58e884a6c1acb75074ad0)\]. These findings underscore the importance of designing attention mechanisms that balance efficiency with model effectiveness in sequential recommendation tasks.

### <a id='category-multi-order-interaction-modeling'></a>Multi-Order Interaction Modeling: 2 papers found.
Modeling complex interactions in sequential recommendation systems has been a focal point in recent research, with a particular emphasis on capturing higher-order relationships between users, items, and sequential patterns \[[19](http://arxiv.org/abs/1707.02410)\]. Traditional approaches often decompose these interactions into pairwise relationships, separately modeling user preferences and sequential item dependencies \[[19](http://arxiv.org/abs/1707.02410)\]. However, this decomposition can limit the ability to fully capture nuanced user behavior. To address this, researchers have proposed unified frameworks that integrate multiple interaction types. For instance, the Multi-order Attentive Ranking Model (MARank) introduces a method that simultaneously captures both individual-level and union-level item interactions by leveraging multi-order features derived from deep neural networks \[[23](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/0763d8f5aefd4a41d264a5392b4f4076761e2d2f)\]. This approach uses attention mechanisms to weigh individual item interactions and aggregates these into a unified embedding, which is then processed through a residual neural network to capture broader interaction patterns \[[23](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/0763d8f5aefd4a41d264a5392b4f4076761e2d2f)\]. Such models aim to provide a more comprehensive understanding of user preferences by considering both short-term and long-term dependencies. These advancements highlight the growing trend toward more holistic interaction modeling in recommendation systems, where the integration of diverse interaction types is essential for improving prediction accuracy and adaptability to varying user behaviors.

### <a id='category-attention-based-approaches'></a>Attention-Based Approaches: 1 papers found.
Attention-based approaches in sequential recommendation have gained significant traction due to their ability to model complex user-item interactions through self-attention mechanisms \[[22](http://arxiv.org/abs/2308.09419)\]. These mechanisms allow models to dynamically assign attention weights to items in a sequence, prioritizing those deemed most relevant for next-item prediction \[[22](http://arxiv.org/abs/2308.09419)\]. However, empirical studies reveal that self-attention can sometimes assign large weights to less relevant items, leading to suboptimal recommendations \[[22](http://arxiv.org/abs/2308.09419)\]. This issue is attributed to factors such as sub-optimal position encoding and noisy input, which can distort the attention distribution \[[22](http://arxiv.org/abs/2308.09419)\]. To address this, researchers have proposed frameworks like AC-TSR, which introduce spatial and adversarial calibrators to refine attention weights by capturing spatial relationships and redistributing weights based on item contributions \[[22](http://arxiv.org/abs/2308.09419)\]. These advancements highlight the ongoing efforts to improve the accuracy and robustness of attention-based models in sequential recommendation tasks.
