## Related Works

The integration of knowledge graphs (KGs) with large language models (LLMs) to enhance reasoning capabilities in claim verification has gained significant attention in recent research. While LLMs have demonstrated strong performance in natural language understanding and generation, their ability to effectively leverage structured knowledge from KGs remains a critical challenge \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2306.08302)\]. KGs provide rich, semantically grounded representations that are well-suited for complex reasoning tasks, yet most existing claim verification methods rely on unstructured text corpora, limiting their ability to fully exploit the benefits of structured knowledge \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2306.08302)\]. 

Several studies have explored the use of KGs in improving the performance of LLMs for reasoning tasks. For instance, the work by \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2306.08302)\] proposes a roadmap for unifying LLMs and KGs, highlighting three frameworks: KG-enhanced LLMs, LLM-augmented KGs, and synergized LLMs + KGs. This work emphasizes the complementary nature of LLMs and KGs, suggesting that integrating them can enhance both models' reasoning abilities \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2306.08302)\]. Similarly, \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/2305.13168)\] presents an exhaustive evaluation of LLMs for KG construction and reasoning, showing that LLMs like GPT-4 excel in reasoning tasks, surpassing fine-tuned models in certain cases \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/2305.13168)\]. 

The ability of LLMs to perform zero-shot reasoning has also been explored. \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/2205.11916)\] demonstrates that LLMs can be zero-shot reasoners by simply adding a prompt such as "Let's think step by step" before each answer. This approach significantly improves performance on various reasoning tasks, including arithmetic and symbolic reasoning, without the need for hand-crafted few-shot examples \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/2205.11916)\]. This finding is relevant to the development of frameworks like ClaimPKG, which aim to leverage the zero-shot reasoning capabilities of LLMs in conjunction with structured knowledge from KGs \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2306.08302)\].

Additionally, the use of KGs for fact checking and claim verification has been investigated. \[[4](http://arxiv.org/abs/1905.00537)\] presents a method that uses reference information from KGs to assess claims and provide interpretable explanations. This approach leverages the semantic richness of KGs to improve the transparency of the fact-checking process \[[4](http://arxiv.org/abs/1905.00537)\]. Similarly, \[[5](http://arxiv.org/abs/2110.14168)\] introduces a framework that combines the in-context learning ability of LLMs with a library of specialized functions to perform program-guided fact-checking. This method enables the decomposition of complex claims into sub-tasks, enhancing the accuracy and explainability of the verification process \[[5](http://arxiv.org/abs/2110.14168)\].
