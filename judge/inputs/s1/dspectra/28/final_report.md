## Related Works

Federated learning (FL) has emerged as a promising paradigm for training machine learning models on distributed, private data without exposing raw data samples \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/1910.01991)\]. However, existing FL approaches often struggle with data heterogeneity across clients, leading to suboptimal model performance \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/1804.08333)\]. This challenge is further exacerbated in federated active learning (FAL), where the goal is to reduce annotation costs by selectively labeling the most informative samples while maintaining data privacy \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/1511.03575)\]. Despite recent advances, many FAL methods fail to account for the dynamic nature of client data distributions and the associated fluctuations in model parameters, which can significantly degrade accuracy \[[4](http://arxiv.org/abs/1909.12488)\].

Several works have explored the use of federated learning for heterogeneous data. For example, \[[5](http://arxiv.org/abs/2002.07948)\] introduces clustered federated learning (CFL), which groups clients based on their data distribution to improve model performance. This approach leverages geometric properties of the loss surface to form clusters and allows for more specialized models without requiring prior knowledge of the number of clusters. Similarly, \[[6](http://arxiv.org/abs/1802.07876)\] proposes a framework for federated multitask learning, which enables clients to train on their own data while sharing knowledge across tasks. These methods highlight the importance of addressing data heterogeneity in FL settings.

In the context of personalized federated learning, \[[7](http://arxiv.org/abs/2006.12097)\] and \[[8](http://arxiv.org/abs/2010.01264)\] explore approaches that allow each client to adapt the global model to their local data. \[[7](http://arxiv.org/abs/2006.12097)\] introduces a meta-learning-based approach to personalize models, while \[[8](http://arxiv.org/abs/2010.01264)\] proposes a method that enables users to fine-tune the global model with minimal additional computation. These works demonstrate that personalization can enhance model accuracy and adaptability, especially when data is non-i.i.d. across clients.

To improve the efficiency and fairness of federated learning, \[[9](http://arxiv.org/abs/2104.13417)\] and \[[10](http://arxiv.org/abs/1106.5730)\] propose methods that address communication bottlenecks and resource constraints. \[[9](http://arxiv.org/abs/2104.13417)\] introduces a framework for fair federated learning, which ensures a more uniform accuracy distribution across clients, while \[[10](http://arxiv.org/abs/1106.5730)\] focuses on optimizing client selection based on their resource capabilities. These approaches are crucial for ensuring that FL can scale effectively in real-world scenarios.

### <a id='category-data-heterogeneity-in-fl'></a>Data Heterogeneity in FL: 11 papers found.
Data heterogeneity in federated learning (FL) poses significant challenges to model performance and convergence, as clients often possess non-IID data, varying computational capabilities, and distinct model architectures \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/1910.01991)\]. To address these issues, several approaches have been proposed. For instance, Clustered FL (CFL) introduces a framework that groups clients based on data distribution without requiring prior knowledge of the number of clusters or modifications to the FL communication protocol \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/1910.01991)\]. Similarly, HeteroFL enables the training of heterogeneous local models with different computation complexities while producing a unified global inference model, challenging the assumption that local models must share the same architecture as the global model \[[8](http://arxiv.org/abs/2010.01264)\]. Synthetic Data Aided Federated Learning (SDA-FL) tackles non-IID data by generating synthetic data and using pseudo labeling to align client data distributions \[[12](http://arxiv.org/abs/2206.05507)\]. FedProto, on the other hand, improves convergence by aggregating class prototypes instead of gradients, reducing misalignment caused by heterogeneous data and model architectures \[[14](http://arxiv.org/abs/2105.00243)\]. Additionally, SCAFFOLD introduces a controlled averaging algorithm that reduces client drift and improves convergence guarantees by using control variates \[[16](http://arxiv.org/abs/1910.06378)\]. FCCL addresses both heterogeneity and catastrophic forgetting by leveraging unlabeled public data and knowledge distillation, enhancing generalization across domains \[[16](http://arxiv.org/abs/1910.06378)\]. Model heterogeneous FL, as explored in RHFL, handles noisy and varying client models by aligning feedback using public data, applying robust loss functions, and weighting clients based on confidence \[[22](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/1ace65d958cd27ae9ad857c49d8caa9aa5740526)\]. These approaches collectively highlight the diverse strategies to mitigate data heterogeneity, ranging from clustering and synthetic data generation to prototype learning and adaptive sampling \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/1910.01991)\]\[[8](http://arxiv.org/abs/2010.01264)\]\[[12](http://arxiv.org/abs/2206.05507)\]\[[14](http://arxiv.org/abs/2105.00243)\]\[[16](http://arxiv.org/abs/1910.06378)\]\[[22](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/1ace65d958cd27ae9ad857c49d8caa9aa5740526)\].

### <a id='category-efficiency-and-fairness-in-fl'></a>Efficiency and Fairness in FL: 3 papers found.
Efficiency and fairness in federated learning (FL) have been central themes in recent research, with multiple approaches addressing the challenges of heterogeneous client environments and resource constraints. For instance, FedCS introduces a novel FL protocol that actively manages clients based on their resource conditions, enabling more efficient aggregation of model updates and faster convergence \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/1910.01991)\]. Similarly, q-Fair Federated Learning (q-FFL) proposes an optimization objective inspired by fair resource allocation, aiming to achieve a more uniform accuracy distribution across devices by introducing a communication-efficient method called q-FedAvg \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/1804.08333)\]. These efforts highlight the growing emphasis on balancing performance with fairness, ensuring that all clients contribute meaningfully to the training process without compromising model quality. Additionally, the use of parallelized stochastic gradient descent (SGD) methods, such as HOGWILD!, has been explored to reduce memory locking and synchronization overhead, allowing for more scalable and efficient training in distributed settings \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/1511.03575)\]. These works collectively underscore the importance of designing FL frameworks that are both computationally efficient and equitable in their treatment of diverse client resources.

### <a id='category-federated-learning-foundations'></a>Federated Learning Foundations: 9 papers found.
Federated learning has emerged as a critical paradigm for training machine learning models over distributed data without compromising user privacy, with a growing focus on communication efficiency and handling heterogeneous data \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/1511.03575)\]. The core challenge lies in training a high-quality centralized model when data is unevenly distributed across a large number of devices, each holding only a small fraction of the total data \[[11](http://arxiv.org/abs/1610.02527)\]. Existing algorithms often fail to address this setting effectively, prompting the development of new methods such as FedMeta, which leverages meta-learning to reduce communication costs and improve accuracy compared to traditional approaches like FedAvg \[[6](http://arxiv.org/abs/1802.07876)\]. Similarly, the problem of semi-supervised learning in federated settings, where data is often partially or completely unlabeled, has led to the introduction of FedMatch, which introduces novel inter-client consistency losses and parameter decomposition strategies to enhance performance \[[7](http://arxiv.org/abs/2006.12097)\]. Additionally, multi-task learning has been proposed as a solution to address statistical and systems challenges in federated learning, with methods like MOCHA offering robustness to communication costs, stragglers, and fault tolerance \[[15](http://arxiv.org/abs/1804.05271)\]. These advancements highlight the evolving landscape of federated learning, emphasizing the need for efficient, privacy-preserving, and adaptable algorithms to handle real-world complexities.

### <a id='category-model-personalization-techniques'></a>Model Personalization Techniques: 5 papers found.
Federated Learning (FL) has emerged as a critical paradigm for training machine learning models on decentralized data while preserving privacy, with a growing focus on personalization to address data heterogeneity across clients \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/1910.01991)\]. Traditional FL approaches, such as Federated Averaging, aim to build a global model that generalizes across all participants, but they often fail to adapt to individual user data distributions, limiting their effectiveness in scenarios where local data is non-iid \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/1804.08333)\]. This challenge has led to the exploration of personalized FL variants, where the goal is to enable each client to fine-tune a shared global model to their local data, often leveraging techniques like Model-Agnostic Meta-Learning (MAML) \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/1511.03575)\]. Personalized FL approaches, such as the Adaptive Personalized Federated Learning (APFL) algorithm, have been proposed to improve local model performance by allowing clients to contribute to the global model while maintaining the flexibility to adapt to their specific data \[[4](http://arxiv.org/abs/1909.12488)\]. Additionally, recent work has emphasized the importance of local adaptation techniques, including fine-tuning, multi-task learning, and knowledge distillation, to enhance the accuracy of federated models for individual users \[[5](http://arxiv.org/abs/2002.07948)\]. These methods aim to balance the trade-off between global model generalization and local personalization, ensuring that the federated model remains both accurate and adaptable to diverse client data \[[6](http://arxiv.org/abs/1802.07876)\].
