## Related Works

Diffusion models have become a cornerstone in generative AI, particularly in image and video generation tasks. These models iteratively refine noisy inputs to produce high-quality outputs, but their computational demands pose challenges for deployment on resource-constrained devices \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2406.02540)\]. Recent advancements have shifted the focus from traditional U-Net architectures to diffusion transformers (DiTs), which offer superior scalability and performance in visual generation \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/2406.17343)\]. However, DiTs also face significant challenges in terms of parameter count and computational complexity, limiting their use in edge computing environments \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/2405.16005)\].

Quantization has emerged as a promising solution to reduce memory footprint and accelerate inference by lowering the bit-width of model parameters \[[4](http://arxiv.org/abs/2312.00845)\]. While post-training quantization (PTQ) is widely used for image generation models, its application to video generation tasks remains limited due to the unique challenges posed by temporal and spatial variability in activations \[[5](http://arxiv.org/abs/2302.04304)\]. Specifically, existing quantization methods often fail to maintain the spatiotemporal coherence required for high-quality video generation \[[6](http://arxiv.org/abs/2106.14156)\].

Several works have attempted to address these issues. For instance, ViDiT-Q was introduced to tackle the quantization challenges specific to DiT models, achieving W8A8 and W4A8 quantization with minimal degradation in visual quality \[[7](http://arxiv.org/abs/2408.06072)\]. Similarly, Q-DiT proposed a novel approach that integrates automatic quantization granularity allocation and sample-wise dynamic activation quantization to handle the spatial and temporal variance in DiTs \[[8](http://arxiv.org/abs/2310.03270)\]. PTQ4DiT further advanced this field by introducing techniques like Channel-wise Salience Balancing (CSB) and Spearmen's $\rho$-guided Salience Calibration (SSC) to address the challenges of salient channels and temporal activation variability \[[9](http://arxiv.org/abs/2306.02316)\].

Temporal Dynamic Quantization for Diffusion Models \[[10](http://arxiv.org/abs/2307.10373)\] introduced a method that dynamically adjusts the quantization interval based on time step information, significantly improving output quality without additional computational overhead during inference. This approach is compatible with both PTQ and quantization-aware training (QAT), making it a versatile solution for diffusion models \[[11](http://arxiv.org/abs/2111.14822)\].

EfficientDM \[[12](http://arxiv.org/abs/2211.11018)\] introduced a data-free and parameter-efficient fine-tuning framework for low-bit diffusion models, achieving QAT-level performance with PTQ-like efficiency. This method leverages a quantization-aware variant of the low-rank adapter (QALoRA) to distill the denoising capabilities of the full-precision model into its quantized counterpart \[[13](http://arxiv.org/abs/2212.11565)\].

### <a id='category-post-training-quantization-(ptq)-for-dits'></a>Post-Training Quantization (PTQ) for DiTs: 8 papers found.
Post-Training Quantization (PTQ) for Diffusion Transformers (DiTs) has emerged as a critical area of research due to the high computational and memory demands of DiT models, which are widely used for image and video generation tasks \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2406.02540)\]. Existing quantization methods, primarily designed for traditional diffusion models, often suffer from performance degradation when applied to DiTs due to the unique architectural characteristics of these models, such as significant spatial and temporal variances in weights and activations \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/2406.17343)\]. To address these challenges, several studies have proposed specialized quantization techniques tailored for DiTs. For instance, ViDiT-Q was introduced to handle the unique challenges of DiT quantization by designing an improved quantization scheme that achieves W8A8 and W4A8 with minimal quality loss \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2406.02540)\]. Similarly, Q-DiT integrates automatic quantization granularity allocation and sample-wise dynamic activation quantization to adaptively capture activation changes across timesteps and samples, achieving a notable reduction in FID when quantizing DiT-XL/2 to W6A8 \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/2406.17343)\]. Additionally, PTQ4DiT was developed to specifically address the quantization challenges of DiTs, including salient channels with extreme magnitudes and temporal variability in activation distributions, by introducing techniques like Channel-wise Salience Balancing (CSB) and Spearmen's $\rho$-guided Salience Calibration (SSC) \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/2405.16005)\]. Furthermore, HQ-DiT leverages 4-bit floating-point (FP) precision for both weights and activations, complemented by a clipping range selection mechanism, to minimize quantization error while maintaining high performance \[[5](http://arxiv.org/abs/2302.04304)\]. These approaches collectively highlight the ongoing efforts to develop efficient and effective PTQ methods for DiTs, ensuring their practical deployment on resource-constrained devices \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2406.02540)\]\[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/2406.17343)\]\[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/2405.16005)\]\[[5](http://arxiv.org/abs/2302.04304)\].

### <a id='category-quantization-techniques-for-image-generation'></a>Quantization Techniques for Image Generation: 3 papers found.
Diffusion models have become a cornerstone in image synthesis, achieving remarkable results through iterative noise estimation using deep neural networks \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2406.02540)\]. However, their deployment is hindered by high computational costs, memory usage, and slow inference speed \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2406.02540)\]. To address these challenges, post-training quantization (PTQ) has been explored as a promising approach to compress and accelerate diffusion models without retraining \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/2406.17343)\]. PTQ, however, faces unique difficulties due to the changing output distributions of noise estimation networks across multiple time steps and the bimodal activation distribution in shortcut layers \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2406.02540)\]. To mitigate these issues, researchers have proposed tailored PTQ methods, such as timestep-aware calibration and split shortcut quantization, which enable 4-bit quantization with minimal performance degradation \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2406.02540)\]. Additionally, data-free and parameter-efficient fine-tuning frameworks like EfficientDM have been introduced, combining the efficiency of PTQ with the performance of quantization-aware training (QAT) by using variants like QALoRA \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/2406.17343)\]. These approaches aim to reduce the computational burden while preserving generation quality, with some methods achieving only a 0.05 sFID increase when quantizing to 4-bit \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/2406.17343)\]. Furthermore, mixed-precision schemes and noise correction techniques have been developed to address quantization noise accumulation during the denoising process, ensuring stable performance across multiple steps \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/2405.16005)\]. These advancements highlight the ongoing efforts to make diffusion models more practical for real-world applications by improving their efficiency and robustness under quantization.
