## Related Works

Table discovery and analysis have garnered significant attention in recent years due to the increasing availability of tabular data in various domains. Traditional methods often rely on query tables or vague keywords to retrieve relevant tables, which can lead to large result sets requiring manual filtering \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/1804.07461)\]. This limitation highlights the need for more sophisticated approaches that can incorporate natural language (NL) requirements to refine search results. Our proposed task, NL-conditional table discovery (nlcTD), addresses this gap by combining a query table with NL conditions to improve search accuracy \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/1804.07461)\].

Several studies have explored the use of pre-trained language models for NLU tasks, emphasizing the importance of transfer learning and domain adaptation \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/2005.08314)\]. For instance, GLUE provides a benchmark for evaluating models across nine diverse NLU tasks, highlighting the challenges of generalization and domain mismatch \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/2005.08314)\]. Similarly, TaBERT extends this work by jointly learning representations for textual and tabular data, enabling better performance in semantic parsing tasks \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/1903.01949)\]. These models demonstrate the potential of leveraging structured data for more accurate understanding.

In the context of table detection and recognition, TableBank and the STDW dataset have been introduced to address the limitations of existing benchmarks, such as lack of diversity and poor generalization \[[4](http://arxiv.org/abs/2209.09207)\]\[[5](http://arxiv.org/abs/2406.10803)\]. TableBank provides a large-scale, high-quality dataset with 417K labeled tables, while STDW offers a diverse set of over 7,000 samples with varied table structures \[[4](http://arxiv.org/abs/2209.09207)\]\[[5](http://arxiv.org/abs/2406.10803)\]. These datasets are crucial for training and evaluating models that can generalize across different table formats and document layouts.

The challenge of handling large tables and ensuring data privacy in table question-answering (TableQA) tasks has also been addressed in recent work. HiddenTables and PyQTax introduce a cooperative game framework to evaluate LLMs' ability to handle complex queries, compositional dependencies, and natural language to programmatic command alignment . These contributions underscore the need for robust and secure methods for interacting with large-scale tabular data.

In addition, the importance of benchmarking and evaluation frameworks for table discovery has been emphasized in several studies. For example, the nlcTables dataset, introduced in this paper, includes 627 diverse queries and 22,200 candidate tables, providing a comprehensive benchmark for evaluating nlcTD methods \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/1804.07461)\]. The dataset also includes 21,200 relevance annotations, enabling a detailed analysis of model performance.
