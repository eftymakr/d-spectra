## Related Works

Recent advancements in understanding the internal mechanisms of large language models (LLMs) have focused on how feature representations evolve across layers and how these changes influence model behavior. Several studies have explored the impact of fine-tuning on model performance and alignment, highlighting the importance of identifying critical layers for efficient adaptation and robustness. For instance, \[[15](http://arxiv.org/abs/2402.11651)\] investigates the value of integrating negative examples during fine-tuning, showing that unsuccessful trajectories can provide valuable insights for improving model performance on tasks such as mathematical reasoning and strategic question answering. This aligns with our findings that certain layers are more sensitive to fine-tuning and play a crucial role in model behavior \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/1409.1556)\].

The effectiveness of fine-tuning strategies has also been extensively studied. \[[16](http://arxiv.org/abs/2202.10054)\] compares full fine-tuning and linear probing, finding that while full fine-tuning improves in-distribution accuracy, it can lead to worse out-of-distribution performance due to the distortion of pretrained features. This highlights the tradeoff between model adaptability and generalization, a key consideration in our work on identifying critical layers. Furthermore, \[[17](http://arxiv.org/abs/2310.03744)\] discusses the benefits of visual instruction tuning in multimodal models, emphasizing the importance of data-efficient training strategies, which parallels our approach to identifying and fine-tuning critical layers in LLMs.

In addition, \[[18](http://arxiv.org/abs/2002.05709)\] introduces SimCLR, a framework for contrastive learning of visual representations, which demonstrates the importance of data augmentation and nonlinear transformations in learning robust representations. While focused on vision, the principles of representation learning and model adaptability are closely related to our work on LLMs. Similarly, \[[19](http://arxiv.org/abs/2305.18290)\] proposes Direct Preference Optimization (DPO), a method for aligning LLMs with human preferences through a simple classification loss, which is more efficient than reinforcement learning approaches. This highlights the broader trend of simplifying fine-tuning processes while maintaining model performance.

The role of parameter-efficient fine-tuning methods has also been explored. \[[7](http://arxiv.org/abs/2110.04366)\] provides a unified view of parameter-efficient transfer learning, showing how various methods can be framed as modifications to specific hidden states in pre-trained models. This aligns with our approach of identifying and fine-tuning only the most critical layers, thereby reducing computational costs while preserving performance. ### <a id='category-parameter-efficient-transfer-learning'></a>Parameter-Efficient Transfer Learning: 4 papers found.
Parameter-efficient transfer learning has emerged as a critical area of research in NLP, particularly due to the computational and resource constraints associated with fine-tuning large pre-trained language models. Recent studies have shown that fine-tuning a model with hundreds of millions of parameters on small datasets does not necessarily lead to overfitting, suggesting that there exists a low-dimensional reparameterization that captures the essential dynamics of fine-tuning \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/2012.13255)\]. This phenomenon is further supported by the Neural Tangent Kernel (NTK) framework, which has been extended to Adam optimization and used to analyze the gradient descent dynamics of pre-trained models, revealing kernel-based behaviors during fine-tuning \[[6](http://arxiv.org/abs/2210.05643)\]. Additionally, researchers have proposed a unified framework to understand and design parameter-efficient transfer learning methods, which reframe these techniques as modifications to specific hidden states in pre-trained models, allowing for a systematic comparison of different approaches \[[7](http://arxiv.org/abs/2110.04366)\]. These methods often involve tuning only a small number of parameters, significantly reducing computational costs while maintaining strong performance. Furthermore, the integration of federated learning with parameter-efficient fine-tuning has been explored to address privacy concerns and resource limitations, enabling collaborative model training without direct data sharing \[[9](http://arxiv.org/abs/2309.00363)\]. Overall, these works highlight the growing interest in understanding and optimizing the efficiency of transfer learning in NLP, with a focus on reducing computational overhead while preserving model performance.

Instruction Tuning and Zero-Shot Performance has been a focal area in improving the adaptability and effectiveness of language models. Recent studies have demonstrated that instruction tuning—fine-tuning models on tasks described via natural language instructions—significantly enhances zero-shot performance on unseen tasks \[[10](http://arxiv.org/abs/2109.01652)\]. This approach has been successfully applied to both large language models (LLMs) and large multimodal models (LMMs), where instruction-tuned models outperform their unmodified counterparts and even surpass state-of-the-art models like GPT-3 in several benchmarks \[[10](http://arxiv.org/abs/2109.01652)\]. For instance, instruction-tuned models such as FLAN have shown substantial improvements in tasks like ANLI, RTE, BoolQ, and others, often outperforming few-shot GPT-3 by a large margin \[[10](http://arxiv.org/abs/2109.01652)\]. Additionally, research on LMMs has highlighted the importance of vision-language connectors and the effectiveness of using academic-task-oriented VQA data with response formatting prompts to achieve state-of-the-art results across multiple benchmarks \[[17](http://arxiv.org/abs/2310.03744)\]. These findings underscore the critical role of instruction tuning in enhancing model performance, particularly in zero-shot and few-shot settings, and highlight the importance of model scale, natural language instructions, and data efficiency in achieving superior results \[[10](http://arxiv.org/abs/2109.01652)\]\[[17](http://arxiv.org/abs/2310.03744)\].
