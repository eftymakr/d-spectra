## Related Works

Personalized outfit generation has been a focal point in recent research, driven by the need to recommend or generate fashion items that are both compatible and aligned with user preferences. Early approaches relied on rule-based systems and collaborative filtering, but these lacked the flexibility and personalization required for modern fashion recommendation systems \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2305.18290)\]. With the advent of deep learning, several works have explored neural network-based methods to improve compatibility and personalization in outfit generation.

A significant body of work focuses on outfit compatibility modeling. For instance, \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/1905.01866)\] proposes a system that scores fashion outfit candidates based on appearance and metadata, leveraging popularity on fashion websites to supervise the scoring component. This approach achieves an AUC of 85% for scoring and an accuracy of 77% for constrained composition tasks. Similarly, \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/1608.03016)\] introduces a multimodal multiinstance deep learning system that evaluates instance aesthetics and set compatibility simultaneously, further improving the quality of generated outfits.

Personalization in outfit recommendation has also been extensively studied. \[[4](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/ca3ad34ff2652cd922c3476514a758674bc7e76b)\] presents a framework that jointly considers compatibility and personalization, using generative adversarial training to synthesize new items for recommendation. This method employs a convolutional network to map query images into latent vectors and combines them with user preference vectors to generate target item images. Two discriminators are used to guide the generation process, ensuring compatibility and personalization. The results show that the generated items are more realistic and better matched than those from alternative methods.

Another line of research focuses on the use of transformer architectures for outfit generation and recommendation. \[[5](http://arxiv.org/abs/1807.03133)\] introduces OutfitTransformer, which models outfits as unordered sets of items and uses self-attention mechanisms to learn relationships between items. This framework is trained using a set-wise outfit ranking loss and achieves superior performance on compatibility prediction and complementary item retrieval tasks. The model's ability to capture global compatibility relations makes it a strong candidate for personalized outfit generation.

In addition to compatibility and personalization, some works have explored the use of reinforcement learning for fine-tuning generative models. \[[6](http://arxiv.org/abs/1810.02443)\] introduces Direct Preference Optimization (DPO), a method that simplifies the RLHF process by directly optimizing the reward model without requiring a separate reward model training phase. Unlike RLHF, which requires first training a reward model and then performing reinforcement learning to maximize this reward, DPO introduces a novel parameterization of the reward model that allows for the extraction of the optimal policy in closed form \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2305.18290)\]. This simplification eliminates the need for sampling from the language model during fine-tuning and reduces the reliance on extensive hyperparameter tuning, making the process more stable and computationally efficient \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2305.18290)\]. The approach has shown promising results in fine-tuning models to better align with human preferences, particularly in controlling the sentiment of generated text and improving the quality of responses in tasks such as summarization and single-turn dialogue \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2305.18290)\]. This method is particularly relevant in applications like personalized outfit generation, where the goal is to create compatible and personalized fashion items, and where traditional supervised learning paradigms may lack diversity and adaptability \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2305.18290)\].

### <a id='category-rule-based-systems-and-collaborative-filtering'></a>Rule-based Systems and Collaborative Filtering: 1 papers found.
In the realm of item recommendation, particularly with implicit feedback, several approaches have been proposed to improve personalized ranking. Traditional methods such as matrix factorization (MF) and adaptive k-nearest-neighbor (kNN) have been widely used, but they are not directly optimized for ranking tasks \[[14](http://arxiv.org/abs/1205.2618)\]. This limitation has led to the development of more sophisticated optimization criteria, such as BPR-Opt, which is derived from a Bayesian analysis of the problem and aims to maximize the posterior probability of user preferences \[[14](http://arxiv.org/abs/1205.2618)\]. The BPR-Opt criterion enables a more accurate and personalized ranking by focusing on the relative preferences between items rather than just predicting individual item scores. This approach is implemented through a learning algorithm based on stochastic gradient descent with bootstrap sampling, allowing for efficient and effective model training \[[14](http://arxiv.org/abs/1205.2618)\]. The application of BPR-Opt has shown promising results when applied to existing models like MF and kNN, demonstrating its potential to enhance the performance of recommendation systems in terms of personalized ranking \[[14](http://arxiv.org/abs/1205.2618)\].
