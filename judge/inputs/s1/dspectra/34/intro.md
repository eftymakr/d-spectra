## Related Works

Time series forecasting has been extensively studied, with recent advances focusing on improving accuracy, interpretability, and scalability. Traditional approaches, such as autoregressive models and Gaussian processes, have limitations in capturing complex temporal dependencies and handling large-scale datasets \[[15](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/1025b16f5fe8c0cc44d6775b6caf0bf7ceba3118)\]. More recently, deep learning models, particularly recurrent neural networks (RNNs) and transformers, have emerged as powerful tools for time series forecasting \[[17](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/0600a8982abb5ce087efd6ded007c19e4bfcad5d)\]. 

Transformers, in particular, have shown great potential in modeling long-range dependencies due to their self-attention mechanism. However, they face challenges in terms of computational efficiency and memory usage, especially for long time series \[[5](http://arxiv.org/abs/1907.00235)\]. To address these issues, several works have proposed modifications to the transformer architecture. For instance, PatchTST introduces a patching design that reduces the computational complexity of attention maps while retaining local semantic information \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2211.14730)\]. Similarly, Autoformer employs an Auto-Correlation mechanism to improve efficiency and accuracy in long-term forecasting \[[4](http://arxiv.org/abs/2106.13008)\]. 

The issue of non-stationarity in time series has also been a focus of recent research. Non-stationary Transformers address this by introducing a framework that balances series predictability with model capability, reducing mean squared error by up to 49.43% compared to state-of-the-art models \[[6](http://arxiv.org/abs/2205.14415)\]. Additionally, the use of self-supervised learning has gained traction, with models like PatchTST demonstrating superior performance when pre-trained on large datasets \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2211.14730)\]. 

In the context of financial time series forecasting, recent studies have explored the integration of large language models (LLMs) to enhance reasoning and interpretability. For example, a study on NASDAQ-100 stocks demonstrated that LLMs can effectively reason over textual news and price data, providing explainable forecasts \[[11](http://arxiv.org/abs/2306.11025)\]. This aligns with the goal of the proposed RTS-PnO framework, which aims to improve both forecasting accuracy and allocation decisions in financial applications.

The challenge of multi-horizon forecasting has also been addressed through various architectures. Temporal Fusion Transformers (TFT) combine recurrent and self-attention layers to model both short- and long-term dependencies, while also providing interpretable insights into temporal dynamics \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/1912.09363)\]. Similarly, the LSTNet framework leverages convolutional and recurrent networks to capture both local and global patterns in time series data \[[9](http://arxiv.org/abs/1703.07015)\].
