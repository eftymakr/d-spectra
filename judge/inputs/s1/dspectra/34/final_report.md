## Related Works

Time series forecasting has been extensively studied, with recent advances focusing on improving accuracy, interpretability, and scalability. Traditional approaches, such as autoregressive models and Gaussian processes, have limitations in capturing complex temporal dependencies and handling large-scale datasets \[[15](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/1025b16f5fe8c0cc44d6775b6caf0bf7ceba3118)\]. More recently, deep learning models, particularly recurrent neural networks (RNNs) and transformers, have emerged as powerful tools for time series forecasting \[[17](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/0600a8982abb5ce087efd6ded007c19e4bfcad5d)\]. 

Transformers, in particular, have shown great potential in modeling long-range dependencies due to their self-attention mechanism. However, they face challenges in terms of computational efficiency and memory usage, especially for long time series \[[5](http://arxiv.org/abs/1907.00235)\]. To address these issues, several works have proposed modifications to the transformer architecture. For instance, PatchTST introduces a patching design that reduces the computational complexity of attention maps while retaining local semantic information \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2211.14730)\]. Similarly, Autoformer employs an Auto-Correlation mechanism to improve efficiency and accuracy in long-term forecasting \[[4](http://arxiv.org/abs/2106.13008)\]. 

The issue of non-stationarity in time series has also been a focus of recent research. Non-stationary Transformers address this by introducing a framework that balances series predictability with model capability, reducing mean squared error by up to 49.43% compared to state-of-the-art models \[[6](http://arxiv.org/abs/2205.14415)\]. Additionally, the use of self-supervised learning has gained traction, with models like PatchTST demonstrating superior performance when pre-trained on large datasets \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2211.14730)\]. 

In the context of financial time series forecasting, recent studies have explored the integration of large language models (LLMs) to enhance reasoning and interpretability. For example, a study on NASDAQ-100 stocks demonstrated that LLMs can effectively reason over textual news and price data, providing explainable forecasts \[[11](http://arxiv.org/abs/2306.11025)\]. This aligns with the goal of the proposed RTS-PnO framework, which aims to improve both forecasting accuracy and allocation decisions in financial applications.

The challenge of multi-horizon forecasting has also been addressed through various architectures. Temporal Fusion Transformers (TFT) combine recurrent and self-attention layers to model both short- and long-term dependencies, while also providing interpretable insights into temporal dynamics \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/1912.09363)\]. Similarly, the LSTNet framework leverages convolutional and recurrent networks to capture both local and global patterns in time series data \[[9](http://arxiv.org/abs/1703.07015)\].

### <a id='category-time-series-forecasting-models'></a>Time Series Forecasting Models: 14 papers found.
Time series forecasting models have seen significant advancements, particularly in handling multivariate and long-term dependencies. Recent approaches leverage Transformer architectures, such as PatchTST \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2211.14730)\], which employs subseries-level patches and channel-independent embeddings to improve long-term forecasting accuracy. Similarly, Autoformer \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/1912.09363)\] introduces an Auto-Correlation mechanism to capture dependencies at the sub-series level, achieving state-of-the-art performance on multiple benchmarks. Other models, like TPA-LSTM \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/1809.04206)\], utilize frequency domain analysis to extract temporal patterns, while LogSparse Transformer \[[4](http://arxiv.org/abs/2106.13008)\] addresses memory bottlenecks by reducing space complexity to $O(L(\log L)^2)$. Additionally, models such as StemGNN \[[6](http://arxiv.org/abs/2205.14415)\] combine spectral domain techniques with graph neural networks to jointly capture inter-series and temporal dependencies. These innovations highlight the growing trend of integrating advanced attention mechanisms, decomposition strategies, and domain-specific adaptations to enhance forecasting accuracy and efficiency. The proposed RTS-PnO framework \[[15](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/1025b16f5fe8c0cc44d6775b6caf0bf7ceba3118)\] builds upon these advancements by introducing a risk-aware approach that aligns prediction with optimization objectives, ensuring robustness in financial fund allocation tasks.

[Snippet]: «Transformers have shown great power in time series forecasting due to their global-range modeling ability. However, their performance can degenerate terribly on non-stationary real-world data in which the joint distribution changes over time. Previous studies primarily adopt stationarization to attenuate the non-stationarity of original series for better predictability. But the stationarized series deprived of inherent non-stationarity can be less instructive for real-world bursty events forecasting. This problem, termed over-stationarization in this paper, leads Transformers to generate indistinguishable temporal attentions for different series and impedes the predictive capability of deep models. To tackle the dilemma between series predictability and model capability, we propose Non-stationary Transformers as a generic framework with two interdependent modules: Series Stationarization and De-stationary Attention. Concretely, Series Stationarization unifies the statistics of each input and converts the output with restored statistics for better predictability. To address the over-stationarization problem, De-stationary Attention is devised to recover the intrinsic non-stationary information into temporal dependencies by approximating distinguishable attentions learned from raw series. Our Non-stationary Transformers framework consistently boosts mainstream Transformers by a large margin, which reduces MSE by 49.43% on Transformer, 47.34% on Informer, and 46.89% on Reformer, making them the state-of-the-art in time series forecasting. Code is available at this repository: https://github.com/thuml/Nonstationary_Transformers.»

The category "Deep Learning Approaches in Time Series" encompasses a range of methodologies aimed at improving the accuracy and robustness of time series forecasting and analysis. These approaches leverage deep learning architectures such as Convolutional Neural Networks (CNNs), Recurrent Neural Networks (RNNs), and more recently, 2D tensor-based models to capture both short-term and long-term dependencies in temporal data \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2211.14730)\]. For instance, the LSTNet framework integrates CNNs and RNNs to effectively model complex temporal patterns, while also incorporating traditional autoregressive models to address scale insensitivity \[[9](http://arxiv.org/abs/1703.07015)\]. Similarly, the TimesNet model introduces a 2D transformation of time series data, enabling the modeling of intraperiod and interperiod variations through 2D kernels, leading to state-of-the-art performance across multiple tasks \[[12](http://arxiv.org/abs/2210.02186)\]. Additionally, the DLSTM architecture extends traditional RNNs by using genetic algorithms to optimize network architecture, demonstrating superior performance in production forecasting scenarios \[[15](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/1025b16f5fe8c0cc44d6775b6caf0bf7ceba3118)\]. Furthermore, recent advancements highlight the potential of large models in time series analysis, emphasizing their ability to handle massive volumes of temporal data and improve pattern recognition across diverse domains \[[19](http://arxiv.org/abs/2310.10196)\]. These developments collectively underscore the growing importance of deep learning in addressing the challenges of time series forecasting, particularly in financial and industrial applications.
