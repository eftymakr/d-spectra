## Related Works

Efficient serving of large language models (LLMs) has been a focal point of recent research, with significant emphasis on optimizing memory usage and inference speed through key-value (KV) cache management \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2412.19442)\]. KV caches store attention keys and values, reducing redundant computations and improving latency in autoregressive generation \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/2310.07240)\]. However, the design of caching strategies remains highly workload-dependent, with limited studies addressing real-world patterns \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/2402.02750)\]. This section reviews existing work on KV cache optimization, focusing on compression, reuse, and system-level improvements.

Several approaches have been proposed to reduce the memory footprint of KV caches. Quantization techniques, such as those in KIVI, enable significant memory savings by leveraging the distributional properties of KV cache elements \[[4](http://arxiv.org/abs/2407.01527)\]. KIVI achieves a 2.6x reduction in peak memory usage by applying 2-bit quantization per-channel for keys and per-token for values \[[5](http://arxiv.org/abs/2406.19707)\]. Similarly, MiniCache compresses KV caches across layers by exploiting high similarity between adjacent layers, achieving up to 5.02x compression ratios on LLaMA-2-7B \[[6](http://arxiv.org/abs/2502.16002)\]. These methods highlight the potential of quantization and layer-wise compression to enhance memory efficiency.

KV cache reuse has also been explored to minimize redundant computations. CacheGen introduces a custom tensor encoder that compresses KV caches into compact bitstream representations, reducing cache size by 3.5–4.3x and improving context-loading speed \[[7](http://arxiv.org/abs/2405.14366)\]. KVLink further enhances reuse by precomputing and concatenating KV caches for shared documents, reducing time-to-first-token by up to 96% \[[8](http://arxiv.org/abs/2503.16525)\]. These approaches demonstrate the importance of precomputing and efficiently managing cached states for repeated queries.

System-level optimizations have also been proposed to improve KV cache management. InfiniGen introduces dynamic KV cache management for long-text generation, prefetching only essential cache entries to reduce fetch overhead \[[9](http://arxiv.org/abs/2503.24000)\]. KVShare extends this idea by enabling multi-tenant KV cache reuse, improving throughput by up to 1.2x and reducing TTFT by 9.39x . These systems highlight the need for workload-aware policies that adapt to varying request patterns.

Despite these advances, challenges remain. KV cache compression can lead to increased end-to-end latency due to longer outputs . Additionally, existing methods often lack comprehensive benchmarking across diverse tasks and datasets . Recent work has emphasized the need for more rigorous evaluation of compression techniques, including their impact on accuracy and throughput .

### <a id='category-kv-cache-management-techniques'></a>KV Cache Management Techniques: 5 papers found.
Key-value (KV) cache management techniques have emerged as critical for optimizing the performance of large language models (LLMs) in real-world applications \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2412.19442)\]. These techniques aim to reduce redundant computations and improve memory utilization during inference, particularly in scenarios involving long contexts and high request volumes \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2412.19442)\]. Various approaches have been proposed to address the challenges of KV cache management, including token-level, model-level, and system-level optimizations \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2412.19442)\]. Token-level strategies such as quantization and low-rank decomposition have been explored to reduce the memory footprint of the KV cache \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/2402.02750)\]. For instance, KIVI, a 2-bit quantization method, enables significant memory reduction while maintaining model quality \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/2402.02750)\]. Similarly, CacheGen introduces a compression framework that adapts to varying bandwidth conditions, reducing KV cache size and delay with minimal impact on generation quality \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/2310.07240)\]. At the system level, approaches like InfiniGen leverage the ability to speculate on essential tokens to prefetch only necessary KV cache entries, thereby reducing fetch overhead in offloading-based systems \[[4](http://arxiv.org/abs/2407.01527)\]. Additionally, MiniCache focuses on compressing KV caches across layers by exploiting similarities between adjacent layers, achieving high compression ratios and improved throughput \[[5](http://arxiv.org/abs/2406.19707)\]. These methods collectively highlight the importance of tailored KV cache management strategies to enhance the efficiency and scalability of LLM serving systems.

[Snippet]: «We describe KVLink, an approach for efficient key-value (KV) cache reuse in large language models (LLMs). In many LLM applications, different inputs can share overlapping context, such as the same retrieved document appearing in multiple queries. However, the LLMs still need to encode the entire context for each query, leading to redundant computation. In this paper, we investigate a new strategy to eliminate such inefficiency, where the KV cache of each document is precomputed independently. During inference, the KV caches of retrieved documents are concatenated, allowing the model to reuse cached representations instead of recomputing them. To mitigate the performance degradation when using KV caches computed independently for each document, KVLink introduces two key techniques: adjusting positional embeddings of the KV cache at inference to match the global position after concatenation, and using trainable special tokens to restore self-attention across independently encoded documents. Experiments across 7 datasets demonstrate that KVLink improves question answering accuracy by an average of 4% over state-of-the-art methods. Furthermore, by leveraging precomputed KV caches, our approach reduces time-to-first-token by up to 96% compared to standard LLM inference, making it a scalable and efficient solution for context reuse. Additionally, KVLink can be combined with KV cache compression to further save cache loading and storage overhead while outperforming the baselines.»

[Snippet]: «Recent advances in long-text understanding have pushed the context length of large language models (LLMs) up to one million tokens. It boosts LLMs's accuracy and reasoning capacity but causes exorbitant computational costs and unsatisfactory Time to First Token (TTFT). KV cache reuse, which reuses the exact same KV cache of prefixes and templates or shares similar ones but with extra selective recomputation, offers a promising way to tackle this issue. However, prior studies overlook the cross-request KV reuse and the attention deviations introduced by new tokens during the decoding stage. In this paper, we present a KV cache management module that shares the KV cache across requests under multi-tenant scenarios without sacrificing model accuracy. Our system, KVShare, enables accurate and efficient LLM serving by 1. a Dual-Stage High Deviation algorithm (DHD) that conditionally selects a small portion of KV cache to be recomputed during both prefill and decode phases, and 2. a cache-aware scheduler that prioritizes requests based on their KV cache hit rates and orchestrates continuous batching to achieve enhanced system efficiency and faster TTFT. Multi-task experiments conducted on models such as Qwen2.5-7B, Llama3.1-8B and Yi1.5-9B demonstrate that KVShare reduces TTFT by up to 9.39x and increases 1.2x of the throughput compared to the full KV recompute. Moreover, KVShare achieves 20.38% boost in terms of accuracy compared to SOTA methods.»

### <a id='category-impact-on-latency-and-throughput'></a>Impact on Latency and Throughput: 1 papers found.
The category "Impact on Latency and Throughput" focuses on the trade-offs and performance implications of key-value (KV) cache compression techniques in large language model (LLM) serving. Existing compression algorithms aim to reduce memory consumption, thereby lowering computational costs, but their practical deployment in production environments remains limited \[[9](http://arxiv.org/abs/2503.24000)\]. While these methods can decrease memory usage, they often fail to optimize for real-world LLM serving, leading to suboptimal throughput and increased end-to-end latency due to longer outputs \[[9](http://arxiv.org/abs/2503.24000)\]. Furthermore, the accuracy of individual samples under compression is not well understood, revealing intrinsic limitations in handling specific LLM tasks \[[9](http://arxiv.org/abs/2503.24000)\]. These findings highlight the need for more robust evaluation frameworks and practical tools to guide the development and deployment of KV cache compression solutions in production settings \[[9](http://arxiv.org/abs/2503.24000)\].
