## Related Works

Fine-grained emotion classification has emerged as a critical area within sentiment analysis, particularly in the context of user-generated content such as app reviews. While traditional sentiment analysis often focuses on polarity (positive, negative, neutral) \[[17](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/12d0353ce8b41b7e5409e5a4a611110aee33c7bc)\], recent work has emphasized the need for more nuanced emotion detection that captures the complexity of human emotional expression \[[13](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/6a6f1b8ef3eefaf8027a76134859b44886a5f795)\]. This is especially relevant in app reviews, where users often express a wide range of emotions that go beyond simple sentiment labels \[[15](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/030ea3f854d688659500a949eac095cdba087682)\]. 

Several studies have explored the creation of large-scale emotion datasets to facilitate research in this area. The GoEmotions dataset, for instance, includes 58,000 manually annotated Reddit comments, labeled for 27 emotion categories or neutral \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2005.00547)\]. This dataset has been used to evaluate the performance of BERT-based models, achieving an average F1-score of 0.46 \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2005.00547)\]. Similarly, the CROWDSOURCING A WORD–EMOTION ASSOCIATION LEXICON paper describes the use of crowdsourcing to generate a large, high-quality word-emotion lexicon, demonstrating that this approach can yield reliable results \[[4](http://arxiv.org/abs/1308.6297)\]. 

In the context of app reviews, the On the Emotion of Users in App Reviews paper analyzed over seven million reviews from the Apple AppStore, highlighting the importance of emotional sentiment in understanding user experiences \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/1703.02256)\]. The paper also discusses the challenges of using sentiment analysis for software engineering applications, emphasizing the need for more sophisticated models \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/1703.02256)\]. 

The development of structured annotation frameworks has also been a focus of recent research. The Annotation, Modelling and Analysis of Fine-Grained Emotions on a Stance and Sentiment Detection Corpus paper extends the SemEval 2016 dataset with emotion annotations, analyzing annotation reliability and exploring the relationship between emotion and other annotation layers such as stance and sentiment \[[5](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/7d0531aaaa4dbf4033da964289843e7812262702)\]. This work contributes to the growing body of research on fine-grained emotion detection in social media \[[5](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/7d0531aaaa4dbf4033da964289843e7812262702)\]. 

The use of large language models (LLMs) for emotion classification has also been explored. The Sentiment Analysis in the Era of Large Language Models: A Reality Check paper evaluates the performance of LLMs on various sentiment analysis tasks, showing that while they perform well on simpler tasks, they lag behind in more complex ones \[[12](http://arxiv.org/abs/2305.15005)\]. This aligns with findings from the EmoNet paper, which developed a large dataset for fine-grained emotion detection and achieved state-of-the-art results using gated recurrent neural networks \[[13](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/6a6f1b8ef3eefaf8027a76134859b44886a5f795)\].
