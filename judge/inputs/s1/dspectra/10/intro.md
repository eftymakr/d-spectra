## Related Works

Multi-token prediction (MTP) has emerged as an alternative to the traditional next-token prediction (NTP) objective in pre-training large language models (LLMs). While NTP focuses on predicting one token at a time, MTP extends this by predicting multiple future tokens simultaneously, using multiple independent output heads that share a common model trunk \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2404.19737)\]. This approach has been shown to improve downstream performance, inference speed, and training efficiency, particularly for large models \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2404.19737)\]. However, smaller language models (SLMs) face challenges in effectively leveraging the MTP objective \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2404.19737)\].

Prior work has explored the limitations of NTP, highlighting issues such as error compounding during autoregressive inference and the failure of teacher-forcing to learn accurate next-token predictors in certain tasks \[[5](http://arxiv.org/abs/2403.06963)\]. These concerns have led to the development of alternative training strategies, such as teacherless training, which predicts multiple tokens in advance \[[5](http://arxiv.org/abs/2403.06963)\]. Additionally, the use of curriculum learning has been proposed to gradually introduce complexity during training, improving model performance \[[13](http://arxiv.org/abs/1704.03003)\]. For example, competence-based curriculum learning for neural machine translation has been shown to reduce training time and improve accuracy \[[10](http://arxiv.org/abs/1903.09848)\].

Curriculum learning has also been applied to natural language understanding (NLU) tasks, where models benefit from an easy-to-difficult training order, similar to human learning \[[11](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/2d315208dd88eed245dc4ae1ae398af75aa291f8)\]. This approach has been demonstrated to yield significant performance improvements without requiring manual design or external data \[[11](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/2d315208dd88eed245dc4ae1ae398af75aa291f8)\]. Similarly, scheduled sampling for sequence prediction has been used to gradually shift the training process from fully guided to less guided, reducing error accumulation \[[9](http://arxiv.org/abs/1506.03099)\].

In the context of reasoning, recent studies have explored the effectiveness of outcome supervision versus process supervision. Process supervision, which provides feedback for each intermediate reasoning step, has been shown to significantly outperform outcome supervision for tasks like solving mathematical problems \[[12](http://arxiv.org/abs/2305.20050)\]. This highlights the importance of structured feedback during training for complex reasoning tasks.

Furthermore, the use of reinforcement learning with human feedback has been proposed to align language models with user intent, improving truthfulness and reducing toxic outputs while maintaining performance on standard benchmarks \[[14](http://arxiv.org/abs/2203.02155)\]. This approach has been successfully applied to fine-tune models for instruction-following tasks \[[14](http://arxiv.org/abs/2203.02155)\].

In summary, the MTP objective represents a shift from the traditional NTP paradigm, offering potential benefits in efficiency and performance. However, its effectiveness varies across model sizes, prompting the need for curriculum learning strategies to optimize training \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2404.19737)\].
