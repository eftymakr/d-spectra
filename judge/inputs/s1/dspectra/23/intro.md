## Related Works

Radar and camera sensor fusion has been extensively studied for autonomous driving, with a focus on improving object detection, distance estimation, and calibration accuracy. Early approaches often relied on late fusion strategies, where outputs from individual sensors were combined at the result level \[[13](http://arxiv.org/abs/2209.06535)\]. However, these methods limited the ability to fully exploit the complementary properties of radar and camera data \[[13](http://arxiv.org/abs/2209.06535)\]. More recent works have explored early fusion strategies, where features from both modalities are combined at the feature level to enhance performance \[[13](http://arxiv.org/abs/2209.06535)\].

Several studies have focused on the challenges of radar data, particularly due to its sparsity and measurement uncertainty. For instance, \[[10](http://arxiv.org/abs/2007.14366)\] highlights the limitations of using radar for perception due to noise and ambiguities, and proposes a voxel-based early fusion and attention-based late fusion approach to better exploit geometric and dynamic information. This work demonstrates that combining radar with LiDAR can improve object detection and velocity estimation in real-world scenarios \[[10](http://arxiv.org/abs/2007.14366)\].

The importance of extrinsic calibration in sensor fusion has also been widely discussed. \[[12](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/0955326c67a947b5fac5fbae7238d04fc9adc251)\] addresses the calibration of radar-LiDAR-camera systems, emphasizing the challenges posed by the sparse informativeness of radar measurements. They propose a novel calibration method involving a special target design and a two-step optimization procedure to improve the accuracy of extrinsic parameters \[[12](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/0955326c67a947b5fac5fbae7238d04fc9adc251)\]. Similarly, \[[17](http://arxiv.org/abs/2103.07505)\] presents a continuous-time 3D radar-to-camera extrinsic calibration algorithm that utilizes radar velocity measurements without requiring specialized retroreflectors, making it more practical for real-world deployment \[[17](http://arxiv.org/abs/2103.07505)\].

In the context of radar-camera fusion, \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2009.08428)\] introduces a middle-fusion approach that combines radar point clouds with RGB images for object detection and distance estimation. This method uses a Radar Proposal Refinement (RPR) network to generate accurate object proposals and distance estimations by leveraging both radar and image information \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2009.08428)\]. \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/2011.04841)\] further advances this idea with CenterFusion, which uses a center point detection network and a frustum-based method to associate radar detections with object center points, improving velocity estimation accuracy without additional temporal information \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/2011.04841)\].

Recent works have also explored the use of transformer-based architectures for radar-camera fusion. \[[13](http://arxiv.org/abs/2209.06535)\] proposes a spatio-contextual fusion transformer that effectively exploits both spatial and contextual properties of camera and radar data for 3D object detection. This approach achieves state-of-the-art performance on the nuScenes dataset, demonstrating the potential of transformer-based methods in this domain \[[13](http://arxiv.org/abs/2209.06535)\].
