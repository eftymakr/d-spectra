## Related Works

Radar and camera sensor fusion has been extensively studied for autonomous driving, with a focus on improving object detection, distance estimation, and calibration accuracy. Early approaches often relied on late fusion strategies, where outputs from individual sensors were combined at the result level \[[13](http://arxiv.org/abs/2209.06535)\]. However, these methods limited the ability to fully exploit the complementary properties of radar and camera data \[[13](http://arxiv.org/abs/2209.06535)\]. More recent works have explored early fusion strategies, where features from both modalities are combined at the feature level to enhance performance \[[13](http://arxiv.org/abs/2209.06535)\].

Several studies have focused on the challenges of radar data, particularly due to its sparsity and measurement uncertainty. For instance, \[[10](http://arxiv.org/abs/2007.14366)\] highlights the limitations of using radar for perception due to noise and ambiguities, and proposes a voxel-based early fusion and attention-based late fusion approach to better exploit geometric and dynamic information. This work demonstrates that combining radar with LiDAR can improve object detection and velocity estimation in real-world scenarios \[[10](http://arxiv.org/abs/2007.14366)\].

The importance of extrinsic calibration in sensor fusion has also been widely discussed. \[[12](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/0955326c67a947b5fac5fbae7238d04fc9adc251)\] addresses the calibration of radar-LiDAR-camera systems, emphasizing the challenges posed by the sparse informativeness of radar measurements. They propose a novel calibration method involving a special target design and a two-step optimization procedure to improve the accuracy of extrinsic parameters \[[12](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/0955326c67a947b5fac5fbae7238d04fc9adc251)\]. Similarly, \[[17](http://arxiv.org/abs/2103.07505)\] presents a continuous-time 3D radar-to-camera extrinsic calibration algorithm that utilizes radar velocity measurements without requiring specialized retroreflectors, making it more practical for real-world deployment \[[17](http://arxiv.org/abs/2103.07505)\].

In the context of radar-camera fusion, \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2009.08428)\] introduces a middle-fusion approach that combines radar point clouds with RGB images for object detection and distance estimation. This method uses a Radar Proposal Refinement (RPR) network to generate accurate object proposals and distance estimations by leveraging both radar and image information \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2009.08428)\]. \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/2011.04841)\] further advances this idea with CenterFusion, which uses a center point detection network and a frustum-based method to associate radar detections with object center points, improving velocity estimation accuracy without additional temporal information \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/2011.04841)\].

Recent works have also explored the use of transformer-based architectures for radar-camera fusion. \[[13](http://arxiv.org/abs/2209.06535)\] proposes a spatio-contextual fusion transformer that effectively exploits both spatial and contextual properties of camera and radar data for 3D object detection. This approach achieves state-of-the-art performance on the nuScenes dataset, demonstrating the potential of transformer-based methods in this domain \[[13](http://arxiv.org/abs/2209.06535)\].

### <a id='category-radar-camera-fusion-approaches'></a>Radar-Camera Fusion Approaches: 11 papers found.
The related works in radar-camera fusion approaches focus on improving 3D object detection and distance estimation by combining the strengths of radar and camera data. Early methods often used late fusion strategies, which combined outputs at the result level, but these methods were limited in exploiting the complementary properties of the sensors \[[7](http://arxiv.org/abs/2102.05150)\]. More recent approaches, such as CenterFusion, have introduced middle-fusion strategies that integrate radar and camera features at an intermediate stage, improving accuracy and robustness \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/2011.04841)\]. These methods typically involve generating object proposals from radar data and refining them using image features, as seen in the Radar Proposal Refinement (RPR) network \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2009.08428)\]. Additionally, some works have focused on aligning radar and camera data in a joint 3D space, using mechanisms like cross-attention to resolve geometric correspondences \[[5](http://arxiv.org/abs/2205.02111)\]. The use of 4-D radar, which provides elevation information, has also been explored to enhance the richness of radar data, enabling more accurate 3D object detection \[[8](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/31cddde4f300fe043e0de0d0373affedd0539685)\]. Furthermore, some studies have emphasized the importance of data association, using techniques like frustum-based methods to link radar detections to object center points \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/2011.04841)\]. The development of datasets such as nuScenes and CRUW has been crucial for evaluating and advancing these fusion techniques \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/2008.05711)\]\[[9](http://arxiv.org/abs/2210.09267)\]. Overall, these works highlight the challenges of sensor fusion, including coordinate system discrepancies and data sparsity, and propose innovative solutions to improve the reliability and performance of radar-camera systems in autonomous driving \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2009.08428)\]\[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/2011.04841)\]\[[5](http://arxiv.org/abs/2205.02111)\]\[[7](http://arxiv.org/abs/2102.05150)\].

### <a id='category-extrinsic-calibration-methods'></a>Extrinsic Calibration Methods: 4 papers found.
Extrinsic calibration of multi-sensor systems, such as radar, LiDAR, and camera, remains a critical challenge in autonomous systems due to the heterogeneity of sensor data and the difficulty in accurately aligning their coordinate frames \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2009.08428)\]. Recent approaches have focused on addressing the limitations of traditional calibration methods, particularly in handling sparse and uncertain radar data \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2009.08428)\]. For instance, some studies have introduced novel calibration targets and optimization strategies to improve the accuracy of parameter estimation \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2009.08428)\]. Others have explored the use of continuous-time algorithms that leverage radar velocity measurements without requiring specialized retroreflectors, enhancing the practicality of calibration in real-world scenarios \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/2011.04841)\]. Additionally, there has been a growing interest in automatic, targetless calibration methods that enable calibration using only short driving distances, making the process more efficient and adaptable to dynamic environments \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/2008.05711)\]. These methods often rely on minimizing point-to-plane distances or incorporating multi-modal cross-attention mechanisms to improve feature matching and reduce the impact of height uncertainty \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/2008.05711)\]. Furthermore, some works have emphasized the importance of observability analysis and identifiability studies to ensure robustness and reliability in calibration outcomes \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2009.08428)\]. Overall, the field is advancing through the integration of geometric constraints, probabilistic modeling, and real-time optimization techniques to achieve accurate and reliable extrinsic calibration for multi-sensor systems.
