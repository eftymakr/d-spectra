## Related Works

Narrative summarization, particularly for long-form texts such as books, movies, and TV scripts, remains a challenging task due to the need to capture intricate plotlines, character interactions, and thematic coherence \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2105.08209)\]. Existing large language models (LLMs) often struggle with maintaining coherence and accuracy in such tasks \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2105.08209)\]. Several works have explored methods to improve summarization quality and address specific limitations of current approaches.

Early efforts in text summarization focused on extractive methods, which select and rearrange content from the source text \[[10](http://arxiv.org/abs/1704.04368)\]. However, these methods often fail to capture the deeper semantic structure and coherence required for narrative summaries \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2105.08209)\]. Abstractive summarization, which generates new content based on the source, has shown promise in producing more fluent and coherent summaries \[[10](http://arxiv.org/abs/1704.04368)\]. Nevertheless, abstractive models can suffer from repetition, inaccuracy, and lack of factual correctness \[[11](http://arxiv.org/abs/1908.08960)\]. To address these issues, researchers have proposed various architectures, such as pointer-generator networks, which allow models to copy words from the source text while generating novel content \[[10](http://arxiv.org/abs/1704.04368)\]. Additionally, reinforcement learning has been used to improve the quality of generated summaries by encouraging more readable and accurate outputs \[[12](http://arxiv.org/abs/1705.04304)\].

The challenge of handling long documents has also been addressed through the development of models that can process long sequences efficiently. For example, Longformer introduces an attention mechanism that scales linearly with sequence length, enabling the processing of very long documents \[[15](http://arxiv.org/abs/2004.05150)\]. Similarly, SEAL proposes a Transformer-based model that dynamically selects input snippets for each output segment, improving the ability to generate coherent summaries from long-form texts \[[23](http://arxiv.org/abs/2006.10213)\]. These models demonstrate the potential of advanced architectures to handle the complexity of long narratives.

Recent work has also focused on improving control over generated summaries, allowing users to specify desired attributes such as length, focus, or content \[[20](http://arxiv.org/abs/2012.04281)\]. CTRLsum, for instance, enables control over summaries through a set of keywords, allowing for more tailored outputs without affecting the model's training process \[[20](http://arxiv.org/abs/2012.04281)\]. This level of control is particularly important for narrative summarization, where the ability to highlight specific aspects of the story is crucial.

In addition to model improvements, there has been growing interest in evaluating the quality of summaries more effectively. SummEval re-evaluates summarization evaluation metrics and proposes a comprehensive framework for assessing both automated and human judgments \[[8](http://arxiv.org/abs/2007.12626)\]. This work highlights the importance of developing robust evaluation protocols to ensure that models produce summaries that are not only fluent but also factually accurate and coherent \[[8](http://arxiv.org/abs/2007.12626)\].
