## Related Works

Prosody modeling in text-to-speech (TTS) has been a central research focus, with numerous approaches aiming to improve the naturalness and controllability of synthesized speech. Early methods often relied on handcrafted rules or statistical models to estimate pitch, duration, and energy \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2011.06465)\]. However, these approaches were limited in their ability to capture the complex dependencies between linguistic features and prosody \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/2202.07816)\]. More recently, deep learning has enabled end-to-end TTS systems that implicitly model prosody through acoustic features \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/1710.07654)\]. Despite these advancements, controlling prosody remains challenging, especially when no explicit prosody labels are available in the training data \[[4](http://arxiv.org/abs/1909.10302)\].

Several works have attempted to enhance prosody modeling by incorporating pre-trained language models or latent representations. For instance, BERT embeddings have been used to encode semantic and syntactic information, which helps TTS systems generate more natural prosody \[[5](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/fbf2a6a887ea92311cf207d522c535daf867a6ba)\]. Similarly, quantized latent vectors pre-trained on large-scale unpaired data have been proposed to improve prosody expressiveness \[[6](http://arxiv.org/abs/1705.08947)\]. These methods often rely on additional training or specialized modules, which can limit their flexibility for post-hoc adjustments \[[7](http://arxiv.org/abs/2010.11439)\].

Non-autoregressive TTS models have also gained attention for their efficiency and parallelizability. For example, Parallel Tacotron uses a variational autoencoder to relax the one-to-many mapping problem and improve naturalness \[[8](http://arxiv.org/abs/1710.08969)\]. Similarly, FastSpeech and its variants have been developed to generate speech in parallel, reducing inference time while maintaining quality \[[9](http://arxiv.org/abs/1811.02122)\]. However, these models often struggle with long-range dependencies and require careful design to ensure prosody consistency \[[10](http://arxiv.org/abs/2006.03575)\].

Mispronunciation correction in TTS has traditionally relied on grapheme-to-phoneme (G2P) dictionaries, which are not practical in low-resource settings \[[11](http://arxiv.org/abs/2011.05161)\]. Recent approaches have explored end-to-end mispronunciation detection and diagnosis using attention mechanisms and data augmentation techniques \[[12](http://arxiv.org/abs/2206.14643)\]. These methods aim to improve the robustness of TTS systems by aligning audio with phoneme sequences and detecting mispronunciations during inference \[[13](http://arxiv.org/abs/2002.03788)\].

The challenge of post-hoc control of prosody and pronunciation has motivated the development of model-agnostic methods that manipulate internal representations of pre-trained TTS models. For example, hierarchical prosody modeling has been proposed to condition phoneme-level prosody on word-level features, improving both audio quality and naturalness \[[14](http://arxiv.org/abs/2106.06103)\].
