## Related Works

Maximum Inner Product Search (MIPS) has been extensively studied in recent years due to its critical role in machine learning and information retrieval, especially in high-dimensional data applications \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/1405.5869)\]. Traditional approaches to MIPS either rely solely on inner product (IP) similarity, which can suffer from local optima and redundant computations \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/1405.5869)\], or transform the problem into a nearest neighbor search (NNS) under the Euclidean metric, which often leads to topology distortion and information loss \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/1405.5869)\]. While these two paradigms have been widely explored, recent research suggests that the distinction between IP and Euclidean metrics is not inherently binary \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/1405.5869)\]. Instead, integrating both metrics in the design of indexing and search algorithms can significantly enhance MIPS performance \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/1405.5869)\].

Several methods have been proposed to address the challenges of MIPS. Asymmetric LSH (ALSH) was introduced to convert the problem of approximate MIPS into an approximate near neighbor search, enabling sublinear time algorithms \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/1509.01469)\]. This approach was later improved by introducing a new transformation that converts MIPS into a cosine similarity search, leveraging signed random projections \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/1410.5410)\]. Additionally, quantization-based approaches have been developed to approximate inner product search by decomposing vectors into subspaces and minimizing quantization error \[[4](http://arxiv.org/abs/1610.03317)\]. These methods avoid the need for higher-dimensional feature space augmentation, improving both efficiency and accuracy \[[4](http://arxiv.org/abs/1610.03317)\].

Graph-based approaches have also gained traction for MIPS. The inner-product navigable small world graph (ip-NSW) represents a state-of-the-art method, achieving an order of magnitude speedup over baseline methods \[[5](http://arxiv.org/abs/1909.13459)\]. However, ip-NSW exhibits strong norm bias, where large norm items dominate search results \[[5](http://arxiv.org/abs/1909.13459)\]. To address this, ip-NSW+ was introduced, combining angular and inner-product proximity graphs to improve robustness and performance \[[5](http://arxiv.org/abs/1909.13459)\]. Similarly, the norm-adjusted proximity graph (NAPG) was proposed to enhance graph-based indexing by incorporating norm-aware adjustments during construction \[[7](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/25454f456c514219c9d6eeb42200ebbf378db9ca)\].

Clustering-based methods, such as spherical $k$-means, have also been explored for approximate MIPS, offering competitive performance compared to LSH-based methods \[[8](http://arxiv.org/abs/1507.05910)\]. Furthermore, product quantization has been applied to approximate nearest neighbor search, with extensions to MIPS by incorporating score-aware loss functions \[[17](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/23cff8ecfc5feb4208dbb90ff3fa7a6adb214e9c)\]. These methods, while effective, often suffer from distortion errors or data distribution imbalances \[[19](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/865488b467250ceb85bb59d35b8d4fb4f10ae421)\].

### <a id='category-traditional-mips-approaches'></a>Traditional MIPS Approaches: 5 papers found.
The category "Traditional MIPS Approaches" encompasses a range of methods aimed at improving the efficiency of Maximum Inner Product Search (MIPS), a critical task in high-dimensional data applications such as recommender systems. Early approaches primarily relied on brute-force computation, which is computationally expensive and impractical for large-scale datasets \[[4](http://arxiv.org/abs/1610.03317)\]. To address this, researchers explored hashing techniques, such as Locality Sensitive Hashing (LSH), but found them insufficient for MIPS due to the asymmetry of inner product similarity \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/1405.5869)\]. As a result, novel hashing frameworks like Asymmetric LSH (ALSH) were developed, leveraging mathematical insights to convert inner product search into a near neighbor search problem, enabling sublinear time complexity \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/1405.5869)\]. Additionally, tree-based structures, such as cone trees and Ball-Cone Trees (BC-Tree), were introduced to enhance search efficiency by partitioning Euclidean space and enabling faster traversal during query processing \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/1509.01469)\]\[[5](http://arxiv.org/abs/1909.13459)\]. These methods often involved post-processing steps to align with traditional nearest neighbor algorithms, which could introduce additional computational overhead \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/1410.5410)\]. More recently, efforts have shifted toward integrating indexing and inference directly into the model training process, as seen in Indexable Probabilistic Matrix Factorization (IPMF), which allows for sublinear retrieval times without requiring separate post-processing \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/1410.5410)\]. Furthermore, data-dependent optimizers like OPTIMUS have been proposed to dynamically select the most suitable MIPS solver based on input characteristics, improving overall performance \[[4](http://arxiv.org/abs/1610.03317)\]. These traditional approaches highlight the ongoing challenges in balancing accuracy, efficiency, and adaptability in MIPS, setting the stage for more advanced methods like the Metric-Amphibious Graph (MAG) and Adaptive Navigation with Metric Switch (ANMS) introduced in the paper \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/1405.5869)\]\[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/1509.01469)\]\[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/1410.5410)\]\[[4](http://arxiv.org/abs/1610.03317)\]\[[5](http://arxiv.org/abs/1909.13459)\].

### <a id='category-quantization-based-techniques'></a>Quantization-Based Techniques: 3 papers found.
Quantization-based techniques have emerged as a powerful approach for efficient approximate Maximum Inner Product Search (MIPS) and nearest neighbor search in high-dimensional spaces. These methods leverage the decomposition of data into low-dimensional subspaces, enabling efficient indexing and retrieval by quantizing vectors into compact codes \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/1509.01469)\]. For instance, product quantization decomposes the space into a Cartesian product of subspaces, allowing for the estimation of Euclidean distances from quantized codes, which significantly improves search efficiency, especially when combined with inverted file systems \[[14](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/4748d22348e72e6e06c2476486afddbc76e5eca7)\]. Similarly, quantization-based approaches for MIPS utilize multiple subspaces with learned codebooks to approximate inner products, avoiding the need for high-dimensional feature space augmentation \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/1509.01469)\]. Furthermore, recent advancements such as Anisotropic Additive Quantization combine score-aware loss functions with additive quantization to reduce approximation errors, offering improved accuracy over product quantization \[[17](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/23cff8ecfc5feb4208dbb90ff3fa7a6adb214e9c)\]. These techniques highlight the versatility of quantization in balancing search speed and accuracy, making them a critical area of research in information retrieval and machine learning.

### <a id='category-budgeted-mips-algorithms'></a>Budgeted MIPS Algorithms: 4 papers found.
Recent research on Budgeted MIPS algorithms has focused on improving search efficiency while maintaining a high level of search quality, particularly in large-scale machine learning applications such as recommender systems \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/1405.5869)\]. Several approaches have been proposed to address the limitations of traditional methods, which often suffer from linear time complexity or lack flexibility in controlling the trade-off between efficiency and accuracy \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/1509.01469)\]. For instance, the Greedy-MIPS algorithm was introduced to handle budgeted MIPS by design, achieving significant speedups over naive approaches while maintaining competitive performance \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/1405.5869)\]. Similarly, wedge sampling has been explored as a method to approximate top-k MIPS under computational budgets, with theoretical analysis showing its superiority over diamond sampling in both efficiency and accuracy \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/1509.01469)\]. Additionally, there has been a growing interest in developing algorithms that do not require preprocessing and allow users to directly control the suboptimality of their results, such as the first approximate algorithm for MIPS that introduces a bandit setting to exploit the structure of the problem \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/1410.5410)\]. These advancements highlight the ongoing efforts to balance computational efficiency with search quality in the context of budgeted MIPS, addressing both theoretical and practical challenges in real-world applications.
