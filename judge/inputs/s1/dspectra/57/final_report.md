## Related Works

Generative retrieval (GR) has recently gained significant attention in the field of information retrieval (IR) as a promising alternative to traditional retrieval paradigms \[[2](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/5beaeee1e27007bd12967480bb9c379cebd15e6e)\]. Unlike conventional index-retrieve frameworks, GR models directly generate document identifiers (docids) for a given query using a sequence-to-sequence approach \[[6](http://arxiv.org/abs/2304.04171)\]. This paradigm allows for end-to-end optimization of retrieval tasks by integrating document encoding and query processing within a single model \[[17](http://arxiv.org/abs/2310.12455)\]. However, most existing GR models are primarily evaluated on static document collections, with limited exploration of their performance in dynamic environments where documents are continuously added \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2308.14968)\].

Several works have focused on improving the efficiency and effectiveness of GR models. For instance, CLEVER introduces an incremental product quantization method to encode new documents with low computational cost, enabling continual learning in dynamic settings \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2308.14968)\]. Similarly, GenRet proposes a discrete auto-encoding approach to learn semantic docids, which are optimized through training to better capture document relationships \[[6](http://arxiv.org/abs/2304.04171)\]. These methods highlight the importance of docid design in achieving robust performance in evolving corpora.

The design of docids plays a crucial role in the performance of GR models. Numeric-based docids, while efficient, often suffer from poor generalization over dynamic corpora due to their tendency to overfit the initial document set \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2308.14968)\]. This limitation is exacerbated by the lack of semantic alignment with language models' pre-trained knowledge \[[14](http://arxiv.org/abs/2305.13859)\]. In contrast, text-based docids, such as n-grams or synthetic queries, offer finer-grained representation and better semantic alignment, leading to improved performance in dynamic environments \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2308.14968)\]. This aligns with findings from \[[14](http://arxiv.org/abs/2305.13859)\], which show that term-set generation can enhance retrieval accuracy by capturing document semantics more effectively.

Efforts to improve GR scalability have also been explored. For example, the RIPOR framework introduces a prefix-oriented ranking optimization algorithm and constructs docids based on relevance associations between queries and documents, achieving significant improvements on large-scale benchmarks \[[8](http://arxiv.org/abs/2311.09134)\]. Similarly, the DynamicRetriever system eliminates the need for sparse or dense indexes by embedding document identifiers directly into the model, enabling end-to-end retrieval \[[9](http://arxiv.org/abs/2203.00537)\]. These approaches demonstrate the potential of model-based IR systems in handling large and evolving document collections.

Furthermore, the importance of docid diversity and semantic richness has been emphasized in recent studies. ### <a id='category-generative-retrieval-(gr)-paradigm-and-its-evolution'></a>Generative Retrieval (GR) Paradigm and Its Evolution: 5 papers found.
Generative retrieval (GR) has evolved as a promising paradigm in information retrieval, shifting from traditional index-retrieve-rank pipelines to models that directly generate document identifiers (docids) from queries \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2308.14968)\]. Recent advances focus on improving adaptability to dynamic corpora, where document collections continuously evolve \[[19](http://arxiv.org/abs/2403.12499)\]. Existing GR methods often rely on training-based indexing, which incurs high costs and underutilizes pre-trained knowledge in large language models (LLMs) \[[11](http://arxiv.org/abs/2408.02152)\]. To address these limitations, few-shot indexing-based GR frameworks have been proposed, enabling models to generate docids without explicit training \[[11](http://arxiv.org/abs/2408.02152)\]. Additionally, the reliance on preprocessed docids has been identified as a constraint, limiting the ability to retrieve new documents \[[17](http://arxiv.org/abs/2310.12455)\]. To overcome this, fully end-to-end retrieval paradigms have been introduced, allowing models to automatically learn optimal docids for both existing and new documents \[[17](http://arxiv.org/abs/2310.12455)\]. Furthermore, the performance of GR models over dynamic corpora has been evaluated, revealing that text-based docids, with their fine-grained design and semantic alignment with LLMs, outperform numeric-based docids, which tend to overfit to initial document sets \[[19](http://arxiv.org/abs/2403.12499)\]. These findings highlight the importance of docid design in GR and pave the way for hybrid approaches that combine the efficiency of numeric-based docids with the effectiveness of text-based ones \[[19](http://arxiv.org/abs/2403.12499)\].

Furthermore, generative models that align token-level docid generation with document-level relevance estimation have been developed to improve retrieval effectiveness without relying on complex reinforcement learning frameworks \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/2404.07220)\]. These advancements highlight the ongoing efforts to optimize GR models for both efficiency and effectiveness, particularly in dynamic environments where document collections evolve continuously.
