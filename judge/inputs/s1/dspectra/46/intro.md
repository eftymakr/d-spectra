## Related Works

Cross-domain sequential recommendation (CDSR) has gained significant attention in recent years due to its ability to alleviate data sparsity and cold-start problems in recommender systems by leveraging user behavior across multiple domains \[[1](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/a8f2764a76772ab957b31f5d7bb88527d46fbdc8)\]. Traditional approaches to CDSR often rely on self-attention mechanisms within transformer models, incorporating domain-specific modules to enhance performance \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/2311.04590)\]. However, these methods typically overlook the potential of the self-attention module itself, which is a core component of the transformer architecture \[[3](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/6bceab2405e4bbd4dadc16e8b946aa12dbddefc7)\]. The self-attention mechanism is naturally capable of capturing complex correlations among user behaviors, yet it is underutilized in CDSR frameworks \[[4](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/96bfe33a2503e960153a934e10be91da4d8b13a6)\].

Several studies have attempted to improve CDSR by introducing domain-aware components into transformer-based models. For instance, \[[5](http://arxiv.org/abs/2312.10325)\] proposes a domain-aware module to better align user preferences across domains. Similarly, \[[6](http://arxiv.org/abs/2304.03891)\] introduces a multi-task framework that combines next-item prediction with contrastive learning to derive self-supervision signals. These approaches, while effective, often suffer from the limitations of data sparsity and the inability to fully exploit the self-attention mechanism \[[7](http://arxiv.org/abs/2108.11333)\].

To address these challenges, researchers have explored alternative strategies such as contrastive learning and data augmentation. For example, \[[8](http://arxiv.org/abs/2209.10163)\] proposes a multi-task framework called CL4SRec, which integrates contrastive learning to extract more meaningful user patterns. Additionally, \[[9](http://arxiv.org/abs/2311.04760)\] introduces an Adaptive Multi-Interest Debiasing framework (AMID) that enhances cross-domain information propagation under open-world assumptions. These methods demonstrate the potential of leveraging cross-domain information to improve recommendation accuracy \[[10](http://arxiv.org/abs/2108.07976)\].

Another line of research focuses on the sparsity problem in recommendations by utilizing cross-domain item embeddings. \[[11](http://arxiv.org/abs/1802.08452)\] introduces CDIE-C, a method that learns comprehensive item representations by leveraging both single-domain and cross-domain sessions. This approach effectively addresses the sparsity issue by integrating information from related domains \[[12](http://arxiv.org/abs/2401.04971)\]. Similarly, \[[13](http://arxiv.org/abs/2407.11245)\] proposes a dual dynamic graph with hybrid metric training to enhance cross-domain sequential recommendations \[[14](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/6dd0605f7de7581c69b4acf0ab78000748a09f3e)\].

The issue of negative transfer in CDSR has also been explored. \[[15](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/63555b4f363ee72dd8d49d8deca68760542c0cd2)\] introduces a cooperative learning framework between single- and cross-domain sequential recommendation, aiming to mitigate the negative effects of domain mismatch \[[16](http://arxiv.org/abs/2405.12473)\]. This work highlights the importance of balancing knowledge transfer between domains to achieve better performance \[[17](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/2ca1abd015c72e7c04d63fa653b0a27d9592fc02)\].
