## Related Works

Neural retrieval methods using transformer-based pre-trained language models have advanced multilingual and cross-lingual retrieval \[[23](http://arxiv.org/abs/1810.04805)\]. However, their effectiveness for low-resource, morphologically rich languages such as Amharic remains underexplored due to data scarcity and suboptimal tokenization \[[24](http://arxiv.org/abs/2503.18570)\]. This gap is addressed by introducing Amharic-specific dense retrieval models based on pre-trained Amharic BERT and RoBERTa backbones \[[24](http://arxiv.org/abs/2503.18570)\].

Additionally, we train a ColBERT-based late interaction retrieval model that achieves the highest MRR@10 score (0.843) among all evaluated models \[[24](http://arxiv.org/abs/2503.18570)\]. The category "Research Trends in Neural Retrieval and Language Modeling" encompasses a range of advancements in leveraging pre-trained language models for information retrieval and natural language processing tasks. Recent studies have focused on improving the efficiency and effectiveness of these models, particularly in handling low-resource languages and complex tasks like open-domain question answering. For instance, models like Primer have been developed to reduce the computational costs of large Transformer models by introducing architectural modifications such as squaring ReLU activations and adding depthwise convolutions \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2109.08668)\]. This has enabled significant improvements in training efficiency, with Primer achieving up to a 4X reduction in training cost compared to the original T5 architecture \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2109.08668)\]. Similarly, the introduction of dense retrieval techniques has allowed for more accurate semantic matching between queries and documents, as seen in models like RepBERT, which uses contextualized embeddings to achieve state-of-the-art results on the MS MARCO Passage Ranking task \[[11](http://arxiv.org/abs/1609.08144)\]. Furthermore, the development of models such as BERT and its variants has demonstrated the potential of pre-trained language models to capture world knowledge, enabling them to perform well on tasks like semantic textual similarity and question answering \[[14](http://arxiv.org/abs/1906.08237)\]\[[15](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/ad880bf603b7acbeb1583e725e5453fb7dd94253)\]. Additionally, the integration of retrieval mechanisms with language models, such as in Retrieval-Augmented Generation (RAG), has shown promise in enhancing the ability of models to access and manipulate knowledge during generation tasks \[[10](http://arxiv.org/abs/2207.00785)\]. These developments highlight the ongoing efforts to improve both the efficiency and effectiveness of neural retrieval and language modeling approaches.
