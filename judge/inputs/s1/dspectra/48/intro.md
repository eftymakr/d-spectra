## Related Works

Neural retrieval methods using transformer-based pre-trained language models have advanced multilingual and cross-lingual retrieval \[[23](http://arxiv.org/abs/1810.04805)\]. However, their effectiveness for low-resource, morphologically rich languages such as Amharic remains underexplored due to data scarcity and suboptimal tokenization \[[24](http://arxiv.org/abs/2503.18570)\]. This gap is addressed by introducing Amharic-specific dense retrieval models based on pre-trained Amharic BERT and RoBERTa backbones \[[24](http://arxiv.org/abs/2503.18570)\]. 

The RoBERTa-Base-Amharic-Embed model (110M parameters) achieves a 17.6% relative improvement in MRR@10 and a 9.86% gain in Recall@10 over the strongest multilingual baseline, Arctic Embed 2.0 (568M parameters) \[[24](http://arxiv.org/abs/2503.18570)\]. More compact variants, such as RoBERTa-Medium-Amharic-Embed (42M), remain competitive while being over 13x smaller \[[24](http://arxiv.org/abs/2503.18570)\]. This aligns with findings that smaller models can be effective in low-resource settings \[[10](http://arxiv.org/abs/2207.00785)\]. Additionally, we train a ColBERT-based late interaction retrieval model that achieves the highest MRR@10 score (0.843) among all evaluated models \[[24](http://arxiv.org/abs/2503.18570)\]. This is consistent with the effectiveness of late interaction models in improving retrieval performance \[[8](http://arxiv.org/abs/2004.12832)\].

We benchmark our proposed models against both sparse and dense retrieval baselines to systematically assess retrieval effectiveness in Amharic \[[24](http://arxiv.org/abs/2503.18570)\]. This approach is similar to the evaluation of dense retrieval models in other languages \[[13](http://arxiv.org/abs/2004.04906)\]. Our analysis highlights key challenges in low-resource settings and underscores the importance of language-specific adaptation \[[24](http://arxiv.org/abs/2503.18570)\]. This is in line with the need for language-specific models in low-resource settings \[[24](http://arxiv.org/abs/2503.18570)\].

To foster future research in low-resource IR, we publicly release our dataset, codebase, and trained models \[[24](http://arxiv.org/abs/2503.18570)\]. This is in line with the open-source movement in NLP research \[[25](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/1e077413b25c4d34945cc2707e17e46ed4fe784a)\]. The importance of open-source models in advancing research is well documented \[[25](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/1e077413b25c4d34945cc2707e17e46ed4fe784a)\].

Recent work has also shown that pre-trained language models can be adapted for specific tasks with minimal fine-tuning \[[25](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/1e077413b25c4d34945cc2707e17e46ed4fe784a)\]. This is similar to the approach taken in this work, where pre-trained models are adapted for Amharic retrieval \[[24](http://arxiv.org/abs/2503.18570)\]. The effectiveness of such approaches has been demonstrated in other tasks \[[25](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/1e077413b25c4d34945cc2707e17e46ed4fe784a)\].
