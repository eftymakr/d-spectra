## Related Works

Long-tailed recognition remains a challenging problem in visual recognition due to the imbalance in class distribution, where a few majority classes dominate the training data while most minority classes contain only a limited number of samples \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2207.09052)\]. This imbalance leads to poor generalization for tail classes, as standard supervised learning algorithms are primarily designed for balanced training sets \[[19](http://arxiv.org/abs/1611.08976)\]. Several approaches have been proposed to address this issue, including class-balancing strategies, data resampling, and contrastive learning methods.

Contrastive learning has emerged as a promising approach for representation learning, particularly in balanced data settings \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2207.09052)\]. However, its performance on long-tailed data is limited due to issues such as gradient conflicts and imbalanced attraction and repulsion gradients between positive and negative pairs \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2207.09052)\]. For instance, supervised contrastive learning (SCL) tends to bias toward high-frequency classes, making it difficult to learn effective representations for tail classes \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/2107.12028)\]. This issue is further exacerbated by the lack of uniformity in feature distribution, which can lead to poor separability of minority class samples \[[7](http://arxiv.org/abs/2111.13998)\].

To address these challenges, several works have proposed modifications to contrastive learning. For example, Balanced Contrastive Learning (BCL) introduces class-averaging and class-complement strategies to balance the gradient contribution of negative classes and ensure that all classes appear in every mini-batch \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2207.09052)\]. Similarly, Parametric Contrastive Learning (PaCo) introduces class-wise learnable centers to rebalance the optimization process and improve the performance of long-tailed recognition \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/2107.12028)\]. These approaches aim to mitigate the biases introduced by imbalanced data distributions.

Another line of research focuses on decoupling representation learning from classifier learning. For example, Decoupling Representation and Classifier for Long-Tailed Recognition \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/1910.09217)\] shows that data imbalance may not be an issue in learning high-quality representations, and that strong long-tailed recognition can be achieved by adjusting only the classifier \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/1910.09217)\]. This approach has led to new state-of-the-art performance on common long-tailed benchmarks like ImageNet-LT and iNaturalist \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/1910.09217)\].

Additionally, methods such as Subclass-Balancing Contrastive Learning (SBCL) and Targeted Supervised Contrastive Learning (TSC) have been proposed to address the limitations of existing contrastive learning approaches. SBCL clusters head classes into subclasses to enforce a two-layer class hierarchy, while TSC improves the uniformity of the feature distribution on the hypersphere \[[7](http://arxiv.org/abs/2111.13998)\].
