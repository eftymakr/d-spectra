## Related Works

De novo genome assembly has been a focal point of genomics research, driven by the advent of next-generation sequencing (NGS) technologies that generate massive volumes of short reads \[[1](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/c6e319a023f932d6d2ff8897c2c91e56e872db8e)\]. Early efforts, such as the de novo assembly of human genomes using short reads, highlighted the challenges posed by the short read length and the need for sophisticated algorithms to achieve high-quality assemblies \[[1](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/c6e319a023f932d6d2ff8897c2c91e56e872db8e)\]. These studies demonstrated that while NGS provides ultrahigh throughput at a lower cost, the short read length makes de novo assembly extremely challenging \[[1](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/c6e319a023f932d6d2ff8897c2c91e56e872db8e)\].

Several assemblers have been developed to address these challenges. SOAPdenovo, for instance, was one of the early tools that enabled the assembly of many genomes, but it had limitations in terms of continuity, accuracy, and coverage, especially in repeat regions \[[2](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/f8d1052e2c9d1cdd63093427ccc9169e848d777d)\]. To overcome these issues, its successor, SOAPdenovo2, was introduced, offering improvements in memory efficiency, repeat resolution, and coverage \[[2](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/f8d1052e2c9d1cdd63093427ccc9169e848d777d)\]. This tool was benchmarked against other assemblers and showed significant improvements in assembly length and accuracy \[[2](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/f8d1052e2c9d1cdd63093427ccc9169e848d777d)\].

The development of long-read sequencing technologies, such as PacBio and Oxford Nanopore, has also contributed to advancements in de novo assembly. Canu, designed specifically for noisy single-molecule sequences, has shown promising results in assembling complete microbial genomes and near-complete eukaryotic chromosomes \[[4](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/2b59fd18ada769ef76fb7fcc7382f063791abfab)\]. It utilizes adaptive k-mer weighting and repeat separation to improve assembly continuity and reduce runtime \[[4](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/2b59fd18ada769ef76fb7fcc7382f063791abfab)\]. Similarly, the Minimap2 aligner has been optimized for long reads, providing fast and accurate alignment of DNA or long mRNA sequences against large reference databases \[[18](http://arxiv.org/abs/1708.01492)\].

The computational challenges of de novo assembly have also been addressed through algorithmic improvements. For example, the SHARCGS algorithm was developed to assemble short-read data with high accuracy and speed, demonstrating its effectiveness on various genomes \[[8](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/fbd59e17f72c3aedb3cd06af2e50a91526ccd81a)\]. Additionally, the ALLPATHS assembler was introduced to handle the challenges of de novo assembly from whole-genome shotgun microreads, achieving high-quality assemblies for bacterial and larger genomes \[[6](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/9cbccbe327e51dffd5c9b8b3199b6958d000b902)\].

Theoretical studies have also contributed to the understanding of the complexity of sequence assembly. A paper on the parametric complexity of sequence assembly explored the connections between repeat complexity, read lengths, and coverage, providing insights into the “hard” instances of the assembly problem \[[3](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/d04b3cc774151260a59a5e6768b6cfae7fc3e512)\]. This work suggests ways to improve existing assemblers and guide future theoretical investigations \[[3](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/d04b3cc774151260a59a5e6768b6cfae7fc3e512)\].

The theoretical studies on sequence assembly complexity highlight the challenges and opportunities in genome assembly, particularly with the advent of new DNA sequencing technologies. These studies emphasize that while advances in sequencing have drastically reduced costs and increased data output, the computational complexity of assembling these sequences remains a significant hurdle. The parametric complexity of the assembly problem is influenced by factors such as repeat complexity, read lengths, overlap lengths, and coverage, which determine the difficulty of assembling "hard" instances \[[3](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/d04b3cc774151260a59a5e6768b6cfae7fc3e512)\]. Theoretical frameworks have been developed to address these challenges, including the exploration of de novo assembly from short reads, such as microreads, which are characteristic of next-generation sequencing technologies \[[6](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/9cbccbe327e51dffd5c9b8b3199b6958d000b902)\]. These studies have demonstrated that high-quality assemblies can be achieved for bacterial genomes, even with short reads, and have introduced novel methods for representing assemblies in graph form to capture ambiguities and polymorphisms \[[6](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/9cbccbe327e51dffd5c9b8b3199b6958d000b902)\]. Theoretical investigations have also aimed to provide a general method applicable to various types of DNA sequence data, not limited to short reads \[[6](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/9cbccbe327e51dffd5c9b8b3199b6958d000b902)\]. These contributions have laid the groundwork for more efficient and accurate assembly algorithms, which are critical for advancing personalized medicine and disease management through de novo assembly \[[3](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/d04b3cc774151260a59a5e6768b6cfae7fc3e512)\].

### <a id='category-long-read-sequencing-technologies-and-their-assemblers'></a>Long-Read Sequencing Technologies and Their Assemblers: 6 papers found.
Long-read sequencing technologies have significantly advanced de novo genome assembly, enabling the reconstruction of high-quality reference genomes from complex and repetitive regions \[[1](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/c6e319a023f932d6d2ff8897c2c91e56e872db8e)\]. These technologies, such as PacBio and Oxford Nanopore, produce long reads that reduce the need for high coverage and improve the resolution of structural variations \[[1](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/c6e319a023f932d6d2ff8897c2c91e56e872db8e)\]. However, the inherent error rates of these technologies pose challenges in assembling large repeats and haplotypes \[[1](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/c6e319a023f932d6d2ff8897c2c91e56e872db8e)\]. To address these issues, tools like Canu have been developed, offering improved assembly continuity, reduced runtime, and support for nanopore data \[[1](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/c6e319a023f932d6d2ff8897c2c91e56e872db8e)\]. Additionally, methods such as MHAP, which uses probabilistic hashing for overlapping long reads, have enhanced the accuracy and contiguity of assemblies for eukaryotic genomes \[[4](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/2b59fd18ada769ef76fb7fcc7382f063791abfab)\]. Despite these advances, short-read assemblies still suffer from significant gaps and missing regions, particularly in repetitive and duplicated sequences, highlighting the necessity of long-read technologies for comprehensive genome coverage \[[3](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/d04b3cc774151260a59a5e6768b6cfae7fc3e512)\]. Furthermore, the integration of long-read data with scaffolding techniques and graph-based assembly outputs has enabled more complete and accurate genome assemblies \[[1](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/c6e319a023f932d6d2ff8897c2c91e56e872db8e)\]. These developments underscore the importance of long-read sequencing in achieving high-quality, complete genome assemblies for both microbial and eukaryotic organisms \[[1](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/c6e319a023f932d6d2ff8897c2c91e56e872db8e)\]\[[4](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/2b59fd18ada769ef76fb7fcc7382f063791abfab)\].

### <a id='category-performance-metrics-and-benchmarks-of-assemblers'></a>Performance Metrics and Benchmarks of Assemblers: 3 papers found.
The related works section focuses on the performance metrics and benchmarks of assemblers, which are critical for evaluating the quality and efficiency of genome assembly tools. Existing studies have highlighted the challenges of de novo assembly, particularly with large datasets and complex genomic structures, and have proposed various metrics to assess assembly accuracy and integrity \[[1](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/c6e319a023f932d6d2ff8897c2c91e56e872db8e)\]. For instance, string-based assemblers and overlap-layout-consensus (OLC) assemblers have been shown to be well-suited for short and longer reads, respectively, while De Bruijn graph-based assemblers are more appropriate for handling large datasets with millions of short reads \[[5](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/7279acd9c61a8de019ea2d55d23124dfbb6b5269)\]. Additionally, there has been a push for more objective and scalable metrics to evaluate assembly quality without relying on reference genomes, such as probabilistic measures that estimate the likelihood of observing reads from an assembled sequence \[[10](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/d7827222e6d751e13b1fe74b97ce413a037abfaf)\]. These metrics offer a more practical and reliable way to compare different assemblers, especially in the absence of gold-standard references. Furthermore, tools like BLAT have been developed to improve the speed and accuracy of sequence alignment, which is a key step in genome assembly \[[19](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/2878976835c73ed0906e17a7336b9bf10d491a1a)\]. These advancements underscore the ongoing efforts to enhance the performance and reliability of genome assemblers, which is essential for the growing field of personalized medicine and disease management. The development of new metrics and tools continues to shape the landscape of genome assembly, addressing the computational and algorithmic challenges that arise from the increasing complexity of genomic data.
