## Related Works

Zero-Knowledge Proofs (ZKPs) have emerged as a critical cryptographic primitive for privacy-preserving computation, enabling a prover to demonstrate the validity of a statement without revealing additional information \[[12](http://arxiv.org/abs/2012.10452)\]. Despite their potential, ZKPs face significant challenges in practical deployment, primarily due to the computational complexity of the proof generation process \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2408.05890)\]. Recent efforts have focused on accelerating the key primitives of ZKP protocols, particularly on GPU and ASIC platforms \[[11](http://arxiv.org/abs/2412.12481)\]. For instance, GZKP introduces a GPU-accelerated system that improves performance for polynomial computations and multi-scalar multiplications (MSMs) by optimizing memory access patterns and parallelization strategies \[[3](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/e149d93531d8c07cf43f6cae327a4795144d1304)\]. Similarly, cuZK presents a faster parallel MSM algorithm on GPUs, achieving nearly linear speedup over serial implementations \[[8](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/e4a4608e8e35379e63b9df5bda1b7599ee8ab9ac)\]. These works highlight the importance of hardware acceleration in reducing the time required for proof generation.

However, existing accelerators often face limitations. For example, some require a trusted setup for each application, which restricts their use in decentralized environments \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2408.05890)\]. Others generate larger proof sizes with higher verification costs, limiting their applicability in scenarios with numerous verifiers or strict time constraints \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2408.05890)\]. In contrast, the proposed accelerator, zkSpeed, addresses these challenges by supporting a one-time, universal setup and generating small proof sizes, making it suitable for publicly verifiable, consensus-based systems \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2408.05890)\]. This is a significant advancement over prior works, which either lacked universal setup support or resulted in increased verification overhead \[[3](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/e149d93531d8c07cf43f6cae327a4795144d1304)\].

The acceleration of ZKP protocols typically involves optimizing two major primitives: SumCheck and MSMs. PipeZK, for example, employs a pipelined architecture with two subsystems to handle these tasks efficiently, achieving 10x speedup on cryptographic benchmarks \[[2](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/6ee84674b7c426849c3da56cb278c08eb7a903cf)\]. Similarly, SZKP introduces a scalable ASIC framework that accelerates both NTT and MSM computations, achieving speedups of over 400×, 3×, and 12× over CPU, ASIC, and GPU implementations \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2408.05890)\]. These works demonstrate the importance of structured dataflows and efficient memory access in improving performance. The proposed zkSpeed architecture builds on these insights, using a full-chip design with 366.46 mm² and 2 TB/s bandwidth to accelerate the entire proof generation process \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2408.05890)\].

In addition to hardware accelerators, there are also theoretical advancements in ZKP protocols. For example, the paper on quantum zero-knowledge proofs establishes that several interactive proof systems are zero-knowledge against general quantum attacks

### <a id='category-hardware-accelerator-architectures'></a>Hardware Accelerator Architectures: 2 papers found.
Hardware accelerators for Zero-Knowledge Proof (ZKP) protocols have been a focal point in improving the efficiency of proof generation, which remains a critical bottleneck in practical ZKP applications \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2408.05890)\]. Prior works have explored GPU and ASIC implementations to accelerate key primitives such as Number Theoretic Transform (NTT) and Multi-scalar Multiplication (MSM), which are computationally intensive \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2408.05890)\]. For instance, SZKP introduces an ASIC-based accelerator that leverages structured dataflows for both NTT and MSM, achieving significant speedups over CPU, ASIC, and GPU implementations \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2408.05890)\]. Similarly, PipeZK proposes a pipelined accelerator with two subsystems to handle large polynomial computations and multi-scalar multiplications on elliptic curves, achieving up to 10x speedup on cryptographic benchmarks \[[2](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/6ee84674b7c426849c3da56cb278c08eb7a903cf)\]. These efforts highlight the need for optimized hardware solutions that can handle the irregular memory access patterns and data-dependent operations inherent in ZKP computations \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2408.05890)\]. The recent work on zkSpeed further advances this field by accelerating the entire HyperPlonk protocol, including SumCheck and MSM, with a full-chip architecture that delivers geometric mean speedups of 801× over CPU baselines \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2408.05890)\]. These accelerators aim to address the challenges of trusted setup and proof size, making ZKPs more viable for real-world applications such as blockchain and verifiable computing \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2408.05890)\].

### <a id='category-optimization-of-multi-scalar-multiplication-(msm)'></a>Optimization of Multi-Scalar Multiplication (MSM): 3 papers found.
Optimization of Multi-Scalar Multiplication (MSM) has been a focal point in improving the performance of zero-knowledge proof (ZKP) systems, which are critical for privacy-preserving applications such as blockchain and verifiable computing \[[3](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/e149d93531d8c07cf43f6cae327a4795144d1304)\]. Several recent works have addressed the computational bottlenecks in MSM, a key operation in ZKP proof generation. For instance, GZKP introduces a GPU-accelerated approach that enhances speed by optimizing memory access patterns and eliminating costly external shuffles, achieving up to 48.1× speedup on standard benchmarks \[[3](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/e149d93531d8c07cf43f6cae327a4795144d1304)\]. Similarly, cuZK presents a parallel MSM algorithm that nearly linearly scales over the Pippenger algorithm, along with parallelized matrix-vector multiplication and number-theoretic transform operations, resulting in up to 4.86× speedup on real-world applications like Filecoin \[[8](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/e4a4608e8e35379e63b9df5bda1b7599ee8ab9ac)\]. Additionally, an FPGA-based architecture for zk-SNARKs leverages inherent parallelism in MSM using optimized modular arithmetic, achieving 110x-150x speedup over software implementations for elliptic curves such as BLS12-381 and BN128 \[[11](http://arxiv.org/abs/2412.12481)\]. These advancements collectively highlight the importance of parallelism and hardware-specific optimizations in reducing the computational overhead of ZKP systems, thereby enhancing their practicality for widespread adoption.

### <a id='category-zkp-protocol-overview-and-importance'></a>ZKP Protocol Overview and Importance: 4 papers found.
Zero-knowledge proofs (ZKPs) have emerged as a critical tool in privacy-preserving systems, enabling one party to prove the validity of a statement without revealing additional information \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2408.05890)\]. This concept is foundational in various applications, including blockchain, verifiable machine learning, and secure electronic voting \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2408.05890)\]. The core idea of ZKPs lies in their ability to ensure that the only knowledge transferred is the correctness of the proposition, without exposing any extra details \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2408.05890)\]. For instance, proving that a graph is Hamiltonian through a Hamiltonian tour conveys more knowledge than simply stating the graph is Hamiltonian, which is why ZKPs aim to eliminate such extra information \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2408.05890)\]. Recent advancements have focused on improving the efficiency and practicality of ZKPs, particularly in terms of computational complexity and scalability. Streaming interactive proofs (SIPs) have been introduced to address the challenges of verifying computations on massive data streams, offering zero-knowledge SIPs for key protocols like sumcheck and polynomial evaluation \[[2](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/6ee84674b7c426849c3da56cb278c08eb7a903cf)\]. Additionally, the development of zero-knowledge protocols for NP-Complete problems, such as Subset Sum and 3SAT, has been achieved using homomorphic commitment schemes, ensuring security even against quantum adversaries \[[3](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/e149d93531d8c07cf43f6cae327a4795144d1304)\]. These developments highlight the growing importance of ZKPs in both theoretical and applied contexts, with ongoing efforts to enhance their performance and usability in real-world scenarios \[[3](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/e149d93531d8c07cf43f6cae327a4795144d1304)\].

### <a id='category-theoretical-advances-in-zkp-security'></a>Theoretical Advances in ZKP Security: 3 papers found.
The category "Theoretical Advances in ZKP Security" explores the foundational and theoretical progress in ensuring the security of zero-knowledge proof (ZKP) systems, particularly in the context of quantum computing and computational assumptions. Recent studies have demonstrated that several classical interactive proof systems, such as the Goldreich-Micali-Wigderson protocols for Graph Isomorphism and Graph 3-Coloring, can be proven zero-knowledge against general quantum attacks, assuming the existence of quantum computationally concealing commitment schemes \[[7](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/c3d1262160d759f424a83aae1d053b59d3352e7d)\]. Furthermore, it has been established that honest-verifier quantum zero-knowledge equals general quantum zero-knowledge, and public-coin quantum zero-knowledge also equals general quantum zero-knowledge, with these results holding unconditionally without relying on computational assumptions \[[10](http://arxiv.org/abs/0705.1129)\]. Additionally, it has been shown that any quantum zero-knowledge proof system can be transformed into a three-message public-coin quantum zero-knowledge proof system with polynomially small error in soundness, enhancing the practicality of such systems \[[10](http://arxiv.org/abs/0705.1129)\]. These theoretical advancements provide a solid foundation for the security of ZKP protocols in both classical and quantum computational environments, addressing critical gaps in prior research that were limited to restricted settings such as the honest-verifier and common reference string models \[[7](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/c3d1262160d759f424a83aae1d053b59d3352e7d)\]. The work also highlights the importance of physical principles, such as special relativity, in enforcing security in zero-knowledge protocols without relying on computational assumptions \[[12](http://arxiv.org/abs/2012.10452)\]. These contributions are essential for advancing the theoretical underpinnings of ZKP systems, which are crucial for privacy-preserving technologies like blockchain and verifiable computing.
