## Related Works

Zero-Knowledge Proofs (ZKPs) have emerged as a critical cryptographic primitive for privacy-preserving computation, enabling a prover to demonstrate the validity of a statement without revealing additional information \[[12](http://arxiv.org/abs/2012.10452)\]. Despite their potential, ZKPs face significant challenges in practical deployment, primarily due to the computational complexity of the proof generation process \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2408.05890)\]. Recent efforts have focused on accelerating the key primitives of ZKP protocols, particularly on GPU and ASIC platforms \[[11](http://arxiv.org/abs/2412.12481)\]. For instance, GZKP introduces a GPU-accelerated system that improves performance for polynomial computations and multi-scalar multiplications (MSMs) by optimizing memory access patterns and parallelization strategies \[[3](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/e149d93531d8c07cf43f6cae327a4795144d1304)\]. Similarly, cuZK presents a faster parallel MSM algorithm on GPUs, achieving nearly linear speedup over serial implementations \[[8](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/e4a4608e8e35379e63b9df5bda1b7599ee8ab9ac)\]. These works highlight the importance of hardware acceleration in reducing the time required for proof generation.

However, existing accelerators often face limitations. For example, some require a trusted setup for each application, which restricts their use in decentralized environments \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2408.05890)\]. Others generate larger proof sizes with higher verification costs, limiting their applicability in scenarios with numerous verifiers or strict time constraints \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2408.05890)\]. In contrast, the proposed accelerator, zkSpeed, addresses these challenges by supporting a one-time, universal setup and generating small proof sizes, making it suitable for publicly verifiable, consensus-based systems \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2408.05890)\]. This is a significant advancement over prior works, which either lacked universal setup support or resulted in increased verification overhead \[[3](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/e149d93531d8c07cf43f6cae327a4795144d1304)\].

The acceleration of ZKP protocols typically involves optimizing two major primitives: SumCheck and MSMs. PipeZK, for example, employs a pipelined architecture with two subsystems to handle these tasks efficiently, achieving 10x speedup on cryptographic benchmarks \[[2](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/6ee84674b7c426849c3da56cb278c08eb7a903cf)\]. Similarly, SZKP introduces a scalable ASIC framework that accelerates both NTT and MSM computations, achieving speedups of over 400×, 3×, and 12× over CPU, ASIC, and GPU implementations \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2408.05890)\]. These works demonstrate the importance of structured dataflows and efficient memory access in improving performance. The proposed zkSpeed architecture builds on these insights, using a full-chip design with 366.46 mm² and 2 TB/s bandwidth to accelerate the entire proof generation process \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2408.05890)\].

In addition to hardware accelerators, there are also theoretical advancements in ZKP protocols. For example, the paper on quantum zero-knowledge proofs establishes that several interactive proof systems are zero-knowledge against general quantum attacks
