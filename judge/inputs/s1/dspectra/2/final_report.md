## Related Works

Nonverbal communication plays a crucial role in human interaction, encompassing gestures, facial expressions, and body language that convey intent and emotion \[[25](http://arxiv.org/abs/1811.09362)\]. However, most large language models (LLMs) are limited in their ability to incorporate these nonverbal elements, which restricts their capacity to create fully immersive conversational experiences \[[25](http://arxiv.org/abs/1811.09362)\]. This limitation has motivated research into multimodal models that can understand and generate nonverbal cues alongside text \[[25](http://arxiv.org/abs/1811.09362)\].

Several studies have explored the integration of vision and language in large models. For instance, ViLBERT \[[9](http://arxiv.org/abs/1908.02265)\] extends the BERT architecture to a multi-modal two-stream model, processing both visual and textual inputs in separate streams that interact through co-attentional transformer layers. This approach enables the model to learn task-agnostic joint representations of image content and natural language. Similarly, Visual Genome \[[16](http://arxiv.org/abs/1602.07332)\] provides dense annotations of objects, attributes, and relationships within images, which are essential for understanding complex visual scenes. These datasets and models have laid the groundwork for more advanced multimodal systems.

Recent work has focused on training models to understand and generate nonverbal cues using large-scale datasets. For example, LLaVA \[[22](http://arxiv.org/abs/2304.08485)\] introduces a multimodal model that connects a vision encoder and a large language model (LLM) for general-purpose visual and language understanding. The model is trained using instruction-tuned data generated by GPT-4, enabling it to handle a wide range of tasks. Similarly, Video-LLaMA \[[13](http://arxiv.org/abs/2306.02858)\] enables video comprehension by tackling two challenges: capturing the temporal changes in visual scenes and integrating audio-visual signals. This model uses a Video Q-former to assemble a pre-trained image encoder into a video encoder and introduces a video-to-text generation task to learn video-language correspondence.

Another notable work is Kosmos-1 \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/2302.14045)\], a Multimodal Large Language Model (MLLM) that can perceive general modalities, learn in context, and follow instructions. It is trained on web-scale multimodal corpora, including arbitrarily interleaved text and images, image-caption pairs, and text data. Kosmos-1 demonstrates impressive performance on a wide range of tasks, including language understanding, generation, and vision tasks such as image recognition with descriptions. These models highlight the potential of integrating vision and language in large models to achieve more comprehensive understanding and generation capabilities.

### <a id='category-multimodal-models-for-vision-and-language-integration'></a>Multimodal Models for Vision and Language Integration: 18 papers found.
The category "Multimodal Models for Vision and Language Integration" encompasses a range of research efforts aimed at developing models that can effectively process and generate both visual and textual information. These models are designed to understand and interact with multimodal data, such as images, videos, and text, to perform tasks like visual question answering, image captioning, and conversational dialogue. For instance, Kosmos-1 demonstrates the ability to perceive general modalities, learn in context, and follow instructions without requiring fine-tuning \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2303.04671)\]. Similarly, Video-ChatGPT merges a video-adapted visual encoder with a large language model (LLM) to understand and generate detailed conversations about videos \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/2302.14045)\]. The integration of vision and language is further advanced by models like ViLBERT, which extends the BERT architecture to a multi-modal two-stream model, enabling joint representations of image content and natural language \[[6](http://arxiv.org/abs/1412.2306)\]. These models often rely on large-scale datasets, such as the Conceptual Captions dataset, to pretrain and transfer knowledge across tasks \[[6](http://arxiv.org/abs/1412.2306)\]. Additionally, models like mPLUG-Owl introduce modularized learning approaches that combine foundation LLMs with visual knowledge modules to enhance multi-modal generation and instruction-following capabilities \[[5](http://arxiv.org/abs/2303.03378)\]. The development of these models highlights the importance of cross-modal transfer and the ability to ground language in visual perception, as seen in PaLM-E, which incorporates real-world sensor modalities into language models for embodied tasks \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/2306.05424)\]. Overall, the research in this category emphasizes the convergence of vision and language to achieve more natural and effective human-computer interaction.
