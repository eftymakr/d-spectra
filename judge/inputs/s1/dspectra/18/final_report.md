## Related Works

Anomaly detection has become a critical component in modern industrial systems, particularly in the context of intelligent manufacturing, where maintaining quality control is essential \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/1607.00148)\]. Traditional methods for anomaly detection, such as threshold-based approaches, are often inadequate due to the dynamic and complex nature of real-world systems \[[12](http://arxiv.org/abs/1901.04997)\]. More recently, deep learning approaches have shown significant promise in improving the accuracy and robustness of anomaly detection, especially in industrial settings \[[6](http://arxiv.org/abs/2301.11514)\].

Several studies have explored the use of deep learning for anomaly detection in time-series data, which is common in industrial environments. For instance, LSTM-based Encoder-Decoder models have been proposed to detect anomalies in multivariate time series by learning to reconstruct 'normal' behavior and using reconstruction error as a detection metric \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/1607.00148)\]. These models have demonstrated robustness across different types of time-series data, including predictable, unpredictable, and periodic signals \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/1607.00148)\]. Similarly, the OmniAnomaly framework employs a stochastic recurrent neural network to capture normal patterns in multivariate time series and uses reconstruction probabilities to determine anomalies \[[7](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/440d248d148f7e36dad232e48f1c5c1cbc556d86)\]. This approach has achieved an overall F1-Score of 0.86 on real-world datasets, outperforming existing baseline methods \[[7](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/440d248d148f7e36dad232e48f1c5c1cbc556d86)\].

In the realm of image-based anomaly detection, deep learning models such as convolutional autoencoders and generative adversarial networks (GANs) have been widely explored. The MVTec AD dataset, which contains 5354 high-resolution color images with various types of defects, has been used to evaluate the performance of these models \[[13](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/3aa681914a7da79f7d7293f51a058eefe61c8bb7)\]. The MVTec 3D-AD dataset extends this work to 3D anomaly detection, providing a comprehensive benchmark for unsupervised anomaly detection and localization \[[11](http://arxiv.org/abs/2112.09045)\]. Additionally, the Real-IAD dataset addresses the limitations of existing datasets by offering a large-scale, multi-view dataset with a higher number of images and more challenging defect types \[[10](http://arxiv.org/abs/2403.12580)\].

For real-time anomaly detection in embedded systems, there is a growing need for lightweight and efficient models. The TinyAD framework proposes a memory-efficient approach for deploying CNNs on resource-constrained IoT devices by using depthwise separable convolutions and memory rescheduling techniques \[[9](http://arxiv.org/abs/2303.03611)\]. This framework reduces peak memory consumption by 2–5× with minimal computation overhead, making it suitable for deployment on microcontroller units (MCUs) \[[9](http://arxiv.org/abs/2303.03611)\].

[Snippet]: «Deep approaches to anomaly detection have recently shown promising results over shallow methods on large and complex datasets. Typically anomaly detection is treated as an unsupervised learning problem. In practice however, one may have---in addition to a large set of unlabeled samples---access to a small pool of labeled samples, e.g. a subset verified by some domain expert as being normal or anomalous. Semi-supervised approaches to anomaly detection aim to utilize such labeled samples, but most proposed methods are limited to merely including labeled normal samples. Only a few methods take advantage of labeled anomalies, with existing deep approaches being domain-specific. In this work we present Deep SAD, an end-to-end deep methodology for general semi-supervised anomaly detection. We further introduce an information-theoretic framework for deep anomaly detection based on the idea that the entropy of the latent distribution for normal data should be lower than the entropy of the anomalous distribution, which can serve as a theoretical interpretation for our method. In extensive experiments on MNIST, Fashion-MNIST, and CIFAR-10, along with other anomaly detection benchmark datasets, we demonstrate that our method is on par or outperforms shallow, hybrid, and deep competitors, yielding appreciable performance improvements even when provided with only little labeled data.»

Furthermore, by adjusting the dimension of convolution filters, these strategies apply to both univariate time series and multidomain time series features. Extensive experiments on real-world industrial datasets show that our framework can reduce peak memory consumption by 2–5× with negligible computation overhead.»
