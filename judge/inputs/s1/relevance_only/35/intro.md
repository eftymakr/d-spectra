## Related Works

Protein dynamics are essential for understanding biological functions and properties, yet traditional methods such as molecular dynamics (MD) simulations are computationally expensive and limited in their ability to sample rare conformational states \[[5](http://arxiv.org/abs/2411.08154)\]. Recent advances in generative modeling, particularly denoising diffusion models, have enabled efficient and accurate protein structure prediction and conformation sampling by learning distributions over crystallographic structures \[[19](http://arxiv.org/abs/2408.12419)\]. However, integrating physical supervision into these data-driven approaches remains challenging, as standard energy-based objectives often lead to intractable optimization \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2004.13167)\]. In this context, Energy-based Alignment (EBA) introduces a novel method that aligns generative models with feedback from physical models, efficiently calibrating them to balance conformational states based on energy differences \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2004.13167)\].

Several studies have explored the use of energy-based models for protein conformation prediction. For instance, an energy-based model (EBM) was proposed to operate at atomic scale, trained solely on crystallized protein data, achieving comparable performance to the Rosetta energy function \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2004.13167)\]. This model captures physicochemical properties relevant to protein energy, demonstrating the potential of EBMs in conformational sampling. Similarly, DeepConformer, a diffusion generative model, was developed to sample protein conformation distributions from a given amino acid sequence, capturing conformational flexibility and dynamics similar to MD simulations \[[7](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/43389c0e79b5db0b210bef998f867910db9d8d44)\]. These models highlight the importance of learning energy landscapes from experimental data to improve the physical plausibility of generated structures.

The integration of physical principles into generative models has also been explored through hybrid approaches. For example, aSAM, a latent diffusion model trained on MD data, generates heavy atom protein ensembles and models atoms in a latent space, facilitating accurate sampling of side chain and backbone torsion angle distributions \[[9](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/bcf4f0cd55f0888b80c2bf7d47969aa34b222a99)\]. Additionally, aSAMt, an extension of aSAM conditioned on temperature, captures temperature-dependent ensemble properties and demonstrates generalization beyond training temperatures \[[9](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/bcf4f0cd55f0888b80c2bf7d47969aa34b222a99)\]. These models show that incorporating physical constraints can enhance the accuracy and efficiency of conformational sampling.

Moreover, the use of transformers and diffusion models for protein conformational ensemble generation has gained traction. A modular strategy based on "backmapping" from a fixed protein backbone was introduced to maintain conformational diversity of the side chains and couple side-chain fluctuations using global information about the protein conformation \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/2311.11459)\]. This approach combines simple statistical models of side-chain conformations with transformer architectures to sample with atomistic accuracy.
