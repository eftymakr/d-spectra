## Related Works

Generative retrieval (GR) has gained significant attention in information retrieval (IR) as an alternative to traditional index-retrieve frameworks \[[1](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/5beaeee1e27007bd12967480bb9c379cebd15e6e)\]. Unlike conventional methods that rely on precomputed indices, GR models directly generate document identifiers (docids) based on queries, enabling end-to-end optimization \[[1](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/5beaeee1e27007bd12967480bb9c379cebd15e6e)\]. However, most existing GR approaches are evaluated on static document collections, with limited exploration of their performance in dynamic environments where documents are continuously updated \[[1](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/5beaeee1e27007bd12967480bb9c379cebd15e6e)\]. This section reviews related works that address the challenges of GR in dynamic settings, docid design, and performance evaluation.

Several studies have explored the adaptability of GR models to dynamic corpora. For instance, \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/2305.18952)\] evaluates GR on the StreamingQA benchmark and finds that it is more adaptable to evolving knowledge compared to dual encoders (DE). The study highlights GR's efficiency in terms of inference FLOPs, indexing time, and storage footprint. Similarly, \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/2308.14968)\] introduces the CLEVER model, which addresses continual learning in GR by enabling incremental indexing of new documents while preserving retrieval accuracy over both old and new data. The paper proposes Incremental Product Quantization for efficient docid encoding and a memory-augmented learning mechanism to prevent catastrophic forgetting.

Docid design is a critical factor in GR performance. \[[4](http://arxiv.org/abs/2406.01197)\] discusses various docid strategies, including numerical and string-based identifiers, and emphasizes the importance of learnable docids that capture semantic relationships between documents. \[[5](http://arxiv.org/abs/2409.18409)\] introduces the NOVO docid framework, which generates unique n-gram sets for each document, allowing for semantic alignment with language models' pre-trained knowledge. Experiments show that NOVO improves retrieval performance by optimizing both semantic and token representations. \[[6](http://arxiv.org/abs/2408.02152)\] further explores the trade-offs between efficiency and effectiveness, proposing a few-shot indexing approach that avoids the high training costs of traditional GR methods.

The performance of GR models in dynamic environments is also influenced by the quality of docid generation. \[[7](http://arxiv.org/abs/2403.12499)\] analyzes the limitations of numeric-based docids, which, while efficient, suffer from overfitting to the initial document set and poor generalization to unseen data. The study finds that text-based docids, which are more semantically rich, outperform numeric-based ones in dynamic settings. \[[8](http://arxiv.org/abs/2402.01176)\] introduces the D2-DocID framework, which designs docids that are both descriptive and discriminative, enabling better retrieval of similar documents. The approach uses contrastive learning to enhance the model's understanding of document semantics.
