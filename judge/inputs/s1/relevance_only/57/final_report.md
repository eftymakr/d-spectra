## Related Works

Generative retrieval (GR) has gained significant attention in information retrieval (IR) as an alternative to traditional index-retrieve frameworks \[[1](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/5beaeee1e27007bd12967480bb9c379cebd15e6e)\]. Unlike conventional methods that rely on precomputed indices, GR models directly generate document identifiers (docids) based on queries, enabling end-to-end optimization \[[1](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/5beaeee1e27007bd12967480bb9c379cebd15e6e)\]. However, most existing GR approaches are evaluated on static document collections, with limited exploration of their performance in dynamic environments where documents are continuously updated \[[1](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/5beaeee1e27007bd12967480bb9c379cebd15e6e)\]. This section reviews related works that address the challenges of GR in dynamic settings, docid design, and performance evaluation.

Several studies have explored the adaptability of GR models to dynamic corpora. For instance, \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/2305.18952)\] evaluates GR on the StreamingQA benchmark and finds that it is more adaptable to evolving knowledge compared to dual encoders (DE). The study highlights GR's efficiency in terms of inference FLOPs, indexing time, and storage footprint. Similarly, \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/2308.14968)\] introduces the CLEVER model, which addresses continual learning in GR by enabling incremental indexing of new documents while preserving retrieval accuracy over both old and new data. The paper proposes Incremental Product Quantization for efficient docid encoding and a memory-augmented learning mechanism to prevent catastrophic forgetting.

Docid design is a critical factor in GR performance. \[[4](http://arxiv.org/abs/2406.01197)\] discusses various docid strategies, including numerical and string-based identifiers, and emphasizes the importance of learnable docids that capture semantic relationships between documents. \[[5](http://arxiv.org/abs/2409.18409)\] introduces the NOVO docid framework, which generates unique n-gram sets for each document, allowing for semantic alignment with language models' pre-trained knowledge. Experiments show that NOVO improves retrieval performance by optimizing both semantic and token representations. \[[6](http://arxiv.org/abs/2408.02152)\] further explores the trade-offs between efficiency and effectiveness, proposing a few-shot indexing approach that avoids the high training costs of traditional GR methods.

The performance of GR models in dynamic environments is also influenced by the quality of docid generation. \[[7](http://arxiv.org/abs/2403.12499)\] analyzes the limitations of numeric-based docids, which, while efficient, suffer from overfitting to the initial document set and poor generalization to unseen data. The study finds that text-based docids, which are more semantically rich, outperform numeric-based ones in dynamic settings. \[[8](http://arxiv.org/abs/2402.01176)\] introduces the D2-DocID framework, which designs docids that are both descriptive and discriminative, enabling better retrieval of similar documents. The approach uses contrastive learning to enhance the model's understanding of document semantics.

### <a id='category-generative-retrieval-(gr)-frameworks-and-their-evolution'></a>Generative Retrieval (GR) Frameworks and Their Evolution: 5 papers found.
Generative retrieval (GR) has evolved as a promising paradigm in information retrieval, shifting from traditional index-retrieve-rank pipelines to models that directly generate document identifiers (docids) from queries \[[1](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/5beaeee1e27007bd12967480bb9c379cebd15e6e)\]. Recent advances focus on improving efficiency and adaptability, particularly in dynamic corpora \[[13](http://arxiv.org/abs/2311.09134)\]. For instance, Few-Shot GR eliminates the need for extensive training by prompting large language models (LLMs) to generate docids during indexing, creating a docid bank for retrieval \[[6](http://arxiv.org/abs/2408.02152)\]. This approach enhances adaptability but still faces challenges in handling complex document structures, such as those found in book search \[[12](http://arxiv.org/abs/2501.11034)\]. To address this, the GBS framework introduces data augmentation and outline-oriented encoding to preserve hierarchical information, achieving notable improvements in retrieval performance \[[12](http://arxiv.org/abs/2501.11034)\]. Additionally, RIPOR introduces a novel optimization framework that improves relevance estimation and document ID generation, demonstrating strong performance on large-scale benchmarks \[[13](http://arxiv.org/abs/2311.09134)\]. Meanwhile, research highlights the importance of docid design, with text-based docids showing superior generalization over dynamic corpora due to their semantic alignment with pre-trained models and fine-grained design \[[1](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/5beaeee1e27007bd12967480bb9c379cebd15e6e)\]. These developments underscore the ongoing evolution of GR, emphasizing the balance between efficiency and effectiveness in real-world applications.

### <a id='category-docid-design-strategies-and-their-implications'></a>DocID Design Strategies and Their Implications: 5 papers found.
Generative retrieval (GR) has gained significant attention as an emerging paradigm in information retrieval, offering end-to-end optimization by directly mapping queries to document identifiers (DocIDs) \[[1](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/5beaeee1e27007bd12967480bb9c379cebd15e6e)\]. Unlike traditional retrieval methods that rely on dense vector representations, GR leverages generative models to produce DocIDs, which can be either text-based or numeric-based \[[1](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/5beaeee1e27007bd12967480bb9c379cebd15e6e)\]. Text-based DocIDs, such as n-grams or synthetic queries, enable semantic alignment with pre-trained language models, enhancing retrieval performance, especially in dynamic corpora where document collections evolve continuously \[[1](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/5beaeee1e27007bd12967480bb9c379cebd15e6e)\]. In contrast, numeric-based DocIDs, often derived from fixed rules or clustering methods, exhibit high efficiency but suffer from poor generalization to unseen documents due to overfitting on the training set \[[1](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/5beaeee1e27007bd12967480bb9c379cebd15e6e)\]. This limitation is further exacerbated by their inability to capture fine-grained semantic information, which is crucial for distinguishing similar documents \[[1](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/5beaeee1e27007bd12967480bb9c379cebd15e6e)\]. Several studies have proposed alternative DocID design strategies to address these challenges, such as Neural Optimized VOcabularial (NOVO) docids, which are learnable and optimized for semantic understanding \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/2305.18952)\]. Similarly, the D2-DocID approach introduces descriptive and discriminative identifiers that enhance retrieval performance by distinguishing similar documents \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/2308.14968)\]. The GenRet method also addresses DocID design by learning semantic representations through a discrete auto-encoding framework, enabling end-to-end optimization \[[4](http://arxiv.org/abs/2406.01197)\]. Additionally, the Term-Set Generation (TSGen) framework uses permutation-invariant decoding to generate DocIDs as sets of terms, improving reliability and performance \[[5](http://arxiv.org/abs/2409.18409)\]. These approaches collectively highlight the importance of DocID design in GR, emphasizing the trade-offs between efficiency and effectiveness, as well as the need for learnable and semantically rich identifiers to support dynamic and evolving document collections.
