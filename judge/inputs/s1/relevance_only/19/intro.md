## Related Works

Speech-driven 3D facial animation has been a focal point in recent research, with numerous approaches aiming to generate realistic and synchronized facial expressions from audio input. Early methods primarily relied on hand-crafted features and rule-based systems, which were limited in their ability to capture the complex dynamics of facial motion \[[1](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/435b15745fb279db4ff8daf9d5facb9b21d58f3b)\]. With the advent of deep learning, significant progress has been made in modeling the relationship between audio and facial expressions. For instance, Voice2Face \[[1](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/435b15745fb279db4ff8daf9d5facb9b21d58f3b)\] introduced a deep learning model that generates face and tongue animations directly from recorded speech using conditional Variational Autoencoders (cVAEs). This approach demonstrated the potential of deep learning in capturing detailed facial movements without relying on speaker-dependent characteristics.

The challenge of lip synchronization remains a critical issue in audio-driven facial animation. Speech2Lip \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/2309.04814)\] proposed a decomposition-synthesis-composition framework that disentangles speech-sensitive and speech-insensitive motion/appearance to facilitate effective learning from limited training data. This method enables the generation of natural-looking videos by focusing on learning speech-sensitive motion and appearance while modeling the major speech-insensitive motion. Additionally, the introduction of a Blend-Net and a contrastive sync loss further enhances the overall synthesis performance.

Another notable approach, PMMTalk \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/2312.02781)\], integrates complementary pseudo multi-modal features to improve the accuracy of facial animation. This framework utilizes visual and textual cues extracted from speech, aligning audio-image-text features at both temporal and semantic levels. The PMMTalk decoder then predicts lip-syncing facial blendshape coefficients, resulting in more accurate and coherent facial animations. This method is particularly useful in scenarios where visual and textual information can provide additional context to the audio input.

Learning audio-driven viseme dynamics \[[4](http://arxiv.org/abs/2301.06059)\] has also been explored, with a focus on generating realistic lip-synchronized 3D facial animations. This approach utilizes phoneme priors to extract viseme parameters from speech videos, enabling more controllable and animator-friendly viseme curves. The use of deep audio feature models pretrained on multiple languages allows for the generalization of the audio-to-curves mapping to unseen voices and speech conditions.

Content and style aware audio-driven facial animation \[[5](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/e80391ba7a62bd8db2f7e7c529182fa916b7d918)\] has further advanced the field by enabling precise control over both the content and style of the final performance.
