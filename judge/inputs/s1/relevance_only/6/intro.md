## Related Works

Narrative summarization, which involves condensing long-form texts such as books, movies, and TV scripts, has gained significant attention due to its complexity and the need to preserve intricate plotlines, character interactions, and thematic coherence \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2105.08209)\]. Early approaches primarily relied on extractive methods, which often failed to capture the nuanced structure of narratives \[[17](http://arxiv.org/abs/1602.06023)\]. Recent advances have shifted toward abstractive summarization, leveraging large language models (LLMs) to generate more coherent and contextually relevant summaries \[[17](http://arxiv.org/abs/1602.06023)\]. However, LLMs still struggle with long-form content due to issues such as hallucinations, lack of structured planning, and poor handling of causal and temporal dependencies \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2105.08209)\].

Several studies have explored multi-agent frameworks to address these challenges. For instance, Agents' Room proposes a collaborative approach where specialized agents handle different subtasks in the narrative generation process, leading to higher-quality outputs \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/2410.02603)\]. Similarly, MetaGPT introduces a structured framework that incorporates human-like workflows and modular agents to improve the accuracy and coherence of generated narratives \[[20](http://arxiv.org/abs/2308.00352)\]. These approaches emphasize the importance of decomposition and collaboration in handling complex narrative tasks.

The issue of hallucinations in small language models (SLMs) has also been studied, with some works proposing plan-guided summarization to improve faithfulness \[[4](http://arxiv.org/abs/2504.09071)\]. However, these methods often fail to significantly improve summary quality or faithfulness, as plans themselves can contain hallucinations \[[4](http://arxiv.org/abs/2504.09071)\]. This highlights the need for more robust and structured approaches to narrative summarization.

To address the challenges of long-form summarization, SEAL introduces a Transformer-based model that dynamically selects input snippets for each output segment, improving content selection and interpretability \[[12](http://arxiv.org/abs/2006.10213)\]. This method is particularly useful for long documents where traditional models struggle with input length constraints \[[12](http://arxiv.org/abs/2006.10213)\]. Additionally, Bottom-Up Abstractive Summarization explores content selectors to improve content selection, leading to more compressed and fluent summaries \[[13](http://arxiv.org/abs/1808.10792)\].

The need for structured and controllable summarization has also been addressed by frameworks such as CTRLsum, which allows users to control generated summaries through keywords, enhancing the relevance and quality of outputs \[[7](http://arxiv.org/abs/2012.04281)\]. This is particularly useful in domains where specific aspects of the narrative need to be emphasized, such as scientific papers or patent filings \[[7](http://arxiv.org/abs/2012.04281)\].

In summary, while significant progress has been made in narrative summarization, challenges remain in terms of coherence, faithfulness, and structured planning.
