## Related Works

Long-tailed recognition remains a challenging problem in computer vision due to the significant imbalance in class distribution, where a few dominant (head) classes occupy most of the data while the majority of minority (tail) classes are severely underrepresented \[[18](http://arxiv.org/abs/1910.09217)\]. This imbalance leads to biased feature spaces, poor generalization, and suboptimal performance on rare classes \[[18](http://arxiv.org/abs/1910.09217)\]. Various approaches have been proposed to address this issue, primarily focusing on class-balancing strategies, data augmentation, and contrastive learning \[[22](http://arxiv.org/abs/2110.04596)\].

Contrastive learning has shown promising results in representation learning, but its performance on long-tailed data is often suboptimal. Supervised Contrastive Learning (SCL) has been widely used to optimize feature distributions by pulling positive samples together and pushing negative samples apart \[[11](http://arxiv.org/abs/2211.11938)\]. However, SCL tends to bias towards high-frequency classes, exacerbating the imbalance \[[10](http://arxiv.org/abs/2107.12028)\]. To mitigate this, several works have introduced class-balancing mechanisms. For instance, Rebalanced Contrastive Learning (RCL) introduces a class frequency-based SoftMax loss balancing to address class imbalance \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2312.01753)\]. Similarly, Parametric Contrastive Learning (PaCo) introduces class-wise learnable centers to rebalance the optimization process \[[10](http://arxiv.org/abs/2107.12028)\]. These methods aim to improve the representation of tail classes by adjusting the loss function or introducing additional constraints.

Another line of research focuses on decoupling representation learning from classifier training. Decoupled Contrastive Learning (DCL) separates the learning process into representation and classifier learning, allowing for more flexible and effective optimization \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/2403.06151)\]. This approach has shown that it is possible to achieve strong long-tailed recognition ability by adjusting only the classifier, even when representations are learned with simple sampling strategies \[[18](http://arxiv.org/abs/1910.09217)\]. Similarly, the Equalization Loss \[[19](http://arxiv.org/abs/2210.05566)\] introduces a gradient-driven training mechanism to balance the positive and negative gradients, improving the performance of tail classes.

In addition, some works have explored the use of self-distillation and knowledge transfer to improve the performance of tail classes. For example, Decoupled Contrastive Learning for Long-Tailed Recognition introduces a patch-based self-distillation to transfer knowledge from head to tail classes \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/2403.06151)\]. Similarly, the Balanced Contrastive Learning (BCL) framework introduces class-averaging and class-complement to balance the gradient contributions of negative classes and ensure all classes appear in every mini-batch \[[7](http://arxiv.org/abs/2207.09052)\]. These methods aim to improve the generalization ability of models by addressing the imbalanced data distribution during training.

The category "Class-Balancing Mechanisms in Contrastive Learning" encompasses a range of approaches aimed at improving long-tailed recognition by addressing class imbalance in contrastive learning frameworks. Existing methods such as supervised contrastive learning (SCL) \[[10](http://arxiv.org/abs/2107.12028)\] and balanced contrastive learning (BCL) \[[7](http://arxiv.org/abs/2207.09052)\] have been proposed to mitigate the bias toward head classes, which often leads to poor performance on tail classes. These methods introduce class-balancing strategies, such as adjusting loss functions or modifying feature spaces, to ensure more equitable representation across classes. For instance, Rebalanced Contrastive Learning (RCL) \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2312.01753)\] introduces feature space balancing, intra-class compactness, and regularization to enhance tail class performance. Similarly, Subclass-Balancing Contrastive Learning (SBCL) \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/2306.15925)\] clusters head classes into subclasses to maintain semantic substructures while enforcing balance. Meanwhile, Long-tail Augmented Graph Contrastive Learning (LAGCL) \[[4](http://arxiv.org/abs/2309.11177)\] tackles node degree disparity in recommendation systems by introducing long-tail augmentation and auto-drop modules. Theoretical analyses \[[7](http://arxiv.org/abs/2207.09052)\] further reveal that SCL struggles to form a regular simplex on long-tailed data, motivating the development of BCL, which improves gradient balance and representation learning. Parametric Contrastive Learning (PaCo) \[[10](http://arxiv.org/abs/2107.12028)\] introduces class-wise learnable centers to rebalance gradients, while probabilistic frameworks like Continuous Contrastive Learning (CCL) \[[15](http://arxiv.org/abs/2410.06109)\] unify class-balanced loss through kernel density estimation. These approaches collectively highlight the importance of addressing class imbalance in contrastive learning to improve generalization and performance on long-tailed datasets.

### <a id='category-class-imbalance-in-recognition-tasks'></a>Class Imbalance in Recognition Tasks: 15 papers found.
The long-tailed recognition problem, where class distributions are highly imbalanced, has been extensively studied, with a focus on improving model generalization for both head and tail classes \[[13](http://arxiv.org/abs/2111.13998)\]. Supervised Contrastive Learning (SCL) has gained popularity for its ability to pull positive samples and push negative samples apart, but it struggles with long-tailed data due to imbalanced intra-category distances and overlooked inter-class relationships \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2312.01753)\]. To address these issues, researchers have proposed various methods, such as decoupling positive samples and optimizing them toward different objectives \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2312.01753)\], introducing patch-based self-distillation to transfer knowledge from head to tail classes \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2312.01753)\], and integrating adversarial-inspired sample policies to enhance inter-class relationship modeling \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/2403.06151)\]. Additionally, some works have focused on reinterpreting long-tailed recognition as a mutual information maximization between latent features and ground-truth labels, leading to more effective integration with logit adjustment techniques \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/2306.15925)\]. Others have explored the use of Mixup-based data augmentation and new loss functions like MixCo to better align with augmented data and improve optimization efficiency \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/2403.06151)\]. Furthermore, methods such as interpolative centroid contrastive learning (ICCL) have been introduced to improve long-tailed representation learning by interpolating images from class-agnostic and class-aware samplers \[[4](http://arxiv.org/abs/2309.11177)\]. These approaches collectively highlight the challenges and potential solutions for long-tailed recognition, emphasizing the need for more robust and generalized learning strategies \[[13](http://arxiv.org/abs/2111.13998)\].
