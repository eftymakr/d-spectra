## Related Works

Recent advancements in neural retrieval methods, particularly those leveraging transformer-based pre-trained language models, have significantly improved multilingual and cross-lingual retrieval capabilities \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2011.01154)\]. However, the effectiveness of these models for low-resource, morphologically rich languages such as Amharic remains underexplored due to data scarcity and suboptimal tokenization \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2011.01154)\]. This section reviews related works that address these challenges and provide foundational insights for the proposed work.

Several studies have focused on developing semantic models for Amharic, a morphologically complex language. For instance, \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2011.01154)\] introduced various semantic models for Amharic, including Word2Vec embeddings, distributional thesaurus (DT), BERT-like contextual embeddings, and DT embeddings obtained via network embedding algorithms. They found that newly-trained models outperformed pre-trained multilingual models, and models based on contextual embeddings from FLAIR and RoBERTa performed better than word2Vec models for named entity recognition (NER) and part-of-speech (POS) tagging tasks \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2011.01154)\]. This highlights the importance of language-specific adaptation in improving performance for low-resource languages.

Dense retrieval models have also been explored for low-resource languages. \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/2503.18570)\] investigated dense retrieval models for Amharic, constructing training datasets tailored to dense retrieval and evaluating model performance by comparing dense and sparse retrieval approaches. They emphasized the challenges and efforts involved in advancing retrieval systems for low-resource languages \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/2503.18570)\]. Similarly, \[[9](http://arxiv.org/abs/2408.11942)\] analyzed the capabilities of the multi-lingual Dense Passage Retriever (mDPR) for extremely low-resource languages, finding that while mDPR achieves success on multilingual open QA benchmarks, its performance for Amharic and Khmer remains modest \[[9](http://arxiv.org/abs/2408.11942)\].

The use of RoBERTa for Amharic NER has also been explored. \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/2207.00785)\] annotated a large Amharic named entity recognition dataset and built multiple systems based on recent deep learning approaches, including transfer learning with RoBERTa. Their best performing model achieved an F1-score of 93%, setting a new state-of-the-art result for Amharic NER \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/2207.00785)\]. This demonstrates the potential of RoBERTa for low-resource language tasks when combined with sufficient training data and appropriate fine-tuning strategies.
