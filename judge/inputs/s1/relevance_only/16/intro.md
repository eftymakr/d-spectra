## Related Works

Open-vocabulary object detection (OVD) has emerged as a critical research direction, enabling detectors to identify objects from novel categories described by arbitrary text inputs \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2306.05493)\]. Early approaches relied on zero-shot learning, where models were trained on a fixed set of categories and then extended to new ones without additional annotations \[[4](http://arxiv.org/abs/2011.10678)\]. However, these methods often suffered from poor generalization due to the lack of fine-grained alignment between visual features and textual descriptions \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/2211.14843)\]. More recent work has focused on leveraging pre-trained vision-language models (VLMs) like CLIP and ALIGN to bridge the visual and linguistic modalities \[[5](http://arxiv.org/abs/2309.00227)\]. These models are trained on large-scale image-text pairs, enabling zero-shot recognition of novel objects \[[6](http://arxiv.org/abs/2111.09452)\].

Several methods have explored the use of language descriptions to guide object detection. For instance, \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2306.05493)\] proposed a multi-modal classifier that combines language-based and vision-based classifiers, demonstrating that such fusion improves performance over either modality alone. Similarly, \[[7](http://arxiv.org/abs/2205.06230)\] introduced a framework that directly learns from image-text pairs, formulating object-language alignment as a set matching problem between image region features and word embeddings. This approach achieved superior performance on novel categories, with 32.0% mAP on COCO and 21.7% mask mAP on LVIS \[[7](http://arxiv.org/abs/2205.06230)\]. Another work, \[[8](http://arxiv.org/abs/2503.14862)\], focused on open-vocabulary attribute detection, introducing a benchmark that evaluates the ability of vision-language models to detect object-level attributes \[[8](http://arxiv.org/abs/2503.14862)\].

The challenge of aligning visual and textual representations has also been addressed through region-level prompting. For example, \[[9](http://arxiv.org/abs/2411.16833)\] proposed a method that uses CLIP to generate pseudo bounding-box labels from image-caption pairs, enabling the training of object detectors without requiring additional annotations \[[9](http://arxiv.org/abs/2411.16833)\]. This approach significantly improved performance on COCO novel categories, achieving an 8% AP gain over the state-of-the-art \[[9](http://arxiv.org/abs/2411.16833)\]. Similarly, \[[10](http://arxiv.org/abs/2203.16513)\] introduced a self-training strategy that treats background proposals as novel classes during training, enhancing recall and accuracy for unseen categories \[[10](http://arxiv.org/abs/2203.16513)\].

Recent advances have also focused on improving localization accuracy for novel objects. \[[11](http://arxiv.org/abs/2203.11876)\] proposed a DETR-based detector (OV-DETR) that uses conditional matching to align input queries (text or image) with corresponding objects, achieving non-trivial improvements over existing methods \[[11](http://arxiv.org/abs/2203.11876)\].
