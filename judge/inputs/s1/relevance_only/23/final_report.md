## Related Works

Radar-camera calibration is a critical component in autonomous driving systems, enabling accurate sensor fusion for robust perception. Several recent works have addressed this challenge, particularly focusing on targetless and automatic calibration methods. Early approaches relied on target-based calibration, which required specialized environments and well-designed experimental setups \[[6](http://arxiv.org/abs/2309.00787)\]. However, these methods are not practical for real-world deployment due to their complexity and dependency on controlled conditions \[[6](http://arxiv.org/abs/2309.00787)\]. 

To overcome these limitations, several targetless calibration techniques have been proposed. For instance, \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2307.15264)\] introduced a flexible and accurate method for extrinsic calibration of 3D radar and camera, using a single corner reflector and ROS for data collection. This method leverages point correspondences and solves the perspective-n-point (PnP) problem to estimate the extrinsic calibration matrix. The method also incorporates RANSAC and Levenberg-Marquardt (LM) optimization for robustness and accuracy. Similarly, \[[16](http://arxiv.org/abs/1904.08743)\] proposed a data-driven method for automatic rotational radar-camera calibration without dedicated calibration targets, using a coarse and fine convolutional neural network. This approach demonstrated robust sensor registration across different sensor alignments and perspectives.

Another notable work, \[[7](http://arxiv.org/abs/2211.01871)\], introduced an online targetless calibration method based on common features extracted from radar and camera data. This method implicitly utilizes common features to match identical objects from both data sources and estimates the extrinsic transformation matrix through a feature-based approach. The method also applies RANSAC and LM optimization to enhance accuracy and robustness. This aligns with our approach of using feature-based matching to address the sparsity and uncertainty in radar data.

In addition to calibration, several works have focused on radar-camera fusion for 3D object detection. For example, \[[13](http://arxiv.org/abs/2509.05785)\] proposed CRAB, a novel camera-radar fusion framework that reduces depth ambiguity in backward projection-based view transformation. This method leverages radar data to mitigate depth ambiguity and improves depth distinction among queries along the same ray. Similarly, \[[14](http://arxiv.org/abs/2210.09267)\] introduced CramNet, a camera-radar matching network that uses a ray-constrained cross-attention mechanism to resolve geometric correspondence ambiguities between camera and radar features. This approach supports training with sensor modality dropout, enhancing robustness in case of sensor failure.

Extrinsic calibration of radar and camera systems remains a critical challenge in autonomous driving and advanced driver assistance systems, with significant implications for sensor fusion accuracy \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2307.15264)\]. Traditional methods often rely on complex setups, such as specially designed calibration environments or radar retroreflectors, which limit their practicality \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2307.15264)\]\[[4](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/31cddde4f300fe043e0de0d0373affedd0539685)\]. Recent research has focused on developing more flexible and efficient approaches. For instance, a method utilizing a single corner reflector (CR) and ROS for data collection has been proposed, enabling accurate extrinsic calibration without requiring extensive setup \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2307.15264)\]. Similarly, a continuous-time 3D radar-to-camera calibration algorithm has been introduced, which leverages radar velocity measurements and does not require specialized retroreflectors, thereby improving adaptability to real-world conditions \[[11](http://arxiv.org/abs/2103.07505)\]. Another approach integrates trajectory events from sensor measurements to address modality disparity and achieve stable target representation at a lower computational cost, demonstrating effectiveness in real-world scenarios \[[8](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/75f57abbdcf8740c40575bb5bcab25d98a1925d3)\]. Additionally, a novel calibration method for radar–LiDAR–camera systems has been introduced, addressing challenges such as sparse radar data by employing a two-step optimization procedure and geometric constraints \[[12](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/0955326c67a947b5fac5fbae7238d04fc9adc251)\]. These advancements highlight ongoing efforts to enhance the robustness and efficiency of extrinsic calibration, particularly in dynamic and challenging environments.

### <a id='category-sensor-fusion-frameworks'></a>Sensor Fusion Frameworks: 9 papers found.
Recent advancements in radar-camera fusion frameworks have focused on improving 3D object detection and geometric calibration in autonomous driving systems. Several studies have explored the integration of radar and camera data to enhance perception robustness, particularly in adverse weather conditions where cameras struggle with depth estimation \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/2009.08428)\]. For instance, the RCFusion framework leverages 4-D radar data to generate dense point clouds and employs a unified bird’s-eye view (BEV) space for multimodal feature fusion, achieving robust 3D object detection \[[4](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/31cddde4f300fe043e0de0d0373affedd0539685)\]. Similarly, the RAFDet framework integrates radar RF images and point clouds to refine depth estimation and improve detection accuracy, demonstrating significant improvements in depth prediction and overall performance \[[15](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/f2eebc8211e2dd9aa7fe9e0b1384d286048863cc)\]. Another approach, CramNet, introduces a ray-constrained cross-attention mechanism to resolve geometric ambiguities between camera and radar features, enabling efficient 3D object detection even with sensor failures \[[14](http://arxiv.org/abs/2210.09267)\]. Additionally, the RC-BEVFusion framework utilizes BEV features for radar-camera fusion, achieving up to 28% improvement in detection scores on the nuScenes dataset \[[20](http://arxiv.org/abs/2305.15883)\]. These methods highlight the importance of addressing the inherent challenges of radar and camera data integration, such as sparse radar point clouds and the need for robust calibration techniques \[[18](http://arxiv.org/abs/2411.13311)\]. The proposed RC-AutoCalib method builds upon these advancements by introducing an online automatic geometric calibration approach that mitigates data sparsity and height uncertainty through dual-perspective representations and selective fusion mechanisms \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2307.15264)\].
