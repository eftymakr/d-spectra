## Related Works

Generative retrieval has emerged as a promising alternative to traditional retrieval methods, which rely on static index structures and separate encoding and decoding stages \[[10](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/5beaeee1e27007bd12967480bb9c379cebd15e6e)\]. Instead, generative retrieval integrates the entire retrieval process into a single, differentiable model, enabling the direct generation of document identifiers (docids) from queries \[[10](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/5beaeee1e27007bd12967480bb9c379cebd15e6e)\]. This paradigm has attracted significant attention due to its potential for improved efficiency and seamless integration with large language models (LLMs) \[[10](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/5beaeee1e27007bd12967480bb9c379cebd15e6e)\]. However, the generalization capabilities of generative retrieval on out-of-distribution corpora remain a critical challenge \[[10](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/5beaeee1e27007bd12967480bb9c379cebd15e6e)\].

Several studies have explored the limitations of generative retrieval, particularly in terms of constrained decoding and beam search strategies. For instance, \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2305.11841)\] highlights that existing generative retrieval techniques have primarily been evaluated on small-scale corpora, typically up to 100k documents, and that scaling to larger datasets remains a significant challenge. This underscores the need for more robust methods that can generalize effectively across diverse and large-scale document collections.

In the context of book search, \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/2501.11034)\] introduces the Generative Retrieval for Book Search (GBS) framework, which addresses the unique challenges of handling complex, multi-faceted book information. GBS incorporates data augmentation and outline-oriented book encoding to improve the model's ability to retain hierarchical information and generate accurate docids. This work demonstrates the potential of generative retrieval in specialized domains where document structure plays a crucial role.

The issue of multi-graded relevance has also been addressed in generative retrieval. \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/2409.18409)\] proposes the GR$^2$ framework, which extends generative retrieval to accommodate multiple relevance levels by incorporating multi-graded constrained contrastive training. This approach ensures that the generated docids are both relevant and distinct, improving the model's ability to handle nuanced relevance judgments.

In addition to relevance, the interpretability and accuracy of retrieved results have been a focus of recent research. \[[4](http://arxiv.org/abs/2412.11919)\] introduces RetroLLM, a unified framework that integrates retrieval and generation, enabling LLMs to generate fine-grained evidence directly from the corpus. RetroLLM mitigates hallucinations by incorporating hierarchical FM-Index constraints and forward-looking constrained decoding strategies, enhancing the reliability of generated results.
