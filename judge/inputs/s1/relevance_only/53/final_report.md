## Related Works

Sequential recommendation has emerged as a critical component of modern recommender systems, aiming to predict the next item a user is likely to interact with based on their historical behavior \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2502.08132)\]. Early methods, such as Markov Chains (MCs), assumed that a user's next action depends only on their last few interactions, while Recurrent Neural Networks (RNNs) were introduced to capture longer-term dependencies \[[13](http://arxiv.org/abs/1808.09781)\]. However, RNNs often suffer from vanishing gradients and computational inefficiencies, especially with long sequences \[[16](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/0b42168f22713c48d8b0ce3bc8f680d347184d88)\]. More recently, self-attention mechanisms have gained popularity due to their ability to capture both local and global dependencies in user-item interaction sequences \[[13](http://arxiv.org/abs/1808.09781)\]. 

Despite these advances, existing models often struggle with modeling sequence length variations and capturing diverse interaction patterns \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2502.08132)\]. For instance, Transformer-based models, while effective in modeling sequential dependencies, typically require quadratic computational complexity due to the attention mechanism, which limits their scalability for long sequences \[[17](http://arxiv.org/abs/2409.05022)\]. Additionally, many models fail to account for the temporal dynamics of user behavior, such as the impact of irregular time intervals between interactions \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2502.08132)\]. 

To address these limitations, several works have explored alternative architectures. For example, the Mamba model, based on state space models (SSMs), offers a more efficient approach by leveraging hardware-aware designs to process long sequences with linear complexity \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/2403.03900)\]. Similarly, the S3-Rec model introduces self-supervised learning with mutual information maximization to enhance the representation of sequential data, particularly in scenarios with limited training data \[[8](http://arxiv.org/abs/2008.07873)\]. 

Another line of research focuses on modeling user preferences more effectively. The KATRec model integrates knowledge graph information to enhance item representation by capturing both short-term and long-term user interests \[[20](http://arxiv.org/abs/2012.03323)\]. Similarly, the ARERec model introduces a local interaction-aware mechanism to better capture the specific characteristics of user and item interactions within a sequence \[[9](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/656670a2f0b632a41eae8b20e28d1f80ec12b697)\]. 

Temporal awareness has also been a key focus in recent studies. The TiSASRec model explicitly models time intervals between interactions, improving prediction accuracy by incorporating both absolute and relative temporal information \[[12](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/9c7a455b1e48d01a99e58e884a6c1acb75074ad0)\]. The TLSRec model further addresses the challenge of modeling user preference changes over time by integrating a hierarchical self-attention network and a time lag-sensitive fusion mechanism \[[6](http://arxiv.org/abs/2208.04760)\]. 

In addition to temporal modeling, some works have explored the integration of social and contextual information. The STRec model leverages social influence and cross-domain interactions to provide a more holistic understanding of user preferences \[[4](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/dd27b3098dd3974068bed4e6291612c1c5bc3550)\]. ### <a id='category-state-space-models-for-sequences'></a>State-Space Models for Sequences: 4 papers found.
State-space models (SSMs) have emerged as a promising approach for sequential recommendation, addressing limitations of traditional recurrent and attention-based models \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2502.08132)\]. These models excel at capturing the dynamic evolution of user interests over time, particularly in scenarios with irregular time intervals \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2502.08132)\]. However, existing SSM-based methods often overlook the impact of uneven time intervals in user interactions, hindering their ability to model complex user-item transitions \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2502.08132)\]. To overcome this, SS4Rec was introduced, integrating a time-aware SSM to handle irregular intervals and a relation-aware SSM to model contextual dependencies, enabling both temporal and sequential understanding of user behavior \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2502.08132)\]. Similarly, the Mamba architecture, built on SSMs with hardware-aware designs, has shown great potential for efficient sequence modeling, inspiring frameworks like SSD4Rec, which adapts Mamba for recommendation tasks by incorporating structured state space duality and sequence registers \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/2409.01192)\]. Additionally, the inefficiency of attention mechanisms in handling long sequences has led to the development of Mamba4Rec, which leverages selective SSMs to achieve both effectiveness and efficiency in sequential recommendation \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/2403.03900)\]. Building on these advancements, EchoMamba4Rec further enhances performance by integrating bi-directional processing, frequency-domain filtering, and gate linear units to capture complex patterns and dependencies in user interactions \[[11](http://arxiv.org/abs/2406.02638)\]. These works collectively highlight the growing interest in SSMs for sequential recommendation, emphasizing their ability to model long-range dependencies, handle variable-length sequences, and improve computational efficiency \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2502.08132)\]\[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/2409.01192)\]\[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/2403.03900)\]\[[11](http://arxiv.org/abs/2406.02638)\].

### <a id='category-temporal-dynamics-in-user-behavior'></a>Temporal Dynamics in User Behavior: 3 papers found.
In the realm of temporal dynamics in user behavior, recent studies have focused on improving sequential recommendation systems by incorporating time-aware mechanisms to better capture user preferences and interaction patterns \[[12](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/9c7a455b1e48d01a99e58e884a6c1acb75074ad0)\]. Traditional models such as Markov Chains (MCs) and Recurrent Neural Networks (RNNs) have been widely used for sequential recommendation, but they often overlook the time intervals between interactions, treating sequences purely as ordered lists without considering the actual timestamps \[[12](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/9c7a455b1e48d01a99e58e884a6c1acb75074ad0)\]. To address this limitation, researchers have proposed models like TiSASRec, which explicitly models both the absolute positions of items and the time intervals between interactions, thereby enhancing the accuracy of next-item predictions \[[12](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/9c7a455b1e48d01a99e58e884a6c1acb75074ad0)\]. Similarly, the TLSTSRec model introduces a time-aware Transformer architecture that leverages temporal information through a trainable timestamp matrix, capturing both time duration and time spectrum perspectives \[[5](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/417f89b6992377e3e5d706672bfa4da837f011df)\]. This approach enables more accurate modeling of user behavior by accounting for the evolving nature of preferences over time. Furthermore, the TemProxRec model extends this idea by incorporating contrastive learning and self-attention mechanisms to consider both vertical and horizontal temporal proximities, ensuring that interactions within and across users are effectively captured \[[18](http://arxiv.org/abs/2402.09784)\]. These advancements highlight the growing emphasis on temporal modeling in sequential recommendation, aiming to improve personalization and adaptability to diverse user behaviors.

### <a id='category-sequential-recommendation-models'></a>Sequential Recommendation Models: 12 papers found.
Recent advances in sequential recommendation models have focused on addressing challenges such as capturing diverse user preferences, handling variable-length sequences, and modeling both short-term and long-term behaviors \[[13](http://arxiv.org/abs/1808.09781)\]. Many approaches rely on self-attention mechanisms to learn sequential dependencies, but they often neglect the impact of time intervals between interactions and fail to account for item frequency, which can significantly affect prediction accuracy \[[10](http://arxiv.org/abs/2406.18470)\]. To mitigate these issues, researchers have proposed methods like TLSRec, which integrates hierarchical modeling of user preferences and time-lag-sensitive fusion of long-term and short-term preferences \[[6](http://arxiv.org/abs/2208.04760)\], and LimaRec, which employs a lifelong incremental multi-interest self-attention mechanism to adaptively capture diverse user interests \[[7](http://arxiv.org/abs/2105.14060)\]. Furthermore, models like SASRec combine the strengths of Markov Chains and RNNs by using self-attention to identify relevant items from a user's history while maintaining computational efficiency \[[13](http://arxiv.org/abs/1808.09781)\]. These approaches highlight the ongoing efforts to improve the robustness and generalization of sequential recommendation models by incorporating temporal dynamics, user preferences, and interaction patterns more effectively.

However, traditional attention mechanisms, such as dot-product attention, suffer from quadratic computational complexity due to the need to compute an attention matrix, which becomes prohibitive for long sequences \[[19](http://arxiv.org/abs/2411.01537)\]. To address this challenge, researchers have proposed efficient alternatives that maintain the effectiveness of attention while reducing computational overhead. For instance, the LinRec model introduces an L2-Normalized Linear Attention mechanism, which achieves linear complexity by leveraging the equivalence conditions of efficient attention mechanisms \[[19](http://arxiv.org/abs/2411.01537)\]. This approach not only improves time and memory efficiency but also preserves the learning capabilities of traditional attention mechanisms \[[19](http://arxiv.org/abs/2411.01537)\]. Such advancements are crucial for handling the variability in sequence lengths and capturing diverse interaction patterns, which are key challenges in deep sequential recommendation models \[[19](http://arxiv.org/abs/2411.01537)\].
