## Related Works

Sequential recommendation has emerged as a critical component of modern recommender systems, aiming to predict the next item a user is likely to interact with based on their historical behavior \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2502.08132)\]. Early methods, such as Markov Chains (MCs), assumed that a user's next action depends only on their last few interactions, while Recurrent Neural Networks (RNNs) were introduced to capture longer-term dependencies \[[13](http://arxiv.org/abs/1808.09781)\]. However, RNNs often suffer from vanishing gradients and computational inefficiencies, especially with long sequences \[[16](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/0b42168f22713c48d8b0ce3bc8f680d347184d88)\]. More recently, self-attention mechanisms have gained popularity due to their ability to capture both local and global dependencies in user-item interaction sequences \[[13](http://arxiv.org/abs/1808.09781)\]. 

Despite these advances, existing models often struggle with modeling sequence length variations and capturing diverse interaction patterns \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2502.08132)\]. For instance, Transformer-based models, while effective in modeling sequential dependencies, typically require quadratic computational complexity due to the attention mechanism, which limits their scalability for long sequences \[[17](http://arxiv.org/abs/2409.05022)\]. Additionally, many models fail to account for the temporal dynamics of user behavior, such as the impact of irregular time intervals between interactions \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2502.08132)\]. 

To address these limitations, several works have explored alternative architectures. For example, the Mamba model, based on state space models (SSMs), offers a more efficient approach by leveraging hardware-aware designs to process long sequences with linear complexity \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/2403.03900)\]. Similarly, the S3-Rec model introduces self-supervised learning with mutual information maximization to enhance the representation of sequential data, particularly in scenarios with limited training data \[[8](http://arxiv.org/abs/2008.07873)\]. 

Another line of research focuses on modeling user preferences more effectively. The KATRec model integrates knowledge graph information to enhance item representation by capturing both short-term and long-term user interests \[[20](http://arxiv.org/abs/2012.03323)\]. Similarly, the ARERec model introduces a local interaction-aware mechanism to better capture the specific characteristics of user and item interactions within a sequence \[[9](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/656670a2f0b632a41eae8b20e28d1f80ec12b697)\]. 

Temporal awareness has also been a key focus in recent studies. The TiSASRec model explicitly models time intervals between interactions, improving prediction accuracy by incorporating both absolute and relative temporal information \[[12](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/9c7a455b1e48d01a99e58e884a6c1acb75074ad0)\]. The TLSRec model further addresses the challenge of modeling user preference changes over time by integrating a hierarchical self-attention network and a time lag-sensitive fusion mechanism \[[6](http://arxiv.org/abs/2208.04760)\]. 

In addition to temporal modeling, some works have explored the integration of social and contextual information. The STRec model leverages social influence and cross-domain interactions to provide a more holistic understanding of user preferences \[[4](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/dd27b3098dd3974068bed4e6291612c1c5bc3550)\].
