## Related Works

Anomaly detection in industrial settings has garnered significant attention due to its critical role in quality control and operational efficiency. Several recent works have addressed the challenges of deploying anomaly detection models on resource-constrained embedded devices, which is a key concern in Small and Medium Enterprises (SMEs) \[[16](http://arxiv.org/abs/2012.03586)\]. For instance, \[[15](http://arxiv.org/abs/1607.00148)\] proposed an encoder-decoder scheme based on Long Short Term Memory (LSTM) networks for multi-sensor anomaly detection, demonstrating robustness in detecting anomalies from both predictable and unpredictable time-series data. However, these methods often rely on complex architectures that are not suitable for deployment on embedded systems \[[15](http://arxiv.org/abs/1607.00148)\].

To address the computational demands of deep learning models in industrial anomaly detection, \[[7](http://arxiv.org/abs/2403.04932)\] introduced a tiled ensemble approach that reduces memory consumption by dividing images into tiles and training dedicated models for each tile. This approach enables high-resolution processing while maintaining GPU memory constraints, making it more practical for deployment. Similarly, \[[11](http://arxiv.org/abs/2303.14535)\] proposed EfficientAD, which achieves millisecond-level latency for visual anomaly detection by using a lightweight feature extractor and a student-teacher framework. This model significantly improves detection performance while maintaining low computational costs, making it suitable for real-time applications \[[11](http://arxiv.org/abs/2303.14535)\].

In the context of industrial IoT, \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/2303.03611)\] introduced TinyAD, a memory-efficient framework for anomaly detection in time-series data. By leveraging depthwise separable convolutions and memory rescheduling strategies, TinyAD reduces peak memory consumption by up to 5× with negligible computation overhead, enabling the deployment of complex models on microcontroller-based devices \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/2303.03611)\]. This aligns with the goal of KairosAD, which is designed for deployment on resource-constrained embedded devices such as NVIDIA Jetson NX and AGX \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2403.12580)\].

Another important direction in industrial anomaly detection is the use of synthetic data to improve generalization. \[[4](http://arxiv.org/abs/2503.04997)\] introduced the ISP-AD dataset, which combines synthetic and real defects collected directly from factory floors, allowing for more realistic benchmarking. The dataset is particularly useful for training models that can generalize to real-world conditions, which is crucial for industrial applications \[[4](http://arxiv.org/abs/2503.04997)\]. Industrial anomaly detection (IAD) has seen significant advancements, particularly in addressing the challenges of real-world applicability and generalization. Existing methods often struggle with dataset limitations, such as insufficient scale and diversity, which hinder their performance in practical scenarios \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2403.12580)\]. To overcome these issues, researchers have introduced large-scale, real-world datasets like Real-IAD, which contains 150K high-resolution images of 30 different objects, offering a more challenging and representative benchmark \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2403.12580)\]. Additionally, multimodal approaches, such as CMDIAD, have been proposed to leverage the complementarity of different data modalities (e.g., 3D point clouds and RGB images) for improved anomaly detection in industrial settings \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/2405.13571)\]. These methods aim to handle incomplete modalities during inference, which is common in real-world applications \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/2405.13571)\]. Furthermore, data-efficient architectures, such as those based on energy consumption time series, have been developed to address the lack of labeled data and improve performance with active learning strategies \[[9](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/e4d713a97daf5acc250432246b45c9558a5e8b87)\]. These approaches demonstrate the potential of using easily accessible data for anomaly detection in manufacturing \[[9](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/e4d713a97daf5acc250432246b45c9558a5e8b87)\]. Moreover, system monitoring techniques employing parallel multi-view neural networks have been introduced to detect anomalous behavior in industrial control systems by analyzing both data flow and sensor signals \[[10](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/6c6c14f42a07de45d70828eab3c272b76056e0ea)\]. These methods enable semi-supervised monitoring and improve reliability by detecting deviations from expected operational states \[[10](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/6c6c14f42a07de45d70828eab3c272b76056e0ea)\]. Finally, pose-agnostic anomaly detection has gained attention due to the need for models that can generalize across different object orientations and poses \[[12](http://arxiv.org/abs/2310.07716)\]. These developments highlight the ongoing efforts to enhance the generalization and real-world applicability of anomaly detection techniques in industrial settings.

[Snippet]: «Monitoring and detecting abnormal events in cyber-physical systems is crucial to industrial production. With the prevalent deployment of the industrial Internet of Things (IIoTs), an enormous amount of time-series data is collected to facilitate machine learning models for anomaly detection, and it is of the utmost importance to directly deploy the trained models on the IIoT devices. However, it is most challenging to deploy complex deep learning models such as convolutional neural networks (CNNs) on these memory-constrained IIoT devices embedded with microcontrollers (MCUs). To alleviate the memory constraints of MCUs, we propose a novel framework named Tiny Anomaly Detection (TinyAD) to efficiently facilitate onboard inference of CNNs for real-time anomaly detection. First, we conduct a comprehensive analysis of depthwise separable CNNs and regular CNNs for anomaly detection and find that the depthwise separable convolution operation can reduce the model size by 50%–90% compared with the traditional CNNs. Then, to reduce the peak memory consumption of CNNs, we explore two complementary strategies, 1) in-place; and 2) patch-by-patch memory rescheduling, and integrate them into a unified framework. The in-place method decreases the peak memory of the depthwise convolution by sparing a temporary buffer to transfer the activation results, while the patch-by-patch method further reduces the peak memory of layer-wise execution by slicing the input data into corresponding receptive fields and executing in order. Furthermore, by adjusting the dimension of convolution filters, these strategies apply to both univariate time series and multidomain time series features. Extensive experiments on real-world industrial datasets show that our framework can reduce peak memory consumption by 2–5× with negligible computation overhead.»

[Snippet]: «Payload-based anomaly detection (PAD) model is commonly built upon a big data of normal payload samples, and hence is able to discover zero-day attacks and unknown faults without the need of any negative samples in training phase. But such detection model encounters new challenges to adapt well to the emerging Industrial Internet of Things (IIoT). That is, the modern industrial processes are usually running in a very high complexity, resulting the payloads much more complex and diverse. Further, the industrial data is likely too sensitive to be shared in public, and thus induces a new privacy concern. To tackle these challenges, we propose $(\mathrm( P))^(3)(\mathrm( AD))$ , a novel privacy-preserved payload-based anomaly detection model for IIoT. The basic idea is to train a two-dimensional convolutional neural network (2D-CNN) based auto-encoder using normal payloads over a federated GAN (Generative Adversarial Network) architecture, and then to detect anomalies by an unexpected dissimilarity between the original payloads and the payloads reconstructed by the auto-encoder. By the 2D-CNN, we can model the normal payloads from both the request and response directions simultaneously, and thus have more opportunities to capture the complex and dynamic industrial behaviors that are possibly reflected in the bi-directional network communications. By the GAN, we can train a more generalized auto-encoder that is able to reconstruct more general payload samples without the need to have them in advance for model training. By the federated architecture, we can remove the need of direct sharing of normal payloads, and learn them indirectly by aggregating local models across different industrial data owners, hence ensuring the payload privacy. We have evaluated $(\mathrm( P))^(3)(\mathrm( AD))$ using four public industrial payload datasets as well as considering four typical IIoT PAD scenarios. The detection results achieve more than 0.966 in terms of F1 score for global condition and at least 0.753 for all kinds of federated settings, proving the effectiveness of our $(\mathrm( P))^(3)(\mathrm( AD))$ with privacy preserved.»

[Snippet]: «Detecting anomalies in images is an important task, especially in real-time computer vision applications. In this work, we focus on computational efficiency and propose a lightweight feature extractor that processes an image in less than a millisecond on a modern GPU. We then use a student–teacher approach to detect anomalous features. We train a student network to predict the extracted features of normal, i.e., anomaly-free training images. The detection of anomalies at test time is enabled by the student failing to predict their features. We propose a training loss that hinders the student from imitating the teacher feature extractor beyond the normal images. It allows us to drastically reduce the computational cost of the student–teacher model, while improving the detection of anomalous features. We furthermore address the detection of challenging logical anomalies that involve invalid combinations of normal local features, for example, a wrong ordering of objects. We detect these anomalies by efficiently incorporating an autoencoder that analyzes images globally. We evaluate our method, called EfficientAD, on 32 datasets from three industrial anomaly detection dataset collections. EfficientAD sets new standards for both the detection and the localization of anomalies. At a latency of two milliseconds and a throughput of six hundred images per second, it enables a fast handling of anomalies. Together with its low error rate, this makes it an economical solution for real-world applications and a fruitful basis for future research.»
