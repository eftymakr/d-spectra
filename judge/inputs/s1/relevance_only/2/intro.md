## Related Works

Nonverbal communication plays a crucial role in human interaction, encompassing gestures, facial expressions, and body language that convey intent and emotion \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/1811.09362)\]. Existing large language models (LLMs) often struggle to effectively integrate these nonverbal elements, limiting their ability to create immersive conversational experiences \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/1811.09362)\]. This section reviews relevant works that have explored nonverbal communication, multimodal models, and datasets for nonverbal cues, providing a foundation for understanding the contributions of MARS and VENUS.

Several studies have focused on modeling nonverbal cues in human communication. For instance, \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/1811.09362)\] discusses the importance of nonverbal behaviors in face-to-face communication and proposes the Recurrent Attended Variation Embedding Network (RAVEN) to dynamically shift word representations based on nonverbal cues. This work highlights the need to consider both verbal and nonverbal contexts when modeling human language \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/1811.09362)\]. Similarly, \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/2502.16671)\] introduces MimeQA, a dataset of mime videos designed to evaluate AI's ability to understand nonverbal social interactions, revealing that current models struggle with this task \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/2502.16671)\].

The integration of nonverbal cues with language models has also been explored in multimodal settings. \[[5](http://arxiv.org/abs/1802.00923)\] presents the Multi-attention Recurrent Network (MARN), which captures interactions between modalities using a multi-attention block and stores them in a hybrid memory. This model achieves state-of-the-art results in multimodal sentiment analysis and emotion recognition \[[5](http://arxiv.org/abs/1802.00923)\]. Additionally, \[[20](http://arxiv.org/abs/2306.09093)\] introduces Macaw-LLM, a multi-modal LLM that integrates visual, audio, and textual information, demonstrating the potential for models to handle diverse data modalities \[[20](http://arxiv.org/abs/2306.09093)\].

Datasets play a vital role in advancing research on nonverbal communication. \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/2410.14050)\] presents a dataset annotated with nonverbal cues of uncertainty, developed in collaboration with psychologists, to study how uncertainty is expressed through multimodal signals \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/2410.14050)\]. \[[14](http://arxiv.org/abs/2406.11833)\] introduces MMDU, a benchmark and instruction-tuning dataset for evaluating large vision-language models (LVLMs) in multi-turn, multi-image dialog scenarios, highlighting the challenges of real-world conversational settings \[[14](http://arxiv.org/abs/2406.11833)\]. Similarly, \[[25](http://arxiv.org/abs/2310.20550)\] discusses the importance of high-quality multimodal data for training large models, proposing CAPSFUSION to refine and consolidate image-text pairs and synthetic captions for better performance \[[25](http://arxiv.org/abs/2310.20550)\].
