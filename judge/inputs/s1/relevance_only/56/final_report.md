## Related Works

Cross-Domain Sequential Recommendation (CDSR) has attracted significant attention due to its ability to alleviate data sparsity and cold-start issues by leveraging sequential interactions across multiple domains \[[1](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/e33f51c13728bedeeb54a76b398949a381d6152f)\]. Traditional approaches often rely on overlapping users to transfer knowledge between domains, but this limits their applicability to real-world scenarios where such users are rare \[[5](http://arxiv.org/abs/2410.13588)\]. Moreover, these methods struggle to capture complex transition patterns from mixed behavior sequences \[[5](http://arxiv.org/abs/2410.13588)\]. Recent studies have explored the integration of Large Language Models (LLMs) into CDSR to address these limitations by leveraging their semantic reasoning capabilities \[[15](http://arxiv.org/abs/2209.10163)\].

Several works have attempted to enhance CDSR through the use of LLMs. For instance, \[[1](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/e33f51c13728bedeeb54a76b398949a381d6152f)\] proposes LLMCDSR, which employs LLMs to predict pseudo items within single-domain interactions and uses a Collaborative-Textual Contrastive Pre-Training strategy to infuse collaborative information into textual features. This approach aims to improve the generation reliability of LLMs by mitigating hallucination issues. Similarly, \[[15](http://arxiv.org/abs/2209.10163)\] introduces URLLM, which integrates user retrieval and domain grounding into LLMs to improve the alignment of domain-specific knowledge. This method uses a dual-graph sequential model and alignment and contrastive learning techniques to facilitate domain knowledge transfer.

Another line of research focuses on zero-shot sequential recommendation, where models must generalize to unseen domains without additional training \[[14](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/756e3e6c0956d1daa19367eb13115151b20eeb7f)\]. \[[14](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/756e3e6c0956d1daa19367eb13115151b20eeb7f)\] proposes a semantic bias-aware framework that enhances LLM-based zero-shot sequential recommendation by improving cross-domain alignment at both the item and sequential levels. This includes a generalization loss to align item embeddings across domains while preserving intra-domain diversity. Additionally, \[[14](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/756e3e6c0956d1daa19367eb13115151b20eeb7f)\] develops a method to transfer user behavioral patterns by clustering source domain user sequences and applying attention-based aggregation during target domain inference.

In contrast, \[[15](http://arxiv.org/abs/2209.10163)\] explores the use of LLMs for cross-domain recommendation by leveraging their reasoning capabilities and designing prompt-based strategies to improve performance across multiple domain pairs. This work demonstrates that LLMs, when prompted effectively, can outperform state-of-the-art CDSR baselines across various metrics and domain combinations.

Moreover, \[[15](http://arxiv.org/abs/2209.10163)\] investigates the effectiveness of LLMs in cross-domain recommendation by proposing LLM4CDR, a novel pipeline that constructs context-aware prompts using users' purchase history sequences and shared features between source and target domains. This approach shows strong performance, particularly when using LLMs with large parameter sizes and when the source and target domains exhibit smaller domain gaps.

Overall, these studies underscore the growing interest in utilizing LLMs for CDSR, emphasizing the need for robust alignment strategies and domain adaptation techniques to enhance performance across diverse and dynamic recommendation scenarios.

### <a id='category-cross-domain-sequential-recommendation-(cdsr)-overview'></a>Cross-Domain Sequential Recommendation (CDSR) Overview: 3 papers found.
Cross-Domain Sequential Recommendation (CDSR) focuses on enhancing recommendation accuracy by leveraging user interactions across multiple domains, addressing challenges such as data sparsity, class imbalance, and the interplay between single- and cross-domain preferences \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/2401.04971)\]. Existing approaches often rely on transferring knowledge from rich domains to target domains, but this method overlooks the inter-sequence item relationships and limits the potential for mutual enhancement between domains \[[12](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/96bfe33a2503e960153a934e10be91da4d8b13a6)\]. Additionally, the integration of cross-domain sequences, which are typically noisy, presents a significant challenge due to their complexity and the difficulty in directly learning from them \[[15](http://arxiv.org/abs/2209.10163)\]. To tackle these issues, recent work has introduced innovative frameworks like DREAM, which employs decoupled representations and supervised contrastive learning to simultaneously model single- and cross-domain preferences while addressing data sparsity and class imbalance through focal loss \[[12](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/96bfe33a2503e960153a934e10be91da4d8b13a6)\]. Similarly, the DDGHM framework combines dual dynamic graph modeling with hybrid metric training to capture both intra- and inter-domain transitions, further alleviating data sparsity and improving prediction accuracy \[[15](http://arxiv.org/abs/2209.10163)\]. These advancements highlight the evolving strategies in CDSR, emphasizing the need for more sophisticated modeling techniques that can effectively integrate cross-domain information while preserving domain-specific characteristics.
