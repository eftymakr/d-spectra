## Related Works

The rapid growth of open-source machine learning (ML) resources has significantly impacted information retrieval (IR) research, prompting the need for more sophisticated tools to manage and analyze these resources \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2005.00687)\]. Existing platforms, such as Hugging Face, provide access to a vast array of ML models and datasets but lack structured representations that enable advanced queries and analyses \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2005.00687)\]. This limitation hinders tasks such as tracing model evolution and recommending relevant datasets \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2005.00687)\]. To address these challenges, recent work has explored the use of knowledge graphs (KGs) to organize and enhance the management of ML resources \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2005.00687)\].

Knowledge graphs have been widely used in various domains, including social networks, biological networks, and knowledge graphs, to represent complex relationships between entities \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/2408.08921)\]. These KGs often include rich textual attributes and domain-specific relations, which are essential for advanced querying and analysis \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2005.00687)\]. For example, the Open Graph Benchmark (OGB) provides a set of large-scale datasets for graph machine learning, emphasizing scalability and out-of-distribution generalization \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/2408.08921)\]. The OGB datasets are designed to challenge existing models and encourage the development of more robust and scalable graph ML techniques \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/2408.08921)\].

In the context of knowledge graph reasoning, several studies have explored the use of graph-based methods to enhance the accuracy and efficiency of knowledge graph completion tasks \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/1808.09040)\]. For instance, relational graph convolutional networks (R-GCNs) have been introduced to handle the highly multi-relational data characteristic of knowledge bases \[[8](http://arxiv.org/abs/1703.06103)\]. These models have shown significant improvements in tasks such as link prediction and entity classification \[[8](http://arxiv.org/abs/1703.06103)\]. Additionally, one-shot relational learning frameworks have been proposed to address the challenge of predicting new facts with limited training data \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/1808.09040)\]. These frameworks utilize embedding models and graph structures to achieve better performance without requiring extensive training \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/1808.09040)\].

The use of knowledge graphs in information retrieval has also been explored in the context of user intent capture and recommendation systems \[[9](http://arxiv.org/abs/2411.01023)\]. Knowledge graphs can be used to store not only workflow components and datasets but also user intents and feedback, enabling more personalized and context-aware recommendations \[[9](http://arxiv.org/abs/2411.01023)\]. This approach has been applied in intelligent discovery assistants (IDAs) and automated machine learning (AutoML) systems to enhance user interaction with data \[[9](http://arxiv.org/abs/2411.01023)\].

In summary, the construction of HuggingKG and the development of HuggingBench are informed by existing research on knowledge graphs and their applications in information retrieval and machine learning resource management \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2005.00687)\]. ### <a id='category-graph-based-ml-models'></a>Graph-Based ML Models: 5 papers found.
The category "Graph-Based ML Models" encompasses a range of approaches that leverage graph structures to enhance machine learning tasks, particularly in scenarios involving knowledge representation and reasoning. Recent advancements include Retrieval-Augmented Generation (RAG), which integrates external knowledge bases to improve the accuracy and reliability of Large Language Models (LLMs) by mitigating issues like hallucination and outdated information \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/2408.08921)\]. Building on this, GraphRAG extends RAG by incorporating structural information from graphs, enabling more precise retrieval and context-aware generation through Graph-Based Indexing, Graph-Guided Retrieval, and Graph-Enhanced Generation \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/2408.08921)\]. Additionally, knowledge graphs (KGs) play a crucial role in natural language processing, where they are used to expand coverage and improve reasoning capabilities, especially in the presence of long-tail relations that lack sufficient training data \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/1808.09040)\]. One-shot relational learning frameworks have been proposed to address this challenge by utilizing embedding models and graph structures to predict new facts without requiring extensive training data \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/1808.09040)\]. Knowledge graph reasoning (KGR) further extends these capabilities by deducing new facts from existing ones, with models evolving from static to temporal and multi-modal approaches to better reflect real-world dynamics \[[4](http://arxiv.org/abs/2212.05767)\]. These developments highlight the growing importance of graph-based methods in enhancing the efficiency and effectiveness of machine learning systems, particularly in scenarios where labeled data is limited or where relational knowledge is critical \[[5](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/dcbb5a87d2c93ff200e665847b5eaccfc186dedc)\].

### <a id='category-knowledge-graph-construction'></a>Knowledge Graph Construction: 2 papers found.
Knowledge graph construction has emerged as a critical area for enhancing the quality and utility of structured data in various applications, including search engines and question-answering systems \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2005.00687)\]. The importance of high-quality knowledge graphs (KGs) is underscored by the fact that errors, duplicates, and missing values can significantly hinder their effectiveness in real-world scenarios \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2005.00687)\]. To address these challenges, researchers have proposed frameworks that focus on defining quality metrics, validating KGs through cleaning tasks, and employing strategies for duplicate detection and knowledge fusion to enrich KGs \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2005.00687)\]. Additionally, there is a growing body of work on knowledge graph representation learning, which explores methods for embedding entities and relations into vector spaces to facilitate more effective querying and analysis \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/2408.08921)\]. This includes the use of scoring functions, encoding models, and auxiliary information to improve the accuracy and robustness of knowledge graph embeddings \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/2408.08921)\]. Furthermore, the field has seen advancements in knowledge acquisition and completion, with techniques such as path inference and logical rule reasoning being employed to enhance the completeness of KGs \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/2408.08921)\]. The construction of large-scale KGs, such as HuggingKG, demonstrates the potential of these approaches in managing and analyzing open-source machine learning resources by capturing domain-specific relations and textual attributes \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/1808.09040)\]. These developments highlight the ongoing efforts to improve the quality and functionality of knowledge graphs for a wide range of applications.

Despite the great effort invested in their creation and maintenance, even the largest (e.g., Yago, DBPedia or Wikidata) remain incomplete. We introduce Relational Graph Convolutional Networks (R-GCNs) and apply them to two standard knowledge base completion tasks: Link prediction (recovery of missing facts, i.e. subject-predicate-object triples) and entity classification (recovery of missing entity attributes). R-GCNs are related to a recent class of neural networks operating on graphs, and are developed specifically to handle the highly multi-relational data characteristic of realistic knowledge bases. We further show that factorization models for link prediction such as DistMult can be significantly improved through the use of an R-GCN encoder model to accumulate evidence over multiple inference steps in the graph, demonstrating a large improvement of 29.8% on FB15k-237 over a decoder-only baseline.»
