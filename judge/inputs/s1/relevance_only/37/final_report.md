## Related Works

Vision Transformers (ViTs) have emerged as a powerful alternative to convolutional neural networks (CNNs) in computer vision tasks, leveraging self-attention mechanisms to capture global context \[[4](http://arxiv.org/abs/2405.00314)\]. However, the deployment of ViTs on hardware platforms, especially FPGAs, remains challenging due to their high computational and memory demands \[[1](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/0ade4d722e48379761d17cf1efc05200315c27d9)\]. Existing accelerators often rely on floating-point arithmetic, which is inefficient for hardware implementation \[[16](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/b2e51759f910101691039846bec4401a85b53309)\]. Additionally, complex operations such as Softmax and LayerNorm are difficult to map onto low-precision hardware without significant accuracy loss \[[1](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/0ade4d722e48379761d17cf1efc05200315c27d9)\].

Several works have explored hardware acceleration for ViTs. HDViT introduces a heterogeneous FPGA accelerator with adaptive dataflow mapping to improve performance \[[1](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/0ade4d722e48379761d17cf1efc05200315c27d9)\]. Auto-ViT-Acc proposes an FPGA-aware framework with mixed-scheme quantization, achieving up to 5.6x speedup on frame rate with minimal accuracy loss \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/2208.05163)\]. HeatViT employs adaptive token pruning and 8-bit quantization to reduce computation and memory usage while maintaining accuracy \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/2211.08110)\]. ME-ViT focuses on minimizing memory traffic by implementing a single-load policy, achieving significant improvements in memory bandwidth and throughput \[[5](http://arxiv.org/abs/2402.09709)\]. ReViT leverages visual semantics to optimize attention mechanisms, achieving speedups of up to 2.3x and energy efficiency improvements of 3.6x \[[6](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/f47b726ad00e1a93adc5b3e266c71d75bb05e1ff)\].

Quantization has also been extensively studied for ViTs. Q-ViT introduces an information rectification module and distribution-guided distillation to maintain accuracy during full quantization, achieving up to 6.14x speedup \[[9](http://arxiv.org/abs/2210.06707)\]. Trio-ViT eliminates the Softmax operation and integrates linear attention, achieving up to 7.3x speedup in FPS \[[8](http://arxiv.org/abs/2405.03882)\]. GhostViT reduces parameters and FLOPs by generating diverse features through cheap operations, achieving up to 17% FLOP reduction without accuracy loss \[[10](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/37c4b720e1468ca18efb79aa383ffd72a761757b)\]. CHOSEN introduces a software-hardware co-design framework with approximate non-linear functions and efficient use of FPGA resources, achieving up to 1.5x throughput improvement \[[11](http://arxiv.org/abs/2407.12736)\]. ViTA is a configurable accelerator for edge devices, achieving nearly 90% hardware utilization and low power consumption \[[12](http://arxiv.org/abs/2302.09108)\].

### <a id='category-fpga-based-acceleration-approaches'></a>FPGA-Based Acceleration Approaches: 5 papers found.
FPGA-based acceleration approaches for Vision Transformers (ViTs) have been extensively explored to address the computational and memory challenges inherent in their complex architectures. Several works, such as HDViT \[[1](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/0ade4d722e48379761d17cf1efc05200315c27d9)\], HeatViT \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/2211.08110)\], and ME-ViT \[[5](http://arxiv.org/abs/2402.09709)\], have proposed hardware accelerators tailored for ViTs, focusing on reducing off-chip memory access and improving throughput. HDViT introduces a heterogeneous architecture with multiple processing engines (PEs) to accelerate various operators, along with a hybrid dataflow mapping strategy to minimize data transmission granularity \[[1](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/0ade4d722e48379761d17cf1efc05200315c27d9)\]. HeatViT, on the other hand, employs an image-adaptive token pruning framework to reduce computation and enhance accuracy, leveraging hardware-efficient implementations with 8-bit fixed-point quantization \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/2211.08110)\]. ME-ViT further minimizes memory traffic by adopting a single-load policy and integrating critical operations like Softmax and LayerNorm into a memory-efficient processing element (ME-PE) \[[5](http://arxiv.org/abs/2402.09709)\]. Additionally, CHOSEN \[[11](http://arxiv.org/abs/2407.12736)\] introduces a software-hardware co-design framework to optimize ViT deployment on FPGAs, emphasizing multi-kernel design, approximate non-linear functions, and efficient use of logic blocks. These approaches collectively highlight the importance of custom hardware designs and efficient dataflow strategies to achieve high performance and low resource utilization in ViT acceleration on FPGAs.

### <a id='category-quantization-techniques-for-vits'></a>Quantization Techniques for ViTs: 5 papers found.
The related works on quantization techniques for Vision Transformers (ViTs) highlight the challenges and solutions in reducing computational and memory demands while maintaining accuracy. Early efforts focused on algorithmic quantization without hardware acceleration, resulting in limited performance gains \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/2208.05163)\]. More recent approaches, such as those in \[[4](http://arxiv.org/abs/2405.00314)\], emphasize the need for algorithm-hardware co-design to optimize ViTs for resource-constrained devices. These works identify the non-linear operations, particularly Softmax, as a major barrier to effective quantization due to their sensitivity to precision changes \[[8](http://arxiv.org/abs/2405.03882)\]. To address this, researchers have proposed efficient ViT architectures that eliminate Softmax and integrate linear attention mechanisms, such as Trio-ViT, which achieves significant improvements in inference speed and hardware efficiency \[[8](http://arxiv.org/abs/2405.03882)\]. Additionally, methods like Q-ViT introduce information rectification modules and distribution-guided distillation to mitigate performance degradation caused by low-bit quantization \[[9](http://arxiv.org/abs/2210.06707)\]. These techniques demonstrate that with careful design, quantization can be applied to ViTs without compromising accuracy, paving the way for more efficient hardware accelerators. The exploration of hardware-friendly quantization schemes, such as dynamic patch and weight bit-group quantization in BSViT, further illustrates the potential of combining algorithmic and hardware innovations to enhance ViT performance \[[16](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/b2e51759f910101691039846bec4401a85b53309)\]. Overall, these studies underscore the importance of tailored quantization strategies and hardware co-design in advancing ViT deployment on edge devices.

### <a id='category-efficiency-and-resource-utilization'></a>Efficiency and Resource Utilization: 5 papers found.
Efficiency and resource utilization in Vision Transformers (ViTs) have been a focal point of recent research due to their high computational demands and parameter counts, which hinder deployment on resource-constrained devices \[[12](http://arxiv.org/abs/2302.09108)\]. Several approaches have been proposed to address these challenges, including compact architectures, pruning, knowledge distillation, and quantization strategies \[[7](http://arxiv.org/abs/2309.02031)\]. For instance, GhostViT introduces a method to generate diverse features using low-cost operations in the multi-head self-attention (MHSA) and feed-forward network (FFN) modules, significantly reducing parameters and FLOPs while maintaining accuracy \[[10](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/37c4b720e1468ca18efb79aa383ffd72a761757b)\]. Similarly, SuperViT presents a training paradigm that enables models to handle varying computational costs and input sizes, achieving performance improvements with reduced FLOPs \[[15](http://arxiv.org/abs/2205.11397)\]. Additionally, EViT reorganizes image tokens during the feed-forward process to preserve attentive tokens and fuse inattentive ones, enhancing inference efficiency without additional parameters \[[17](http://arxiv.org/abs/2202.07800)\]. These methods collectively highlight the ongoing efforts to optimize ViTs for practical deployment, focusing on reducing computational costs and improving hardware efficiency \[[12](http://arxiv.org/abs/2302.09108)\]\[[10](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/37c4b720e1468ca18efb79aa383ffd72a761757b)\]\[[15](http://arxiv.org/abs/2205.11397)\]\[[17](http://arxiv.org/abs/2202.07800)\].
