## Related Works

Recent research has focused on understanding how large language models (LLMs) evolve during fine-tuning and how specific layers contribute to model behavior. A key challenge in this area is identifying which layers are most critical for alignment, performance, and robustness. Several studies have explored the role of individual layers in LLMs, often using data-dependent analyses of fine-tuned models \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2410.17875)\]. However, these approaches are limited to post-hoc settings, making them less useful for pre-fine-tuned model analysis. In contrast, our work introduces a data-oblivious method for identifying intrinsic critical layers in pre-fine-tuned LLMs, based on representation dynamics \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2410.17875)\].

The importance of layer-specific tuning has been explored in several works. For instance, \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/2503.04346)\] proposes a method to incorporate alignment control into a single model by adding an identity layer and performing preference learning only on this layer. This approach allows for efficient fine-tuning and enables interpolation between aligned and unaligned behaviors. Similarly, \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/2505.03793)\] investigates the effectiveness of freezing non-essential layers during fine-tuning, finding that it improves overall model performance. These findings align with our results, which show that freezing critical layers during backdoor defense reduces attack success rates by up to 40% \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2410.17875)\].

Another line of work focuses on the efficiency of fine-tuning methods. \[[4](http://arxiv.org/abs/2303.18223)\] introduces a framework called LensLLM, which uses a PAC-Bayesian Generalization Bound to model the dynamic behaviors of LLMs during fine-tuning. This approach enables accurate performance prediction across diverse tasks while maintaining computational efficiency. Our work complements this by showing that identifying critical layers through representation dynamics can lead to more efficient domain adaptation, where fine-tuning critical layers results in greater loss reduction \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2410.17875)\].

The role of layer-specific fine-tuning in improving model performance has also been studied in the context of multimodal models. \[[5](http://arxiv.org/abs/2210.12770)\] investigates catastrophic forgetting in multimodal large language models (MLLMs), finding that fine-tuning often leads to performance degradation on original tasks. To address this, \[[6](http://arxiv.org/abs/2402.16363)\] proposes a post-training adjustment method called Model Tailor, which preserves pre-trained parameters while replacing a small number of fine-tuned parameters. This approach maintains high effectiveness on original tasks while improving performance on new tasks, similar to our findings on backdoor defense \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2410.17875)\].
