## Related Works

Recent research has focused on understanding how large language models (LLMs) evolve during fine-tuning and how specific layers contribute to model behavior. A key challenge in this area is identifying which layers are most critical for alignment, performance, and robustness. Several studies have explored the role of individual layers in LLMs, often using data-dependent analyses of fine-tuned models \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2410.17875)\]. However, these approaches are limited to post-hoc settings, making them less useful for pre-fine-tuned model analysis. In contrast, our work introduces a data-oblivious method for identifying intrinsic critical layers in pre-fine-tuned LLMs, based on representation dynamics \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2410.17875)\].

The importance of layer-specific tuning has been explored in several works. For instance, \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/2503.04346)\] proposes a method to incorporate alignment control into a single model by adding an identity layer and performing preference learning only on this layer. This approach allows for efficient fine-tuning and enables interpolation between aligned and unaligned behaviors. Similarly, \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/2505.03793)\] investigates the effectiveness of freezing non-essential layers during fine-tuning, finding that it improves overall model performance. These findings align with our results, which show that freezing critical layers during backdoor defense reduces attack success rates by up to 40% \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2410.17875)\].

Another line of work focuses on the efficiency of fine-tuning methods. \[[4](http://arxiv.org/abs/2303.18223)\] introduces a framework called LensLLM, which uses a PAC-Bayesian Generalization Bound to model the dynamic behaviors of LLMs during fine-tuning. This approach enables accurate performance prediction across diverse tasks while maintaining computational efficiency. Our work complements this by showing that identifying critical layers through representation dynamics can lead to more efficient domain adaptation, where fine-tuning critical layers results in greater loss reduction \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2410.17875)\].

The role of layer-specific fine-tuning in improving model performance has also been studied in the context of multimodal models. \[[5](http://arxiv.org/abs/2210.12770)\] investigates catastrophic forgetting in multimodal large language models (MLLMs), finding that fine-tuning often leads to performance degradation on original tasks. Fine-tuning efficiency and performance have been extensively studied, with researchers proposing various methods to reduce computational costs while maintaining model effectiveness. One approach involves incorporating alignment control into a single model, such as CLM, which adds an identity layer to map unaligned input embeddings into an aligned space, achieving performance comparable to full fine-tuning \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/2503.04346)\]. Additionally, the use of conditional random fields (CRF) layers has shown promise in improving model performance, particularly in clinical NLP tasks, where models trained from scratch with CRF layers achieved comparable results to fine-tuned LLMs with significantly lower training costs \[[5](http://arxiv.org/abs/2210.12770)\]. Efficient training techniques, such as data down-sampling, have also been explored to reduce training costs without sacrificing performance \[[5](http://arxiv.org/abs/2210.12770)\]. Furthermore, localized fine-tuning methods, such as LoFiT, have been introduced to modify only a sparse subset of attention heads, achieving comparable performance to parameter-efficient methods like LoRA while modifying fewer parameters \[[16](http://arxiv.org/abs/2406.01563)\]. These strategies highlight the ongoing efforts to enhance fine-tuning efficiency, balancing performance with resource constraints.

### <a id='category-neural-tangent-kernel-applications'></a>Neural Tangent Kernel Applications: 2 papers found.
The category "Neural Tangent Kernel Applications" focuses on leveraging Neural Tangent Kernels (NTK) to better understand and improve the performance of large language models (LLMs) during fine-tuning. Recent work has explored how NTKs can model the dynamic behaviors of LLMs, offering insights into their generalization capabilities and enabling more efficient model selection \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/2505.03793)\]. This is particularly relevant in scenarios where fine-tuning large models is computationally prohibitive, as NTK-based approaches can predict performance across diverse downstream tasks with reduced computational cost \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/2505.03793)\]. Additionally, studies have extended NTK formalism to optimization algorithms like Adam, using Tensor Programs to analyze the conditions under which NTK dynamics accurately describe fine-tuning updates in pre-trained language models \[[7](http://arxiv.org/abs/2210.05643)\]. These findings have implications for understanding why parameter-efficient fine-tuning methods succeed, as they suggest that fine-tuning operates within a kernel-based framework \[[7](http://arxiv.org/abs/2210.05643)\]. The application of NTKs has also been linked to the analysis of representation dynamics in LLMs, where shifts in representation space during fine-tuning are closely tied to changes in the top principal components, which encode semantic transitions \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2410.17875)\]. This aligns with broader efforts to improve interpretability and robustness by identifying critical layers in LLMs through methods like Centered Kernel Alignment (CKA) \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2410.17875)\]. Overall, NTK-based approaches provide a theoretical and practical lens for understanding and optimizing the fine-tuning process in LLMs \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/2505.03793)\]\[[7](http://arxiv.org/abs/2210.05643)\].

### <a id='category-model-preservation-and-parameter-adjustment'></a>Model Preservation and Parameter Adjustment: 2 papers found.
The category "Model Preservation and Parameter Adjustment" addresses critical challenges in maintaining model performance during adaptation. Catastrophic forgetting is a significant issue when fine-tuning multi-modal large language models (MLLMs), as improving performance on new tasks often leads to a decline in original task performance \[[19](http://arxiv.org/abs/2402.12048)\]. To mitigate this, the Model Tailor method was introduced, which preserves pre-trained parameters while selectively adjusting a small subset of fine-tuned parameters, achieving up to 99% effectiveness on original tasks and 97% on new tasks compared to standard fine-tuning \[[19](http://arxiv.org/abs/2402.12048)\]. This approach uses a sparse mask derived from a fusion of salience and sensitivity analysis to identify critical "model patches," followed by a compensation mechanism to enhance performance on both original and new tasks \[[19](http://arxiv.org/abs/2402.12048)\]. Additionally, this method is adaptable to multi-task scenarios, demonstrating significant task adaptability while preserving pre-trained capabilities \[[19](http://arxiv.org/abs/2402.12048)\]. In contrast, incremental learning paradigms such as continual learning, meta-learning, and parameter-efficient learning have also been explored, though many do not update the core model or achieve real-time adaptation \[[14](http://arxiv.org/abs/2404.18311)\]. These approaches highlight the ongoing challenges in balancing model preservation with effective parameter adjustment in large language models.
