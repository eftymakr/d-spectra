## Related Works

Recent advances in deep learning have enabled significant progress in understanding genomic data, particularly through the use of self-supervised pre-training methods. These approaches have been widely adopted in various domains, including natural language processing and computer vision, where they have demonstrated effectiveness in learning robust and generalizable representations \[[19](http://arxiv.org/abs/1901.09005)\]. In the context of genomics, several studies have explored the application of self-supervised learning to DNA sequences. For instance, DNABERT is a pre-trained bidirectional encoder representation model for DNA sequences that leverages both upstream and downstream nucleotide contexts to capture global and transferable understanding of genomic DNA \[[2](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/c43d9cade31600400a0f62beb5bbcc1b548e009e)\]. Similarly, GeneBERT introduces a multi-modal and self-supervised pre-training approach that integrates 1D genome sequences with 2D matrices of transcription factors and regions, enhancing the model's ability to generalize across cell types \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2110.05231)\].

However, these methods often focus solely on DNA sequences and may not account for the regulatory elements and genomic profiles that influence gene function, such as chromatin accessibility and DNA methylation \[[27](http://arxiv.org/abs/1308.2134)\]. This limitation has led researchers to explore supervised learning approaches for genomic profile prediction, which can better capture the complex relationships between DNA sequences and their functional annotations. For example, SNP2Vec is a self-supervised pre-training method designed to understand single nucleotide polymorphisms (SNPs) and has been successfully applied to predict disease risk \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/2204.06699)\]. Additionally, Enformer is a deep learning architecture that integrates long-range interactions in the genome to improve gene expression prediction, demonstrating the importance of considering regulatory elements in genomic modeling \[[9](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/e12e837cb2e9baeaefdcab06fe1c75add8f46389)\].

Furthermore, the integration of multiple genomic profiles and species-specific variations has been explored in several works. The SPACE model, which leverages a Mixture of Experts (MoE) framework, is designed to capture the relationships between DNA sequences across different species and genomic profiles, thereby learning more effective DNA representations \[[24](http://arxiv.org/abs/2107.13586)\]. This approach builds on earlier work in self-supervised learning, such as Contrastive Predictive Coding, which has been applied to various modalities including speech, images, and text \[[8](http://arxiv.org/abs/1807.03748)\]. The success of these methods highlights the potential of combining supervised and self-supervised learning strategies to enhance genomic representation learning.

In addition to these models, several studies have investigated the use of contrastive learning for unsupervised representation learning, demonstrating that such approaches can achieve state-of-the-art results on various benchmarks \[[25](http://arxiv.org/abs/2005.04966)\]. These findings underscore the importance of incorporating diverse learning strategies to improve the performance of genomic models.
