## Related Works

Federated unlearning has emerged as a critical research area to address the "right to be forgotten" in privacy-sensitive applications such as federated learning (FL) and federated online learning to rank (FOLTR) \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2412.19069)\]. This concept enables the removal of specific data contributions from a global model without requiring full retraining, thereby preserving both privacy and model performance \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/2401.05146)\]. Several studies have explored the design and evaluation of federated unlearning methods, highlighting the challenges of balancing efficiency, accuracy, and privacy \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/2310.20448)\].

In the context of FL, early work such as FedEraser introduced a method to remove a client's influence by leveraging historical parameter updates, significantly reducing the time required for unlearning compared to retraining from scratch \[[4](http://arxiv.org/abs/2310.19218)\]. Similarly, the work in \[[5](http://arxiv.org/abs/2406.03078)\] proposed a framework that allows for the removal of specific clients or data samples while maintaining model accuracy. These methods, however, often rely on storing historical model updates, which can be computationally intensive and pose privacy risks \[[6](http://arxiv.org/abs/2403.02437)\].

Recent efforts have focused on improving the efficiency and effectiveness of federated unlearning. For example, \[[7](http://arxiv.org/abs/2210.10958)\] introduced a lightweight unlearning module integrated into the learning process, enabling efficient and accurate unlearning without requiring additional training rounds. This approach is particularly relevant for FOLTR, where the continuous updating of ranking models based on implicit feedback necessitates mechanisms to remove outdated or sensitive data \[[8](http://arxiv.org/abs/2308.12502)\]. The paper also highlights the importance of using multiple evaluation metrics to assess unlearning success, as single metrics may not capture the full impact of unlearning on model performance \[[9](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/a52c3ec7325e81bd87fdf8d74bf9acfd1e1ffe5f)\].

The challenge of unlearning in FOLTR is further compounded by the need for verifiable methods that ensure the complete removal of a client's contribution. In this regard, \[[10](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/39da2d48f2132b919405e3bb6df6ece44ac6e15a)\] proposed a framework that enables the verification of unlearning effects through systematic analysis and quantification of model changes. This is crucial for ensuring compliance with legal and ethical standards, especially in domains where user privacy is paramount \[[11](http://arxiv.org/abs/2306.02216)\].

Moreover, the integration of differential privacy into unlearning processes has been explored to enhance security and prevent information leakage \[[12](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/c78de63d34189a0d6b7a6407781415cbe024e9af)\]. Techniques such as FedRecovery \[[13](http://arxiv.org/abs/2012.13891)\] and Starfish \[[14](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/a08f1c5c9f0bdb24f0dc75c0d969eb353899156b)\] have been developed to address these concerns by ensuring that unlearning operations do not compromise the privacy of the data or the integrity of the model. These methods also aim to reduce the computational overhead associated with unlearning, making them more practical for real-world applications \[[15](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/4e26fc4fe687c7471e44f2a5e6feef0facfc4be1)\].
