## Related Works

Brain tumor segmentation remains a challenging task in medical image analysis due to the high variability in tumor shapes, sizes, and locations, as well as the complexity of brain tissue structures \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2011.11052)\]. Traditional segmentation methods often rely on manual delineation, which is time-consuming and prone to inter-observer variability \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2011.11052)\]. With the advent of deep learning, particularly convolutional neural networks (CNNs), significant progress has been made in automating this process \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2011.11052)\]. U-Net and its variants have become the de facto standard for brain tumor segmentation due to their encoder-decoder architecture and ability to capture contextual information \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2011.11052)\].

Several studies have focused on improving the efficiency and performance of U-Net-based models. For instance, the 3D U-Net architecture has been widely adopted for volumetric MRI data, allowing for better spatial context capture compared to 2D models \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2011.11052)\]. However, 3D models often suffer from high computational costs and limited receptive fields \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2011.11052)\]. To address these issues, researchers have introduced modifications such as group normalization \[[2](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/e0bb56b61c0f6e4f36be5e56b37268d2ff5210b4)\], attention mechanisms \[[3](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/ea1672b029da4bcb3648ec5c7db616b303756db6)\], and residual blocks \[[4](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/281ede296764b95c329a7093e3b40b0b0c3ddee8)\]. These enhancements aim to reduce computational complexity while maintaining or improving segmentation accuracy \[[2](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/e0bb56b61c0f6e4f36be5e56b37268d2ff5210b4)\].

Transfer learning has also been explored to mitigate the lack of annotated data in medical imaging. Pre-trained models, such as those trained on ImageNet, have been adapted for brain tumor segmentation by fine-tuning on medical datasets \[[5](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/d68724bb11a1ccb790955dcc4647faf40898b8c1)\]. This approach helps reduce training time and improves generalization, especially when labeled data is limited \[[5](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/d68724bb11a1ccb790955dcc4647faf40898b8c1)\]. For example, the work by \[[6](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/05ca6bf812bb1c18493d2ee31fd71c282203970a)\] demonstrates that transfer learning can significantly enhance performance in postoperative tumor segmentation using limited data \[[6](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/05ca6bf812bb1c18493d2ee31fd71c282203970a)\].

Moreover, the integration of multi-modal data has proven beneficial for improving segmentation accuracy. Multi-modal MRI scans (e.g., T1, T2, FLAIR, and contrast-enhanced T1) provide complementary information that can be leveraged by deep learning models \[[7](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/6c9c65db8099038733cc0644265764c0570d4fbe)\]. The use of multimodal fusion techniques, such as feature concatenation or attention-based mechanisms, has been shown to enhance the model's ability to capture complex tumor characteristics \[[7](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/6c9c65db8099038733cc0644265764c0570d4fbe)\]. For instance, the SF-Net model \[[8](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/6b44c5ac2f0b264a84cd78bffaef060d6ea1514a)\] incorporates image fusion as an additional regularization during training, leading to improved segmentation accuracy compared to standard multi-task models \[[8](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/6b44c5ac2f0b264a84cd78bffaef060d6ea1514a)\].

Recent advances have also focused on optimizing model architecture to balance performance and computational efficiency. The nnU-Net framework \[[9](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/029c0eaae4b76f2a980ab196202c7b42318c7b1e)\] has been modified to incorporate dense connectivity and dynamic convolution layers, resulting in better feature representation and reduced computational overhead \[[9](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/029c0eaae4b76f2a980ab196202c7b42318c7b1e)\].
