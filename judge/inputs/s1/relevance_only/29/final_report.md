## Related Works

Maximum Inner Product Search (MIPS) has been extensively studied in recent years due to its critical role in recommendation systems, information retrieval, and high-dimensional data processing. Early approaches to MIPS often relied solely on inner product similarity, which can lead to local optima and redundant computations \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2503.06882)\]. Alternatively, some methods attempted to reduce MIPS to Nearest Neighbor Search (NNS) by projecting data into Euclidean space, but this often resulted in topology distortion and information loss \[[2](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/25454f456c514219c9d6eeb42200ebbf378db9ca)\]. These two paradigms have been considered as distinct, yet recent work suggests that there is no inherent binary opposition between inner product and Euclidean metrics \[[3](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/386854741f828d30e25a5e01d75e3363d49cad1e)\]. Instead, combining both can lead to significant performance improvements.

Graph-based methods have emerged as a promising direction for efficient MIPS. For instance, the inner-product navigable small world graph (ip-NSW) achieves an order of magnitude speedup over existing methods by leveraging the norm bias inherent in MIPS \[[4](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/92fb60fde7aa7c94a22571dec416d4a795548f67)\]. This bias means that large-norm items are more likely to be the result of MIPS, and ip-NSW exploits this by assigning higher in-degrees to such items \[[5](http://arxiv.org/abs/2201.09290)\]. Building on this, ip-NSW+ further improves performance by incorporating an angular proximity graph to refine search \[[6](http://arxiv.org/abs/1610.03317)\]. Similarly, the Proximity Graph with Spherical Pathway (PSP) was introduced to address clustering issues in MIPS solutions \[[7](http://arxiv.org/abs/1909.13459)\].

Another line of work focuses on the computational budget in MIPS. The Greedy-MIPS algorithm was designed to handle budgeted MIPS by design, offering superior performance compared to state-of-the-art methods \[[8](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/0a27db33fcdb1117c971263ada20718828a250d1)\]. Additionally, the Reinforcement Routing on Proximity Graph approach uses reinforcement learning to train an agent for efficient MIPS on a proximity graph, showing promising results \[[9](http://arxiv.org/abs/2212.07551)\]. This approach is particularly useful when ground truths are unavailable \[[10](http://arxiv.org/abs/2309.09013)\].

Efforts to improve search efficiency have also included the use of quantization techniques. For example, Anisotropic Additive Quantization (AAQ) combines score-aware loss with additive quantization to achieve lower approximation error than product quantization \[[11](http://arxiv.org/abs/1908.08656)\]. Similarly, the Quantization-based Fast Inner Product Search method uses codebooks to approximate inner products, avoiding the need for higher-dimensional feature space augmentation \[[12](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/23cff8ecfc5feb4208dbb90ff3fa7a6adb214e9c)\].

The work in \[[13](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/90d3d3c77e3acb0f455278af86c316f0ab893f8f)\] introduces GAIPS, a GPU-based framework for efficient MIPS that leverages norm-based pruning and other techniques to reduce computation. This method significantly outperforms FAISS in query processing time while maintaining the same recall \[[14](http://arxiv.org/abs/1509.01469)\].

The category "Graph-Based MIPS Techniques" encompasses a range of methods aimed at improving the efficiency and effectiveness of Maximum Inner Product Search (MIPS) in high-dimensional spaces. These techniques leverage graph-based indexing and search strategies to address the challenges posed by the non-metric nature of inner product similarity, which does not adhere to the triangle inequality \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2503.06882)\]. Unlike traditional approaches that either rely solely on inner product similarity or reduce MIPS to Euclidean nearest neighbor search, graph-based methods aim to combine the strengths of both paradigms \[[2](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/25454f456c514219c9d6eeb42200ebbf378db9ca)\]. For instance, the introduction of the Proximity Graph with Spherical Pathway (PSP) and Adaptive Early Termination (AET) has been instrumental in mitigating clustering issues around large-norm vectors and reducing redundant computations \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2503.06882)\]. Similarly, the norm adjusted proximity graph (NAPG) enhances the selection of meaningful data points during graph construction, leading to more efficient inner product search \[[2](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/25454f456c514219c9d6eeb42200ebbf378db9ca)\]. The inner-product navigable small world graph (ip-NSW) has also demonstrated exceptional performance by aligning with the norm bias inherent in MIPS, where large-norm items dominate the search process \[[7](http://arxiv.org/abs/1909.13459)\]. Building on such insights, the Metric-Amphibious Graph (MAG) and Adaptive Navigation with Metric Switch (ANMS) have been proposed to dynamically switch between inner product and Euclidean metrics during indexing and search, thereby enhancing adaptability and performance \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2503.06882)\]. These advancements highlight the growing importance of graph-based techniques in addressing the unique challenges of MIPS, offering significant improvements in search efficiency and scalability \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2503.06882)\]\[[2](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/25454f456c514219c9d6eeb42200ebbf378db9ca)\]\[[7](http://arxiv.org/abs/1909.13459)\].

### <a id='category-inner-product-similarity-approaches'></a>Inner Product Similarity Approaches: 12 papers found.
The category "Inner Product Similarity Approaches" encompasses a range of methods aimed at solving the Maximum Inner Product Search (MIPS) problem, which is critical in recommendation systems and high-dimensional data retrieval. Traditional approaches often rely on brute-force or nearest neighbor search techniques, which suffer from high computational complexity and limited scalability \[[3](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/386854741f828d30e25a5e01d75e3363d49cad1e)\]. To address these challenges, recent work has explored alternative strategies, such as transforming MIPS into a nearest neighbor problem using locality-sensitive hashing (LSH) or tree-based structures, enabling sublinear retrieval times \[[3](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/386854741f828d30e25a5e01d75e3363d49cad1e)\]. However, these methods often introduce additional overhead or lose important structural information during the transformation process \[[3](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/386854741f828d30e25a5e01d75e3363d49cad1e)\]. Another line of research focuses on integrating the indexing and inference phases during training, as seen in Indexable Probabilistic Matrix Factorization (IPMF), which achieves faster retrieval times by natively embedding sublinear search capabilities into the model \[[3](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/386854741f828d30e25a5e01d75e3363d49cad1e)\]. Additionally, methods like BanditMIPS have been proposed to reduce dependency on dimensionality by using adaptive sampling strategies inspired by multi-armed bandits, achieving constant-time complexity independent of the input dimension \[[9](http://arxiv.org/abs/2212.07551)\]. Furthermore, diversity-aware approaches have been introduced to enhance user satisfaction by ensuring recommendation diversity, such as the diversity-aware k-MIPS problem and its solutions like IP-Greedy \[[15](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/87bfc511e4ef4a8aeddad903b06036dbe7177625)\]. These methods highlight the ongoing efforts to balance efficiency, accuracy, and diversity in inner product similarity search, with a growing emphasis on hybrid and adaptive techniques that leverage both inner product and Euclidean metrics \[[3](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/386854741f828d30e25a5e01d75e3363d49cad1e)\].
