## Related Works

Economic inequality remains a critical challenge, influencing disparities in education, healthcare, and social stability \[[16](http://arxiv.org/abs/2303.13994)\]. Traditional systems, such as the U.S. federal income tax, aim to reduce inequality but often lack adaptability to heterogeneous taxpayer behaviors and irrational decision-making \[[16](http://arxiv.org/abs/2303.13994)\]. While models like the Saez Optimal Taxation framework attempt to dynamically adjust tax policies, they fail to fully account for the complexity of taxpayer heterogeneity and behavioral irrationality \[[16](http://arxiv.org/abs/2303.13994)\]. This paper introduces TaxAgent, a novel integration of large language models (LLMs) with agent-based modeling (ABM) to design adaptive tax policies \[[16](http://arxiv.org/abs/2303.13994)\]. 

Agent-based modeling (ABM) has been widely used in economic and social policy analysis to simulate complex systems and emergent behaviors \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/2504.01242)\]. ABM allows for the representation of heterogeneous agents with diverse behavioral rules, making it a suitable framework for modeling taxpayer behavior and policy impacts \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/2504.01242)\]. The integration of LLMs into ABM enhances the ability to simulate human-like reasoning and decision-making, offering a more realistic representation of economic agents \[[5](http://arxiv.org/abs/2312.11970)\]. This approach aligns with recent efforts to leverage LLMs in agent-based modeling and simulation, which have shown promise in capturing complex interactions and emergent behaviors \[[5](http://arxiv.org/abs/2312.11970)\].

The use of reinforcement learning (RL) in ABMs has also been explored to improve the accuracy of agent behavioral models \[[6](http://arxiv.org/abs/2006.05048)\]. RL-based agents can learn to outperform traditional adaptive behavioral models by adapting to changing environments and optimizing policy outcomes \[[6](http://arxiv.org/abs/2006.05048)\]. This is particularly relevant to the TaxAgent framework, which utilizes LLMs to iteratively optimize tax rates based on real-time feedback from simulated economic agents \[[16](http://arxiv.org/abs/2303.13994)\]. The ability of LLMs to process and generate natural language makes them well-suited for simulating policy interactions and decision-making processes \[[5](http://arxiv.org/abs/2312.11970)\].

Furthermore, the calibration of ABMs is a critical aspect of ensuring their validity and reliability \[[15](http://arxiv.org/abs/1902.05938)\]. Recent work has focused on developing automatic calibration frameworks that combine dynamic and heterogeneous calibration methods to improve the accuracy of simulation results \[[17](http://arxiv.org/abs/2203.03147)\]. These approaches are essential for ensuring that models like TaxAgent can produce reliable policy recommendations that reflect real-world economic conditions \[[17](http://arxiv.org/abs/2203.03147)\].

In addition, the use of ABMs to study economic inequality and the role of Islamic charity in alleviating it has also been explored \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/1804.09284)\]. These models demonstrate how simple behavioral rules at the micro-level can generate complex patterns at the macro-level, highlighting the potential of ABMs to inform policy design \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/1804.09284)\].
