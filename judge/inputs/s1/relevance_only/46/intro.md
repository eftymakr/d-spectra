## Related Works

Cross-domain sequential recommendation (CDSR) has gained significant attention in recent years due to its ability to alleviate data sparsity and cold-start problems by leveraging user interactions across multiple domains \[[1](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/27670ed18e96550c674e022e284fc5356a7458c2)\]. Traditional approaches to CDSR often rely on self-attention mechanisms within transformer-based models, such as SASRec and Bert4Rec, to capture sequential dependencies \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/2407.11245)\]. However, these methods typically inject domain-specific components as auxiliary modules without thoroughly exploring the potential of the core self-attention mechanism \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/2401.04971)\]. 

Several works have attempted to improve CDSR by incorporating domain-aware modules. For example, \[[4](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/96bfe33a2503e960153a934e10be91da4d8b13a6)\] introduces a shared proxy item encoder to generate universal representations for items across domains, while \[[5](http://arxiv.org/abs/2405.12473)\] proposes a time-interval-aware attention encoder to better model sequential behavior. Additionally, \[[6](http://arxiv.org/abs/2311.04760)\] develops a contrastive learning auxiliary task to enhance cross-domain sequence representation. These methods primarily focus on domain alignment and transfer learning, but they often overlook the importance of dynamically adjusting attention scores to mitigate negative transfer \[[7](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/10715bc315c7c1075b5bf35e603fc08aaf5dff36)\].

Another line of research explores cooperative learning between single- and cross-domain sequential recommendation tasks. For instance, \[[8](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/6dd0605f7de7581c69b4acf0ab78000748a09f3e)\] introduces a cooperative learning framework that compares the performance of models trained on multiple domains with those trained on specific domains to estimate the degree of negative transfer. Similarly, \[[9](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/11da511e9baafc1dfdf58a287c1ead787c9345bc)\] proposes an auxiliary loss that maximizes mutual information between representation pairs from different tasks. These approaches aim to improve knowledge transfer but may not fully address the challenges of aligning item representations and capturing inter-sequence relationships \[[10](http://arxiv.org/abs/2304.03891)\].

Recent studies have also focused on the alignment of item representations in cross-domain settings. \[[11](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/5a653a8060bf9f714866be2823cef71c0fbf9a34)\] proposes a model-agnostic framework called CA-CDSR that achieves sequence-aware generation and adaptive partial alignment for item representations. This work highlights the importance of aligning item representations to enhance sequential modeling and user preference alignment. Furthermore, \[[12](http://arxiv.org/abs/2311.04590)\] introduces a contrastive cross-domain sequential recommendation technique with an attention-aware mechanism to better capture user preferences across domains. These methods emphasize the need for more sophisticated attention mechanisms and better modeling of inter-sequence relationships \[[13](http://arxiv.org/abs/2410.13588)\].

In addition, \[[14](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/c13603992b90529918bd170e2f7a10858a340d43)\] presents a multi-agent reinforcement learning framework for CDSR, where agents select relevant items from source domain sequences to improve target domain recommendations. This approach optimizes the transfer process by coordinating agents' strategies and aligning embeddings between source and target domains.
