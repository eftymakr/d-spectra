## Related Works

Recent advancements in Text-to-Speech (TTS) systems have significantly improved speech naturalness, enabling high-quality speech synthesis that is often indistinguishable from human speech \[[24](http://arxiv.org/abs/2009.06775)\]. However, precise prosody control and mispronunciation correction remain challenging. Traditional approaches for prosody manipulation often rely on specialized modules or require additional training, which limits their flexibility for post-hoc adjustments \[[24](http://arxiv.org/abs/2009.06775)\]. Similarly, conventional mispronunciation correction methods depend on grapheme-to-phoneme dictionaries, which are less practical in low-resource settings \[[24](http://arxiv.org/abs/2009.06775)\].

Several studies have explored methods for improving prosody control in TTS. For instance, \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/1909.10302)\] introduced a fully automatic method to make the system aware of prosody, enabling sentence-wise control of speaking pace and expressiveness. This work also proposed a novel augmented attention mechanism to enhance pace control sensitivity. Similarly, \[[14](http://arxiv.org/abs/2110.02952)\] proposed a hierarchical prosody modeling approach for non-autoregressive TTS, allowing for fine-grained control over prosody dimensions. These methods, however, often require specific training or integration into the model architecture, which limits their applicability for post-hoc adjustments \[[14](http://arxiv.org/abs/2110.02952)\].

In the context of mispronunciation correction, \[[29](http://arxiv.org/abs/2202.07200)\] explored unsupervised word-level prosody tagging for controllable speech synthesis. This approach enables the manipulation of word-level prosody without requiring explicit prosody tags during training. However, such methods are still limited in their ability to correct mispronunciations in a post-hoc manner \[[29](http://arxiv.org/abs/2202.07200)\].

Recent work has also focused on improving the controllability of TTS systems through explicit prosody features. \[[18](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/230fcc0981965a56242ef5990e64e59fe32a7518)\] proposed an end-to-end TTS system that enables emphasis prosody using explicit symbols, allowing for fine-grained control over the speech output. This system was trained using emphasis tags generated by comparing professional voice actor readings with synthesized speech. The results demonstrated that explicit prosody control can significantly improve the naturalness of synthesized speech \[[18](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/230fcc0981965a56242ef5990e64e59fe32a7518)\].

Another notable approach is the use of neural vocoders and sequence-to-sequence models for prosody modeling. \[[24](http://arxiv.org/abs/2009.06775)\] introduced a sequence-to-sequence neural network conditioned on acoustic speech features to learn a latent prosody space with intuitive dimensions. This model enabled the generation of a wide variety of speaking styles while maintaining high speech quality \[[24](http://arxiv.org/abs/2009.06775)\].
