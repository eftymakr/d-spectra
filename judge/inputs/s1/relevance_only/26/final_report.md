## Related Works

Differential privacy (DP) has become the de facto standard for privacy-preserving data analysis, offering rigorous mathematical guarantees against privacy breaches \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2406.18940)\]. However, its implementation is vulnerable to malicious behaviors by servers, particularly in distributed settings, where servers may sample noise from incorrect distributions or generate correlated noise while appearing to follow established protocols \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2406.18940)\]. These vulnerabilities have been explored in several prior works.

Several studies have focused on improving the utility of DP mechanisms while maintaining privacy guarantees. For instance, \[[17](http://arxiv.org/abs/2410.18404)\] introduces Bayesian Coordinate Differential Privacy (BCDP), which enables feature-specific privacy quantification, improving performance of downstream tasks without compromising privacy. Similarly, \[[20](http://arxiv.org/abs/1807.11317)\] proposes utility-optimized local differential privacy (ULDP) mechanisms that provide equivalent privacy guarantees to LDP only for sensitive data, thereby reducing unnecessary obfuscation and improving utility.

The problem of malicious server behavior has also been addressed in the context of local differential privacy (LDP). \[[4](http://arxiv.org/abs/2104.06569)\] shows that LDP is vulnerable to malicious clients who can perform both input and output manipulation attacks, and proposes verifiable randomization mechanisms to prevent such attacks. \[[10](http://arxiv.org/abs/1911.02046)\] further explores data poisoning attacks on LDP protocols, where attackers inject fake users to skew the results of frequency estimation and heavy hitter identification tasks. These attacks highlight the need for robust verification mechanisms to ensure correct execution of DP protocols.

To address these challenges, \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2406.18940)\] proposes a novel definition called Verifiable Distributed Differential Privacy (VDDP), which incorporates additional verification mechanisms to ensure that servers correctly execute DP protocols. This work also explores the relationship between zero-knowledge proofs (ZKP) and DP, demonstrating that while ZKPs are sufficient for achieving DP under verifiability requirements, they are not necessary. This insight is crucial for designing efficient and practical verification mechanisms.

In terms of efficient mechanisms, \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2406.18940)\] introduces two novel and efficient VDDP-compliant mechanisms. The first is the Verifiable Distributed Discrete Laplacian Mechanism (VDDLM), which offers up to $4 \times 10^5$x improvement in proof generation efficiency with only 0.1–0.2x error compared to the previous state-of-the-art verifiable differentially private mechanism. The second is an improved solution to Verifiable Randomized Response (VRR) under local DP, achieving up to a 5000x reduction in communication costs and the verifier's overhead. These mechanisms are designed to be both efficient and practical, making them suitable for real-world deployment.

The category "Verification Mechanisms in DP" explores the challenges and solutions related to ensuring the correctness and reliability of differential privacy (DP) mechanisms, particularly in the presence of adversarial behavior. Existing DP frameworks, such as the local model, are vulnerable to malicious clients who can manipulate inputs or outputs to skew results \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2406.18940)\]. To address these vulnerabilities, researchers have proposed verifiable DP (VD) schemes that allow clients to verify the correctness of the DP mechanism without revealing sensitive information \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/2208.09011)\]. These schemes often rely on zero-knowledge proofs (ZKPs) to ensure that the output adheres to DP guarantees while maintaining privacy \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/2208.09011)\]. However, ZKPs are not strictly necessary, as alternative approaches can also achieve verifiability \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/2208.09011)\]. In addition to ZKP-based methods, recent work has focused on improving the efficiency and usability of verification tools, such as LightDP and ShadowDP, which provide formal verification of DP algorithms with minimal programmer annotations \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/2409.10667)\]\[[8](http://arxiv.org/abs/1607.08228)\]. These tools leverage novel type systems and proof techniques to ensure correctness while reducing the burden on developers \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/2409.10667)\]\[[8](http://arxiv.org/abs/1607.08228)\]. Furthermore, the shuffle model of DP has been explored for its potential to enhance privacy by disconnecting clients from the aggregator, although it introduces new verification challenges \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2406.18940)\]. Overall, the field is moving toward more practical and scalable verification mechanisms that balance privacy, correctness, and usability \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2406.18940)\]\[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/2208.09011)\]\[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/2409.10667)\].

### <a id='category-local-differential-privacy-challenges'></a>Local Differential Privacy Challenges: 5 papers found.
Local Differential Privacy (LDP) has been widely studied for its ability to protect individual privacy by perturbing data at the source before aggregation \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2406.18940)\]. However, recent research highlights several challenges, including vulnerabilities to malicious data providers who can manipulate outputs to skew estimations \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2406.18940)\]. This issue arises because data collectors rely on randomized data to infer background distributions, making them susceptible to attacks that exploit the normalization process within LDP protocols \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2406.18940)\]. To address these concerns, some studies propose verifiable randomization mechanisms that allow data collectors to verify the correctness of randomization applied by each data provider, thereby preventing output manipulation and enhancing protocol security \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2406.18940)\]. Additionally, there is ongoing work to improve the accuracy of LDP mechanisms, especially for multidimensional data, where existing methods often suffer from high noise variance \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/2208.09011)\]. Researchers have introduced novel LDP mechanisms that maintain or improve accuracy while ensuring privacy, and these have been extended to handle both numeric and categorical attributes \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/2208.09011)\]. Furthermore, LDP is being integrated into machine learning frameworks, such as stochastic gradient descent, to enable privacy-preserving training in federated learning settings \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/2208.09011)\]. In parallel, studies have explored how users perceive different privacy mechanisms, finding that explanations focusing on the implications of LDP lead to better comprehension and greater willingness to share data compared to technical descriptions of DP or LDP \[[4](http://arxiv.org/abs/2104.06569)\]. Another challenge involves the uniform application of privacy guarantees across all data features, which can degrade downstream task performance. To mitigate this, a Bayesian framework called Bayesian Coordinate Differential Privacy (BCDP) has been proposed, allowing for feature-specific privacy quantification and improved accuracy without compromising privacy \[[5](http://arxiv.org/abs/2404.15686)\]. These developments reflect the growing interest in refining LDP to address both security and utility challenges in real-world applications.

### <a id='category-efficient-dp-mechanism-improvements'></a>Efficient DP Mechanism Improvements: 3 papers found.
Efficient improvements in differential privacy (DP) mechanisms have been a focal point in recent research, particularly in addressing the trade-off between privacy and data utility. Traditional DP methods often add excessive noise, especially to small datasets, leading to significant utility loss \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2406.18940)\]. To mitigate this, per-instance DP (pDP) has been introduced, where privacy loss is measured individually for each data instance, allowing for tailored noise optimization \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2406.18940)\]. This approach is framed as a sequential game, with the Nash equilibrium ensuring pDP for all instances \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2406.18940)\]. Additionally, existing DP implementations often assume one-to-one mappings between individuals and database records, which is not practical in real-world scenarios \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/2208.09011)\]. A generic and scalable method has been proposed to handle cases where individuals may be associated with multiple records, implemented as an operator in relational algebra and tested on industry benchmarks \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/2208.09011)\]. Furthermore, the local model of DP, while privacy-preserving, sacrifices utility and algorithmic expressibility compared to the central model \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/2409.10667)\]. To address this, Cryptε was introduced, enabling the accuracy and expressibility of the central model without requiring a trusted data collector by utilizing two non-colluding untrusted servers and encrypted data \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/2409.10667)\]. These advancements highlight the ongoing efforts to enhance DP mechanisms, ensuring both privacy and utility in practical applications.

The category "Privacy-Utility Trade-offs" explores the challenges of balancing privacy protection with data utility in differential privacy (DP) frameworks. Existing research highlights that traditional DP mechanisms, such as differential privacy, provide uniform privacy guarantees, which may not align with individual user expectations, leading to either over-protection or under-protection \[[6](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/e40f49994b0d5122e2c45ee04e38defb973a9d52)\]. To address this, personalized differential privacy (PDP) has been proposed, allowing users to specify their own privacy requirements, thereby improving utility by avoiding unnecessary noise addition \[[6](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/e40f49994b0d5122e2c45ee04e38defb973a9d52)\]. Additionally, studies have examined discrete noise-adding mechanisms for discretely distributed data, establishing conditions for differential privacy and optimizing utility by characterizing the similarity between input and output data distributions \[[18](http://arxiv.org/abs/2203.10323)\]. In the context of local differential privacy (LDP), researchers have introduced utility-optimized LDP (ULDP) to reduce excessive obfuscation, particularly for non-sensitive data, thereby enhancing utility without compromising privacy \[[20](http://arxiv.org/abs/1807.11317)\]. Furthermore, in dynamic systems such as smart grids or intelligent transportation, differential privacy has been extended to ensure privacy while maintaining the accuracy of filtered signals, demonstrating its applicability in real-time data processing \[[21](http://arxiv.org/abs/1207.4305)\]. These contributions collectively emphasize the ongoing efforts to refine DP mechanisms to better accommodate diverse privacy needs and maximize data utility.

The premise of this first vulnerability is similar to the one carried out by Mironov in 2011 against the Laplace mechanism. In the second part of the paper we study discrete counterparts of the Laplace and Gaussian mechanisms that were previously proposed to alleviate the shortcomings of floating-point representation of real numbers.
