## Related Works

Multi-Domain Recommendation (MDR) has emerged as a promising approach to enhance recommendation performance by leveraging knowledge transfer across different domains \[[11](http://arxiv.org/abs/2103.01696)\]. Traditional MDR methods often assume a single transfer structure, which may not be effective when domain-specific knowledge conflicts or when some domains are of poor quality \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2201.05973)\]. This can lead to the Negative Transfer Problem (NTP), where unselective knowledge transfer degrades recommendation accuracy \[[15](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/cdc38867ade499e320e4ed3f93473a7cd67ecad4)\]. To address this, recent works have focused on improving domain alignment and selecting relevant domains for knowledge transfer \[[10](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/9c3180d1be4710f24fc6035db30df9d0da306ada)\].

Several approaches have been proposed to mitigate NTP by aligning domain preferences. For instance, \[[10](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/9c3180d1be4710f24fc6035db30df9d0da306ada)\] introduces TrineCDR, which enhances knowledge transferability at three levels: feature, interaction, and domain. This method identifies three fundamental causes of negative transfer and improves the transferability of knowledge by filtering out noise and irrelevant information. Similarly, \[[15](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/cdc38867ade499e320e4ed3f93473a7cd67ecad4)\] proposes AMLCDR, which aligns user preference distributions across domains using a domain adaptation network. This ensures that user embeddings in the target domain align with item embeddings, thereby improving recommendation quality. These methods highlight the importance of domain alignment and selective knowledge transfer \[[10](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/9c3180d1be4710f24fc6035db30df9d0da306ada)\]\[[15](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/cdc38867ade499e320e4ed3f93473a7cd67ecad4)\].

In addition to domain alignment, some works have focused on disentangling domain-specific and domain-shared representations. For example, \[[16](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/4bfdfc54c7a4714fe4fefb8af2cd87bbe90ed9e7)\] proposes DDCDR, a disentangle-based distillation framework that separates domain-shared from domain-specific representations. This enables more effective knowledge transfer by preserving domain-specific insights while leveraging shared knowledge. Similarly, \[[5](http://arxiv.org/abs/2308.05508)\] introduces EDDA, which uses embedding disentangling and domain alignment to improve knowledge transfer across domains with small overlaps. These approaches demonstrate the effectiveness of disentangling and aligning representations for better cross-domain performance \[[5](http://arxiv.org/abs/2308.05508)\]\[[16](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/4bfdfc54c7a4714fe4fefb8af2cd87bbe90ed9e7)\].

Another line of research has explored multi-view and adaptive preference learning for cross-domain recommendation. \[[6](http://arxiv.org/abs/2410.05877)\] proposes MDAP, a framework that uses a multi-view encoder to capture diverse user preferences and a gated decoder to adaptively combine embeddings from different views. This enhances the adaptability and effectiveness of cross-domain recommendations. \[[13](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/e776b35d50f07998e0c4fdca6bfe1f21c364fddf)\] introduces AutoTransfer, which selectively transfers instances from source to target domains using an instance transfer policy network. This method improves recommendation performance by focusing on transferable and informative instances \[[6](http://arxiv.org/abs/2410.05877)\]\[[13](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/e776b35d50f07998e0c4fdca6bfe1f21c364fddf)\].

Furthermore, \[[12](http://arxiv.org/abs/2202.12524)\] presents MAMDR, a model-agnostic learning framework that enables scalable multi-domain recommendation. This framework addresses domain conflicts and overfitting by using domain negotiation and domain regularization strategies.
