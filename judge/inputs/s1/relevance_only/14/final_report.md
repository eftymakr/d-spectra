## Related Works

Video super-resolution (VSR) has been a focal point in computer vision, aiming to reconstruct high-resolution (HR) videos from low-resolution (LR) inputs. Early approaches relied on traditional image restoration techniques, but recent advances in deep learning, particularly diffusion models, have significantly improved the quality of generated videos \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2312.00853)\]. However, existing diffusion-based VSR methods often introduce artifacts and complex degradations due to their inherent randomness \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/2311.15908)\]. This issue is exacerbated in real-world scenarios where videos suffer from various degradations such as compression artifacts, noise, and misalignment \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/2502.03502)\].

To address these challenges, several works have focused on improving temporal consistency and reducing artifacts. For instance, MGLD \[[4](http://arxiv.org/abs/2501.10110)\] introduces a motion-guided loss to ensure content consistency across frames, while StableVSR \[[5](http://arxiv.org/abs/2502.07381)\] employs a Temporal Conditioning Module (TCM) to synthesize temporally consistent details. Both methods emphasize the importance of temporal alignment and content coherence in generating high-quality videos. Additionally, DC-VSR \[[6](http://arxiv.org/abs/2211.01639)\] proposes a novel approach to achieve spatial and temporal consistency by leveraging spatial and temporal attention propagation schemes. These methods demonstrate that temporal modeling is crucial for achieving perceptual quality in VSR.

Another line of research explores the use of recurrent architectures to model temporal dependencies. For example, the Recurrent Residual Network (RRN) \[[7](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/9b0a12d1641d92469426d6c19b5e90cb06de2896)\] utilizes residual learning to stabilize the training of recurrent neural networks (RNNs) and improve super-resolution performance. Similarly, the Sliding Window Recurrent Network (SWRN) \[[8](http://arxiv.org/abs/2008.05765)\] enables real-time inference by efficiently aggregating temporal cues across frames. These methods highlight the potential of recurrent networks in capturing long-term dependencies in video sequences.

Recent works have also focused on reducing computational complexity while maintaining high performance. The Frames-Shift Diffusion Model \[[9](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/be7e2dd603587f9707f84a6f0402ca6e0451f857)\] simplifies the integration of temporal data by redesigning the diffusion process of image models without introducing complex temporal modules. This approach allows for efficient video super-resolution while maintaining perceptual quality. Additionally, the RealVSR dataset \[[10](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/c6a8ff4d9dcdf9e5d82310b847af999ed6655f65)\] was introduced to bridge the gap between synthetic and real-world VSR tasks, providing a benchmark for evaluating the effectiveness of VSR models in real-world settings.

In summary, while diffusion models have shown great promise in VSR, challenges remain in ensuring temporal consistency and reducing artifacts. Recent advancements in diffusion models have significantly impacted video super-resolution (VSR) by enabling the generation of high-quality, temporally consistent videos from low-resolution inputs \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2312.00853)\]. However, early approaches faced challenges in maintaining content consistency across frames due to the inherent randomness in diffusion processes \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/2311.15908)\]. To address these issues, researchers have introduced various techniques, such as motion-guided loss functions and temporal modules, to enhance the coherence of generated videos \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2312.00853)\]. Additionally, methods like the Temporal Conditioning Module (TCM) have been proposed to incorporate spatially-aligned texture information from adjacent frames, improving the perceptual quality and temporal consistency of the output \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/2311.15908)\]. Furthermore, the introduction of the Frame-wise Bidirectional Sampling strategy has allowed models to utilize information from both past and future frames, further enhancing the quality of generated videos \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/2311.15908)\]. Another approach, the Progressive Learning Strategy (PLS), has been developed to systematically decompose the learning burden, enabling better performance on severely degraded videos \[[4](http://arxiv.org/abs/2501.10110)\]. This strategy, combined with an Interweaved Latent Transition (ILT) technique, has shown promising results in maintaining temporal consistency without additional training overhead \[[4](http://arxiv.org/abs/2501.10110)\]. Moreover, the use of diffusion models for compressed video super-resolution has gained attention, with methods like the Spatial Degradation-Aware and Temporal Consistent (SDATC) diffusion model being proposed to mitigate spatial distortions and refine temporal consistency \[[5](http://arxiv.org/abs/2502.07381)\]. These methods incorporate modules such as the distortion control module (DCM) and the compression-aware prompt module (CAPM) to adapt to different degradation levels and enhance the quality of reconstructed videos \[[5](http://arxiv.org/abs/2502.07381)\]. Additionally, the Frames-Shift Diffusion Model has been introduced to simplify the integration of temporal data into image-based diffusion models, achieving efficient video super-resolution while maintaining computational efficiency \[[7](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/9b0a12d1641d92469426d6c19b5e90cb06de2896)\]. Overall, these approaches highlight the ongoing efforts to improve the perceptual quality and temporal consistency of diffusion-based VSR methods, addressing the challenges posed by real-world video degradation \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2312.00853)\]\[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/2311.15908)\]\[[4](http://arxiv.org/abs/2501.10110)\]\[[5](http://arxiv.org/abs/2502.07381)\]\[[7](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/9b0a12d1641d92469426d6c19b5e90cb06de2896)\].

### <a id='category-spatio-temporal-attention-mechanisms'></a>Spatio-Temporal Attention Mechanisms: 8 papers found.
Video super-resolution (VSR) has been a focal point in recent research, with a growing emphasis on spatio-temporal attention mechanisms to address challenges such as spatial and temporal consistency, artifact propagation, and computational efficiency \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/2502.03502)\]. Existing methods often struggle with maintaining coherence across frames due to inherent randomness in diffusion models or limitations in fusing spatial-temporal information \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/2502.03502)\]. To overcome these issues, several approaches have been proposed, such as DC-VSR, which introduces Spatial Attention Propagation (SAP) and Temporal Attention Propagation (TAP) to ensure consistency across tiles \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/2502.03502)\]. Similarly, the Collaborative Transformer for Video Super-Resolution (CTVSR) integrates Transformer-based and recurrent-based models to better handle long-range dependencies while reducing computational overhead \[[9](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/be7e2dd603587f9707f84a6f0402ca6e0451f857)\]. Another approach, the Trajectory-aware Transformer for Video Super-Resolution (TTVSR), enhances spatio-temporal learning by aligning video frames into trajectories and applying self-attention selectively along these trajectories \[[17](http://arxiv.org/abs/2204.04216)\]. Additionally, the Video Restoration Transformer (VRT) leverages parallel frame prediction and temporal reciprocal self attention (TRSA) to improve robustness against motion and misalignment \[[30](http://arxiv.org/abs/2201.12288)\]. These methods collectively highlight the importance of spatio-temporal attention in achieving high-quality, artifact-free VSR results, with varying strategies to balance performance and efficiency \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/2502.03502)\]\[[9](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/be7e2dd603587f9707f84a6f0402ca6e0451f857)\]\[[17](http://arxiv.org/abs/2204.04216)\]\[[30](http://arxiv.org/abs/2201.12288)\].

[Snippet]: «Video super-resolution (VSR) aims to improve the spatial resolution of low-resolution (LR) videos. Existing VSR methods are mostly trained and evaluated on synthetic datasets, where the LR videos are uniformly downsampled from their high-resolution (HR) counterparts by some simple operators (e.g., bicubic downsampling). Such simple synthetic degradation models, however, cannot well describe the complex degradation processes in real-world videos, and thus the trained VSR models become ineffective in real-world applications. As an attempt to bridge the gap, we build a real-world video super-resolution (RealVSR) dataset by capturing paired LR-HR video sequences using the multi-camera system of iPhone 11 Pro Max. Since the LR-HR video pairs are captured by two separate cameras, there are inevitably certain misalignment and luminance/color differences between them. To more robustly train the VSR model and recover more details from the LR inputs, we convert the LR-HR videos into YCbCr space and decompose the luminance channel into a Laplacian pyramid, and then apply different loss functions to different components. Experiments validate that VSR models trained on our RealVSR dataset demonstrate better visual quality than those trained on synthetic datasets under real-world settings. They also exhibit good generalization capability in cross-camera tests. The dataset and code can be found at https://github.com/IanYeung/RealVSR.»

[Snippet]: «Recent single-image super-resolution (SISR) networks, which can adapt their network parameters to specific input images, have shown promising results by exploiting the information available within the input data as well as large external datasets. However, the extension of these self-supervised SISR approaches to video handling has yet to be studied. Thus, we present a new learning algorithm that allows conventional video super-resolution (VSR) networks to adapt their parameters to test video frames without using the ground-truth datasets. By utilizing many self-similar patches across space and time, we improve the performance of fully pre-trained VSR networks and produce temporally consistent video frames. Moreover, we present a test-time knowledge distillation technique that accelerates the adaptation speed with less hardware resources. In our experiments, we demonstrate that our novel learning algorithm can fine-tune state-of-the-art VSR networks and substantially elevate performance on numerous benchmark datasets.»
