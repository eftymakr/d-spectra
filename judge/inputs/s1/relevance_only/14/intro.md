## Related Works

Video super-resolution (VSR) has been a focal point in computer vision, aiming to reconstruct high-resolution (HR) videos from low-resolution (LR) inputs. Early approaches relied on traditional image restoration techniques, but recent advances in deep learning, particularly diffusion models, have significantly improved the quality of generated videos \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2312.00853)\]. However, existing diffusion-based VSR methods often introduce artifacts and complex degradations due to their inherent randomness \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/2311.15908)\]. This issue is exacerbated in real-world scenarios where videos suffer from various degradations such as compression artifacts, noise, and misalignment \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/2502.03502)\].

To address these challenges, several works have focused on improving temporal consistency and reducing artifacts. For instance, MGLD \[[4](http://arxiv.org/abs/2501.10110)\] introduces a motion-guided loss to ensure content consistency across frames, while StableVSR \[[5](http://arxiv.org/abs/2502.07381)\] employs a Temporal Conditioning Module (TCM) to synthesize temporally consistent details. Both methods emphasize the importance of temporal alignment and content coherence in generating high-quality videos. Additionally, DC-VSR \[[6](http://arxiv.org/abs/2211.01639)\] proposes a novel approach to achieve spatial and temporal consistency by leveraging spatial and temporal attention propagation schemes. These methods demonstrate that temporal modeling is crucial for achieving perceptual quality in VSR.

Another line of research explores the use of recurrent architectures to model temporal dependencies. For example, the Recurrent Residual Network (RRN) \[[7](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/9b0a12d1641d92469426d6c19b5e90cb06de2896)\] utilizes residual learning to stabilize the training of recurrent neural networks (RNNs) and improve super-resolution performance. Similarly, the Sliding Window Recurrent Network (SWRN) \[[8](http://arxiv.org/abs/2008.05765)\] enables real-time inference by efficiently aggregating temporal cues across frames. These methods highlight the potential of recurrent networks in capturing long-term dependencies in video sequences.

Recent works have also focused on reducing computational complexity while maintaining high performance. The Frames-Shift Diffusion Model \[[9](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/be7e2dd603587f9707f84a6f0402ca6e0451f857)\] simplifies the integration of temporal data by redesigning the diffusion process of image models without introducing complex temporal modules. This approach allows for efficient video super-resolution while maintaining perceptual quality. Additionally, the RealVSR dataset \[[10](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/c6a8ff4d9dcdf9e5d82310b847af999ed6655f65)\] was introduced to bridge the gap between synthetic and real-world VSR tasks, providing a benchmark for evaluating the effectiveness of VSR models in real-world settings.

In summary, while diffusion models have shown great promise in VSR, challenges remain in ensuring temporal consistency and reducing artifacts.
