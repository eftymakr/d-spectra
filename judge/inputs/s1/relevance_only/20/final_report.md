## Related Works

Plug-and-Play (PnP) and deep unrolling approaches have emerged as dominant paradigms for solving inverse problems in imaging, particularly in single-pixel imaging (SPI) \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2201.13256)\]. PnP methods integrate pre-trained deep denoisers into proximal algorithms such as Proximal Gradient Descent (PGD) or Alternating Direction Method of Multipliers (ADMM) to perform regularization \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/2311.01216)\]. These methods are flexible for varying compression ratios (CRs) but often suffer from limited reconstruction accuracy and speed \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/1809.04693)\]. On the other hand, unrolling approaches transform iterative optimization processes into end-to-end trainable neural networks, typically achieving better accuracy and faster inference \[[4](http://arxiv.org/abs/2301.13731)\]. However, they require fine-tuning or retraining when CRs change \[[5](http://arxiv.org/abs/2402.01779)\].

Several works have explored the convergence properties of PnP algorithms. For instance, \[[6](http://arxiv.org/abs/2207.07321)\] shows that when the denoiser is trained as a gradient descent step, it can correspond to the proximal operator of another scalar function, enabling convergence for PnP-PGD and PnP-ADMM. Additionally, \[[7](http://arxiv.org/abs/2012.05703)\] presents a novel convergence proof for PnP-DRS that does not impose restrictive conditions on the regularization parameter, improving the flexibility of these methods. \[[8](http://arxiv.org/abs/2204.13940)\] further examines a relaxed version of PGD, which converges across a broader range of regularization parameters, enhancing the restoration capacity of PnP algorithms.

Unrolling methods have also been extensively studied. \[[9](http://arxiv.org/abs/2002.09611)\] introduces an online PnP algorithm based on the proximal gradient method (PGM), which uses a subset of measurements at every iteration, making it scalable to large datasets. \[[10](http://arxiv.org/abs/2104.10348)\] proposes a convergent PnP algorithm that uses a relaxed version of PGD, allowing for more accurate image restoration. \[[11](http://arxiv.org/abs/1704.03488)\] discusses the use of deep denoisers as implicit regularizers within proximal algorithms, showing that they can achieve state-of-the-art results in image reconstruction tasks.

Recent works have focused on combining the strengths of both PnP and unrolling approaches. \[[12](http://arxiv.org/abs/2202.05820)\] presents a tuning-free PnP proximal algorithm that automatically determines internal parameters such as penalty parameters and denoising strength, improving the efficiency and effectiveness of these methods. \[[13](http://arxiv.org/abs/2212.06881)\] introduces a general convergent PnP framework based on primal-dual splitting (PDS), which efficiently handles a broad class of image restoration problems with guaranteed theoretical convergence.

Plug-and-play (PnP) algorithm is a powerful framework for sparse regularization problems, which plug any advanced denoiser into traditional proximal algorithms. In this paper, we propose a PnP-ADMM algorithm for CS-MRI reconstruction with CNC sparse regularization. We first obtain the proximal operator for CNC sparse regularization. Then we present PnP-ADMM algorithm by replacing the proximal operator of ADMM with properly pre-trained denoisers. Furthermore, we conduct comparison experiments using various denoisers under different sampling templates for different images. The experimental results verify the effectiveness of the proposed algorithm with both numerical criteria and visual effects
