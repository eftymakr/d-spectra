## Related Works

The integration of knowledge graphs (KGs) into large language models (LLMs) for enhancing reasoning capabilities in claim verification has gained significant attention in recent research. While LLMs have demonstrated strong natural language understanding and generation abilities, their performance on complex reasoning tasks remains limited, particularly when dealing with structured knowledge \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2305.13168)\]. KGs, on the other hand, provide semantically rich, structured representations that are well-suited for reasoning tasks \[[7](http://arxiv.org/abs/2306.08302)\]. However, most existing claim verification methods rely on unstructured text corpora, which limits their ability to effectively leverage the structured knowledge provided by KGs \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2305.13168)\].

Several studies have explored the synthesis of LLMs and KGs for QA tasks, aiming to address the limitations of LLMs in reasoning and factual accuracy \[[4](http://arxiv.org/abs/2505.20099)\]. For instance, the R2-KG framework introduces a dual-agent approach that separates reasoning into an Operator and a Supervisor, enhancing reliability and reducing dependency on high-capacity LLMs \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/2502.12767)\]. Similarly, the KELM model integrates KGs into pre-trained language models through a hierarchical relational-graph-based message passing mechanism, enabling dynamic knowledge context embedding \[[13](http://arxiv.org/abs/2109.04223)\]. These approaches highlight the potential of combining LLMs with KGs for improved reasoning and factual accuracy.

The use of KGs for claim verification has also been explored in the context of explainable fact-checking, where KGs provide formal representations of knowledge that can be used to generate interpretable explanations \[[10](http://arxiv.org/abs/1906.09198)\]. The FOLK reasoning approach leverages first-order logic to translate claims into logical clauses, enabling structured reasoning over knowledge-grounded question-and-answer pairs \[[16](http://arxiv.org/abs/2310.05253)\]. This aligns with the broader goal of integrating structured knowledge into LLMs to improve their reasoning capabilities.

Furthermore, the KGLens framework has been developed to efficiently measure the alignment between KGs and LLMs, enabling the identification of knowledge blind spots and improving factual accuracy \[[5](http://arxiv.org/abs/2312.11539)\]. This work demonstrates the importance of aligning LLMs with structured knowledge sources to enhance their reliability in claim verification tasks.

In addition, the use of KGs for multi-document question answering has been explored, where knowledge graph prompting methods are used to construct and traverse knowledge graphs to gather relevant information \[[17](http://arxiv.org/abs/2308.11730)\]. These methods highlight the potential of KGs in enhancing the retrieval and reasoning capabilities of LLMs in complex tasks.
