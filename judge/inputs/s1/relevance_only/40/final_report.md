## Related Works

Zero-Knowledge Proofs (ZKPs) have emerged as a critical cryptographic primitive for privacy-preserving computation, enabling a prover to demonstrate the truth of a statement without revealing additional information \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2408.05890)\]. Despite their potential, the computational complexity of ZKP generation has limited their adoption in real-world applications \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2408.05890)\]. Recent efforts have focused on accelerating the key operations in ZKP protocols, particularly on GPU and ASIC platforms \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2408.05890)\]. For instance, GZKP presents a GPU-accelerated ZKP system that improves performance by eliminating costly external shuffles and optimizing memory access patterns \[[2](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/e149d93531d8c07cf43f6cae327a4795144d1304)\]. Similarly, cuZK introduces a faster parallel multi-scalar multiplication (MSM) algorithm on GPUs, achieving nearly linear speedup over the Pippenger algorithm \[[6](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/e4a4608e8e35379e63b9df5bda1b7599ee8ab9ac)\]. These works highlight the importance of optimizing the computationally intensive primitives such as NTT and MSM, which are central to ZKP generation \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2408.05890)\].

Several studies have explored hardware acceleration for ZKP. SZKP is the first ASIC to accelerate an entire proof on-chip, leveraging structured dataflows for both NTT and MSM, achieving speedups of over 400× compared to CPU implementations \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2408.05890)\]. ReZK, on the other hand, presents a highly reconfigurable accelerator that supports variable sizes and bit-widths for NTT/INTT and MSM operations, enhancing flexibility and performance \[[9](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/8b12309fd3c5a1851ff8b6d9cfff018811d08d93)\]. PipeZK introduces a pipelined architecture with two subsystems to handle large polynomial computations and MSMs, achieving 10× speedup on standard benchmarks \[[5](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/6ee84674b7c426849c3da56cb278c08eb7a903cf)\]. These accelerators demonstrate the potential of specialized hardware to significantly reduce the time required for ZKP generation.

However, many existing accelerators face limitations, such as requiring a trusted setup for each application or generating larger proof sizes with higher verification costs \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2408.05890)\]. For example, some protocols, like Groth16, require a trusted setup and produce proofs that are not suitable for scenarios with numerous verifiers or strict verification time constraints \[[6](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/e4a4608e8e35379e63b9df5bda1b7599ee8ab9ac)\]. In contrast, HyperPlonk, the protocol accelerated by zkSpeed, supports one-time, universal setup and generates small proof sizes, making it more suitable for publicly verifiable systems \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2408.05890)\]. This distinction is crucial for applications requiring high throughput and low verification latency.

In addition to hardware acceleration, there are ongoing efforts to improve the usability and performance of ZKP frameworks. A systematic survey of 25 general-purpose ZKP frameworks highlights the need for better usability and performance evaluation \[[4](http://arxiv.org/abs/2502.07063)\]. The survey assesses frameworks based on their usability and performance

These primitives are critical to the proof generation process, which is often the most computationally intensive part of ZKP protocols \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/2412.12481)\]. Prior research has explored hardware acceleration for these operations, with notable efforts including GPU implementations and custom ASIC designs \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2408.05890)\]. For example, cuZK introduces a new parallel MSM algorithm that achieves nearly perfect linear speedup over the Pippenger algorithm, significantly improving performance \[[6](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/e4a4608e8e35379e63b9df5bda1b7599ee8ab9ac)\]. Similarly, ReZK proposes a reconfigurable accelerator that can handle NTT/INTT and MSM operations with variable sizes and bit-widths, offering flexibility for different elliptic curves \[[9](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/8b12309fd3c5a1851ff8b6d9cfff018811d08d93)\]. Additionally, FPGA-based solutions have been developed to exploit the inherent parallelism in MSM, achieving speedups of up to 150x over software implementations \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/2412.12481)\]. These advancements highlight the importance of optimizing NTT and MSM to reduce the computational overhead of ZKP systems, enabling broader adoption in privacy-preserving applications \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2408.05890)\]\[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/2412.12481)\]\[[6](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/e4a4608e8e35379e63b9df5bda1b7599ee8ab9ac)\]\[[9](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/8b12309fd3c5a1851ff8b6d9cfff018811d08d93)\].

### <a id='category-zkp-fundamentals-and-applications'></a>ZKP Fundamentals and Applications: 5 papers found.
Zero-knowledge proofs (ZKPs) have emerged as a critical tool for privacy-preserving computations, enabling parties to verify the truth of a statement without disclosing additional information \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2408.05890)\]. Their applications span various domains, including blockchain technologies, verifiable machine learning, and secure electronic voting \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2408.05890)\]. However, the computational complexity of ZKP protocols has hindered their widespread adoption \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2408.05890)\]. Recent advancements have focused on optimizing key primitives such as SumCheck and Multi-scalar Multiplications (MSMs) to improve performance on hardware accelerators like GPUs and ASICs \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2408.05890)\]. Despite these improvements, many protocols still require a trusted setup or generate large proof sizes, limiting their use in scenarios with numerous verifiers or strict time constraints \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2408.05890)\]. To address these challenges, researchers have developed new ZKP schemes that leverage intractable problems in group theory, offering more secure and efficient alternatives \[[2](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/e149d93531d8c07cf43f6cae327a4795144d1304)\]. Additionally, there is growing interest in quantum-resistant ZKP protocols, such as relativistic ZKP schemes that integrate special relativity and quantum theory to resist quantum attacks \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/2412.12481)\]. These developments highlight the ongoing efforts to enhance the efficiency, security, and practicality of ZKP systems for real-world applications \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2408.05890)\]\[[2](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/e149d93531d8c07cf43f6cae327a4795144d1304)\]\[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/2412.12481)\].
