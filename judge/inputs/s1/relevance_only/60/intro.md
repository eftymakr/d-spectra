## Related Works

Ranking models traditionally focus on modeling the relative order of predictions, often at the expense of the accuracy of their absolute values \[[9](http://arxiv.org/abs/2406.08010)\]. This limitation has led to a growing body of work on calibrating ranking models to ensure both accurate relative ordering and reliable absolute predictions \[[3](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/7f6bb146b9c1504ec77100e640d624bdc204f0ac)\]. Calibration in ranking tasks is particularly challenging because existing methods often rely on predefined transformation functions with order-preserving properties, such as piece-wise linear functions \[[3](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/7f6bb146b9c1504ec77100e640d624bdc204f0ac)\]. These fixed forms, while simple, exhibit limited expressiveness and flexibility, which restricts their effectiveness in complex calibration scenarios \[[9](http://arxiv.org/abs/2406.08010)\].

Several studies have explored the calibration of deep ranking models, highlighting the importance of scale calibration in practical applications like advertising \[[3](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/7f6bb146b9c1504ec77100e640d624bdc204f0ac)\]. For example, in ads ranking, the predicted Click-Through Rate (CTR) is used for ranking and must be calibrated for downstream cost-per-click bidding \[[3](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/7f6bb146b9c1504ec77100e640d624bdc204f0ac)\]. This has motivated the development of calibration losses that can be integrated during training to improve both ranking quality and score scale calibration \[[3](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/7f6bb146b9c1504ec77100e640d624bdc204f0ac)\]. However, many existing methods suffer from training instability due to translation-invariance in their loss functions \[[3](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/7f6bb146b9c1504ec77100e640d624bdc204f0ac)\].

To address these limitations, recent work has proposed novel calibration approaches that incorporate flexible neural network architectures. For instance, the Unconstrained Monotonic Neural Network (UMNN) has been introduced as a universal approximator of continuous monotonic functions, enabling the modeling of arbitrary monotonic transformations \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/1908.05164)\]. This architecture has been applied to distributional reinforcement learning, where it allows for the efficient decoupling of function approximator class from probability metric \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/1908.05164)\]. The UMNN's ability to model monotonic functions without enforcing strict constraints on weights or activation functions makes it particularly suitable for calibration tasks \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/1908.05164)\].

In addition to UMNN-based approaches, other studies have explored the use of soft calibration objectives and loss-controlling calibration methods to improve predictive uncertainty \[[5](http://arxiv.org/abs/2108.00106)\]\[[6](http://arxiv.org/abs/2301.04378)\]. For example, the Soft Calibration Objectives for Neural Networks \[[5](http://arxiv.org/abs/2108.00106)\] propose differentiable losses that improve calibration based on a soft version of binning, achieving state-of-the-art results with minimal accuracy loss. Similarly, the Loss-Controlling Calibration for Predictive Models \[[6](http://arxiv.org/abs/2301.04378)\] introduces a framework that allows for flexible loss functions without monotonic assumptions, enabling more precise control over predictive uncertainty.

The importance of calibration in deep learning has also been extensively studied, with a focus on metrics like Expected Calibration Error (ECE) and the development of alternative calibration measures \[[7](http://arxiv.org/abs/1904.01685)\].
