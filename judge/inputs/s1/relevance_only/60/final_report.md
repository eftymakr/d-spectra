## Related Works

Ranking models traditionally focus on modeling the relative order of predictions, often at the expense of the accuracy of their absolute values \[[9](http://arxiv.org/abs/2406.08010)\]. This limitation has led to a growing body of work on calibrating ranking models to ensure both accurate relative ordering and reliable absolute predictions \[[3](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/7f6bb146b9c1504ec77100e640d624bdc204f0ac)\]. Calibration in ranking tasks is particularly challenging because existing methods often rely on predefined transformation functions with order-preserving properties, such as piece-wise linear functions \[[3](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/7f6bb146b9c1504ec77100e640d624bdc204f0ac)\]. These fixed forms, while simple, exhibit limited expressiveness and flexibility, which restricts their effectiveness in complex calibration scenarios \[[9](http://arxiv.org/abs/2406.08010)\].

Several studies have explored the calibration of deep ranking models, highlighting the importance of scale calibration in practical applications like advertising \[[3](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/7f6bb146b9c1504ec77100e640d624bdc204f0ac)\]. For example, in ads ranking, the predicted Click-Through Rate (CTR) is used for ranking and must be calibrated for downstream cost-per-click bidding \[[3](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/7f6bb146b9c1504ec77100e640d624bdc204f0ac)\]. This has motivated the development of calibration losses that can be integrated during training to improve both ranking quality and score scale calibration \[[3](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/7f6bb146b9c1504ec77100e640d624bdc204f0ac)\]. However, many existing methods suffer from training instability due to translation-invariance in their loss functions \[[3](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/7f6bb146b9c1504ec77100e640d624bdc204f0ac)\].

To address these limitations, recent work has proposed novel calibration approaches that incorporate flexible neural network architectures. The UMNN's ability to model monotonic functions without enforcing strict constraints on weights or activation functions makes it particularly suitable for calibration tasks \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/1908.05164)\].

In addition to UMNN-based approaches, other studies have explored the use of soft calibration objectives and loss-controlling calibration methods to improve predictive uncertainty \[[5](http://arxiv.org/abs/2108.00106)\]\[[6](http://arxiv.org/abs/2301.04378)\]. ### <a id='category-monotonic-neural-networks-(umnn)'></a>Monotonic Neural Networks (UMNN): 2 papers found.
Monotonic neural networks, specifically the Unconstrained Monotonic Neural Network (UMNN) architecture, have been explored for their ability to model continuous monotonic functions, which is crucial for tasks requiring invertible transformations and accurate probability modeling \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/1908.05164)\]. UMNNs ensure monotonicity by enforcing the positiveness of the network's output, allowing for greater flexibility compared to traditional constrained architectures \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/1908.05164)\]. This property makes UMNNs particularly suitable for applications such as density estimation and variational inference, where maintaining monotonic relationships is essential \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/1908.05164)\]. In the context of distributional reinforcement learning, UMNNs have been utilized to model different representations of the random return distribution, including probability density functions (PDF), cumulative distribution functions (CDF), and quantile functions (QF), enabling efficient decoupling of function approximators from probability metrics \[[4](http://arxiv.org/abs/2106.03228)\]. Additionally, UMNNs have been applied to improve calibration in ranking models by learning arbitrary monotonic functions, thereby enhancing the accuracy of absolute predictions without compromising the order-preserving properties \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/1908.05164)\]. This approach addresses the limitations of traditional calibration methods that rely on fixed transformation functions, such as piece-wise linear functions, which lack the flexibility needed for complex calibration scenarios \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/1908.05164)\]. The introduction of a novel loss function, the Smooth Calibration Loss (SCLoss), further optimizes the performance of UMNN-based calibrators by ensuring the necessary conditions for ideal calibration \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/1908.05164)\].
