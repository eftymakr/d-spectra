## Related Works

Fairness in re-ranking has garnered significant attention in recent years, driven by the increasing deployment of recommendation systems in socially impactful domains such as employment and housing \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/2005.12974)\]. Early work primarily focused on optimizing recommendation accuracy while incorporating fairness metrics, often at the expense of user or item fairness \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/2005.12974)\]. However, the interplay between accuracy and fairness remains complex, with prior studies highlighting the inherent trade-offs between these objectives \[[15](http://arxiv.org/abs/1802.04422)\]. 

Several approaches have been proposed to address fairness in ranking. For instance, \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/1809.02921)\] introduced a taxation perspective to fair re-ranking, reformulating fairness as a tax policy that redistributes ranking slots. This work demonstrated that existing methods could be viewed as item-level tax policies, though they lacked continuity and controllability over accuracy loss. To overcome these limitations, \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/1809.02921)\] proposed Tax-rank, which leverages the Sinkhorn algorithm for optimal transport to achieve both fairness and accuracy. 

In parallel, \[[18](http://arxiv.org/abs/2204.08085)\] presented a re-ranking approach that integrates fairness from both consumer and producer perspectives, addressing the two-sided nature of recommender systems. This method improved fairness without sacrificing recommendation quality, demonstrating the importance of considering multiple stakeholders. Similarly, \[[21](http://arxiv.org/abs/2402.00485)\] proposed CP-FairRank, an optimization-based re-ranking algorithm that jointly enforces consumer and producer fairness, showing that fairness can be enhanced without significant accuracy loss.

The concept of fairness in rankings has also been explored through the lens of exposure and diversity. \[[14](http://arxiv.org/abs/1802.07281)\] introduced a framework for fairness constraints in rankings, focusing on exposure allocation to ensure equitable treatment of items. This work developed efficient algorithms to maximize user utility while satisfying fairness constraints. Additionally, \[[27](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/1e213c3cde47d2ae4bf4492b30874e3fc47e0d92)\] proposed a fairness-aware re-distribution framework that leverages fair utility estimation to improve both item fairness and recommendation quality, highlighting the importance of accurate utility modeling.

Recent work has also examined the fairness-accuracy trade-off in multi-task learning \[[24](http://arxiv.org/abs/2106.02705)\], revealing that traditional approaches focusing on accuracy may not adequately address fairness goals. This study proposed new metrics and a Multi-Task-Aware Fairness (MTA-F) approach to better capture the multi-dimensional Pareto frontier of fairness-accuracy trade-offs. 

Moreover, \[[26](http://arxiv.org/abs/1903.00780)\] introduced a set of novel metrics for evaluating fairness in recommender systems, emphasizing the importance of pairwise comparisons for quantifying fairness in rankings. This approach was applied to a large-scale production system, demonstrating significant improvements in pairwise fairness.
