## Related Works

Active learning (AL) has been widely studied for reducing annotation costs by selecting the most informative unlabeled samples for labeling \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2504.01921)\]. However, traditional AL approaches often suffer from limited data diversity and annotation budget constraints, especially in distributed settings \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/2504.11216)\]. Federated Active Learning (FAL) extends AL to distributed environments, enabling collaborative model training while preserving data privacy \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/2310.00198)\]. Despite these advancements, existing FAL methods often fail to account for the heterogeneity of data distribution across clients and the associated fluctuations in global and local model parameters, which can negatively impact model accuracy \[[4](http://arxiv.org/abs/2206.10032)\].

Several works have addressed the challenges of data heterogeneity in federated learning (FL). For instance, \[[5](http://arxiv.org/abs/2010.01264)\] proposes a client selection method that accounts for both data and communication heterogeneity, improving convergence speed. Similarly, \[[6](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/514b57ac2810be1242395b15d5e57635c93635de)\] introduces a framework that leverages statistical heterogeneity to enhance model performance. These approaches, however, typically focus on either data or communication heterogeneity in isolation, rather than addressing the combined impact of both \[[7](http://arxiv.org/abs/2011.06393)\].

In the context of model heterogeneity, \[[8](http://arxiv.org/abs/1804.08333)\] presents a framework that allows for heterogeneous local models while still producing a unified global inference model. This is particularly relevant for FAL, where clients may have varying computational capabilities and data distributions \[[9](http://arxiv.org/abs/2112.11256)\]. Additionally, \[[10](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/1ace65d958cd27ae9ad857c49d8caa9aa5740526)\] explores the use of self-supervised learning in heterogeneous settings, demonstrating the potential of leveraging unlabeled data to improve model generalization \[[11](http://arxiv.org/abs/2205.12493)\].

To address the issue of skewed data distributions, \[[12](http://arxiv.org/abs/2207.08187)\] proposes a method that uses pseudo-labeling to handle unlabeled data in FL, while \[[13](http://arxiv.org/abs/2110.06978)\] introduces a framework that generates synthetic data to balance data distributions across clients \[[14](http://arxiv.org/abs/2010.01243)\]. These approaches are complementary to FAL, as they aim to improve model performance in the presence of skewed or imbalanced data \[[15](http://arxiv.org/abs/2210.04505)\].

In terms of client selection strategies, \[[16](http://arxiv.org/abs/2411.01240)\] presents a method that selects clients based on entropy maximization to ensure diverse label representation in the global model. This aligns with the goal of CHASe to identify samples with high epistemic variation \[[17](http://arxiv.org/abs/1909.12488)\]. Furthermore, \[[18](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/6b0d47501ad9efd386837959ea31cd3f58199797)\] introduces a client selection algorithm that accounts for both system and statistical heterogeneity, aiming to reduce convergence time \[[19](http://arxiv.org/abs/2206.05507)\].

For example, \[[20](http://arxiv.org/abs/2408.07966)\] proposes a method that uses knowledge distillation to prevent models from forgetting previously learned knowledge during local updates \[[21](http://arxiv.org/abs/2104.13417)\].
