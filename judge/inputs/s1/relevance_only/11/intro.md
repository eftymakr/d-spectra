## Related Works

3D human pose estimation has attracted significant research interest due to its applications in human-computer interaction, virtual reality, and rehabilitation. Most existing methods rely on either graph convolutional networks (GCNs) or transformers to model spatial and temporal dependencies in human motion \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2505.01003)\]. GCNs are effective at capturing local joint relationships but often neglect long-range dependencies and global body structure \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/2405.03055)\]. Transformers, on the other hand, excel at modeling long-range interactions but may struggle with fine-grained local details due to their global attention mechanism \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/1708.01101)\]. Several works have attempted to combine the strengths of both approaches.

For instance, \[[4](http://arxiv.org/abs/2308.05298)\] introduces a multi-hop graph transformer network that leverages multi-head self-attention and multi-hop graph convolutional layers to capture both local and global dependencies. This approach also integrates dilated convolutional layers to enhance spatial detail capture. Similarly, \[[5](http://arxiv.org/abs/2412.19676)\] proposes a model that combines GCN and Transformer modules to capture multi-level joint dependencies. The Local Constraint Module (LCM) and Global Constraint Module (GCM) are designed to model local and global constraints, respectively, while a Feature Interaction Module (FIM) further enhances the interaction between features \[[5](http://arxiv.org/abs/2412.19676)\].

Another line of research focuses on improving the modeling of spatial-temporal relationships. \[[6](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/50167a9e6bdc1f64a58fe8a3c990105fec0bd2bc)\] introduces a higher-order graph convolutional framework with residual connections to capture long-range dependencies between body joints. The residual connections ensure that important information from the initial features is retained throughout the network. Additionally, \[[7](http://arxiv.org/abs/2111.00950)\] proposes a multi-stage framework that utilizes spatial and temporal modules to capture spatio-temporal correlation from multi-view video datasets. The self-attention mechanism is used to eliminate interference from non-human body parts and reduce computational resources \[[7](http://arxiv.org/abs/2111.00950)\].

The challenge of modeling long-range dependencies is also addressed in \[[8](http://arxiv.org/abs/2401.16700)\], where a dual-stream model is proposed to integrate local features with global dependencies. This model introduces a module called SSRFormer, which uses a skeleton selective refine attention (SSRA) mechanism to capture fine-grained local dependencies. This complements the global dependencies modeled by the Transformer, enabling a more comprehensive understanding of human motion \[[8](http://arxiv.org/abs/2401.16700)\].

In addition to modeling spatial and temporal dependencies, some works focus on improving the robustness of pose estimation under challenging conditions. \[[9](http://arxiv.org/abs/2204.04913)\] introduces a model that leverages spatial-temporal relationships via graph convolutional networks to alleviate depth ambiguity and self-occlusions. The model incorporates domain knowledge about human hand and body configurations into the graph convolutional operations to meet the specific demands of 3D pose estimation \[[9](http://arxiv.org/abs/2204.04913)\].
