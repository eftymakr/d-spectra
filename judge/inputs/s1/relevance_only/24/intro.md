## Related Works

Diffusion models have gained significant attention for their ability to generate high-quality images and videos, with Diffusion Transformers (DiT) showing exceptional performance in video generation \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2406.02540)\]. However, the large number of parameters and high computational complexity of DiT models limit their deployment on edge devices \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2406.02540)\]. Quantization is a promising technique to reduce storage requirements and accelerate inference by lowering the bit-width of model parameters \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2406.02540)\]. Existing quantization methods for image generation models, such as those based on U-Net architectures, have not been effectively generalized to video generation tasks \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2406.02540)\]. This limitation is due to two primary challenges: the loss of information during quantization and the misalignment between optimization objectives and the unique requirements of video generation \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2406.02540)\].

Several studies have explored quantization techniques for diffusion models. Q-DiT \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/2406.17343)\] introduces a novel approach that integrates automatic quantization granularity allocation and sample-wise dynamic activation quantization to handle the significant variance of weights and activations across input channels and timesteps. This method achieves a notable reduction in FID when quantizing DiT-XL/2 to W6A8 on ImageNet \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/2406.17343)\]. Similarly, ViDiT-Q \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/2505.18663)\] is tailored specifically for DiT models, achieving W8A8 and W4A8 with negligible degradation in visual quality and metrics \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/2505.18663)\]. DVD-Quant \[[4](http://arxiv.org/abs/2502.04056)\] is another data-free quantization framework for Video DiTs, achieving an approximately 2× speedup while maintaining visual fidelity \[[4](http://arxiv.org/abs/2502.04056)\].

Temporal Dynamic Quantization \[[5](http://arxiv.org/abs/2411.14172)\] dynamically adjusts the quantization interval based on time step information, significantly improving output quality without computational overhead during inference \[[5](http://arxiv.org/abs/2411.14172)\]. TaQ-DiT \[[6](http://arxiv.org/abs/2306.02316)\] introduces time-aware quantization for DiTs, addressing the non-convergence issue during quantization and the sensitivity of Post-GELU activations to quantization \[[6](http://arxiv.org/abs/2306.02316)\]. These methods demonstrate that quantization can be effectively applied to DiT models, although challenges remain in maintaining performance at low bit-widths.

PTQ4DiT \[[7](http://arxiv.org/abs/2405.16005)\] is a specifically designed PTQ method for DiTs, addressing challenges such as salient channels with extreme magnitudes and temporal variability in activation distributions \[[7](http://arxiv.org/abs/2405.16005)\]. HQ-DiT \[[8](http://arxiv.org/abs/2405.19751)\] introduces hybrid floating-point quantization for DiT inference, achieving extremely low-precision quantization (i.e., 4 bits) with negligible impact on performance \[[8](http://arxiv.org/abs/2405.19751)\].
