## Related Works

Generative retrieval (GR) has recently emerged as a promising paradigm in information retrieval (IR), shifting the focus from traditional index-retrieve pipelines to end-to-end document ID generation \[[16](http://arxiv.org/abs/2310.12455)\]. This paradigm leverages large language models to directly map queries to document identifiers (docids), eliminating the need for external indices \[[6](http://arxiv.org/abs/2202.06991)\]. However, most existing GR models are primarily evaluated on static document collections, with limited exploration of their performance on dynamic corpora where document sets evolve over time \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2308.14968)\]. This section reviews related works that address the challenges of dynamic document retrieval and the design of docids in generative retrieval.

Several studies have explored the use of differentiable search indices (DSI) to encode document corpora into model parameters, allowing direct query-to-docid mapping \[[6](http://arxiv.org/abs/2202.06991)\]. The DSI framework has been extended to handle cross-lingual retrieval and has shown strong performance on standard benchmarks \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/2206.10128)\]. However, DSI models face challenges when dealing with dynamic corpora, as retraining is required to incorporate new documents \[[7](http://arxiv.org/abs/2212.09744)\]. To address this, DSI++ was proposed, which enables continual learning by incrementally updating the model with new documents while preserving retrieval performance over previously indexed documents \[[7](http://arxiv.org/abs/2212.09744)\]. This approach mitigates forgetting and improves retrieval accuracy, as demonstrated by improvements in Hits@10 \[[7](http://arxiv.org/abs/2212.09744)\].

The design of docids plays a crucial role in the effectiveness of generative retrieval. While numeric-based docids are often favored for their efficiency, they suffer from poor generalization over dynamic corpora due to overfitting on the initial document set \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2308.14968)\]. In contrast, text-based docids, which are more semantically aligned with language models' pretrained knowledge, show superior performance in dynamic settings \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2308.14968)\]. This aligns with findings from studies that highlight the importance of semantic alignment and fine-grained docid design in improving retrieval over evolving document collections \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2308.14968)\]. For example, the work of \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2308.14968)\] shows that text-based docids outperform numeric-based ones in dynamic settings, particularly when combined with high lexical diversity.

Existing GR models, which typically use text-based document identifiers (docids), demonstrate superior generalization to unseen documents compared to numeric-based docids, which suffer from performance degradation in dynamic settings \[[6](http://arxiv.org/abs/2202.06991)\]. This is attributed to the overfitting of numeric-based docids to the initial training set, leading to a strong bias toward the initial document collection \[[6](http://arxiv.org/abs/2202.06991)\]. Text-based docids, on the other hand, benefit from semantic alignment with pre-trained language models, fine-grained design, and high lexical diversity, making them more effective in dynamic environments \[[6](http://arxiv.org/abs/2202.06991)\]. These advantages are supported by prior work that highlights the importance of semantic alignment and fine-grained representation in generative retrieval \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/2311.09134)\]. Additionally, research has shown that generative retrieval models can be trained to perform effectively on large-scale benchmarks, demonstrating their potential for real-world applications \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/2311.09134)\]. However, challenges remain in scaling generative retrieval to very large corpora, as existing approaches often struggle with computational efficiency and performance consistency \[[9](http://arxiv.org/abs/2305.11841)\]. The proposed multi-docid design aims to address these limitations by combining the efficiency of numeric-based docids with the effectiveness of text-based ones, without requiring additional retraining \[[6](http://arxiv.org/abs/2202.06991)\]. This approach is inspired by earlier efforts to improve generative retrieval through better indexing strategies and end-to-end optimization \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/2311.09134)\]. Overall, the findings highlight the importance of docid design in generative retrieval and pave the way for more generalized and efficient models in real-world search engines \[[6](http://arxiv.org/abs/2202.06991)\].

### <a id='category-docid-design-and-its-impact-on-retrieval-performance'></a>DocID Design and Its Impact on Retrieval Performance: 6 papers found.
Document retrieval has evolved significantly with the advent of generative retrieval (GR) methods, which aim to directly generate document identifiers (docids) from queries in an end-to-end manner \[[16](http://arxiv.org/abs/2310.12455)\]. Traditional index-retrieve frameworks, while effective, are limited by their inability to jointly optimize indexing and retrieval \[[11](http://arxiv.org/abs/2208.09257)\]. In response, several approaches have been proposed to enhance the semantic representation of docids, such as GenRet, which uses a discrete auto-encoding approach to learn semantic docids \[[8](http://arxiv.org/abs/2304.04171)\], and Ultron, which introduces semantically rich docids and a three-stage training workflow to improve retrieval performance \[[11](http://arxiv.org/abs/2208.09257)\]. The Differentiable Search Index (DSI) paradigm further advances this by parameterizing the entire retrieval pipeline, allowing for direct mapping of queries to docids \[[12](http://arxiv.org/abs/2305.15115)\]. Additionally, PAG introduces a set-based and sequential identifier for documents, leveraging lexical tokens and relevance-based representations to improve retrieval accuracy and speed \[[13](http://arxiv.org/abs/2404.14600)\]. NOVO docids, on the other hand, focus on optimizing n-gram-based identifiers through query denoising and retrieval tasks, enhancing semantic understanding and performance \[[14](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/6682a3679a17d090830635f1c420126d20eae54a)\]. These approaches highlight the importance of docid design in generative retrieval, with text-based docids showing superior generalization to unseen documents and dynamic corpora \[[16](http://arxiv.org/abs/2310.12455)\]. The challenge remains in balancing efficiency and effectiveness, as numeric-based docids, while efficient, suffer from performance degradation in dynamic settings due to overfitting \[[16](http://arxiv.org/abs/2310.12455)\].

### <a id='category-semantic-alignment-with-pretrained-models'></a>Semantic Alignment with Pretrained Models: 2 papers found.
The category "Semantic Alignment with Pretrained Models" explores the integration of classical information retrieval systems with pre-trained language models to enhance the delivery of domain expert-like advice. Traditional information retrieval systems provide references to authoritative answers, while pre-trained models can generate prose directly but lack the depth of domain expertise and the ability to justify responses with supporting documents \[[15](http://arxiv.org/abs/2105.02274)\]. Recent advancements, such as the DynamicRetriever system, propose a new paradigm where pre-trained models are used to parameterize static indexes, enabling dynamic and updatable document semantic mapping without relying on sparse or dense indexes \[[19](http://arxiv.org/abs/2203.00537)\]. This approach captures both term-level and document-level information, improving the model's ability to handle evolving document collections. The semantic alignment between pre-trained models and document content is crucial for generating accurate and justifiable responses, especially in dynamic environments where document sets continuously change. This alignment is further emphasized by the effectiveness of text-based docids in generative retrieval models, which leverage the semantic knowledge of pre-trained models to achieve better generalization over unseen documents \[[15](http://arxiv.org/abs/2105.02274)\].
