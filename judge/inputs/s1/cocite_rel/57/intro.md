## Related Works

Generative retrieval (GR) has recently emerged as a promising paradigm in information retrieval (IR), shifting the focus from traditional index-retrieve pipelines to end-to-end document ID generation \[[16](http://arxiv.org/abs/2310.12455)\]. This paradigm leverages large language models to directly map queries to document identifiers (docids), eliminating the need for external indices \[[6](http://arxiv.org/abs/2202.06991)\]. However, most existing GR models are primarily evaluated on static document collections, with limited exploration of their performance on dynamic corpora where document sets evolve over time \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2308.14968)\]. This section reviews related works that address the challenges of dynamic document retrieval and the design of docids in generative retrieval.

Several studies have explored the use of differentiable search indices (DSI) to encode document corpora into model parameters, allowing direct query-to-docid mapping \[[6](http://arxiv.org/abs/2202.06991)\]. The DSI framework has been extended to handle cross-lingual retrieval and has shown strong performance on standard benchmarks \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/2206.10128)\]. However, DSI models face challenges when dealing with dynamic corpora, as retraining is required to incorporate new documents \[[7](http://arxiv.org/abs/2212.09744)\]. To address this, DSI++ was proposed, which enables continual learning by incrementally updating the model with new documents while preserving retrieval performance over previously indexed documents \[[7](http://arxiv.org/abs/2212.09744)\]. This approach mitigates forgetting and improves retrieval accuracy, as demonstrated by improvements in Hits@10 \[[7](http://arxiv.org/abs/2212.09744)\].

The design of docids plays a crucial role in the effectiveness of generative retrieval. While numeric-based docids are often favored for their efficiency, they suffer from poor generalization over dynamic corpora due to overfitting on the initial document set \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2308.14968)\]. In contrast, text-based docids, which are more semantically aligned with language models' pretrained knowledge, show superior performance in dynamic settings \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2308.14968)\]. This aligns with findings from studies that highlight the importance of semantic alignment and fine-grained docid design in improving retrieval over evolving document collections \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2308.14968)\]. For example, the work of \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2308.14968)\] shows that text-based docids outperform numeric-based ones in dynamic settings, particularly when combined with high lexical diversity.

To address the limitations of purely numeric or text-based docids, recent work has proposed hybrid approaches that combine the strengths of both.
