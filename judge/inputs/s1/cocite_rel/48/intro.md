## Related Works

Recent advancements in neural retrieval methods, particularly those leveraging transformer-based pre-trained language models, have significantly improved multilingual and cross-lingual retrieval capabilities \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/1810.04805)\]. However, the effectiveness of these models for low-resource, morphologically rich languages such as Amharic remains underexplored due to data scarcity and suboptimal tokenization \[[17](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/0b212e1f3a1459cbd89c2bca19e63195c1980ba4)\]. This gap is addressed by introducing Amharic-specific dense retrieval models based on pre-trained Amharic BERT and RoBERTa backbones \[[20](http://arxiv.org/abs/2503.18570)\]. Our proposed RoBERTa-Base-Amharic-Embed model (110M parameters) achieves a 17.6% relative improvement in MRR@10 and a 9.86% gain in Recall@10 over the strongest multilingual baseline, Arctic Embed 2.0 (568M parameters) \[[20](http://arxiv.org/abs/2503.18570)\]. More compact variants, such as RoBERTa-Medium-Amharic-Embed (42M), remain competitive while being over 13x smaller \[[20](http://arxiv.org/abs/2503.18570)\]. 

The use of dense retrieval models has also been explored in other low-resource languages, such as Urdu, where the first large-scale Urdu IR dataset was created by translating the MS MARCO dataset through machine translation \[[24](http://arxiv.org/abs/2412.12997)\]. This work established baseline results through zero-shot learning for IR in Urdu and demonstrated that fine-tuned models can achieve significant improvements over zero-shot results \[[24](http://arxiv.org/abs/2412.12997)\]. Similarly, the mMARCO dataset, a multilingual version of the MS MARCO passage ranking dataset comprising 13 languages, was created using machine translation and has been used to evaluate multilingual reranking models \[[25](http://arxiv.org/abs/2108.13897)\]. These datasets and models have been instrumental in advancing multilingual IR research.

The effectiveness of late interaction retrieval models has also been studied, with ColBERT being introduced as a novel ranking model that adapts deep LMs (in particular, BERT) for efficient retrieval \[[11](http://arxiv.org/abs/2004.12832)\]. ColBERT introduces a late interaction architecture that independently encodes the query and the document using BERT and then employs a cheap yet powerful interaction step that models their fine-grained similarity \[[11](http://arxiv.org/abs/2004.12832)\]. This approach has been shown to be competitive with existing BERT-based models while executing two orders-of-magnitude faster and requiring up to four orders-of-magnitude fewer FLOPs per query \[[11](http://arxiv.org/abs/2004.12832)\].
