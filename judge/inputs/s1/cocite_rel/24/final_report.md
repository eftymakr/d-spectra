## Related Works

Diffusion models have gained significant traction in image and video generation due to their ability to produce high-quality outputs \[[13](http://arxiv.org/abs/2105.05233)\]. However, their deployment on edge devices is hindered by their large parameter counts and high computational demands \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2302.04304)\]. To address this, quantization has emerged as a promising approach to reduce model size and accelerate inference by lowering the bit-width of model parameters \[[14](http://arxiv.org/abs/2106.14156)\]. While post-training quantization (PTQ) has been successfully applied to image generation models, its generalization to video generation tasks remains limited \[[20](http://arxiv.org/abs/2406.02540)\]. This is primarily due to two challenges: the loss of information during quantization and the misalignment between optimization objectives and the unique requirements of video generation \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2302.04304)\].

Existing PTQ methods for diffusion models, such as Q-Diffusion \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2302.04304)\], PTQ4DiT \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/2405.16005)\], and PTQD \[[4](http://arxiv.org/abs/2305.10657)\], have made strides in reducing the bit-width of diffusion models while maintaining performance. For instance, Q-Diffusion compresses full-precision diffusion models into 4-bit while maintaining comparable performance \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2302.04304)\]. PTQ4DiT, on the other hand, successfully quantizes DiTs to 8-bit precision (W8A8) and further enables 4-bit weight precision (W4A8) \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/2405.16005)\]. PTQD introduces a unified formulation for quantization noise and diffusion perturbed noise, improving the quality of quantized diffusion models \[[4](http://arxiv.org/abs/2305.10657)\]. These methods, however, do not directly address the unique challenges of video generation, such as maintaining spatiotemporal consistency across frames \[[20](http://arxiv.org/abs/2406.02540)\].

### <a id='category-post-training-quantization-methods-for-diffusion-models'></a>Post-Training Quantization Methods for Diffusion Models: 7 papers found.
Diffusion models have gained significant attention for their ability to generate high-quality images and videos, but their computational demands hinder deployment on edge devices \[[6](http://arxiv.org/abs/2306.02316)\]. Post-training quantization (PTQ) has emerged as a promising approach to reduce model size and accelerate inference without retraining \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2302.04304)\]. However, existing PTQ methods face challenges when applied to diffusion transformers (DiTs), particularly in video generation tasks, due to the unique characteristics of DiTs such as spatial and temporal variance in activations and weights \[[6](http://arxiv.org/abs/2306.02316)\]. Several studies have addressed these issues by proposing tailored PTQ methods for DiTs. For instance, PTQ4DiT introduces Channel-wise Salience Balancing (CSB) and Spearmen's ρ-guided Salience Calibration (SSC) to handle salient channels and temporal activation variations \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/2405.16005)\]. Similarly, Q-DiT integrates automatic quantization granularity allocation and sample-wise dynamic activation quantization to adapt to the spatial and temporal variability in DiTs \[[9](http://arxiv.org/abs/2406.17343)\]. Additionally, ViDiT-Q was designed specifically for DiT models, achieving W8A8 and W4A8 quantization with minimal degradation in visual quality \[[20](http://arxiv.org/abs/2406.02540)\]. These methods demonstrate that specialized PTQ approaches are essential for effectively quantizing DiTs while preserving generation quality. Furthermore, the challenge of quantization noise accumulation during the denoising process has been addressed by proposing a unified formulation to correct both quantization and diffusion perturbed noise \[[4](http://arxiv.org/abs/2305.10657)\]. These advancements highlight the growing body of research focused on improving PTQ for diffusion models, particularly DiTs, to enable efficient deployment in real-world applications \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2302.04304)\]\[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/2405.16005)\]\[[4](http://arxiv.org/abs/2305.10657)\]\[[6](http://arxiv.org/abs/2306.02316)\]\[[9](http://arxiv.org/abs/2406.17343)\]\[[20](http://arxiv.org/abs/2406.02540)\].

As such, we are able to show that: (1) ZeroQuant can reduce the precision for weights and activations to INT8 in a cost-free way for both BERT and GPT3-style models with minimal accuracy impact, which leads to up to 5.19x/4.16x speedup on those models compared to FP16 inference; (2) ZeroQuant plus LKD affordably quantize the weights in the fully-connected module to INT4 along with INT8 weights in the attention module and INT8 activations, resulting in 3x memory footprint reduction compared to the FP16 model; (3) ZeroQuant can be directly applied to two of the largest open-sourced language models, including GPT-J6B and GPT-NeoX20, for which our INT8 model achieves similar accuracy as the FP16 model but achieves up to 5.2x better efficiency.»
