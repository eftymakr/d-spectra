## Related Works

Zero-Knowledge Proofs (ZKPs) have garnered significant attention for their ability to enable secure and private computation, allowing a prover to demonstrate the truth of a statement without revealing additional information \[[9](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/6bcd2606c8f0099a82bd03e520659058a5b12e51)\]. This capability has led to their adoption in various domains, including blockchain technologies, verifiable machine learning, and electronic voting \[[1](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/e149d93531d8c07cf43f6cae327a4795144d1304)\]. However, the computational complexity of the proving process has hindered their widespread adoption \[[1](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/e149d93531d8c07cf43f6cae327a4795144d1304)\]. Recent research has focused on accelerating the key primitives of ZKP protocols using hardware accelerators such as GPUs and ASICs \[[1](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/e149d93531d8c07cf43f6cae327a4795144d1304)\]. 

Several works have explored methods to improve the performance of ZKP systems. For instance, GZKP introduces a GPU-accelerated approach that significantly reduces proof generation time by optimizing memory access patterns and eliminating costly external shuffles \[[1](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/e149d93531d8c07cf43f6cae327a4795144d1304)\]. Similarly, PipeZK proposes a pipelined architecture that handles large polynomial computations and multi-scalar multiplications on elliptic curves, achieving up to 10x speedup on standard benchmarks \[[2](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/6ee84674b7c426849c3da56cb278c08eb7a903cf)\]. cuZK further enhances performance by introducing a faster parallel multi-scalar multiplication algorithm on GPUs, achieving up to 4.86x speedup on cryptographic benchmarks \[[4](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/e4a4608e8e35379e63b9df5bda1b7599ee8ab9ac)\]. 

The challenge of achieving both small proof sizes and universal setup has also been addressed in recent works. HyperPlonk, a state-of-the-art ZKP protocol, supports one-time, universal setup and small proof sizes, making it suitable for publicly verifiable systems \[[1](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/e149d93531d8c07cf43f6cae327a4795144d1304)\]. The proposed accelerator, zkSpeed, is designed to enhance the performance of HyperPlonk by accelerating two critical primitives: SumCheck and Multi-scalar Multiplications (MSMs) \[[1](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/e149d93531d8c07cf43f6cae327a4795144d1304)\]. This is crucial as these operations are often the most computationally intensive parts of the proof generation process \[[4](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/e4a4608e8e35379e63b9df5bda1b7599ee8ab9ac)\]. 

Efforts to reduce the overhead of proof generation have also focused on optimizing the interaction between hardware and software. For example, cuZK reduces the latency overhead caused by CPU-GPU data transfer by minimizing redundant transfers and overlapping computation with data movement \[[4](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/e4a4608e8e35379e63b9df5bda1b7599ee8ab9ac)\]. Similarly, the work on zkCNN demonstrates how ZKPs can be applied to convolutional neural networks (CNNs), enabling the verification of machine learning predictions without exposing model details \[[5](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/6336149196f02d0eb6c1a89bc6b662e4422bd794)\]. This highlights the broader applicability of ZKPs beyond traditional cryptographic applications \[[5](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/6336149196f02d0eb6c1a89bc6b662e4422bd794)\].
