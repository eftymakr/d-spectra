## Related Works

Federated unlearning has emerged as a critical research area in the context of privacy-preserving machine learning, particularly in response to the "right to be forgotten" (RTBF) legislation \[[1](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/eadeffdec9fac8fd7f9aea732ca410eb082b7dcf)\]. This concept enables users to remove their data contributions from a global model trained across decentralized data sources, ensuring compliance with privacy regulations. In federated learning (FL), where data remains on local devices and only model parameters are shared, unlearning methods must address the challenge of removing a client's influence without requiring full retraining \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/2203.07320)\]. Several approaches have been proposed to achieve this goal, with varying degrees of efficiency and effectiveness.

Early efforts in federated unlearning focused on retraining the global model from scratch after removing the targeted client's data \[[3](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/39da2d48f2132b919405e3bb6df6ece44ac6e15a)\]. However, this approach is computationally expensive and impractical for large-scale FL systems \[[4](http://arxiv.org/abs/2210.10958)\]. To address this, researchers have explored alternative methods such as using historical parameter updates to reconstruct the unlearned model \[[5](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/06edb1f6eadb6c141d720fcca4c854578dc5d670)\]. This technique, known as FedEraser, leverages the retained updates from the central server to quickly rebuild the model while maintaining its performance \[[6](http://arxiv.org/abs/2205.12709)\]. Similarly, the work in \[[7](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/a52c3ec7325e81bd87fdf8d74bf9acfd1e1ffe5f)\] proposes a rapid retraining approach that allows data holders to jointly conduct unlearning while keeping their training data locally, ensuring both efficiency and privacy.

Another line of research focuses on the calibration of historical updates to ensure the unlearned model aligns with the original model's performance \[[8](http://arxiv.org/abs/2207.05521)\]. This is particularly important in scenarios where the unlearning process may inadvertently affect the model's accuracy \[[9](http://arxiv.org/abs/2209.02299)\]. The paper \[[10](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/a08f1c5c9f0bdb24f0dc75c0d969eb353899156b)\] introduces a method that uses reverse stochastic gradient ascent (SGA) and elastic weight consolidation (EWC) to preserve model utility during unlearning. This approach has been validated on multiple datasets, demonstrating its effectiveness in both unlearning efficacy and efficiency \[[11](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/a5f39bcd6198b26997b77233ff81a14e6bc8a32d)\].

In addition to efficiency, verifiability has become an essential aspect of federated unlearning. The work in \[[12](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/efab3b30d6f67f55c68f9dc7c65b2b54b1789030)\] introduces VeriFi, a framework that allows users to independently verify the unlearning effect, ensuring that the model has indeed forgotten the targeted data \[[13](http://arxiv.org/abs/2110.11794)\]. This is crucial for regulatory compliance and user trust. Similarly, \[[14](http://arxiv.org/abs/2201.09441)\] proposes a method that enables users to verify the unlearning process through local checks, ensuring that the model's performance is not compromised \[[15](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/70954dd11d1463bc665f803d84b15a4d83805ec0)\].
