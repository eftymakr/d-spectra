## Related Works

Narrative summarization, particularly for long-form texts such as books, movies, and TV scripts, remains a challenging task due to the need for capturing intricate plotlines, character interactions, and thematic coherence \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/1908.08960)\]. Early approaches focused on extractive summarization, where key sentences are selected from the source text \[[15](http://arxiv.org/abs/1909.03186)\]. However, these methods often fail to produce coherent and concise abstractive summaries that capture the essence of the narrative \[[12](http://arxiv.org/abs/1808.10792)\]. Recent advances in neural sequence-to-sequence models have enabled more flexible and expressive summarization, allowing for the generation of novel content while maintaining factual accuracy \[[7](http://arxiv.org/abs/1704.04368)\]. 

Several studies have explored the use of pre-trained language models for summarization, such as BERT and GPT-3, which have demonstrated strong performance on various summarization tasks \[[18](http://arxiv.org/abs/1910.13461)\]. However, these models often struggle with long-form narratives due to limitations in handling long-range dependencies and maintaining narrative coherence \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/1908.08960)\]. For instance, the work by \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/1908.08960)\] highlights that existing models often overfit to layout biases of current datasets and offer limited diversity in their outputs. Additionally, the evaluation of summarization systems has been criticized for its weak correlation with human judgment and lack of consideration for factual correctness \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/1908.08960)\].

To address these challenges, researchers have proposed various frameworks for improving summarization quality. For example, the PEGASUS model introduces a self-supervised objective for abstractive summarization, achieving state-of-the-art performance on multiple downstream tasks \[[5](http://arxiv.org/abs/1912.08777)\]. Similarly, the BART model, a denoising autoencoder, has shown effectiveness in both generation and comprehension tasks, achieving new state-of-the-art results on summarization benchmarks \[[18](http://arxiv.org/abs/1910.13461)\]. These models, however, still face limitations in handling long-form narratives due to their reliance on fixed-length inputs and outputs \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/1908.08960)\].

The introduction of multi-agent frameworks, such as MetaGPT, has also been explored for complex task-solving, including narrative generation \[[11](http://arxiv.org/abs/2308.00352)\]. These frameworks leverage modular outputs and structured coordination to enhance the quality of generated content. Similarly, the Re3 framework for generating longer stories uses recursive reprompting and revision to improve plot coherence and relevance \[[17](http://arxiv.org/abs/2210.06774)\]. These approaches demonstrate the potential of collaborative and iterative methods in enhancing the quality of narrative summaries.
