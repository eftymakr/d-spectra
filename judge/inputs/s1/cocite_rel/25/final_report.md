## Related Works

The integration of knowledge graphs (KGs) into large language models (LLMs) for enhancing reasoning capabilities has gained significant attention in recent years. While LLMs have demonstrated strong reasoning abilities, they often struggle with structured knowledge representation and multi-step reasoning tasks \[[18](http://arxiv.org/abs/2005.14165)\]. This challenge is further compounded by the limitations of traditional fact-checking methods, which typically rely on unstructured text corpora rather than structured knowledge sources \[[15](http://arxiv.org/abs/2305.13168)\]. 

Several approaches have been proposed to address these limitations. For instance, Program-Guided Fact-Checking (ProgramFC) introduces a novel framework that decomposes complex claims into sub-tasks, leveraging a shared library of specialized functions to guide the verification process \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2305.12744)\]. This method enhances transparency and data efficiency by providing clear explanations of the reasoning process. Similarly, Retrieval-Augmented Generation (RAG) models combine pre-trained language models with non-parametric memory, such as Wikipedia, to improve the factual accuracy of generated responses \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/2005.11401)\]. These models demonstrate that integrating external knowledge can significantly enhance the performance of LLMs on knowledge-intensive tasks.

Another line of research focuses on zero-shot reasoning capabilities of LLMs. Zero-shot Chain-of-Thought (Zero-shot-CoT) prompting has shown that LLMs can perform complex reasoning tasks without the need for task-specific examples, simply by adding a "Let's think step by step" prompt \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/2205.11916)\]. This approach has led to substantial improvements in benchmark tasks such as arithmetic and symbolic reasoning. However, LLMs still face challenges in reasoning over structured knowledge, such as KGs, without additional adaptation \[[15](http://arxiv.org/abs/2305.13168)\].

To address these issues, several studies have explored the use of KGs in conjunction with LLMs. For example, KEPLER proposes a unified model that integrates knowledge embedding with pre-trained language representation, enabling better integration of factual knowledge into LLMs \[[6](http://arxiv.org/abs/1911.06136)\]. Similarly, JAKET introduces a joint pre-training framework that models both KGs and language, allowing for more effective knowledge integration \[[8](http://arxiv.org/abs/2010.00796)\]. These models demonstrate that combining KGs with LLMs can lead to improved performance on knowledge-aware NLP tasks.

In addition, the use of knowledge graphs for commonsense reasoning has also been explored. KagNet, for instance, utilizes a knowledge-aware graph network module to perform explainable inferences based on external knowledge graphs \[[11](http://arxiv.org/abs/1909.02151)\]. This approach enables the model to generate transparent and interpretable reasoning paths, which is crucial for trustworthiness in real-world applications.
