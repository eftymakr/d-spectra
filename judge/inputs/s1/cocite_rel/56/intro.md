## Related Works

Cross-domain Sequential Recommendation (CDSR) has gained significant attention in recent years due to its ability to alleviate data sparsity and cold-start problems by leveraging user interactions across multiple domains \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2304.03891)\]. Traditional approaches often rely on overlapping users to transfer knowledge between domains, which limits their applicability in real-world scenarios where user overlap is limited \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2304.03891)\]. Several studies have attempted to address these challenges by introducing novel architectures and training strategies. For instance, \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/2106.02768)\] proposed a dual attentive sequential learning model that transfers information between two domains iteratively, improving click-through rate prediction. This model utilizes dual embeddings and dual attention mechanisms to enhance cross-domain recommendations. Similarly, \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/2012.00485)\] introduced a mixed information flow network that considers both behavioral and knowledge flows between domains, enabling more accurate recommendations by integrating cross-domain information effectively.

Another line of research focuses on improving the modeling of sequential patterns and cross-domain interactions. \[[4](http://arxiv.org/abs/2311.04590)\] introduced the DDTCDR model, which employs a latent orthogonal mapping to preserve relationships between users across different latent spaces while extracting preferences from multiple domains. This model also integrates an autoencoder approach to capture latent features, leading to significant performance improvements. Additionally, \[[5](http://arxiv.org/abs/2305.19860)\] proposed the DisenCDR model, which disentangles domain-shared and domain-specific information to enhance the effectiveness of cross-domain recommendations. By introducing mutual-information-based regularizers, DisenCDR ensures that shared information is effectively transferred without negatively impacting target domain performance.

In the context of shared-account cross-domain sequential recommendation, \[[6](http://arxiv.org/abs/2105.03300)\] introduced π-Net, a parallel information-sharing network that addresses the challenges of shared accounts and cross-domain interactions. π-Net includes a shared account filter unit and a cross-domain transfer unit, enabling the system to generate accurate recommendations for multiple domains simultaneously. Similarly, \[[7](http://arxiv.org/abs/1804.06769)\] proposed PSJNet, a parallel split-join network that splits user behavior into role-specific representations and joins them to obtain cross-domain recommendations. This approach effectively handles shared accounts and cross-domain interactions, demonstrating improved performance over existing methods.

Recent advances in large language models (LLMs) have also been explored for cross-domain recommendation tasks. \[[8](http://arxiv.org/abs/1910.05189)\] introduced BERT4Rec, a bidirectional encoder model that captures both left and right context in user behavior sequences, leading to more accurate recommendations. \[[9](http://arxiv.org/abs/2103.01696)\] further extended this idea by proposing a dual dynamic graph with hybrid metric training, which improves cross-domain sequential recommendations by capturing intra- and inter-domain transitions.
