Several studies have attempted to address these challenges by introducing novel architectures and training strategies. This model utilizes dual embeddings and dual attention mechanisms to enhance cross-domain recommendations. Similarly, \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/2012.00485)\] introduced a mixed information flow network that considers both behavioral and knowledge flows between domains, enabling more accurate recommendations by integrating cross-domain information effectively.

### <a id='category-large-language-model-integration'></a>Large Language Model Integration: 3 papers found.
The integration of Large Language Models (LLMs) into recommendation systems has gained significant attention for their ability to enhance the modeling of user preferences and item relationships through semantic understanding and reasoning capabilities \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2304.03891)\]. LLMs, trained on vast amounts of text data, offer high-quality representations of textual features and access to external knowledge, enabling them to capture complex interactions between users and items across domains \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2304.03891)\]. This has led to the development of two major paradigms in LLM-based recommendation systems: Discriminative LLM for Recommendation (DLLM4Rec) and Generative LLM for Recommendation (GLLM4Rec), with the latter being systematically categorized for the first time \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2304.03891)\]. Furthermore, recent approaches such as Chat-Rec leverage LLMs to build conversational recommender systems by converting user profiles and interactions into prompts, enabling more interactive and explainable recommendations \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/2106.02768)\]. These models also address challenges such as cold-start scenarios and cross-domain recommendations by utilizing prompt-based information injection and in-context learning \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/2106.02768)\]. Additionally, studies have explored the use of LLMs to improve the generalization and reasoning capabilities of recommender systems, particularly in handling sequential and cross-domain recommendation tasks \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/2012.00485)\]. The application of LLMs in recommendation systems is still evolving, with ongoing research focusing on pre-training, fine-tuning, and prompting strategies to further enhance performance and adaptability \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/2012.00485)\].
