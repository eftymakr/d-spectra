## Related Works

The growing adoption of cloud-native applications has led to increased interest in optimizing both resource usage and financial expenditures. Several studies have explored the use of container orchestration platforms like Kubernetes for managing workloads and improving operational efficiency. For instance, Kubernetes has been widely adopted for deploying and managing containerized applications due to its robust scheduling, scaling, and monitoring capabilities \[[4](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/b5634b15badc73f3cda72b960fd51ef03ac1265d)\]. However, the financial implications of using such platforms, particularly in terms of network costs, remain underexplored in existing literature.

FinOps, an emerging discipline that combines financial management with cloud operations, has gained traction as a way to optimize cloud costs and improve accountability in cloud spend \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2501.14753)\]. ABACUS, a FinOps solution, addresses these challenges by setting budgets, enforcing them through blocking new deployments, and alerting teams when spending exceeds thresholds \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2501.14753)\]. This approach aligns with the broader goal of achieving cost visibility and optimization in cloud environments. While FinOps has been applied to general cloud cost management, its application to network costs within Kubernetes clusters is a relatively new area of research.

Several studies have examined the performance and resource management of containerized applications in cloud-native environments.
