## Related Works

Protein dynamics are essential for understanding biological functions and properties, yet traditional methods such as molecular dynamics (MD) simulations are computationally expensive and time-consuming \[[1](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/21a4afa74caf78e28880b7fe4c30a09a5f9c14e3)\]. Recent advances in generative modeling have enabled more efficient and accurate protein structure prediction and conformation sampling by learning distributions over crystallographic structures \[[2](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/2cfc26b4af99f195b433fa7aa00f221b111c7cd4)\]. However, integrating physical supervision into these data-driven approaches remains a challenge, as standard energy-based objectives often lead to intractable optimization \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/2304.02198)\]. This section reviews related works that address these challenges and contribute to the field of protein structure prediction and conformation sampling.

Several studies have explored the use of generative models for protein structure prediction. For example, AlphaFold has achieved atomic accuracy in predicting protein structures, even in cases where no similar structure is available \[[4](http://arxiv.org/abs/2209.15611)\]. Similarly, EigenFold uses diffusion models to sample a distribution of structures from a given protein sequence, achieving a median TMScore of 0.84 \[[5](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/dc32a984b651256a8ec282be52310e6bd33d9815)\]. These models rely on learning from large datasets of known protein structures and have demonstrated significant improvements in accuracy and efficiency \[[6](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/18a93dc1558bf9d7534d0b416633cebaf75c1145)\].

Diffusion models have also been applied to protein conformation sampling. For instance, the work by \[[7](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/2eb1ed9d4baf548391d8220c0a8a3d0be79845ed)\] presents a diffusion-based generative model that generates protein backbone structures by denoising from a random, unfolded state towards a stable folded structure. This approach mirrors the natural folding process and has been shown to generate highly realistic protein structures \[[8](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/c49a0912595a1cc70aab63524f64ed08c92194a8)\]. Similarly, the Str2Str framework leverages an amortized denoising score matching objective trained on general crystal structures to enable zero-shot conformation sampling without reliance on simulation data \[[9](http://arxiv.org/abs/2306.03117)\]. These methods demonstrate the potential of diffusion models to generate diverse and accurate protein conformations.

The integration of physical supervision into generative models has also been explored. For example, the Boltzmann generators use neural networks to learn a coordinate transformation from a system’s configurations to a latent space representation, enabling efficient sampling of equilibrium states \[[10](http://arxiv.org/abs/2306.05445)\]. This approach combines deep learning with statistical mechanics to generate unbiased equilibrium samples of many-body systems and protein molecules \[[11](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/1ccd3a55ec1200295ce99ec8d934668dc8f753d8)\]. Similarly, the Distributional Graphormer (DiG) framework employs deep neural networks to transform a simple distribution towards the equilibrium distribution, conditioned on a descriptor of a molecular system \[[12](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/d9bb6d9590949392cae342b75323826188b1b8a2)\]. These methods highlight the importance of incorporating physical principles into generative models to improve their accuracy and efficiency.
