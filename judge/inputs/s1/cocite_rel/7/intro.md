## Related Works

Recent research has explored various methods for steering and controlling large language models (LLMs) to align with specific behaviors or preferences. A key challenge is identifying and manipulating internal activation directions that govern model behavior without relying on predefined templates or manual analysis. Prior work has focused on fine-tuning models or using prompting strategies to achieve desired outputs \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/2406.00034)\]. However, these approaches often require extensive training or are limited in their ability to generalize across different tasks and models.

Several studies have investigated the use of steering vectors to modify model behavior during inference. For example, \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2205.05124)\] introduced a method for extracting steering vectors directly from pretrained language models without fine-tuning, achieving high-quality text generation with minimal intervention. This approach leverages the latent space of the model to steer outputs toward desired targets. Similarly, \[[4](http://arxiv.org/abs/2312.06681)\] proposed Contrastive Activation Addition (CAA), which computes steering vectors by comparing residual activations between positive and negative examples of a behavior, enabling precise control over model outputs. CAA has been shown to significantly alter model behavior and improve performance on various tasks.

Another line of research has focused on improving the truthfulness of LLMs. \[[6](http://arxiv.org/abs/2306.03341)\] introduced Inference-Time Intervention (ITI), which shifts model activations during inference to enhance truthfulness. ITI operates by modifying a limited number of attention heads, achieving improvements on benchmarks like TruthfulQA without requiring extensive training data. This method demonstrates that LLMs may internally represent truth, even when they produce false outputs on the surface. Similarly, \[[16](http://arxiv.org/abs/2305.04160)\] proposed X-LLM, which converts multimodal inputs into language-like representations using X2L interfaces, enabling LLMs to handle visual, speech, and video inputs without requiring specialized architectures.

In the realm of refusal behavior, \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/2406.11717)\] showed that refusal in chat models is mediated by a single one-dimensional subspace, which can be manipulated to disable or elicit refusal. This finding highlights the importance of understanding model internals to develop practical methods for controlling behavior. Furthermore, \[[20](http://arxiv.org/abs/2503.02989)\] introduced a theoretical framework for model steering, proposing a method called CONFST that aligns multiple user preferences simultaneously by constructing confident directions within the model's activation space. This approach enables more flexible and powerful steering compared to existing bidirectional methods.

Additionally, \[[15](http://arxiv.org/abs/2204.14198)\] introduced Flamingo, a family of Visual Language Models capable of few-shot learning across multiple modalities, including images and videos. Flamingo's ability to rapidly adapt to new tasks through prompting has made it a significant advancement in multimodal learning. \[[18](http://arxiv.org/abs/2304.08485)\]
