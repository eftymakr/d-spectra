## Related Works

Recent advancements in information retrieval have seen a shift from traditional sparse vector space models to dense retrieval methods that leverage deep neural networks for improved semantic understanding \[[14](http://arxiv.org/abs/2004.04906)\]. These methods typically follow a representation-index-search pipeline, where tables are encoded into dense vectors, indexed, and then retrieved based on query similarity \[[15](http://arxiv.org/abs/2105.06365)\]. However, this approach is not without limitations. Errors during the representation and indexing phases can propagate and negatively affect search accuracy \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2204.10628)\]. Additionally, insufficient interaction between queries and tables hinders effective semantic alignment, limiting performance improvements \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2204.10628)\].

Several works have explored alternative approaches to address these challenges. The Differentiable Search Index (DSI) paradigm aims to unify indexing and retrieval into a single model, eliminating the need for separate stages \[[4](http://arxiv.org/abs/2202.06991)\]. This approach allows for direct query-to-docid mapping without the need for explicit indexing \[[4](http://arxiv.org/abs/2202.06991)\]. However, DSI models face challenges in handling dynamic updates, as retraining is required when new documents are added \[[6](http://arxiv.org/abs/2212.09744)\]. To address this, DSI++ was introduced, which enables continual learning by isolating parameters for new documents, thereby mitigating catastrophic forgetting \[[6](http://arxiv.org/abs/2212.09744)\].

Another line of research focuses on improving query-table interaction through semantic alignment. For instance, the ColBERT model introduces a late interaction architecture that independently encodes queries and documents, then computes fine-grained similarity through a cost-effective interaction step \[[9](http://arxiv.org/abs/2004.12832)\]. This approach enables efficient retrieval while maintaining high accuracy. Similarly, the TILDE model ranks documents based on both query and document likelihood, reducing the need for slow inference steps during query time \[[19](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/1a887834a7045d58e7655227dd95f7dd57f5ff4a)\].

In the context of table retrieval, several works have focused on semantic matching between queries and tables. The MTR model employs Gated Multimodal Units (GMUs) to learn joint representations of queries and tables, while also incorporating co-learning strategies to enhance performance \[[10](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/373588873bef360e3ecad2e091ac17895eb863af)\]. The Semantic Table Retrieval work \[[15](http://arxiv.org/abs/2105.06365)\] explores multiple semantic spaces and similarity measures to improve alignment between queries and tables, demonstrating significant improvements over existing baselines.

The Neural Corpus Indexer (NCI) proposes a sequence-to-sequence network that generates document identifiers directly for a given query, optimizing recall performance through a prefix-aware decoder and semantic document identifiers \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/2206.02743)\]. This approach has shown notable improvements on academic benchmarks, achieving a +21.4% relative enhancement for Recall@1 on the NQ320k dataset \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/2206.02743)\].
