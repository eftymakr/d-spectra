## Related Works

Fairness in re-ranking has garnered significant attention in recent years, with a growing body of research addressing the trade-offs between recommendation accuracy and fairness \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/1104.3913)\]. Early work on fairness in classification laid the groundwork for understanding how fairness constraints can be integrated into machine learning models \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/1104.3913)\]. This was followed by studies on fairness in recommendation systems, which highlighted the need to consider both consumer and producer fairness \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/2204.08085)\]. These works demonstrated that traditional approaches often focus on one side of the market, potentially leading to unfair exposure distribution \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/2204.08085)\].

The concept of fairness in rankings has also been explored in the context of exposure allocation, with researchers proposing frameworks to ensure that rankings are fair to both users and items \[[6](http://arxiv.org/abs/1802.07281)\]. These frameworks often rely on fairness constraints, such as demographic parity or disparate impact, to ensure equitable treatment \[[6](http://arxiv.org/abs/1802.07281)\]. However, the inherent trade-offs between fairness and accuracy have been a recurring theme in the literature \[[7](http://arxiv.org/abs/1609.05807)\]. This trade-off is often analyzed through the lens of utility maximization under fairness constraints \[[7](http://arxiv.org/abs/1609.05807)\].

Recent work has extended the discussion to two-sided fairness in recommender systems, emphasizing the need to balance fairness for both customers and producers \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/1704.06840)\]. This has led to the development of algorithms that ensure fair exposure for producers while maintaining recommendation quality for users \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/1704.06840)\]. These algorithms often employ fairness-aware re-ranking techniques, which adjust rankings to meet fairness criteria while preserving overall performance \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/1704.06840)\].

The issue of fairness in recommendation has also been addressed from a user-centric perspective, with studies showing that current systems often favor active users over inactive ones, leading to biased recommendations \[[5](http://arxiv.org/abs/2104.10671)\]. To mitigate this, researchers have proposed re-ranking approaches that incorporate fairness constraints to improve group fairness \[[5](http://arxiv.org/abs/2104.10671)\]. These methods aim to balance the quality of recommendations with the fairness of exposure \[[5](http://arxiv.org/abs/2104.10671)\].

In the context of ranking algorithms, fairness has been studied in relation to diversity and the distribution of content \[[4](http://arxiv.org/abs/2002.10764)\]. Researchers have proposed constrained ranking models that incorporate fairness metrics to ensure that the output rankings are both diverse and fair \[[4](http://arxiv.org/abs/2002.10764)\]. These models often use optimization techniques to balance fairness and utility \[[4](http://arxiv.org/abs/2002.10764)\].

The trade-off between fairness and accuracy has also been explored in the context of algorithmic decision-making, with studies showing that fairness constraints can lead to reduced performance \[[18](http://arxiv.org/abs/1701.08230)\]. This trade-off is often analyzed using economic principles, such as elasticity, to quantify the impact of fairness on accuracy \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/1104.3913)\]. The concept of elasticity has been used to model how changes in fairness constraints affect the overall performance of re-ranking algorithms \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/1104.3913)\].
