## Related Works

Anomaly detection in industrial settings has garnered significant attention due to its critical role in quality control and fault prevention in modern manufacturing processes. A wide range of approaches have been proposed, from classical statistical methods to deep learning-based solutions. Early methods often relied on handcrafted features and statistical models, but these were limited in their ability to generalize across diverse defect types \[[5](http://arxiv.org/abs/2301.11514)\]. With the advent of deep learning, several works have focused on improving detection accuracy while addressing computational and deployment constraints.

One of the earliest deep learning approaches for anomaly detection is based on autoencoders, which learn to reconstruct normal data and detect anomalies by identifying reconstruction errors \[[14](http://arxiv.org/abs/2201.10703)\]. However, these models often require large amounts of data and are computationally expensive. More recently, methods such as PatchCore \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2106.08265)\] and PaDiM \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/2011.08785)\] have been introduced to address the cold-start problem, where models are trained only on normal data. PatchCore leverages a maximally representative memory bank of nominal patch features, achieving state-of-the-art performance on the MVTec AD benchmark with an AUROC score of up to 99.6% \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2106.08265)\]. Similarly, PaDiM uses a pretrained CNN for patch embedding and multivariate Gaussian distributions to model the normal class, outperforming existing methods on both detection and localization \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/2011.08785)\].

The need for efficient and deployable models has also driven research into lightweight architectures. For example, the MVTec AD dataset \[[6](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/3aa681914a7da79f7d7293f51a058eefe61c8bb7)\] provides a comprehensive benchmark for evaluating unsupervised anomaly detection methods, including convolutional autoencoders, GANs, and feature descriptors. The dataset includes over 5,354 high-resolution images with pixel-accurate ground truth annotations, making it a key resource for evaluating new methods \[[6](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/3aa681914a7da79f7d7293f51a058eefe61c8bb7)\]. Additionally, the MVTec 3D-AD dataset \[[4](http://arxiv.org/abs/2112.09045)\] extends this work to 3D data, enabling the detection of geometric anomalies in manufactured objects.

Recent advances have also focused on hybrid fusion schemes for multimodal anomaly detection. The M3DM method \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/2303.00601)\] combines 3D point clouds and RGB images using a hybrid fusion approach, achieving superior performance on the MVTec-3D AD dataset. This work highlights the importance of aligning features across modalities and using memory banks to retain discriminative information.

Another important direction is the development of lightweight models for deployment on embedded devices. The MCUNet framework \[[15](http://arxiv.org/abs/2007.10319)\] enables efficient inference on microcontrollers, achieving ImageNet-level accuracy with significantly reduced memory usage.
