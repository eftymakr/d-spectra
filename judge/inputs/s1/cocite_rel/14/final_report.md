## Related Works

Video super-resolution (VSR) has been a focal point in computer vision, with recent advances leveraging deep learning to enhance the quality and efficiency of high-resolution video generation. Early methods relied on explicit motion compensation and optical flow estimation to align frames, but these approaches often introduced artifacts or were computationally intensive \[[1](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/c6a8ff4d9dcdf9e5d82310b847af999ed6655f65)\]. For instance, methods like Deep Video Super-Resolution Network Using Dynamic Upsampling Filters \[[1](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/c6a8ff4d9dcdf9e5d82310b847af999ed6655f65)\] proposed an end-to-end framework that avoids explicit motion compensation by generating dynamic upsampling filters directly from the input. This approach significantly improved temporal consistency and reduced computational overhead.

Subsequent work introduced spatio-temporal networks and motion compensation techniques to better exploit temporal correlations \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/1611.05250)\]. The Real-Time Video Super-Resolution with Spatio-Temporal Networks and Motion Compensation \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/1611.05250)\] utilized early fusion, slow fusion, and 3D convolutions to process multiple frames jointly, improving reconstruction accuracy while maintaining real-time performance. Additionally, the Frame-Recurrent Video Super-Resolution \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/1801.04590)\] introduced a recurrent framework that uses previously inferred high-resolution estimates to super-resolve subsequent frames, reducing computational cost and improving temporal consistency.

The challenge of temporal alignment remains a critical issue in VSR. TDAN \[[4](http://arxiv.org/abs/1812.02898)\] addressed this by proposing a temporally-deformable alignment network that aligns reference and supporting frames at the feature level without computing optical flow, thereby reducing artifacts and improving alignment accuracy. Similarly, the Efficient Video Super-Resolution through Recurrent Latent Space Propagation \[[6](http://arxiv.org/abs/1909.08080)\] introduced a recurrent latent space propagation (RLSP) algorithm that implicitly propagates temporal information between frames, achieving significant speed improvements over existing methods.

The need for real-world performance has led to the development of methods that handle complex degradations and ensure temporal consistency. The Real-world Video Super-resolution: A Benchmark Dataset and A Decomposition based Learning Scheme \[[18](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/2d5f68c536b12c2ac52168c8928e5ec887679211)\] emphasized the importance of real-world datasets and decomposition-based learning schemes to better capture the complexities of real-world video degradation. This was further extended by BasicVSR++ \[[15](http://arxiv.org/abs/2104.13371)\], which improved upon BasicVSR by introducing second-order grid propagation and flow-guided deformable alignment, achieving better performance with similar computational constraints.

Recent approaches have also focused on integrating attention mechanisms and recurrent structures to enhance temporal modeling. Video super-resolution (VSR) has seen significant advancements with the integration of deep learning techniques, particularly focusing on improving temporal consistency and computational efficiency. Early methods relied heavily on motion estimation and compensation, which often introduced artifacts and increased computational costs \[[1](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/c6a8ff4d9dcdf9e5d82310b847af999ed6655f65)\]. Subsequent approaches, such as those using convolutional neural networks with motion compensation, attempted to merge information from multiple low-resolution frames but faced limitations in temporal consistency and computational efficiency \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/1801.04590)\]. To address these issues, recurrent frameworks were introduced, allowing for the use of previously inferred high-resolution estimates to super-resolve subsequent frames, thereby promoting temporal consistency and reducing computational load \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/1801.04590)\]. Further improvements were made with the introduction of progressive fusion networks, which effectively utilize spatio-temporal information and avoid complex motion estimation processes, leading to faster and more efficient results \[[7](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/755227d7ea41b1d90df31f411f9ecbc998c20d26)\]. Additionally, transformer-based models, such as the Video Restoration Transformer (VRT), have been proposed to handle multiple video restoration tasks by leveraging parallel frame prediction and advanced attention mechanisms, achieving superior performance across various benchmarks \[[8](http://arxiv.org/abs/2201.12288)\]. The recurrent structure has also been enhanced with innovations like second-order grid propagation and flow-guided deformable alignment, leading to improved performance and generalizability \[[15](http://arxiv.org/abs/2104.13371)\]. Moreover, methods like the Hidden State Attention (HSA) module have been developed to mitigate artifacts in real-world scenarios by replacing hidden states with cleaner counterparts, resulting in faster and more accurate results \[[17](http://arxiv.org/abs/2212.07339)\]. These advancements highlight the ongoing efforts to enhance the efficiency, quality, and robustness of VSR techniques.
