## Related Works

Understanding the evolution of feature representations across layers in large language models (LLMs) is critical for improving interpretability and robustness \[[17](http://arxiv.org/abs/2410.17875)\]. Recent studies have identified critical layers linked to specific functions or behaviors, but these efforts typically rely on data-dependent analyses of fine-tuned models, limiting their use to post-hoc settings \[[17](http://arxiv.org/abs/2410.17875)\]. In contrast, we introduce a data-oblivious approach to identify intrinsic critical layers in pre-fine-tuned LLMs by analyzing representation dynamics via Centered Kernel Alignment (CKA) \[[17](http://arxiv.org/abs/2410.17875)\]. This method enables a more generalizable understanding of how layers are affected during fine-tuning, independent of the specific dataset or task \[[17](http://arxiv.org/abs/2410.17875)\].

Several works have explored parameter-efficient fine-tuning methods, which aim to reduce the number of trainable parameters while maintaining performance \[[8](http://arxiv.org/abs/2106.09685)\]. For example, LoRA introduces low-rank adaptation by injecting trainable matrices into each layer of the Transformer architecture, significantly reducing the number of parameters and computational costs \[[8](http://arxiv.org/abs/2106.09685)\]. Similarly, BitFit focuses on modifying only the bias terms of the model, showing that this approach can be competitive with full fine-tuning, especially when using small-to-medium training data \[[5](http://arxiv.org/abs/2106.10199)\]. These methods align with our goal of identifying critical layers for efficient fine-tuning, as they suggest that only a subset of parameters may be necessary to achieve desired performance \[[5](http://arxiv.org/abs/2106.10199)\]\[[8](http://arxiv.org/abs/2106.09685)\].

The concept of skill localization has also been explored, where a small subset of parameters is identified as responsible for most of the model's performance \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/2302.06600)\]. This approach, which involves grafting the fine-tuned values of a small subset of parameters onto the pre-trained model, has shown significant improvements in calibration and out-of-distribution performance \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/2302.06600)\]. This is relevant to our work, as it suggests that identifying critical layers could lead to more efficient and effective fine-tuning strategies \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/2302.06600)\].

Instruction tuning has been shown to improve zero-shot learning performance by fine-tuning models on tasks described via natural language instructions \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/2109.01652)\]. This method, which involves training a model on a collection of tasks verbalized via instruction templates, has demonstrated substantial improvements in performance on unseen tasks \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/2109.01652)\]. While our work focuses on layer-level analysis, the success of instruction tuning highlights the importance of task-specific adaptation, which can be achieved through efficient fine-tuning strategies \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/2109.01652)\].
