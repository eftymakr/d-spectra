## Related Works

Vision Transformers (ViTs) have emerged as a powerful alternative to convolutional neural networks (CNNs) in computer vision tasks, leveraging the transformer architecture to effectively capture global context \[[5](http://arxiv.org/abs/2010.11929)\]. However, the deployment of ViTs on hardware, particularly FPGAs, has been challenging due to the complexity of arithmetic operations such as Softmax and LayerNorm, which are sensitive to model accuracy and difficult to map onto low-precision hardware \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2208.05163)\]. Existing approaches often rely on CPU execution for these complex operations, leading to suboptimal performance due to communication overhead between the CPU and accelerator \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2208.05163)\].

Several works have explored hardware acceleration for ViTs. Auto-ViT-Acc proposes an FPGA-aware acceleration framework that integrates mixed-scheme quantization, achieving higher accuracy than state-of-the-art quantization methods while improving frame rates \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2208.05163)\]. VAQF introduces a fully automatic software-hardware co-design framework for low-bit ViTs, enabling real-time execution on FPGAs by optimizing quantization precision and accelerator settings \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/2201.06618)\]. ViTCoD further advances this by introducing algorithm and accelerator co-design, significantly reducing data movement costs and achieving substantial speedups over general computing platforms \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/2210.09573)\]. These methods, however, often focus on specific aspects of ViT acceleration and do not fully address the mapping of complex operations onto hardware.

The need for efficient integer-only inference has also been addressed. I-ViT introduces an integer-only quantization scheme for ViTs, enabling inference with integer arithmetic and bit-shifting, and achieving comparable accuracy to full-precision models \[[8](http://arxiv.org/abs/2207.01405)\]. Similarly, the integer-only and group-vector systolic accelerator proposed in another work enables efficient mapping of ViTs on edge devices by simplifying non-linear operations and accelerating matrix multiplication \[[10](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/a6a828dcaaf373554b53b8f8002de126f3e38695)\]. These approaches demonstrate the potential of integer arithmetic for ViT inference but often require custom hardware designs.

Recent efforts have also focused on improving the efficiency of ViT inference through architectural innovations. LeViT introduces a hybrid architecture that combines the efficiency of CNNs with the flexibility of ViTs, achieving faster inference speeds \[[4](http://arxiv.org/abs/2104.01136)\]. CvT integrates convolutions into ViTs to improve performance and efficiency, leveraging the strengths of both architectures \[[6](http://arxiv.org/abs/2103.15808)\]. CrossViT proposes a multi-scale transformer architecture that combines small and large patches to enhance feature representation \[[7](http://arxiv.org/abs/2103.14899)\]. These models demonstrate the ongoing exploration of hybrid and specialized architectures for ViT efficiency.

### <a id='category-quantization-and-precision-trade-offs-in-vit-acceleration'></a>Quantization and Precision Trade-offs in ViT Acceleration: 3 papers found.
Vision transformers (ViTs) have gained significant attention for their ability to capture global context in computer vision tasks, yet their deployment on hardware remains challenging due to their complex arithmetic operations, such as Softmax and LayerNorm, which are sensitive to precision \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2208.05163)\]. Prior work has primarily focused on optimizing parallelism in matrix multiplication while offloading these complex operations to the CPU, leading to communication overhead and suboptimal performance \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2208.05163)\]. Recent efforts have aimed to address this by integrating quantization strategies with hardware acceleration, particularly on FPGAs, to reduce both computation and storage demands \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/2201.06618)\]. For instance, mixed-scheme quantization has been explored to maintain high accuracy while improving frame rates, achieving up to 5.6x speedup with minimal accuracy loss \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2208.05163)\]. Additionally, frameworks like VAQF have been introduced to automatically determine quantization precision and optimize hardware settings based on target frame rates, enabling real-time execution of state-of-the-art ViT models on FPGAs \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/2201.06618)\]. Another approach, Fully Quantized Vision Transformer (FQ-ViT), has demonstrated that performance degradation can be minimized through methods like Power-of-Two Factor (PTF) and Log-Int-Softmax (LIS), achieving near-lossless accuracy with 4-bit quantization \[[17](http://arxiv.org/abs/2111.13824)\]. These advancements highlight the growing interest in combining quantization with hardware-specific optimizations to enable efficient ViT acceleration, addressing both accuracy and performance trade-offs.

### <a id='category-challenges-in-vit-hardware-acceleration'></a>Challenges in ViT Hardware Acceleration: 1 papers found.

[Snippet]: «Vision Transformers (ViTs) have achieved state-of-the-art performance on various vision tasks. However, ViTs’ self-attention module is still arguably a major bottleneck, limiting their achievable hardware efficiency and more extensive applications to resource constrained platforms. Meanwhile, existing accelerators dedicated to NLP Transformers are not optimal for ViTs. This is because there is a large difference between ViTs and Transformers for natural language processing (NLP) tasks: ViTs have a relatively fixed number of input tokens, whose attention maps can be pruned by up to 90% even with fixed sparse patterns, without severely hurting the model accuracy (e.g., <=1.5% under 90% pruning ratio); while NLP Transformers need to handle input sequences of varying numbers of tokens and rely on on-the-fly predictions of dynamic sparse attention patterns for each input to achieve a decent sparsity (e.g., >=50%). To this end, we propose a dedicated algorithm and accelerator co-design framework dubbed ViTCoD for accelerating ViTs. Specifically, on the algorithm level, ViTCoD prunes and polarizes the attention maps to have either denser or sparser fixed patterns for regularizing two levels of workloads without hurting the accuracy, largely reducing the attention computations while leaving room for alleviating the remaining dominant data movements; on top of that, we further integrate a lightweight and learnable auto-encoder module to enable trading the dominant high-cost data movements for lower-cost computations. On the hardware level, we develop a dedicated accelerator to simultaneously coordinate the aforementioned enforced denser and sparser workloads for boosted hardware utilization, while integrating on-chip encoder and decoder engines to leverage ViTCoD’s algorithm pipeline for much reduced data movements. Extensive experiments and ablation studies validate that ViTCoD largely reduces the dominant data movement costs, achieving speedups of up to 235.3×, 142.9×, 86.0×, 10.1×, and 6.8× over general computing platforms CPUs, EdgeGPUs, GPUs, and prior-art Transformer accelerators SpAtten and Sanger under an attention sparsity of 90%, respectively. Our code implementation is available at https://github.com/GATECH-EIC/ViTCoD.»

### <a id='category-architectural-innovations-in-vit-models'></a>Architectural Innovations in ViT Models: 10 papers found.
The category "Architectural Innovations in ViT Models" highlights various advancements aimed at improving the performance, efficiency, and applicability of Vision Transformers (ViTs) in computer vision tasks. These innovations include hybrid architectures that combine the strengths of convolutional neural networks (CNNs) and transformers, such as LeViT \[[4](http://arxiv.org/abs/2104.01136)\], which optimizes the speed/accuracy trade-off for fast inference, and Convolutional vision Transformer (CvT) \[[6](http://arxiv.org/abs/2103.15808)\], which integrates convolutions into ViT to enhance performance and efficiency while maintaining the benefits of transformers. Additionally, models like CrossViT \[[7](http://arxiv.org/abs/2103.14899)\] introduce dual-branch transformers to process image patches of different sizes, improving feature representation through attention mechanisms. The PiT \[[9](http://arxiv.org/abs/2103.16302)\] model further explores spatial dimension reduction principles from CNNs, enhancing transformer-based architectures for better generalization. MobileViT \[[11](http://arxiv.org/abs/2110.02178)\] combines CNNs and ViTs to create a lightweight network suitable for mobile vision tasks, while T2T-ViT \[[12](http://arxiv.org/abs/2101.11986)\] reduces computational costs by structuring tokens more effectively. The TNT \[[13](http://arxiv.org/abs/2103.00112)\] architecture introduces nested attention mechanisms to enhance feature aggregation, and Evo-ViT \[[14](http://arxiv.org/abs/2108.01390)\] proposes a token evolution approach to reduce computational costs without sacrificing performance. Finally, the Swin Transformer \[[15](http://arxiv.org/abs/2103.14030)\] introduces a hierarchical design with shifted windows, enabling efficient and scalable vision tasks. These works collectively demonstrate the ongoing efforts to refine ViT architectures for diverse applications, balancing accuracy, efficiency, and adaptability.

### <a id='category-integer-only-quantization-techniques-for-vits'></a>Integer-Only Quantization Techniques for ViTs: 1 papers found.

[Snippet]: «Vision Transformers (ViTs) have achieved state-of-the-art performance on various computer vision applications. However, these models have considerable storage and computational overheads, making their deployment and efficient inference on edge devices challenging. Quantization is a promising approach to reducing model complexity, and the dyadic arithmetic pipeline can allow the quantized models to perform efficient integer-only inference. Unfortunately, dyadic arithmetic is based on the homogeneity condition in convolutional neural networks, which is not applicable to the non-linear components in ViTs, making integer-only inference of ViTs an open issue. In this paper, we propose I-ViT, an integer-only quantization scheme for ViTs, to enable ViTs to perform the entire computational graph of inference with integer arithmetic and bit-shifting, and without any floating-point arithmetic. In I-ViT, linear operations (e.g., MatMul and Dense) follow the integer-only pipeline with dyadic arithmetic, and non-linear operations (e.g., Softmax, GELU, and LayerNorm) are approximated by the proposed light-weight integer-only arithmetic methods. More specifically, I-ViT applies the proposed Shiftmax and ShiftGELU, which are designed to use integer bit-shifting to approximate the corresponding floating-point operations. We evaluate I-ViT on various benchmark models and the results show that integer-only INT8 quantization achieves comparable (or even slightly higher) accuracy to the full-precision (FP) baseline. Furthermore, we utilize TVM for practical hardware deployment on the GPU’s integer arithmetic units, achieving 3.72 ~ 4.11 inference speedup compared to the FP model. Code of both Pytorch and TVM is released at https://github.com/zkkli/I-ViT.»
