## Related Works

Economic inequality remains a critical challenge for policymakers globally, affecting education, healthcare, and social stability \[[5](http://arxiv.org/abs/1804.09284)\]. Traditional tax systems, such as the U.S. federal income tax, aim to reduce inequality but often lack the adaptability required to address heterogeneous taxpayer behavior and irrational decision-making \[[5](http://arxiv.org/abs/1804.09284)\]. Recent research has explored dynamic taxation models, such as the Saez Optimal Taxation framework, which adjusts tax rates based on economic conditions \[[5](http://arxiv.org/abs/1804.09284)\]. However, these models typically assume rational agents and fail to account for the complexity of human behavior \[[5](http://arxiv.org/abs/1804.09284)\]. 

Agent-based modeling (ABM) has emerged as a powerful tool for simulating economic systems, allowing for the representation of heterogeneous agents and their interactions \[[15](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/ebc3c689a69f536d0b089d00cb19c8e8cce0f648)\]. ABM enables the exploration of how individual behaviors and local interactions can lead to macro-level economic patterns \[[15](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/ebc3c689a69f536d0b089d00cb19c8e8cce0f648)\]. This approach has been applied to various economic and social policy domains, including the simulation of tax policies and their impact on inequality \[[15](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/ebc3c689a69f536d0b089d00cb19c8e8cce0f648)\]. 

Large language models (LLMs) have also gained traction in economic modeling due to their ability to process and generate natural language, enabling more sophisticated agent behaviors \[[21](http://arxiv.org/abs/2402.15116)\]. The integration of LLMs with ABM, as demonstrated in the TaxAgent framework, allows for the simulation of adaptive tax policies that can respond to changing economic conditions \[[5](http://arxiv.org/abs/1804.09284)\]. This approach leverages the reasoning capabilities of LLMs to optimize tax rates while considering the diverse behaviors of taxpayers \[[5](http://arxiv.org/abs/1804.09284)\]. 

Several studies have explored the use of reinforcement learning (RL) in ABMs to improve the accuracy of agent decision-making \[[13](http://arxiv.org/abs/2006.05048)\]. RL-based agents can learn to maximize rewards, leading to more realistic economic simulations \[[13](http://arxiv.org/abs/2006.05048)\]. Additionally, multi-agent reinforcement learning (MARL) has been used to model complex interactions between agents in economic systems \[[6](http://arxiv.org/abs/1711.00832)\]. These approaches highlight the potential of combining RL with ABM to create more dynamic and responsive economic models \[[6](http://arxiv.org/abs/1711.00832)\]. 

The use of procedural generation in reinforcement learning benchmarks has also been explored, providing diverse environments for training and evaluating agents \[[8](http://arxiv.org/abs/1912.01588)\]. This technique is particularly useful in economic modeling, where the ability to simulate a wide range of scenarios is essential \[[8](http://arxiv.org/abs/1912.01588)\]. 

In the context of tax policy, recent work has focused on automating the maintenance of tax software using LLMs, which can interpret and translate tax law amendments into executable code \[[19](http://arxiv.org/abs/2504.18693)\]. This approach aims to improve the accuracy and efficiency of tax preparation systems, which is crucial for maintaining compliance with evolving regulations \[[19](http://arxiv.org/abs/2504.18693)\].

### <a id='category-integration-of-large-language-models-in-economic-modeling'></a>Integration of Large Language Models in Economic Modeling: 5 papers found.
The integration of large language models (LLMs) into economic modeling has gained significant traction, with researchers exploring their potential to enhance decision-making, policy analysis, and adaptive taxation frameworks. Several studies have demonstrated the ability of LLMs to simulate heterogeneous economic agents and model complex decision-making processes, such as in multi-LLM-agent-based (MLAB) frameworks \[[4](http://arxiv.org/abs/2502.16879)\]. These frameworks leverage the inherent variability in LLMs' analytical capabilities to represent diverse cognitive traits among agents, offering a more realistic simulation of economic behavior. Additionally, the Experiential Learning (ExpeL) agent has been introduced as a method to enable LLMs to learn from past experiences without requiring parametric updates, thereby improving their adaptability and generalization \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2308.10144)\]. Retrieval-Augmented Generation (RAG) has also emerged as a promising approach to enhance the accuracy and reliability of LLMs by integrating external knowledge sources, addressing issues such as hallucination and outdated information \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/2312.10997)\]. Furthermore, the ReAct approach has been proposed to combine reasoning and action generation, allowing LLMs to interact with external environments and generate more interpretable and trustworthy decision-making processes \[[10](http://arxiv.org/abs/2210.03629)\]. These advancements highlight the growing potential of LLMs in economic modeling, particularly in simulating heterogeneous agents, improving policy analysis, and enhancing decision-making through adaptive learning and external knowledge integration.

### <a id='category-reinforcement-learning-in-multi-agent-systems'></a>Reinforcement Learning in Multi-Agent Systems: 7 papers found.
In the context of multi-agent reinforcement learning (MARL), several studies have explored methods to improve coordination and adaptability in complex environments. Traditional approaches like Q-learning face challenges due to the non-stationarity of multi-agent environments, while policy gradient methods suffer from increased variance with more agents \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/1706.02275)\]. To address these issues, researchers have proposed actor-critic methods that consider the policies of other agents, enabling more effective coordination \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/1706.02275)\]. Additionally, some studies have introduced training regimens using ensembles of policies to enhance robustness in multi-agent systems \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/1706.02275)\]. In contrast, independent reinforcement learning (InRL) has been found to lead to overfitting, where agents fail to generalize during execution \[[6](http://arxiv.org/abs/1711.00832)\]. To mitigate this, new metrics like joint-policy correlation have been introduced to quantify the impact of policy interactions \[[6](http://arxiv.org/abs/1711.00832)\]. Furthermore, scalable implementations have been developed to reduce memory requirements, allowing for the generalization of policies across various scenarios such as gridworld coordination games and poker \[[6](http://arxiv.org/abs/1711.00832)\]. The integration of large language models (LLMs) with agent-based modeling (ABM) has also been explored to create adaptive systems capable of handling heterogeneous agents and dynamic environments \[[13](http://arxiv.org/abs/2006.05048)\]. These approaches highlight the ongoing efforts to develop more effective and scalable MARL methods that can address the complexities of multi-agent interactions.

### <a id='category-agent-based-modeling-for-economic-simulation'></a>Agent-Based Modeling for Economic Simulation: 8 papers found.
Agent-based modeling (ABM) has gained significant traction as a powerful tool for simulating complex socio-economic systems, offering a flexible framework to explore emergent behaviors and adaptive interactions among heterogeneous agents \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2308.10144)\]. Unlike traditional economic models that often rely on reductionist assumptions and unbounded rationality, ABM incorporates boundedly rational agents that interact locally, leading to macro-level patterns that reflect real-world complexities \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2308.10144)\]. This approach has been applied to study economic inequality, where ABM simulations demonstrate how simple behavioral rules can generate systemic disparities \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2308.10144)\]. Moreover, recent advancements integrate large language models (LLMs) into ABM, enhancing the ability to model adaptive policies and simulate real-world scenarios with greater accuracy \[[4](http://arxiv.org/abs/2502.16879)\]. For instance, the ABIDES-Economist simulator incorporates reinforcement learning to refine agent behaviors based on economic data, improving the realism and policy relevance of simulations \[[5](http://arxiv.org/abs/1804.09284)\]. Additionally, ABM has been used to analyze tax evasion dynamics, leveraging multi-dimensional models to capture social and economic interactions that influence taxpayer behavior \[[7](http://arxiv.org/abs/2504.01242)\]. These developments highlight the growing potential of ABM in addressing economic challenges through data-driven, adaptive policy design \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2308.10144)\]\[[4](http://arxiv.org/abs/2502.16879)\]\[[5](http://arxiv.org/abs/1804.09284)\]\[[7](http://arxiv.org/abs/2504.01242)\].
