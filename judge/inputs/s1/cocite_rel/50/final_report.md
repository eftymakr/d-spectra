## Related Works

Knowledge graphs (KGs) have emerged as a critical tool for representing and reasoning over structured knowledge, enabling applications such as question answering, information retrieval, and link prediction \[[9](http://arxiv.org/abs/2002.00388)\]. Early approaches focused on embedding entities and relations into continuous vector spaces, with models like TransE \[[6](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/2a3f862199883ceff5e3c74126f0c80770653e05)\] and DistMult \[[7](http://arxiv.org/abs/1707.01476)\] being among the first to achieve state-of-the-art results. However, these models often struggled with capturing complex relational patterns and preserving mapping properties such as reflexivity and many-to-many relations \[[6](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/2a3f862199883ceff5e3c74126f0c80770653e05)\]. To address these limitations, more advanced models such as TransH \[[6](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/2a3f862199883ceff5e3c74126f0c80770653e05)\] and ConvE \[[7](http://arxiv.org/abs/1707.01476)\] were introduced. TransH models relations as hyperplanes, allowing for better preservation of relational properties while maintaining efficiency, whereas ConvE leverages multi-layer convolutional networks to achieve high performance on link prediction tasks with fewer parameters \[[7](http://arxiv.org/abs/1707.01476)\]. 

The importance of relational modeling in KGs has also been highlighted in the development of Relational Graph Convolutional Networks (R-GCNs) \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/1703.06103)\], which are specifically designed to handle the multi-relational nature of knowledge bases. R-GCNs have shown significant improvements over traditional factorization models, particularly in tasks such as link prediction and entity classification \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/1703.06103)\]. Additionally, the challenge of handling long-tail relations, where many relations have few known triples, has led to the development of one-shot relational learning frameworks \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/1808.09040)\]. These frameworks aim to predict new facts with minimal training data, leveraging both learned embeddings and graph structures to improve performance \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/1808.09040)\].

Recent work has also explored the integration of large language models (LLMs) with KGs to enhance reasoning and knowledge representation \[[4](http://arxiv.org/abs/2306.08302)\]. While LLMs excel in generalization and natural language understanding, they often lack structured factual knowledge, which KGs can provide. Conversely, KGs are difficult to scale and update, making it essential to develop methods that allow for the dynamic integration of new knowledge \[[4](http://arxiv.org/abs/2306.08302)\]. This has led to the development of hybrid approaches, such as KEPLER \[[5](http://arxiv.org/abs/1911.06136)\], which combines pre-trained language models with knowledge embedding techniques to better capture factual knowledge and improve performance on both NLP tasks and KG link prediction.

In parallel, the need for robust and scalable KGs has driven the development of large-scale knowledge graph benchmarks and datasets \[[8](http://arxiv.org/abs/1612.03975)\]. For example, ConceptNet \[[8](http://arxiv.org/abs/1612.03975)\] provides a multilingual graph of general knowledge, enhancing word embeddings with semantic relationships. Such resources are crucial for training and evaluating KG embedding models, as well as for advancing research in knowledge acquisition and completion \[[9](http://arxiv.org/abs/2002.00388)\].

### <a id='category-graph-neural-networks-for-kgs'></a>Graph Neural Networks for KGs: 3 papers found.
The category "Graph Neural Networks for KGs" focuses on the application of graph neural networks (GNNs) to knowledge graph (KG) completion tasks, such as link prediction and entity classification. These models are designed to handle the complex, multi-relational nature of KGs, which are often incomplete and require capturing intricate relationships between entities \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/1703.06103)\]. For instance, Relational Graph Convolutional Networks (R-GCNs) have been introduced to improve link prediction by leveraging multiple inference steps across the graph, achieving significant improvements over baseline models like DistMult, with a 29.8% increase in performance on the FB15k-237 dataset \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/1703.06103)\]. Similarly, ConvE, a multi-layer convolutional model, has demonstrated state-of-the-art results while being highly parameter efficient, outperforming models like DistMult and R-GCN with fewer parameters \[[7](http://arxiv.org/abs/1707.01476)\]. Additionally, challenges such as test set leakage in datasets like WN18 and FB15k have been identified, prompting the development of robust variants to ensure fair evaluation \[[7](http://arxiv.org/abs/1707.01476)\]. Furthermore, recent work has addressed the issue of inductive learning in KGs by introducing neighborhood aggregators that consider the unordered and unequal nature of entity neighbors, leading to the development of models like the Logic Attention Network (LAN) \[[11](http://arxiv.org/abs/1811.01399)\]. These advancements highlight the ongoing efforts to enhance the expressiveness and efficiency of GNNs for KGs, addressing both structural and practical challenges in real-world applications.

### <a id='category-entity-and-relation-embedding-models'></a>Entity and Relation Embedding Models: 3 papers found.
The category "Entity and Relation Embedding Models" focuses on the development of methods for representing entities and relations in knowledge graphs using continuous vector spaces. These models aim to improve link prediction and other tasks by capturing the semantic relationships between entities. Early approaches like TransE \[[2](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/994afdf0db0cb0456f4f76468380822c2f532726)\] and TransH \[[6](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/2a3f862199883ceff5e3c74126f0c80770653e05)\] have been pivotal, with TransE being noted for its efficiency and state-of-the-art performance, although it struggles with certain mapping properties such as one-to-many or many-to-one relations. To address these limitations, TransH introduces a hyperplane-based approach that preserves these properties while maintaining model efficiency \[[6](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/2a3f862199883ceff5e3c74126f0c80770653e05)\]. More recently, models like the bilinear formulation have shown significant improvements in link prediction tasks, achieving higher accuracy than TransE on datasets like Freebase \[[10](http://arxiv.org/abs/1412.6575)\]. These models often operate under a unified learning framework where entities are represented as vectors and relations are modeled as bilinear or linear mappings \[[10](http://arxiv.org/abs/1412.6575)\]. The effectiveness of these models is further demonstrated through their ability to capture relational semantics and support compositional reasoning, as evidenced by their success in mining logical rules from knowledge graphs \[[10](http://arxiv.org/abs/1412.6575)\]. The ongoing advancements in this area highlight the importance of balancing model complexity with efficiency to enable scalable and accurate knowledge graph completion.

[Snippet]: «Large language models (LLMs), such as ChatGPT and GPT4, are making new waves in the field of natural language processing and artificial intelligence, due to their emergent ability and generalizability. However, LLMs are black-box models, which often fall short of capturing and accessing factual knowledge. In contrast, Knowledge Graphs (KGs), Wikipedia, and Huapu for example, are structured knowledge models that explicitly store rich factual knowledge. KGs can enhance LLMs by providing external knowledge for inference and interpretability. Meanwhile, KGs are difficult to construct and evolve by nature, which challenges the existing methods in KGs to generate new facts and represent unseen knowledge. Therefore, it is complementary to unify LLMs and KGs together and, simultaneously, leverage their advantages. In this article, we present a forward-looking roadmap for the unification of LLMs and KGs. Our roadmap consists of three general frameworks, namely: 1) KG-enhanced LLMs, which incorporate KGs during the pre-training and inference phases of LLMs, or for the purpose of enhancing understanding of the knowledge learned by LLMs; 2) LLM-augmented KGs, that leverage LLMs for different KG tasks such as embedding, completion, construction, graph-to-text generation, and question answering; and 3) Synergized LLMs + KGs, in which LLMs and KGs play equal roles and work in a mutually beneficial way to enhance both LLMs and KGs for bidirectional reasoning driven by both data and knowledge. We review and summarize existing efforts within these three frameworks in our roadmap and pinpoint their future research directions.»

### <a id='category-knowledge-graph-foundations'></a>Knowledge Graph Foundations: 2 papers found.
The development of knowledge graphs has significantly advanced the field of natural language processing (NLP) by providing structured representations of semantic relationships between entities \[[8](http://arxiv.org/abs/1612.03975)\]. These graphs, such as ConceptNet, integrate diverse sources of knowledge, including expert-curated data, crowd-sourced information, and game-based contributions, to capture general linguistic understanding \[[8](http://arxiv.org/abs/1612.03975)\]. This structured knowledge enhances machine learning models by enabling them to better interpret the meaning behind words, particularly when combined with distributional semantic techniques like word embeddings \[[8](http://arxiv.org/abs/1612.03975)\]. Furthermore, research on knowledge graph representation learning has expanded to include various aspects such as scoring functions, encoding models, and the integration of auxiliary information to improve the accuracy and richness of the representations \[[9](http://arxiv.org/abs/2002.00388)\]. The ability to model temporal and metarelational aspects of knowledge has also been explored, allowing for more nuanced reasoning and understanding of dynamic information \[[9](http://arxiv.org/abs/2002.00388)\]. These advancements highlight the importance of knowledge graphs in improving both theoretical understanding and practical applications in NLP and beyond.
