## Related Works

Neural retrieval models have seen significant advancements in recent years, particularly in the domain of Web search. These models often rely on large amounts of labeled query-document pairs for training, which can be costly to obtain \[[8](http://arxiv.org/abs/1611.09268)\]. To address this limitation, synthetic queries generated by large language models have emerged as a scalable alternative \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2305.18290)\]. However, the quality of these synthetic queries can vary, leading to suboptimal performance in downstream retrieval tasks \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2305.18290)\]. 

Existing methods typically filter out noisy query-document pairs using external re-rankers \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2305.18290)\]. In contrast, our work proposes a framework that integrates ranking signals directly into the query generation process using Direct Preference Optimization (DPO) \[[6](http://arxiv.org/abs/2402.13228)\]. This approach aims to optimize the model to generate high-quality queries that enhance downstream retrieval performance \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2305.18290)\]. 

DPO has been shown to improve the alignment of large language models with human preferences, particularly in tasks such as reasoning, summarization, and alignment \[[6](http://arxiv.org/abs/2402.13228)\]. It eliminates the need for complex reinforcement learning procedures and provides a stable, computationally lightweight alternative to existing methods \[[6](http://arxiv.org/abs/2402.13228)\]. The integration of DPO into the query generation process allows for direct optimization of retrieval effectiveness, as opposed to relying on post-hoc filtering mechanisms \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2305.18290)\].

The MS MARCO dataset, which contains a large-scale collection of real-world queries and passages, has been widely used to evaluate retrieval models \[[8](http://arxiv.org/abs/1611.09268)\]. Our experiments demonstrate that DPO improves the relevance between query-document pairs, as measured by ranker-assessed metrics, leading to stronger performance on the MS MARCO benchmark compared to baseline models trained with synthetic data \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2305.18290)\]. 

Several related works have explored the use of dense retrieval models for open-domain question answering \[[11](http://arxiv.org/abs/2004.04906)\]. These models often rely on dual-encoder architectures to learn semantic representations of questions and passages \[[11](http://arxiv.org/abs/2004.04906)\]. However, training such models can be challenging due to the discrepancy between training and inference, the presence of unlabeled positives, and limited training data \[[11](http://arxiv.org/abs/2004.04906)\]. 

To address these challenges, researchers have proposed various training strategies, including hard negative sampling and dynamic training approaches \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/2104.08051)\]. The use of hard negatives has been shown to improve retrieval performance by focusing on the most challenging examples during training \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/2104.08051)\]. Additionally, the introduction of dynamic training strategies, such as the Stable Training Algorithm for dense Retrieval (STAR) and the query-side training Algorithm for Directly Optimizing Ranking pErformance (ADORE), has led to significant improvements in retrieval accuracy \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/2104.08051)\].

### <a id='category-human-preference-alignment-in-llms'></a>Human Preference Alignment in LLMs: 4 papers found.
The category "Human Preference Alignment in LLMs" explores methods to align large language models with human preferences, primarily through reinforcement learning from human feedback (RLHF) and its variants. RLHF involves training a reward model based on human preferences and then fine-tuning the language model to maximize this reward, but it is often complex and unstable \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2305.18290)\]. To address these challenges, Direct Preference Optimization (DPO) was introduced, which simplifies the process by using a closed-form solution to extract the optimal policy, eliminating the need for reinforcement learning and reducing computational overhead \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2305.18290)\]. DPO has been shown to match or exceed the performance of PPO-based RLHF in controlling sentiment and improving response quality in tasks like summarization and dialogue \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2305.18290)\]. Additionally, research has demonstrated that training models to optimize for human preferences can significantly improve performance in tasks such as summarization, where models trained on human-preference data outperform those trained on reference summaries \[[5](http://arxiv.org/abs/2009.01325)\]. Furthermore, theoretical work has examined the assumptions underlying RLHF and DPO, proposing new objectives like $\Psi$PO that bypass key approximations and enable more robust alignment with human preferences \[[7](http://arxiv.org/abs/2310.12036)\]. Recent approaches, such as Direct Large Model Alignment (DLMA), have also emerged, leveraging contrastive prompts and self-rewarding scores to align models without relying on human-annotated data \[[17](http://arxiv.org/abs/2402.11907)\]. These developments highlight the ongoing efforts to improve the alignment of LLMs with human expectations through more efficient and effective methods.

### <a id='category-training-strategies-for-retrieval-models'></a>Training Strategies for Retrieval Models: 8 papers found.
The category "Training Strategies for Retrieval Models" encompasses a range of approaches aimed at improving the effectiveness and efficiency of training retrieval systems. These strategies often focus on addressing limitations such as vocabulary mismatch, data distribution mismatches, and the reliance on static negative sampling. For instance, the use of dense retrieval (DR) models has gained traction due to their ability to produce better ranking performance through representation learning, although their effectiveness is often constrained by the quality of training examples \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2305.18290)\]. To mitigate these issues, researchers have proposed dynamic sampling strategies like the Stable Training Algorithm for dense Retrieval (STAR) and the query-side training Algorithm for Directly Optimizing Ranking performance (ADORE), which enhance training stability and ranking performance by introducing random negatives and dynamic negative sampling, respectively \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2305.18290)\]. Additionally, the Differentiable Search Index (DSI) paradigm has been introduced to unify indexing and retrieval within a single model, but challenges such as data distribution mismatches between indexing and retrieval phases have been identified and addressed through innovations like DSI-QG, which generates relevant queries during indexing to align with retrieval needs \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/2010.08191)\]. Similarly, the Neural Corpus Indexer (NCI) demonstrates the potential of end-to-end deep neural networks to improve recall performance by integrating training and indexing stages \[[7](http://arxiv.org/abs/2310.12036)\]. Furthermore, the integration of reinforcement learning techniques, such as Proximal Policy Optimization (PPO), has shown promise in enhancing the training of retrieval models by enabling multiple epochs of minibatch updates \[[14](http://arxiv.org/abs/1707.06347)\]. These strategies collectively highlight the ongoing efforts to refine training methodologies in retrieval models, emphasizing the importance of dynamic, adaptive, and theoretically grounded approaches to achieve better performance and scalability.

### <a id='category-dense-retrieval-models-for-question-answering'></a>Dense Retrieval Models for Question Answering: 3 papers found.
In the realm of dense retrieval models for question answering, recent advancements have focused on improving the efficiency and effectiveness of passage retrieval. Traditional methods such as TF-IDF or BM25 have been widely used, but dense retrieval models, which rely on neural networks to learn semantic representations, have shown significant improvements \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/2010.08191)\]. These models often employ a dual-encoder architecture to match questions with relevant passages, though challenges such as training inefficiency and the difficulty of handling unlabeled data persist \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2305.18290)\]. To address these issues, approaches like RocketQA have been proposed, introducing techniques such as cross-batch negatives and data augmentation to enhance retrieval performance \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/2010.08191)\]. Similarly, ColBERT has emerged as a promising model that leverages BERT for efficient retrieval by using a late interaction architecture, allowing for faster query processing and reduced computational costs \[[10](http://arxiv.org/abs/2004.12832)\]. These models demonstrate the potential of dense retrieval to outperform traditional methods, as evidenced by their superior performance on benchmarks like MSMARCO and Natural Questions \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/2010.08191)\]. Additionally, the integration of synthetic data and direct preference optimization has been explored to improve the quality of training queries, leading to better downstream retrieval results \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2305.18290)\]. Overall, the field is rapidly evolving, with a focus on balancing model expressiveness, computational efficiency, and retrieval accuracy \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/2010.08191)\]\[[10](http://arxiv.org/abs/2004.12832)\].

### <a id='category-direct-preference-optimization-(dpo)-framework'></a>Direct Preference Optimization (DPO) Framework: 1 papers found.
Direct Preference Optimization (DPO) has emerged as a promising approach for improving the performance of large language models (LLMs) on various downstream tasks, including reasoning, summarization, and alignment \[[6](http://arxiv.org/abs/2402.13228)\]. By leveraging pairs of preferred and dispreferred data, DPO models the relative probability of selecting one response over another, enabling more targeted fine-tuning \[[6](http://arxiv.org/abs/2402.13228)\]. However, the standard DPO loss function has been shown to potentially reduce the likelihood of preferred examples, especially when the edit distance between completions is low \[[6](http://arxiv.org/abs/2402.13228)\]. This issue has led to the development of DPO-Positive (DPOP), a novel loss function and training procedure designed to mitigate this failure mode \[[6](http://arxiv.org/abs/2402.13228)\]. Empirical results demonstrate that DPOP not only outperforms the original DPO but also surpasses other fine-tuning methods across diverse datasets and tasks, including those with high edit distances between completions \[[6](http://arxiv.org/abs/2402.13228)\]. Furthermore, DPOP-tuned models achieve superior performance on independent benchmarks such as MT-Bench, highlighting their robustness and generalization capabilities \[[6](http://arxiv.org/abs/2402.13228)\]. The success of DPOP has also facilitated the creation of large-scale models like Smaug-34B and Smaug-72B, with the latter achieving notable performance on the HuggingFace Open LLM Leaderboard \[[6](http://arxiv.org/abs/2402.13228)\]. These findings underscore the significance of DPO and its variants in advancing the state-of-the-art in LLM fine-tuning and downstream task performance.

### <a id='category-ms-marco-benchmark-evaluation'></a>MS MARCO Benchmark Evaluation: 2 papers found.
The MS MARCO dataset is a large-scale, real-world benchmark designed to address the limitations of previous question-answering datasets by incorporating real user queries and web documents \[[8](http://arxiv.org/abs/1611.09268)\]. This dataset is notable for its comprehensive nature, with the goal of including one million queries, though an initial release of 100,000 queries was made to encourage research and gather feedback \[[8](http://arxiv.org/abs/1611.09268)\]. The dataset's construction involves advanced Bing search engine technology to extract context passages and human-generated answers, ensuring both quality and relevance \[[8](http://arxiv.org/abs/1611.09268)\]. Additionally, the dataset includes queries with multiple answers, which adds complexity and realism to the task of reading comprehension \[[8](http://arxiv.org/abs/1611.09268)\]. In terms of evaluation, recent approaches such as RepBERT have demonstrated state-of-the-art performance on the MS MARCO Passage Ranking task by using contextualized embeddings to represent queries and documents, leading to improved relevance scores \[[16](http://arxiv.org/abs/2006.15498)\]. This highlights the ongoing efforts to enhance retrieval effectiveness through innovative methods that integrate ranking signals directly into the query generation process \[[16](http://arxiv.org/abs/2006.15498)\].
