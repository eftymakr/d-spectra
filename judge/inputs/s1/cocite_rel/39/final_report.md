## Related Works

De novo genome assembly has been a focal point of computational genomics, driven by the advent of next-generation sequencing technologies that produce massive volumes of short reads \[[1](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/7279acd9c61a8de019ea2d55d23124dfbb6b5269)\]. These technologies, such as Solexa and Illumina, have enabled the sequencing of complex genomes, but the challenge lies in assembling these short reads into contiguous sequences \[[2](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/c6e319a023f932d6d2ff8897c2c91e56e872db8e)\]. Early approaches relied on string-based or overlap-layout-consensus (OLC) assemblers, which were effective for small genomes but struggled with large datasets \[[1](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/7279acd9c61a8de019ea2d55d23124dfbb6b5269)\]. As sequencing throughput increased, the need for scalable and efficient assemblers became more pressing \[[3](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/fe8d86bc5520b5049104ff58ee7e980982816ebf)\].

Several studies have explored the use of de Bruijn graph-based assemblers for handling large datasets of short reads \[[1](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/7279acd9c61a8de019ea2d55d23124dfbb6b5269)\]. These assemblers, such as SOAPdenovo and ABySS, have been optimized for performance and memory usage, but they still face limitations in terms of scalability and computational efficiency \[[13](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/7bf2f1c55f90e11a4d1df4dd608ac621a08f8b33)\]. For instance, PASHA, a parallelized assembler using de Bruijn graphs, demonstrated improved performance on large genomes by leveraging hybrid computing architectures \[[13](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/7bf2f1c55f90e11a4d1df4dd608ac621a08f8b33)\]. However, these assemblers often require significant computational resources and may not be suitable for extreme-scale genome assembly \[[1](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/7279acd9c61a8de019ea2d55d23124dfbb6b5269)\].

Long-read sequencing technologies, such as PacBio and Oxford Nanopore, have introduced new challenges due to their high error rates \[[4](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/2b59fd18ada769ef76fb7fcc7382f063791abfab)\]. Canu, a tool specifically designed for noisy single-molecule sequences, addresses these issues by introducing adaptive k-mer weighting and repeat separation strategies, resulting in high-quality assemblies \[[4](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/2b59fd18ada769ef76fb7fcc7382f063791abfab)\]. Similarly, Minimap and miniasm have been developed to efficiently map and assemble long reads without an error correction stage, achieving fast assembly times for bacterial and eukaryotic genomes \[[6](http://arxiv.org/abs/1512.01801)\]. These tools represent significant advancements in handling long reads, but they still face challenges in terms of accuracy and computational complexity \[[6](http://arxiv.org/abs/1512.01801)\].

The computational complexity of de novo assembly has also been studied from a theoretical perspective, with researchers exploring the parametric complexity of the assembly problem \[[5](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/d04b3cc774151260a59a5e6768b6cfae7fc3e512)\]. This work highlights the inherent challenges in assembling genomes from short reads, including repeat complexity and coverage biases, which can affect the quality of the final assembly \[[5](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/d04b3cc774151260a59a5e6768b6cfae7fc3e512)\]. To address these issues, researchers have proposed entropy-compressed data structures that reduce storage requirements and improve scalability \[[8](http://arxiv.org/abs/1008.2555)\]. These structures enable the practical representation of de Bruijn graphs, which are essential for large-scale genome assembly \[[8](http://arxiv.org/abs/1008.2555)\].

### <a id='category-de-novo-assembly-techniques-and-challenges'></a>De Novo Assembly Techniques and Challenges: 9 papers found.
The category "De Novo Assembly Techniques and Challenges" highlights the complexities and advancements in genome assembly methods. De novo assembly remains a critical process for studying unknown genomes, but it faces significant challenges due to the short read lengths and high error rates of current sequencing technologies \[[1](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/7279acd9c61a8de019ea2d55d23124dfbb6b5269)\]. These limitations make it difficult to achieve high accuracy and contiguity, especially for large and complex genomes \[[2](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/c6e319a023f932d6d2ff8897c2c91e56e872db8e)\]. To address these issues, various assemblers have been developed, such as string-based, overlap-layout-consensus (OLC), and De Bruijn graph-based tools, each with its own strengths depending on the read length and genome size \[[1](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/7279acd9c61a8de019ea2d55d23124dfbb6b5269)\]. For instance, string-based assemblers are more suitable for small genomes with short reads, while De Bruijn graph-based assemblers are better for large datasets \[[1](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/7279acd9c61a8de019ea2d55d23124dfbb6b5269)\]. Additionally, long-read technologies like SMRT sequencing have been used to improve assembly quality by enabling more accurate and continuous genome reconstruction \[[11](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/618d82a202bb0408e388eba250c66c8a90700c81)\]. However, even with these advances, challenges such as sequencing errors, repetitive regions, and heterozygosity continue to hinder the accuracy of de novo assemblies \[[3](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/fe8d86bc5520b5049104ff58ee7e980982816ebf)\]. To overcome these limitations, researchers have proposed novel methods, such as the use of k-mer frequency analysis to estimate genomic characteristics without relying on assembly \[[16](http://arxiv.org/abs/1308.2012)\], and tools like BELLA that enhance overlap detection using probabilistic models \[[15](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/f27decfab0389e2003e7ca66260b34eb3c1b8827)\]. Furthermore, hybrid approaches combining different sequencing technologies, such as Roche/454 and Illumina, have shown promise in reducing the number of contigs and errors in genome assembly \[[12](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/38410204a2977a9484e6d1205697b4103b5482f7)\]. These developments underscore the ongoing efforts to improve the efficiency and accuracy of de novo assembly techniques \[[1](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/7279acd9c61a8de019ea2d55d23124dfbb6b5269)\]\[[2](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/c6e319a023f932d6d2ff8897c2c91e56e872db8e)\]\[[3](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/fe8d86bc5520b5049104ff58ee7e980982816ebf)\]\[[11](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/618d82a202bb0408e388eba250c66c8a90700c81)\]\[[12](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/38410204a2977a9484e6d1205697b4103b5482f7)\]\[[15](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/f27decfab0389e2003e7ca66260b34eb3c1b8827)\]\[[16](http://arxiv.org/abs/1308.2012)\].

### <a id='category-parallel-and-distributed-computing-frameworks'></a>Parallel and Distributed Computing Frameworks: 5 papers found.
The category "Parallel and Distributed Computing Frameworks" encompasses several approaches aimed at improving the efficiency and scalability of genome assembly and sequence analysis. Existing assemblers, such as ABySS, Ray, and PASHA, have been developed to handle the computational demands of large-scale sequencing data \[[3](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/fe8d86bc5520b5049104ff58ee7e980982816ebf)\]. However, these tools often require significant computational resources and face challenges related to memory usage and irregular data patterns \[[3](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/fe8d86bc5520b5049104ff58ee7e980982816ebf)\]. PASHA, for instance, leverages hybrid parallelism, combining multi-threading on multi-core CPUs with message passing on distributed clusters, to achieve efficient and scalable assembly \[[13](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/7bf2f1c55f90e11a4d1df4dd608ac621a08f8b33)\]. Similarly, SWAP-Assembler introduces a scalable framework using a multi-step bi-directed graph (MSG) to manage computational interdependencies and improve performance on thousands of cores \[[3](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/fe8d86bc5520b5049104ff58ee7e980982816ebf)\]. The diBELLA 2D pipeline further advances distributed-memory parallel algorithms for overlap detection and layout simplification, achieving near-linear scaling and significant runtime reductions \[[17](http://arxiv.org/abs/2010.10055)\]. Additionally, tools like PASTIS and SimilarityAtScale focus on distributed-memory systems for protein similarity search and Jaccard similarity computation, respectively, highlighting the broader applicability of parallel computing in bioinformatics \[[14](http://arxiv.org/abs/2009.14467)\]\[[18](http://arxiv.org/abs/1911.04200)\]. These frameworks collectively address the computational bottlenecks in de novo assembly and sequence analysis, offering promising solutions for handling the growing volume of genomic data.

However, these technologies are characterized by relatively high error rates, which pose challenges in assembling large repeats and haplotypes \[[1](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/7279acd9c61a8de019ea2d55d23124dfbb6b5269)\]. To address these limitations, tools like Canu have been developed, offering improved assembly continuity, reduced runtime, and support for nanopore sequencing \[[1](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/7279acd9c61a8de019ea2d55d23124dfbb6b5269)\]. These tools also support formats like GFA for graph-based assembly, facilitating integration with complementary phasing and scaffolding techniques \[[1](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/7279acd9c61a8de019ea2d55d23124dfbb6b5269)\]. Furthermore, Minimap2 has been developed to handle long reads and large genomic contigs, offering faster alignment speeds and improved accuracy over existing aligners \[[3](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/fe8d86bc5520b5049104ff58ee7e980982816ebf)\]. These advancements highlight the ongoing efforts to enhance the efficiency and accuracy of long-read sequencing technologies in genome assembly.
