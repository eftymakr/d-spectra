## Related Works

Maximum Inner Product Search (MIPS) has been a focal point in machine learning and information retrieval, particularly in high-dimensional data applications \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/1405.5869)\]. Traditional approaches to MIPS often rely solely on inner product (IP) similarity, which can lead to issues such as local optima and redundant computations \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/1405.5869)\]. Alternatively, some methods reduce the MIPS problem to nearest neighbor search (NNS) under the Euclidean metric through space projection, which can result in topology destruction and information loss \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/1405.5869)\]. Despite these divergent paradigms, recent work suggests that there is no inherent binary opposition between IP and Euclidean metrics \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/1405.5869)\]. Instead, integrating both metrics in the design of indexing and search algorithms can significantly enhance MIPS performance \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/1405.5869)\].

Several studies have explored sublinear time algorithms for approximate MIPS. Asymmetric LSH (ALSH) was introduced to address this challenge by transforming the problem into approximate near neighbor search, enabling efficient hashing for MIPS \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/1610.03317)\]. This approach was later refined with improved asymmetric transformations that reduce distortion error and improve search accuracy \[[18](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/865488b467250ceb85bb59d35b8d4fb4f10ae421)\]. Additionally, the Norm-Ranging LSH method was proposed to address long-tailed 2-norm distributions, partitioning datasets into sub-datasets to improve query efficiency \[[6](http://arxiv.org/abs/1809.08782)\]. These methods highlight the importance of adapting hashing schemes to the specific characteristics of the data \[[6](http://arxiv.org/abs/1809.08782)\].

Graph-based approaches have also gained traction for efficient MIPS. The inner-product navigable small world graph (ip-NSW) was introduced as a state-of-the-art method, achieving significant speedups over baseline approaches by leveraging norm bias in the MIPS problem \[[4](http://arxiv.org/abs/1909.13459)\]. This was further enhanced with the ip-NSW+ algorithm, which combines angular and inner-product proximity graphs to improve search robustness and performance \[[4](http://arxiv.org/abs/1909.13459)\]. Similarly, the norm adjusted proximity graph (NAPG) was proposed to construct more meaningful connections during graph-based indexing, leading to improved search efficiency \[[3](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/25454f456c514219c9d6eeb42200ebbf378db9ca)\]. These graph-based methods demonstrate the potential of leveraging structural properties of the data to enhance MIPS performance \[[3](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/25454f456c514219c9d6eeb42200ebbf378db9ca)\].

Quantization-based approaches have also been explored for fast approximate MIPS. A quantization method was proposed that approximates inner products using subspace quantizers, avoiding the need for higher-dimensional feature space augmentation \[[16](http://arxiv.org/abs/1509.01469)\]. This method was further improved with a modified codebook learning procedure that enhances accuracy when example queries are available during training \[[16](http://arxiv.org/abs/1509.01469)\]. These quantization techniques provide an alternative to hashing-based methods, offering a balance between accuracy and efficiency \[[16](http://arxiv.org/abs/1509.01469)\].
