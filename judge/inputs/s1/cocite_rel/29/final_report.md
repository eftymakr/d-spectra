## Related Works

Maximum Inner Product Search (MIPS) has been a focal point in machine learning and information retrieval, particularly in high-dimensional data applications \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/1405.5869)\]. Traditional approaches to MIPS often rely solely on inner product (IP) similarity, which can lead to issues such as local optima and redundant computations \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/1405.5869)\]. Alternatively, some methods reduce the MIPS problem to nearest neighbor search (NNS) under the Euclidean metric through space projection, which can result in topology destruction and information loss \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/1405.5869)\]. Despite these divergent paradigms, recent work suggests that there is no inherent binary opposition between IP and Euclidean metrics \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/1405.5869)\]. Instead, integrating both metrics in the design of indexing and search algorithms can significantly enhance MIPS performance \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/1405.5869)\].

Several studies have explored sublinear time algorithms for approximate MIPS. Asymmetric LSH (ALSH) was introduced to address this challenge by transforming the problem into approximate near neighbor search, enabling efficient hashing for MIPS \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/1610.03317)\]. This approach was later refined with improved asymmetric transformations that reduce distortion error and improve search accuracy \[[18](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/865488b467250ceb85bb59d35b8d4fb4f10ae421)\]. Additionally, the Norm-Ranging LSH method was proposed to address long-tailed 2-norm distributions, partitioning datasets into sub-datasets to improve query efficiency \[[6](http://arxiv.org/abs/1809.08782)\]. These methods highlight the importance of adapting hashing schemes to the specific characteristics of the data \[[6](http://arxiv.org/abs/1809.08782)\].

Graph-based approaches have also gained traction for efficient MIPS. The inner-product navigable small world graph (ip-NSW) was introduced as a state-of-the-art method, achieving significant speedups over baseline approaches by leveraging norm bias in the MIPS problem \[[4](http://arxiv.org/abs/1909.13459)\]. This was further enhanced with the ip-NSW+ algorithm, which combines angular and inner-product proximity graphs to improve search robustness and performance \[[4](http://arxiv.org/abs/1909.13459)\]. Similarly, the norm adjusted proximity graph (NAPG) was proposed to construct more meaningful connections during graph-based indexing, leading to improved search efficiency \[[3](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/25454f456c514219c9d6eeb42200ebbf378db9ca)\]. These graph-based methods demonstrate the potential of leveraging structural properties of the data to enhance MIPS performance \[[3](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/25454f456c514219c9d6eeb42200ebbf378db9ca)\].

Quantization-based approaches have also been explored for fast approximate MIPS. A quantization method was proposed that approximates inner products using subspace quantizers, avoiding the need for higher-dimensional feature space augmentation \[[16](http://arxiv.org/abs/1509.01469)\]. This method was further improved with a modified codebook learning procedure that enhances accuracy when example queries are available during training \[[16](http://arxiv.org/abs/1509.01469)\]. These quantization techniques provide an alternative to hashing-based methods, offering a balance between accuracy and efficiency \[[16](http://arxiv.org/abs/1509.01469)\].

The category "Hashing-Based Methods for MIPS" explores the challenges and solutions in designing efficient algorithms for Maximum Inner Product Search (MIPS), particularly through hashing techniques. Existing approaches often rely on Locality-Sensitive Hashing (LSH) but face limitations in handling the asymmetric nature of inner product similarity \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/1405.5869)\]. For instance, SIMPLE-LSH, a state-of-the-art hashing method for MIPS, struggles with long-tailed distributions in real datasets, leading to suboptimal performance \[[6](http://arxiv.org/abs/1809.08782)\]. To address this, NORM-RANGING LSH was introduced, which partitions datasets into sub-datasets and builds independent hash indexes, improving query time complexity and reducing the number of probes needed for the same recall \[[6](http://arxiv.org/abs/1809.08782)\]. Similarly, H2-ALSH proposes a novel asymmetric LSH scheme based on homocentric hypersphere partitioning, which reduces distortion errors and enhances search accuracy through query-normalized transformations and early stop pruning \[[18](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/865488b467250ceb85bb59d35b8d4fb4f10ae421)\]. While some researchers argue that symmetric LSH is not feasible for inner product similarity \[[22](http://arxiv.org/abs/1410.5518)\], others demonstrate that symmetric hashing can offer stronger guarantees and better empirical performance than asymmetric variants \[[22](http://arxiv.org/abs/1410.5518)\]. Additionally, recent work has focused on combining inner product and Euclidean metrics in indexing and search algorithms, leading to the development of graph-based methods like the Metric-Amphibious Graph (MAG) and Adaptive Navigation with Metric Switch (ANMS), which leverage data topology for improved performance \[[23](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/88499bc8aaf92e9fa2d7d3ba5b0a1f20b3c07e0a)\]. These advancements highlight the ongoing evolution of hashing-based methods for MIPS, emphasizing both theoretical guarantees and practical efficiency.

Maximum Inner Product Search (MIPS) has been a focal point in recent algorithmic innovations, particularly in high-dimensional data applications such as recommendation systems and similarity search \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/1405.5869)\]. However, recent studies argue that there is no inherent binary opposition between inner product and Euclidean metrics, and that combining them in indexing and search algorithms can significantly enhance performance \[[3](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/25454f456c514219c9d6eeb42200ebbf378db9ca)\]. For instance, some works propose asymmetric transformations to convert MIPS into near neighbor search problems, which can be efficiently solved using hashing techniques \[[4](http://arxiv.org/abs/1909.13459)\]. These advancements highlight the ongoing efforts to balance search efficiency and quality in MIPS, with a growing emphasis on hybrid approaches that integrate multiple metrics and optimization strategies \[[8](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/f2171bc79fb72693f60c8e6b0dd547f7d4f850ac)\].

### <a id='category-graph-based-indexing-for-mips'></a>Graph-Based Indexing for MIPS: 4 papers found.
The category "Graph-Based Indexing for MIPS" focuses on the development of efficient algorithms for Maximum Inner Product Search (MIPS), a critical task in recommendation systems and information retrieval. Existing approaches often struggle with the non-metric nature of inner product, leading to issues such as local optima and redundant computations \[[4](http://arxiv.org/abs/1909.13459)\]. Graph-based methods have emerged as a promising alternative, leveraging proximity graphs to navigate through high-dimensional data \[[15](http://arxiv.org/abs/2201.09290)\]. These methods map data points to graph nodes, enabling search through graph traversal rather than brute-force computation \[[15](http://arxiv.org/abs/2201.09290)\]. The inner-product navigable small world graph (ip-NSW) has been highlighted as a state-of-the-art method, achieving significant speedups by exploiting norm bias, where large norm items dominate search results \[[4](http://arxiv.org/abs/1909.13459)\]. However, ip-NSW's performance is still partially attributed to its alignment with this norm bias, as it prioritizes computation on high-norm items \[[4](http://arxiv.org/abs/1909.13459)\]. To further enhance performance, ip-NSW+ was introduced, combining angular and inner-product proximity graphs to improve robustness across different data distributions \[[4](http://arxiv.org/abs/1909.13459)\]. Additionally, reinforcement learning and imitation learning have been integrated to automatically adapt search strategies, especially when ground truths are unavailable \[[15](http://arxiv.org/abs/2201.09290)\]. These advancements underscore the potential of graph-based indexing in addressing the challenges of MIPS, offering both efficiency and adaptability \[[15](http://arxiv.org/abs/2201.09290)\]. The norm bias in MIPS has also been identified as a key factor influencing the effectiveness of graph-based methods, with large norm items playing a central role in search outcomes \[[4](http://arxiv.org/abs/1909.13459)\]. Overall, these studies highlight the evolving landscape of graph-based indexing for MIPS, emphasizing the importance of aligning indexing strategies with data characteristics to achieve optimal performance \[[4](http://arxiv.org/abs/1909.13459)\]\[[15](http://arxiv.org/abs/2201.09290)\].
