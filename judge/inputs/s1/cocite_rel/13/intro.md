## Related Works

Long-tailed recognition remains a challenging problem in visual recognition due to the skewed class distribution in real-world datasets \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/1910.09217)\]. Many existing methods have attempted to address this issue by either re-balancing the training data or modifying the loss function to account for class imbalance \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/2107.12028)\]. For instance, class-balancing strategies such as loss re-weighting, data re-sampling, or transfer learning from head- to tail-classes have been widely explored \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/1910.09217)\]. However, these approaches typically involve joint learning of representations and classifiers, which may not be optimal for long-tailed recognition \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/1910.09217)\].

Recent work has focused on decoupling representation learning from classifier learning, which has shown promising results in improving performance on long-tailed benchmarks \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/1910.09217)\]. For example, the work by \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/1910.09217)\] demonstrated that data imbalance may not be an issue in learning high-quality representations, and that strong long-tailed recognition ability can be achieved by adjusting only the classifier. This aligns with our findings that contrastive learning does not consistently enhance model generalization as the number of views increases \[[25](http://arxiv.org/abs/2108.07507)\].

Supervised contrastive learning (SCL) has also been explored for long-tailed recognition, but it suffers from issues such as gradient conflicts and imbalanced attraction and repulsion gradients between positive and negative pairs \[[25](http://arxiv.org/abs/2108.07507)\]. This is supported by the observation that SCL tends to bias on high-frequency classes, increasing the difficulty of imbalanced learning \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/2107.12028)\]. To address this, Parametric Contrastive Learning (PaCo) was proposed, introducing parametric class-wise learnable centers to rebalance the optimization process \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/2107.12028)\]. Similarly, Balanced Contrastive Learning (BCL) was introduced to address the failure of SCL to form a regular simplex in long-tailed data \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/2207.09052)\].

The concept of contrastive learning has also been extended to hybrid networks, where a supervised contrastive loss is combined with a cross-entropy loss to progressively transition from feature learning to classifier learning \[[8](http://arxiv.org/abs/2103.14267)\]. This approach aims to leverage the benefits of both contrastive learning and cross-entropy for long-tailed classification. Furthermore, the work by \[[15](http://arxiv.org/abs/2007.07314)\] proposed logit adjustment techniques to address the bias in softmax cross-entropy training, encouraging a larger relative margin between logits of rare versus dominant labels.
