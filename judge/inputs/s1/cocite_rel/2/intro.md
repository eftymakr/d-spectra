## Related Works

Nonverbal communication plays a crucial role in human interaction, encompassing gestures, facial expressions, and body language that convey intent and emotion \[[11](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/75d17e8fa5165a849ebe5f0475bdf77bf0b6be74)\]. Existing large language models (LLMs) have primarily focused on textual inputs, leading to limitations in their ability to incorporate nonverbal cues and create immersive conversational experiences \[[11](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/75d17e8fa5165a849ebe5f0475bdf77bf0b6be74)\]. This gap has motivated recent research into multimodal language models that integrate text with visual and acoustic modalities \[[11](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/75d17e8fa5165a849ebe5f0475bdf77bf0b6be74)\].

Several studies have explored the integration of multiple modalities in language models. For instance, Kosmos-1 is a Multimodal Large Language Model (MLLM) that can perceive general modalities, learn in context, and follow instructions \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2302.14045)\]. It is trained on web-scale multimodal corpora, including text, images, and image-caption pairs, and demonstrates impressive performance on tasks such as image recognition with descriptions and visual question answering \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2302.14045)\]. Similarly, the Multimodal Transformer (MulT) addresses the challenges of modeling non-aligned multimodal time-series data by introducing directional pairwise crossmodal attention \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/1906.00295)\]. This mechanism enables the model to capture interactions between modalities across different time steps \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/1906.00295)\].

The Multi-attention Recurrent Network (MARN) further advances the understanding of human communication by incorporating multiple modalities—language, vision, and acoustic—into a single framework \[[4](http://arxiv.org/abs/1802.00923)\]. MARN uses a neural component called the Multi-attention Block (MAB) to discover interactions between modalities over time and stores them in a hybrid memory component called the Long-short Term Hybrid Memory (LSTHM) \[[4](http://arxiv.org/abs/1802.00923)\]. This architecture achieves state-of-the-art results on multimodal sentiment analysis and emotion recognition tasks \[[4](http://arxiv.org/abs/1802.00923)\].

The CMU-MOSEI dataset, introduced for multimodal sentiment analysis, provides a large-scale resource for studying how modalities interact in human language \[[5](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/006fdeff6e1a81c404317ee4056d6cc72f9c0e50)\]. It includes asynchronous coordinated sequences of text, visual, and acoustic data, and a novel fusion technique called the Dynamic Fusion Graph (DFG) is used to explore these interactions \[[5](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/006fdeff6e1a81c404317ee4056d6cc72f9c0e50)\]. DFG is highly interpretable and achieves competitive performance compared to previous state-of-the-art methods \[[5](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/006fdeff6e1a81c404317ee4056d6cc72f9c0e50)\].

In addition, Video-ChatGPT is a multimodal model that merges a video-adapted visual encoder with a large language model (LLM) to understand and generate detailed conversations about videos \[[6](http://arxiv.org/abs/2306.05424)\]. It is trained on a dataset of 100,000 video-instruction pairs and includes a quantitative evaluation framework for video-based dialogue models \[[6](http://arxiv.org/abs/2306.05424)\].
