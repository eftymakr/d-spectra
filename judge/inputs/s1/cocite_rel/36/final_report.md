## Related Works

Deploying large language models (LLMs) on edge devices presents significant challenges due to the limited storage, power, and computational capabilities of such hardware \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2309.06180)\]. Several recent works have addressed these challenges by focusing on efficient inference, memory management, and hardware acceleration. For instance, **vLLM** introduces PagedAttention to manage the key-value (KV) cache more efficiently, reducing memory waste and enabling flexible sharing of cache across requests \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2309.06180)\]. This approach significantly improves throughput by up to 2–4× compared to state-of-the-art systems like FasterTransformer and Orca \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2309.06180)\]. Similarly, **FlashAttention** improves the efficiency of attention mechanisms by reducing memory reads/writes and optimizing for GPU memory hierarchy, achieving up to 2× speedup over previous methods \[[18](http://arxiv.org/abs/2205.14135)\]. 

Quantization techniques have also been widely explored to reduce memory and computational demands. **SmoothQuant** enables 8-bit weight and activation quantization (W8A8) for LLMs without significant accuracy loss, achieving up to 1.56× speedup and 2× memory reduction \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/2211.10438)\]. **AWQ** introduces activation-aware weight quantization, which reduces model size while preserving performance, enabling 4× model size reduction and 3–4× acceleration across various edge platforms \[[4](http://arxiv.org/abs/2306.00978)\]. **OmniQuant** further advances quantization by introducing learnable weight clipping and equivalent transformation, allowing efficient optimization of quantization parameters for diverse settings such as W4A4, W6A6, and W2A16 \[[22](http://arxiv.org/abs/2308.13137)\].

Hardware accelerators play a crucial role in enabling efficient LLM deployment. **MECLA** proposes a memory-compute-efficient accelerator with sub-matrix partitioning, reducing memory access and computation by 83.6% and 72.2%, respectively \[[12](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/06046794314216dbdb53aeb0263ee41026e78574)\]. **DFX** presents a multi-FPGA appliance that accelerates GPT-2 inference with low latency and high throughput, achieving 5.58× speedup and 3.99× energy efficiency over NVIDIA V100 GPUs \[[14](http://arxiv.org/abs/2209.10797)\]. **LlamaF** focuses on FPGA-based acceleration for LLM inference, achieving up to 15.8× speedup and 6.1× power efficiency improvement on embedded FPGAs \[[15](http://arxiv.org/abs/2409.11424)\].

### <a id='category-memory-management-and-cache-optimization'></a>Memory Management and Cache Optimization: 5 papers found.
Memory management and cache optimization are critical for efficiently deploying large language models (LLMs) on resource-constrained devices and in high-throughput serving systems. Existing approaches struggle with the dynamic memory requirements of key-value (KV) caches, leading to significant memory waste and limiting batch sizes \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2309.06180)\]. To address these challenges, PagedAttention was introduced, inspired by virtual memory paging techniques, enabling near-zero memory waste and flexible KV cache sharing \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2309.06180)\]. Similarly, StreamingLLM tackles the issue of generalizing LLMs to long sequences by introducing attention sink mechanisms, allowing models to maintain performance even when text length exceeds the cache size \[[5](http://arxiv.org/abs/2309.17453)\]. FlashAttention and its improved variant, FlashAttention-2, further optimize memory and runtime by reducing the quadratic memory footprint of attention layers and improving GPU utilization through better work partitioning \[[6](http://arxiv.org/abs/2307.08691)\]\[[18](http://arxiv.org/abs/2205.14135)\]. Additionally, methods like windowing and row-column bundling have been proposed to reduce data transfer from flash memory to DRAM, enabling the execution of models larger than available DRAM \[[19](http://arxiv.org/abs/2312.11514)\]. These techniques collectively enhance the efficiency and scalability of LLMs in memory-constrained environments.

### <a id='category-hardware-acceleration-for-llms'></a>Hardware Acceleration for LLMs: 6 papers found.
Hardware acceleration for large language models (LLMs) has been a focal point in recent research, addressing the computational and energy efficiency challenges inherent in deploying LLMs on resource-constrained devices. Multiple studies have explored hardware solutions tailored for LLM inference, including GPUs, FPGAs, and custom architectures. For instance, MECLA, a memory-compute-efficient LLM accelerator, employs a parameter-efficient scaling sub-matrix partition method (SSMP) to reduce memory access and computation by 83.6% and 72.2%, achieving an energy efficiency of 7088GOPS/W \[[12](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/06046794314216dbdb53aeb0263ee41026e78574)\]. Similarly, DFX, a multi-FPGA acceleration appliance, leverages model parallelism and optimized dataflow to execute GPT-2 inference with low latency and high throughput, achieving a 5.58× speedup and 3.99× energy efficiency over NVIDIA V100 GPUs \[[14](http://arxiv.org/abs/2209.10797)\]. FPGA-based accelerators, such as those designed for TinyLlama 1.1B, also demonstrate significant improvements, offering a 14.3–15.8× speedup and a 6.1× power efficiency gain over CPU-based implementations \[[15](http://arxiv.org/abs/2409.11424)\]. Additionally, speculative inference techniques have been enhanced through hardware-software co-design, as seen in SpecPIM, which achieves 1.52×/2.02× speedup and 6.67×/2.68× energy efficiency improvements over existing PIM-based accelerators \[[16](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/2774fb46e2078396e556704c4b325f027e9d3bf9)\]. These approaches collectively highlight the ongoing efforts to optimize LLM deployment on edge devices by improving both performance and energy efficiency.

### <a id='category-efficient-inference-techniques'></a>Efficient Inference Techniques: 3 papers found.
Efficient inference techniques for large language models (LLMs) have gained significant attention due to the computational demands of deploying these models on resource-constrained devices \[[10](http://arxiv.org/abs/2310.17157)\]. Recent studies highlight the challenges of inference latency and energy consumption, particularly when running LLMs on edge devices with limited storage, power, and processing capabilities \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2309.06180)\]. To address these challenges, researchers have explored various methods, including architectural modifications, hardware optimizations, and algorithmic improvements \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2309.06180)\]. For instance, contextual sparsity has been proposed as a promising approach to reduce inference costs without compromising model quality or in-context learning capabilities \[[10](http://arxiv.org/abs/2310.17157)\]. This method leverages input-dependent sparsity in attention heads and MLP parameters, allowing for significant speedups in inference time \[[10](http://arxiv.org/abs/2310.17157)\]. Additionally, full-stack co-design approaches, such as those implemented in the Gemmini accelerator, have demonstrated substantial improvements in inference efficiency, achieving up to 88.7x speedups with minimal performance degradation \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2309.06180)\]. These efforts underscore the importance of integrating both algorithmic and hardware-level optimizations to enable efficient LLM deployment in latency-sensitive and energy-constrained environments \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2309.06180)\]. Furthermore, system-level optimizations, such as those in the CLONE framework, demonstrate how algorithm-hardware co-design can enhance inference performance while maintaining model accuracy \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2309.06180)\]. Overall, these studies highlight the growing focus on efficient inference techniques for LLMs, emphasizing the need for comprehensive solutions that address both computational and practical deployment challenges \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2309.06180)\]\[[10](http://arxiv.org/abs/2310.17157)\].

### <a id='category-model-size-reduction-techniques'></a>Model Size Reduction Techniques: 2 papers found.
Model size reduction techniques are critical for deploying large language models (LLMs) on resource-constrained devices, such as edge systems \[[17](http://arxiv.org/abs/2306.11695)\]. Recent approaches, like magnitude pruning, aim to reduce model size by removing less important weights, but they often require retraining or rely on computationally expensive second-order optimization \[[17](http://arxiv.org/abs/2306.11695)\]. In contrast, Wanda introduces a novel pruning method that leverages the observation of emergent large magnitude features in LLMs, allowing for effective sparsity induction without retraining or weight updates \[[17](http://arxiv.org/abs/2306.11695)\]. This approach enables the direct use of pruned models, making it particularly suitable for deployment scenarios where retraining is impractical. Additionally, compact models like TinyLlama, which is a 1.1B parameter model pretrained on 1 trillion tokens, demonstrate that smaller models can achieve competitive performance through architectural and training innovations \[[23](http://arxiv.org/abs/2401.02385)\]. These models benefit from techniques such as FlashAttention and Lit-GPT, enhancing computational efficiency while maintaining high accuracy \[[23](http://arxiv.org/abs/2401.02385)\]. Together, these methods highlight the growing importance of model compression and optimization strategies in enabling efficient LLM deployment across diverse hardware platforms.

### <a id='category-energy-efficiency-and-power-optimization'></a>Energy Efficiency and Power Optimization: 1 papers found.
Edge computing devices face significant resource constraints when deploying Deep Neural Networks (DNNs), particularly due to their high memory and compute demands \[[21](http://arxiv.org/abs/2110.00478)\]. FPGAs are commonly used in edge devices because they offer higher throughput and lower power consumption compared to general-purpose processors, making them ideal for DNN acceleration \[[21](http://arxiv.org/abs/2110.00478)\]. However, existing FPGA-based DNN accelerator designs often involve high development overheads, including repeated FPGA synthesis passes, reimplementation in Hardware Description Languages (HDL), and system integration \[[21](http://arxiv.org/abs/2110.00478)\]. To address these challenges, SECDA was introduced as a hardware/software co-design methodology that reduces design time by combining SystemC simulation with hardware execution, thereby streamlining design space exploration and development \[[21](http://arxiv.org/abs/2110.00478)\]. This approach enables efficient exploration of the hardware/software stack and identification of performance bottlenecks. As a case study, SECDA was used to develop two DNN accelerator designs on a PYNQ-Z1 board, achieving an average performance speedup of up to 3.5× and a 2.9× reduction in energy consumption compared to CPU-only inference \[[21](http://arxiv.org/abs/2110.00478)\]. These results highlight the potential of SECDA to improve the efficiency of DNN inference on edge devices with FPGAs.
