## Related Works

Speech-driven 3D facial animation has been a focal point in computer vision and machine learning research, aiming to generate realistic facial expressions that match speech content. Early approaches often relied on phoneme-level feature extraction, which limited the ability to capture complex facial dynamics \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2104.08223)\]. For instance, the work in \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/2112.05329)\] proposed a Transformer-based model to encode long-term audio context and autoregressively predict 3D face meshes, improving upon phoneme-level feature learning. Similarly, \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/2303.11089)\] introduced a method that disentangles emotion and content in speech, enabling more expressive facial animations. These methods, however, often struggle with capturing the full range of facial movements, particularly in the upper face and non-lip regions \[[4](http://arxiv.org/abs/1807.07860)\].

Recent advances have focused on improving lip synchronization and facial expression realism. The paper \[[5](http://arxiv.org/abs/2104.11116)\] introduced a method that learns viseme dynamics from speech videos, producing animator-friendly viseme curves and supporting multilingual speech inputs. This approach was further enhanced in \[[6](http://arxiv.org/abs/2104.07452)\], where a parametric viseme fitting algorithm was proposed to better correlate viseme curves with phonemes, leading to more controllable and realistic facial animations. Additionally, \[[7](http://arxiv.org/abs/2205.15278)\] proposed a framework that learns audio-visual representations in a disentangled manner, enabling arbitrary-subject talking face generation with clearer lip motion patterns than previous work.

Another key challenge in this field is the lack of high-quality, aligned 3D emotional datasets with synchronized audio. To address this, \[[8](http://arxiv.org/abs/2301.06059)\] introduced a large-scale 3D emotional talking face dataset (3D-ETF), which was used to train models that can generate facial expressions with rich emotional content. This dataset was also utilized in \[[9](http://arxiv.org/abs/2301.02379)\], where a method was proposed to generate one-shot emotional talking faces by involving an emotion source video. These works highlight the importance of emotional disentanglement and the need for diverse, high-quality datasets to train robust models.

The use of self-supervised learning has also gained traction, as it allows models to learn from large amounts of unlabeled data. For example, \[[10](http://arxiv.org/abs/2008.10010)\] proposed a method that learns audio-driven facial animations by integrating self-supervised pre-trained speech representations, improving the generalization of the models to unseen data. Similarly, \[[11](http://arxiv.org/abs/1905.03079)\] introduced a system that generates high-quality video portraits with vivid emotional dynamics driven by audio, leveraging cross-reconstructed emotion disentanglement techniques.
