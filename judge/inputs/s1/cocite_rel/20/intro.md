## Related Works

Deep learning has significantly impacted the field of image restoration, particularly in solving inverse problems such as deblurring, super-resolution, and inpainting. Two prominent paradigms have emerged: plug-and-play (PnP) methods and deep unrolling approaches. PnP methods integrate pre-trained denoisers into iterative algorithms like ADMM or proximal gradient descent, allowing for flexible regularization while maintaining the plug-and-play nature \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2201.13256)\]. However, these methods often lack rigorous convergence guarantees unless the denoisers satisfy specific conditions, such as Lipschitz continuity or nonexpansiveness \[[6](http://arxiv.org/abs/1905.05406)\]. Recent works have proposed training denoisers to satisfy these conditions, ensuring convergence \[[20](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/257a1d17a22753dfeb368b91a8e4d30a37651104)\]. 

On the other hand, deep unrolling approaches transform iterative optimization algorithms into end-to-end trainable neural networks by unfolding the iterations. This approach typically leads to better accuracy and faster inference compared to PnP methods, but it requires retraining or fine-tuning when the compression ratio (CR) changes \[[26](http://arxiv.org/abs/1712.02862)\]. A notable exception is the work by \[[29](http://arxiv.org/abs/1703.09912)\], which proposes a single network capable of solving various linear inverse problems, offering flexibility without sacrificing performance. However, such networks often lack the adaptability of PnP methods to varying CRs \[[27](http://arxiv.org/abs/2002.09611)\].

To address the limitations of both paradigms, recent research has focused on hybrid approaches that combine the flexibility of PnP with the accuracy of unrolling. For example, \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/2012.13247)\] introduces a new paradigm for solving regularized variational problems by learning maximally monotone operators, which can be used as regularization terms in optimization algorithms. This approach allows for greater flexibility and includes convex regularizations as special cases. Similarly, \[[10](http://arxiv.org/abs/2207.07321)\] proposes contractive and averaged denoisers by unfolding splitting-based optimization algorithms, demonstrating that these can match the performance of CNN denoisers while enabling convergence guarantees.

The use of deep neural networks as denoisers has also been explored in the context of regularization by denoising (RED) and plug-and-play priors. RED leverages denoisers to implicitly define the regularizer, while PnP methods integrate denoisers directly into iterative algorithms \[[7](http://arxiv.org/abs/1611.02862)\]. However, the convergence of PnP methods remains an open question unless the denoisers meet certain conditions \[[18](http://arxiv.org/abs/2004.03145)\]. Recent advances have shown that training denoisers to satisfy Lipschitz conditions can ensure convergence \[[6](http://arxiv.org/abs/1905.05406)\]. Additionally, \[[20](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/257a1d17a22753dfeb368b91a8e4d30a37651104)\] proposes a method for building firmly nonexpansive CNNs, which are essential for convergent PnP algorithms.
