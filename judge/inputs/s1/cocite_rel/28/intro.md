## Related Works

Federated Learning (FL) has emerged as a promising paradigm for training machine learning models on decentralized data while preserving privacy \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/1912.04977)\]. Early works such as \[[11](http://arxiv.org/abs/1602.05629)\] introduced the concept of federated learning for deep networks, emphasizing the need for communication-efficient methods due to the decentralized nature of the data. This was further expanded by \[[13](http://arxiv.org/abs/1812.06127)\], which introduced FedProx, a framework that generalizes FedAvg to handle statistical and system heterogeneity, demonstrating improved convergence behavior in heterogeneous settings. 

Active learning (AL) has also been explored in federated settings, aiming to reduce annotation costs by selecting the most informative samples for labeling \[[27](http://arxiv.org/abs/1909.12488)\]. However, existing methods often fail to account for the heterogeneity of data distribution across clients, leading to suboptimal model performance \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/1812.01097)\]. This challenge is addressed by CHASe, which specifically targets the issue of client heterogeneity in federated active learning (FAL) by identifying samples with high epistemic variation (EVs) \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/1812.01097)\]. 

The importance of handling non-IID data in FL has been extensively studied. For example, \[[14](http://arxiv.org/abs/1806.00582)\] showed that non-IID data can significantly reduce model accuracy, and proposed strategies to improve training by sharing global data subsets. Similarly, \[[15](http://arxiv.org/abs/1909.06335)\] investigated the effects of non-identical data distributions on visual classification, showing that performance degrades as distributions differ more and proposing mitigation strategies such as server momentum. 

To enhance the efficiency and effectiveness of FL, several approaches have been proposed. \[[4](http://arxiv.org/abs/1610.05492)\] introduced methods to reduce communication costs by using structured and sketched updates, while \[[5](http://arxiv.org/abs/2003.00295)\] proposed adaptive optimizers like Adam and Yogi for federated learning, showing significant performance improvements. \[[18](http://arxiv.org/abs/2010.01264)\] introduced HeteroFL, a framework that allows for heterogeneous local models and efficient global inference, challenging the assumption that local models must share the same architecture as the global model. 

Moreover, \[[19](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/a0393b0c4bd76dd272f099411148f51d2cee9519)\] explored the problem of biased client selection in FL, highlighting the need for more representative sampling strategies. \[[17](http://arxiv.org/abs/2105.05883)\] introduced clustered sampling, which improves client representativeness and reduces variance in stochastic aggregation weights, leading to better training convergence. 

Personalization in FL has also been a focus of recent research. \[[8](http://arxiv.org/abs/2002.07948)\] proposed a meta-learning approach to create personalized models for each user, while \[[20](http://arxiv.org/abs/2102.07078)\] introduced a framework that learns shared representations across clients and unique local heads for each client, improving performance in heterogeneous settings.
