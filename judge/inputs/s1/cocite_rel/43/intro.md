## Related Works

Performance modeling and prediction for large-scale machine learning systems have been a focal point of recent research due to the increasing complexity of training large language models (LLMs) in distributed environments. Early efforts in this area focused on establishing benchmarks for ML training, highlighting the challenges of optimizing throughput while maintaining accuracy and scalability \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/1910.01500)\]. These challenges include stochastic training times, the difficulty of fair benchmarking across diverse hardware, and the need for models that can adapt to varying configurations \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/1910.01500)\].

Several studies have attempted to predict execution times for deep learning models. For instance, one work proposed an approach where a deep learning network is trained to predict execution times for parts of a model, allowing for more accurate predictions of overall execution time \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/1811.11880)\]. This method accounts for non-linear relationships between operations and execution time, which are often ignored in linear models \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/1811.11880)\]. Another study introduced a framework for analyzing training time for convolutional neural networks (CNNs) on multi-GPU platforms, decomposing the model and mapping it to constraints such as software platforms, GPU architectures, and parallel strategies \[[7](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/55fd0eefc23f262c2875ec4c1c3472a689d88c50)\]. This framework demonstrated improved prediction accuracy with an average error of less than 15% across various scenarios \[[7](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/55fd0eefc23f262c2875ec4c1c3472a689d88c50)\].

Efforts to improve the efficiency of large-scale LLM training have also led to the development of advanced model parallelism techniques. For example, Megatron-LM introduced tensor and pipeline parallelism to scale training to thousands of GPUs, achieving high throughput and reducing memory footprint \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/2104.04473)\]. These techniques have been further refined to enable the training of models with up to 1 trillion parameters, demonstrating significant improvements in throughput and scalability \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/2104.04473)\]. Additionally, the development of FlashAttention has addressed the memory and computational challenges of long-sequence attention in Transformers, achieving significant speedups and enabling longer context lengths \[[11](http://arxiv.org/abs/2205.14135)\].

The importance of accurate performance models is further emphasized by the need to guide optimization efforts and system-level studies. For example, the MLPerf benchmark has been used to evaluate the performance of various hardware and software solutions, highlighting the importance of standardized benchmarks in driving improvements \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/1910.01500)\]. Similarly, the vLLM system has been developed to improve the throughput of large language model serving by efficiently managing memory for key-value caches \[[6](http://arxiv.org/abs/2309.06180)\]. These systems demonstrate the ongoing need for performance modeling tools that can accurately capture and predict execution behaviors across different configurations and hardware platforms.
