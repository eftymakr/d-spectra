## Related Works

Calibration of deep learning models has been a critical area of research, particularly in ensuring that predicted probabilities reflect the true likelihood of correct predictions. Early studies highlighted that modern neural networks, unlike older models, are poorly calibrated, with confidence scores often not aligning with actual accuracy \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/1706.04599)\]. This issue is exacerbated by factors such as network depth, width, and training procedures \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/1706.04599)\]. To address this, various post-processing calibration methods have been proposed, including temperature scaling, which has shown effectiveness in improving calibration across multiple datasets \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/1706.04599)\]. However, these methods often rely on fixed functional forms, such as piece-wise linear functions, which limit their flexibility and expressiveness in complex scenarios \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/1706.04599)\].

Recent work has explored more flexible calibration approaches. For instance, the use of deep ensembles has been shown to provide well-calibrated uncertainty estimates, often matching or surpassing Bayesian neural networks in performance \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/1612.01474)\]. Similarly, mixup training has been found to improve calibration by reducing overconfidence in predictions, especially on out-of-distribution data \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/1905.11001)\]. This is attributed to the label smoothing effect in mixup, which encourages more conservative predictions \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/1905.11001)\].

Another approach to calibration involves the use of Dirichlet calibration, which extends the beta calibration method to multiclass settings by leveraging Dirichlet distributions \[[11](http://arxiv.org/abs/1910.12656)\]. This method is particularly useful for models that output uncalibrated probabilities, as it can be implemented with minimal changes to existing architectures \[[11](http://arxiv.org/abs/1910.12656)\]. Additionally, the concept of intra order-preserving functions has been introduced to allow for more general post-hoc calibration, preserving the top-k predictions of deep networks while improving calibration \[[12](http://arxiv.org/abs/2003.06820)\].

The need for flexible calibration has also led to the development of the Unconstrained Monotonic Neural Network (UMNN), which can learn arbitrary monotonic functions, offering greater modeling power compared to traditional calibration methods \[[13](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/7f6bb146b9c1504ec77100e640d624bdc204f0ac)\]. This approach avoids the limitations of fixed functional forms and allows for more accurate calibration without distorting the original predictions \[[13](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/7f6bb146b9c1504ec77100e640d624bdc204f0ac)\]. To further enhance the performance of such flexible calibrators, the Smooth Calibration Loss (SCLoss) has been introduced, which helps achieve the ideal calibration state by fulfilling necessary conditions \[[13](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/7f6bb146b9c1504ec77100e640d624bdc204f0ac)\].
