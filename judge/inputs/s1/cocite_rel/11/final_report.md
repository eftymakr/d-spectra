## Related Works

3D human pose estimation has attracted significant research interest due to its applications in human-computer interaction, augmented reality, and motion analysis. Early methods primarily relied on 2D pose estimation followed by 3D reconstruction using geometric constraints or prior knowledge of human body structure \[[21](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/442928cd95d7bca15800b29ce2e2f8db5461bd58)\]. However, these methods often struggled with depth ambiguity and self-occlusion \[[1](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/50167a9e6bdc1f64a58fe8a3c990105fec0bd2bc)\]. Recent advances have focused on leveraging deep learning techniques, particularly convolutional neural networks (CNNs) and graph-based models, to improve accuracy and robustness \[[22](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/0646857f867d9a624f2b53d1ffb4de99e225b2d3)\].

Several works have explored the use of graph convolutional networks (GCNs) to model spatial dependencies in human pose estimation. For instance, \[[1](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/50167a9e6bdc1f64a58fe8a3c990105fec0bd2bc)\] proposed a graph-based method that incorporates domain knowledge about human body configurations to improve 3D pose estimation. Similarly, \[[22](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/0646857f867d9a624f2b53d1ffb4de99e225b2d3)\] introduced a modulated GCN that addresses limitations of traditional GCNs by allowing different feature transformations for different nodes, thereby improving performance. These methods emphasize the importance of modeling both local and global relationships between body joints.

Temporal consistency is another key challenge in 3D human pose estimation, especially when dealing with video sequences. To address this, \[[7](http://arxiv.org/abs/1711.08585)\] designed a sequence-to-sequence network with LSTM units to capture temporal dependencies and reduce jitter in 3D pose estimates. Similarly, \[[8](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/30de7fecb146356d7ae6cafa072997674601e0f5)\] proposed an attention-based framework that adaptively identifies significant frames and tensor outputs from deep neural net layers, enabling the model to capture long-range temporal dependencies. These approaches highlight the need for temporal modeling to improve the smoothness and accuracy of 3D pose sequences.

Transformers have also gained traction in 3D human pose estimation due to their ability to model long-range dependencies. \[[15](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/22e305b7b66bdedf379fa678802cb46e8bb546b1)\] introduced GraFormer, a transformer-based architecture that integrates graph convolution layers after multi-head attention blocks to better capture joint relationships. \[[25](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/92d61a05d76cc859c073e57eedbd82186aecdaff)\] further advanced this idea by proposing a Spatio-Temporal Criss-Cross attention (STC) block, which decomposes correlation learning into space and time, enabling efficient modeling of spatio-temporal dependencies. These transformer-based approaches demonstrate the potential of attention mechanisms in capturing complex human motion patterns.

Moreover, \[[26](http://arxiv.org/abs/2310.16288)\] proposed MotionAGFormer, which combines transformer and GCNFormer modules to better capture both global and local dependencies. This hybrid approach allows for more accurate 3D pose estimation while maintaining computational efficiency. The work highlights the importance of integrating different modeling strategies to address the challenges of 3D human pose estimation.

[Snippet]: «Recent transformer-based approaches have demonstrated excellent performance in 3D human pose estimation. However, they have a holistic view and by encoding global relationships between all the joints, they do not capture the local dependencies precisely. In this paper, we present a novel Attention-GCNFormer (AGFormer) block that divides the number of channels by using two parallel transformer and GCNFormer streams. Our proposed GCNFormer module exploits the local relationship between adjacent joints, outputting a new representation that is complementary to the transformer output. By fusing these two representation in an adaptive way, AGFormer exhibits the ability to better learn the underlying 3D structure. By stacking multiple AGFormer blocks, we propose MotionAGFormer in four different variants, which can be chosen based on the speed-accuracy trade-off. We evaluate our model on two popular benchmark datasets: Human3.6M and MPI-INF-3DHP. MotionAGFormer-B achieves state-of-the-art results, with P1 errors of 38.4 mm and 16.2 mm, respectively. Remarkably, it uses a quarter of the parameters and is three times more computationally efficient than the previous leading model on Human3.6M dataset. Code and models are available at https://github.com/TaatiTeam/MotionAGFormer.»
