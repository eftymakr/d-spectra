## Related Works

Sensor calibration is a fundamental step in multi-sensor fusion for autonomous driving systems, ensuring accurate alignment between heterogeneous sensors such as radar and camera \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2103.07505)\]. Early approaches for radar-camera calibration often relied on dedicated calibration targets, which limited their applicability in dynamic environments \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/1904.08743)\]. More recent methods have sought to automate this process without requiring external targets, leveraging data-driven techniques and deep learning \[[3](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/4d4dbbcf27d991aeab43dafb82a105bb5512b799)\]. For example, \[[4](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/fa7f4199640500d48c0671181ea6a8dce3141123)\] proposed a targetless method for rotational radar-camera calibration using a coarse and fine convolutional neural network, demonstrating robustness in real-world conditions. Similarly, \[[5](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/0955326c67a947b5fac5fbae7238d04fc9adc251)\] introduced a method for joint extrinsic calibration of radar, camera, and lidar, emphasizing the importance of multi-modal data fusion for improved accuracy.

The challenge of sparse and uncertain radar data has motivated the development of specialized calibration techniques. \[[6](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/c80265fa719eb85c8a56a6b36c74b99b793f39b2)\] addressed this by proposing a dual-perspective representation that combines frontal and bird's-eye views to mitigate height uncertainty. This approach aligns with the current work, which also leverages dual perspectives to improve calibration robustness. Additionally, \[[7](http://arxiv.org/abs/2011.04841)\] introduced a method that uses radar cross section estimates to enhance the identifiability of extrinsic parameters, which is crucial for reliable calibration. The use of cross-modal matching and attention mechanisms to align features from different sensors has also been explored in \[[8](http://arxiv.org/abs/2210.09267)\], where a novel ray-constrained cross-attention mechanism was proposed to improve feature correspondence in radar-camera fusion.

Several studies have focused on online calibration methods, which are essential for long-term system reliability. \[[9](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/0e88fc24ae7694d0b1a2fe78322936fc568bc7d2)\] proposed an online calibration approach based on moving object tracking, using detection and tracking pipelines to maintain sensor alignment. This aligns with the current work's goal of achieving automatic calibration during system operation. Additionally, \[[10](http://arxiv.org/abs/2008.05711)\] introduced an online multi-sensor calibration method that detects decalibration and estimates rotation, demonstrating the feasibility of real-time calibration in dynamic environments.

Deep learning has also been applied to radar-camera calibration, with several works exploring the use of neural networks for feature matching and data association. \[[11](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/2726fb04936e6c668dff18b2117e92e34c826698)\] proposed a method that uses a multi-step optimization process to refine extrinsic parameters, similar to the approach taken in the current work. Furthermore, \[[12](http://arxiv.org/abs/2209.06535)\] introduced a framework for early fusion of camera and radar data, leveraging spatio-contextual information to improve 3D object detection. These methods highlight the growing interest in deep learning-based approaches for sensor calibration.

### <a id='category-multi-sensor-fusion-techniques'></a>Multi-Sensor Fusion Techniques: 13 papers found.
The related works in multi-sensor fusion for autonomous driving primarily focus on improving the accuracy and robustness of radar-camera systems through advanced calibration and fusion techniques. Early approaches often relied on rule-based association methods, which struggled with complex driving scenarios and noisy data \[[13](http://arxiv.org/abs/2103.07825)\]. More recent methods have shifted toward deep learning-based solutions, leveraging feature-level interactions and global reasoning to enhance data association \[[13](http://arxiv.org/abs/2103.07825)\]. For instance, CenterFusion employs a middle-fusion approach to combine radar and camera data for 3D object detection, using a novel frustum-based method to associate radar detections with image features \[[7](http://arxiv.org/abs/2011.04841)\]. Similarly, CramNet introduces a ray-constrained cross-attention mechanism to resolve geometric ambiguities between camera and radar features, enabling more accurate depth and elevation estimation \[[8](http://arxiv.org/abs/2210.09267)\]. The proposed Selective Fusion Mechanism in this paper builds upon these advancements by addressing the challenge of height uncertainty in radar data through a Dual-Perspective representation that integrates both frontal and bird's-eye views \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2103.07505)\]. This dual-view strategy is further supported by Multi-Modal Cross-Attention Mechanisms, which enable precise location correspondence between modalities \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2103.07505)\]. Additionally, the Noise-Resistant Matcher designed during training enhances the robustness of the calibration process against data sparsity and uncertainty \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2103.07505)\]. These contributions align with broader efforts in the field, such as BEVFusion, which unifies multi-modal features in a shared bird's-eye view representation space to preserve both geometric and semantic information \[[18](http://arxiv.org/abs/2205.13542)\]. The success of these methods is evidenced by their performance on benchmark datasets like nuScenes, where they consistently outperform single-sensor approaches \[[7](http://arxiv.org/abs/2011.04841)\]\[[8](http://arxiv.org/abs/2210.09267)\]\[[18](http://arxiv.org/abs/2205.13542)\]. The proposed method in this paper advances the state of the art by introducing an online automatic geometric calibration method, which is critical for real-time operation and system reliability \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2103.07505)\].

We exploit a pattern discovered in the radar cross section estimation that is correlated to the missing elevation angle. Additionally, we carry out identifiability analysis based on the Fisher Information Matrix to show minimal requirements on the dataset and to verify the method through simulations. We test the calibration method on a variety of sensor configurations and address the problem of radar vertical misalignment. In the end, we show via extensive experiment analysis that the proposed method is able to reliably estimate all the six parameters of the extrinsic calibration.»
