## Related Works

Time series forecasting has been a cornerstone in financial applications, with recent advancements focusing on improving accuracy and robustness in the presence of uncertainty. Traditional approaches such as ARIMA and exponential smoothing have been widely used, but they often struggle with capturing complex temporal dependencies and non-stationarity \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/1912.09363)\]. Modern deep learning methods have emerged as a powerful alternative, offering greater flexibility and performance in forecasting tasks. For instance, DeepAR \[[5](http://arxiv.org/abs/1704.04110)\] introduces an autoregressive recurrent network that produces probabilistic forecasts, achieving significant improvements over classical methods. Similarly, the Temporal Fusion Transformer (TFT) \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/1912.09363)\] is designed for multi-horizon forecasting, incorporating attention mechanisms to model both local and long-term dependencies. These models are particularly useful in financial forecasting, where accurate predictions are essential for decision-making.

In the context of asset allocation, several studies have explored the integration of forecasting with optimization. The Predict-then-Optimize (PnO) framework \[[10](http://arxiv.org/abs/2310.06625)\] is a notable approach that combines forecasting with optimization, but it often assumes a fixed forecasting model, which can introduce biases. The introduction of more sophisticated forecasting models, such as those based on Transformers, has raised new challenges in terms of uncertainty calibration. For example, the work on Non-stationary Transformers \[[4](http://arxiv.org/abs/2205.14415)\] addresses the issue of non-stationarity in time series by introducing a framework that enhances the model's ability to capture dynamic patterns. This is particularly relevant in financial applications where data is often non-stationary and volatile.

Another important line of research focuses on the use of pre-trained models for time series analysis. The work on Chronos \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/2403.07815)\] introduces a framework for pretrained probabilistic time series models, leveraging the T5 family of models to tokenize and process time series data. This approach has shown promising results in both training and zero-shot scenarios, demonstrating the potential of pre-trained models in financial forecasting. Similarly, the iTransformer \[[10](http://arxiv.org/abs/2310.06625)\] model proposes an inverted architecture that improves the handling of large lookback windows and enables better generalization across different variates.

The integration of large language models (LLMs) into time series forecasting has also gained attention. While some studies suggest that LLMs can provide valuable insights through cross-sequence reasoning and interpretation \[[11](http://arxiv.org/abs/2306.11025)\], others have found that they do not necessarily outperform simpler models in terms of forecasting accuracy \[[13](http://arxiv.org/abs/2406.16964)\]. This highlights the importance of carefully evaluating the suitability of different models for specific forecasting tasks.
