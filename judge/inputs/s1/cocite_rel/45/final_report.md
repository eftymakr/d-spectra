## Related Works

Fine-grained emotion classification in text has been a growing area of research, particularly in the context of social media and user-generated content. While sentiment analysis has been extensively studied, focusing on polarity and subjectivity \[[1](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/7d0531aaaa4dbf4033da964289843e7812262702)\], the detection of discrete emotions remains less explored, especially in domain-specific contexts like app reviews \[[2](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/6a6f1b8ef3eefaf8027a76134859b44886a5f795)\]. This gap is addressed by several recent works that have focused on creating annotated datasets and developing models for emotion detection.

The SemEval-2016 dataset was extended with emotion annotations, highlighting the challenges of aligning emotion labels with stance and sentiment \[[1](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/7d0531aaaa4dbf4033da964289843e7812262702)\]. Similarly, the GoEmotions dataset, consisting of 58k Reddit comments, provides a large-scale resource for fine-grained emotion classification, though it is limited to English and requires further validation \[[5](http://arxiv.org/abs/2005.00547)\]. The DENS dataset, collected from narratives, also contributes to emotion analysis by offering a multi-class classification task, though it is focused on long-form texts \[[12](http://arxiv.org/abs/1910.11769)\].

Efforts to automate emotion detection have also been explored. EmoNet, for instance, uses deep learning models to achieve high accuracy in detecting 24 fine-grained emotions, with an average accuracy of 87.58% \[[2](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/6a6f1b8ef3eefaf8027a76134859b44886a5f795)\]. The dataset is also extended to model Plutchik’s 8 primary emotion dimensions, achieving an accuracy of 95.68% \[[2](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/6a6f1b8ef3eefaf8027a76134859b44886a5f795)\]. These models demonstrate the potential of deep learning in emotion detection, though they are typically evaluated on general text rather than app reviews.

This approach is useful for initial sentiment classification but does not capture the fine-grained emotional nuances of app reviews.
