## Related Works

The problem of learning from noisy labels has been extensively studied in recent years, with a variety of approaches proposed to mitigate the adverse effects of label noise on deep learning models. Traditional methods often rely on modifying the loss function to make it more robust to label noise, such as the use of symmetric cross entropy \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/1908.06112)\] or generalized cross entropy loss \[[9](http://arxiv.org/abs/1805.07836)\]. These approaches aim to address both overfitting to noisy labels and under-learning on hard examples by introducing noise-tolerant components into the training process. For instance, symmetric cross entropy (SL) was proposed to balance the learning of easy and hard classes by incorporating a reverse cross entropy term that is more robust to label noise \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/1908.06112)\]. Similarly, generalized cross entropy loss was introduced as a generalization of mean absolute error and categorical cross entropy, offering a theoretically grounded alternative for training with noisy labels \[[9](http://arxiv.org/abs/1805.07836)\].

Another line of research focuses on the memorization of noisy labels during training. It has been observed that deep neural networks first fit clean labels during an early learning phase before eventually memorizing noisy labels \[[6](http://arxiv.org/abs/2007.00151)\]. This phenomenon has motivated the development of regularization techniques that exploit the early learning phase to prevent overfitting. For example, early-learning regularization was proposed to capitalize on the progress made during the early learning phase by introducing a regularization term that steers the model toward target probabilities derived from semi-supervised learning techniques \[[6](http://arxiv.org/abs/2007.00151)\]. This approach has shown robustness to noisy annotations on several benchmarks.

In addition to loss function modifications and regularization strategies, several methods have been proposed to explicitly detect and correct noisy labels during training. Co-teaching, for instance, involves training two neural networks simultaneously, where each network selects data with potentially clean labels and uses the other network’s selection for training \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/1804.06872)\]. This approach has been shown to significantly improve robustness against label noise. Similarly, DivideMix leverages semi-supervised learning techniques to dynamically divide the training data into labeled and unlabeled sets, with the latter being treated as noisy samples \[[7](http://arxiv.org/abs/2002.07394)\]. This framework allows for semi-supervised training on both sets, improving generalization in the presence of label noise.

### <a id='category-loss-function-modifications-for-label-noise'></a>Loss Function Modifications for Label Noise: 4 papers found.
The category "Loss Function Modifications for Label Noise" focuses on improving deep neural network training in the presence of noisy labels. Several approaches have been proposed to address the limitations of traditional loss functions like Cross Entropy (CE), which can lead to overfitting on easy classes and underlearning on hard classes \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/1908.06112)\]. To mitigate these issues, researchers have introduced modified loss functions, such as Symmetric Cross Entropy (SL), which combines CE with a noise-robust counterpart, Reverse Cross Entropy (RCE), to balance learning and avoid overfitting \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/1908.06112)\]. Additionally, methods have been developed to handle class-dependent label noise by introducing loss correction procedures that rely on estimating corruption probabilities between classes \[[4](http://arxiv.org/abs/1609.03683)\]. These corrections can be applied across various architectures, including recurrent networks, and are often based on matrix operations \[[4](http://arxiv.org/abs/1609.03683)\]. Another line of work explores noise-robust alternatives to CE, such as mean absolute error (MAE), though it has been shown that MAE can also struggle with certain datasets \[[9](http://arxiv.org/abs/1805.07836)\]. Theoretical analyses have further supported these approaches, demonstrating their effectiveness in a range of noisy label scenarios \[[9](http://arxiv.org/abs/1805.07836)\]. These methods aim to enhance the robustness of deep learning models by modifying the loss function to better handle label noise, ensuring more reliable training outcomes.

### <a id='category-noise-robustness-in-deep-learning'></a>Noise Robustness in Deep Learning: 6 papers found.
The category "Noise Robustness in Deep Learning" encompasses several approaches aimed at improving the performance of deep neural networks in the presence of noisy labels. Existing methods often focus on mitigating the memorization of incorrect labels by either modifying the training process or adjusting the loss functions used during training. For instance, Co-teaching \[[11](http://arxiv.org/abs/1901.04215)\] introduces a paradigm where two neural networks are trained simultaneously, with each network using the predictions of the other to refine its own training data, thereby reducing the impact of noisy labels. Similarly, the Co-teaching+ framework \[[11](http://arxiv.org/abs/1901.04215)\] further enhances this by incorporating a "Update by Disagreement" strategy, which helps maintain diversity in the training data and prevents premature convergence. Another approach, such as the dimensionality-driven learning strategy \[[8](http://arxiv.org/abs/1806.02612)\], leverages the structure of the deep representation space to adapt the loss function dynamically, improving tolerance to noisy labels. Additionally, research has explored loss functions that are inherently robust to label noise, such as the mean absolute error loss \[[13](http://arxiv.org/abs/1712.09482)\], which enables standard backpropagation to learn accurate classifiers even with noisy data. These methods highlight the growing interest in developing robust training strategies that can effectively handle noisy labels without requiring extensive manual intervention or high-quality annotated data \[[5](http://arxiv.org/abs/1706.00038)\].

When trained on noisy labels, deep neural networks have been observed to first fit the training data with clean labels during an "early learning" phase, before eventually memorizing the examples with false labels. We prove that early learning and memorization are fundamental phenomena in high-dimensional classification tasks, even in simple linear models, and give a theoretical explanation in this setting. Motivated by these findings, we develop a new technique for noisy classification tasks, which exploits the progress of the early learning phase.
