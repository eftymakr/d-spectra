## Related Works

Unsupervised pre-training has emerged as a powerful paradigm for learning rich representations from raw data, particularly in computer vision and natural language processing \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/1807.03748)\]. However, its application to DNA pre-training has been limited, with prior work primarily focusing on sequence-based models \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/1907.11692)\]. These approaches often fail to capture the complex regulatory signals that govern DNA function, such as chromatin accessibility and histone modifications \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/1805.01978)\]. This limitation has motivated the development of supervised training methods for genomic profile prediction, which can better capture the relationships between DNA sequences and their regulatory contexts \[[4](http://arxiv.org/abs/1505.05192)\].

Several studies have explored unsupervised learning for visual representations, such as Contrastive Predictive Coding (CPC) \[[5](http://arxiv.org/abs/1206.5538)\], which learns representations by predicting future samples in latent space \[[6](http://arxiv.org/abs/1603.09246)\]. Similarly, self-supervised learning methods like SimCLR \[[7](http://arxiv.org/abs/1910.10683)\] and MoCo \[[8](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/c43d9cade31600400a0f62beb5bbcc1b548e009e)\] have demonstrated effectiveness in learning robust visual features without explicit labels. These methods rely on contrastive learning, where models are trained to distinguish between similar and dissimilar examples \[[9](http://arxiv.org/abs/1803.07728)\]. While these approaches have achieved impressive results, they often lack the ability to capture the nuanced regulatory signals that are critical for DNA function \[[10](http://arxiv.org/abs/1808.06670)\].

In the context of DNA representation learning, recent work has focused on improving the efficiency and effectiveness of pre-training strategies. For example, RoBERTa \[[11](http://arxiv.org/abs/1905.09272)\] has shown that careful hyperparameter tuning and training data selection can significantly improve the performance of pre-trained language models. Similarly, BERT \[[12](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/cd18800a0fe0b668a1cc19f2ec95b5003d0a5035)\] has demonstrated that bidirectional training of transformers can lead to state-of-the-art results on various NLP tasks. These findings suggest that the design of pre-training objectives and architectures plays a crucial role in the success of representation learning \[[13](http://arxiv.org/abs/1703.03400)\].

This approach allows the model to adapt to the specific characteristics of each genomic profile, leading to more effective DNA representations \[[15](http://arxiv.org/abs/2104.14294)\]. The success of SPACE highlights the importance of combining supervised training with domain-specific knowledge to improve the performance of DNA pre-training \[[16](http://arxiv.org/abs/2003.04297)\].
