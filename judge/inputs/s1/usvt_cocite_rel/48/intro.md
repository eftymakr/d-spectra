## Related Works

Recent advancements in neural retrieval methods, particularly those leveraging transformer-based pre-trained language models, have significantly improved multilingual and cross-lingual retrieval capabilities \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/1907.11692)\]. However, the effectiveness of these models for low-resource, morphologically rich languages such as Amharic remains underexplored due to data scarcity and suboptimal tokenization \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/1911.02116)\]. This challenge is echoed in other studies that highlight the limitations of multilingual models in low-resource settings \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/1810.04805)\]. For instance, the XNLI benchmark shows that cross-lingually transferred models often lag behind monolingual models in syntactic and sentence retrieval tasks \[[4](http://arxiv.org/abs/2004.12832)\]. Similarly, the XTREME benchmark underscores the need for benchmarks that evaluate cross-lingual generalization across a diverse set of languages and tasks \[[5](http://arxiv.org/abs/2004.04906)\].

Pre-trained language models like BERT and RoBERTa have been shown to achieve state-of-the-art results on various NLP tasks, including question answering and language inference \[[6](http://arxiv.org/abs/1901.04085)\]. However, their performance on low-resource languages is often suboptimal. This is partly due to the lack of large-scale, high-quality annotated data for these languages \[[7](http://arxiv.org/abs/2103.11811)\]. For example, in the case of Amharic, the performance of current POS taggers is not as good as that of contemporary models for English and other European languages \[[8](http://arxiv.org/abs/1911.03894)\]. This gap motivates the development of language-specific models that can better capture the linguistic nuances of low-resource languages.

To address these challenges, several studies have explored the use of dense retrieval models for open-domain question answering \[[9](http://arxiv.org/abs/2005.14165)\]. For instance, the Dense Passage Retrieval (DPR) approach has shown that dense representations can outperform traditional sparse vector space models like TF-IDF or BM25 \[[10](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/1e077413b25c4d34945cc2707e17e46ed4fe784a)\]. Furthermore, the ColBERT model introduces a late interaction architecture that independently encodes queries and documents and then models their fine-grained similarity \[[11](http://arxiv.org/abs/1103.0398)\]. This approach has been shown to be more efficient and effective than traditional BERT-based models \[[12](http://arxiv.org/abs/2007.00808)\].

The importance of language-specific adaptation is also emphasized in the development of models like PhoBERT for Vietnamese \[[13](http://arxiv.org/abs/2002.08909)\], which outperforms multilingual models like XLM-R \[[14](http://arxiv.org/abs/1908.10084)\]. Similarly, the RoBERTa model has been adapted for French, demonstrating that relatively small datasets can lead to results as good as those obtained using larger datasets \[[15](http://arxiv.org/abs/2010.08191)\]. These findings underscore the need for language-specific models that can better capture the unique characteristics of low-resource languages.
