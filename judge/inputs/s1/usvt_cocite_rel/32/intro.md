## Related Works

Recent advancements in large language models (LLMs) have emphasized the importance of understanding how feature representations evolve across layers, as this knowledge is critical for improving interpretability and robustness \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2307.09288)\]. Several studies have explored the role of specific layers in LLMs, often focusing on how they contribute to particular functions or behaviors. For example, some works have identified critical layers linked to specific tasks or behaviors, typically through data-dependent analyses of fine-tuned models \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2307.09288)\]. However, these approaches are limited to post-hoc settings, as they rely on fine-tuned models rather than pre-fine-tuned ones \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2307.09288)\]. 

In contrast, our work introduces a data-oblivious approach to identify intrinsic critical layers in pre-fine-tuned LLMs by analyzing representation dynamics using Centered Kernel Alignment (CKA) \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2307.09288)\]. This method enables the identification of critical layers without requiring fine-tuning, offering a more general and interpretable framework. Our findings show that layers with significant shifts in representation space are also those most affected during fine-tuning—a pattern that holds consistently across tasks for a given model \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2307.09288)\]. This insight is crucial for understanding how LLMs adapt to new tasks and how their internal representations change during training.

Our spectral analysis further reveals that these shifts are driven by changes in the top principal components, which encode semantic transitions from rationales to conclusions \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2307.09288)\]. This finding aligns with prior work on the importance of principal components in capturing semantic information \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2307.09288)\]. For instance, studies on deep contextualized word representations have shown that such representations can capture complex syntactic and semantic features, which are essential for downstream tasks \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/1802.05365)\]. Additionally, research on parameter-efficient transfer learning has demonstrated that modifying only a subset of parameters can significantly improve model performance, which is relevant to our findings on critical layers \[[7](http://arxiv.org/abs/2110.04366)\].

We apply these insights to two practical scenarios: efficient domain adaptation and backdoor defense \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2307.09288)\]. In domain adaptation, fine-tuning critical layers leads to greater loss reduction compared to non-critical layers, suggesting that these layers play a key role in adapting to new domains \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2307.09288)\]. In backdoor defense, freezing critical layers reduces attack success rates by up to 40%, highlighting their importance in maintaining model security \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2307.09288)\]. These results are supported by prior work on the effectiveness of parameter-efficient fine-tuning methods, such as adapters and LoRA, which show that modifying only a subset of parameters can yield significant improvements \[[7](http://arxiv.org/abs/2110.04366)\]\[[8](http://arxiv.org/abs/2106.09685)\].
