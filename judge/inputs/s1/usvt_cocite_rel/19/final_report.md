## Related Works

Speech-driven 3D facial animation has been a focal point in computer vision and machine learning research, aiming to generate realistic facial expressions that match speech content and emotion. Early approaches primarily relied on handcrafted features and rule-based systems, which lacked the flexibility and realism required for complex facial animations \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2112.05329)\]. With the advent of deep learning, several methods have been proposed to improve the synchronization between speech and facial movements.

One notable approach is FaceFormer, which introduces a Transformer-based autoregressive model to encode long-term audio context and predict a sequence of animated 3D face meshes \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2112.05329)\]. This method addresses the issue of limited context in prior works by incorporating self-supervised pre-trained speech representations and designing specialized attention mechanisms to improve alignment between audio and motion \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2112.05329)\]. Similarly, MeshTalk proposes a categorical latent space that disentangles audio-correlated and audio-uncorrelated information, leading to more realistic and diverse facial animations \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/2104.08223)\].

EmoTalk further advances the field by focusing on emotional disentanglement, introducing an emotion disentangling encoder to separate speech content from emotional expressions \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/2303.11089)\]. This allows for the generation of more expressive and emotionally rich 3D facial animations. Additionally, the use of facial blendshapes as supervision helps in reconstructing plausible 3D faces from 2D emotional data, contributing to a large-scale dataset for training \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/2303.11089)\].

Another significant contribution is the development of neural networks that can generate talking face videos from a single reference image and audio input, such as MakeItTalk \[[17](http://arxiv.org/abs/2004.12992)\]. This method disentangles content and speaker information in the input audio, enabling the synthesis of photorealistic videos with full range of motion. It also supports the animation of artistic paintings and 2D characters, demonstrating the versatility of such approaches \[[17](http://arxiv.org/abs/2004.12992)\].

The importance of lip synchronization has been extensively studied, with methods like Wav2Lip focusing on generating lip movements that match a target speech segment for arbitrary identities \[[11](http://arxiv.org/abs/2008.10010)\].
