## Related Works

Diffusion models have become a cornerstone in generative modeling, particularly for image and video synthesis, due to their ability to produce high-quality outputs through iterative denoising processes \[[16](http://arxiv.org/abs/2105.05233)\]. However, the computational and memory demands of these models, especially diffusion transformers (DiTs), hinder their deployment on edge devices \[[10](http://arxiv.org/abs/2212.09748)\]. To address this, quantization techniques have been explored to reduce model size and accelerate inference without retraining \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2302.04304)\]. 

Several works have focused on quantizing diffusion models, but most have been limited to image generation tasks \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2302.04304)\]. For instance, Q-Diffusion \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2302.04304)\] introduced a post-training quantization (PTQ) method tailored to diffusion models, achieving 4-bit quantization with minimal performance degradation. Similarly, PTQD \[[4](http://arxiv.org/abs/2305.10657)\] proposed a unified formulation for quantization noise and diffusion perturbed noise, enabling 4-bit inference with only a 0.06 increase in FID score. These methods, however, have not been effectively adapted to video generation tasks, where the temporal dimension introduces additional challenges \[[18](http://arxiv.org/abs/2204.03458)\].

Recent advances in diffusion transformers (DiTs) have enabled scalable and high-quality video generation \[[10](http://arxiv.org/abs/2212.09748)\]. However, the large parameter count and computational complexity of DiTs limit their practical deployment, especially on resource-constrained devices \[[17](http://arxiv.org/abs/2405.19751)\]. To mitigate this, several works have explored post-training quantization for DiTs. Q-DiT \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/2406.17343)\] introduced a method that addresses spatial and temporal variance in DiT weights and activations, achieving significant FID improvements. PTQ4DiT \[[5](http://arxiv.org/abs/2405.16005)\] further advanced this by introducing channel-wise salience balancing and dynamic activation calibration, enabling 8-bit quantization with minimal performance loss. These methods have primarily focused on image generation, with limited exploration into video-specific challenges.

Video generation models, such as Latte \[[15](http://arxiv.org/abs/2401.03048)\] and Stable Video Diffusion \[[22](http://arxiv.org/abs/2311.15127)\], have demonstrated the potential of diffusion models for video synthesis. However, quantizing these models remains a challenge due to the temporal coherence required in video sequences \[[18](http://arxiv.org/abs/2204.03458)\]. ### <a id='category-quantization-techniques-for-diffusion-models'></a>Quantization Techniques for Diffusion Models: 10 papers found.
Diffusion models have seen significant advancements in image and video generation, with Diffusion Transformers (DiTs) emerging as a powerful alternative to traditional U-Net-based architectures due to their scalability and performance \[[5](http://arxiv.org/abs/2405.16005)\]. However, the large parameter count and computational complexity of DiTs hinder their deployment on resource-constrained devices \[[5](http://arxiv.org/abs/2405.16005)\]. Quantization techniques, which reduce model size and accelerate inference by lowering bit-width, have been explored for diffusion models, but existing methods struggle to generalize to video generation tasks due to unique challenges such as information loss during quantization and misalignment between optimization objectives and video-specific requirements \[[5](http://arxiv.org/abs/2405.16005)\]. Recent works have addressed these issues by introducing specialized quantization frameworks tailored for DiTs. For instance, Q-DiT \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/2406.17343)\] tackles spatial and temporal variance in DiT weights and activations through automatic quantization granularity allocation and sample-wise dynamic activation quantization, achieving notable improvements in FID scores. Similarly, PTQ4DiT \[[5](http://arxiv.org/abs/2405.16005)\] introduces Channel-wise Salience Balancing (CSB) and Spearmen's ρ-guided Salience Calibration (SSC) to handle salient channels and temporal activation variations, enabling 8-bit and even 4-bit quantization without significant performance degradation. Additionally, HQ-DiT \[[10](http://arxiv.org/abs/2212.09748)\] utilizes 4-bit floating-point (FP) precision for both weights and activations, reducing quantization error through a clipping range selection mechanism and a universal identity mathematical transform. These methods highlight the growing interest in developing efficient quantization strategies for DiTs, particularly for real-time applications. The proposed Q-VDiT framework builds on these advancements by introducing Token-aware Quantization Estimator (TQE) and Temporal Maintenance Distillation (TMD) to address the specific challenges of video generation, achieving state-of-the-art results \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2302.04304)\].

### <a id='category-diffusion-models-in-generative-modeling'></a>Diffusion Models in Generative Modeling: 4 papers found.
Diffusion models have emerged as a powerful framework for generative modeling, particularly in image and video synthesis, due to their ability to produce high-quality samples through a sequential denoising process \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2302.04304)\]. Early approaches focused on pixel-space operations, which, while effective, were computationally intensive, requiring significant GPU resources for training and inference \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2302.04304)\]. To address these limitations, researchers introduced latent diffusion models (LDMs), which operate in the latent space of pretrained autoencoders, enabling a better balance between computational efficiency and visual fidelity \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2302.04304)\]. This shift allowed for the development of more scalable models, such as Diffusion Transformers (DiTs), which replace traditional U-Net architectures with transformers that process latent patches, leading to improved scalability and performance on high-resolution tasks \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/2406.17343)\]. Additionally, advancements in conditioning mechanisms, such as cross-attention layers, have enabled models to incorporate external inputs like text or bounding boxes, enhancing flexibility and control over the generation process \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2302.04304)\]. Furthermore, techniques like classifier guidance have been introduced to improve sample quality by trading off diversity for fidelity, achieving competitive results on standard benchmarks \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/2211.15736)\]. These developments highlight the ongoing evolution of diffusion models, with a focus on reducing computational demands while maintaining or improving the quality of generated outputs.

### <a id='category-post-training-quantization-methods'></a>Post-Training Quantization Methods: 7 papers found.
Post-training quantization methods have been extensively studied to reduce the computational and memory demands of deep learning models, particularly for deployment on edge devices and resource-constrained environments \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2302.04304)\]. These methods aim to compress models by reducing the bit-width of weights and activations while minimizing accuracy loss. However, applying these techniques to vision transformers (ViTs) and large-scale models like GPT has posed significant challenges. For instance, previous post-training quantization approaches have resulted in over 1% accuracy drop even at 8-bit quantization due to the non-Gaussian distribution of activation values and the inaccuracy of common quantization metrics like MSE and cosine distance \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2302.04304)\]. To address these issues, researchers have proposed specialized methods such as twin uniform quantization and Hessian-guided metrics to improve calibration accuracy \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2302.04304)\]. Additionally, methods like Power-of-Two Factor (PTF) and Log-Int-Softmax (LIS) have been introduced to handle the inter-channel variation in LayerNorm inputs and extreme non-uniformity in attention maps, enabling efficient 4-bit quantization \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/2406.17343)\]. Furthermore, recent works like ZeroQuant and GPTQ have demonstrated the feasibility of achieving lossless accuracy degradation on fully quantized models by employing hardware-friendly quantization schemes and one-shot weight quantization based on approximate second-order information \[[5](http://arxiv.org/abs/2405.16005)\]\[[6](http://arxiv.org/abs/2310.03270)\]. These advancements highlight the ongoing efforts to balance model efficiency with performance, especially for transformer-based architectures \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2302.04304)\]\[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/2406.17343)\]\[[5](http://arxiv.org/abs/2405.16005)\]\[[6](http://arxiv.org/abs/2310.03270)\].
