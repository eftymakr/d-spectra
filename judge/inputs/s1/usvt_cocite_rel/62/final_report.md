## Related Works

Generative retrieval has emerged as a promising paradigm in information retrieval, shifting from traditional index-based methods to end-to-end neural network models that directly generate document identifiers \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2204.10628)\]. This approach leverages large language models to perform both indexing and retrieval in a unified framework, offering potential improvements in efficiency and integration with generative models \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2204.10628)\]. Prior work has explored various aspects of this paradigm, including the design of retrieval models, the optimization of decoding strategies, and the generalization of models to unseen data.

Several studies have investigated the use of autoregressive models for document retrieval. For instance, SEAL \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2204.10628)\] proposes using ngrams as document identifiers, allowing autoregressive models to generate and score distinctive ngrams, which are then mapped to full passages. This method outperforms prior autoregressive approaches and establishes new state-of-the-art results on the KILT benchmark \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2204.10628)\]. Similarly, CorpusBrain \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/2208.07652)\] introduces a pre-trained generative retrieval model that replaces traditional index-retrieve pipelines with a single-step generative model, achieving significant improvements in retrieval performance \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/2208.07652)\]. The model is trained on a set of pre-defined tasks and can be fine-tuned for various downstream tasks \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/2208.07652)\].

Another line of research focuses on the challenges of generating document identifiers in a constrained manner. For example, GENRE \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/2010.00904)\] introduces an autoregressive system for entity retrieval, generating entity names token-by-token while conditioning on the context. This approach mitigates issues related to dense vector representations and memory footprint, enabling efficient and accurate entity retrieval \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/2010.00904)\]. Similarly, the Differentiable Search Index (DSI) \[[9](http://arxiv.org/abs/2202.06991)\] represents documents as sequences of document ID tokens, allowing a single transformer model to perform both indexing and retrieval. This paradigm simplifies the retrieval process and enables end-to-end optimization \[[9](http://arxiv.org/abs/2202.06991)\].

The effectiveness of beam search in generative retrieval has also been a topic of investigation. For example, DSI-QG \[[6](http://arxiv.org/abs/2206.10128)\] addresses the issue of data distribution mismatch between indexing and retrieval by generating relevant queries during indexing. These queries help align the document identifiers with the query distribution, improving retrieval performance in both mono-lingual and cross-lingual settings \[[6](http://arxiv.org/abs/2206.10128)\]. Additionally, PAG \[[7](http://arxiv.org/abs/2404.14600)\] introduces a novel decoding approach that guides autoregressive generation through simultaneous decoding, achieving significant improvements in MRR and query latency \[[7](http://arxiv.org/abs/2404.14600)\].

### <a id='category-autoregressive-document-retrieval-models'></a>Autoregressive Document Retrieval Models: 2 papers found.
Autoregressive document retrieval models have gained traction as a promising alternative to traditional search index structures, leveraging large-scale neural networks to generate document identifiers directly from a corpus. These models are particularly advantageous for seamless integration with generative large language models, offering improved efficiency and flexibility \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2204.10628)\]. Recent work has focused on optimizing the autoregressive generation of document identifiers through advanced decoding strategies. For instance, PAG introduces a novel optimization and decoding approach that constructs both a set-based and sequential identifier for each document, leveraging lexical tokens and relevance-based representations, respectively \[[7](http://arxiv.org/abs/2404.14600)\]. This method not only enhances retrieval performance but also significantly reduces query latency, achieving a 22× speedup compared to state-of-the-art models \[[7](http://arxiv.org/abs/2404.14600)\]. Additionally, other approaches have explored the use of ngrams as identifiers, allowing for a more flexible and unstructured search space by mapping generated ngrams to full passages through efficient data structures \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2204.10628)\]. These innovations highlight the evolving landscape of autoregressive retrieval, emphasizing the importance of both constraint design and decoding strategies in achieving robust and generalizable performance.

### <a id='category-end-to-end-retrieval-system-design'></a>End-to-End Retrieval System Design: 5 papers found.
End-to-end retrieval system design has seen significant advancements with the introduction of generative models that replace traditional index-retrieve pipelines. These models, such as CorpusBrain \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2204.10628)\], Neural Corpus Indexer (NCI) \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/2208.07652)\], and GenRet \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/2010.00904)\], aim to unify training and indexing stages, enabling end-to-end optimization of retrieval tasks. CorpusBrain leverages a pre-trained generative model to encode all corpus information within its parameters, eliminating the need for additional indexing structures \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2204.10628)\]. Similarly, NCI introduces a sequence-to-sequence network that generates document identifiers directly for a query, improving recall performance through a prefix-aware weight-adaptive decoder \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/2208.07652)\]. GenRet further advances this approach by learning semantic document identifiers via a discrete auto-encoding framework, allowing for fully end-to-end training and improved performance on benchmark datasets \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/2010.00904)\]. These models address the limitations of traditional methods by enabling more flexible and efficient retrieval, particularly in zero- and low-resource settings. Additionally, Ultron \[[4](http://arxiv.org/abs/2206.02743)\] explores model-based indexers that encode document knowledge directly into the model, enhancing retrieval accuracy through richer semantic docids and a three-stage training workflow. These developments highlight the growing trend toward end-to-end generative retrieval systems that integrate seamlessly with large language models, offering improved efficiency and generalization capabilities.

### <a id='category-generative-retrieval-paradigm-overview'></a>Generative Retrieval Paradigm Overview: 3 papers found.
The Generative Retrieval Paradigm Overview category highlights the shift from traditional retrieval methods to generative approaches that leverage large language models for more efficient and flexible information retrieval. This paradigm focuses on generating document identifiers or entity names directly from context, rather than relying on pre-built indexes \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/2010.00904)\]. For instance, GENRE introduces an autoregressive model that generates entity names token-by-token, allowing for better context-entity interactions and reduced memory usage compared to dense representation methods \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/2010.00904)\]. Similarly, LTRGR bridges the gap between autoregressive generation and passage ranking by incorporating a learning-to-rank framework, optimizing the model for final ranking objectives without increasing inference complexity \[[12](http://arxiv.org/abs/2306.15222)\]. In the context of fact verification, GERE extends this approach by generating both document titles and evidence sentence identifiers, enabling dynamic evidence selection and reducing computational overhead \[[15](http://arxiv.org/abs/2204.05511)\]. These systems demonstrate that generative retrieval can offer significant improvements in efficiency and adaptability, particularly when integrated with constrained decoding strategies tailored to specific corpora. The theoretical analysis of constraints and beam search further underscores the need for refined decoding strategies to enhance generalization capabilities \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/2010.00904)\].

### <a id='category-differentiable-search-index-frameworks'></a>Differentiable Search Index Frameworks: 3 papers found.
The category "Differentiable Search Index Frameworks" explores the use of transformer-based models to replace traditional search index structures, enabling end-to-end retrieval through learned differentiable search indexes. These frameworks, such as the Differentiable Search Index (DSI) \[[9](http://arxiv.org/abs/2202.06991)\], represent document corpora within a single Transformer, allowing direct generation of document identifiers from queries without external indices. This approach simplifies the retrieval process and enhances integration with generative large language models. However, existing models face challenges in scaling to large corpora, as demonstrated by the need for synthetic queries during indexing and the limitations of naive parameter scaling \[[11](http://arxiv.org/abs/2305.11841)\]. Recent advancements, such as RIPOR \[[8](http://arxiv.org/abs/2311.09134)\], address these issues by introducing prefix-oriented ranking optimization and relevance-based document ID construction, achieving significant performance improvements on benchmarks like MSMARCO. These frameworks highlight the potential of generative retrieval while underscoring the need for further research to overcome scalability and generalization challenges.
