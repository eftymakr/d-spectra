## Related Works

Quantitative susceptibility mapping (QSM) has emerged as a powerful tool for characterizing multiple sclerosis (MS) lesions, particularly in distinguishing between "rim" and "non-rim" lesion types \[[13](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/455e181fe505a36406af032700fea2ca87a2e9b9)\]. Rim lesions, which are surrounded by paramagnetic iron deposits, are associated with more aggressive disease progression and are considered an emerging biomarker in MS \[[2](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/bb5b80dc4165d0ac3e28d1882b898633e84779c2)\]. The ability to differentiate these lesions is crucial for monitoring disease progression and evaluating treatment efficacy \[[1](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/09b2a6040c15e8672169531fc76bba0b5a04296b)\]. However, the rarity of rim lesions poses a significant challenge in terms of class imbalance, which hinders the performance of classification models \[[1](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/09b2a6040c15e8672169531fc76bba0b5a04296b)\].

Several studies have explored automated methods for detecting and segmenting MS lesions. For instance, a fully automated technique was developed to identify paramagnetic rim lesions (PRLs) using standard T1 and FLAIR sequences along with a T2* phase sequence \[[1](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/09b2a6040c15e8672169531fc76bba0b5a04296b)\]. This method achieved a high correlation with gold-standard counts of PRLs and demonstrated promising classification accuracy using radiomic features \[[1](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/09b2a6040c15e8672169531fc76bba0b5a04296b)\]. Similarly, QSMRim-Net, a deep neural network, was introduced to address the class imbalance issue by fusing lesion-level radiomic and convolutional image features \[[3](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/45bf918158d3f779cb41b982fa9237682c11dd96)\]. This model outperformed other state-of-the-art methods in terms of area under the precision-recall curve (PR AUC) and demonstrated strong correlation with expert annotations \[[3](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/45bf918158d3f779cb41b982fa9237682c11dd96)\].

The challenge of class imbalance in lesion detection has also been addressed through synthetic data generation. For example, the use of generative adversarial networks (GANs) to create synthetic QSM maps of PRLs has been proposed to improve classifier performance and increase the minority class representation \[[1](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/09b2a6040c15e8672169531fc76bba0b5a04296b)\]. This approach not only enhances the training data but also enables the generation of accompanying contrasts and probabilistic segmentation maps \[[1](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/09b2a6040c15e8672169531fc76bba0b5a04296b)\]. The proposed method leverages the projection capability of the trained generative network to demonstrate a novel denoising approach that allows training on ambiguous rim cases, thereby improving detection accuracy \[[1](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/09b2a6040c15e8672169531fc76bba0b5a04296b)\].

In addition to synthetic data generation, other studies have focused on improving segmentation accuracy through advanced neural network architectures. This method outperformed state-of-the-art approaches in terms of lesion segmentation accuracy \[[7](http://arxiv.org/abs/2002.12470)\].
