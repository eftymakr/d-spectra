## Related Works

Dense retrievers have gained significant traction in information retrieval due to their ability to encode text into dense vector representations that can be efficiently compared using shallow similarity operations such as inner product \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2108.05540)\]. These models typically rely on pre-trained language models (e.g., BERT, LLaMA) that are fine-tuned using contrastive learning to align queries with relevant documents \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/2104.08253)\]. However, recent research has questioned the role of fine-tuning versus pre-training in dense retrievers, suggesting that retrieval knowledge is primarily acquired during pre-training, and that fine-tuning may not significantly alter the underlying knowledge \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/1810.04805)\].

This idea was initially explored in the context of BERT-based encoders using the Dense Passage Retrieval (DPR) framework \[[4](http://arxiv.org/abs/1907.11692)\]. The study found that pre-trained knowledge underpins retrieval performance, with fine-tuning primarily adjusting neuron activation rather than reorganizing knowledge \[[5](http://arxiv.org/abs/2002.08909)\]. However, this pattern does not hold universally. For instance, in mean-pooled models like Contriever, and decoder-based models like LLaMA, the role of fine-tuning appears to be more pronounced \[[6](http://arxiv.org/abs/2007.00808)\].

Several works have investigated the effectiveness of different pre-training architectures for dense retrieval. The Condenser architecture was proposed to improve dense encoders by pre-training with a novel Transformer structure that conditions on dense representations \[[7](http://arxiv.org/abs/2112.09118)\]. Similarly, the coCondenser model introduced an unsupervised corpus-level contrastive loss to warm up the passage embedding space, reducing the need for heavy data engineering \[[8](http://arxiv.org/abs/2010.08191)\]. These models demonstrate that pre-training strategies can significantly impact the performance of dense retrievers.

The importance of contrastive learning in dense retrieval has also been highlighted in other works. For example, the ANCE model constructs negatives from an Approximate Nearest Neighbor (ANN) index of the corpus, resolving the discrepancy between training and testing data distributions \[[9](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/5be217c678916543884c354654263f27c0a6bd9f)\]. Similarly, the REALM model augments language model pre-training with a latent knowledge retriever, allowing the model to retrieve and attend over documents during pre-training, fine-tuning, and inference \[[10](http://arxiv.org/abs/2006.03659)\].

Moreover, the effectiveness of contrastive learning has been explored in the context of unsupervised dense retrievers.
