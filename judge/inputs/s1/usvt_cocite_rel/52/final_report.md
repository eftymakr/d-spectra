## Related Works

Dense retrievers have gained significant traction in information retrieval due to their ability to encode text into dense vector representations that can be efficiently compared using shallow similarity operations such as inner product \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2108.05540)\]. These models typically rely on pre-trained language models (e.g., BERT, LLaMA) that are fine-tuned using contrastive learning to align queries with relevant documents \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/2104.08253)\]. However, recent research has questioned the role of fine-tuning versus pre-training in dense retrievers, suggesting that retrieval knowledge is primarily acquired during pre-training, and that fine-tuning may not significantly alter the underlying knowledge \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/1810.04805)\].

This idea was initially explored in the context of BERT-based encoders using the Dense Passage Retrieval (DPR) framework \[[4](http://arxiv.org/abs/1907.11692)\]. The study found that pre-trained knowledge underpins retrieval performance, with fine-tuning primarily adjusting neuron activation rather than reorganizing knowledge \[[5](http://arxiv.org/abs/2002.08909)\]. However, this pattern does not hold universally. For instance, in mean-pooled models like Contriever, and decoder-based models like LLaMA, the role of fine-tuning appears to be more pronounced \[[6](http://arxiv.org/abs/2007.00808)\].

Several works have investigated the effectiveness of different pre-training architectures for dense retrieval. The Condenser architecture was proposed to improve dense encoders by pre-training with a novel Transformer structure that conditions on dense representations \[[7](http://arxiv.org/abs/2112.09118)\]. Similarly, the coCondenser model introduced an unsupervised corpus-level contrastive loss to warm up the passage embedding space, reducing the need for heavy data engineering \[[8](http://arxiv.org/abs/2010.08191)\]. These models demonstrate that pre-training strategies can significantly impact the performance of dense retrievers.

Similarly, the REALM model augments language model pre-training with a latent knowledge retriever, allowing the model to retrieve and attend over documents during pre-training, fine-tuning, and inference \[[10](http://arxiv.org/abs/2006.03659)\].

The category "Pre-trained Language Models in Retrieval" explores how pre-trained language models (LMs) are leveraged for dense retrieval tasks, emphasizing the role of pre-training versus fine-tuning. Recent studies highlight that dense retrievers, such as those based on BERT or LLaMA, rely heavily on pre-trained knowledge, with fine-tuning primarily adjusting neuron activations rather than reorganizing learned representations \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2108.05540)\]. This aligns with findings that standard LMs' internal attention structures are not inherently suitable for dense encoders, necessitating novel architectures like Condenser, which pre-trains LMs to condition on dense representations \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/2104.08253)\]. Moreover, research on retrieval-augmented generation (RAG) demonstrates that combining pre-trained parametric and non-parametric memory can enhance performance on knowledge-intensive tasks \[[6](http://arxiv.org/abs/2007.00808)\]. These works collectively underscore the importance of pre-training in shaping the capabilities of dense retrievers, while also identifying challenges such as data dependency and the need for specialized architectures \[[7](http://arxiv.org/abs/2112.09118)\].

The category "Encoder Architectures (BERT vs. LLaMA)" explores the design and performance of different encoder models in natural language processing. BERT, introduced as a bidirectional encoder, pre-trains deep representations from unlabeled text by conditioning on both left and right context, leading to state-of-the-art results on various NLP tasks \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/1810.04805)\]. These advancements highlight the ongoing evolution of encoder architectures, with a focus on balancing performance, efficiency, and adaptability across different tasks and computational constraints. The comparison between BERT and LLaMA, particularly in terms of encoder-only versus decoder-only designs, further underscores the importance of architectural choices in shaping model behavior and performance in dense retrievers \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/1810.04805)\].
