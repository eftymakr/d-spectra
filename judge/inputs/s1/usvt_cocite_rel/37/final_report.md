## Related Works

Vision Transformers (ViTs) have emerged as a powerful alternative to convolutional neural networks (CNNs) in computer vision tasks, leveraging the transformer architecture to effectively capture global context \[[4](http://arxiv.org/abs/2010.11929)\]. However, the deployment of ViTs on hardware, especially FPGAs, remains challenging due to the complex arithmetic operations such as Softmax and LayerNorm, which are sensitive to model accuracy and difficult to map onto efficient hardware with low precision \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2208.05163)\]. Prior work has primarily focused on optimizing matrix multiplication operations, leaving these complex operations to be executed on the CPU, resulting in suboptimal performance due to communication overhead between the CPU and accelerator \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2208.05163)\].

Several studies have explored quantization techniques to reduce the computational and memory demands of ViTs. For instance, Auto-ViT-Acc proposes an FPGA-aware automatic acceleration framework with mixed-scheme quantization, achieving a 5.6x improvement in frame rate with minimal accuracy loss \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2208.05163)\]. Similarly, I-ViT introduces an integer-only quantization scheme that enables ViTs to perform inference using only integer arithmetic, achieving comparable or slightly higher accuracy than full-precision models \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/2207.01405)\]. FQ-ViT further advances this by introducing the Power-of-Two Factor (PTF) and Log-Int-Softmax (LIS) to reduce performance degradation in fully quantized ViTs \[[6](http://arxiv.org/abs/2111.13824)\]. VAQF presents a fully automatic framework for low-bit ViT acceleration on FPGAs, achieving real-time execution with 8-bit activation quantization \[[7](http://arxiv.org/abs/2201.06618)\].

Token pruning and attention map optimization have also been explored to improve hardware efficiency. HeatViT introduces a hardware-efficient adaptive token pruning framework, achieving significant computation reduction while maintaining high accuracy \[[8](http://arxiv.org/abs/2211.08110)\]. Patch Slimming further reduces computational costs by discarding redundant patches in a top-down manner, achieving over 45% FLOPs reduction with minimal accuracy drop \[[9](http://arxiv.org/abs/2106.02852)\]. Chasing Sparsity explores end-to-end sparsity in ViTs, achieving computational cost reduction and almost unimpaired generalization \[[10](http://arxiv.org/abs/2106.04533)\].

### <a id='category-integer-only-quantization-schemes'></a>Integer-Only Quantization Schemes: 2 papers found.
Integer-only quantization schemes have emerged as a critical area of research for improving the efficiency of Vision Transformers (ViTs) on edge devices. These schemes aim to reduce the computational and storage overheads of ViTs by replacing floating-point arithmetic with integer-only operations, which are more suitable for hardware acceleration \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2208.05163)\]. However, traditional approaches face challenges due to the non-linear nature of operations such as Softmax, GELU, and LayerNorm, which are not easily approximated using dyadic arithmetic \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/2207.01405)\]. To address this, I-ViT introduces a novel integer-only quantization method that enables the entire inference pipeline of ViTs to operate with integer arithmetic and bit-shifting, eliminating the need for floating-point operations \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2208.05163)\]. This is achieved through the development of lightweight integer-only approximations like Shiftmax and ShiftGELU, which closely mimic their floating-point counterparts while maintaining high accuracy \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2208.05163)\]. Similarly, other works have focused on simplifying non-linear operations in Transformer models by proposing integer-only arithmetic methods and algorithm-hardware co-design strategies to ensure both accuracy and efficiency \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/2207.01405)\]. Additionally, the use of specialized hardware architectures, such as group vector systolic arrays, has been explored to accelerate matrix multiplication and self-attention operations in Transformers, further enhancing performance on FPGAs \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/2207.01405)\]. These advancements highlight the growing interest in integer-only quantization for deploying ViTs on resource-constrained hardware.

### <a id='category-token-pruning-and-attention-map-optimization'></a>Token Pruning and Attention Map Optimization: 4 papers found.
Vision Transformers (ViTs) have emerged as a powerful architecture for computer vision tasks, but their deployment on resource-constrained platforms remains challenging due to their high computational and memory demands \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2208.05163)\]. Existing approaches often focus on optimizing parallelism in matrix multiplication operations while offloading complex operations like Softmax and LayerNorm to the CPU, leading to inefficiencies caused by CPU-accelerator communication overhead \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2208.05163)\]. To address these limitations, recent research has explored token pruning and attention map optimization as a means to reduce computational costs without compromising model accuracy. For instance, methods like HeatViT introduce hardware-efficient token pruning frameworks that dynamically identify and remove non-informative tokens, achieving significant computation reductions while maintaining high accuracy \[[8](http://arxiv.org/abs/2211.08110)\]. Similarly, the patch slimming approach proposed in another study identifies and discards redundant patches in a top-down manner, reducing computational costs by over 45% with minimal accuracy loss \[[9](http://arxiv.org/abs/2106.02852)\]. These techniques highlight the potential of structured pruning strategies to enhance hardware efficiency. Furthermore, the ViTCoD framework integrates algorithmic and hardware-level optimizations, including attention map pruning and data movement reduction, to achieve substantial speedups on general computing platforms \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/2210.09573)\]. These efforts collectively emphasize the importance of combining algorithmic innovations with hardware-specific optimizations to enable efficient ViT deployment.

### <a id='category-vision-transformer-(vit)-architecture-overview'></a>Vision Transformer (ViT) Architecture Overview: 5 papers found.
Vision Transformer (ViT) architectures have gained significant traction in computer vision due to their ability to capture global context effectively, as demonstrated in various benchmarks \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2208.05163)\]. However, early ViT models faced challenges in performance compared to convolutional neural networks (CNNs), particularly when trained from scratch on mid-sized datasets like ImageNet, due to issues such as inefficient tokenization and limited feature richness \[[4](http://arxiv.org/abs/2010.11929)\]. To address these limitations, researchers proposed advanced variants like LeViT, which integrates attention mechanisms with CNN principles to optimize the speed-accuracy tradeoff, achieving superior performance on both CPU and GPU \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/2207.01405)\]. Additionally, the Swin Transformer introduced a hierarchical design with shifted windows, enabling efficient modeling of visual data at multiple scales while maintaining linear computational complexity \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/2210.09573)\]. Another notable advancement is T2T-ViT, which improves tokenization by progressively structurizing images into tokens, leading to better performance and reduced computational requirements \[[5](http://arxiv.org/abs/2104.01136)\]. Furthermore, MobileViT combines the strengths of CNNs and ViTs to create a lightweight model suitable for mobile vision tasks, achieving competitive accuracy with fewer parameters \[[6](http://arxiv.org/abs/2111.13824)\]. These developments highlight the ongoing efforts to enhance ViT efficiency and performance, addressing key challenges in hardware acceleration and model deployment.
