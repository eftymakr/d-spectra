## Related Works

The field of sensor calibration for autonomous systems has seen significant advancements, particularly in the integration of radar and camera data. Early approaches focused on offline calibration using dedicated calibration targets, which limited their applicability in dynamic environments \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/1904.08743)\]. More recently, researchers have explored targetless calibration methods to enable real-time operation. For instance, \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/1904.08743)\] proposed an automatic rotational radar-camera calibration method without the need for calibration targets, leveraging deep learning techniques. Similarly, \[[10](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/fbd0a52edc6ee6c8dd8a6992eaee20d66b982d65)\] introduced a targetless calibration approach for radar and camera systems, emphasizing the use of real-world data to train models. However, these methods often rely on specific sensor configurations and may not generalize well across different environments.

Several studies have addressed the challenges of sparsity and uncertainty in radar data. \[[12](http://arxiv.org/abs/2211.01871)\] introduced a targetless spatiotemporal calibration method for 3D millimetre-wavelength radar-camera pairs, leveraging the radar's ability to measure ego-velocity. This approach allows for calibration in arbitrary environments without specialized infrastructure. \[[17](http://arxiv.org/abs/2403.07746)\] further advanced this by introducing HyDRa, a hybrid fusion architecture that combines radar and camera features to improve depth prediction and 3D object detection. The method utilizes a Height Association Transformer module to enhance depth accuracy, demonstrating robustness in adverse conditions.

In the context of online calibration, \[[6](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/c80265fa719eb85c8a56a6b36c74b99b793f39b2)\] proposed an online multi-sensor calibration method based on moving object tracking, which does not require ego-motion estimation. This method leverages the detection and tracking of moving objects to continuously update calibration parameters. \[[13](http://arxiv.org/abs/2010.03320)\] introduced an uncertainty-based sensor fusion approach for vehicle detection using camera and radar data, combining outputs from two neural networks in an uncertainty-aware manner. This method was evaluated on the nuScenes dataset, showing significant performance gains over single-sensor baselines.

The use of deep learning for radar-camera fusion has also gained traction. \[[18](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/cc183eba49f926d84b3a401490c819be64b9b222)\] proposed a spatial attention fusion method for obstacle detection using mmWave radar and vision sensors, considering the sparsity of radar points. The method was trained end-to-end and demonstrated superior performance on public benchmarks. \[[14](http://arxiv.org/abs/2404.03015)\] introduced DPFT, a novel camera-radar fusion approach that leverages lower-level radar data (the radar cube) to preserve information and employs projections in both the camera and ground planes to simplify fusion. This method showed state-of-the-art performance on the K-Radar dataset while maintaining robustness against adverse weather.

To address these challenges, researchers have proposed data-driven techniques that leverage neural networks and real-world data to achieve robust sensor registration. Additionally, a spatiotemporal calibration method has been introduced that utilizes radar's ability to measure ego-velocity, enabling calibration in infrastructure-free environments \[[12](http://arxiv.org/abs/2211.01871)\]. These approaches demonstrate the growing trend toward self-calibrating systems that can adapt to changing conditions and operate reliably in dynamic environments.
