## Related Works

Unsupervised domain adaptation (UDA) for semantic segmentation has attracted significant research interest due to its ability to transfer knowledge from labeled source data to unlabeled target data without requiring additional annotations \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/1910.13049)\]. Traditional UDA methods typically assume that the category settings between source and target domains are known, which is often not the case in real-world scenarios \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/1910.13049)\]. This assumption leads to performance degradation when private classes exist in the target domain \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/1910.13049)\]. To address this limitation, several approaches have been proposed to handle unknown category settings.

One line of research focuses on improving the alignment of features across domains while considering class-level differences. For instance, \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/2004.07703)\] introduces a method that leverages self-supervision to minimize both inter-domain and intra-domain gaps. This approach first separates the target domain into easy and hard subsets based on entropy, then performs self-supervised adaptation from easy to hard subdomains \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/2004.07703)\]. Similarly, \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/2007.08702)\] proposes a method that mixes images from source and target domains along with corresponding labels and pseudo-labels, enabling more effective domain adaptation \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/2007.08702)\]. These methods aim to improve the generalization of models by addressing domain shifts at different levels.

Another approach involves the use of pseudo-labels to facilitate training on the target domain. \[[4](http://arxiv.org/abs/1909.13589)\] introduces a maximum squares loss to prevent the training process from being dominated by easy-to-transfer samples, thereby improving the quality of pseudo-labels \[[4](http://arxiv.org/abs/1909.13589)\]. \[[5](http://arxiv.org/abs/2008.12197)\] further enhances pseudo-label generation by introducing an instance adaptive selector and region-guided regularization to refine the pseudo-labels \[[5](http://arxiv.org/abs/2008.12197)\]. These methods highlight the importance of carefully managing pseudo-labels to avoid introducing noise into the training process.

To address the issue of domain shift, \[[6](http://arxiv.org/abs/2004.05498)\] proposes a method that swaps the low-frequency spectrum of images between domains, effectively reducing domain discrepancy without requiring adversarial optimization \[[6](http://arxiv.org/abs/2004.05498)\]. \[[7](http://arxiv.org/abs/2003.03773)\] introduces a method that explicitly estimates prediction uncertainty during training to rectify pseudo label learning, dynamically adjusting confidence thresholds based on prediction variance \[[7](http://arxiv.org/abs/2003.03773)\]. These methods emphasize the role of uncertainty estimation in improving the reliability of pseudo-labels.

Furthermore, \[[8](http://arxiv.org/abs/1904.10620)\] introduces a bidirectional learning framework that alternates between image translation and segmentation adaptation, promoting mutual improvement between the two tasks \[[8](http://arxiv.org/abs/1904.10620)\]. \[[9](http://arxiv.org/abs/1809.09478)\] proposes a category-level adversarial network to enforce local semantic consistency during global alignment, improving the alignment of features across domains \[[9](http://arxiv.org/abs/1809.09478)\]. These approaches demonstrate the potential of combining adversarial learning with domain-specific considerations to enhance adaptation performance.

### <a id='category-feature-alignment-across-domains'></a>Feature Alignment Across Domains: 17 papers found.
Unsupervised domain adaptation for semantic segmentation (UDA-SS) has been extensively studied, with a focus on aligning features across domains to improve generalization \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/1910.13049)\]. Early methods primarily relied on adversarial learning to minimize distribution discrepancies between source and target domains \[[6](http://arxiv.org/abs/2004.05498)\]. However, these approaches often fail to account for class-level structure, leading to poor performance on unseen or private classes \[[11](http://arxiv.org/abs/2007.09222)\]. More recent work has introduced fine-grained adversarial strategies that preserve semantic internal structures and enable class-level alignment \[[13](http://arxiv.org/abs/1802.10349)\]. For instance, methods like CAG-UDA explicitly enforce category-aware feature alignment to learn shared discriminative features \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/1910.13049)\]. Similarly, the SSF-DAN framework introduces a semantic-wise separable discriminator to address class-wise adaptation issues \[[14](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/3f9f801aec1f87ae81524f1928407673805927cb)\]. Additionally, methods such as HRDA have been proposed to handle domain shifts by leveraging multi-resolution training, preserving fine details while capturing long-range context \[[13](http://arxiv.org/abs/1802.10349)\]. The use of Fourier Transform-based domain alignment has also shown promise, achieving state-of-the-art results without complex adversarial optimization \[[6](http://arxiv.org/abs/2004.05498)\]. Furthermore, the introduction of multi-source domain adaptation frameworks, such as the one based on collaborative learning, has enabled better performance by leveraging multiple source domains \[[12](http://arxiv.org/abs/1912.08954)\]. These approaches collectively highlight the ongoing efforts to improve domain adaptation for semantic segmentation by addressing both global and class-level feature alignment challenges.

### <a id='category-pseudo-label-generation-techniques'></a>Pseudo-Label Generation Techniques: 9 papers found.
In the field of pseudo-label generation techniques for semantic segmentation, several approaches have been proposed to address the challenges of domain adaptation. These methods aim to improve the quality of pseudo-labels generated from unlabeled target data, which is crucial for unsupervised domain adaptation (UDA) tasks. For instance, entropy minimization has been widely used to refine pseudo-labels by focusing on confident predictions \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/2007.08702)\]. However, this approach tends to favor easy-to-transfer samples, leading to biased training \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/2007.08702)\]. To counteract this, researchers have introduced techniques such as maximum squares loss, which balances the gradient contributions from both easy and hard samples \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/2007.08702)\]. Additionally, instance adaptive self-training frameworks have been developed to enhance pseudo-label quality by incorporating an instance adaptive selector that dynamically adjusts the selection of pseudo-labels \[[4](http://arxiv.org/abs/1909.13589)\]. Furthermore, methods like DACS (Domain Adaptation via Cross-domain mixed Sampling) have been proposed to mix images and labels from both source and target domains, enabling more robust training \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/2004.07703)\]. These approaches highlight the ongoing efforts to improve pseudo-label generation, ensuring better generalization across domains \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/2004.07703)\]\[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/2007.08702)\]\[[4](http://arxiv.org/abs/1909.13589)\].
