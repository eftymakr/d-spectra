## Related Works

The problem of learning from noisy labels has been extensively studied in the context of deep learning, with various approaches proposed to mitigate the negative impact of label noise on model performance. Early works focused on modifying network architectures or estimating true labels from noisy ones \[[6](http://arxiv.org/abs/1706.00038)\]. For instance, \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/1609.03683)\] proposed a loss correction approach that assumes knowledge of class-dependent label noise probabilities, enabling matrix inversion and multiplication to correct the loss function. This method was validated across multiple datasets, including MNIST and CIFAR-10, demonstrating robustness to label noise. Similarly, \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/1803.11364)\] introduced a joint optimization framework that simultaneously learns DNN parameters and estimates true labels, achieving significant improvements over state-of-the-art methods on noisy CIFAR-10 and Clothing1M datasets.

Another line of research explores the memorization effects of deep neural networks, suggesting that models first memorize clean samples before noisy ones \[[15](http://arxiv.org/abs/1706.05394)\]. This insight has led to the development of Co-teaching, a method that trains two neural networks in parallel, allowing them to teach each other and reduce the impact of noisy labels \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/1804.06872)\]. Empirical results show that Co-teaching significantly outperforms existing methods in robustness to label noise.

In addition to architectural modifications, loss function design has been explored as a way to improve robustness. \[[9](http://arxiv.org/abs/1712.09482)\] investigated noise-tolerant loss functions for multiclass classification, showing that the mean absolute error (MAE) loss is inherently robust to label noise. This finding was further generalized in \[[14](http://arxiv.org/abs/1805.07836)\], where a set of noise-robust loss functions was proposed as a generalization of MAE and categorical cross entropy (CCE). These losses were tested on various datasets, including CIFAR-10 and FASHION-MNIST, demonstrating their effectiveness in noisy label scenarios.

Label reweighting has also been proposed as a way to mitigate the impact of noisy labels. \[[8](http://arxiv.org/abs/1803.09050)\] introduced a meta-learning algorithm that learns to assign weights to training examples based on gradient directions, avoiding the need for manual hyperparameter tuning. This method was shown to be effective in handling class imbalance and corrupted labels with minimal clean validation data.

Furthermore, \[[11](http://arxiv.org/abs/1802.05300)\] proposed a loss correction method that leverages a small set of trusted clean labels to improve robustness against severe label noise. This approach was validated on vision and natural language processing tasks, showing significant improvements over existing methods.
