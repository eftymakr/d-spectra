## Related Works

The problem of learning from noisy labels has been extensively studied in the context of deep learning, with various approaches proposed to mitigate the negative impact of label noise on model performance. Early works focused on modifying network architectures or estimating true labels from noisy ones \[[6](http://arxiv.org/abs/1706.00038)\]. For instance, \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/1609.03683)\] proposed a loss correction approach that assumes knowledge of class-dependent label noise probabilities, enabling matrix inversion and multiplication to correct the loss function. This method was validated across multiple datasets, including MNIST and CIFAR-10, demonstrating robustness to label noise. Similarly, \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/1803.11364)\] introduced a joint optimization framework that simultaneously learns DNN parameters and estimates true labels, achieving significant improvements over state-of-the-art methods on noisy CIFAR-10 and Clothing1M datasets.

Another line of research explores the memorization effects of deep neural networks, suggesting that models first memorize clean samples before noisy ones \[[15](http://arxiv.org/abs/1706.05394)\]. This insight has led to the development of Co-teaching, a method that trains two neural networks in parallel, allowing them to teach each other and reduce the impact of noisy labels \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/1804.06872)\]. Empirical results show that Co-teaching significantly outperforms existing methods in robustness to label noise.

In addition to architectural modifications, loss function design has been explored as a way to improve robustness. \[[9](http://arxiv.org/abs/1712.09482)\] investigated noise-tolerant loss functions for multiclass classification, showing that the mean absolute error (MAE) loss is inherently robust to label noise. This finding was further generalized in \[[14](http://arxiv.org/abs/1805.07836)\], where a set of noise-robust loss functions was proposed as a generalization of MAE and categorical cross entropy (CCE). These losses were tested on various datasets, including CIFAR-10 and FASHION-MNIST, demonstrating their effectiveness in noisy label scenarios.

Label reweighting has also been proposed as a way to mitigate the impact of noisy labels. \[[8](http://arxiv.org/abs/1803.09050)\] introduced a meta-learning algorithm that learns to assign weights to training examples based on gradient directions, avoiding the need for manual hyperparameter tuning. This method was shown to be effective in handling class imbalance and corrupted labels with minimal clean validation data.

Furthermore, \[[11](http://arxiv.org/abs/1802.05300)\] proposed a loss correction method that leverages a small set of trusted clean labels to improve robustness against severe label noise. This approach was validated on vision and natural language processing tasks, showing significant improvements over existing methods.

### <a id='category-loss-function-design-for-robustness'></a>Loss Function Design for Robustness: 3 papers found.
The category "Loss Function Design for Robustness" focuses on improving the performance of deep neural networks (DNNs) in the presence of noisy labels by designing loss functions that are inherently robust to label noise. Existing approaches often modify network architectures or estimate true labels from noisy ones, but an alternative approach is to develop loss functions that minimize risk under noisy conditions \[[9](http://arxiv.org/abs/1712.09482)\]. For instance, the mean absolute error (MAE) has been proposed as a noise-robust alternative to categorical cross entropy (CCE), although it can perform poorly with DNNs and challenging datasets \[[14](http://arxiv.org/abs/1805.07836)\]. To address these limitations, researchers have introduced theoretically grounded loss functions that generalize both MAE and CCE, enabling robust learning across various noisy label scenarios \[[14](http://arxiv.org/abs/1805.07836)\]. Additionally, symmetric cross entropy learning (SL) has been proposed, which combines cross entropy with a noise-robust counterpart, reverse cross entropy (RCE), to simultaneously address under learning and overfitting issues caused by noisy labels \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/1609.03683)\]. These advancements highlight the importance of designing loss functions that are not only effective in mitigating the impact of label noise but also compatible with existing DNN architectures \[[9](http://arxiv.org/abs/1712.09482)\].

[Snippet]: «Deep neural networks have been shown to be very powerful modeling tools for many supervised learning tasks involving complex input patterns. However, they can also easily overfit to training set biases and label noises. In addition to various regularizers, example reweighting algorithms are popular solutions to these problems, but they require careful tuning of additional hyperparameters, such as example mining schedules and regularization hyperparameters. In contrast to past reweighting methods, which typically consist of functions of the cost value of each example, in this work we propose a novel meta-learning algorithm that learns to assign weights to training examples based on their gradient directions. To determine the example weights, our method performs a meta gradient descent step on the current mini-batch example weights (which are initialized from zero) to minimize the loss on a clean unbiased validation set. Our proposed method can be easily implemented on any type of deep network, does not require any additional hyperparameter tuning, and achieves impressive performance on class imbalance and corrupted label problems where only a small amount of clean validation data is available.»

[Snippet]: «Despite their massive size, successful deep artificial neural networks can exhibit a remarkably small difference between training and test performance. Conventional wisdom attributes small generalization error either to properties of the model family, or to the regularization techniques used during training. 
Through extensive systematic experiments, we show how these traditional approaches fail to explain why large neural networks generalize well in practice. Specifically, our experiments establish that state-of-the-art convolutional networks for image classification trained with stochastic gradient methods easily fit a random labeling of the training data. This phenomenon is qualitatively unaffected by explicit regularization, and occurs even if we replace the true images by completely unstructured random noise. We corroborate these experimental findings with a theoretical construction showing that simple depth two neural networks already have perfect finite sample expressivity as soon as the number of parameters exceeds the number of data points as it usually does in practice. 
We interpret our experimental findings by comparison with traditional models.»
