## Related Works

Generative retrieval (GR) has gained significant attention in information retrieval (IR) as a promising alternative to traditional index-retrieve pipelines \[[15](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/5beaeee1e27007bd12967480bb9c379cebd15e6e)\]. Unlike conventional methods that rely on static indexes, GR models directly generate document identifiers (docids) for a given query using a sequence-to-sequence framework \[[15](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/5beaeee1e27007bd12967480bb9c379cebd15e6e)\]. This paradigm has been explored in several recent works, each proposing novel approaches to enhance retrieval performance and adaptability to dynamic corpora.

Early efforts in GR focused on static document collections, where models were trained to generate docids based on fixed representations of documents \[[15](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/5beaeee1e27007bd12967480bb9c379cebd15e6e)\]. However, the ability to handle dynamic corpora, where documents are continuously added, has been a critical challenge \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2308.14968)\]. To address this, several works have introduced continual learning strategies to incrementally index new documents while preserving retrieval performance over both old and new data \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2308.14968)\]. For example, the CLEVER model proposed Incremental Product Quantization to efficiently encode new documents and introduced a memory-augmented mechanism to prevent catastrophic forgetting \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2308.14968)\].

The design of docids plays a crucial role in GR performance. While numeric-based docids are efficient, they often suffer from poor generalization over dynamic corpora due to their tendency to overfit the initial document set \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2308.14968)\]. In contrast, text-based docids, which are more semantically aligned with language models' pretrained knowledge, show superior performance in dynamic settings \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2308.14968)\]. This aligns with findings from the NOVO paper, which highlights the importance of learnable and interpretable docids that capture semantic relationships between documents \[[7](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/6682a3679a17d090830635f1c420126d20eae54a)\].

Several works have also explored the use of autoregressive models to generate docids, with some proposing novel tokenization strategies to improve retrieval effectiveness \[[10](http://arxiv.org/abs/2304.04171)\]. For instance, GenRet introduces a discrete auto-encoding approach to learn semantic docids in an end-to-end manner, significantly improving performance on unseen documents \[[10](http://arxiv.org/abs/2304.04171)\]. Similarly, the DSI framework encodes all corpus information into model parameters, enabling direct query-to-docid mapping without the need for external indexes \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/2202.06991)\].

Recent advancements have also focused on improving the scalability of GR models to large-scale corpora \[[13](http://arxiv.org/abs/2305.11841)\]. The DSI++ model, for instance, addresses the computational burden of retraining models when new documents are added, demonstrating that continual indexing can be achieved with minimal forgetting \[[8](http://arxiv.org/abs/2212.09744)\]. Additionally, the PAG approach introduces simultaneous decoding to guide autoregressive generation of docids, achieving faster query latency and improved performance over existing models \[[18](http://arxiv.org/abs/2404.14600)\].

### <a id='category-generative-retrieval-(gr)-frameworks'></a>Generative Retrieval (GR) Frameworks: 10 papers found.
Generative retrieval (GR) has gained significant traction as a novel paradigm in information retrieval, shifting from traditional index-based methods to a single model that encodes the entire corpus \[[8](http://arxiv.org/abs/2212.09744)\]. This approach, exemplified by the Differentiable Search Index (DSI), enables direct query-to-docid mapping without external indexes \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2308.14968)\]. Early works demonstrated the potential of GR models, particularly in small-scale settings, but challenges remain in scaling to large corpora and handling dynamic document collections \[[6](http://arxiv.org/abs/2208.09257)\]. Recent advancements, such as RIPOR, have addressed these limitations by introducing novel optimization frameworks and ranking algorithms that improve performance on standard benchmarks \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/2206.02743)\]. Additionally, DSI-QG mitigates data distribution mismatches by incorporating query generation during indexing, enhancing retrieval accuracy for both monolingual and cross-lingual tasks \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/2202.06991)\]. The KILT benchmark has also seen improvements through models like SEAL, which leverage autoregressive generation of ngrams to map to full passages, achieving state-of-the-art results \[[4](http://arxiv.org/abs/2311.09134)\]. Similarly, CorpusBrain demonstrates the viability of end-to-end generative retrieval by encoding all corpus information within a single model, bypassing the need for traditional index-retrieve-rank pipelines \[[5](http://arxiv.org/abs/2206.10128)\]. These developments highlight the growing interest in GR models, which aim to simplify retrieval while maintaining or improving performance over dynamic and large-scale datasets \[[10](http://arxiv.org/abs/2304.04171)\]. However, challenges persist, particularly in scaling to millions of passages and ensuring robustness across varying document collections \[[6](http://arxiv.org/abs/2208.09257)\]. The proposed multi-docid design in the paper builds on these insights, combining the efficiency of numeric-based docids with the effectiveness of text-based ones to achieve better performance in dynamic environments \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2308.14968)\].

### <a id='category-docid-design-approaches'></a>DocID Design Approaches: 6 papers found.
Document retrieval has evolved significantly with the advent of model-based approaches that aim to unify indexing and retrieval in an end-to-end manner \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2308.14968)\]. Traditional index-retrieve frameworks have limitations in optimizing retrieval quality due to the separation of indexing and retrieval stages \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2308.14968)\]. In response, several studies have proposed model-based IR systems that generate document identifiers (docids) directly from language models, enabling more semantically rich and flexible retrieval \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/2206.02743)\]. For instance, Ultron encodes document knowledge into a model to directly retrieve relevant documents, addressing the issue of semantically deficient docids and limited supervised data \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2308.14968)\]. Similarly, NOVO introduces learnable docids as unique n-gram sets, optimized through query denoising and retrieval tasks to better capture document semantics and relationships \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/2206.02743)\]. GenRet further advances this by learning semantic docids via a discrete auto-encoding approach, allowing for fully end-to-end modeling of retrieval tasks \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/2202.06991)\]. The DSI paradigm also aligns with this trend, parameterizing traditional index-retrieve pipelines within a single neural model \[[4](http://arxiv.org/abs/2311.09134)\]. SE-DSI improves upon DSI by incorporating cognitive psychology-inspired strategies for document identifiers and associations, enhancing retrieval performance \[[4](http://arxiv.org/abs/2311.09134)\]. PAG introduces a novel approach to generate document identifiers through simultaneous decoding, combining set-based and sequential identifiers for improved performance \[[5](http://arxiv.org/abs/2206.10128)\]. These methods collectively highlight the importance of semantic alignment, fine-grained docid design, and efficient generation mechanisms in modern generative retrieval systems \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2308.14968)\]\[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/2206.02743)\]\[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/2202.06991)\]\[[4](http://arxiv.org/abs/2311.09134)\]\[[5](http://arxiv.org/abs/2206.10128)\].
