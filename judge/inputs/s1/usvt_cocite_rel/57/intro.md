## Related Works

Generative retrieval (GR) has gained significant attention in information retrieval (IR) as a promising alternative to traditional index-retrieve pipelines \[[15](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/5beaeee1e27007bd12967480bb9c379cebd15e6e)\]. Unlike conventional methods that rely on static indexes, GR models directly generate document identifiers (docids) for a given query using a sequence-to-sequence framework \[[15](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/5beaeee1e27007bd12967480bb9c379cebd15e6e)\]. This paradigm has been explored in several recent works, each proposing novel approaches to enhance retrieval performance and adaptability to dynamic corpora.

Early efforts in GR focused on static document collections, where models were trained to generate docids based on fixed representations of documents \[[15](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/5beaeee1e27007bd12967480bb9c379cebd15e6e)\]. However, the ability to handle dynamic corpora, where documents are continuously added, has been a critical challenge \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2308.14968)\]. To address this, several works have introduced continual learning strategies to incrementally index new documents while preserving retrieval performance over both old and new data \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2308.14968)\]. For example, the CLEVER model proposed Incremental Product Quantization to efficiently encode new documents and introduced a memory-augmented mechanism to prevent catastrophic forgetting \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2308.14968)\].

The design of docids plays a crucial role in GR performance. While numeric-based docids are efficient, they often suffer from poor generalization over dynamic corpora due to their tendency to overfit the initial document set \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2308.14968)\]. In contrast, text-based docids, which are more semantically aligned with language models' pretrained knowledge, show superior performance in dynamic settings \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2308.14968)\]. This aligns with findings from the NOVO paper, which highlights the importance of learnable and interpretable docids that capture semantic relationships between documents \[[7](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/6682a3679a17d090830635f1c420126d20eae54a)\].

Several works have also explored the use of autoregressive models to generate docids, with some proposing novel tokenization strategies to improve retrieval effectiveness \[[10](http://arxiv.org/abs/2304.04171)\]. For instance, GenRet introduces a discrete auto-encoding approach to learn semantic docids in an end-to-end manner, significantly improving performance on unseen documents \[[10](http://arxiv.org/abs/2304.04171)\]. Similarly, the DSI framework encodes all corpus information into model parameters, enabling direct query-to-docid mapping without the need for external indexes \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/2202.06991)\].

Recent advancements have also focused on improving the scalability of GR models to large-scale corpora \[[13](http://arxiv.org/abs/2305.11841)\]. The DSI++ model, for instance, addresses the computational burden of retraining models when new documents are added, demonstrating that continual indexing can be achieved with minimal forgetting \[[8](http://arxiv.org/abs/2212.09744)\]. Additionally, the PAG approach introduces simultaneous decoding to guide autoregressive generation of docids, achieving faster query latency and improved performance over existing models \[[18](http://arxiv.org/abs/2404.14600)\].
