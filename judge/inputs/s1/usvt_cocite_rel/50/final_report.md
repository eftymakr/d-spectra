## Related Works

Knowledge graphs (KGs) have emerged as a critical tool for representing and reasoning over structured data, enabling applications such as question answering, information retrieval, and link prediction \[[7](http://arxiv.org/abs/2003.02320)\]. Early approaches to KG embedding focused on shallow models that could scale to large graphs, but these often lacked the expressive power of deeper architectures \[[5](http://arxiv.org/abs/1707.01476)\]. Recent work has introduced more sophisticated models, such as Relational Graph Convolutional Networks (R-GCNs), which are specifically designed to handle the multi-relational nature of KGs \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/1703.06103)\]. R-GCNs have shown significant improvements in link prediction tasks, particularly on datasets like FB15k-237, where they outperformed decoder-only baselines by over 29.8% \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/1703.06103)\]. Similarly, ConvE, a multi-layer convolutional model, has achieved state-of-the-art results on several benchmark datasets while being highly parameter efficient \[[5](http://arxiv.org/abs/1707.01476)\]. These models highlight the importance of capturing complex relational patterns in KGs.

In addition to link prediction, KGs are also used for entity classification and knowledge graph completion, where the goal is to infer missing attributes or facts \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/1703.06103)\]. One-shot relational learning has been proposed to address the challenge of predicting new facts when only a single training instance is available, leveraging both learned embeddings and graph structures to improve performance \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/1808.09040)\]. This approach is particularly useful for long-tail relations, which are common in real-world KGs \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/1808.09040)\]. Furthermore, the integration of textual information into KG representations has been explored, with models like KEPLER combining pre-trained language representations with knowledge embedding techniques to enhance both language understanding and factual knowledge \[[4](http://arxiv.org/abs/1911.06136)\]. This dual approach has led to improvements in tasks such as link prediction and knowledge graph completion \[[4](http://arxiv.org/abs/1911.06136)\].

The use of knowledge graphs in natural language processing (NLP) has also been extensively studied, with models like ConceptNet providing a rich, multilingual graph of general knowledge that complements word embeddings \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/1612.03975)\]. By combining ConceptNet with distributional semantics models such as word2vec, researchers have demonstrated improvements in tasks like word relatedness and analogy solving \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/1612.03975)\]. Similarly, the QA-GNN model integrates language models with KGs to perform joint reasoning over questions and knowledge, enabling more accurate and interpretable results \[[11](http://arxiv.org/abs/2104.06378)\]. This approach addresses the challenge of identifying relevant knowledge from large KGs and performing structured reasoning over both the question context and the graph \[[11](http://arxiv.org/abs/2104.06378)\].

In summary, the field of knowledge graph research has seen significant advancements in modeling relational data, integrating textual information, and improving reasoning capabilities
