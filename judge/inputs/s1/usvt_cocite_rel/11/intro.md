## Related Works

3D human pose estimation has attracted significant research interest due to its applications in robotics, virtual reality, and human-computer interaction. Early approaches primarily relied on 2D pose estimation followed by a mapping to 3D space, often using regression or optimization techniques \[[10](http://arxiv.org/abs/1711.08585)\]. However, these methods struggled with temporal consistency and depth ambiguity \[[10](http://arxiv.org/abs/1711.08585)\]. Recent advances have focused on leveraging deep learning architectures, particularly convolutional networks, to improve accuracy and robustness \[[8](http://arxiv.org/abs/1603.06937)\]. 

Several works have explored the use of graph-based models to capture spatial relationships among body joints. For instance, \[[1](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/50167a9e6bdc1f64a58fe8a3c990105fec0bd2bc)\] proposed a graph convolutional network to exploit spatial-temporal relationships for 3D pose estimation, incorporating domain knowledge about human body configurations. Similarly, \[[25](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/2170b81eaac4095f5948b7922f29200a25db9b2b)\] introduced high-order graph convolutional networks to model long-range dependencies between body joints, improving performance over vanilla graph convolutions. These methods demonstrate the effectiveness of graph-based approaches in capturing complex human body structures \[[1](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/50167a9e6bdc1f64a58fe8a3c990105fec0bd2bc)\]\[[25](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/2170b81eaac4095f5948b7922f29200a25db9b2b)\].

Temporal consistency is another critical aspect in 3D human pose estimation, especially when dealing with video sequences. \[[10](http://arxiv.org/abs/1711.08585)\] introduced a sequence-to-sequence network with LSTM units to enforce temporal smoothness, significantly improving results on the Human3.6M dataset. \[[7](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/30de7fecb146356d7ae6cafa072997674601e0f5)\] further advanced this idea by proposing an attention-based framework that adaptively identifies significant frames and tensor outputs, leading to more accurate and temporally coherent 3D pose estimates. The use of attention mechanisms has also been explored in \[[22](http://arxiv.org/abs/2103.14304)\], where a strided transformer architecture was introduced to reduce redundancy and improve efficiency in processing long 2D pose sequences.

In addition to graph-based and attention-based methods, transformer architectures have gained popularity due to their ability to model long-range dependencies. \[[6](http://arxiv.org/abs/2103.10455)\] proposed PoseFormer, a purely transformer-based approach for 3D human pose estimation, which effectively captures both spatial and temporal correlations. \[[12](http://arxiv.org/abs/2111.12707)\] introduced MHFormer, a multi-hypothesis transformer that learns multiple plausible pose hypotheses, enhancing accuracy by considering diverse solutions. These works highlight the growing interest in transformer-based models for 3D human pose estimation \[[6](http://arxiv.org/abs/2103.10455)\]\[[12](http://arxiv.org/abs/2111.12707)\].
