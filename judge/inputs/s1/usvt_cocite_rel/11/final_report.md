## Related Works

3D human pose estimation has attracted significant research interest due to its applications in robotics, virtual reality, and human-computer interaction. Early approaches primarily relied on 2D pose estimation followed by a mapping to 3D space, often using regression or optimization techniques \[[10](http://arxiv.org/abs/1711.08585)\]. However, these methods struggled with temporal consistency and depth ambiguity \[[10](http://arxiv.org/abs/1711.08585)\]. Recent advances have focused on leveraging deep learning architectures, particularly convolutional networks, to improve accuracy and robustness \[[8](http://arxiv.org/abs/1603.06937)\]. 

Several works have explored the use of graph-based models to capture spatial relationships among body joints. For instance, \[[1](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/50167a9e6bdc1f64a58fe8a3c990105fec0bd2bc)\] proposed a graph convolutional network to exploit spatial-temporal relationships for 3D pose estimation, incorporating domain knowledge about human body configurations. Similarly, \[[25](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/2170b81eaac4095f5948b7922f29200a25db9b2b)\] introduced high-order graph convolutional networks to model long-range dependencies between body joints, improving performance over vanilla graph convolutions. These methods demonstrate the effectiveness of graph-based approaches in capturing complex human body structures \[[1](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/50167a9e6bdc1f64a58fe8a3c990105fec0bd2bc)\]\[[25](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/2170b81eaac4095f5948b7922f29200a25db9b2b)\].

Temporal consistency is another critical aspect in 3D human pose estimation, especially when dealing with video sequences. \[[10](http://arxiv.org/abs/1711.08585)\] introduced a sequence-to-sequence network with LSTM units to enforce temporal smoothness, significantly improving results on the Human3.6M dataset. \[[7](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/30de7fecb146356d7ae6cafa072997674601e0f5)\] further advanced this idea by proposing an attention-based framework that adaptively identifies significant frames and tensor outputs, leading to more accurate and temporally coherent 3D pose estimates. ### <a id='category-graph-based-models-for-human-pose-estimation'></a>Graph-Based Models for Human Pose Estimation: 8 papers found.
Graph-based models have been extensively explored for human pose estimation, particularly in addressing the challenges of depth ambiguity and self-occlusion in 3D pose estimation from 2D inputs \[[1](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/50167a9e6bdc1f64a58fe8a3c990105fec0bd2bc)\]. These models leverage the inherent structure of the human body, represented as graphs where joints are nodes and bones are edges, to capture spatial and temporal dependencies \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/1705.03098)\]. Early approaches utilized Graph Convolutional Networks (GCNs) to model relationships between body parts, but they were limited in capturing long-range dependencies due to their reliance on one-hop neighbors \[[7](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/30de7fecb146356d7ae6cafa072997674601e0f5)\]. To overcome this, researchers introduced high-order GCNs that aggregate features from nodes at various distances, enabling the modeling of complex interactions among body joints \[[7](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/30de7fecb146356d7ae6cafa072997674601e0f5)\]. Additionally, multi-scale architectures such as the Graph Stacked Hourglass Networks were proposed to learn both local and global features, improving performance on 3D human pose estimation tasks \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/1704.00159)\]. Furthermore, methods like the Modulated GCN introduced weight and affinity modulation to adaptively adjust feature transformations and graph structures, enhancing performance while maintaining model efficiency \[[4](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/db466d02ec9f7f47ee11590d0feeb6330256c98d)\]. The introduction of semantic graph convolutional networks (SemGCN) further advanced the field by capturing semantic relationships between nodes through end-to-end learning, achieving superior performance with fewer parameters \[[5](http://arxiv.org/abs/2103.16385)\]. Recent works have also focused on dynamic graph construction and hierarchical fusion layers to improve robustness and accuracy, particularly in handling challenging poses with occlusions and depth ambiguities \[[6](http://arxiv.org/abs/2103.10455)\]. These advancements collectively highlight the growing importance of graph-based models in human pose estimation, emphasizing their ability to incorporate domain knowledge and adapt to complex human motion patterns.
