## Related Works

Maximum Inner Product Search (MIPS) has been extensively studied in recent years due to its critical role in recommendation systems, information retrieval, and high-dimensional data applications \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/1909.13459)\]. Traditional approaches to MIPS either rely solely on inner product similarity, which can suffer from local optima and redundant computations \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/1909.13459)\], or reduce the problem to Euclidean metric-based nearest neighbor search through space projection, which may lead to topology distortion and information loss \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/1909.13459)\]. However, recent works have demonstrated that these paradigms are not mutually exclusive and that integrating inner product and Euclidean metrics can lead to significant performance improvements \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/1909.13459)\].

The inner-product navigable small world graph (ip-NSW) has been proposed as a state-of-the-art method for approximate MIPS, achieving an order of magnitude speedup over existing baselines \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/1405.5869)\]. This method leverages a strong norm bias in the MIPS problem, where large norm items are more likely to be the result of MIPS \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/1405.5869)\]. The ip-NSW+ algorithm further improves performance by introducing an angular proximity graph, which enhances search efficiency by focusing on angular neighbors before conducting inner-product-based search \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/1405.5869)\].

Asymmetric LSH (ALSH) was introduced as the first provably sublinear time algorithm for approximate MIPS, enabling efficient hashing for inner product similarity \[[3](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/25454f456c514219c9d6eeb42200ebbf378db9ca)\]. This approach extends the LSH framework to allow asymmetric transformations, converting inner products into approximate near neighbor search problems \[[3](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/25454f456c514219c9d6eeb42200ebbf378db9ca)\]. The proposed method has been evaluated on real-world datasets such as Netflix and Movielens, demonstrating its effectiveness in collaborative filtering tasks \[[3](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/25454f456c514219c9d6eeb42200ebbf378db9ca)\].

Norm-adjusted proximity graphs (NAPG) have also been proposed to improve MIPS performance by adjusting factors based on sampled data, leading to more meaningful connections in graph-based indexing \[[4](http://arxiv.org/abs/1610.03317)\]. This approach has been validated on a variety of datasets, showing strong empirical advantages over hash-based and quantization-based methods \[[4](http://arxiv.org/abs/1610.03317)\].

The greedy approach for budgeted MIPS, known as Greedy-MIPS, has been shown to outperform existing methods by allowing control over the trade-off between search efficiency and quality \[[5](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/f2171bc79fb72693f60c8e6b0dd547f7d4f850ac)\]. This method has been tested on large-scale datasets, achieving high precision with significant speedup \[[5](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/f2171bc79fb72693f60c8e6b0dd547f7d4f850ac)\].

In addition, the use of product quantization for nearest neighbor search has been explored, decomposing high-dimensional spaces into low-dimensional subspaces for efficient search \[[13](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/4748d22348e72e6e06c2476486afddbc76e5eca7)\]. This approach has been validated on large-scale datasets, demonstrating excellent search accuracy and scalability \[[13](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/4748d22348e72e6e06c2476486afddbc76e5eca7)\].
