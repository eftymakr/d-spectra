## Related Works

Multi-Domain Recommendation (MDR) has emerged as a promising approach to improve recommendation performance by leveraging knowledge transfer across different domains \[[15](http://arxiv.org/abs/2308.05508)\]. Traditional MDR methods often assume a uniform transfer mechanism, which may not be suitable for all scenarios due to varying domain characteristics and potential conflicts \[[15](http://arxiv.org/abs/2308.05508)\]. This limitation has led to the emergence of more sophisticated models that consider domain-specific features and dynamic transfer strategies \[[14](http://arxiv.org/abs/2211.11191)\].

Several approaches have been proposed to address the challenges of MDR. For instance, the work in \[[14](http://arxiv.org/abs/2211.11191)\] introduces a hierarchical hypergraph network to model multi-domain user-item interactions, enabling correlative preference transfer. This method captures both domain-specific and shared preferences by integrating high-order connections and dynamic item transfer mechanisms. Similarly, \[[15](http://arxiv.org/abs/2308.05508)\] proposes a method that disentangles domain-shared and domain-specific knowledge through embedding disentangling and domain alignment. This approach enhances knowledge transfer by aligning user and item representations across domains using graph-based random walks.

The issue of negative transfer, where unselective knowledge transfer leads to performance degradation, has also been explored. In \[[16](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/3ec388df2f38ebeeb4d184ec3e305a5aecd0d080)\], the authors propose an equivalent transformation learner (ETL) that models the joint distribution of user behaviors across domains, allowing for principled learning of domain-specific and shared features. This method avoids the suppression of domain-specific features that often occurs in shared-user representation approaches \[[16](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/3ec388df2f38ebeeb4d184ec3e305a5aecd0d080)\].

Cross-domain recommendation (CDR) has also contributed to the development of MDR techniques. For example, \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/1910.05189)\] introduces a dual learning mechanism to transfer information between domains iteratively, while \[[2](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/2b031c7170c9dccbe506f3d3a1d6104f65e835ae)\] proposes a framework that leverages multi-source content information and multi-task learning to improve performance on both rich and sparse domains. The work in \[[4](http://arxiv.org/abs/1804.06769)\] presents a deep transfer learning approach using neural networks to capture complex user-item interactions, while \[[5](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/d5630291a4ba3f6266c77e6d00920502f986afa6)\] introduces a bi-directional transfer method that exploits common users as bridges for knowledge transfer.

The challenge of data sparsity and the need for efficient transfer mechanisms have also been addressed. \[[10](http://arxiv.org/abs/1203.3535)\] proposes a probabilistic framework for multi-domain collaborative filtering that allows knowledge to be adaptively transferred across domains. \[[11](http://arxiv.org/abs/2203.16863)\] introduces a variational information bottleneck approach to capture domain-shared information for cold-start users, improving the quality of transferred representations. \[[12](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/fa990aee9a8f157b5d393f5f3eaa014e1e5c67aa)\] presents a parallel information-sharing network for shared-account cross-domain sequential recommendations, addressing the challenges of shared user behavior and cross-domain interactions.

The category "Cross-Domain Recommendation (CDR) Techniques" encompasses a range of approaches aimed at improving recommendation performance by leveraging knowledge across multiple domains. These techniques primarily address the data sparsity issue in recommender systems by transferring information from richer to sparser domains \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/1910.05189)\]. Various methods have been proposed to enhance this transfer, such as the Deep Dual Transfer Cross Domain Recommendation (DDTCDR) model, which utilizes dual learning and latent orthogonal mapping to preserve user-item relations across domains \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/1910.05189)\]. Similarly, the Dual-Target Cross-Domain Recommendation (DTCDR) framework employs multi-task learning to simultaneously improve performance on both richer and sparser domains by sharing embeddings of common users \[[2](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/2b031c7170c9dccbe506f3d3a1d6104f65e835ae)\]. The Preference Propagation GraphNet (PPGN) further advances this by constructing a Cross-Domain Preference Matrix (CDPM) to model interactions across domains and capturing high-order information propagation through its propagation layer \[[6](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/24a473700894dbfab0bfd9dc3d542acfeef66bca)\]. Additionally, the Multi-Sparse-Domain Collaborative Recommendation (MSDCR) model treats all domains as sparse and integrates both domain-specific and domain-invariant preferences to achieve comprehensive user understanding \[[7](http://arxiv.org/abs/2201.05973)\]. The GA framework also contributes by introducing a unified approach for dual-target, multi-target, and cross-system recommendation scenarios using graph embedding and attention techniques \[[9](http://arxiv.org/abs/2108.07976)\].
