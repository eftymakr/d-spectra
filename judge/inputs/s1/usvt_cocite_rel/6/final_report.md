## Related Works

Narrative summarization, particularly for long-form texts such as books, movies, and TV scripts, has been a challenging task due to the complexity of capturing intricate plotlines, character interactions, and thematic coherence \[[16](http://arxiv.org/abs/2105.08209)\]. Early approaches primarily relied on extractive methods, which often failed to capture the full semantic and structural nuances of such texts \[[13](http://arxiv.org/abs/1909.03186)\]. More recent work has explored abstractive summarization techniques, which allow for the generation of more coherent and concise summaries \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/1912.08777)\]. However, these methods typically require extensive fine-tuning on domain-specific data, which limits their applicability to new or diverse domains \[[10](http://arxiv.org/abs/1908.08345)\].

Several studies have investigated the use of pre-trained language models for summarization tasks. For example, PEGASUS \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/1912.08777)\] introduces a self-supervised pre-training objective tailored for abstractive summarization, achieving state-of-the-art results across multiple domains. Similarly, BART \[[20](http://arxiv.org/abs/1910.13461)\] employs a denoising autoencoder approach, which has shown effectiveness in both generation and comprehension tasks. These models, however, often struggle with long documents due to limitations in handling long-range dependencies \[[15](http://arxiv.org/abs/2004.05150)\]. The Longformer \[[15](http://arxiv.org/abs/2004.05150)\] addresses this by introducing an attention mechanism that scales linearly with sequence length, enabling the processing of very long texts. This is particularly relevant for narrative summarization, where document length can be extensive.

Another line of research focuses on improving the fluency and coherence of generated summaries. The pointer-generator network \[[5](http://arxiv.org/abs/1704.04368)\] introduces a hybrid architecture that allows for both copying and generating new content, reducing repetition and improving factual accuracy. Similarly, the reinforcement learning approach in \[[9](http://arxiv.org/abs/1705.04304)\] enhances the readability of summaries by combining standard word prediction with global sequence prediction. These methods, while effective, often require substantial fine-tuning and are not easily transferable to new domains.

The use of multi-agent systems for summarization has also been explored. MetaGPT \[[12](http://arxiv.org/abs/2308.00352)\] introduces a framework that incorporates human workflows into multi-agent collaboration, enabling structured coordination and reducing errors. This is particularly relevant to the current work, as it aligns with the idea of using multiple LLMs to process long-form texts in a structured manner. Additionally, the ReAct framework \[[19](http://arxiv.org/abs/2210.03629)\] synergizes reasoning and acting in language models, allowing for more interpretable and accurate task-solving. These approaches highlight the potential of multi-agent systems in enhancing the quality and reliability of generated summaries.
