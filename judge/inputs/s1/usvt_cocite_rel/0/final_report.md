## Related Works

The study of economic policy design has long been intertwined with the development of computational models capable of simulating complex human behaviors and optimizing policy outcomes. Traditional approaches to economic inequality reduction, such as the U.S. federal income tax, rely on static rules that lack adaptability to dynamic economic conditions \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/1605.06676)\]. While models like the Saez Optimal Taxation framework attempt to introduce dynamic adjustments, they often fail to account for the heterogeneity of taxpayers and irrational behaviors \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/1605.06676)\]. Recent advances in agent-based modeling (ABM) have provided a more flexible framework for simulating economic systems, enabling the exploration of policy interventions in a decentralized, heterogeneous environment \[[10](http://arxiv.org/abs/2006.05048)\]. 

The integration of large language models (LLMs) into policy design represents a novel approach to address these limitations. LLMs have demonstrated remarkable abilities to understand and generate natural language, making them suitable for modeling complex decision-making processes \[[13](http://arxiv.org/abs/2211.05100)\]. This capability is further enhanced by reinforcement learning (RL) techniques, which allow agents to learn optimal strategies through interaction with their environment \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/1605.06676)\]. ### <a id='category-multi-agent-reinforcement-learning'></a>Multi-Agent Reinforcement Learning: 9 papers found.
Multi-Agent Reinforcement Learning (MARL) has seen significant advancements in recent years, with a focus on developing methods that enable agents to learn effective communication protocols and coordination strategies in complex, partially observable environments \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/1605.06676)\]. Researchers have proposed various approaches, such as Reinforced Inter-Agent Learning (RIAL) and Differentiable Inter-Agent Learning (DIAL), which leverage deep neural networks to enable end-to-end learning of communication protocols \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/1605.06676)\]. Additionally, actor-critic methods have been adapted to address the challenges of multi-agent coordination, with innovations like the counterfactual multi-agent (COMA) policy gradients that use a centralised critic to estimate the Q-function and decentralised actors to optimise policies \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/1705.08926)\]. The importance of decentralised policies in real-world applications, such as network packet routing and autonomous vehicle coordination, has driven the development of methods that balance centralised training with decentralised execution \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/1705.08926)\]. Furthermore, the challenge of learning in environments with non-stationary dynamics and the need for robust policies have led to the introduction of new training regimens and metrics, such as joint-policy correlation, to evaluate and improve multi-agent learning \[[4](http://arxiv.org/abs/2302.04761)\]. These developments highlight the ongoing efforts to create scalable and efficient MARL methods that can handle the complexities of real-world multi-agent systems.

### <a id='category-reinforcement-learning-for-economic-policy'></a>Reinforcement Learning for Economic Policy: 3 papers found.
Agent-based models (ABMs) have been increasingly utilized in policy analysis to simulate complex socio-economic systems and evaluate the impact of various interventions \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/1605.06676)\]. These models allow researchers to explore emergent behaviors and outcomes that arise from interactions among heterogeneous agents, making them particularly useful for studying economic policies \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/1605.06676)\]. However, the effectiveness of ABMs hinges on the accuracy of the behavioral models used to represent agents, which are often based on heuristic rules or historical data \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/1605.06676)\]. Recent advancements have introduced reinforcement learning (RL) as a more adaptive and behaviorally valid alternative, enabling agents to learn optimal strategies through interaction with their environment \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/1605.06676)\]. RL models have shown promise in improving the performance of ABMs, particularly in scenarios involving strategic behavior and dynamic policy adjustments \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/1605.06676)\]. Additionally, frameworks like the AI Economist leverage two-level RL and data-driven simulations to optimize real-world policies, demonstrating the potential of AI to enhance policy design and social welfare \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/1706.02275)\]. These developments underscore the growing importance of integrating machine learning techniques into economic modeling to address the complexities of modern policy challenges.
