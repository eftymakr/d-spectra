## Related Works

The study of economic policy design has long been intertwined with the development of computational models capable of simulating complex human behaviors and optimizing policy outcomes. Traditional approaches to economic inequality reduction, such as the U.S. federal income tax, rely on static rules that lack adaptability to dynamic economic conditions \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/1605.06676)\]. While models like the Saez Optimal Taxation framework attempt to introduce dynamic adjustments, they often fail to account for the heterogeneity of taxpayers and irrational behaviors \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/1605.06676)\]. Recent advances in agent-based modeling (ABM) have provided a more flexible framework for simulating economic systems, enabling the exploration of policy interventions in a decentralized, heterogeneous environment \[[10](http://arxiv.org/abs/2006.05048)\]. 

The integration of large language models (LLMs) into policy design represents a novel approach to address these limitations. LLMs have demonstrated remarkable abilities to understand and generate natural language, making them suitable for modeling complex decision-making processes \[[13](http://arxiv.org/abs/2211.05100)\]. This capability is further enhanced by reinforcement learning (RL) techniques, which allow agents to learn optimal strategies through interaction with their environment \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/1605.06676)\]. In this context, the TaxAgent framework leverages LLMs to iteratively optimize tax policies, balancing equity and productivity \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/1605.06676)\]. This approach is grounded in the broader field of multi-agent reinforcement learning (MARL), which has been extensively studied for its ability to model cooperative and competitive interactions among agents \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/1605.06676)\].

Several studies have explored the use of MARL in economic and social systems. For instance, the work by \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/1605.06676)\] introduces a framework for multi-agent reinforcement learning that enables agents to learn optimal policies in cooperative settings. Similarly, \[[17](http://arxiv.org/abs/1811.07029)\] presents ATT-MADDPG, an extension of the Deep Deterministic Policy Gradient (DDPG) algorithm that incorporates attention mechanisms to model the dynamic joint policies of teammates. These methods are relevant to the TaxAgent framework, as they demonstrate the potential of RL in modeling complex economic interactions \[[17](http://arxiv.org/abs/1811.07029)\]. 

In addition to MARL, the use of agent-based models for policy evaluation has been extensively explored. The AI Economist framework, for example, uses two-level reinforcement learning to optimize economic policies, demonstrating the potential of RL in addressing complex policy design challenges \[[11](http://arxiv.org/abs/2108.02904)\]. This aligns with the TaxAgent framework, which employs ABM to simulate heterogeneous taxpayer behaviors and optimize tax policies \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/1605.06676)\].
