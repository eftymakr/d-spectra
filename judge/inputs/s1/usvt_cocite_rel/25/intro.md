## Related Works

The integration of knowledge graphs (KGs) into large language models (LLMs) for enhancing reasoning capabilities in claim verification has gained significant attention in recent research. While KGs offer structured and semantically rich representations suitable for reasoning, most existing verification methods rely on unstructured text corpora, limiting their ability to effectively leverage KGs \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2005.11401)\]. This limitation is particularly evident in tasks requiring multi-step reasoning and modular pipelines, where LLMs often struggle without adaptation \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/2305.12744)\]. 

Several approaches have explored the use of KGs in conjunction with LLMs. For instance, the work by \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/1905.07129)\] introduces a retrieval-augmented generation (RAG) framework that combines pre-trained sequence-to-sequence models with dense vector indexes of Wikipedia, enabling more accurate and factually grounded language generation. Similarly, \[[4](http://arxiv.org/abs/2205.11916)\] proposes a model that integrates knowledge graphs into language representation, demonstrating improved performance on knowledge-driven tasks. However, these methods often focus on extractive tasks rather than complex reasoning pipelines \[[5](http://arxiv.org/abs/2312.10997)\].

In the realm of fact-checking, \[[6](http://arxiv.org/abs/1911.06136)\] presents a novel model called Program-Guided Fact-Checking (ProgramFC), which decomposes complex claims into sub-tasks and executes them using a shared library of specialized functions. This approach enhances explainability and data efficiency, making it a promising direction for structured knowledge integration. Additionally, \[[7](http://arxiv.org/abs/2004.14974)\] explores the use of knowledge graphs in the context of scientific claim verification, proposing a system that leverages T5 for abstract retrieval and label prediction. These models demonstrate the potential of KGs in improving the accuracy of claim verification tasks.

Another line of research focuses on the zero-shot reasoning capabilities of LLMs. \[[8](http://arxiv.org/abs/1909.07606)\] shows that by simply adding a "Let's think step by step" prompt, LLMs can perform complex multi-step reasoning without any few-shot examples. This finding highlights the inherent reasoning abilities of LLMs and suggests that integrating structured knowledge from KGs could further enhance these capabilities. However, the lack of access to external knowledge bases often leads to hallucinations, as noted in \[[9](http://arxiv.org/abs/2212.10511)\].

To address these challenges, recent works have explored retrieval-augmented models that combine the strengths of LLMs with external knowledge sources. \[[10](http://arxiv.org/abs/2109.11427)\] introduces REALM, a retrieval-augmented language model pre-training approach that allows models to retrieve and attend over documents during pre-training, fine-tuning, and inference. This method significantly improves performance on open-domain question answering tasks.
