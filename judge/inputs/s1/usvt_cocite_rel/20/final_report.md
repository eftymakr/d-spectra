## Related Works

Deep-unrolling and Plug-and-Play (PnP) approaches have emerged as dominant methods for solving single-pixel imaging (SPI) inverse problems \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2201.13256)\]. PnP methods are iterative algorithms where regularization is implicitly performed by an off-the-shelf deep denoiser \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/1704.03488)\]. These methods are flexible for varying compression ratios (CRs) but are limited in reconstruction accuracy and speed \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/2012.13247)\]. Conversely, unrolling approaches, which transform a truncated iterative optimization process into an end-to-end trainable network, typically achieve better accuracy with faster inference \[[4](http://arxiv.org/abs/1809.04693)\]. However, they require fine-tuning or retraining when CR changes \[[5](http://arxiv.org/abs/2008.13751)\].

Early work on PnP methods demonstrated that replacing proximal operators with denoisers could lead to state-of-the-art visual performance for image restoration \[[6](http://arxiv.org/abs/1704.03264)\]. This approach has been extended to incorporate deep learning-based denoisers, which serve as implicit image priors \[[7](http://arxiv.org/abs/2110.03220)\]. However, convergence guarantees for PnP methods have been limited, often requiring nonexpansive denoisers or strong convexity assumptions \[[8](http://arxiv.org/abs/1611.02862)\]. Recent advances have proposed training denoisers as gradient descent steps on a functional parameterized by a deep neural network, ensuring convergence of PnP algorithms \[[9](http://arxiv.org/abs/1905.05406)\]. This approach has been applied to deblurring, super-resolution, and inpainting tasks, showing promising results \[[10](http://arxiv.org/abs/2006.03224)\].

Unrolling methods, on the other hand, have been explored for image restoration by integrating deep neural networks with iterative optimization algorithms \[[11](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/257a1d17a22753dfeb368b91a8e4d30a37651104)\]. These methods, such as the deep unfolding network, leverage both learning-based and model-based approaches to handle different scale factors, blur kernels, and noise levels \[[12](http://arxiv.org/abs/2011.02281)\]. The half-quadratic splitting (HQS) algorithm has been used to unfold the MAP inference process, resulting in an end-to-end trainable network \[[13](http://arxiv.org/abs/1508.02848)\]. This approach has been shown to inherit the flexibility of model-based methods while maintaining the advantages of learning-based methods \[[14](http://arxiv.org/abs/1806.02296)\].

Recent work has focused on improving the convergence of PnP methods by ensuring that the denoiser corresponds to a proximal operator \[[15](http://arxiv.org/abs/1605.01710)\]. This has led to the development of convergent PnP algorithms that can handle a wide range of regularization parameters \[[16](http://arxiv.org/abs/2002.09611)\]. Additionally, the use of relaxed proximal gradient descent algorithms has been proposed to improve the flexibility and accuracy of PnP methods \[[17](http://arxiv.org/abs/1512.07331)\]. These algorithms allow for more accurate image restoration by converging across a broader range of regularization parameters \[[18](http://arxiv.org/abs/2003.10428)\].

### <a id='category-integration-of-deep-learning-and-optimization'></a>Integration of Deep Learning and Optimization: 13 papers found.
The integration of deep learning with optimization techniques has seen significant advancements, particularly in the context of plug-and-play (PnP) and deep-unrolling methods for solving inverse problems in imaging. PnP approaches leverage off-the-shelf deep denoisers as implicit regularizers within iterative algorithms, offering flexibility across varying compression ratios but often at the cost of reconstruction accuracy and speed \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/2012.13247)\]. In contrast, unrolling methods transform truncated iterative optimization processes into end-to-end trainable neural networks, typically achieving better accuracy and faster inference but requiring retraining when conditions change \[[8](http://arxiv.org/abs/1611.02862)\]. Recent works have aimed to combine the strengths of both paradigms, such as by developing efficient deep image restorers that approximate proximal operators of ideal regularizers \[[13](http://arxiv.org/abs/1508.02848)\]. Additionally, efforts have been made to train fast and effective convolutional neural networks (CNNs) as denoisers, which can be integrated into model-based optimization methods to enhance performance across various low-level vision tasks \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/1704.03488)\]. Theoretical foundations have also been established, including the development of nonexpansive CNNs and the analysis of convergence properties for PnP algorithms \[[4](http://arxiv.org/abs/1809.04693)\]\[[10](http://arxiv.org/abs/2006.03224)\]. Furthermore, the use of policy networks for automatic parameter tuning has been proposed to eliminate the need for manual adjustments, improving the adaptability and efficiency of PnP frameworks \[[7](http://arxiv.org/abs/2110.03220)\]. These contributions collectively highlight the ongoing efforts to bridge the gap between traditional optimization methods and deep learning, aiming for more flexible, accurate, and scalable solutions to inverse problems in imaging \[[12](http://arxiv.org/abs/2011.02281)\].
