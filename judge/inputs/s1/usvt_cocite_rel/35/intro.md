## Related Works

Protein structure prediction and conformation sampling have seen significant advancements in recent years, driven by the integration of deep learning and physical modeling. Traditional methods such as molecular dynamics (MD) simulations remain computationally expensive, often requiring extensive computational resources to explore the conformational space of proteins \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2304.02198)\]. Recent work has focused on leveraging generative models, particularly denoising diffusion models, to efficiently sample protein structures and conformational ensembles \[[2](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/2eb1ed9d4baf548391d8220c0a8a3d0be79845ed)\]. These models learn distributions over crystallographic structures, enabling the generation of high-quality protein structures without the need for exhaustive simulations \[[3](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/21a4afa74caf78e28880b7fe4c30a09a5f9c14e3)\].

Several approaches have been proposed to improve the accuracy and efficiency of protein structure prediction. AlphaFold \[[4](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/dc32a984b651256a8ec282be52310e6bd33d9815)\] has demonstrated remarkable success in predicting protein structures with atomic accuracy, even in cases where no homologous structures are available. Similarly, AlphaFold 3 \[[5](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/c49a0912595a1cc70aab63524f64ed08c92194a8)\] extends this capability to predict the joint structure of protein complexes, including nucleic acids, small molecules, and modified residues. These models incorporate physical and biological knowledge into their architectures, enhancing their predictive power \[[6](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/08a05b9e8548354e24542c2b5f68b1432b50b27d)\].

Diffusion-based generative models have also been applied to protein conformation generation. EigenFold \[[7](http://arxiv.org/abs/2306.03117)\] introduces a diffusion process that models protein structures as harmonic oscillators, enabling the sampling of conformational ensembles with high accuracy. Str2Str \[[8](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/2cfc26b4af99f195b433fa7aa00f221b111c7cd4)\] proposes a structure-to-structure translation framework that enables zero-shot conformation sampling, leveraging an amortized denoising score matching objective trained on general crystal structures. This approach avoids the need for simulation data during training and inference, making it highly efficient \[[9](http://arxiv.org/abs/2209.15611)\].

The integration of physical supervision into generative models has been a key challenge. Boltzmann generators \[[10](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/f374055a0b388d07702abd4277a5010c3932c45c)\] combine deep learning and statistical mechanics to sample equilibrium states of many-body systems, learning invertible transformations between complex configurations and simpler latent space representations. This approach enables the generation of unbiased equilibrium samples and the computation of free-energy differences between metastable states \[[11](http://arxiv.org/abs/2302.02277)\].

Recent work has also focused on improving the physical plausibility of generated structures. ConfDiff \[[12](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/7572ba7f604ef95d7acdd657ebac458106bd35df)\] introduces a force-guided SE(3) diffusion model that incorporates physical prior knowledge to guide the generation process, resulting in more accurate and diverse protein conformations. This model outperforms existing methods in conformation prediction tasks, demonstrating the importance of incorporating physical guidance into generative models \[[13](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/18a93dc1558bf9d7534d0b416633cebaf75c1145)\].
