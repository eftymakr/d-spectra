## Related Works

Deep sequential recommendation models have seen significant advancements, driven by the need to capture complex user behavior patterns and efficiently handle variable-length sequences. Traditional approaches such as Markov Chains (MCs) and Recurrent Neural Networks (RNNs) have been widely used, but they face limitations in modeling long-range dependencies and capturing diverse preferences \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/1808.09781)\]. MCs assume that a user's next action depends only on their last few actions, while RNNs, though capable of modeling longer-term semantics, suffer from computational inefficiency and difficulty in capturing diverse interactions \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/1808.09781)\]. 

To address these limitations, attention-based models such as SASRec and BERT4Rec have been proposed. SASRec introduces a self-attention mechanism that allows for capturing long-term semantics while making predictions based on a subset of relevant actions \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/1808.09781)\]. BERT4Rec extends this idea by employing a bidirectional self-attention mechanism, enabling the model to consider both left and right context for each item in the sequence, thus improving the representation of user behavior \[[4](http://arxiv.org/abs/1904.06690)\]. However, these attention-based models often suffer from quadratic computational complexity, which limits their scalability for long sequences \[[8](http://arxiv.org/abs/2403.03900)\].

To improve efficiency, models like Mamba4Rec and SSD4Rec have been developed, leveraging state space models (SSMs) with selective mechanisms. Mamba4Rec introduces a selective SSM that enables efficient and effective modeling of long sequences, achieving linear complexity while maintaining high performance \[[8](http://arxiv.org/abs/2403.03900)\]. SSD4Rec builds upon Mamba by introducing a structured state space duality model, allowing for efficient and accurate modeling of long behavioral sequences \[[10](http://arxiv.org/abs/2409.01192)\]. These models demonstrate that SSMs can effectively capture temporal dynamics and provide efficient inference, making them suitable for real-time recommendation systems \[[10](http://arxiv.org/abs/2409.01192)\].

Additionally, models such as Caser and LinRec have been proposed to address specific challenges in sequential recommendation. Caser uses convolutional filters to capture local features in item sequences, providing a flexible and unified framework for modeling sequential patterns \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/1809.07426)\]. LinRec introduces a linear attention mechanism that reduces computational complexity while preserving the learning capabilities of traditional attention mechanisms \[[5](http://arxiv.org/abs/2411.01537)\]. These models highlight the ongoing efforts to balance efficiency and effectiveness in sequential recommendation.

In summary, recent works have focused on improving the modeling of user behavior by leveraging attention mechanisms, state space models, and hybrid approaches. These advancements have led to more accurate and efficient sequential recommendation systems, addressing key challenges in capturing diverse preferences and handling variable-length sequences \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/1808.09781)\]\[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/1809.07426)\]\[[4](http://arxiv.org/abs/1904.06690)\]\[[5](http://arxiv.org/abs/2411.01537)\]\[[8](http://arxiv.org/abs/2403.03900)\]\[[10](http://arxiv.org/abs/2409.01192)\].

### <a id='category-attention-based-sequential-recommendation-models'></a>Attention-Based Sequential Recommendation Models: 4 papers found.
Attention-based sequential recommendation models have gained significant traction in recent years for their ability to capture complex user behavior patterns. These models leverage attention mechanisms to prioritize relevant items in a user's action history, enabling more accurate predictions of next-item choices \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/1808.09781)\]. For instance, SASRec introduces a self-attention based model that combines the strengths of Markov Chains and RNNs by using attention to focus on relevant actions while capturing long-term semantics \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/1808.09781)\]. Similarly, NARM extends this idea by incorporating a hybrid encoder with attention to model both sequential behavior and the user's main purpose within a session \[[12](http://arxiv.org/abs/1711.04725)\]. These models address the limitations of traditional approaches by adaptively handling varying session lengths and capturing both frequent and infrequent item interactions \[[14](http://arxiv.org/abs/2304.09184)\]. Furthermore, models like MLP4Rec have explored alternative architectures, such as MLP-based designs, to achieve linear computational complexity and reduce model parameters while maintaining sensitivity to item order \[[22](http://arxiv.org/abs/2204.11510)\]. Overall, these attention-based models represent a critical advancement in sequential recommendation, offering improved performance and flexibility across diverse datasets.

### <a id='category-theoretical-foundations-and-unified-frameworks'></a>Theoretical Foundations and Unified Frameworks: 3 papers found.
Theoretical Foundations and Unified Frameworks section highlights the importance of modeling sequential user-item interactions and capturing both general preferences and sequential patterns. Existing methods often decompose higher-order interactions into pairwise relationships, separately capturing user preferences and sequential patterns \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/1809.07426)\]. To address these limitations, unified approaches like TransRec have been proposed, which model third-order interactions by embedding items into a 'transition space' where users are represented as translation vectors \[[17](http://arxiv.org/abs/1707.02410)\]. Similarly, the Caser model employs convolutional filters to learn sequential patterns from item sequences, embedding them into an image in the time and latent spaces \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/1808.09781)\]. These approaches aim to provide a more holistic understanding of user behavior by integrating both preference and sequential aspects. Additionally, the Linear State-Space Layer (LSSL) offers a generalization of recurrent neural networks, temporal convolutions, and neural differential equations, enabling efficient modeling of time-series data with continuous-time dynamics \[[18](http://arxiv.org/abs/2110.13985)\]. These foundational works underpin the development of more advanced models like STAR-Rec, which integrates preference-aware attention and state-space modeling to address challenges in sequence length variations and diverse interaction patterns.

### <a id='category-state-space-model-(ssm)-based-approaches'></a>State Space Model (SSM) Based Approaches: 6 papers found.
State space model (SSM) based approaches have emerged as a promising direction for sequential recommendation, addressing the limitations of traditional attention-based models in terms of computational efficiency and long-range dependency modeling \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/2406.02638)\]. These models draw inspiration from control theory, where SSMs are used to manage dynamic systems by capturing temporal dependencies through structured state matrices \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/2406.02638)\]. Recent works such as EchoMamba4Rec \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/2406.02638)\], RecMamba \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/1809.07426)\], and Mamba4Rec \[[8](http://arxiv.org/abs/2403.03900)\] have demonstrated the effectiveness of SSMs in sequential recommendation, particularly in handling long user behavior sequences with linear complexity. For instance, Mamba4Rec introduces a selective SSM with an efficient hardware-aware parallel algorithm, enabling both high inference efficiency and model performance \[[8](http://arxiv.org/abs/2403.03900)\]. Similarly, RecMamba leverages the Mamba block to model lifelong user sequences, achieving significant improvements in training efficiency and memory usage \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/1809.07426)\]. Additionally, the Mamba architecture, as explored in foundational studies \[[4](http://arxiv.org/abs/1904.06690)\], has shown superior performance in sequence modeling across various modalities, including language and audio, with linear scaling in sequence length and faster inference speeds compared to Transformers \[[4](http://arxiv.org/abs/1904.06690)\]. These advancements highlight the growing interest in SSMs for sequential recommendation, offering a balance between effectiveness and computational efficiency \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/2406.02638)\]\[[8](http://arxiv.org/abs/2403.03900)\]\[[4](http://arxiv.org/abs/1904.06690)\].

### <a id='category-user-behavior-modeling-and-preference-capture'></a>User Behavior Modeling and Preference Capture: 4 papers found.
The category "User Behavior Modeling and Preference Capture" focuses on the challenges and advancements in modeling sequential user behaviors for recommendation systems. Existing methods often rely on sequential neural networks to encode historical interactions, but these unidirectional models are limited in their ability to capture complex user preferences due to rigid sequence assumptions and restricted hidden representations \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/1808.09781)\]. To address these limitations, researchers have proposed bidirectional models like BERT4Rec, which use self-attention mechanisms to fuse information from both left and right contexts in user behavior sequences, improving recommendation accuracy \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/1809.07426)\]. Additionally, graph neural networks such as SURGE have been introduced to handle noisy and implicit user behaviors by constructing interest graphs that cluster core preferences and dynamically extract activated interests \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/2406.02638)\]. Furthermore, signal processing techniques have been integrated into deep recommendation models to mitigate noise in logged data, with approaches like FMLP-Rec demonstrating effectiveness through learnable filters that adaptively attenuate noise in the frequency domain \[[4](http://arxiv.org/abs/1904.06690)\]. These methods collectively highlight the evolving strategies to better model user preferences and behaviors in sequential recommendation systems.

Similarly, TiSASRec explicitly models time intervals between interactions, enhancing next-item prediction by incorporating both absolute item positions and temporal dynamics \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/1809.07426)\]. These approaches highlight the growing emphasis on efficient and temporally aware mechanisms in sequential recommendation systems.

### <a id='category-efficiency-and-scalability-in-sequential-recommendation'></a>Efficiency and Scalability in Sequential Recommendation: 1 papers found.
Efficiency and Scalability in Sequential Recommendation has been a focal point in recent advancements, particularly due to the computational challenges posed by self-attention-based models in real-time applications \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/1808.09781)\]. These models, while effective in capturing complex sequential patterns, suffer from high computational costs and lack of incremental inference capabilities \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/1808.09781)\]. To address these limitations, researchers have explored alternative architectures that offer faster inference and more efficient training. One such approach is the Linear Recurrent Units for Sequential Recommendation (LRURec), which draws inspiration from efficient language modeling techniques \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/1808.09781)\]. LRURec introduces a linear recurrent structure that enables rapid inference and supports incremental processing of sequential inputs, making it suitable for real-time recommendation systems \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/1808.09781)\]. Additionally, the model incorporates recursive parallelization strategies to reduce model size and enhance training efficiency \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/1808.09781)\]. These innovations are crucial for improving the scalability of sequential recommendation systems, especially when dealing with long sequences and large-scale datasets. The effectiveness of such approaches is further validated through extensive experiments on real-world datasets, demonstrating significant improvements over existing methods \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/1808.09781)\].
