## Related Works

Anomaly detection in industrial settings has garnered significant attention due to its critical role in quality control and fault prevention in modern manufacturing systems. A wide range of approaches have been proposed, from traditional statistical methods to deep learning-based solutions. Early methods often relied on handcrafted features and statistical models, but these were limited in their ability to generalize to complex, real-world scenarios \[[15](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/e26851c89afcfb1a0fe00292590c9f6e830c4ec0)\]. With the advent of deep learning, researchers have focused on leveraging neural networks to detect anomalies in images and time-series data \[[11](http://arxiv.org/abs/1901.04997)\].

In the field of image-based anomaly detection, several methods have been introduced to address the challenges of unsupervised learning, where only normal data is available. For instance, the MVTec-AD dataset has been widely used as a benchmark for evaluating anomaly detection models, particularly those that operate in an unsupervised setting \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2112.09045)\]. This dataset includes various types of defects such as scratches, dents, and holes, and provides precise ground-truth annotations for evaluation \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2112.09045)\]. The MVTec 3D-AD dataset extends this work to 3D point cloud data, enabling more comprehensive anomaly detection in industrial inspection \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2112.09045)\]. 

Several deep learning models have been proposed to improve detection accuracy and efficiency. PaDiM, for example, uses a patch distribution modeling framework to detect and localize anomalies in images, achieving state-of-the-art results on the MVTec AD and STC datasets \[[7](http://arxiv.org/abs/2011.08785)\]. Similarly, DRÆM introduces a discriminatively trained reconstruction embedding model that enables direct anomaly localization without the need for additional post-processing \[[8](http://arxiv.org/abs/2108.07610)\]. These methods rely on deep neural networks trained on normal data to reconstruct or classify anomalies based on deviations from learned normal patterns.

For real-time applications, models such as CFLOW-AD and FastFlow have been developed to reduce computational complexity while maintaining high detection accuracy \[[9](http://arxiv.org/abs/2107.12571)\]\[[12](http://arxiv.org/abs/2111.07677)\]. CFLOW-AD, based on conditional normalizing flows, achieves faster inference times and better localization performance compared to prior methods. FastFlow, implemented with 2D normalizing flows, offers a flexible plug-in module that can be integrated with various backbone networks, enabling efficient anomaly detection and localization \[[12](http://arxiv.org/abs/2111.07677)\].

In the realm of time-series data, methods such as MAD-GAN and OmniAnomaly have been proposed to address the challenges of multivariate anomaly detection. MAD-GAN leverages generative adversarial networks to model the joint distribution of sensor data, while OmniAnomaly uses stochastic recurrent neural networks to capture complex temporal dependencies and provide interpretable anomaly explanations \[[11](http://arxiv.org/abs/1901.04997)\][1

### <a id='category-deep-learning-approaches-for-image-based-anomaly-detection'></a>Deep Learning Approaches for Image-Based Anomaly Detection: 9 papers found.
In the realm of image-based anomaly detection, recent advancements have focused on improving performance while reducing computational demands to enable deployment on resource-constrained devices \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2112.09045)\]. Several approaches have emerged that leverage deep learning techniques to address the cold-start problem, where models are trained using only nominal (non-defective) examples \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2112.09045)\]. For instance, PatchCore utilizes a maximally representative memory bank of nominal patch-features to achieve state-of-the-art performance on the MVTec AD benchmark with an AUROC score of up to 99.6% \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2112.09045)\]. Similarly, PaDiM introduces a framework for patch distribution modeling, combining pretrained CNNs with multivariate Gaussian distributions to detect and localize anomalies in a one-class learning setting \[[4](http://arxiv.org/abs/2301.11514)\]. The method also exploits correlations between semantic levels of CNNs for better localization accuracy \[[4](http://arxiv.org/abs/2301.11514)\]. Another approach, FastFlow, employs 2D normalizing flows to estimate probability distributions, enabling efficient anomaly detection and localization with various backbone networks \[[6](http://arxiv.org/abs/2210.07829)\]. This method achieves a high AUROC of 99.4% on the MVTec AD dataset \[[6](http://arxiv.org/abs/2210.07829)\]. Additionally, methods like DRÆM cast surface anomaly detection as a discriminative problem, learning a joint representation of an anomalous image and its reconstruction while establishing a decision boundary between normal and anomalous examples \[[5](http://arxiv.org/abs/2303.00601)\]. These techniques collectively highlight the ongoing efforts to enhance detection accuracy and efficiency, particularly for industrial applications where real-time performance is critical \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2112.09045)\]\[[4](http://arxiv.org/abs/2301.11514)\]\[[5](http://arxiv.org/abs/2303.00601)\]\[[6](http://arxiv.org/abs/2210.07829)\].

[Snippet]: «Mechanical devices such as engines, vehicles, aircrafts, etc., are typically instrumented with numerous sensors to capture the behavior and health of the machine. However, there are often external factors or variables which are not captured by sensors leading to time-series which are inherently unpredictable. Detecting anomalies in such scenarios becomes challenging using standard approaches based on mathematical models that rely on stationarity, or prediction models that utilize prediction errors to detect anomalies. We propose a Long Short Term Memory Networks based Encoder-Decoder scheme for Anomaly Detection (EncDec-AD) that learns to reconstruct 'normal' time-series behavior, and thereafter uses reconstruction error to detect anomalies. We experiment with three publicly available quasi predictable time-series datasets: power demand, space shuttle, and ECG, and two real-world engine datasets with both predictive and unpredictable behavior. We show that EncDec-AD is robust and can detect anomalies from predictable, unpredictable, periodic, aperiodic, and quasi-periodic time-series. Further, we show that EncDec-AD is able to detect anomalies from short time-series (length as small as 30) as well as long time-series (length as large as 500).»

[Snippet]: «The prevalence of networked sensors and actuators in many real-world systems such as smart buildings, factories, power plants, and data centers generate substantial amounts of multivariate time series data for these systems. The rich sensor data can be continuously monitored for intrusion events through anomaly detection. However, conventional threshold-based anomaly detection methods are inadequate due to the dynamic complexities of these systems, while supervised machine learning methods are unable to exploit the large amounts of data due to the lack of labeled data. On the other hand, current unsupervised machine learning approaches have not fully exploited the spatial-temporal correlation and other dependencies amongst the multiple variables (sensors/actuators) in the system for detecting anomalies. In this work, we propose an unsupervised multivariate anomaly detection method based on Generative Adversarial Networks (GANs). Instead of treating each data stream independently, our proposed MAD-GAN framework considers the entire variable set concurrently to capture the latent interactions amongst the variables. We also fully exploit both the generator and discriminator produced by the GAN, using a novel anomaly score called DR-score to detect anomalies by discrimination and reconstruction. We have tested our proposed MAD-GAN using two recent datasets collected from real-world CPS: the Secure Water Treatment (SWaT) and the Water Distribution (WADI) datasets. Our experimental results showed that the proposed MAD-GAN is effective in reporting anomalies caused by various cyber-intrusions compared in these complex real-world systems.»
