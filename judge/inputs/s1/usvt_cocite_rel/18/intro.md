## Related Works

Anomaly detection in industrial settings has garnered significant attention due to its critical role in quality control and fault prevention in modern manufacturing systems. A wide range of approaches have been proposed, from traditional statistical methods to deep learning-based solutions. Early methods often relied on handcrafted features and statistical models, but these were limited in their ability to generalize to complex, real-world scenarios \[[15](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/e26851c89afcfb1a0fe00292590c9f6e830c4ec0)\]. With the advent of deep learning, researchers have focused on leveraging neural networks to detect anomalies in images and time-series data \[[11](http://arxiv.org/abs/1901.04997)\].

In the field of image-based anomaly detection, several methods have been introduced to address the challenges of unsupervised learning, where only normal data is available. For instance, the MVTec-AD dataset has been widely used as a benchmark for evaluating anomaly detection models, particularly those that operate in an unsupervised setting \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2112.09045)\]. This dataset includes various types of defects such as scratches, dents, and holes, and provides precise ground-truth annotations for evaluation \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2112.09045)\]. The MVTec 3D-AD dataset extends this work to 3D point cloud data, enabling more comprehensive anomaly detection in industrial inspection \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2112.09045)\]. 

Several deep learning models have been proposed to improve detection accuracy and efficiency. PaDiM, for example, uses a patch distribution modeling framework to detect and localize anomalies in images, achieving state-of-the-art results on the MVTec AD and STC datasets \[[7](http://arxiv.org/abs/2011.08785)\]. Similarly, DRÆM introduces a discriminatively trained reconstruction embedding model that enables direct anomaly localization without the need for additional post-processing \[[8](http://arxiv.org/abs/2108.07610)\]. These methods rely on deep neural networks trained on normal data to reconstruct or classify anomalies based on deviations from learned normal patterns.

For real-time applications, models such as CFLOW-AD and FastFlow have been developed to reduce computational complexity while maintaining high detection accuracy \[[9](http://arxiv.org/abs/2107.12571)\]\[[12](http://arxiv.org/abs/2111.07677)\]. CFLOW-AD, based on conditional normalizing flows, achieves faster inference times and better localization performance compared to prior methods. FastFlow, implemented with 2D normalizing flows, offers a flexible plug-in module that can be integrated with various backbone networks, enabling efficient anomaly detection and localization \[[12](http://arxiv.org/abs/2111.07677)\].

In the realm of time-series data, methods such as MAD-GAN and OmniAnomaly have been proposed to address the challenges of multivariate anomaly detection.
