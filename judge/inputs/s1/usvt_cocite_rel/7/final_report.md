## Related Works

Recent advancements in large language models (LLMs) have focused on understanding and controlling the internal activation spaces to steer model behavior. Prior work on controllable text generation has primarily relied on trainable decoding, smart-prompt design, or fine-tuning based on a desired objective \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2205.05124)\]. However, these methods often require explicit training or assumptions about the model's behavior, such as the presence of specific refusal tokens \[[15](http://arxiv.org/abs/2406.11717)\]. In contrast, our work introduces COSMIC, an automated framework for direction selection that identifies viable steering directions and target layers using cosine similarity, entirely independent of model outputs \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2205.05124)\].

The concept of steering LLMs through latent spaces has been explored in several studies. For example, \[[6](http://arxiv.org/abs/2312.06681)\] introduced Contrastive Activation Addition (CAA), which computes steering vectors by averaging the difference in residual stream activations between positive and negative examples of a behavior. This method allows precise control over targeted behaviors during inference. Similarly, \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2205.05124)\] proposed extracting latent steering vectors directly from pretrained language model decoders without fine-tuning, demonstrating that these vectors can generate target sentences with high accuracy (> 99 BLEU) for English sentences. These approaches, however, often require manual intervention or extensive training data.

In the realm of multimodal models, \[[14](http://arxiv.org/abs/2304.08485)\] introduced LLaVA, an end-to-end trained large multimodal model that connects a vision encoder and LLM for general-purpose visual and language understanding. The model was trained using instruction-tuned data generated by GPT-4, enabling it to perform well on visual question-answering and captioning tasks. Similarly, \[[11](http://arxiv.org/abs/2306.02858)\] proposed Video-LLaMA, which integrates visual and auditory information to enable video understanding by addressing the challenges of temporal changes in visual scenes and integrating audio-visual signals. These models demonstrate the potential of multimodal instruction tuning to enhance model capabilities across different modalities.

Another line of research focuses on improving the truthfulness of LLMs through inference-time interventions. \[[5](http://arxiv.org/abs/2306.03341)\] introduced Inference-Time Intervention (ITI), a technique that shifts model activations during inference to improve truthfulness on benchmarks like TruthfulQA. ITI is minimally invasive and computationally efficient, requiring only a few hundred examples to locate truthful directions. This approach aligns with our goal of steering models without relying on extensive training data or predefined templates.

### <a id='category-inference-time-intervention-techniques'></a>Inference-Time Intervention Techniques: 3 papers found.
Inference-Time Intervention Techniques focus on modifying large language models (LLMs) during inference to steer their behavior without requiring extensive retraining. These methods operate by altering model activations, either through shifting attention heads \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2205.05124)\] or adding steering vectors derived from activation differences between desired and undesired behaviors \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/2310.03744)\]. For instance, Inference-Time Intervention (ITI) enhances truthfulness by adjusting activations across a limited number of attention heads, improving performance on benchmarks like TruthfulQA \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2205.05124)\]. Similarly, Contrastive Activation Addition (CAA) computes steering vectors by comparing residual stream activations of factual versus hallucinatory responses, enabling precise control over model outputs during inference \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/2310.03744)\]. These techniques are minimally invasive, computationally efficient, and data-efficient, as they require only a few hundred examples to identify directional adjustments \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2205.05124)\]. They also offer insights into how high-level concepts are represented within LLMs, such as the internal likelihood of statements being true \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2205.05124)\]. Moreover, these methods can be applied on top of traditional approaches like fine-tuning and system prompt design, demonstrating their versatility in improving model alignment and safety \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/2310.03744)\]. Overall, inference-time interventions represent a promising direction for dynamically steering LLM behavior without compromising model capabilities \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/2310.03744)\].
