## Related Works

Zero-Knowledge Proofs (ZKPs) have garnered significant attention for their ability to enable secure and private computation, allowing a prover to demonstrate the truth of a statement without revealing additional information \[[1](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/6ee84674b7c426849c3da56cb278c08eb7a903cf)\]. Despite their potential, ZKPs have not yet achieved widespread adoption due to the computational complexity of the proving process, which involves intensive operations such as polynomial computations and multi-scalar multiplications on elliptic curves \[[2](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/e149d93531d8c07cf43f6cae327a4795144d1304)\]. Recent research has focused on accelerating these key primitives using hardware accelerators, particularly on GPUs and ASICs, to make ZKPs more practical for real-world applications \[[3](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/e4a4608e8e35379e63b9df5bda1b7599ee8ab9ac)\].

Several works have explored GPU-based acceleration for ZKP protocols. GZKP, for instance, introduces a GPU-accelerated zero-knowledge proof system that significantly improves performance by optimizing memory access patterns and parallelizing operations such as multi-scalar multiplication \[[4](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/3becc2935d1f52c82ffd7216c28bc86f5e393208)\]. Similarly, cuZK presents a GPU implementation of zkSNARKs with a faster parallel multi-scalar multiplication algorithm, achieving substantial speedups over existing implementations \[[5](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/9ccf7b6cb32cf89752a35bd910555adac54773e0)\]. These approaches highlight the potential of GPU acceleration in reducing the computational overhead of ZKP generation.

However, many existing accelerators face limitations. Some require a trusted setup for each application, which restricts their usability in scenarios with multiple verifiers or strict verification time constraints \[[6](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/6bcd2606c8f0099a82bd03e520659058a5b12e51)\]. Others generate larger proof sizes or have higher verification costs, limiting their applicability in resource-constrained environments \[[7](http://arxiv.org/abs/2408.05890)\]. To address these challenges, recent works have proposed hardware accelerators that support universal setup and small proof sizes, such as the proposed zkSpeed accelerator for HyperPlonk \[[8](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/6336149196f02d0eb6c1a89bc6b662e4422bd794)\]. This work accelerates the entire protocol, including critical primitives like SumCheck and Multi-scalar Multiplications (MSMs), using a full-chip architecture with 366.46 mm² and 2 TB/s of bandwidth, achieving geometric mean speedups of 801× over CPU baselines \[[9](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/b718a4b59ee6ee21c7c2ee1aa64085231853535e)\].

Other related works focus on improving the efficiency of ZKP generation through specialized hardware. PipeZK introduces a pipelined architecture with two subsystems to handle large polynomial computations and multi-scalar multiplications efficiently \[[10](http://arxiv.org/abs/2109.05371)\].
