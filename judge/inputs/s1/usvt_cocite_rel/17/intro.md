## Related Works

Semi-supervised learning (SSL) has emerged as a powerful paradigm for improving model performance when labeled data is scarce \[[1](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/f3df804a7eef204b0c4fc4b81166dec9b4abc073)\]. Traditional SSL methods assume that labeled and unlabeled data share the same class distribution \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/2007.11330)\], which is often violated in practice due to the presence of out-of-distribution (OOD) or outlier samples in unlabeled data \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/2001.07685)\]. These outliers can significantly degrade model performance, as they are typically treated as noise \[[4](http://arxiv.org/abs/2105.14148)\]. To address this challenge, numerous approaches have been proposed, focusing on outlier detection, robust pseudo-labeling, and handling open-set scenarios \[[5](http://arxiv.org/abs/2108.05617)\].

Early SSL methods, such as Temporal Ensembling \[[6](http://arxiv.org/abs/1905.02249)\], leveraged self-ensembling to improve robustness by using predictions from different training epochs. However, these methods often fail to handle OOD samples effectively \[[7](http://arxiv.org/abs/2107.08943)\]. More recently, FixMatch \[[8](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/9e4376e0572de32236e408469ffb2173d89eaab5)\] introduced a simple yet effective approach combining consistency regularization and pseudo-labeling, achieving state-of-the-art results on standard benchmarks. Despite its success, FixMatch assumes that all unlabeled data belongs to the same class distribution as the labeled data, making it vulnerable to OOD samples \[[9](http://arxiv.org/abs/1610.02242)\].

To address the open-set scenario, where unlabeled data may contain unseen classes, several methods have been proposed. OpenMatch \[[10](http://arxiv.org/abs/1606.04586)\] integrates novelty detection with consistency regularization, using one-vs-all classifiers to identify inliers and outliers. Similarly, IOMatch \[[11](http://arxiv.org/abs/2308.13168)\] jointly utilizes inliers and outliers by introducing a multi-binary classifier, which treats outliers as a single new class. This approach improves performance by leveraging both types of data, even when distinguishing them is challenging \[[12](http://arxiv.org/abs/2102.03526)\].

Another line of research focuses on improving pseudo-label quality and robustness in the presence of OOD data. Class-Aware Contrastive Semi-Supervised Learning (CCSSL) \[[13](http://arxiv.org/abs/1904.12848)\] enhances pseudo-label quality by separately handling in-distribution and out-of-distribution data, using class-wise clustering for inliers and image-wise contrastive learning for outliers. This method reduces confirmation bias and improves generalization \[[14](http://arxiv.org/abs/2109.00650)\].

Additionally, methods like SemiMatch \[[15](http://arxiv.org/abs/2205.07246)\] and SimMatch \[[16](http://arxiv.org/abs/2110.08263)\] incorporate similarity matching at both semantic and instance levels, encouraging consistency between different augmented views of the same instance. These approaches have shown significant improvements in performance, especially when labeled data is limited \[[17](http://arxiv.org/abs/2311.10572)\].
