## Related Works

Semi-supervised learning (SSL) has emerged as a powerful paradigm for improving model performance when labeled data is scarce \[[1](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/f3df804a7eef204b0c4fc4b81166dec9b4abc073)\]. Traditional SSL methods assume that labeled and unlabeled data share the same class distribution \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/2007.11330)\], which is often violated in practice due to the presence of out-of-distribution (OOD) or outlier samples in unlabeled data \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/2001.07685)\]. These outliers can significantly degrade model performance, as they are typically treated as noise \[[4](http://arxiv.org/abs/2105.14148)\]. To address this challenge, numerous approaches have been proposed, focusing on outlier detection, robust pseudo-labeling, and handling open-set scenarios \[[5](http://arxiv.org/abs/2108.05617)\].

Early SSL methods, such as Temporal Ensembling \[[6](http://arxiv.org/abs/1905.02249)\], leveraged self-ensembling to improve robustness by using predictions from different training epochs. However, these methods often fail to handle OOD samples effectively \[[7](http://arxiv.org/abs/2107.08943)\]. More recently, FixMatch \[[8](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/9e4376e0572de32236e408469ffb2173d89eaab5)\] introduced a simple yet effective approach combining consistency regularization and pseudo-labeling, achieving state-of-the-art results on standard benchmarks. Despite its success, FixMatch assumes that all unlabeled data belongs to the same class distribution as the labeled data, making it vulnerable to OOD samples \[[9](http://arxiv.org/abs/1610.02242)\].

To address the open-set scenario, where unlabeled data may contain unseen classes, several methods have been proposed. OpenMatch \[[10](http://arxiv.org/abs/1606.04586)\] integrates novelty detection with consistency regularization, using one-vs-all classifiers to identify inliers and outliers. Similarly, IOMatch \[[11](http://arxiv.org/abs/2308.13168)\] jointly utilizes inliers and outliers by introducing a multi-binary classifier, which treats outliers as a single new class. This approach improves performance by leveraging both types of data, even when distinguishing them is challenging \[[12](http://arxiv.org/abs/2102.03526)\].

Another line of research focuses on improving pseudo-label quality and robustness in the presence of OOD data. Class-Aware Contrastive Semi-Supervised Learning (CCSSL) \[[13](http://arxiv.org/abs/1904.12848)\] enhances pseudo-label quality by separately handling in-distribution and out-of-distribution data, using class-wise clustering for inliers and image-wise contrastive learning for outliers. This method reduces confirmation bias and improves generalization \[[14](http://arxiv.org/abs/2109.00650)\].

Additionally, methods like SemiMatch \[[15](http://arxiv.org/abs/2205.07246)\] and SimMatch \[[16](http://arxiv.org/abs/2110.08263)\] incorporate similarity matching at both semantic and instance levels, encouraging consistency between different augmented views of the same instance. These approaches have shown significant improvements in performance, especially when labeled data is limited \[[17](http://arxiv.org/abs/2311.10572)\].

Open-set semi-supervised learning (open-set SSL) addresses the challenge where unlabeled data may contain out-of-distribution (OOD) samples, which traditional SSL methods assume are absent \[[1](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/f3df804a7eef204b0c4fc4b81166dec9b4abc073)\]. The SAFE-R-STUDENT framework introduces a scoring framework and calibration loss to enhance the identification of seen and unseen classes \[[5](http://arxiv.org/abs/2108.05617)\]. IOMatch jointly utilizes inliers and outliers by employing multi-binary classifiers to generate unified pseudo-labels, improving performance even with limited labeled data \[[6](http://arxiv.org/abs/1905.02249)\]. ORCA introduces an uncertainty adaptive margin mechanism to reduce the gap between seen and novel classes during training \[[7](http://arxiv.org/abs/2107.08943)\]. The SSB method improves both inlier classification and outlier detection by using non-linear transformations and pseudo-negative mining \[[8](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/9e4376e0572de32236e408469ffb2173d89eaab5)\]. These approaches highlight the importance of detecting and handling OOD samples effectively to enhance SSL performance in real-world scenarios \[[9](http://arxiv.org/abs/1610.02242)\].

### <a id='category-pseudo-labeling-and-robustness-techniques'></a>Pseudo-Labeling and Robustness Techniques: 5 papers found.
In the context of pseudo-labeling and robustness techniques, several approaches have been proposed to improve semi-supervised learning (SSL) performance. For instance, FixMatch \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/2001.07685)\] combines consistency regularization with pseudo-labeling, generating pseudo-labels for unlabeled data only when the model is confident, and then training the model to predict these labels on strongly augmented versions of the same data. This approach achieves state-of-the-art results on benchmarks like CIFAR-10 \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/2001.07685)\]. Similarly, Dash \[[14](http://arxiv.org/abs/2109.00650)\] introduces a dynamic thresholding mechanism to select only the most informative pseudo-labeled examples, improving model robustness by focusing on high-quality pseudo-labels. Another method, FlexMatch \[[4](http://arxiv.org/abs/2105.14148)\], enhances FixMatch by introducing a curriculum learning strategy that adjusts thresholds per class, allowing for more adaptive and effective use of unlabeled data, especially when labeled data is scarce. Additionally, CCSSL \[[5](http://arxiv.org/abs/2108.05617)\] addresses the issue of confirmation bias in pseudo-labeling by improving pseudo-label quality through contrastive learning and class-aware clustering, leading to better performance on both standard and real-world datasets. These methods collectively highlight the importance of pseudo-labeling strategies and robustness mechanisms in SSL, with varying approaches to handle noise and outliers in unlabeled data.

### <a id='category-state-of-the-art-ssl-approaches'></a>State-of-the-Art SSL Approaches: 2 papers found.
State-of-the-art semi-supervised learning (SSL) approaches have focused on improving model robustness and performance in scenarios with limited labeled data. One notable method is self-ensembling, which leverages the outputs of a network-in-training under different regularization and input augmentation conditions to form a consensus prediction for unknown labels \[[1](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/f3df804a7eef204b0c4fc4b81166dec9b4abc073)\]. This approach has been successfully applied to standard benchmarks, achieving significant improvements in classification accuracy, such as reducing the classification error rate in SVHN from 18.44% to 7.05% with 500 labels \[[1](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/f3df804a7eef204b0c4fc4b81166dec9b4abc073)\]. Another promising technique is virtual adversarial training (VAT), which introduces a regularization method based on the local smoothness of the conditional label distribution. VAT defines adversarial directions without label information, making it suitable for semi-supervised learning, and has achieved state-of-the-art results on datasets like SVHN and CIFAR-10 \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/2007.11330)\]. These methods highlight the ongoing efforts to enhance SSL by incorporating diverse training strategies and robustness mechanisms.
