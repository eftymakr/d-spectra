## Related Works

Instruction-following text embedding has emerged as a critical area in natural language processing, aiming to generate dynamic embeddings that adapt to user instructions. This concept aligns with broader efforts to improve the adaptability and generalization of text representations across diverse tasks. Early work in this area focused on unsupervised learning of sentence representations using large-scale datasets, such as the Stanford Natural Language Inference (SNLI) corpus \[[14](http://arxiv.org/abs/1508.05326)\]. These methods, while useful, often struggled with transferability to new tasks due to limited supervision and domain-specific constraints.

Recent advances have introduced supervised learning frameworks that leverage annotated instruction pairs for contrastive learning. SimCSE, for instance, uses natural language inference datasets to train sentence embeddings through a contrastive objective, achieving strong performance on semantic textual similarity tasks \[[5](http://arxiv.org/abs/2104.08821)\]. Similarly, INSTRUCTOR extends this idea by incorporating task-specific instructions during embedding generation, allowing a single model to adapt to various downstream tasks without additional training \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2212.09741)\]. These approaches demonstrate the importance of instruction alignment in improving the relevance and adaptability of text embeddings.

The challenge of computational efficiency in instruction-following remains a key limitation. Many existing methods require re-encoding the entire corpus for each new instruction, leading to significant overhead \[[21](http://arxiv.org/abs/2402.14334)\]. This issue is addressed by models like Sentence-T5, which explore scalable sentence encoders from pre-trained text-to-text models, enabling efficient fine-tuning and inference \[[4](http://arxiv.org/abs/2108.08877)\]. Additionally, E5 introduces a contrastive pre-training approach with weak supervision, allowing models to generalize well across tasks without labeled data \[[8](http://arxiv.org/abs/2212.03533)\].

Instruction-tuning has also been explored as a way to improve zero-shot learning capabilities. FLAN, for example, demonstrates that instruction-tuned models significantly outperform unmodified counterparts on unseen tasks, highlighting the value of task-specific training \[[16](http://arxiv.org/abs/2109.01652)\]. Similarly, InstructGPT shows that fine-tuning with human feedback improves alignment with user intent, reducing toxic outputs and increasing truthfulness \[[18](http://arxiv.org/abs/2203.02155)\]. These findings support the idea that instruction-based training can enhance both performance and alignment in language models.

Despite these advancements, the field still lacks a comprehensive benchmark for evaluating instruction-following text embeddings across a wide range of tasks. The category "Pre-Trained Language Models for Embedding" encompasses a range of approaches aimed at improving the generation of high-quality text embeddings for various NLP tasks. Early efforts focused on unsupervised methods like SkipThought vectors, which struggled to produce robust sentence-level representations \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2212.09741)\]. This led to the development of supervised models, such as those trained on the Stanford Natural Language Inference datasets, which demonstrated superior performance in transfer learning tasks \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2212.09741)\]. The introduction of the Transformer architecture marked a significant shift, offering a more efficient and parallelizable approach to sequence transduction, achieving state-of-the-art results in machine translation and other tasks \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/1705.02364)\]. Subsequent work explored encoder-only models, such as T5, which enabled the creation of sentence embeddings with improved performance on tasks like semantic textual similarity \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/1706.03762)\]. The BERT model further advanced the field by introducing bidirectional representations, achieving new benchmarks across multiple NLP tasks \[[4](http://arxiv.org/abs/2108.08877)\]. More recently, models like SGPT have addressed the limitations of decoder-based models in semantic search and sentence embeddings, demonstrating competitive performance even at lower parameter counts \[[5](http://arxiv.org/abs/2104.08821)\]. Additionally, document-level models like SPECTER have been developed to handle scientific documents by leveraging citation graphs during pre-training, enhancing their ability to capture inter-document relationships \[[6](http://arxiv.org/abs/2210.07316)\]. These advancements highlight the ongoing evolution of pre-trained language models, emphasizing their versatility and effectiveness in a wide array of language processing applications.
