## Related Works

Table discovery and semantic parsing have seen significant advancements in recent years, driven by the increasing availability of structured and semi-structured data. Existing methods primarily rely on query tables or vague keywords to retrieve relevant tables, often requiring manual filtering of large result sets \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2005.08314)\]. This limitation highlights the need for more sophisticated approaches that can integrate natural language (NL) requirements with tabular data to refine search results. Our proposed task, NL-conditional table discovery (nlcTD), aligns with this need by enabling users to combine a query table with NL conditions to improve search accuracy \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2005.08314)\].

Pretrained language models have played a crucial role in improving the understanding of both textual and tabular data. TaBERT, for instance, is a pretrained model that jointly learns representations for natural language sentences and semi-structured tables, trained on a large corpus of 26 million tables and their English contexts \[[2](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/bff7592f28354b3be632f4bf678b622f765a2b8d)\]. This model has been shown to achieve new best results on the WikiTableQuestions benchmark, demonstrating its effectiveness in semantic parsing tasks involving structured data \[[2](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/bff7592f28354b3be632f4bf678b622f765a2b8d)\]. Similarly, compositional semantic parsing on semi-structured tables has been explored, where a logical-form driven parsing algorithm guided by strong typing constraints outperforms natural baselines \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/1508.00305)\]. This work also introduced a new dataset of 22,033 complex questions on Wikipedia tables, which has been widely used for benchmarking \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/1508.00305)\].

The challenge of accurately identifying and extracting tables from scientific documents has also been addressed through the use of distant supervision and neural networks. A notable effort in this area is the development of a dataset with over 5.5 million induced labels for figure extraction, including tables, achieved through automated methods that leverage auxiliary data from large scientific document collections \[[4](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/9f7a62139dfd09bfad667691332bd55e31736887)\]. This dataset, with an average precision of 96.8%, has enabled the training of deep neural networks for end-to-end figure detection, which has been successfully deployed in Semantic Scholar to extract figures from 13 million scientific documents \[[4](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/9f7a62139dfd09bfad667691332bd55e31736887)\].

In addition to these efforts, the task of semantic parsing has been extended to handle complex queries involving multiple conditions, such as union, join, and fuzzy conditions. The nlcTables dataset, introduced in this work, comprises 627 diverse queries and 22,080 candidate tables, providing a comprehensive benchmark for evaluating NL-conditional table discovery methods \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2005.08314)\]. The evaluation of six state-of-the-art methods on this dataset reveals substantial performance gaps, underscoring the need for further research into advanced techniques for this challenging task \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2005.08314)\].
