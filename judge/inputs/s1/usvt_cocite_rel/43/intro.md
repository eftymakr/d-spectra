## Related Works

The field of large-scale language model (LLM) training has seen significant advancements in recent years, driven by the need for efficient execution, scalable deployment, and accurate performance prediction. Early work focused on addressing the challenges of training very large models, such as memory constraints and computational inefficiencies. For instance, Megatron-LM introduced techniques for training multi-billion parameter language models using model parallelism, enabling the training of up to 8.3 billion parameter models on 512 GPUs \[[4](http://arxiv.org/abs/1909.08053)\]. This work emphasized the importance of intra-layer model parallelism and demonstrated that careful attention to layer normalization in BERT-like models is critical for performance \[[4](http://arxiv.org/abs/1909.08053)\].

To further scale training to trillion parameter models, ZeRO was proposed as a memory optimization technique that eliminates redundancies in data- and model-parallel training while maintaining high computational granularity and low communication volume \[[8](http://arxiv.org/abs/1910.02054)\]. ZeRO enabled the training of models with over 100 billion parameters on 400 GPUs, achieving a throughput of 15 Petaflops and an 8x increase in model size compared to previous methods \[[8](http://arxiv.org/abs/1910.02054)\]. Later, ZeRO-Infinity extended these ideas by leveraging heterogeneous memory systems (GPU, CPU, NVMe) to train models with tens to hundreds of trillions of parameters on limited resources without requiring model code refactoring \[[10](http://arxiv.org/abs/2104.07857)\].

Efforts to improve training throughput and reduce idle time have also been explored. For example, the DAPPLE framework combines data and pipeline parallelism to improve device utilization and reduce memory usage, achieving up to 3.23× speedup over existing strategies \[[9](http://arxiv.org/abs/2007.01045)\]. Similarly, Pollux introduced a co-adaptive cluster scheduling approach that optimizes both per-job and cluster-wide performance, reducing job completion time by 25–50% compared to state-of-the-art schedulers \[[13](http://arxiv.org/abs/2008.12260)\].

In the context of performance modeling, several studies have focused on predicting training time and execution behavior. The MLPerf Training Benchmark was introduced to address the challenges of benchmarking ML training, including the stochastic nature of training and the diversity of hardware/software systems \[[5](http://arxiv.org/abs/1910.01500)\]. This benchmark has driven improvements in performance and scalability across multiple vendors. Additionally, the work on predicting the computational cost of deep learning models proposed an alternative approach to linear time estimation by training a deep learning network to predict execution times for parts of a model, enabling more accurate predictions for unseen scenarios \[[7](http://arxiv.org/abs/1811.11880)\].
