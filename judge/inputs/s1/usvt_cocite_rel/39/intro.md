## Related Works

De novo genome assembly has been a focal point of computational biology, especially with the advent of next-generation sequencing (NGS) technologies that generate massive volumes of short reads \[[7](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/c6e319a023f932d6d2ff8897c2c91e56e872db8e)\]. Early assemblers, such as SOAPdenovo and Velvet, relied on de Bruijn graph representations to manage the complexity of short reads, but they struggled with memory usage and scalability for large genomes \[[17](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/f8d1052e2c9d1cdd63093427ccc9169e848d777d)\]. The need for more efficient algorithms led to the development of parallel and distributed assemblers, such as PASHA and SWAP-Assembler, which leverage hybrid computing architectures to improve performance and scalability \[[19](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/7bf2f1c55f90e11a4d1df4dd608ac621a08f8b33)\]\[[18](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/fe8d86bc5520b5049104ff58ee7e980982816ebf)\]. These tools demonstrated significant improvements in handling large datasets, but they still faced challenges with memory-bound operations and irregular data patterns \[[19](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/7bf2f1c55f90e11a4d1df4dd608ac621a08f8b33)\].

Long-read sequencing technologies, such as PacBio and Oxford Nanopore, have introduced new challenges due to their high error rates and the need for specialized assembly strategies \[[4](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/2b59fd18ada769ef76fb7fcc7382f063791abfab)\]. Tools like Canu and Minimap2 have been developed to address these challenges by incorporating adaptive k-mer weighting, repeat separation, and efficient mapping algorithms \[[4](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/2b59fd18ada769ef76fb7fcc7382f063791abfab)\]\[[12](http://arxiv.org/abs/1708.01492)\]. Canu, for example, improves assembly continuity and reduces runtime by an order of magnitude compared to earlier assemblers, while Minimap2 offers faster alignment for long reads with high accuracy \[[12](http://arxiv.org/abs/1708.01492)\]\[[4](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/2b59fd18ada769ef76fb7fcc7382f063791abfab)\]. These advancements have enabled the assembly of complex genomes with high contiguity and accuracy \[[4](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/2b59fd18ada769ef76fb7fcc7382f063791abfab)\].

Despite these improvements, existing assemblers still face limitations in terms of computational efficiency and memory usage. For instance, the MHAP algorithm, combined with the Celera Assembler, has shown promise in assembling large eukaryotic genomes from SMRT sequencing data, achieving near-complete assemblies with high accuracy \[[5](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/618d82a202bb0408e388eba250c66c8a90700c81)\]. Similarly, BELLA has introduced a novel approach for overlapping long reads using probabilistic models and reliable k-mers, which reduces false positives and improves recall and precision \[[8](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/f27decfab0389e2003e7ca66260b34eb3c1b8827)\]. These methods highlight the importance of addressing the inherent challenges of de novo assembly, such as handling repetitive regions and improving contiguity \[[8](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/f27decfab0389e2003e7ca66260b34eb3c1b8827)\].

The development of software tools like hifiasm has further advanced haplotype-resolved assembly, enabling the accurate representation of genetic variation in complex genomes \[[16](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/788f8a32ed116f928f3cab554c6efd55385f2a80)\]. These tools demonstrate the potential of combining long-read technologies with sophisticated algorithms to achieve high-quality assemblies. However, the computational demands of these methods remain a barrier to widespread adoption, especially for large-scale genomic studies \[[16](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/788f8a32ed116f928f3cab554c6efd55385f2a80)\].
