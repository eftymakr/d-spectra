## Related Works

Federated learning (FL) has emerged as a promising paradigm for training machine learning models on decentralized data while preserving privacy \[[5](http://arxiv.org/abs/1912.04977)\]. In FL, a central server coordinates the training of a global model using updates from multiple clients, each holding their own private data \[[5](http://arxiv.org/abs/1912.04977)\]. However, the heterogeneity of data distributions across clients poses significant challenges to model convergence and accuracy \[[5](http://arxiv.org/abs/1912.04977)\]. This issue is further compounded by the non-IID (non-independent and identically distributed) nature of data in many real-world scenarios, which can lead to substantial performance degradation \[[5](http://arxiv.org/abs/1912.04977)\]. 

Several studies have explored strategies to improve the communication efficiency and convergence of FL algorithms. For example, structured and sketched updates have been proposed to reduce the uplink communication costs by compressing model updates before transmission \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/1610.05492)\]. Similarly, adaptive optimization methods such as Adagrad, Adam, and Yogi have been adapted for federated settings to improve performance under heterogeneous data conditions \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/2003.00295)\]. These methods aim to address the convergence issues that arise when clients have varying data distributions and computational capabilities \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/2003.00295)\].

Client selection has also been investigated as a means to enhance the efficiency of FL. FedCS, for instance, actively manages clients based on their resource conditions, allowing the server to aggregate more client updates and accelerate model improvement \[[4](http://arxiv.org/abs/1804.08333)\]. This approach is particularly useful in mobile edge computing environments where clients may have limited computational resources or face poor network conditions \[[4](http://arxiv.org/abs/1804.08333)\].

To address the challenges of non-IID data, several works have proposed novel optimization objectives and algorithms. For example, q-Fair Federated Learning (q-FFL) introduces a fairness-aware objective that encourages a more uniform accuracy distribution across devices \[[6](http://arxiv.org/abs/1905.10497)\]. Similarly, FedProx generalizes FedAvg to handle statistical and system heterogeneity by incorporating a proximal term that allows for variable client participation \[[10](http://arxiv.org/abs/1812.06127)\]. These methods demonstrate that accounting for data and system heterogeneity is essential for achieving robust convergence in FL.

In addition to addressing convergence, recent works have focused on improving the fairness of FL models. For example, the q-FedAvg algorithm, which is designed to work with q-FFL, ensures that the model performance is more uniform across clients \[[6](http://arxiv.org/abs/1905.10497)\]. Furthermore, the SCAFFOLD algorithm introduces control variates to reduce the drift between client updates, leading to faster convergence and fewer communication rounds \[[15](http://arxiv.org/abs/1910.06378)\]. These approaches highlight the importance of addressing both statistical and communication challenges in FL.
