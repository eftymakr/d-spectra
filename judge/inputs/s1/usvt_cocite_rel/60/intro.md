## Related Works

Calibration of predictive models has been a central topic in machine learning, particularly for classification tasks where accurate probability estimates are essential for reliable decision-making. Traditional approaches to calibration often rely on post-hoc methods such as Platt scaling and temperature scaling, which adjust model outputs to better align with empirical frequencies \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/1706.04599)\]. However, these methods are limited in their flexibility, as they typically assume fixed functional forms, such as piece-wise linear transformations, which may not adequately capture complex calibration patterns \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/1706.04599)\]. This limitation has led to the development of more expressive calibration techniques, including deep ensembles and Bayesian neural networks, which can provide better uncertainty estimates \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/1612.01474)\]. 

Recent work has also explored the use of deep learning for calibration, such as mixup training, which improves calibration by encouraging the model to learn more robust and well-calibrated predictions \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/1905.11001)\]. Additionally, methods like Bayesian binning and histogram binning have been proposed to address calibration issues, although they often require a significant number of samples to achieve accurate results \[[4](http://arxiv.org/abs/1909.10155)\]. In contrast, the proposed approach in this paper introduces an Unconstrained Monotonic Neural Network (UMNN) to learn arbitrary monotonic functions, offering greater flexibility and expressiveness while preserving the order of predictions \[[5](http://arxiv.org/abs/1906.02530)\]. This aligns with prior work that emphasizes the importance of monotonicity in calibration functions \[[6](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/8d115c3b2ee80e0754360a154a9369bc1658b607)\].

The calibration of ranking models has also received attention, particularly in the context of learning-to-rank (LTR) systems. Existing methods often focus on improving ranking performance but neglect the calibration of absolute scores, which can be critical for downstream tasks such as ad ranking \[[7](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/7f6bb146b9c1504ec77100e640d624bdc204f0ac)\]. Some studies have attempted to address this by introducing scale-calibrated ranking losses that ensure the scores are consistent with the true probabilities \[[8](http://arxiv.org/abs/1703.04977)\]. However, these approaches often suffer from training instability due to their translation-invariant properties \[[9](http://arxiv.org/abs/1506.02142)\]. The proposed UMNN-based calibration method overcomes this by enforcing monotonicity during training, thus improving both calibration and stability \[[10](http://arxiv.org/abs/1905.10713)\].

Furthermore, the importance of uncertainty estimation in deep learning has been widely discussed, with methods like dropout being used as a Bayesian approximation to model uncertainty \[[11](http://arxiv.org/abs/1807.00263)\]. While these approaches can provide uncertainty estimates, they often require additional computational overhead or are not easily integrated into existing models \[[12](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/d388760ce74792bb764ccf77ea8620ca77406e56)\]. The proposed calibration method in this paper addresses these limitations by offering a flexible and efficient solution that can be applied to a wide range of models without significant modifications \[[13](http://arxiv.org/abs/2002.09437)\].
