## Related Works

Deep-unrolling and Plug-and-Play (PnP) approaches have emerged as dominant methodologies for solving inverse problems in imaging, particularly in single-pixel imaging (SPI) \[[1' 2023-06-12](http://arxiv.org/abs/2306.07233v1)\]. PnP methods integrate pre-trained deep denoisers into iterative algorithms such as ADMM or ISTA, replacing the proximal step with a denoiser \[[2' 2022-04-29](http://arxiv.org/abs/2204.13940v3)\]. These methods offer flexibility in handling varying compression ratios (CRs) but often suffer from limited reconstruction accuracy and speed \[[1' 2023-06-12](http://arxiv.org/abs/2306.07233v1)\]. In contrast, unrolling approaches transform iterative optimization processes into end-to-end trainable neural networks, typically achieving higher accuracy and faster inference \[[3' 2020-06-05](http://arxiv.org/abs/2006.03224v2)\]. However, they require fine-tuning or retraining when CR changes, which limits their adaptability \[[1' 2023-06-12](http://arxiv.org/abs/2306.07233v1)\].

Several works have explored the use of PnP methods for various imaging tasks. For instance, GPnP extends PnP to sample from the posterior distribution by using proximal generators \[[4' 2024-04-22](http://arxiv.org/abs/2404.13866v1)\]. PnP-ReG introduces a learned regularizing gradient for Plug-and-Play gradient descent, improving results over generic PnP approaches \[[5' 2023-11-02](http://arxiv.org/abs/2311.01216v1)\]. Scalable Plug-and-Play ADMM with convergence guarantees addresses the computational and memory limitations of PnP algorithms in large-scale settings \[[6' 2018-01-02](http://arxiv.org/abs/1801.00605v1)\]. The convergence of PnP algorithms has also been analyzed from the perspective of stochastic differential equations, revealing that bounded denoisers with Lipschitz continuous measurement functions are sufficient for convergence \[[7' 2024-02-20](http://arxiv.org/abs/2402.12872v2)\].

The convergence of PnP algorithms with nonconvex denoisers has been a topic of recent research. A paper on convergent PnP with proximal denoisers and unconstrained regularization parameters presents two remedies: a novel convergence proof for PnP-DRS without restrictions on the regularization parameter and a relaxed version of the PGD algorithm that converges across a broader range of regularization parameters \[[8' 2024-12-15](http://arxiv.org/abs/2412.11108v1)\]. These advancements enhance the accuracy of image restoration in deblurring and super-resolution tasks \[[8' 2024-12-15](http://arxiv.org/abs/2412.11108v1)\].

Unrolling techniques have also been extensively studied. For example, deep, convergent, unrolled half-quadratic splitting for image deconvolution establishes convergence of the proposed unrolled network as the number of layers approaches infinity, and characterizes its convergence rate \[[9' 2020-05-15](http://arxiv.org/abs/2005.07685v1)\]. Similarly, the paper on algorithm unrolling discusses the benefits of interpretable, efficient deep learning for signal and image processing, highlighting the connection between iterative algorithms and deep neural networks \[[10' 2021-05-11](http://arxiv.org/abs/2105.05177v1)\].

In the context of single-pixel imaging, recent works have focused on improving reconstruction quality and speed. For instance, single-pixel imaging based on deep learning has demonstrated exceptional reconstruction quality and fast reconstruction speed

### <a id='category-pnp-methodologies-and-their-limitations'></a>PnP Methodologies and Their Limitations: 10 papers found.
The Plug-and-Play (PnP) framework has gained significant attention for its ability to integrate advanced image denoising priors into optimization algorithms for solving inverse problems, often formulated as Maximum A Posteriori (MAP) estimation \[[2' 2022-04-29](http://arxiv.org/abs/2204.13940v3)\]. This approach typically uses proximal algorithms like ADMM or FISTA, where the proximal map associated with a regularizer is replaced by a denoiser such as BM3D or a CNN \[[10' 2021-05-11](http://arxiv.org/abs/2105.05177v1)\]. However, PnP methods face challenges in terms of theoretical guarantees, convergence, and stability, especially when using pre-trained deep learning-based denoisers, which can introduce hallucinated features or instabilities not inherent to the original ill-posed problem \[[14' 2021-08-17](http://arxiv.org/abs/2109.01655v1)\]. To address these limitations, recent work has focused on improving the theoretical foundations of PnP algorithms, such as proving that a broader class of linear denoisers can be expressed as proximal maps of convex regularizers, thus enabling convergence guarantees \[[10' 2021-05-11](http://arxiv.org/abs/2105.05177v1)\]. Additionally, efforts have been made to enhance scalability and efficiency, such as proposing an incremental variant of PnP-ADMM for large-scale datasets \[[3' 2020-06-05](http://arxiv.org/abs/2006.03224v2)\] and introducing non-Euclidean settings using Bregman distances for better convergence \[[16' 2022-02-04](http://arxiv.org/abs/2202.02388v1)\]. Furthermore, novel approaches like PnP Flow Matching have been developed to combine the strengths of PnP with generative models, offering computationally efficient and memory-friendly solutions for tasks like denoising and inpainting \[[18' 2024-10-03](http://arxiv.org/abs/2410.02423v3)\]. These advancements highlight the ongoing evolution of PnP methodologies to overcome existing limitations while maintaining flexibility and performance.

### <a id='category-convergence-analysis-of-pnp-algorithms'></a>Convergence Analysis of PnP Algorithms: 8 papers found.
The convergence analysis of Plug-and-Play (PnP) algorithms has been a focal point in recent research, addressing both theoretical foundations and practical limitations. Early work established that PnP methods, which replace proximal operators with powerful denoisers, can be modeled as continuous stochastic differential equations (SDEs) or Markov processes, offering a unified framework for convergence analysis \[[4' 2024-04-22](http://arxiv.org/abs/2404.13866v1)\]. This approach revealed that weaker conditions, such as bounded denoisers with Lipschitz continuous measurement functions, are sufficient for convergence, relaxing previous requirements \[[4' 2024-04-22](http://arxiv.org/abs/2404.13866v1)\]. Subsequent studies further explored the convergence of PnP algorithms under various settings, including ISTA and ADMM, demonstrating linear convergence for symmetric denoisers and kernel denoisers under mild conditions \[[15' 2023-09-28](http://arxiv.org/abs/2309.16899v3)\]\[[20' 2025-05-21](http://arxiv.org/abs/2505.15318v1)\]. These analyses also extended to non-convex scenarios, showing that PnP-ISTA and PnP-ADMM can converge linearly for tasks like image inpainting and deblurring \[[15' 2023-09-28](http://arxiv.org/abs/2309.16899v3)\]. Additionally, researchers have proposed frameworks to ensure convergence without restrictive stepsize conditions, such as real spectral normalization for deep learning-based denoisers \[[25' 2019-05-14](http://arxiv.org/abs/1905.05406v1)\]. Theoretical results have been validated through experiments on tasks like hyperspectral sharpening, image deblurring, and sparse signal recovery, highlighting the effectiveness of PnP methods in practical applications \[[3' 2020-06-05](http://arxiv.org/abs/2006.03224v2)\]\[[6' 2018-01-02](http://arxiv.org/abs/1801.00605v1)\]\[[7' 2024-02-20](http://arxiv.org/abs/2402.12872v2)\]. These studies collectively emphasize the importance of rigorous convergence guarantees for PnP algorithms, enabling their broader adoption in imaging inverse problems.

These methods often rely on frameworks like ADMM, where the proximal mapping is replaced by a deep denoiser, allowing for the incorporation of complex regularization on magnitude or phase \[[13' 2023-12-26](http://arxiv.org/abs/2312.16024v2)\]. Recent work has also explored the use of score-based diffusion models (SBMs) within PnP, reinterpreting their score-based nature to align with proximal optimization, thus enabling the reuse of powerful SBMs without retraining \[[8' 2024-12-15](http://arxiv.org/abs/2412.11108v1)\]. In video restoration, PnP methods have been adapted to handle temporal consistency, where a single video denoising network can be repurposed for various restoration tasks, offering better performance and temporal stability compared to single-image networks \[[22' 2022-09-06](http://arxiv.org/abs/2209.02854v2)\]. Additionally, deep denoisers have been trained specifically for medical imaging applications, demonstrating their potential in improving image reconstruction and diagnostic accuracy \[[29' 2020-08-10](http://arxiv.org/abs/2008.04221v4)\]. These developments underscore the growing importance of deep denoisers in enhancing the performance and adaptability of PnP frameworks across diverse imaging modalities.
