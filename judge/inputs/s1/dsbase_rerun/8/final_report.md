## Related Works

Fairness in machine learning has gained significant attention as AI systems increasingly influence critical aspects of human life. Traditional fairness metrics, such as Equalized Odds \[[7' 2017-07-03](http://arxiv.org/abs/1707.00391v1)\], focus on discrete accuracy-based evaluations, often overlooking the implicit impact of model uncertainty \[[1' 2024-07-15](http://arxiv.org/abs/2407.10853v6)\]. This limitation highlights the need for more nuanced fairness measures that account for model confidence and decision bias. Recent work has explored fairness in recommendation systems, emphasizing the need for multisided fairness that considers outcomes for multiple stakeholders \[[5' 2017-07-01](http://arxiv.org/abs/1707.00093v2)\]. Similarly, studies on LLMs have shown that fairness risks vary across deployment contexts, underscoring the importance of context-specific evaluation metrics \[[1' 2024-07-15](http://arxiv.org/abs/2407.10853v6)\]. 

The role of gender and occupation in LLM representations has also been examined, revealing that LLMs' first-name gender representations correlate with real-world gender statistics and are influenced by co-occurrence of stereotypically feminine or masculine occupations \[[2' 2025-03-09](http://arxiv.org/abs/2503.06792v1)\]. This suggests that internal gender representations can be used as an internal metric for bias detection, though challenges remain in reliably using these for fairness assessment. Furthermore, research has shown that LLMs encode significant gender and country biases, with intersectional occupational biases persisting even when models show parity for individual axes of bias \[[4' 2025-05-05](http://arxiv.org/abs/2505.02456v2)\]. This highlights the need for intersectional and multilingual approaches in fairness research.

Fairness in recommendation systems has also been extended to consider fairness as a multisided concept, where fair outcomes for multiple individuals must be considered \[[5' 2017-07-01](http://arxiv.org/abs/1707.00093v2)\]. This aligns with the broader goal of ensuring fairness in AI systems that affect diverse populations. Additionally, fairness research has emphasized the importance of pre-processing, in-processing, and post-processing mechanisms to mitigate bias \[[3' 2020-01-21](http://arxiv.org/abs/2001.09784v1)\]. These mechanisms are crucial for developing fair AI systems that are not only accurate but also objective and equitable.

In the context of LLMs, studies have shown that conventional fairness metrics fail to capture the implicit impact of model uncertainty, such as higher confidence in incorrect predictions across different groups \[[1' 2024-07-15](http://arxiv.org/abs/2407.10853v6)\]. This gap motivates the development of uncertainty-aware fairness metrics, such as UCerF, which enable a more fine-grained evaluation of model fairness by incorporating model confidence \[[1' 2024-07-15](http://arxiv.org/abs/2407.10853v6)\]. Furthermore, the introduction of new datasets, such as the gender-occupation fairness evaluation dataset with 31,756 samples, provides a more diverse and suitable benchmark for evaluating modern LLMs \[[1' 2024-07-15](http://arxiv.org/abs/2407.10853v6)\]. These datasets are essential for establishing benchmarks that reflect real-world deployment scenarios.
