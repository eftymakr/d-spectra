## Related Works

Economic inequality remains a critical challenge, with significant implications for education, healthcare, and social stability \[[1' 2025-02-24](http://arxiv.org/abs/2502.16879v1)\]. Traditional tax systems, such as the U.S. federal income tax, aim to reduce inequality but often lack the adaptability required to address dynamic economic conditions \[[1' 2025-02-24](http://arxiv.org/abs/2502.16879v1)\]. While models like the Saez Optimal Taxation framework attempt to dynamically adjust tax policies, they often fail to account for taxpayer heterogeneity and irrational behavior \[[1' 2025-02-24](http://arxiv.org/abs/2502.16879v1)\]. This limitation highlights the need for more sophisticated approaches that integrate behavioral modeling with policy optimization.

Agent-based modeling (ABM) has emerged as a promising tool for simulating economic systems and evaluating policy impacts \[[2' 2024-02-14](http://arxiv.org/abs/2402.09563v2)\]. ABM allows for the representation of heterogeneous agents, such as households and firms, which interact within a simulated economy to produce emergent behaviors \[[2' 2024-02-14](http://arxiv.org/abs/2402.09563v2)\]. This approach has been applied to various economic scenarios, including the simulation of interest-income taxation and the analysis of socio-economic consequences of aging populations \[[2' 2024-02-14](http://arxiv.org/abs/2402.09563v2)\]\[[5' 2025-04-01](http://arxiv.org/abs/2504.01242v1)\]. These models often incorporate reinforcement learning (RL) to enable agents to adapt their behaviors over time, improving the realism of economic simulations \[[4' 2020-06-09](http://arxiv.org/abs/2006.05048v3)\]\[[6' 2024-08-21](http://arxiv.org/abs/2408.12038v1)\].

Recent advances in large language models (LLMs) have also contributed to the development of more sophisticated economic modeling frameworks \[[14' 2025-02-04](http://arxiv.org/abs/2502.04355v1)\]. LLMs, such as GPT-4o and o1-preview, have demonstrated strong performance in solving complex problems, including algorithmic coding and competitive programming \[[14' 2025-02-04](http://arxiv.org/abs/2502.04355v1)\]. These models can be integrated with ABM to create more adaptive and data-driven policy evaluation tools \[[1' 2025-02-24](http://arxiv.org/abs/2502.16879v1)\]. For instance, the MLAB framework leverages multiple LLMs to simulate heterogeneous economic agents, offering a novel approach to policy analysis \[[1' 2025-02-24](http://arxiv.org/abs/2502.16879v1)\].

The integration of RL with ABM has further enhanced the ability to model complex economic systems \[[4' 2020-06-09](http://arxiv.org/abs/2006.05048v3)\]. RL-based behavioral models allow agents to learn and adapt their strategies over time, improving the accuracy of economic simulations \[[4' 2020-06-09](http://arxiv.org/abs/2006.05048v3)\]. This has been demonstrated in various applications, including the simulation of influenza transmission and the analysis of minority games \[[4' 2020-06-09](http://arxiv.org/abs/2006.05048v3)\]. Additionally, the use of attention mechanisms in multi-agent reinforcement learning has enabled more efficient modeling of dynamic joint policies \[[10' 2018-11-13](http://arxiv.org/abs/1811.07029v1)\].

In the context of tax policy, the use of ABM has been explored to simulate the effects of different taxation strategies on economic outcomes \[[5' 2025-04-01](http://arxiv.org/abs/2504.01242v1)\]. These models can incorporate heterogeneous taxpayer behaviors and evaluate the impact of various tax policies on equity and productivity \[[5' 2025-04-01](http://arxiv.org/abs/2504.01242v1)\].
