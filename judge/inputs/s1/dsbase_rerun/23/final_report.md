Radar-camera fusion has been a focal point in autonomous driving research due to its ability to complement the limitations of individual sensors. Early methods primarily relied on target-based calibration techniques, which required complex setups and specialized infrastructure \[[11' 2023-07-28](http://arxiv.org/abs/2307.15264v1)\]. These approaches, while effective in controlled environments, were not suitable for real-time or online calibration during system operation. To address these limitations, several studies have proposed targetless calibration methods that leverage deep learning to extract common features from raw radar and camera data \[[2' 2023-09-02](http://arxiv.org/abs/2309.00787v2)\]. Similarly, \[[7' 2019-04-18](http://arxiv.org/abs/1904.08743v2)\] proposed a data-driven method for automatic rotational radar-camera calibration without dedicated calibration targets, using a coarse and fine convolutional neural network to achieve precise sensor registration.

The challenge of sparse and noisy radar data has also been a key focus in radar-camera fusion.
