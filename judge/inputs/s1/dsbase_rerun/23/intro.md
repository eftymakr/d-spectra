## Related Works

Radar-camera fusion has been a focal point in autonomous driving research due to its ability to complement the limitations of individual sensors. Early methods primarily relied on target-based calibration techniques, which required complex setups and specialized infrastructure \[[11' 2023-07-28](http://arxiv.org/abs/2307.15264v1)\]. These approaches, while effective in controlled environments, were not suitable for real-time or online calibration during system operation. To address these limitations, several studies have proposed targetless calibration methods that leverage deep learning to extract common features from raw radar and camera data \[[2' 2023-09-02](http://arxiv.org/abs/2309.00787v2)\]. For instance, \[[2' 2023-09-02](http://arxiv.org/abs/2309.00787v2)\] introduced a method that implicitly uses common features to match objects across modalities and applied RANSAC and Levenberg-Marquardt (LM) optimization to enhance calibration accuracy. Similarly, \[[7' 2019-04-18](http://arxiv.org/abs/1904.08743v2)\] proposed a data-driven method for automatic rotational radar-camera calibration without dedicated calibration targets, using a coarse and fine convolutional neural network to achieve precise sensor registration.

The challenge of sparse and noisy radar data has also been a key focus in radar-camera fusion. Traditional methods often struggled with accurate data association due to the complexity of driving scenes and the depth ambiguity of 2D bounding boxes \[[3' 2021-03-14](http://arxiv.org/abs/2103.07825v3)\]. To overcome these challenges, \[[3' 2021-03-14](http://arxiv.org/abs/2103.07825v3)\] proposed a deep representation learning approach that explores feature-level interaction and global reasoning, achieving a performance of 92.2% F1 score. This method was trained with noisy labels generated by a rule-based algorithm and demonstrated the potential for continuous improvement through corner case mining. Similarly, \[[10' 2023-07-17](http://arxiv.org/abs/2307.10249v5)\] introduced a multi-level fusion approach that fuses radar and camera data at both feature and instance levels, achieving state-of-the-art results on the nuScenes dataset.

Recent advancements in bird's-eye view (BEV) feature fusion have enabled more effective integration of radar and camera data. For example, \[[6' 2023-05-25](http://arxiv.org/abs/2305.15883v2)\] proposed RC-BEVFusion, a modular network that enables radar-camera fusion on the BEV plane, achieving significant performance gains on the nuScenes dataset. \[[4' 2024-03-25](http://arxiv.org/abs/2403.16440v1)\] introduced RCBEVDet, a 3D object detection method that uses a dual-stream radar backbone and a radar cross-section (RCS)-aware BEV encoder to enhance feature extraction. These methods demonstrate the importance of BEV representations in achieving robust radar-camera fusion.
