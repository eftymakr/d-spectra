## Related Works

Diffusion transformers (DiT) have emerged as a powerful architecture for video generation, offering high-quality results by combining the strengths of transformers and diffusion models \[[1' 2025-02-06](http://arxiv.org/abs/2502.04056v1)\]. However, their large parameter count and high computational demands hinder deployment on resource-constrained devices \[[1' 2025-02-06](http://arxiv.org/abs/2502.04056v1)\]. To address these challenges, several works have explored quantization techniques to reduce storage and improve inference speed. For instance, TQ-DiT introduces multi-region quantization (MRQ) and time-grouping quantization (TGQ) to minimize quantization errors while maintaining performance \[[1' 2025-02-06](http://arxiv.org/abs/2502.04056v1)\]. Similarly, Hardware-Friendly Static Quantization for Video Diffusion Transformers proposes a static quantization method that achieves video quality comparable to FP16 and dynamically quantized models \[[2' 2025-02-20](http://arxiv.org/abs/2502.15077v3)\]. These methods, however, often rely on dynamic quantization or calibration procedures that are not suitable for resource-constrained environments \[[2' 2025-02-20](http://arxiv.org/abs/2502.15077v3)\].

Another line of research focuses on data-free quantization, such as DVD-Quant, which eliminates the need for calibration data by introducing Bounded-init Grid Refinement (BGR) and Auto-scaling Rotated Quantization (ARQ) \[[3' 2025-05-24](http://arxiv.org/abs/2505.18663v4)\]. This approach enables W4A4 post-training quantization without compromising video quality, achieving approximately 2× speedup over full-precision models \[[3' 2025-05-24](http://arxiv.org/abs/2505.18663v4)\]. Additionally, Compensate Quantization Errors+ introduces Singular-value Diagonal Expansion and Cross-layer Learning to refine weight distributions and distribute errors more evenly across layers, improving quantization alignment \[[5' 2024-07-22](http://arxiv.org/abs/2407.15508v3)\]. These techniques aim to enhance the robustness of quantized models while preserving their performance.

For video diffusion models, temporal consistency remains a critical challenge. Spatiotemporal Skip Guidance (STG) introduces a training-free sampling guidance method that improves sample quality without sacrificing diversity or dynamic degree \[[4' 2024-11-27](http://arxiv.org/abs/2411.18664v1)\]. Similarly, UniCtrl enhances spatiotemporal consistency in text-to-video models by introducing cross-frame self-attention control and motion injection \[[10' 2024-03-04](http://arxiv.org/abs/2403.02332v4)\]. These methods demonstrate the importance of maintaining temporal coherence in video generation tasks.

In the context of video super-resolution and restoration, methods like Upscale-A-Video and VRT have been proposed to ensure temporal consistency through mechanisms such as flow-guided recurrent latent propagation and parallel frame prediction \[[8' 2023-12-11](http://arxiv.org/abs/2312.06640v1)\]\[[22' 2022-01-28](http://arxiv.org/abs/2201.12288v2)\]. These approaches highlight the need for temporal-aware strategies to maintain quality across frames.
