## Related Works

In the context of in-context learning (ICL), the quality of demonstrations is crucial for the performance of large language models (LLMs) \[[1' 2022-12-01](http://arxiv.org/abs/2212.01189v1)\]. Existing approaches typically rely on perplexity-based methods to detect noisy annotations, assuming that noisy samples yield higher perplexities than their clean counterparts \[[1' 2022-12-01](http://arxiv.org/abs/2212.01189v1)\]. However, this assumption is challenged when the noise ratio is high, as many demonstrations may be flawed, leading to unreliable perplexity estimates \[[1' 2022-12-01](http://arxiv.org/abs/2212.01189v1)\]. 

Several studies have explored the challenges of noisy labels in training deep neural networks. For instance, \[[4' 2024-06-22](http://arxiv.org/abs/2406.16966v1)\] investigates the impact of noisy labels on model generalization and proposes a framework that uses synthetic samples with soft labels to mitigate the effects of label noise. This approach leverages the early learning phase to identify clean samples and gradually correct noisy labels, improving the quality of learned representations \[[4' 2024-06-22](http://arxiv.org/abs/2406.16966v1)\]. Similarly, \[[15' 2022-07-27](http://arxiv.org/abs/2207.13316v2)\] addresses noisy ground-truth annotations in scene graph generation (SGG), arguing that existing models often overlook the quality of annotations, leading to biased training. The proposed NICEST method introduces a dual strategy for label correction and training, enhancing the robustness of SGG models against noisy labels \[[15' 2022-07-27](http://arxiv.org/abs/2207.13316v2)\].

Perplexity-based methods for retrieval have also been scrutinized for their inherent biases. \[[2' 2025-03-11](http://arxiv.org/abs/2503.08684v1)\] highlights that PLM-based retrievers tend to overrate low perplexity documents, attributing this to the positive correlation between perplexity features and relevance estimation. This source bias can distort the retrieval process, particularly when the model is trained on LLM-generated content \[[2' 2025-03-11](http://arxiv.org/abs/2503.08684v1)\]. To address this, \[[2' 2025-03-11](http://arxiv.org/abs/2503.08684v1)\] proposes a causal-inspired debiasing method called CDC, which separates the bias effect from the overall relevance score, improving the fairness of retrieval outcomes \[[2' 2025-03-11](http://arxiv.org/abs/2503.08684v1)\].

In the realm of debiasing techniques, \[[13' 2025-03-01](http://arxiv.org/abs/2503.00548v2)\] introduces a dual debiasing framework for video scene graph generation (VidSGG), addressing both visual and semantic biases. This approach uses memory-enhanced temporal integration to enhance object representations and iteratively integrates semantic information from triplet relationships, resulting in more unbiased scene understanding \[[13' 2025-03-01](http://arxiv.org/abs/2503.00548v2)\]. Similarly, \[[9' 2023-05-18](http://arxiv.org/abs/2305.10736v1)\] presents a counterfactual debiasing framework, CoFactSum, designed to alleviate factual inconsistency in text summarization by estimating counterfactual scenarios and adjusting the debiasing degree dynamically \[[9' 2023-05-18](http://arxiv.org/abs/2305.10736v1)\].

The issue of sample selection bias has also been explored in various domains. \[[6' 2022-12-19](http://arxiv.org/abs/2212.09813v1)\] proposes a Bayesian algorithm to correct for non-random sample selection, integrating large-scale social media data with more reliable survey data to improve representativeness \[[6' 2022-12-19](http://arxiv.org/abs/2212.09813v1)\].

### <a id='category-impact-of-noisy-annotations-on-model-generalization'></a>Impact of Noisy Annotations on Model Generalization: 2 papers found.
The impact of noisy annotations on model generalization has been extensively studied, with recent works highlighting how biased or noisy datasets can lead to poor performance in tasks such as debiasing and scene graph generation. For instance, improperly constructed datasets can result in inaccurate inferences, as models trained on biased datasets often fail to generalize due to dataset bias \[[1' 2022-12-01](http://arxiv.org/abs/2212.01189v1)\]. Similarly, in scene graph generation, existing models have overlooked the quality of ground-truth annotations, assuming all manually annotated positive samples are equally correct and all un-annotated negative samples are absolutely background \[[15' 2022-07-27](http://arxiv.org/abs/2207.13316v2)\]. These assumptions are often invalid, as noisy labels can distort training and lead to biased models. To address this, researchers have proposed novel strategies such as denoising after entropy-based debiasing (DENEB), which aims to improve generalization by mitigating the impact of noisy labels \[[1' 2022-12-01](http://arxiv.org/abs/2212.01189v1)\]. Additionally, the NICEST framework has been introduced to correct noisy labels and improve training strategies in scene graph generation by generating high-quality samples and employing multi-teacher knowledge distillation \[[15' 2022-07-27](http://arxiv.org/abs/2207.13316v2)\]. These approaches demonstrate the growing recognition of the challenges posed by noisy annotations and the need for robust methods to enhance model performance and generalization.

In this paper, we dig deeper into the representation distributions in the early learning phase and find that, regardless of their noisy labels, learned representations of images from the same category still congregate together.
