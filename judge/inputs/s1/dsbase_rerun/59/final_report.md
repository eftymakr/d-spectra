Prior studies have identified that fairness considerations in re-ranking often lead to a decline in ranking accuracy, a phenomenon akin to the economic concept of commodity tax transfer \[[1' 2022-04-17](http://arxiv.org/abs/2204.08085v1)\]. This insight highlights the limitations of current fair re-ranking evaluation methods, which often rely on a single fairness metric, thereby hindering a comprehensive assessment of algorithmic fairness \[[1' 2022-04-17](http://arxiv.org/abs/2204.08085v1)\].

The Elastic Fairness Curve (EF-Curve) concept explores the accuracy-fairness trade-off in machine learning models, emphasizing the need for flexible trade-offs that can adapt to different scenarios and user requirements \[[4' 2025-03-10](http://arxiv.org/abs/2503.07066v1)\]. This idea is rooted in the observation that traditional fairness methods often fix the accuracy-fairness trade-off during training, limiting the model's adaptability to varying fairness constraints or stakeholder priorities \[[4' 2025-03-10](http://arxiv.org/abs/2503.07066v1)\]. Recent work has sought to address this limitation by proposing frameworks that allow for dynamic trade-offs at inference time, such as the You Only Debias Once (YODO) method, which enables a single model to achieve multiple accuracy-fairness trade-offs by learning a "line" in the weight space connecting accuracy-optimum and fairness-optimum points \[[4' 2025-03-10](http://arxiv.org/abs/2503.07066v1)\]. This approach contrasts with earlier methods that required training multiple models to achieve similar flexibility, which was computationally expensive and impractical for real-world deployment \[[4' 2025-03-10](http://arxiv.org/abs/2503.07066v1)\]. Theoretical analyses have also begun to clarify the properties of the accuracy-fairness trade-off, such as the existence of a Pareto frontier (FairFrontier) that defines optimal classifiers, and how factors like the interpretability of sensitive attributes can influence the shape of this frontier \[[5' 2023-10-19](http://arxiv.org/abs/2310.12785v1)\]. Additionally, there is growing recognition of the implicit assumptions underlying fairness-accuracy trade-off research, such as the inherent opposition between accuracy and fairness, the use of strict mathematical equality to model fairness, and the independence of fairness and accuracy from historical context \[[13' 2021-02-01](http://arxiv.org/abs/2102.01203v3)\]. These assumptions, if unexamined, can lead to inconsistent or even counterproductive outcomes in fairness-aware machine learning. The EF-Curve concept, therefore, represents a significant step toward a more nuanced and adaptable understanding of fairness in machine learning, aligning with broader efforts to improve the fairness-accuracy trade-off in re-ranking tasks by incorporating economic principles such as elasticity \[[4' 2025-03-10](http://arxiv.org/abs/2503.07066v1)\].
