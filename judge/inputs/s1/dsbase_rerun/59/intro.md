## Related Works

Fairness in re-ranking has garnered significant attention in recent years, with a growing body of research addressing the trade-offs between accuracy and fairness. Prior studies have identified that fairness considerations in re-ranking often lead to a decline in ranking accuracy, a phenomenon akin to the economic concept of commodity tax transfer \[[1' 2022-04-17](http://arxiv.org/abs/2204.08085v1)\]. This trade-off is further formalized using the notion of elasticity, which measures how changes in fairness constraints affect accuracy \[[1' 2022-04-17](http://arxiv.org/abs/2204.08085v1)\]. This insight highlights the limitations of current fair re-ranking evaluation methods, which often rely on a single fairness metric, thereby hindering a comprehensive assessment of algorithmic fairness \[[1' 2022-04-17](http://arxiv.org/abs/2204.08085v1)\].

Several works have explored fairness-aware re-ranking techniques. For instance, \[[2' 2024-04-27](http://arxiv.org/abs/2404.17826v1)\] introduces a taxation perspective for fair re-ranking, reformulating previous methods as item-level tax policies. However, these policies often fail to meet the requirements of continuity and controllability over accuracy loss \[[2' 2024-04-27](http://arxiv.org/abs/2404.17826v1)\]. To address these limitations, \[[3' 2025-01-24](http://arxiv.org/abs/2501.14710v1)\] proposes Tax-rank, which levies taxes based on the utility difference between items and optimizes the objective using the Sinkhorn algorithm in optimal transport. This approach demonstrates superior performance in terms of effectiveness and efficiency on recommendation and advertising tasks \[[3' 2025-01-24](http://arxiv.org/abs/2501.14710v1)\].

In the context of recommender systems, \[[4' 2025-03-10](http://arxiv.org/abs/2503.07066v1)\] emphasizes the importance of provider fairness, noting that systems may inadvertently favor popular items, marginalizing less popular ones. To address this, \[[5' 2023-10-19](http://arxiv.org/abs/2310.12785v1)\] introduces a post-processing re-ranking model that prioritizes provider fairness while maintaining user relevance and recommendation quality. The model is evaluated across multiple datasets, showing improvements in both provider fairness and recommendation quality \[[5' 2023-10-19](http://arxiv.org/abs/2310.12785v1)\].

The concept of fairness-accuracy trade-offs has also been explored from a causal perspective. \[[6' 2020-05-21](http://arxiv.org/abs/2005.12974v1)\] argues that fairness and predictive performance are not inherently in conflict when measured on unbiased data, suggesting that causal pre-processing methods can resolve these trade-offs \[[6' 2020-05-21](http://arxiv.org/abs/2005.12974v1)\]. Similarly, \[[7' 2023-09-08](http://arxiv.org/abs/2309.04250v1)\] presents a causal framework for analyzing trade-offs between fairness parameters and other crucial metrics, proposing domain-specific optimizations to facilitate accurate causal discovery and a unified interface for trade-off analysis \[[7' 2023-09-08](http://arxiv.org/abs/2309.04250v1)\].

In addition to fairness, the role of user traffic in fairness-accuracy trade-offs has been studied. \[[8' 2024-06-17](http://arxiv.org/abs/2406.11720v2)\] proposes BankFair, a fairness-aware re-ranking approach inspired by the bankruptcy problem in economics. This method ensures consistent user service across varying traffic conditions by leveraging the Talmud rule to determine fairness degrees and conducting online re-ranking based on these degrees \[[8' 2024-06-17](http://arxiv.org/abs/2406.11720v2)\].
