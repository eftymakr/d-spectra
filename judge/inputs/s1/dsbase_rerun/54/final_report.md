## Related Works

Graph Convolutional Networks (GCNs) have become a cornerstone in collaborative filtering (CF) for recommendation systems, due to their ability to model complex interactions between users and items \[[1' 2020-07-07](http://arxiv.org/abs/2007.03383v2)\]. However, several limitations in existing GCN-based approaches have been identified. One major issue is the embedding layer, which often results in a latent space with limited capacity, thereby failing to capture locally observed but potentially valuable preference patterns \[[1' 2020-07-07](http://arxiv.org/abs/2007.03383v2)\]. This limitation is closely related to the design of graph filters, which typically involve a cut-off in the frequency spectrum and a restricted linear form \[[1' 2020-07-07](http://arxiv.org/abs/2007.03383v2)\]. 

To address these limitations, several works have explored alternative graph filtering techniques. For instance, RGCF \[[2' 2021-07-10](http://arxiv.org/abs/2107.04755v3)\] proposes a refined graph convolution approach that improves the expressiveness of user and item embeddings by carefully redesigning the aggregation process. Similarly, AutoGCN \[[3' 2024-01-23](http://arxiv.org/abs/2401.12590v2)\] introduces an automatic graph convolutional network that captures the full spectrum of graph signals and dynamically adjusts the bandwidth of filters. These approaches aim to enhance the modeling of high-order connectivities and reduce information loss during the aggregation process.

Another line of research focuses on the use of polynomial graph filters to process interaction signals. PolyCF \[[4' 2022-03-28](http://arxiv.org/abs/2203.14748v1)\] leverages polynomial graph filters to capture spectral features across multiple eigenspaces, enabling the model to approximate the optimal polynomial response function for recovering missing interactions. This approach is grounded in graph signal processing and is designed to improve the accuracy of collaborative filtering tasks.

In addition to spectral-based methods, some works have explored the use of IIR graph filters based on classical analog filter design methods, such as Butterworth, Chebyshev, and Elliptic functions \[[5' 2018-11-23](http://arxiv.org/abs/1811.10435v1)\]. These filters are designed to achieve more accurate frequency responses compared to autoregressive moving average (ARMA) and linear least-squares fitting (LLS) based methods. The design of these filters is particularly useful for applications requiring precise control over the frequency response.

The method has also been employed in root-finding problems, demonstrating its power in simultaneously calculating all roots of a function within an interval \[[27' 2018-09-24](http://arxiv.org/abs/1810.04282v1)\]. These contributions highlight the versatility and robustness of Chebyshev Polynomial-Based Graph Filters across various domains.

	Document 1: [Snippet]: «Recommender systems need to mirror the complexity of the environment they are applied in. The more we know about what might benefit the user, the more objectives the recommender system has. In addition there may be multiple stakeholders - sellers, buyers, shareholders - in addition to legal and ethical constraints. Simultaneously optimizing for a multitude of objectives, correlated and not correlated, having the same scale or not, has proven difficult so far.
  We introduce a stochastic multi-gradient descent approach to recommender systems (MGDRec) to solve this problem. We show that this exceeds state-of-the-art methods in traditional objective mixtures, like revenue and recall. Not only that, but through gradient normalization we can combine fundamentally different objectives, having diverse scales, into a single coherent framework. We show that uncorrelated objectives, like the proportion of quality products, can be improved alongside accuracy. Through the use of stochasticity, we avoid the pitfalls of calculating full gradients and provide a clear setting for its applicability.»
