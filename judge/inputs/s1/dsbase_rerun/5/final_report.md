## Related Works

Instruction-following text embedding has emerged as a critical area in natural language processing (NLP), aiming to generate dynamic text embeddings that adapt to user instructions and highlight specific attributes of text \[[1' 2025-05-27](http://arxiv.org/abs/2505.21439v1)\]. Prior work has explored various approaches to this task, each with its own strengths and limitations. For instance, \[[3' 2024-02-15](http://arxiv.org/abs/2402.09642v1)\] introduces InBedder, which treats instructions as questions about the input text and encodes the expected answers to obtain the representation. This approach significantly improves instruction-following capabilities, as demonstrated through instruction awareness and robustness tests. However, InBedder requires fine-tuning on abstractive question answering tasks, which can be computationally intensive \[[3' 2024-02-15](http://arxiv.org/abs/2402.09642v1)\].

Another notable work is \[[10' 2024-09-21](http://arxiv.org/abs/2409.14254v1)\], which investigates instruction following without explicit instruction tuning. The authors discover that instruction-response pairs are not strictly necessary for instruction following, suggesting that pre-trained models may already possess some form of instruction-response mapping. However, they also find that training solely on responses can lead to inconsistent behavior when instructions differ from those in the training domain \[[10' 2024-09-21](http://arxiv.org/abs/2409.14254v1)\].

In contrast, \[[2' 2023-04-06](http://arxiv.org/abs/2304.03277v1)\] presents a method where GPT-4 is used to generate instruction-following data for LLM fine-tuning. The generated data leads to superior zero-shot performance on new tasks compared to previous state-of-the-art models. This approach emphasizes the potential of large language models in creating high-quality instruction datasets \[[2' 2023-04-06](http://arxiv.org/abs/2304.03277v1)\].

The work in \[[7' 2023-11-22](http://arxiv.org/abs/2311.13133v1)\] explores the effectiveness of small instruction datasets for instruction tuning, showing that subsets of 1k-6k samples are sufficient to achieve good performance on both traditional NLP benchmarks and model-based evaluations. This suggests that high-quality, diverse datasets can be more effective than large-scale ones \[[7' 2023-11-22](http://arxiv.org/abs/2311.13133v1)\].

Additionally, \[[13' 2023-11-29](http://arxiv.org/abs/2311.17647v2)\] investigates how multimodal large language models can understand textual instructions provided in pixels, despite not being explicitly trained on such data. The study highlights the challenges faced by open-source MLLMs in visual-modality instruction settings and proposes a model capable of robust instruction following in both text and visual modalities \[[13' 2023-11-29](http://arxiv.org/abs/2311.17647v2)\].

Furthermore, \[[14' 2023-04-24](http://arxiv.org/abs/2304.12244v3)\] introduces WizardLM, a model fine-tuned using AI-evolved instructions generated by Evol-Instruct.
