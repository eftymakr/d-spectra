## Related Works

Rehearsal-based methods have been widely studied in continual learning (CL) to mitigate catastrophic forgetting by storing and reusing a subset of past data during training \[[1' 2022-03-28](http://arxiv.org/abs/2203.14544v1)\]. These methods are particularly effective in scenarios where the model must learn from a non-stationary data stream while preserving previously acquired knowledge \[[2' 2020-04-22](http://arxiv.org/abs/2004.10862v1)\]. A common approach is concurrent rehearsal, where past and new data are trained together \[[3' 2021-04-15](http://arxiv.org/abs/2104.07446v1)\]. However, recent work has questioned whether this strategy is always optimal, especially in cases where tasks are dissimilar \[[4' 2018-10-24](http://arxiv.org/abs/1810.10612v1)\]. Inspired by human learning, where revisiting tasks sequentially helps reduce forgetting, there is growing interest in sequential rehearsal, where new data is trained first, followed by revisiting past data \[[5' 2018-10-30](http://arxiv.org/abs/1810.12488v4)\]. This approach has shown potential in improving generalization and reducing forgetting in certain settings \[[6' 2021-09-13](http://arxiv.org/abs/2109.06099v3)\].

Several studies have explored the effectiveness of rehearsal strategies in different CL scenarios. For example, \[[7' 2023-08-20](http://arxiv.org/abs/2308.10328v3)\] highlights the limitations of rehearsal, noting that models trained sequentially with rehearsal may overfit to the stored samples, leading to poor generalization. This suggests that while rehearsal can be beneficial, it is not without its challenges. In contrast, \[[8' 2023-08-25](http://arxiv.org/abs/2308.13646v2)\] proposes a gradient-matching coreset selection method that allows for efficient rehearsal without requiring full model training, which could be particularly useful in resource-constrained settings. The work in \[[9' 2023-03-06](http://arxiv.org/abs/2303.02954v1)\] further explores the issue of domain drift in rehearsal-based methods, proposing a centroid distance distillation approach to reduce the risk of forgetting by aligning representations across tasks.

In addition to rehearsal, other strategies have been proposed to address catastrophic forgetting. These include regularization-based methods, such as elastic weight consolidation, which attempt to preserve important parameters for previously learned tasks \[[10' 2025-04-14](http://arxiv.org/abs/2504.10561v2)\]. However, \[[11' 2018-11-29](http://arxiv.org/abs/1811.12273v1)\] demonstrates that such methods often fail in scenarios where task identity must be inferred, suggesting that replay-based approaches may be more effective. This aligns with findings from \[[12' 2019-04-15](http://arxiv.org/abs/1904.07734v1)\], which show that replaying representations of previous experiences is essential for solving class-incremental learning tasks. Furthermore, \[[13' 2023-12-01](http://arxiv.org/abs/2312.00276v3)\] introduces a metalearning approach that trains neural networks to metalearn their own continual learning algorithms, offering a promising direction for automated continual learning.

Recent work has also explored the use of generative models in rehearsal-based methods. ### <a id='category-rehearsal-based-methods-in-continual-learning'></a>Rehearsal-Based Methods in Continual Learning: 3 papers found.
Rehearsal-based methods in continual learning (CL) focus on mitigating catastrophic forgetting by storing and reusing past data during training. These methods typically rely on a rehearsal memory, which is curated to maintain a small, informative subset of historical data \[[1' 2022-03-28](http://arxiv.org/abs/2203.14544v1)\]. The effectiveness of such approaches depends heavily on how the rehearsal memory is managed, with various strategies proposed to optimize performance. For instance, some methods prioritize sample selection based on prototypicality, such as GRASP, which selects easy samples first and gradually includes harder examples, achieving higher accuracy with fewer updates compared to uniform sampling \[[8' 2023-08-25](http://arxiv.org/abs/2308.13646v2)\]. However, rehearsal-based methods also face challenges, such as the risk of overfitting to the rehearsal buffer, which can hinder generalization \[[3' 2021-04-15](http://arxiv.org/abs/2104.07446v1)\]. Additionally, the theoretical understanding of rehearsal strategies remains limited, with recent work analyzing the performance of concurrent versus sequential rehearsal in overparameterized models, showing that sequential rehearsal can be more effective when tasks are less similar \[[1' 2022-03-28](http://arxiv.org/abs/2203.14544v1)\]. These findings have led to the development of hybrid approaches that combine concurrent and sequential rehearsal based on task similarity \[[1' 2022-03-28](http://arxiv.org/abs/2203.14544v1)\]. Overall, the field highlights the importance of memory management and task dynamics in improving CL performance through rehearsal.

### <a id='category-generative-models-in-rehearsal'></a>Generative Models in Rehearsal: 5 papers found.
Generative models in rehearsal have been increasingly explored to address catastrophic forgetting in continual learning (CL). These models leverage the ability to generate samples from past tasks to maintain performance on previously learned tasks while learning new ones. For instance, variational autoencoders (VAEs) have been used to generate replay samples that help retain past knowledge, thereby mitigating catastrophic forgetting \[[18' 2020-11-24](http://arxiv.org/abs/2011.12216v3)\]. Similarly, probabilistic approaches have been introduced to distinguish between known and unknown data, allowing for more robust learning in the presence of out-of-distribution samples \[[14' 2019-05-28](http://arxiv.org/abs/1905.12019v5)\]. These methods often incorporate variational inference to bound the approximate posterior and ensure that the model retains previously learned representations \[[14' 2019-05-28](http://arxiv.org/abs/1905.12019v5)\]. Additionally, energy-based models (EBMs) have been proposed as a promising framework for continual learning, where the training objective is modified to minimize interference with previously learned information \[[18' 2020-11-24](http://arxiv.org/abs/2011.12216v3)\]. The use of generative replay, where generated samples from past tasks are used during the training of new tasks, has also been shown to significantly alleviate catastrophic interference \[[14' 2019-05-28](http://arxiv.org/abs/1905.12019v5)\]. These approaches highlight the potential of generative models in rehearsal to enhance the performance of continual learning systems by maintaining knowledge over time \[[14' 2019-05-28](http://arxiv.org/abs/1905.12019v5)\]\[[18' 2020-11-24](http://arxiv.org/abs/2011.12216v3)\].
