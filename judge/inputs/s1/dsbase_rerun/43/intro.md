## Related Works

Distributed training of large language models (LLMs) has attracted significant attention due to the increasing complexity of model execution and the need for efficient deployment strategies \[[4' 2020-07-08](http://arxiv.org/abs/2007.03970v1)\]. Various approaches have been proposed to optimize the performance of distributed training systems, with a focus on reducing communication overhead and improving scalability \[[7' 2017-12-05](http://arxiv.org/abs/1712.01887v3)\]. For instance, Deep Gradient Compression (DGC) was introduced to reduce the communication bandwidth required for gradient exchange in distributed training, achieving compression ratios of up to 600x without loss of accuracy \[[7' 2017-12-05](http://arxiv.org/abs/1712.01887v3)\]. This technique is particularly relevant for large-scale distributed training on commodity networks, as it enables efficient training on low-bandwidth infrastructure \[[7' 2017-12-05](http://arxiv.org/abs/1712.01887v3)\].

Similarly, RedSync was developed to reduce synchronization traffic in distributed deep learning by implementing Residual Gradient Compression (RGC), which significantly reduces the message size transmitted between nodes while maintaining convergence speed and accuracy \[[13' 2018-08-13](http://arxiv.org/abs/1808.04357v3)\]. This work highlights the importance of communication efficiency in distributed training, especially for large-scale systems with high communication-to-computation ratios \[[13' 2018-08-13](http://arxiv.org/abs/1808.04357v3)\]. The effectiveness of such techniques is crucial for achieving practical scalability in distributed LLM training.

Performance modeling and prediction are also critical for optimizing distributed training systems. Proteus was introduced as a simulator that models the performance of complex parallelization strategies in distributed deep learning, achieving an average prediction error of 3.0% and significantly reducing prediction error compared to state-of-the-art approaches \[[1' 2023-06-04](http://arxiv.org/abs/2306.02267v1)\]. This work emphasizes the need for accurate performance models to guide optimization efforts and system-level studies \[[1' 2023-06-04](http://arxiv.org/abs/2306.02267v1)\]. LLM-Pilot further contributes to this field by providing a system for characterizing and predicting the performance of LLM inference services across different hardware configurations, achieving a 33% improvement in meeting performance requirements while reducing costs by 60% \[[3' 2024-10-03](http://arxiv.org/abs/2410.02425v1)\].

In addition, the development of performance-portable programming models has been a key focus for enabling efficient execution on diverse hardware architectures. A SYCL implementation of CRK-HACC was evaluated for performance portability across GPUs from different vendors, achieving a portability score of 0.96 with minimal code divergence \[[5' 2023-10-24](http://arxiv.org/abs/2310.16122v1)\]. This demonstrates the potential of portable programming models to simplify development and improve performance across heterogeneous systems \[[5' 2023-10-24](http://arxiv.org/abs/2310.16122v1)\].

Furthermore, the performance of distributed training systems is influenced by the choice of training methods, such as full-graph versus mini-batch training. A comprehensive comparison of these methods revealed that mini-batch training systems converge faster and often achieve similar or higher accuracy than full-graph training systems \[[6' 2024-06-01](http://arxiv.org/abs/2406.00552v4)\].
