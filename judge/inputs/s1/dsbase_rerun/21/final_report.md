## Related Works

Video frame interpolation (VFI) has been extensively studied for its ability to synthesize intermediate frames in video sequences, which is crucial for applications such as video restoration, enhancement, and compression. Early methods relied on optical flow estimation and warping techniques \[[16' 2017-03-22](http://arxiv.org/abs/1703.07514v1)\], but recent advances have leveraged deep learning to improve both accuracy and efficiency. For instance, the work by Zhang et al. \[[1' 2021-12-02](http://arxiv.org/abs/2112.01161v1)\] introduces a general curvilinear motion trajectory formula derived from four consecutive sharp frames or two consecutive blurry frames, enabling VFI without temporal priors. This approach addresses the misalignment issues caused by varying exposure times and frame rates, which are common in real-world scenarios. Additionally, the use of optical flow refinement strategies has been proposed to enhance interpolation quality \[[1' 2021-12-02](http://arxiv.org/abs/2112.01161v1)\].

The field has also seen the development of single-network approaches that reduce the complexity of multi-step pipelines. FILM \[[3' 2022-02-10](http://arxiv.org/abs/2202.04901v4)\] presents a unified network for large motion interpolation, using a multi-scale feature extractor and Gram matrix loss to improve perceptual quality. This method outperforms state-of-the-art approaches on benchmark datasets like Xiph and Vimeo-90K. Similarly, the Adaptive Separable Convolution method \[[6' 2017-08-05](http://arxiv.org/abs/1708.01692v1)\] merges motion estimation and synthesis into a single convolution process, reducing computational demands while maintaining high-quality output. These methods highlight the trend toward more efficient and effective VFI solutions.

In the context of video prediction, the work by Zhang et al. \[[2' 2022-06-27](http://arxiv.org/abs/2206.13454v1)\] proposes an optimization framework that leverages a pretrained differentiable VFI module to predict future frames without requiring additional training data. This approach avoids the domain gap issue between training and test data, making it applicable to a wide range of video types. Furthermore, the use of implicit neural representations (INRs) for VFI has been explored, where optical flow constraints are explicitly enforced to improve interpolation accuracy \[[4' 2022-06-22](http://arxiv.org/abs/2206.10886v1)\]. This method achieves state-of-the-art results on limited motion ranges and demonstrates potential applications in video compression and INR optimization.

Recent studies have also focused on perceptual quality assessment for VFI, proposing no-reference metrics that align with human perception \[[14' 2023-12-25](http://arxiv.org/abs/2312.15659v1)\]. Motivated by the observation that a 3D object should look realistic from multiple viewpoints, these methods introduce a multi-view constraint as regularization to learn valid 3D radiance fields from 2D images. Despite the progress, they often fall short of capturing accurate 3D shapes due to the shape-color ambiguity, limiting their applicability in downstream tasks. In this work, we address this ambiguity by proposing a novel shading-guided generative implicit model that is able to learn a starkly improved shape representation. Our key insight is that an accurate 3D shape should also yield a realistic rendering under different lighting conditions. This multi-lighting constraint is realized by modeling illumination explicitly and performing shading with various lighting conditions. Gradients are derived by feeding the synthesized images to a discriminator. To compensate for the additional computational burden of calculating surface normals, we further devise an efficient volume rendering strategy via surface tracking, reducing the training and inference time by 24% and 48%, respectively. Our experiments on multiple datasets show that the proposed approach achieves photorealistic 3D-aware image synthesis while capturing accurate underlying 3D shapes. We demonstrate improved performance of our approach on 3D shape reconstruction against existing methods, and show its applicability on image relighting.
