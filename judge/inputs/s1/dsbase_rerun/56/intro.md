## Related Works

Cross-domain Sequential Recommendation (CDSR) has gained significant attention in recent years due to its ability to alleviate the cold-start problem and improve recommendation accuracy by leveraging user behavior across multiple domains \[[1' 2023-09-17](http://arxiv.org/abs/2309.09261v1)\]. However, existing methods face two major challenges: the overlap dilemma and transition complexity \[[1' 2023-09-17](http://arxiv.org/abs/2309.09261v1)\]. The overlap dilemma refers to the reliance on users with interactions across all domains to learn cross-domain item relationships, which limits the practicality of these methods \[[1' 2023-09-17](http://arxiv.org/abs/2309.09261v1)\]. Transition complexity arises from the difficulty of modeling complex transition patterns from mixed behavior sequences \[[1' 2023-09-17](http://arxiv.org/abs/2309.09261v1)\]. 

To address these challenges, several studies have explored the use of advanced neural architectures and knowledge transfer mechanisms. For instance, \[[2' 2020-12-01](http://arxiv.org/abs/2012.00485v3)\] proposes a mixed information flow network that considers both behavioral and knowledge transfer to improve recommendation performance across domains. This approach enables the model to decide when and how to use cross-domain information based on user preferences \[[2' 2020-12-01](http://arxiv.org/abs/2012.00485v3)\]. Similarly, \[[3' 2024-06-05](http://arxiv.org/abs/2406.03085v1)\] introduces a dual dynamic graph with hybrid metric training to capture intra- and inter-domain sequential transitions, enhancing prediction accuracy and alleviating data sparsity \[[3' 2024-06-05](http://arxiv.org/abs/2406.03085v1)\]. 

Large Language Models (LLMs) have also been explored for their potential to enhance CDSR by capturing semantic relationships and user preferences. \[[4' 2022-09-21](http://arxiv.org/abs/2209.10163v1)\] demonstrates that initializing state-of-the-art sequential recommendation models with LLM embeddings can improve performance by up to 15-20% \[[4' 2022-09-21](http://arxiv.org/abs/2209.10163v1)\]. Additionally, \[[5' 2025-03-17](http://arxiv.org/abs/2503.13254v1)\] explores the integration of LLMs into cross-domain sequential recommendation by proposing a framework that combines user retrieval and domain grounding to improve performance while preventing out-of-domain generation \[[5' 2025-03-17](http://arxiv.org/abs/2503.13254v1)\]. 

Moreover, \[[6' 2024-11-29](http://arxiv.org/abs/2411.19862v1)\] investigates the use of LLMs for cross-domain recommendation by introducing novel prompt designs that enable LLMs to outperform state-of-the-art baselines in rating prediction and ranking tasks \[[6' 2024-11-29](http://arxiv.org/abs/2411.19862v1)\]. This work highlights the potential of LLMs to serve as effective cross-domain recommenders \[[6' 2024-11-29](http://arxiv.org/abs/2411.19862v1)\]. 

In addition to LLMs, other approaches have focused on improving the modeling of domain and feedback transitions. \[[7' 2024-08-15](http://arxiv.org/abs/2408.08209v2)\] proposes a method that models transitions across both domains and user feedback types, using a transition-aware graph encoder and cross-transition multi-head self-attention to enhance representation learning \[[7' 2024-08-15](http://arxiv.org/abs/2408.08209v2)\]. This approach enables the model to capture the nuances of user behavior across different domains and feedback types \[[7' 2024-08-15](http://arxiv.org/abs/2408.08209v2)\].
