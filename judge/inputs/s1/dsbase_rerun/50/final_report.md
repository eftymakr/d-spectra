## Related Works

The rapid growth of open source machine learning (ML) resources has significantly impacted information retrieval (IR) research, prompting the need for more sophisticated tools to manage and analyze these resources \[[1' 2010-04-20](http://arxiv.org/abs/1004.3566v1)\]. Existing platforms, such as Hugging Face, have been instrumental in providing access to a vast array of ML models and datasets, but they often lack structured representations that enable advanced querying and analysis \[[1' 2010-04-20](http://arxiv.org/abs/1004.3566v1)\]. This limitation hinders tasks such as tracing model evolution and recommending relevant datasets \[[1' 2010-04-20](http://arxiv.org/abs/1004.3566v1)\].

Several studies have explored the use of knowledge graphs (KGs) for managing and analyzing ML resources. For instance, the construction of a KG for ML model zoos has been proposed to standardize metadata representation and improve model search and reuse \[[4' 2022-07-19](http://arxiv.org/abs/2207.09315v1)\]. This aligns with the goal of HuggingKG, which is designed to provide a structured representation of ML resources from the Hugging Face community \[[1' 2010-04-20](http://arxiv.org/abs/1004.3566v1)\]. However, unlike previous efforts, HuggingKG captures domain-specific relations and rich textual attributes, enabling more nuanced analyses \[[1' 2010-04-20](http://arxiv.org/abs/1004.3566v1)\].

In the field of knowledge graph embeddings, research has focused on representing entities and relationships in vector spaces to facilitate better querying and reasoning \[[9' 2020-04-30](http://arxiv.org/abs/2004.14843v1)\]. These embeddings have been applied to various domains, including the enrichment of knowledge graphs from product descriptions \[[5' 2022-06-02](http://arxiv.org/abs/2206.01087v1)\]. The use of named entity recognition (NER) and entity linking (EL) techniques has been explored to extract and link metadata from textual descriptions, improving the completeness and utility of knowledge graphs \[[5' 2022-06-02](http://arxiv.org/abs/2206.01087v1)\]. These techniques are also relevant to the construction of HuggingKG, which relies on structured metadata to enable advanced queries \[[1' 2010-04-20](http://arxiv.org/abs/1004.3566v1)\].

The importance of evaluation metrics in ML model development has been emphasized, particularly in scenarios where the practical value of models is critical \[[3' 2021-12-13](http://arxiv.org/abs/2112.06775v1)\]. This is especially relevant for tasks such as selective classification, where the choice of metrics can significantly impact model performance and usability \[[3' 2021-12-13](http://arxiv.org/abs/2112.06775v1)\]. The benchmarking of ML models using domain-specific metrics has also been explored, as seen in the development of a class-importance weighted F2 score for sewer defect classification \[[2' 2021-03-19](http://arxiv.org/abs/2103.10895v1)\]. These insights are applicable to the evaluation of HuggingKG and the tasks it supports, such as resource recommendation and classification \[[1' 2010-04-20](http://arxiv.org/abs/1004.3566v1)\].

In the context of knowledge management, the integration of standards and standardization frameworks into a knowledge graph has been explored to address semantic interoperability issues \[[11' 2021-07-05](http://arxiv.org/abs/2107.01910v1)\]. This approach has enabled the resolution of conflicts and improved communication in smart factories. Similarly, the construction of HuggingKG aims to enhance semantic interoperability by providing a structured representation of ML resources, facilitating better querying and analysis \[[1' 2010-04-20](http://arxiv.org/abs/1004.3566v1)\].

### <a id='category-semantic-interoperability-in-knowledge-graphs'></a>Semantic Interoperability in Knowledge Graphs: 2 papers found.
The category "Semantic Interoperability in Knowledge Graphs" focuses on the integration of standards and standardization frameworks into knowledge graphs to resolve semantic interoperability issues. This is exemplified by the development of the Industry 4.0 Knowledge Graph (I40KG), which integrates over 200 standards and four standardization frameworks, enabling better communication in smart factories \[[11' 2021-07-05](http://arxiv.org/abs/2107.01910v1)\]. The STO ontology plays a key role in representing properties and relationships among standards, allowing for semantic reasoning and linking across different frameworks \[[11' 2021-07-05](http://arxiv.org/abs/2107.01910v1)\]. This approach has been shown to increase connectivity in the knowledge graph by up to 80%, with up to 96% of discovered relations validated \[[11' 2021-07-05](http://arxiv.org/abs/2107.01910v1)\]. Additionally, the use of automated reasoning and semantic linking helps uncover implicit relationships between standards, enhancing the overall coherence and utility of the knowledge graph \[[11' 2021-07-05](http://arxiv.org/abs/2107.01910v1)\]. These efforts highlight the importance of semantic interoperability in enabling more effective data integration and knowledge sharing in complex industrial environments.

In this paper, we describe an application of existing techniques for enriching the large-scale Fashion Knowledge Graph (FKG) that we build at Farfetch. In particular, we apply techniques for named entity recognition (NER) and entity linking (EL) in order to extract and link rich metadata from product textual descriptions to entities in the FKG. Having a complete and enriched FKG as an e-commerce backbone can have a highly valuable impact on downstream applications such as search and recommendations. However, enriching a Knowledge Graph in the fashion domain has its own challenges. Data representation is different from a more generic KG, like Wikidata and Yago, as entities (e.g. product attributes) are too specific to the domain, and long textual descriptions are not readily available. Data itself is also scarce, as labelling datasets to train supervised models is a very laborious task. Even more, fashion products display a high variability and require an intricate ontology of attributes to link to. We use a transfer learning based approach to train an NER module on a small amount of manually labeled data, followed by an EL module that links the previously identified named entities to the appropriate entities within the FKG. Experiments using a pre-trained model show that it is possible to achieve 89.75% accuracy in NER even with a small manually labeled dataset. Moreover, the EL module, despite relying on simple rule-based or ML models (due to lack of training data), is able to link relevant attributes to products, thus automatically enriching the FKG.»

This work highlights how structured representations can provide deeper insights into the relationships between different graph structures, enabling more precise theoretical analysis. Document 2, on the other hand, focuses on the application of structured representations in natural language processing (NLP) tasks, particularly in Word Sense Disambiguation (WSD). It demonstrates how semantic lexical resources like WordNet and WordNet Domains can be integrated into supervised models to improve performance by incorporating structured semantic features \[[10' 2024-02-20](http://arxiv.org/abs/2402.13302v1)\]. Both documents emphasize the importance of structured representations in advancing IR and NLP research, though they apply these concepts to different domains—combinatorics and language processing, respectively. The integration of structured data, whether through graph-theoretic models or semantic lexicons, enables more nuanced and context-aware processing, which is critical for improving the accuracy and efficiency of IR systems. These contributions align with the broader trend of utilizing structured representations to capture complex relationships and enhance the interpretability of machine learning models in IR research.
