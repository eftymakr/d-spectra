## Related Works

Opinion mining has gained significant traction in recent years, with a primary focus on sentiment polarity classification (positive, negative, neutral) \[[11' 2015-11-07](http://arxiv.org/abs/1511.02385v1)\]. However, the need for fine-grained emotion classification in app reviews has remained underexplored, despite its potential to provide deeper insights into user experiences \[[1' 2024-12-10](http://arxiv.org/abs/2412.07906v1)\]. This section reviews related works that contribute to the fields of sentiment analysis, emotion detection, and the use of large language models (LLMs) in annotation tasks.

Early approaches to sentiment analysis relied on rule-based systems and lexicon-based methods \[[7' 2013-09-11](http://arxiv.org/abs/1309.2853v1)\]. These methods, while effective for basic sentiment classification, lacked the ability to capture the nuanced emotional states expressed in text. As a result, more sophisticated models such as recursive auto-encoders and deep learning approaches have been proposed to better capture the complexity of human emotions \[[3' 2014-12-15](http://arxiv.org/abs/1412.4682v1)\]. For instance, the study by \[[3' 2014-12-15](http://arxiv.org/abs/1412.4682v1)\] introduced a method based on Plutchik's wheel of emotions, which categorizes emotions into eight basic types, offering a more detailed and structured approach to emotion detection.

The use of LLMs for annotation tasks has also been explored, particularly in the context of emotion labeling. GPT-4 has shown high agreement with human annotators in emotion annotation tasks, suggesting its potential as a tool for reducing manual effort \[[1' 2024-12-10](http://arxiv.org/abs/2412.07906v1)\]. However, differences between human and LLM annotations highlight the importance of human input in ensuring the accuracy and reliability of emotion labels \[[1' 2024-12-10](http://arxiv.org/abs/2412.07906v1)\]. This aligns with findings from \[[12' 2020-05-20](http://arxiv.org/abs/2005.09837v1)\], which emphasize the value of human annotations in capturing the subtle nuances of emotional expression.

In the context of app reviews, several studies have focused on sentiment classification and feature extraction. For example, \[[5' 2020-06-17](http://arxiv.org/abs/2006.09739v1)\] applied machine learning algorithms using TF-IDF and ensemble learning to classify app reviews, achieving high accuracy. Similarly, \[[11' 2015-11-07](http://arxiv.org/abs/1511.02385v1)\] proposed a technique for review-level sentiment classification by incorporating sentence-level polarity correction, which improved the consistency of sentiment labels across reviews.

The integration of LLMs into annotation pipelines has also been explored. \[[1' 2024-12-10](http://arxiv.org/abs/2412.07906v1)\] investigated the use of GPT-4 to flag low-quality labels and reduce the workload of human annotators, demonstrating its potential to enhance the efficiency of annotation processes. This aligns with the broader trend of using LLMs to assist in annotation tasks, as seen in \[[10' 2022-04-02](http://arxiv.org/abs/2204.00916v1)\], where BERT was used to refine semantic labels in dialog behavior analysis.
