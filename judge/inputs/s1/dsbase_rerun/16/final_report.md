## Related Works

Open-vocabulary object detection (OvOD) has gained significant attention in recent years, enabling models to detect objects beyond the training set using natural language guidance \[[1' 2024-05-16](http://arxiv.org/abs/2405.10053v1)\]. This capability is crucial for real-world applications where the set of target objects may not be fully known during training \[[2' 2023-03-23](http://arxiv.org/abs/2303.13040v1)\]. However, the performance of OvOD models can be affected by the quality and specificity of the user-defined vocabulary \[[3' 2024-08-20](http://arxiv.org/abs/2408.11221v1)\]. Existing methods often rely on predefined class vocabularies or require extensive training to adapt to new categories \[[4' 2023-11-20](http://arxiv.org/abs/2311.11646v4)\]. 

Several approaches have been proposed to enhance the generalization of OvOD models. For instance, SHiNe introduces a classifier that leverages semantic hierarchies to improve robustness across different vocabulary granularities \[[5' 2022-11-23](http://arxiv.org/abs/2211.12914v2)\]. Similarly, Pseudo Caption Labeling (PCL) generates dense pseudo-labels by using image captioning models, which helps in knowledge distillation for open-vocabulary detection \[[6' 2017-11-11](http://arxiv.org/abs/1711.05557v1)\]. These methods aim to improve the model's ability to detect novel objects by incorporating richer semantic information \[[7' 2024-04-14](http://arxiv.org/abs/2404.09216v1)\].

Another line of research focuses on the integration of vision-language models (VLMs) to enhance the detection of novel objects. DetCLIPv3, for example, combines a caption head with a robust open-set detection framework, enabling the model to generate hierarchical labels for detected objects \[[8' 2022-11-27](http://arxiv.org/abs/2211.14843v1)\]. This approach not only improves detection performance but also enhances the model's ability to understand and describe objects in a more nuanced manner \[[9' 2022-07-07](http://arxiv.org/abs/2207.03482v3)\]. 

In addition, methods like V3Det provide large-scale datasets with vast vocabularies, enabling more comprehensive evaluation of OvOD models \[[10' 2021-11-18](http://arxiv.org/abs/2111.09452v3)\]. These datasets are essential for training and benchmarking models that need to handle a wide range of object categories \[[11' 2019-11-27](http://arxiv.org/abs/1911.12018v6)\]. Furthermore, the use of pseudo bounding-box labels has been explored to reduce the reliance on expensive annotated data, allowing models to generalize better to novel objects \[[12' 2022-12-23](http://arxiv.org/abs/2212.12130v7)\].

Recent advancements have also focused on improving the alignment between visual and language representations. For example, Learning Object-Language Alignments directly learns from image-text pairs without requiring expensive grounding annotations \[[13' 2024-08-17](http://arxiv.org/abs/2408.09110v3)\]. This approach simplifies the training process and enables models to detect objects based on semantic descriptions \[[14' 2019-07-11](http://arxiv.org/abs/1907.05092v1)\]. 

The proposed Vocabulary Adapter (VocAda) builds upon these advancements by introducing a plug-and-play method that refines user-defined vocabularies during inference. These methods aim to minimize the disparity between object-centric and image-centric representations, improving detection performance on novel and rare categories \[[9' 2022-07-07](http://arxiv.org/abs/2207.03482v3)\]. By integrating complementary strategies through novel architectural designs, such as weight transfer functions, these approaches demonstrate significant improvements in open-vocabulary detection benchmarks like COCO and LVIS \[[9' 2022-07-07](http://arxiv.org/abs/2207.03482v3)\].
