## Related Works

Nonverbal communication plays a crucial role in human interaction, encompassing gestures, facial expressions, and body language that convey intent and emotion \[[1' 2020-08-31](http://arxiv.org/abs/2008.13369v1)\]. Existing large language models (LLMs) often lack the ability to effectively integrate these nonverbal cues, limiting their capacity to create immersive conversational experiences \[[1' 2020-08-31](http://arxiv.org/abs/2008.13369v1)\]. This section reviews relevant works that address the integration of nonverbal cues in AI systems, focusing on multimodal models, datasets, and approaches for incorporating nonverbal behavior into language understanding and generation.

Several studies have explored the use of multimodal data to enhance the understanding of human behavior and emotion. For instance, \[[1' 2020-08-31](http://arxiv.org/abs/2008.13369v1)\] presents a novel analysis of the discriminative power of dimensional representations of facial affect for automated deception detection, demonstrating that facial valence and arousal significantly differ between deceptive and truthful speakers. This work highlights the importance of facial affect in modeling human deception and informs future research on affect-aware machine learning approaches \[[1' 2020-08-31](http://arxiv.org/abs/2008.13369v1)\]. Similarly, \[[7' 2018-11-23](http://arxiv.org/abs/1811.09362v2)\] introduces the Recurrent Attended Variation Embedding Network (RAVEN), which dynamically shifts word representations based on nonverbal cues, achieving competitive performance on multimodal sentiment analysis and emotion recognition tasks \[[7' 2018-11-23](http://arxiv.org/abs/1811.09362v2)\].

The integration of nonverbal cues into language models has also been explored in the context of human-robot interaction. \[[10' 2023-04-22](http://arxiv.org/abs/2304.11293v1)\] discusses the importance of nonverbal cues in fostering more natural and accessible human-robot interactions, emphasizing the need to incorporate these cues into AI systems to enhance social intelligence \[[10' 2023-04-22](http://arxiv.org/abs/2304.11293v1)\]. This aligns with \[[19' 2023-07-03](http://arxiv.org/abs/2307.00841v1)\], which evaluates the effectiveness of multimodal communication styles in human-robot interaction, showing that multimodal approaches, including gaze and pointing gestures, lead to more natural fixation behavior and improved engagement \[[19' 2023-07-03](http://arxiv.org/abs/2307.00841v1)\].

In addition to these studies, \[[23' 2022-08-23](http://arxiv.org/abs/2208.10822v1)\] presents a multimodal deep architecture for gaze target detection, demonstrating the potential of combining visual and contextual information to infer where a person is looking in a scene \[[23' 2022-08-23](http://arxiv.org/abs/2208.10822v1)\]. This work highlights the importance of multimodal integration in understanding human behavior and interaction.

Furthermore, \[[11' 2024-10-17](http://arxiv.org/abs/2410.14050v1)\] focuses on learning multimodal cues of children's uncertainty, presenting a dataset annotated with nonverbal cues to study how uncertainty is expressed in real-time video clips.
