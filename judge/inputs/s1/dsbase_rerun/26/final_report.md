## Related Works

Differential privacy (DP) has become the de facto standard for data privacy, offering rigorous guarantees against privacy breaches \[[11' 2019-06-04](http://arxiv.org/abs/1906.01337v6)\]. However, its implementation in distributed settings is vulnerable to malicious behaviors by servers, such as sampling noise from incorrect distributions or generating correlated noise \[[1' 2024-09-16](http://arxiv.org/abs/2409.10667v2)\]. This issue has been explored in several works. For instance, \[[10' 2024-06-27](http://arxiv.org/abs/2406.18940v2)\] discusses the vulnerabilities of local differential privacy (LDP) to malicious clients who can manipulate inputs and outputs to skew results. To address this, \[[10' 2024-06-27](http://arxiv.org/abs/2406.18940v2)\] proposes verifiable LDP (VLDP) schemes that prevent such attacks by assuming authenticated inputs and requiring only one-time interaction between client and server. These schemes are highly practical, with client runtimes under 2 seconds and server runtimes of 5-7 milliseconds per client.

The concept of verifiable differential privacy (VDP) was introduced in \[[2' 2022-08-18](http://arxiv.org/abs/2208.09011v2)\], where the authors propose a framework that requires a zero-knowledge proof to ensure that the output is both differentially private and reliable. This approach allows a verifier to confirm the correctness of the output without learning anything about the randomness used. The work in \[[2' 2022-08-18](http://arxiv.org/abs/2208.09011v2)\] demonstrates that computational assumptions are necessary, as a separation exists between information-theoretic DP and computational DP under verifiability. This aligns with the findings in \[[10' 2024-06-27](http://arxiv.org/abs/2406.18940v2)\], which emphasizes the importance of verification mechanisms in distributed settings.

Secure Multi-Party Computation (MPC) and Noise Sampling: Recent studies have focused on integrating differential privacy (DP) with MPC to achieve privacy-preserving data aggregation in distributed settings \[[1' 2024-09-16](http://arxiv.org/abs/2409.10667v2)\]. The central model of DP relies on a trusted server for data aggregation, whereas the local model requires individuals to add noise, which can significantly reduce the utility of aggregated results \[[1' 2024-09-16](http://arxiv.org/abs/2409.10667v2)\]. To address these limitations, researchers have proposed the distributed model, which combines the utility of the central model with the privacy guarantees of the local model under specific security assumptions \[[1' 2024-09-16](http://arxiv.org/abs/2409.10667v2)\]. A key challenge in this approach is efficiently sampling noise with MPC, as existing secure sampling methods vary in security assumptions and theoretical analyses, with limited experimental evaluations to compare their performance \[[1' 2024-09-16](http://arxiv.org/abs/2409.10667v2)\]. To bridge this gap, recent work has benchmarked existing sampling protocols, extended them to be resilient against Byzantine attackers, and implemented discrete sampling protocols under aligned security settings for fair comparison \[[1' 2024-09-16](http://arxiv.org/abs/2409.10667v2)\]. Additionally, secure multi-party computation-based machine learning (MPL) has emerged as a promising technology for privacy-preserving collaborative learning, but it faces challenges in balancing privacy, efficiency, and accuracy due to the accuracy loss from DP and the communication overhead of MPC protocols \[[6' 2022-08-18](http://arxiv.org/abs/2208.08662v1)\]. To address these issues, researchers have proposed optimized training methods, such as data-independent feature extraction and local data-based global model initialization, to improve the efficiency and accuracy of differentially private models \[[6' 2022-08-18](http://arxiv.org/abs/2208.08662v1)\]. These advancements highlight the ongoing efforts to enhance the practicality of DP in distributed settings through improved noise sampling and secure computation techniques.

### <a id='category-differential-privacy-(dp)-foundations'></a>Differential Privacy (DP) Foundations: 9 papers found.
The category "Differential Privacy (DP) Foundations" encompasses foundational research on differential privacy, including its theoretical underpinnings, variants, and practical implementations. Differential privacy is a formal framework for ensuring privacy in data analysis, providing strong guarantees against adversaries with arbitrary side information \[[2' 2022-08-18](http://arxiv.org/abs/2208.09011v2)\]. It has been extended to address various scenarios, such as Bayesian adversaries and outlier data, with notions like Bayesian differential privacy offering protection even for data far apart in the input space \[[7' 2021-04-05](http://arxiv.org/abs/2104.01987v2)\]. The concept of heterogeneous differential privacy further refines the approach by acknowledging that users have varying privacy expectations, both across individuals and within the same user's data \[[3' 2015-04-27](http://arxiv.org/abs/1504.06998v1)\]. This idea has been applied in matrix factorization for recommendation systems, where privacy budgets are allocated to capture both homogeneous and heterogeneous preferences \[[14' 2022-12-01](http://arxiv.org/abs/2212.00306v2)\]. Additionally, mechanisms such as the Gaussian mechanism and its bounded variant have been developed to handle numerical data while maintaining accuracy and privacy \[[8' 2022-11-30](http://arxiv.org/abs/2211.17230v1)\]. The field has also seen efforts to improve the practicality of differentially private machine learning, including tools for sanity checks, adaptive clipping bounds, and large-batch training to enhance performance \[[18' 2018-12-07](http://arxiv.org/abs/1812.02890v1)\]. These contributions highlight the ongoing evolution of differential privacy to better align with real-world applications and diverse privacy requirements.
