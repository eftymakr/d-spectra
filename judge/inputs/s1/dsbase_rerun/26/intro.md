## Related Works

Differential privacy (DP) has become the de facto standard for data privacy, offering rigorous guarantees against privacy breaches \[[11' 2019-06-04](http://arxiv.org/abs/1906.01337v6)\]. However, its implementation in distributed settings is vulnerable to malicious behaviors by servers, such as sampling noise from incorrect distributions or generating correlated noise \[[1' 2024-09-16](http://arxiv.org/abs/2409.10667v2)\]. This issue has been explored in several works. For instance, \[[10' 2024-06-27](http://arxiv.org/abs/2406.18940v2)\] discusses the vulnerabilities of local differential privacy (LDP) to malicious clients who can manipulate inputs and outputs to skew results. To address this, \[[10' 2024-06-27](http://arxiv.org/abs/2406.18940v2)\] proposes verifiable LDP (VLDP) schemes that prevent such attacks by assuming authenticated inputs and requiring only one-time interaction between client and server. These schemes are highly practical, with client runtimes under 2 seconds and server runtimes of 5-7 milliseconds per client.

The concept of verifiable differential privacy (VDP) was introduced in \[[2' 2022-08-18](http://arxiv.org/abs/2208.09011v2)\], where the authors propose a framework that requires a zero-knowledge proof to ensure that the output is both differentially private and reliable. This approach allows a verifier to confirm the correctness of the output without learning anything about the randomness used. The work in \[[2' 2022-08-18](http://arxiv.org/abs/2208.09011v2)\] demonstrates that computational assumptions are necessary, as a separation exists between information-theoretic DP and computational DP under verifiability. This aligns with the findings in \[[10' 2024-06-27](http://arxiv.org/abs/2406.18940v2)\], which emphasizes the importance of verification mechanisms in distributed settings.

In the context of secure multi-party computation (MPC), \[[1' 2024-09-16](http://arxiv.org/abs/2409.10667v2)\] highlights the challenge of efficiently sampling noise with MPC, noting that existing protocols have different security assumptions and lack experimental evaluations. The authors propose a taxonomy of secure sampling protocols and extend distributed noise generation protocols to be resilient against Byzantine attackers. This work is closely related to the proposed Verifiable Distributed Differential Privacy (VDDP) framework, as both aim to enhance the security and verifiability of DP mechanisms in distributed environments.

Zero-knowledge proofs (ZKPs) have also been explored in relation to DP. \[[20' 2019-11-06](http://arxiv.org/abs/1911.02459v2)\] presents a library for composable zero-knowledge proofs, emphasizing their role in privacy-preserving systems. While ZKPs are sufficient for achieving DP under verifiability requirements, as shown in \[[1' 2024-09-16](http://arxiv.org/abs/2409.10667v2)\], they are not necessary. This is supported by the work in \[[10' 2024-06-27](http://arxiv.org/abs/2406.18940v2)\], which demonstrates that verifiable LDP can be achieved without relying on ZKPs.
