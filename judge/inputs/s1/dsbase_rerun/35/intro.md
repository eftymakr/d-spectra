## Related Works

Protein dynamics are essential for understanding biological functions and properties, traditionally studied through time-consuming molecular dynamics (MD) simulations \[[1' 2024-02-16](http://arxiv.org/abs/2402.10433v2)\]. Recent advances in generative modeling, particularly denoising diffusion models, have enabled efficient and accurate protein structure prediction and conformation sampling by learning distributions over crystallographic structures \[[1' 2024-02-16](http://arxiv.org/abs/2402.10433v2)\]. However, integrating physical supervision into these data-driven approaches remains challenging, as standard energy-based objectives often lead to intractable optimization \[[1' 2024-02-16](http://arxiv.org/abs/2402.10433v2)\]. This section reviews related works that address these challenges and contribute to the field of protein conformation generation and dynamics modeling.

Several studies have explored the use of generative models for protein conformation sampling. For instance, ConfRover introduces an autoregressive model that simultaneously learns protein conformation and dynamics from MD trajectories, supporting both time-dependent and time-independent sampling \[[2' 2025-05-23](http://arxiv.org/abs/2505.17478v2)\]. The model comprises an encoding layer, a temporal module, and an SE(3) diffusion model as the structure decoder \[[2' 2025-05-23](http://arxiv.org/abs/2505.17478v2)\]. This approach enables the generation of both conformations and trajectories within a single framework, offering a flexible solution for learning from protein MD data \[[2' 2025-05-23](http://arxiv.org/abs/2505.17478v2)\].

Similarly, ConfDiff proposes a force-guided SE(3) diffusion model for protein conformation generation, incorporating a force-guided network with a mixture of data-based score models to generate high-fidelity conformations \[[3' 2024-03-21](http://arxiv.org/abs/2403.14088v2)\]. This method improves upon existing score-based diffusion methods by better incorporating physical prior knowledge, leading to more accurate and diverse conformational samples \[[3' 2024-03-21](http://arxiv.org/abs/2403.14088v2)\].

Structure Language Models (SLM) offer an alternative approach by encoding protein structures into a compact latent space using a discrete variational autoencoder, followed by conditional language modeling to capture sequence-specific conformation distributions \[[4' 2024-10-24](http://arxiv.org/abs/2410.18403v2)\]. This enables efficient and interpretable exploration of diverse ensemble modes, achieving a 20-100x speedup compared to existing methods \[[4' 2024-10-24](http://arxiv.org/abs/2410.18403v2)\].

The integration of physical principles into generative models has also been explored through energy-based models (EBMs). For example, EigenFold uses a diffusion generative modeling framework to sample a distribution of structures from a given protein sequence, modeling the structure as a system of harmonic oscillators \[[7' 2023-04-05](http://arxiv.org/abs/2304.02198v1)\]. This approach provides a more comprehensive picture of model uncertainty and has shown promising results on recent CAMEO targets \[[7' 2023-04-05](http://arxiv.org/abs/2304.02198v1)\].
