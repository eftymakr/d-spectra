## Related Works

Zero-Knowledge Proofs (ZKPs) have gained significant attention for their ability to enable secure and private computation, allowing a prover to demonstrate the truth of a statement without revealing additional information \[[1' 2024-12-17](http://arxiv.org/abs/2412.12481v1)\]. This has led to their adoption in various domains, including blockchain technologies, verifiable machine learning, and electronic voting \[[1' 2024-12-17](http://arxiv.org/abs/2412.12481v1)\]. However, the computational complexity of ZKP generation has hindered their widespread adoption \[[1' 2024-12-17](http://arxiv.org/abs/2412.12481v1)\]. Recent efforts have focused on accelerating key primitives of ZKP protocols using hardware, particularly GPUs and ASICs \[[1' 2024-12-17](http://arxiv.org/abs/2412.12481v1)\]. For instance, \[[2' 2024-08-12](http://arxiv.org/abs/2408.05890v1)\] presents SZKP, an ASIC-based accelerator that achieves full-proof speedups of over 400×, 3×, and 12× over CPU, ASIC, and GPU implementations, respectively. This work addresses the challenges of complex dataflow patterns in primitives such as Number Theoretic Transform (NTT) and Multi-scalar Multiplication (MSM) \[[2' 2024-08-12](http://arxiv.org/abs/2408.05890v1)\].

The computational bottleneck in ZKP systems often lies in the MSM operation, which is critical for many ZKP protocols \[[1' 2024-12-17](http://arxiv.org/abs/2412.12481v1)\]. In \[[3' 2023-01-05](http://arxiv.org/abs/2301.02161v2)\], the authors propose an FPGA-based architecture that accelerates MSM computations by leveraging parallelism and optimized modular arithmetic libraries, achieving speedups of 110×–150× compared to reference software libraries \[[3' 2023-01-05](http://arxiv.org/abs/2301.02161v2)\]. Similarly, \[[6' 2024-11-10](http://arxiv.org/abs/2411.06350v1)\] introduces AMAZE, a hardware-optimized framework for the MiMC hash function, which is a ZK-friendly cryptographic primitive. AMAZE achieves over 13× speedup over standard CPU implementations, demonstrating the potential of hardware acceleration for ZK-friendly hashing \[[6' 2024-11-10](http://arxiv.org/abs/2411.06350v1)\].

While hardware acceleration has shown promise, many existing solutions require a trusted setup or generate larger proof sizes, which limits their applicability in scenarios with numerous verifiers or strict verification time constraints \[[1' 2024-12-17](http://arxiv.org/abs/2412.12481v1)\]. For example, \[[7' 2023-10-24](http://arxiv.org/abs/2310.15934v1)\] discusses the trade-offs between redactable signatures and ZKPs in decentralized identity systems, noting that while redactable signatures are faster, ZKPs offer greater privacy but may require trusted setups \[[7' 2023-10-24](http://arxiv.org/abs/2310.15934v1)\]. This highlights the importance of developing ZKP protocols that balance efficiency with security and privacy.

To address these challenges, this work introduces zkSpeed, an accelerator for HyperPlonk, a ZKP protocol that supports one-time, universal setup and small proof sizes \[[1' 2024-12-17](http://arxiv.org/abs/2412.12481v1)\]. By accelerating both SumCheck and MSM primitives, zkSpeed achieves geometric mean speedups of 801× over CPU baselines, demonstrating the potential of full-chip architectures for ZKP acceleration \[[1' 2024-12-17](http://arxiv.org/abs/2412.12481v1)\].

### <a id='category-hardware-acceleration-for-zkp-primitives'></a>Hardware Acceleration for ZKP Primitives: 3 papers found.
Hardware acceleration for zero-knowledge proof (ZKP) primitives has emerged as a critical area of research to address the computational inefficiencies inherent in ZKP generation. Key primitives such as the Number Theoretic Transform (NTT) and Multi-scalar Multiplication (MSM) are particularly resource-intensive, with NTTs involving irregular memory accesses and MSMs requiring data-dependent memory operations \[[1' 2024-12-17](http://arxiv.org/abs/2412.12481v1)\]. Prior efforts have explored GPU and custom RTL implementations to accelerate these operations, but these approaches often struggle with the complex dataflow patterns required for efficient execution \[[2' 2024-08-12](http://arxiv.org/abs/2408.05890v1)\]. To overcome these limitations, SZKP was introduced as the first ASIC to accelerate the entire proof generation process on-chip, leveraging structured dataflows for both NTTs and MSMs \[[1' 2024-12-17](http://arxiv.org/abs/2412.12481v1)\]. This approach achieved significant speedups, with full-proof performance improvements of over 400×, 3×, and 12× compared to CPU, ASIC, and GPU implementations, respectively \[[1' 2024-12-17](http://arxiv.org/abs/2412.12481v1)\]. Similarly, the need for efficient cryptographic hashing in ZKP protocols has led to the development of ZK-friendly hash functions like MiMC, which offer a simple algebraic structure suitable for ZKP applications \[[2' 2024-08-12](http://arxiv.org/abs/2408.05890v1)\]. However, the computational demands of these functions necessitate hardware optimization, prompting the creation of frameworks like AMAZE, which provides highly optimized implementations of MiMC for resource-constrained edge devices \[[2' 2024-08-12](http://arxiv.org/abs/2408.05890v1)\]. These advancements highlight the growing importance of dedicated hardware solutions in enabling practical ZKP applications, particularly in scenarios requiring high throughput and low latency \[[2' 2024-08-12](http://arxiv.org/abs/2408.05890v1)\]. The integration of such accelerators into ZKP protocols, such as HyperPlonk, further demonstrates the potential of hardware-optimized designs to reduce computational overhead and improve scalability \[[3' 2023-01-05](http://arxiv.org/abs/2301.02161v2)\].

### <a id='category-zkp-applications-and-importance'></a>ZKP Applications and Importance: 2 papers found.
Zero-knowledge proofs (ZKPs) have emerged as a critical component in privacy-preserving systems, enabling secure verification without revealing sensitive information \[[3' 2023-01-05](http://arxiv.org/abs/2301.02161v2)\]. Recent advancements have focused on improving the efficiency and practicality of ZKP protocols, particularly in the context of data streams, where the challenge lies in verifying computations that require large space \[[3' 2023-01-05](http://arxiv.org/abs/2301.02161v2)\]. This has led to the development of zero-knowledge streaming interactive proofs (SIPs), which allow a space-bounded algorithm to verify a computation by interacting with a powerful but untrusted prover \[[3' 2023-01-05](http://arxiv.org/abs/2301.02161v2)\]. These SIPs are built on foundational protocols such as the sumcheck and polynomial evaluation, which are widely used in streaming algorithms \[[3' 2023-01-05](http://arxiv.org/abs/2301.02161v2)\]. Additionally, efforts have been made to simplify the implementation of ZKPs, with libraries like zksk providing a Python-based domain-specific language for defining and computing sigma protocols \[[4' 2019-11-06](http://arxiv.org/abs/1911.02459v2)\]. Such tools enable developers to construct complex proofs by combining smaller, reusable components, thereby reducing the complexity of implementing ZKPs \[[4' 2019-11-06](http://arxiv.org/abs/1911.02459v2)\]. These developments highlight the growing importance of ZKPs in various applications, including blockchain, verifiable machine learning, and secure voting systems, while also addressing the computational challenges that have hindered their widespread adoption \[[3' 2023-01-05](http://arxiv.org/abs/2301.02161v2)\]\[[4' 2019-11-06](http://arxiv.org/abs/1911.02459v2)\].
