## Related Works

Zero-Knowledge Proofs (ZKPs) have gained significant attention for their ability to enable secure and private computation, allowing a prover to demonstrate the truth of a statement without revealing additional information \[[1' 2024-12-17](http://arxiv.org/abs/2412.12481v1)\]. This has led to their adoption in various domains, including blockchain technologies, verifiable machine learning, and electronic voting \[[1' 2024-12-17](http://arxiv.org/abs/2412.12481v1)\]. However, the computational complexity of ZKP generation has hindered their widespread adoption \[[1' 2024-12-17](http://arxiv.org/abs/2412.12481v1)\]. Recent efforts have focused on accelerating key primitives of ZKP protocols using hardware, particularly GPUs and ASICs \[[1' 2024-12-17](http://arxiv.org/abs/2412.12481v1)\]. For instance, \[[2' 2024-08-12](http://arxiv.org/abs/2408.05890v1)\] presents SZKP, an ASIC-based accelerator that achieves full-proof speedups of over 400×, 3×, and 12× over CPU, ASIC, and GPU implementations, respectively. This work addresses the challenges of complex dataflow patterns in primitives such as Number Theoretic Transform (NTT) and Multi-scalar Multiplication (MSM) \[[2' 2024-08-12](http://arxiv.org/abs/2408.05890v1)\].

The computational bottleneck in ZKP systems often lies in the MSM operation, which is critical for many ZKP protocols \[[1' 2024-12-17](http://arxiv.org/abs/2412.12481v1)\]. In \[[3' 2023-01-05](http://arxiv.org/abs/2301.02161v2)\], the authors propose an FPGA-based architecture that accelerates MSM computations by leveraging parallelism and optimized modular arithmetic libraries, achieving speedups of 110×–150× compared to reference software libraries \[[3' 2023-01-05](http://arxiv.org/abs/2301.02161v2)\]. Similarly, \[[6' 2024-11-10](http://arxiv.org/abs/2411.06350v1)\] introduces AMAZE, a hardware-optimized framework for the MiMC hash function, which is a ZK-friendly cryptographic primitive. AMAZE achieves over 13× speedup over standard CPU implementations, demonstrating the potential of hardware acceleration for ZK-friendly hashing \[[6' 2024-11-10](http://arxiv.org/abs/2411.06350v1)\].

While hardware acceleration has shown promise, many existing solutions require a trusted setup or generate larger proof sizes, which limits their applicability in scenarios with numerous verifiers or strict verification time constraints \[[1' 2024-12-17](http://arxiv.org/abs/2412.12481v1)\]. For example, \[[7' 2023-10-24](http://arxiv.org/abs/2310.15934v1)\] discusses the trade-offs between redactable signatures and ZKPs in decentralized identity systems, noting that while redactable signatures are faster, ZKPs offer greater privacy but may require trusted setups \[[7' 2023-10-24](http://arxiv.org/abs/2310.15934v1)\]. This highlights the importance of developing ZKP protocols that balance efficiency with security and privacy.

To address these challenges, this work introduces zkSpeed, an accelerator for HyperPlonk, a ZKP protocol that supports one-time, universal setup and small proof sizes \[[1' 2024-12-17](http://arxiv.org/abs/2412.12481v1)\]. By accelerating both SumCheck and MSM primitives, zkSpeed achieves geometric mean speedups of 801× over CPU baselines, demonstrating the potential of full-chip architectures for ZKP acceleration \[[1' 2024-12-17](http://arxiv.org/abs/2412.12481v1)\].
