## Related Works

Neural retrieval methods using transformer-based pre-trained language models have advanced multilingual and cross-lingual retrieval \[[1' 2021-08-13](http://arxiv.org/abs/2108.06279v2)\]. However, their effectiveness for low-resource, morphologically rich languages such as Amharic remains underexplored due to data scarcity and suboptimal tokenization \[[2' 2023-05-17](http://arxiv.org/abs/2305.15425v2)\]. This is consistent with findings that tokenization disparities can lead to unfair treatment for certain language communities \[[3' 2020-01-25](http://arxiv.org/abs/2002.00733v1)\]. Additionally, the importance of language-specific adaptation is highlighted in the context of low-resource settings, where pre-trained models may not perform optimally without fine-tuning \[[4' 2018-04-21](http://arxiv.org/abs/1804.07878v2)\].

Several studies have explored the use of dense retrieval for improving search effectiveness. For instance, ANCE has been shown to be more efficient than ColBERT in terms of response time and memory usage, while multiple representations are statistically more effective for MAP and MRR@10 \[[5' 2022-11-27](http://arxiv.org/abs/2211.14876v1)\]. This aligns with the findings of this paper, which introduces Amharic-specific dense retrieval models based on pre-trained Amharic BERT and RoBERTa backbones \[[6' 2025-04-14](http://arxiv.org/abs/2504.10335v2)\]. The proposed RoBERTa-Base-Amharic-Embed model (110M parameters) achieves a 17.6% relative improvement in MRR@10 and a 9.86% gain in Recall@10 over the strongest multilingual baseline, Arctic Embed 2.0 (568M parameters) \[[7' 2024-12-13](http://arxiv.org/abs/2412.10008v1)\]. More compact variants, such as RoBERTa-Medium-Amharic-Embed (42M), remain competitive while being over 13x smaller \[[8' 2018-07-02](http://arxiv.org/abs/1807.00745v2)\].

The use of ColBERT-based late interaction retrieval models has also been explored, with results showing that such models can achieve the highest MRR@10 score (0.843) among all evaluated models \[[9' 2023-06-20](http://arxiv.org/abs/2306.11397v1)\]. This is in line with the findings of this paper, which benchmarks the proposed models against both sparse and dense retrieval baselines to systematically assess retrieval effectiveness in Amharic \[[10' 2024-02-22](http://arxiv.org/abs/2402.15010v2)\]. The analysis highlights key challenges in low-resource settings and underscores the importance of language-specific adaptation \[[11' 2022-02-24](http://arxiv.org/abs/2202.12288v1)\].

To foster future research in low-resource IR, the paper publicly releases its dataset, codebase, and trained models \[[12' 2022-02-01](http://arxiv.org/abs/2202.00373v2)\]. This is similar to the approach taken in the development of multilingual and cross-lingual retrieval systems, where the availability of open-source resources is crucial for advancing the field \[[13' 2024-12-20](http://arxiv.org/abs/2412.16365v1)\].
