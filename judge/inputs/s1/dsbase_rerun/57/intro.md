## Related Works

Generative retrieval (GR) has gained significant attention as a promising alternative to traditional retrieval paradigms \[[3' 2023-04-09](http://arxiv.org/abs/2304.04171v1)\]. Unlike conventional index-retrieve methods, GR models represent documents as identifiers (docids) and generate these identifiers directly for a given query, enabling end-to-end optimization \[[3' 2023-04-09](http://arxiv.org/abs/2304.04171v1)\]. However, most existing GR models are evaluated on static document collections, and their adaptability to dynamic corpora remains underexplored \[[3' 2023-04-09](http://arxiv.org/abs/2304.04171v1)\]. This section reviews related works that address the challenges of GR in dynamic environments, docid design, and retrieval efficiency.

Early GR approaches rely on training-based indexing, where models are fine-tuned to memorize associations between queries and document identifiers \[[1' 2024-08-04](http://arxiv.org/abs/2408.02152v3)\]. This method, however, suffers from high training costs and limited adaptability to evolving document corpora \[[1' 2024-08-04](http://arxiv.org/abs/2408.02152v3)\]. To address these limitations, Few-Shot GR was proposed, which eliminates the need for training by prompting large language models (LLMs) to generate docids for all documents in a corpus \[[1' 2024-08-04](http://arxiv.org/abs/2408.02152v3)\]. This approach enables rapid indexing and retrieval without retraining, making it suitable for dynamic scenarios \[[1' 2024-08-04](http://arxiv.org/abs/2408.02152v3)\].

The adaptability of GR to dynamic corpora has been explored in several studies. One such work demonstrated that GR models outperform Dual Encoders (DE) in terms of adaptability, robustness, and efficiency, particularly in handling evolving knowledge and temporal information \[[2' 2023-05-27](http://arxiv.org/abs/2305.18952v5)\]. Additionally, the performance of GR in dynamic environments was further validated on the StreamingQA benchmark, showing improvements in retrieval accuracy and computational efficiency \[[2' 2023-05-27](http://arxiv.org/abs/2305.18952v5)\]. These findings highlight the potential of GR for real-world applications where document collections are constantly updated \[[2' 2023-05-27](http://arxiv.org/abs/2305.18952v5)\].

Docid design plays a crucial role in the effectiveness of GR models. Current approaches often rely on fixed rule-based docids, such as titles or BERT embeddings, which may fail to capture the full semantic information of a document \[[3' 2023-04-09](http://arxiv.org/abs/2304.04171v1)\]. To address this, GenRet was proposed, which learns to tokenize documents into short discrete representations (docids) via a discrete auto-encoding approach \[[3' 2023-04-09](http://arxiv.org/abs/2304.04171v1)\]. This method enables end-to-end modeling of document retrieval tasks and achieves state-of-the-art performance on benchmark datasets \[[3' 2023-04-09](http://arxiv.org/abs/2304.04171v1)\].

Another study introduced Term-Set Generation (TSGen), which uses a set of terms as docids instead of sequences, allowing for permutation-invariant decoding and improved reliability during retrieval \[[4' 2023-05-23](http://arxiv.org/abs/2305.13859v3)\]. This approach mitigates the issue of token pruning during decoding and enhances the robustness of GR models \[[4' 2023-05-23](http://arxiv.org/abs/2305.13859v3)\].
