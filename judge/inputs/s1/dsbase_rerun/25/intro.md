## Related Works

The integration of knowledge graphs (KGs) into large language models (LLMs) to enhance reasoning capabilities has gained significant attention in recent years. While LLMs have demonstrated strong reasoning abilities, they often struggle with structured reasoning tasks, particularly those involving multi-step reasoning pipelines and reasoning over KGs without adaptation \[[10' 2023-09-05](http://arxiv.org/abs/2309.02144v1)\]. This limitation has motivated researchers to explore methods that combine the strengths of LLMs with the structured knowledge representation provided by KGs \[[1' 2024-10-15](http://arxiv.org/abs/2410.11588v1)\]\[[2' 2023-06-14](http://arxiv.org/abs/2306.08302v3)\].

Several approaches have been proposed to enhance LLMs using KGs. For instance, \[[1' 2024-10-15](http://arxiv.org/abs/2410.11588v1)\] introduces a KG-based random-walk reasoning approach that leverages causal relationships to improve LLM performance on commonsense question answering tasks. This method demonstrates that incorporating KGs into prompts can significantly enhance reasoning capabilities. Similarly, \[[2' 2023-06-14](http://arxiv.org/abs/2306.08302v3)\] presents a roadmap for unifying LLMs and KGs, highlighting the complementary nature of these two technologies. KGs provide structured, semantically rich representations, while LLMs offer generalization and adaptability. This synergy is crucial for tasks requiring both factual accuracy and reasoning \[[2' 2023-06-14](http://arxiv.org/abs/2306.08302v3)\].

In the domain of medical knowledge, \[[4' 2025-02-28](http://arxiv.org/abs/2502.20988v2)\] reviews efforts to integrate clinical knowledge into LLMs using KGs and retrieval-augmented generation (RAG). This work emphasizes the importance of reliable knowledge sources and the challenges of integrating structured knowledge into LLMs. Furthermore, \[[5' 2021-09-09](http://arxiv.org/abs/2109.04223v2)\] proposes a knowledge-aware language model framework that dynamically incorporates KGs into LLMs, enabling more accurate reasoning on machine reading comprehension tasks. This approach demonstrates the potential of KGs to enhance LLMs in complex reasoning scenarios.

The use of KGs for improving LLMs has also been explored in the context of HR data, where \[[3' 2024-08-24](http://arxiv.org/abs/2408.13521v1)\] presents a framework for developing HR knowledge graphs using LLMs. This work highlights the effectiveness of KGs in tasks such as job matching and employee skill gap identification. Additionally, \[[6' 2024-10-15](http://arxiv.org/abs/2410.12096v1)\] introduces LangGSL, a framework that integrates LLMs and graph structure learning models (GSLMs) to jointly enhance both node feature and graph structure learning. This approach demonstrates the potential of combining LLMs with graph-based models to improve robustness and performance.

In the realm of claim verification, \[[7' 2024-08-26](http://arxiv.org/abs/2408.14317v2)\] reviews recent frameworks that leverage LLMs for automated claim verification. This work highlights the growing interest in using LLMs for fact-checking tasks, particularly with the introduction of Retrieval Augmented Generation (RAG).
