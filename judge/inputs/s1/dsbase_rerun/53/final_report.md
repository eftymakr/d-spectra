## Related Works

Deep sequential recommendation models have seen significant advancements, driven by the need to effectively model user behavior patterns and handle variable-length sequences. Traditional approaches, such as RNNs and attention-based models, have been widely used but face challenges in computational efficiency and capturing diverse interaction patterns \[[16' 2023-10-03](http://arxiv.org/abs/2310.02367v2)\]. For instance, self-attention mechanisms, while powerful in modeling long-range dependencies, suffer from quadratic computational complexity, which becomes prohibitive for long sequences \[[1' 2025-01-02](http://arxiv.org/abs/2501.01242v1)\]. This limitation has motivated the exploration of alternative architectures, such as state space models (SSMs), which offer linear complexity and efficient processing of variable-length sequences \[[6' 2024-03-06](http://arxiv.org/abs/2403.03900v2)\].

Several works have attempted to address these challenges by introducing novel attention mechanisms or integrating SSMs into sequential recommendation frameworks. For example, HydraRec proposes a linear attention mechanism that reduces the complexity of attention computation while preserving temporal context \[[1' 2025-01-02](http://arxiv.org/abs/2501.01242v1)\]. Similarly, MLSA4Rec combines Mamba with low-rank decomposed self-attention to achieve linear complexity and structural bias, enhancing the model's ability to capture both local and global patterns \[[2' 2024-07-18](http://arxiv.org/abs/2407.13135v1)\]. These models demonstrate that hybrid approaches can offer a balance between efficiency and effectiveness.

State space models, particularly Mamba, have also gained traction for their ability to handle long sequences with linear complexity \[[6' 2024-03-06](http://arxiv.org/abs/2403.03900v2)\]. These models highlight the growing interest in SSMs for sequential recommendation tasks.
