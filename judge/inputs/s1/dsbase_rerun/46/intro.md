## Related Works

Cross-domain sequential recommendation (CDSR) has gained significant attention in recent years due to its ability to address data sparsity and cold-start problems in recommender systems by leveraging information from multiple domains \[[1' 2025-04-29](http://arxiv.org/abs/2504.20859v1)\]. Traditional approaches to CDSR often rely on self-attention mechanisms within transformer-based models, incorporating domain-specific modules to enhance performance \[[2' 2022-11-02](http://arxiv.org/abs/2211.01297v4)\]. However, these methods typically overlook the potential of the self-attention module itself, which is a core component of the transformer architecture \[[3' 2024-10-17](http://arxiv.org/abs/2410.13588v1)\].

Several works have attempted to improve CDSR by modifying the self-attention mechanism. For instance, \[[4' 2022-12-08](http://arxiv.org/abs/2212.04120v1)\] introduces a self-attentive sequential recommendation model that uses cheap causal convolutions to capture local item interactions and improve sequence embeddings. Similarly, \[[5' 2023-05-31](http://arxiv.org/abs/2305.19798v2)\] proposes a method based on asymmetric Kernel Singular Value Decomposition (KSVD) to enhance self-attention by formulating a primal-dual representation. These approaches focus on improving the attention mechanism but do not explicitly address the challenge of cross-domain knowledge transfer \[[6' 2024-12-31](http://arxiv.org/abs/2502.15694v2)\].

In contrast, \[[7' 2020-12-01](http://arxiv.org/abs/2012.00485v3)\] proposes a model that integrates item image information to better capture visual preferences, demonstrating the effectiveness of incorporating side information. However, this approach is limited to specific types of data and does not generalize well to all domains \[[8' 2024-02-15](http://arxiv.org/abs/2402.09784v2)\]. Another line of work, \[[9' 2023-11-14](http://arxiv.org/abs/2311.08272v1)\], focuses on modeling mixed information flow by considering both behavioral and knowledge transfer between domains, which aligns with the idea of dynamic knowledge exchange in AutoCDSR \[[10' 2023-10-07](http://arxiv.org/abs/2310.04633v3)\].

The importance of temporal context in sequential recommendation has also been explored. For example, \[[11' 2022-09-21](http://arxiv.org/abs/2209.10163v1)\] introduces a model that uses contrastive learning and self-attention to consider temporal proximities both across and within user-item interactions. This work highlights the need for temporal modeling in CDSR, which is also addressed in AutoCDSR by dynamically minimizing cross-domain attention scores \[[12' 2024-10-28](http://arxiv.org/abs/2410.21048v1)\].

Moreover, \[[13' 2023-12-16](http://arxiv.org/abs/2312.10325v2)\] investigates the issue of oversmoothing in self-attention for sequential recommendation and proposes a method that leverages Fourier transforms to inject an inductive bias and integrate low and high-frequency information. This work is closely related to the idea of enhancing self-attention in CDSR, which is central to AutoCDSR \[[14' 2023-11-08](http://arxiv.org/abs/2311.04590v6)\].

In addition, \[[15' 2025-04-25](http://arxiv.org/abs/2504.18383v1)\] explores the use of large language models (LLMs) to bridge items and capture user preferences from a semantic perspective, which is a promising direction for improving cross-domain recommendations. However, this approach requires substantial computational resources and is not as lightweight as AutoCDSR
