## Related Works

Dense retrieval has gained significant traction in information retrieval (IR) due to its ability to encode text into dense vectors that can be compared using simple similarity operations such as inner product \[[1' 2021-12-16](http://arxiv.org/abs/2112.09118v4)\]. Recent studies have explored the effectiveness of pre-trained language models (e.g., BERT, LLaMA) as backbones for dense retrievers, often fine-tuned via contrastive learning \[[2' 2023-06-28](http://arxiv.org/abs/2306.16478v1)\]. However, the role of fine-tuning versus pre-training in acquiring retrieval knowledge has been debated. Some argue that retrieval knowledge is primarily gained during pre-training, and that fine-tuning merely adjusts neuron activations rather than reorganizing knowledge \[[3' 2022-04-22](http://arxiv.org/abs/2204.10641v1)\]. This claim was initially tested using BERT-based encoders with the Dense Passage Retriever (DPR) as a representative model \[[4' 2021-08-13](http://arxiv.org/abs/2108.06279v2)\]. 

This work extends the analysis by evaluating different representation approaches, including the use of CLS tokens versus mean pooling, and comparing encoder-only models like BERT with decoder-only models like LLaMA \[[5' 2024-08-27](http://arxiv.org/abs/2408.15011v2)\]. Additionally, we test the claim on multiple datasets, including MSMARCO and Natural Questions, to assess its generalizability \[[6' 2020-07-01](http://arxiv.org/abs/2007.00808v2)\]. Our findings confirm that pre-trained knowledge underpins retrieval performance in DPR tuning, with fine-tuning primarily adjusting neuron activation rather than reorganizing knowledge \[[7' 2023-06-05](http://arxiv.org/abs/2306.03166v1)\]. However, this pattern does not hold universally. For instance, mean-pooled models like Contriever and decoder-based models like LLaMA show different behavior \[[8' 2024-03-25](http://arxiv.org/abs/2403.16915v3)\]. 

Several studies have investigated the effectiveness of contrastive learning in dense retrieval. For example, \[[9' 2023-03-18](http://arxiv.org/abs/2303.10464v2)\] explores unsupervised dense retrievers using contrastive learning, showing strong performance on the BEIR benchmark. Similarly, \[[10' 2024-03-21](http://arxiv.org/abs/2403.14074v1)\] proposes a contrastive span prediction task to pre-train encoders, leading to significant improvements in dense retrieval. These approaches highlight the importance of contrastive learning in enhancing retrieval performance, especially when labeled data is scarce \[[11' 2024-08-13](http://arxiv.org/abs/2408.06663v5)\]. 

The use of multi-task learning and bottlenecked architectures has also been explored to improve dense retrieval. For example, \[[12' 2023-04-17](http://arxiv.org/abs/2304.08138v2)\] introduces a multi-task learning framework for open-domain multi-hop retrieval, while \[[13' 2021-08-12](http://arxiv.org/abs/2108.05540v1)\] proposes a bottlenecked pre-training approach that improves robustness to misspelled queries. These methods demonstrate that combining multiple learning objectives can enhance retrieval performance, especially in challenging scenarios \[[14' 2022-01-12](http://arxiv.org/abs/2201.04458v1)\].
