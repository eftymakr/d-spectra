## Related Works

Dense retrieval has gained significant traction in information retrieval (IR) due to its ability to encode text into dense vectors that can be compared using simple similarity operations such as inner product \[[1' 2021-12-16](http://arxiv.org/abs/2112.09118v4)\]. Recent studies have explored the effectiveness of pre-trained language models (e.g., BERT, LLaMA) as backbones for dense retrievers, often fine-tuned via contrastive learning \[[2' 2023-06-28](http://arxiv.org/abs/2306.16478v1)\]. However, the role of fine-tuning versus pre-training in acquiring retrieval knowledge has been debated. Some argue that retrieval knowledge is primarily gained during pre-training, and that fine-tuning merely adjusts neuron activations rather than reorganizing knowledge \[[3' 2022-04-22](http://arxiv.org/abs/2204.10641v1)\]. This claim was initially tested using BERT-based encoders with the Dense Passage Retriever (DPR) as a representative model \[[4' 2021-08-13](http://arxiv.org/abs/2108.06279v2)\]. 

This work extends the analysis by evaluating different representation approaches, including the use of CLS tokens versus mean pooling, and comparing encoder-only models like BERT with decoder-only models like LLaMA \[[5' 2024-08-27](http://arxiv.org/abs/2408.15011v2)\]. Additionally, we test the claim on multiple datasets, including MSMARCO and Natural Questions, to assess its generalizability \[[6' 2020-07-01](http://arxiv.org/abs/2007.00808v2)\]. Our findings confirm that pre-trained knowledge underpins retrieval performance in DPR tuning, with fine-tuning primarily adjusting neuron activation rather than reorganizing knowledge \[[7' 2023-06-05](http://arxiv.org/abs/2306.03166v1)\]. However, this pattern does not hold universally. For instance, mean-pooled models like Contriever and decoder-based models like LLaMA show different behavior \[[8' 2024-03-25](http://arxiv.org/abs/2403.16915v3)\]. 

Several studies have investigated the effectiveness of contrastive learning in dense retrieval. Similarly, \[[10' 2024-03-21](http://arxiv.org/abs/2403.14074v1)\] proposes a contrastive span prediction task to pre-train encoders, leading to significant improvements in dense retrieval. These approaches highlight the importance of contrastive learning in enhancing retrieval performance, especially when labeled data is scarce \[[11' 2024-08-13](http://arxiv.org/abs/2408.06663v5)\]. 

The use of multi-task learning and bottlenecked architectures has also been explored to improve dense retrieval. ### <a id='category-contrastive-learning-in-retrieval-models'></a>Contrastive Learning in Retrieval Models: 7 papers found.
Contrastive learning has emerged as a powerful paradigm for training dense retrievers, enabling unsupervised or semi-supervised learning in scenarios with limited training data \[[1' 2021-12-16](http://arxiv.org/abs/2112.09118v4)\]. This approach leverages contrastive loss functions to learn meaningful representations by contrasting relevant and irrelevant examples, thereby improving retrieval performance across various settings \[[6' 2020-07-01](http://arxiv.org/abs/2007.00808v2)\]. Recent studies have shown that contrastive learning can outperform traditional term-frequency methods like BM25 on multiple benchmark datasets, particularly when evaluated on the BEIR benchmark, where unsupervised models achieve strong results on 11 out of 15 datasets for Recall@100 \[[1' 2021-12-16](http://arxiv.org/abs/2112.09118v4)\]. Moreover, contrastive learning enables cross-lingual and cross-script retrieval, allowing models to retrieve English documents from Arabic queries, which is not feasible with term-based methods \[[1' 2021-12-16](http://arxiv.org/abs/2112.09118v4)\]. However, challenges remain, such as the need for representative negative examples during training, which can be addressed through methods like Approximate Nearest Neighbor Negative Contrastive Estimation (ANCE), which constructs more realistic negative instances by leveraging an ANN index \[[6' 2020-07-01](http://arxiv.org/abs/2007.00808v2)\]. Additionally, relevance-aware contrastive learning has been proposed to improve the quality of pseudo-positive examples by estimating the relevance of positive pairs during training, leading to consistent improvements on benchmarks like BEIR and open-domain QA \[[7' 2023-06-05](http://arxiv.org/abs/2306.03166v1)\]. These advancements highlight the versatility and effectiveness of contrastive learning in dense retrieval, though further research is needed to address limitations in multi-task learning and distribution shifts between training and target scenarios \[[10' 2024-03-21](http://arxiv.org/abs/2403.14074v1)\].

It employs a simple bottleneck architecture that learns to compress the passage information into a dense vector through self-supervised pre-training. We use a replaced language modeling objective, which is inspired by ELECTRA, to improve the sample efficiency and reduce the mismatch of the input distribution between pre-training and fine-tuning. SimLM only requires access to unlabeled corpus, and is more broadly applicable when there are no labeled data or queries. We conduct experiments on several large-scale passage retrieval datasets
