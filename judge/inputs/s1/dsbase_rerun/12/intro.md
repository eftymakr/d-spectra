## Related Works

Event cameras, which capture scene changes asynchronously on a per-pixel basis, offer significant advantages in temporal resolution over traditional frame-based cameras \[[12' 2020-03-02](http://arxiv.org/abs/2003.00847v2)\]. However, this high temporal resolution comes with challenges such as high bandwidth, memory, and computational demands \[[12' 2020-03-02](http://arxiv.org/abs/2003.00847v2)\]. Prior work has addressed these issues by exploring event downsampling techniques. Most existing approaches rely on fixed heuristics or threshold-based strategies, which limit their adaptability to different applications \[[12' 2020-03-02](http://arxiv.org/abs/2003.00847v2)\]. These methods often fail to account for scene-specific variations and can lead to suboptimal performance when applied to diverse tasks \[[12' 2020-03-02](http://arxiv.org/abs/2003.00847v2)\].

To overcome these limitations, recent research has proposed adaptive and context-aware event sampling strategies. For example, \[[6' 2024-05-28](http://arxiv.org/abs/2405.17769v1)\] introduced a system that uses event cameras to achieve fast localization and data streaming, mitigating issues related to screen refresh rates and camera motion. Similarly, \[[10' 2025-04-25](http://arxiv.org/abs/2504.18235v2)\] presented a benchmark for tuning the biases of event cameras, emphasizing the importance of optimal parameter settings for performance. These works highlight the need for dynamic and scene-adaptive sampling strategies.

In the field of probabilistic modeling, \[[3' 2012-12-03](http://arxiv.org/abs/1212.0582v1)\] proposed an implicit adaptive importance sampling method that iteratively matches moments of Monte Carlo draws to weighted moments based on importance weights. This approach is particularly useful for complex distributions that are not available in closed form. The method has been applied to Bayesian leave-one-out cross-validation, demonstrating better performance than existing parametric methods while being computationally inexpensive \[[3' 2012-12-03](http://arxiv.org/abs/1212.0582v1)\]. This work aligns with the idea of using probabilistic frameworks to model event importance, which is central to our proposed POLED framework.

Additionally, \[[11' 2012-12-05](http://arxiv.org/abs/1212.0967v1)\] explored the use of relational database schemata to automatically construct Bayesian graphical models, demonstrating the potential of probabilistic modeling in capturing complex dependencies. While not directly related to event cameras, this work supports the broader application of probabilistic models in adaptive sampling and scene understanding.

In the context of event-based vision, \[[8' 2018-07-25](http://arxiv.org/abs/1807.09480v2)\] proposed attention mechanisms for object recognition, emphasizing the importance of focusing on regions of interest. These attention-based methods have been applied to various datasets, improving translation and scale invariance in event-based vision tasks. Similarly, \[[12' 2020-03-02](http://arxiv.org/abs/2003.00847v2)\] explored the use of event cameras for deblurring and generating high frame rate video, demonstrating the potential of event-based data in high-speed applications.
