## Related Works

Multi-token prediction (MTP) has emerged as a promising pre-training objective for language models, offering potential improvements in downstream performance, inference speed, and training efficiency \[[7' 2024-04-30](http://arxiv.org/abs/2404.19737v1)\]. Unlike traditional next-token prediction (NTP), which predicts only the immediate next token, MTP extends the prediction horizon by predicting the next $k$ tokens at each step using multiple prediction heads \[[7' 2024-04-30](http://arxiv.org/abs/2404.19737v1)\]. This approach has shown significant benefits in both text and code generation tasks, particularly for large models, where it enables faster inference and better performance \[[2' 2024-10-23](http://arxiv.org/abs/2410.17765v2)\]. However, prior studies have demonstrated that smaller language models (SLMs) face challenges in effectively leveraging the MTP objective \[[7' 2024-04-30](http://arxiv.org/abs/2404.19737v1)\].

To address these limitations, curriculum learning has been proposed as a strategy to gradually introduce complexity during pre-training \[[1' 2021-08-04](http://arxiv.org/abs/2108.02170v1)\]. Curriculum learning, originally explored in computer vision and machine translation, involves structuring the training process to improve model performance and training speed \[[1' 2021-08-04](http://arxiv.org/abs/2108.02170v1)\]. In the context of MTP, two curriculum variants have been investigated: a forward curriculum, which incrementally increases the complexity from NTP to MTP, and a reverse curriculum, which does the opposite \[[7' 2024-04-30](http://arxiv.org/abs/2404.19737v1)\]. The forward curriculum enables SLMs to better utilize the MTP objective during pre-training, leading to improved downstream NTP performance and enhanced generative output quality, while retaining the benefits of self-speculative decoding \[[7' 2024-04-30](http://arxiv.org/abs/2404.19737v1)\]. In contrast, the reverse curriculum achieves stronger NTP performance and output quality but fails to provide any self-speculative decoding advantages \[[7' 2024-04-30](http://arxiv.org/abs/2404.19737v1)\].

Several prior works have explored multi-token prediction and its implications for language models. For instance, L-MTP introduces a leap-based mechanism that allows models to predict non-sequential tokens in a single forward pass, improving inference efficiency and capturing long-range dependencies \[[3' 2025-05-23](http://arxiv.org/abs/2505.17505v2)\]. Similarly, the Future Token Prediction (FTP) approach generates embedding vectors that better represent the overall meaning of longer sequences, leading to improved topic coherence and performance on coding tasks \[[6' 2024-10-23](http://arxiv.org/abs/2410.18160v1)\]. Additionally, joint multi-token prediction (JTP) has been proposed as a lightweight modification to standard next-token prediction, enriching hidden state representations by jointly predicting multiple future tokens \[[8' 2025-03-24](http://arxiv.org/abs/2503.21801v1)\].

The effectiveness of curriculum learning in MTP training has also been supported by research in reinforcement learning, where curriculum learning has been used to shape exploration by presenting increasingly complex tasks \[[5' 2019-01-31](http://arxiv.org/abs/1901.11478v3)\]. This aligns with the findings in the current work, where curriculum learning strategies are shown to improve the performance of SLMs when training with the MTP objective \[[7' 2024-04-30](http://arxiv.org/abs/2404.19737v1)\].
