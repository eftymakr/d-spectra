## Related Works

Multi-Domain Recommendation (MDR) has emerged as a critical area of research in recommender systems, aiming to leverage knowledge across multiple domains to improve recommendation performance \[[1' 2024-11-11](http://arxiv.org/abs/2411.06826v1)\]. Early approaches often relied on simple parameter sharing between domains, which led to domain interference and limited adaptability \[[3' 2023-09-12](http://arxiv.org/abs/2309.06217v2)\]. More recently, Mixture-of-Experts (MOE) architectures have gained popularity due to their ability to model complex domain interactions \[[1' 2024-11-11](http://arxiv.org/abs/2411.06826v1)\]. However, these methods typically activate all experts for each instance, resulting in scalability issues and poor discriminability between domains and experts \[[1' 2024-11-11](http://arxiv.org/abs/2411.06826v1)\]. 

To address these limitations, several works have introduced more flexible and scalable architectures. For instance, HAMUR proposes a domain-specific adapter and a domain-shared hyper-network to dynamically adapt to domain differences while capturing shared information \[[3' 2023-09-12](http://arxiv.org/abs/2309.06217v2)\]. Similarly, the Adaptive Domain Interest Network (ADI) employs shared and domain-specific networks to model commonalities and diversities across scenarios, along with domain-specific batch normalization and an interest adaptation layer to improve performance \[[7' 2022-06-20](http://arxiv.org/abs/2206.09672v1)\]. These approaches highlight the need for dynamic and adaptive mechanisms to handle domain-specific characteristics.

Negative Transfer Problem (NTP) is another critical challenge in transfer learning and MDR. NTP occurs when knowledge from a less related source negatively impacts the target task, often due to domain mismatch or poor quality data \[[5' 2018-11-24](http://arxiv.org/abs/1811.09751v4)\]. In the context of MDR, unselectively leveraging information from all domains can lead to significant performance degradation \[[1' 2024-11-11](http://arxiv.org/abs/2411.06826v1)\]. Several studies have explored the causes and mitigation strategies of NTP. For example, \[[5' 2018-11-24](http://arxiv.org/abs/1811.09751v4)\] formally defines negative transfer and proposes a technique to filter out unrelated source data using adversarial networks. This approach has been shown to significantly improve performance across multiple transfer learning methods \[[5' 2018-11-24](http://arxiv.org/abs/1811.09751v4)\].

In addition to addressing NTP, recent work has focused on improving the efficiency and scalability of MDR systems. MAMDR, a model-agnostic framework, introduces Domain Negotiation and Domain Regularization strategies to alleviate domain conflicts and improve generalizability \[[6' 2022-02-25](http://arxiv.org/abs/2202.12524v4)\]. This framework is designed to be flexible and scalable, enabling it to handle thousands of domains without requiring domain-specific expertise. The integration of these techniques into a unified framework demonstrates the growing interest in developing robust and efficient MDR systems \[[6' 2022-02-25](http://arxiv.org/abs/2202.12524v4)\].

Moreover, the importance of domain adaptation and knowledge transfer has been emphasized in various studies. For example, \[[11' 2020-02-19](http://arxiv.org/abs/2003.00820v1)\] introduces MADAN, a multi-source domain adaptation framework that aligns features across different domains at the pixel level and aggregates sub-domain information to improve adaptation performance. These methods underscore the need for robust domain adaptation

Furthermore, M3oE extends MDR to multi-task settings by integrating three MOE modules to disentangle complex dependencies across domains and tasks \[[8' 2024-04-29](http://arxiv.org/abs/2404.18465v3)\]. These approaches collectively highlight the ongoing efforts to model domain relationships effectively and avoid negative transfer, as emphasized in the proposed SDSP method \[[1' 2024-11-11](http://arxiv.org/abs/2411.06826v1)\].
