## Related Works

3D human pose estimation has seen significant advancements in recent years, with a growing emphasis on modeling long-range dependencies between body parts. Traditional approaches often rely on convolutional architectures \[[1' 2021-03-18](http://arxiv.org/abs/2103.10455v3)\], while more recent methods have explored the use of transformers for their ability to capture global dependencies \[[2' 2024-07-14](http://arxiv.org/abs/2407.10099v2)\]. However, these methods often struggle with accurately modeling the complex spatial and temporal relationships inherent in human motion.

Several works have attempted to address the challenges of modeling long-range dependencies in 3D human pose estimation. For instance, PoseFormer \[[1' 2021-03-18](http://arxiv.org/abs/2103.10455v3)\] introduces a purely transformer-based approach that captures spatial and temporal correlations in videos. This method, however, does not explicitly model the interactions between body parts or the hierarchical structure of the human skeleton. Similarly, STGFormer \[[2' 2024-07-14](http://arxiv.org/abs/2407.10099v2)\] proposes a spatio-temporal graph attention mechanism to better leverage the inherent graph priors of the human body. While this approach improves the modeling of spatiotemporal dependencies, it still lacks a structured way to capture the hierarchical and cross-scale relationships between body parts.

The problem of modeling long-range dependencies is further explored in the work of IGANet \[[3' 2023-04-27](http://arxiv.org/abs/2304.14045v1)\], which introduces an interweaved graph and attention network to allow bidirectional communication between graph convolutional networks and attention mechanisms. This approach helps in capturing both local and global correlations, but it does not explicitly address the challenge of capturing cross-scale dependencies in a structured manner.

In contrast, the work of \[[4' 2021-11-01](http://arxiv.org/abs/2111.00950v1)\] introduces a higher-order graph convolutional framework with initial residual connections to capture long-range dependencies between body joints. This method improves the modeling of complex joint relationships but does not explicitly address the challenge of capturing hierarchical dependencies or the need for lightweight models.

Recent efforts have also focused on integrating attention mechanisms with graph-based models to better capture the spatial and temporal structure of human motion. For example, the work of \[[5' 2025-05-02](http://arxiv.org/abs/2505.01003v1)\] proposes a method that combines graph modeling capabilities with attention mechanisms to represent each skeleton with multiple graphs of different orders. This approach dynamically emphasizes the most representative orders for each joint, but it does not explicitly address the challenge of modeling cross-scale dependencies in a structured manner.

The challenge of capturing long-range dependencies is further explored in the work of \[[6' 2024-12-27](http://arxiv.org/abs/2412.19676v1)\], which introduces a dual-stream model that integrates local features with global dependencies to enhance 3D human pose estimation. This approach improves the modeling of both local and global dependencies but does not explicitly address the challenge of capturing cross-scale dependencies in a structured manner.
