## Related Works

Narrative summarization remains a challenging task due to the complexity of long-form texts, including intricate plotlines, character interactions, and thematic coherence \[[1' 2022-05-19](http://arxiv.org/abs/2205.09641v2)\]. Existing large language models (LLMs) often struggle with maintaining coherence and consistency in generated summaries, especially when dealing with long narratives such as books, movies, and TV scripts \[[5' 2022-02-03](http://arxiv.org/abs/2202.01709v1)\]. Several works have attempted to address these challenges by introducing novel frameworks and evaluation metrics.

One such effort is SNaC, which introduces a narrative coherence evaluation framework rooted in fine-grained annotations for long summaries \[[1' 2022-05-19](http://arxiv.org/abs/2205.09641v2)\]. This work provides the first characterization of coherence errors generated by state-of-the-art summarization models and develops a protocol for eliciting coherence judgments from crowd annotators. Additionally, it demonstrates the ability to train a strong classifier for automatically localizing coherence errors in generated summaries \[[1' 2022-05-19](http://arxiv.org/abs/2205.09641v2)\]. This is crucial for improving the quality of narrative summaries, as coherence is essential for readability and comprehension.

Another relevant work is CompRes, which introduces a dataset for narrative structure in news articles, focusing on the detection of narrative elements such as complication and resolution \[[3' 2020-07-09](http://arxiv.org/abs/2007.04874v2)\]. This dataset is used to train supervised models to identify narrative elements, achieving an $F_1$ score of up to 0.7 \[[3' 2020-07-09](http://arxiv.org/abs/2007.04874v2)\]. While this work is focused on news articles, it highlights the importance of structured annotation schemes for improving the quality of narrative understanding.

The MLD-EA model further addresses the issue of narrative coherence by leveraging large language models (LLMs) to identify narrative gaps and generate coherent sentences that integrate seamlessly with the story's emotional and logical flow \[[8' 2024-12-03](http://arxiv.org/abs/2412.02897v1)\]. This model demonstrates that LLMs can serve as effective logic checkers in story writing, ensuring logical coherence and emotional consistency. This aligns with the goals of NexusSum, which also emphasizes the importance of coherence in narrative summarization.

In addition, the Skeleton-Based Model for Promoting Coherence Among Sentences in Narrative Story Generation introduces a method to generate more coherent text by first generating critical phrases (skeletons) and then expanding them into complete sentences \[[11' 2018-08-21](http://arxiv.org/abs/1808.06945v2)\]. This approach improves the coherence of generated stories, as measured by both human evaluation and automatic evaluation. This is similar to the Dialogue-to-Description Transformation in NexusSum, which standardizes character dialogue and descriptive text to improve coherence \[[2' 2024-12-28](http://arxiv.org/abs/2412.20138v7)\].
