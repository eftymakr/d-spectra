## Related Works

Understanding the evolution of feature representations across layers in large language models (LLMs) is crucial for improving interpretability and robustness \[[1' 2021-12-17](http://arxiv.org/abs/2112.09305v1)\]. Recent studies have identified critical layers linked to specific functions or behaviors, but these efforts typically rely on data-dependent analyses of fine-tuned models, limiting their use to post-hoc settings \[[2' 2024-11-22](http://arxiv.org/abs/2411.15382v2)\]. In contrast, our approach introduces a data-oblivious method to identify intrinsic critical layers in pre-fine-tuned LLMs by analyzing representation dynamics via Centered Kernel Alignment (CKA) \[[3' 2018-12-19](http://arxiv.org/abs/1812.08238v3)\]. We demonstrate that layers with significant shifts in representation space are also those most affected during fine-tuning—a pattern that holds consistently across tasks for a given model \[[4' 2024-01-22](http://arxiv.org/abs/2401.11824v4)\]. Our spectral analysis further reveals that these shifts are driven by changes in the top principal components, which encode semantic transitions from rationales to conclusions \[[5' 2024-06-03](http://arxiv.org/abs/2406.01563v2)\].

The concept of representation alignment has been explored in various contexts. For instance, Gaussian RBF Centered Kernel Alignment (CKA) has been shown to converge to linear CKA in the large-bandwidth limit, with convergence onset sensitive to the geometry of the feature representations \[[6' 2025-05-01](http://arxiv.org/abs/2505.03793v3)\]. This aligns with our findings that representation shifts are closely tied to the dynamics of principal components. Furthermore, the application of kernel methods to nonlinear demixed component analysis has demonstrated their utility in capturing complex, non-linear structures in neural population data \[[7' 2024-06-05](http://arxiv.org/abs/2406.02913v1)\]. Similarly, the use of kernel methods in knowledge distillation has been investigated, with recent work proposing a novel Relation-Centered Kernel Alignment (RCKA) framework that establishes a connection between CKA and Maximum Mean Discrepancy (MMD) \[[8' 2021-02-12](http://arxiv.org/abs/2102.06605v2)\].

In the realm of fine-tuning, several approaches have been proposed to enhance task-specific performance while mitigating challenges such as catastrophic forgetting and privacy risks \[[9' 2025-01-31](http://arxiv.org/abs/2501.19389v4)\]. For example, localized fine-tuning on LLM representations (LoFiT) has been introduced as an effective alternative to representation intervention methods, identifying a subset of attention heads that are most important for learning a specific task \[[10' 2023-09-01](http://arxiv.org/abs/2309.00363v1)\]. This aligns with our focus on identifying critical layers that are most affected during fine-tuning. Additionally, zeroth-order fine-tuning with extreme sparsity has been explored, integrating sparsity and quantization to enable efficient fine-tuning on resource-constrained devices \[[11' 2024-02-28](http://arxiv.org/abs/2402.18571v3)\]. These methods highlight the ongoing efforts to improve the efficiency and effectiveness of fine-tuning strategies.

### <a id='category-kernel-methods-in-neural-networks'></a>Kernel Methods in Neural Networks: 7 papers found.
Kernel Methods in Neural Networks have been extensively explored for their ability to capture nonlinear structures in data and improve the interpretability of complex models. Recent work has demonstrated that Centered Kernel Alignment (CKA) based on Gaussian RBF kernels converges to linear CKA in the large-bandwidth limit, with convergence influenced by the geometry of feature representations \[[1' 2021-12-17](http://arxiv.org/abs/2112.09305v1)\]. This insight has been leveraged to analyze representation dynamics in large language models (LLMs), where significant shifts in representation space across layers correlate with layers most affected during fine-tuning \[[2' 2024-11-22](http://arxiv.org/abs/2411.15382v2)\]. Nonlinear extensions of traditional dimensionality reduction techniques, such as kernel demixed PCA (kdPCA), have also been introduced to handle neurally relevant nonlinearities, enabling the recovery of interpretable components in neural population recordings \[[3' 2018-12-19](http://arxiv.org/abs/1812.08238v3)\]. Additionally, kernel-based clustering algorithms, like kernel k-means, have been adapted with approximation schemes to handle large datasets more efficiently, improving both performance and computational feasibility \[[15' 2014-02-16](http://arxiv.org/abs/1402.3849v1)\]. These methods highlight the versatility of kernel techniques in addressing challenges related to model interpretability, data structure, and computational efficiency in neural networks.

### <a id='category-fine-tuning-strategies-and-efficiency'></a>Fine-Tuning Strategies and Efficiency: 10 papers found.
Fine-tuning strategies for large language models (LLMs) have been extensively explored to enhance task-specific performance while addressing challenges such as catastrophic forgetting and privacy risks \[[1' 2021-12-17](http://arxiv.org/abs/2112.09305v1)\]. Recent approaches like Reinforcement Learning with Human Feedback (RLHF), supervised fine-tuning (SFT), and Quantized Low-Rank Adapters (Q-LoRA) have shown significant improvements in model performance \[[1' 2021-12-17](http://arxiv.org/abs/2112.09305v1)\]. However, these methods often require careful calibration to maintain the broader reasoning capabilities of LLMs \[[1' 2021-12-17](http://arxiv.org/abs/2112.09305v1)\]. For instance, localized fine-tuning methods such as LoFiT have emerged as efficient alternatives, allowing for targeted modifications to a sparse subset of attention heads, thereby improving truthfulness and reasoning without extensive parameter updates \[[2' 2024-11-22](http://arxiv.org/abs/2411.15382v2)\]. Similarly, zeroth-order optimization (ZO) has been adapted for memory-constrained settings by integrating sparsity and quantization, enabling efficient fine-tuning of a minimal subset of parameters \[[3' 2018-12-19](http://arxiv.org/abs/1812.08238v3)\]. Contrastive self-supervised learning (CSL) has also been leveraged in fine-tuning, with Core-tuning proposed to enhance discriminative representation learning through contrastive loss optimization and hard pair mining \[[4' 2024-01-22](http://arxiv.org/abs/2401.11824v4)\]. In federated learning settings, methods like federated sketching LoRA (FSLoRA) have been developed to address resource heterogeneity by enabling clients to selectively update submatrices of global LoRA modules \[[5' 2024-06-03](http://arxiv.org/abs/2406.01563v2)\]. Additionally, instruction tuning has been studied to optimize the mixing of instruction datasets, revealing that different instruction types can influence performance across various tasks \[[8' 2021-02-12](http://arxiv.org/abs/2102.06605v2)\]. These strategies highlight the ongoing efforts to balance performance gains with computational efficiency and model robustness during fine-tuning.
