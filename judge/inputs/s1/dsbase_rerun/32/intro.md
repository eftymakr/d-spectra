## Related Works

Understanding the evolution of feature representations across layers in large language models (LLMs) is crucial for improving interpretability and robustness \[[1' 2021-12-17](http://arxiv.org/abs/2112.09305v1)\]. Recent studies have identified critical layers linked to specific functions or behaviors, but these efforts typically rely on data-dependent analyses of fine-tuned models, limiting their use to post-hoc settings \[[2' 2024-11-22](http://arxiv.org/abs/2411.15382v2)\]. In contrast, our approach introduces a data-oblivious method to identify intrinsic critical layers in pre-fine-tuned LLMs by analyzing representation dynamics via Centered Kernel Alignment (CKA) \[[3' 2018-12-19](http://arxiv.org/abs/1812.08238v3)\]. We demonstrate that layers with significant shifts in representation space are also those most affected during fine-tuning—a pattern that holds consistently across tasks for a given model \[[4' 2024-01-22](http://arxiv.org/abs/2401.11824v4)\]. Our spectral analysis further reveals that these shifts are driven by changes in the top principal components, which encode semantic transitions from rationales to conclusions \[[5' 2024-06-03](http://arxiv.org/abs/2406.01563v2)\].

The concept of representation alignment has been explored in various contexts. For instance, Gaussian RBF Centered Kernel Alignment (CKA) has been shown to converge to linear CKA in the large-bandwidth limit, with convergence onset sensitive to the geometry of the feature representations \[[6' 2025-05-01](http://arxiv.org/abs/2505.03793v3)\]. This aligns with our findings that representation shifts are closely tied to the dynamics of principal components. Furthermore, the application of kernel methods to nonlinear demixed component analysis has demonstrated their utility in capturing complex, non-linear structures in neural population data \[[7' 2024-06-05](http://arxiv.org/abs/2406.02913v1)\]. Similarly, the use of kernel methods in knowledge distillation has been investigated, with recent work proposing a novel Relation-Centered Kernel Alignment (RCKA) framework that establishes a connection between CKA and Maximum Mean Discrepancy (MMD) \[[8' 2021-02-12](http://arxiv.org/abs/2102.06605v2)\].

In the realm of fine-tuning, several approaches have been proposed to enhance task-specific performance while mitigating challenges such as catastrophic forgetting and privacy risks \[[9' 2025-01-31](http://arxiv.org/abs/2501.19389v4)\]. For example, localized fine-tuning on LLM representations (LoFiT) has been introduced as an effective alternative to representation intervention methods, identifying a subset of attention heads that are most important for learning a specific task \[[10' 2023-09-01](http://arxiv.org/abs/2309.00363v1)\]. This aligns with our focus on identifying critical layers that are most affected during fine-tuning. Additionally, zeroth-order fine-tuning with extreme sparsity has been explored, integrating sparsity and quantization to enable efficient fine-tuning on resource-constrained devices \[[11' 2024-02-28](http://arxiv.org/abs/2402.18571v3)\]. These methods highlight the ongoing efforts to improve the efficiency and effectiveness of fine-tuning strategies.
