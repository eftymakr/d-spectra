## Related Works

Brain tumor segmentation in medical imaging has been extensively studied, with a growing emphasis on leveraging pre-trained models and transfer learning to improve performance while reducing training costs. Early approaches relied on manual segmentation or traditional machine learning methods, which were limited by the lack of large, annotated datasets and the complexity of medical images \[[24' 2019-09-13](http://arxiv.org/abs/1909.06337v1)\]. With the advent of deep learning, convolutional neural networks (CNNs) have become the dominant approach for automated segmentation, particularly in multi-modal MRI data \[[22' 2018-09-17](http://arxiv.org/abs/1809.06191v2)\]. These models often require extensive training, which is computationally expensive and time-consuming \[[25' 2018-11-21](http://arxiv.org/abs/1811.08883v1)\].

To address these challenges, researchers have explored transfer learning, where pre-trained models from natural image domains are fine-tuned for medical tasks \[[26' 2023-06-30](http://arxiv.org/abs/2306.17555v2)\]. For instance, pre-training on ImageNet has shown promise in improving segmentation performance, although the effectiveness varies depending on the domain shift between natural and medical images \[[26' 2023-06-30](http://arxiv.org/abs/2306.17555v2)\]. This has led to the development of specialized pre-training strategies, such as using synthetic data or domain-specific datasets to better align the feature spaces \[[26' 2023-06-30](http://arxiv.org/abs/2306.17555v2)\]. Additionally, parameter-efficient fine-tuning (PEFT) techniques have emerged as a way to reduce the computational burden while maintaining performance, by only adjusting a small number of parameters during adaptation \[[2' 2023-10-04](http://arxiv.org/abs/2310.03059v8)\].

Recent work has also focused on improving the efficiency of 3D CNNs for brain tumor segmentation, as 3D data is more representative of the biological structure but poses challenges in terms of computational resources \[[7' 2020-11-22](http://arxiv.org/abs/2011.11052v1)\]. Techniques such as axial-coronal-sagittal convolutions and the integration of pre-trained weights from ImageNet have been proposed to reduce training epochs and improve performance \[[1' 2021-05-31](http://arxiv.org/abs/2106.00116v4)\]. These approaches aim to preserve the learned relationships and feature representations critical for effective information propagation, ensuring that the benefits of pre-training are retained in the target domain \[[1' 2021-05-31](http://arxiv.org/abs/2106.00116v4)\].

Another line of research has explored the use of multi-modal CNNs, where different imaging modalities are combined to enhance segmentation accuracy \[[22' 2018-09-17](http://arxiv.org/abs/1809.06191v2)\]. This is particularly relevant in brain tumor segmentation, where multi-parametric MRI data provides complementary information. Furthermore, the use of attention mechanisms and variational autoencoders has been investigated to improve segmentation quality and reduce overfitting \[[21' 2020-11-04](http://arxiv.org/abs/2011.02881v2)\]. ### <a id='category-parameter-efficiency-in-deep-learning'></a>Parameter Efficiency in Deep Learning: 3 papers found.
Parameter efficiency in deep learning has gained significant attention, particularly in the context of fine-tuning pre-trained models for downstream tasks. Recent works have focused on minimizing the adaptation cost by introducing Parameter-Efficient Fine-Tuning (PEFT) techniques, which aim to reduce the number of trainable parameters while maintaining performance \[[2' 2023-10-04](http://arxiv.org/abs/2310.03059v8)\]. For instance, Point-PEFT was introduced to address the challenge of adapting 3D pre-trained models by freezing most parameters and only tuning newly added modules, such as a Point-prior Prompt and a Geometry-aware Adapter \[[2' 2023-10-04](http://arxiv.org/abs/2310.03059v8)\]. These modules leverage domain-specific knowledge and spatial interactions to enhance model performance with minimal parameter updates. Similarly, the Hub-Pathway framework was proposed to enable knowledge transfer from a model hub, which contains diverse pre-trained models, by generating data-dependent pathway weights and assigning optimal routes for knowledge aggregation \[[6' 2022-06-08](http://arxiv.org/abs/2206.03726v2)\]. This approach allows for efficient utilization of multiple models, reducing computational costs and improving transfer performance. Another line of research, such as TransTailor, focuses on not only tuning weights but also modifying the model structure to better match the target task, achieving improved performance with smaller models \[[27' 2021-03-02](http://arxiv.org/abs/2103.01542v1)\]. These approaches collectively highlight the importance of parameter efficiency in deep learning, emphasizing the need for methods that balance performance, computational cost, and adaptability across different domains.
