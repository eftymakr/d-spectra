## Related Works

The task of identifying and steering behaviors in large language models (LLMs) has garnered significant attention in recent years. Traditional methods often rely on predefined templates or manual analysis to detect behaviors such as refusal \[[9' 2023-09-05](http://arxiv.org/abs/2309.02144v1)\]. However, these approaches are limited in their ability to generalize across different models and alignment scenarios. Recent work has explored the use of cosine similarity to measure the similarity between embeddings, which has been shown to be effective in various contexts \[[1' 2023-06-23](http://arxiv.org/abs/2306.14920v1)\]\[[2' 2024-06-16](http://arxiv.org/abs/2406.10984v3)\]\[[16' 2024-06-02](http://arxiv.org/abs/2406.00638v1)\]. For instance, \[[1' 2023-06-23](http://arxiv.org/abs/2306.14920v1)\] demonstrates that cosine similarity between test features and typical in-distribution (ID) features can serve as a good indicator of out-of-distribution (OOD) data. Similarly, \[[2' 2024-06-16](http://arxiv.org/abs/2406.10984v3)\] explores the use of normalized ICA-transformed embeddings to enhance the interpretability of cosine similarity. These studies highlight the potential of cosine similarity as a powerful tool for understanding and manipulating model behavior.

In the context of alignment, \[[9' 2023-09-05](http://arxiv.org/abs/2309.02144v1)\] introduces an alignment fine-tuning (AFT) paradigm that addresses the issue of assessment misalignment in large language models. This approach involves fine-tuning models with chain-of-thought (COT) training data, generating multiple COT responses, and calibrating scores using a constraint alignment loss. This method not only improves reasoning capabilities but also ensures that positive scores surpass negative ones, promoting high-quality COTs. The work in \[[9' 2023-09-05](http://arxiv.org/abs/2309.02144v1)\] is particularly relevant to our approach, as it emphasizes the importance of aligning model outputs with human expectations without relying on predefined templates.

Furthermore, \[[16' 2024-06-02](http://arxiv.org/abs/2406.00638v1)\] proposes a hybrid retrieval strategy that integrates cosine similarity and cosine distance measures to improve retrieval performance, especially for sparse data. This approach demonstrates the effectiveness of combining similarity and distance measures to capture both semantic and dissimilarity relationships between vectors. The use of cosine distance in conjunction with cosine similarity can provide a more comprehensive understanding of the relationships between documents or items, which is crucial for steering models effectively.

In addition, \[[17' 2022-03-28](http://arxiv.org/abs/2203.14996v1)\] explores the use of an extended cosine similarity measure to improve performance on word similarity tasks. This study shows that contextualized similarity measures can lead to significant improvements in performance, particularly when the word-similarity pairs share the same context.
