## Related Works

Calibration of neural network predictions is a critical aspect of ensuring reliable model outputs, particularly in applications where the model's confidence must align with its actual accuracy \[[1' 2019-08-14](http://arxiv.org/abs/1908.05164v3)\]. Traditional calibration approaches often rely on predefined transformation functions with order-preserving properties, such as piece-wise linear functions, to adjust predictions \[[1' 2019-08-14](http://arxiv.org/abs/1908.05164v3)\]. However, these methods are limited in their expressiveness and flexibility, which restricts their effectiveness in complex calibration scenarios \[[1' 2019-08-14](http://arxiv.org/abs/1908.05164v3)\]. This limitation has motivated the development of more flexible calibration techniques that can learn arbitrary monotonic functions while preserving the relative order of predictions \[[1' 2019-08-14](http://arxiv.org/abs/1908.05164v3)\].

Unconstrained Monotonic Neural Networks (UMNNs) have been introduced as a promising approach to address these limitations. UMNNs ensure monotonicity by enforcing the positiveness of the network's output, allowing the model to learn arbitrary monotonic functions with high modeling power \[[2' 2023-03-19](http://arxiv.org/abs/2303.10761v1)\]. This approach significantly relaxes the constraints on the calibrator, improving its flexibility and expressiveness while avoiding excessive distortion of the original predictions \[[1' 2019-08-14](http://arxiv.org/abs/1908.05164v3)\]. The use of UMNNs for calibration has been explored in the context of autoregressive flows, where they have demonstrated effectiveness in density estimation tasks \[[2' 2023-03-19](http://arxiv.org/abs/2303.10761v1)\].

To further optimize the flexibility of UMNNs for calibration, a novel loss function called Smooth Calibration Loss (SCLoss) has been introduced. SCLoss aims to fulfill a necessary condition for achieving the ideal calibration state by encouraging the model to learn smooth and accurate calibration mappings \[[1' 2019-08-14](http://arxiv.org/abs/1908.05164v3)\]. This loss function has been shown to enhance the calibration performance of the model, particularly in scenarios where the original predictions require fine-grained adjustments \[[1' 2019-08-14](http://arxiv.org/abs/1908.05164v3)\].

The importance of calibration in practical applications has also been emphasized in various studies. For example, the calibration of neural networks is essential for tasks such as confidence estimation, where the model's confidence must closely reflect its true accuracy \[[3' 2023-05-30](http://arxiv.org/abs/2305.18764v2)\]. This has led to the development of various calibration techniques, including post-processing methods and training-based approaches \[[3' 2023-05-30](http://arxiv.org/abs/2305.18764v2)\]. Additionally, the need for calibration in large-scale systems, such as video ranking systems, has been highlighted, where the calibration improvements can translate into tangible business benefits \[[1' 2019-08-14](http://arxiv.org/abs/1908.05164v3)\].
