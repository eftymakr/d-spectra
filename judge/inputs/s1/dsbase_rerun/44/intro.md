## Related Works

Collaborative filtering (CF) has been a cornerstone in recommendation systems, with recent advancements leveraging graph neural networks (GNNs) to model user-item interactions more effectively \[[1' 2022-03-29](http://arxiv.org/abs/2203.15789v1)\]. Early works focused on link prediction for CF, but the trend has shifted towards aggregating information from user and item nodes rather than directly modeling links \[[1' 2022-03-29](http://arxiv.org/abs/2203.15789v1)\]. This shift has led to the development of models like NGCF, LightGCN, and GTN, which utilize graph structures to capture complex interactions \[[1' 2022-03-29](http://arxiv.org/abs/2203.15789v1)\]. However, these models often overlook the importance of link prediction in improving recommendation accuracy \[[1' 2022-03-29](http://arxiv.org/abs/2203.15789v1)\].

Binarized collaborative filtering has emerged as a promising approach to enhance efficiency in online recommendation systems, particularly when dealing with implicit feedback \[[2' 2019-06-05](http://arxiv.org/abs/1906.01829v1)\]. These methods aim to reduce computational overhead by converting embeddings into binary representations, but they often suffer from significant information loss due to numerical quantization \[[2' 2019-06-05](http://arxiv.org/abs/1906.01829v1)\]. To mitigate this, researchers have proposed frameworks that convert binary constrained optimization problems into continuous ones, allowing for more efficient training with solvers like SGD and Adam \[[2' 2019-06-05](http://arxiv.org/abs/1906.01829v1)\].

Contrastive learning has also been applied to graph collaborative filtering to address data sparsity issues \[[3' 2022-02-13](http://arxiv.org/abs/2202.06200v2)\]. However, existing methods typically construct contrastive pairs through random sampling, which may miss important neighboring relationships \[[3' 2022-02-13](http://arxiv.org/abs/2202.06200v2)\]. To overcome this, Neighborhood-enriched Contrastive Learning (NCL) was introduced, incorporating both structural and semantic neighbors to enhance the learning process \[[3' 2022-02-13](http://arxiv.org/abs/2202.06200v2)\]. This approach has shown significant performance gains on benchmark datasets like Yelp and Amazon-book \[[3' 2022-02-13](http://arxiv.org/abs/2202.06200v2)\].

In addition to contrastive learning, other approaches have focused on disentangling user intents to improve representation quality \[[8' 2020-07-03](http://arxiv.org/abs/2007.01764v1)\]. Disentangled Graph Collaborative Filtering (DGCF) models aim to separate different user intents, leading to more interpretable and accurate recommendations \[[8' 2020-07-03](http://arxiv.org/abs/2007.01764v1)\]. This is achieved by iteratively refining intent-aware interaction graphs and encouraging independence among different intents \[[8' 2020-07-03](http://arxiv.org/abs/2007.01764v1)\].

Furthermore, multi-graph convolution collaborative filtering has been proposed to integrate multiple graphs in the embedding learning process, capturing both high-order and proximal information \[[6' 2020-01-01](http://arxiv.org/abs/2001.00267v1)\]. This approach constructs user-user and item-item graphs alongside the user-item interaction graph, leading to more comprehensive representations \[[6' 2020-01-01](http://arxiv.org/abs/2001.00267v1)\]. The effectiveness of such models has been validated on multiple benchmarks, demonstrating significant improvements over existing methods \[[6' 2020-01-01](http://arxiv.org/abs/2001.00267v1)\].

In the context of behavior data imbalance, multi-behavior graph collaborative filtering has been explored to leverage information from various user actions like clicks, carts, and purchases \[[10' 2023-11-12](http://arxiv.org/abs/2311.06777v1)\].
