## Related Works

Recent advancements in Text-to-Speech (TTS) systems have significantly enhanced speech naturalness, but challenges remain in achieving precise prosody control and mispronunciation correction \[[1' 2020-08-07](http://arxiv.org/abs/2008.03388v2)\]. Traditional approaches for prosody manipulation often rely on specialized modules or additional training, which limits their capacity for post-hoc adjustments \[[1' 2020-08-07](http://arxiv.org/abs/2008.03388v2)\]. Similarly, conventional mispronunciation correction methods depend on grapheme-to-phoneme (G2P) dictionaries, making them less practical in low-resource settings \[[1' 2020-08-07](http://arxiv.org/abs/2008.03388v2)\]. This section reviews related works that address these challenges, focusing on methods for prosody control, mispronunciation detection, and correction.

Several studies have explored methods for prosody control in TTS. Controllable neural prosody synthesis has been proposed to allow users to input prosody constraints for certain time frames and generate the remaining time frames from input text and contextual prosody \[[2' 2024-08-13](http://arxiv.org/abs/2408.06827v1)\]. PRESENT, a zero-shot text-to-prosody control method, exploits explicit prosody prediction in FastSpeech2-based models by modifying the inference process directly \[[3' 2021-10-06](http://arxiv.org/abs/2110.02854v1)\]. This approach enables subphoneme-level control, a first in the field \[[3' 2021-10-06](http://arxiv.org/abs/2110.02854v1)\]. Hierarchical prosody modeling in non-autoregressive parallel neural TTS has also been proposed to enable intuitive and meaningful prosody control \[[4' 2021-08-12](http://arxiv.org/abs/2108.05517v2)\]. These methods, however, often require additional training or specialized modules, limiting their flexibility for post-hoc adjustments \[[1' 2020-08-07](http://arxiv.org/abs/2008.03388v2)\].

Mispronunciation detection and correction have also been extensively studied. Masked acoustic unit methods have been proposed to detect and correct mispronunciations by using acoustic units as an intermediary feature \[[5' 2021-10-06](http://arxiv.org/abs/2110.02952v2)\]. This approach provides speech-based self-imitating feedback, enhancing the effectiveness of Computer-Assisted Pronunciation Training (CAPT) \[[5' 2021-10-06](http://arxiv.org/abs/2110.02952v2)\]. Additionally, mispronunciation detection using self-supervised speech representations has been explored, where models trained on non-native speech data outperform traditional methods \[[6' 2020-11-12](http://arxiv.org/abs/2011.06465v3)\]. These approaches, however, often rely on extensive annotation or specific domain knowledge, which can be impractical in low-resource settings \[[1' 2020-08-07](http://arxiv.org/abs/2008.03388v2)\].

Recent work on zero-shot text-to-speech synthesis has focused on conditioning on self-supervised speech representation models to improve speaker characteristics and rhythm transfer \[[7' 2022-06-24](http://arxiv.org/abs/2206.12229v2)\]. This method uses large-scale data to obtain embedding vectors directly from speech representations, enabling better reproduction performance for unseen speakers \[[7' 2022-06-24](http://arxiv.org/abs/2206.12229v2)\]. Similarly, DiffProsody proposes a diffusion-based latent prosody generation method with prosody conditional adversarial training to improve the speed and quality of prosody generation \[[8' 2022-04-07](http://arxiv.org/abs/2204.03238v1)\].
