## Related Works

Recent advancements in Text-to-Speech (TTS) systems have significantly enhanced speech naturalness, but challenges remain in achieving precise prosody control and mispronunciation correction \[[1' 2020-08-07](http://arxiv.org/abs/2008.03388v2)\]. Traditional approaches for prosody manipulation often rely on specialized modules or additional training, which limits their capacity for post-hoc adjustments \[[1' 2020-08-07](http://arxiv.org/abs/2008.03388v2)\]. Similarly, conventional mispronunciation correction methods depend on grapheme-to-phoneme (G2P) dictionaries, making them less practical in low-resource settings \[[1' 2020-08-07](http://arxiv.org/abs/2008.03388v2)\]. This section reviews related works that address these challenges, focusing on methods for prosody control, mispronunciation detection, and correction.

Several studies have explored methods for prosody control in TTS. Controllable neural prosody synthesis has been proposed to allow users to input prosody constraints for certain time frames and generate the remaining time frames from input text and contextual prosody \[[2' 2024-08-13](http://arxiv.org/abs/2408.06827v1)\]. PRESENT, a zero-shot text-to-prosody control method, exploits explicit prosody prediction in FastSpeech2-based models by modifying the inference process directly \[[3' 2021-10-06](http://arxiv.org/abs/2110.02854v1)\]. This approach enables subphoneme-level control, a first in the field \[[3' 2021-10-06](http://arxiv.org/abs/2110.02854v1)\]. Hierarchical prosody modeling in non-autoregressive parallel neural TTS has also been proposed to enable intuitive and meaningful prosody control \[[4' 2021-08-12](http://arxiv.org/abs/2108.05517v2)\]. These methods, however, often require additional training or specialized modules, limiting their flexibility for post-hoc adjustments \[[1' 2020-08-07](http://arxiv.org/abs/2008.03388v2)\].

Mispronunciation detection and correction have also been extensively studied. Masked acoustic unit methods have been proposed to detect and correct mispronunciations by using acoustic units as an intermediary feature \[[5' 2021-10-06](http://arxiv.org/abs/2110.02952v2)\]. This approach provides speech-based self-imitating feedback, enhancing the effectiveness of Computer-Assisted Pronunciation Training (CAPT) \[[5' 2021-10-06](http://arxiv.org/abs/2110.02952v2)\]. Additionally, mispronunciation detection using self-supervised speech representations has been explored, where models trained on non-native speech data outperform traditional methods \[[6' 2020-11-12](http://arxiv.org/abs/2011.06465v3)\]. These approaches, however, often rely on extensive annotation or specific domain knowledge, which can be impractical in low-resource settings \[[1' 2020-08-07](http://arxiv.org/abs/2008.03388v2)\].

Recent work on zero-shot text-to-speech synthesis has focused on conditioning on self-supervised speech representation models to improve speaker characteristics and rhythm transfer \[[7' 2022-06-24](http://arxiv.org/abs/2206.12229v2)\]. This method uses large-scale data to obtain embedding vectors directly from speech representations, enabling better reproduction performance for unseen speakers \[[7' 2022-06-24](http://arxiv.org/abs/2206.12229v2)\]. Similarly, DiffProsody proposes a diffusion-based latent prosody generation method with prosody conditional adversarial training to improve the speed and quality of prosody generation \[[8' 2022-04-07](http://arxiv.org/abs/2204.03238v1)\]. ### <a id='category-prosody-control-methods-in-tts'></a>Prosody Control Methods in TTS: 14 papers found.
Recent advances in Text-to-Speech (TTS) have significantly improved speech naturalness, increasing the demand for precise prosody control and mispronunciation correction \[[1' 2020-08-07](http://arxiv.org/abs/2008.03388v2)\]. Existing approaches for prosody manipulation often depend on specialized modules or additional training, limiting their capacity for post-hoc adjustments \[[2' 2024-08-13](http://arxiv.org/abs/2408.06827v1)\]. Similarly, traditional mispronunciation correction relies on grapheme-to-phoneme dictionaries, making it less practical in low-resource settings \[[3' 2021-10-06](http://arxiv.org/abs/2110.02854v1)\]. To address these limitations, researchers have proposed various methods to enhance prosody control in TTS. For instance, a user-controllable, context-aware neural prosody generator has been introduced, allowing users to input prosody constraints for certain time frames and generate the remaining time frames from input text and contextual prosody \[[1' 2020-08-07](http://arxiv.org/abs/2008.03388v2)\]. Additionally, a pitch-shifting neural vocoder has been proposed to modify input speech to match the synthesized prosody \[[1' 2020-08-07](http://arxiv.org/abs/2008.03388v2)\]. Another approach, PRESENT, enables zero-shot application of pretrained TTS models by exploiting explicit prosody prediction in FastSpeech2-based models \[[2' 2024-08-13](http://arxiv.org/abs/2408.06827v1)\]. Furthermore, Prosody-TTS has been proposed as an end-to-end speech synthesis model that combines the advantages of statistical parametric models and end-to-end neural network models, allowing for control over fundamental frequency (f0) and phone duration \[[3' 2021-10-06](http://arxiv.org/abs/2110.02854v1)\]. These methods highlight the ongoing efforts to improve prosody control in TTS, enabling more flexible and natural speech synthesis.

### <a id='category-speech-based-feedback-systems'></a>Speech-Based Feedback Systems: 3 papers found.
The category "Speech-Based Feedback Systems" encompasses research aimed at improving the accuracy and effectiveness of pronunciation training through speech-based feedback. Existing methods, such as ASR-based CAPT, rely on annotated ground truth data for supervised training, which is costly and subjective due to the challenges of classifying undefined non-native phonemes \[[4' 2021-08-12](http://arxiv.org/abs/2108.05517v2)\]. These methods also provide only text-based feedback, limiting their ability to guide learners in correcting pronunciation errors \[[4' 2021-08-12](http://arxiv.org/abs/2108.05517v2)\]. To address these limitations, recent approaches have explored using acoustic units (AUs) as an intermediary feature for both mispronunciation detection and correction, enabling more precise and actionable speech-based feedback \[[4' 2021-08-12](http://arxiv.org/abs/2108.05517v2)\]. Additionally, the development of safety-critical control systems with input constraints has led to the introduction of Input Constrained Control Barrier Functions (ICCBF), which ensure system safety while allowing for real-time feedback control \[[26' 2021-04-04](http://arxiv.org/abs/2104.01704v1)\]. Furthermore, research on control systems with feedback channel failures has introduced methods to handle intermittent loss of control signals, ensuring stability and performance under such conditions \[[27' 2023-05-13](http://arxiv.org/abs/2305.07875v3)\]. These advancements highlight the growing interest in robust and adaptive feedback mechanisms that enhance the reliability and effectiveness of speech-based systems in various applications.
