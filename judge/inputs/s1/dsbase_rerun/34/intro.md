## Related Works

Fund allocation in financial domains has attracted significant research interest, with a growing emphasis on integrating forecasting and optimization in a unified framework. Traditional approaches, such as prediction-only or predict-then-optimize (PnO) methods, often suffer from goal misalignment between forecasting and allocation \[[1' 2025-03-10](http://arxiv.org/abs/2503.06928v2)\]. This issue is exacerbated by the inherent uncertainty introduced by state-of-the-art time series forecasting models \[[2' 2023-05-18](http://arxiv.org/abs/2305.10721v2)\]. To address these challenges, recent works have explored hybrid frameworks that jointly optimize forecasting and allocation \[[3' 2023-04-11](http://arxiv.org/abs/2304.04912v1)\].

A notable effort in this direction is the work by \[[4' 2023-06-21](http://arxiv.org/abs/2306.12086v2)\], which introduces a hybrid regime identification-forecasting framework for multi-asset portfolio construction. This approach leverages unsupervised and supervised learning to generate asset-specific regime forecasts, which are then used in Markowitz mean-variance optimization to determine optimal asset allocation weights. The framework demonstrates consistent outperformance across various portfolio models, including minimum-variance and naive-diversified portfolios \[[4' 2023-06-21](http://arxiv.org/abs/2306.12086v2)\].

Another relevant contribution is the Portfolio Transformer (PT) introduced in \[[5' 2023-07-17](http://arxiv.org/abs/2307.08853v1)\], which directly optimizes the Sharpe ratio without requiring explicit asset return forecasts. Inspired by attention mechanisms in natural language processing, the PT network learns long-term dependencies among portfolio assets and adapts quickly to changing market conditions. The PT outperforms traditional LSTM-based models in risk-adjusted performance \[[5' 2023-07-17](http://arxiv.org/abs/2307.08853v1)\].

The importance of uncertainty calibration in forecasting has also been emphasized. For instance, \[[6' 2020-05-26](http://arxiv.org/abs/2005.12496v6)\] presents a calibration method for regression settings that addresses the issue of overconfident uncertainty estimates. This method, called CRUDE, assumes a constant arbitrary shape for error distributions across the output space, leading to sharper and better-calibrated uncertainty estimates compared to state-of-the-art techniques \[[6' 2020-05-26](http://arxiv.org/abs/2005.12496v6)\]. Similarly, \[[7' 2024-06-13](http://arxiv.org/abs/2406.09578v2)\] proposes a novel approach for creating probabilistic forecasts from deterministic forecasts using conditional invertible neural networks (cINN). This method enables the generation of accurate probabilistic forecasts without complex statistical loss functions or additional assumptions \[[7' 2024-06-13](http://arxiv.org/abs/2406.09578v2)\].

In the context of asset allocation, \[[8' 2024-10-18](http://arxiv.org/abs/2410.14630v2)\] introduces a framework that integrates macroeconomic regime detection into tactical asset allocation. By classifying current regimes and forecasting future regimes, the framework optimizes portfolio allocations based on historical performance and expected returns. This approach outperforms traditional benchmarks such as equal-weight and buy-and-hold strategies \[[8' 2024-10-18](http://arxiv.org/abs/2410.14630v2)\].

Moreover, \[[9' 2022-06-07](http://arxiv.org/abs/2206.03246v1)\] explores the use of machine learning algorithms for financial asset price forecasting, demonstrating that modern ML models significantly outperform the Capital Asset Pricing Model (CAPM) on out-of-sample test data.
