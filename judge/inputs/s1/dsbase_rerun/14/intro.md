## Related Works

Video super-resolution (VSR) has been a focal point in computer vision, aiming to reconstruct high-resolution (HR) videos from low-resolution (LR) inputs. Early approaches relied on traditional image restoration techniques, but these often failed to capture temporal consistency in video sequences \[[8' 2020-08-13](http://arxiv.org/abs/2008.05765v2)\]. With the rise of deep learning, methods such as 2D CNN with early fusion, 3D CNN with slow fusion, and Recurrent Neural Networks (RNNs) have been proposed to model temporal dynamics \[[8' 2020-08-13](http://arxiv.org/abs/2008.05765v2)\]. However, these methods often suffer from computational inefficiency and limited modeling capacity, especially for real-world scenarios \[[9' 2022-08-24](http://arxiv.org/abs/2208.11608v1)\]. 

Recent advances in diffusion models have shown significant promise in VSR, offering high-quality texture synthesis and realistic detail generation \[[19' 2025-03-05](http://arxiv.org/abs/2503.03355v5)\]. However, diffusion-based VSR methods are prone to introducing artifacts and inconsistencies due to their inherent randomness and tile-based approaches \[[1' 2025-02-05](http://arxiv.org/abs/2502.03502v2)\]. To address these issues, several works have introduced mechanisms to improve spatio-temporal consistency. For instance, DC-VSR employs a novel Spatial Attention Propagation (SAP) and Temporal Attention Propagation (TAP) scheme based on self-attention to ensure coherence across frames \[[1' 2025-02-05](http://arxiv.org/abs/2502.03502v2)\]. Similarly, STAR integrates text-to-video (T2V) models to enhance temporal modeling, introducing a Local Information Enhancement Module (LIEM) and a Dynamic Frequency (DF) Loss to improve fidelity \[[3' 2025-01-06](http://arxiv.org/abs/2501.02976v1)\]. 

To handle compressed videos, which often exhibit quantization artifacts and spatial distortions, the SDATC diffusion model was proposed. This model incorporates a Distortion Control Module (DCM) to modulate inputs and a Spatio-Temporal Attention Module (STAM) to capture temporal correlations \[[2' 2025-02-11](http://arxiv.org/abs/2502.07381v3)\]. Additionally, optical flow-based alignment is used to enhance output smoothness \[[2' 2025-02-11](http://arxiv.org/abs/2502.07381v3)\]. 

The problem of temporal inconsistency in VSR has also been addressed through self-supervised learning. For example, the Temporal Consistency Learning Network (TCNet) was introduced to enhance the consistency of reconstructed videos by learning self-alignment from inter-frames and employing a self-attention mechanism for adaptive warping \[[7' 2022-11-03](http://arxiv.org/abs/2211.01639v1)\]. Furthermore, the Self-Supervised Adaptation for Video Super-Resolution \[[20' 2021-03-18](http://arxiv.org/abs/2103.10081v1)\] allows conventional VSR networks to adapt to test video frames without ground-truth datasets, improving temporal consistency through self-similar patches and test-time knowledge distillation.
