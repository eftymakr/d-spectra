## Related Works

Personalized outfit generation has gained significant attention in recent years, driven by the increasing demand for tailored fashion recommendations and AI-enabled design. Several approaches have been proposed to address the challenges of generating compatible and personalized fashion items. Early methods focused on evaluating the compatibility of existing outfits and recommending complementary items \[[1' 2025-02-12](http://arxiv.org/abs/2502.08674v1)\]. These methods primarily relied on similarity metrics and rule-based systems, but they lacked the ability to generate new, creative fashion items \[[3' 2025-02-05](http://arxiv.org/abs/2502.06827v1)\]. More recently, generative models have been introduced to synthesize new outfits based on given fashion items. For instance, COutfitGAN \[[1' 2025-02-12](http://arxiv.org/abs/2502.08674v1)\] and OutfitGAN \[[3' 2025-02-05](http://arxiv.org/abs/2502.06827v1)\] propose frameworks that generate photo-realistic fashion items that are compatible with the given ones. These models incorporate advanced neural network architectures, such as UNet-based discriminators and semantic alignment modules, to improve the quality and compatibility of the generated outfits.

Color compatibility has also been recognized as a critical factor in evaluating outfit compatibility \[[2' 2020-07-05](http://arxiv.org/abs/2007.02388v1)\]. Previous studies often neglected this aspect, but recent work has emphasized the importance of color in determining the aesthetic appeal of an outfit. The work in \[[2' 2020-07-05](http://arxiv.org/abs/2007.02388v1)\] introduces a compatibility learning framework that models outfits as graphs and utilizes both ground-truth and pseudo labels to train the compatibility model in a weakly-supervised manner. This approach achieves comparable performance to methods that use deep image features, demonstrating the importance of color compatibility in fashion recommendation.

Another line of research focuses on the use of deep learning models for outfit compatibility prediction. For example, the work in \[[6' 2020-05-13](http://arxiv.org/abs/2005.06584v1)\] introduces a relational network (RN) to learn compatibility between fashion items, allowing for the evaluation of entire outfits with an arbitrary number of items. This approach addresses limitations of previous methods that required rich semantic information or fixed item order. Similarly, \[[7' 2024-04-28](http://arxiv.org/abs/2404.18040v1)\] proposes a graph neural network (GNN) framework that represents outfits as graphs and uses modified GNNs to score the compatibility of items. These models have shown promising results in both compatibility prediction and fill-in-the-blank tasks.

In addition to compatibility, personalization has become a key focus in fashion recommendation systems. The work in \[[9' 2018-03-25](http://arxiv.org/abs/1803.09196v2)\] introduces type-aware embeddings that respect item type and jointly learn notions of item similarity and compatibility. This approach improves performance on outfit compatibility prediction and fill-in-the-blank tasks, demonstrating the importance of incorporating semantic information into the learning process.

Recent advances in self-supervised learning have also contributed to the field. For example, \[[5' 2022-06-13](http://arxiv.org/abs/2206.05982v1)\] proposes a self-supervised learning approach that leverages in-the-wild street fashion images to learn compatibility representations.
