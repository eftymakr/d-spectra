## Related Works

Vision Transformers (ViTs) have emerged as a powerful alternative to convolutional neural networks (CNNs) in computer vision tasks due to their ability to capture global context \[[6' 2024-05-01](http://arxiv.org/abs/2405.00314v1)\]. However, the deployment of ViTs on hardware remains challenging due to the complexity of arithmetic operations such as Softmax and LayerNorm, which are sensitive to model accuracy and difficult to map onto efficient hardware with low precision \[[1' 2020-09-18](http://arxiv.org/abs/2009.08605v1)\]. Existing methods often exploit parallelism in matrix multiplication operations while keeping these complex operations on the CPU, resulting in suboptimal performance due to communication overhead between the CPU and accelerator \[[1' 2020-09-18](http://arxiv.org/abs/2009.08605v1)\]. This paper addresses these challenges by presenting the first ViT accelerator that maps all operations of the ViT models onto FPGAs \[[1' 2020-09-18](http://arxiv.org/abs/2009.08605v1)\].

Several works have focused on accelerating Transformer models by optimizing hardware designs. For instance, \[[1' 2020-09-18](http://arxiv.org/abs/2009.08605v1)\] introduces a hardware accelerator for multi-head attention and position-wise feed-forward networks in the Transformer, achieving significant speedups over GPU implementations. Similarly, \[[2' 2022-08-12](http://arxiv.org/abs/2208.06118v1)\] proposes an algorithm-hardware co-optimized framework for accelerating N:M sparse Transformers, demonstrating improvements in both accuracy and speed. These works highlight the importance of co-designing algorithms and hardware to achieve efficient Transformer inference.

The use of sparsity in Transformers has also been explored to reduce computational and memory demands. \[[3' 2024-02-15](http://arxiv.org/abs/2402.10118v2)\] introduces a framework that leverages N:M sparsity patterns to accelerate Transformers, achieving high training efficiency and improved accuracy. Additionally, \[[4' 2024-12-25](http://arxiv.org/abs/2412.18948v1)\] presents a power-efficient hardware implementation of L-Mul, an approximate multiplication method, for large language models, demonstrating reduced energy consumption and computational complexity. These approaches underscore the potential of approximate computing and sparsity in enhancing Transformer performance.

In the context of Vision Transformers, \[[5' 2023-02-17](http://arxiv.org/abs/2302.09108v1)\] introduces ViTA, a configurable hardware accelerator for inference of vision transformer models on edge devices, achieving nearly 90% hardware utilization efficiency and a power consumption of 0.88W. \[[6' 2024-05-01](http://arxiv.org/abs/2405.00314v1)\] further explores model quantization and hardware acceleration for ViTs, emphasizing the importance of algorithm-hardware co-design to optimize performance. These works highlight the need for tailored hardware solutions that can efficiently handle the unique characteristics of ViTs.

Moreover, \[[7' 2024-05-07](http://arxiv.org/abs/2405.04134v1)\] provides a technical note on the geometry and dynamics of LayerNorm, offering insights into its behavior and implications for hardware design. \[[8' 2023-03-27](http://arxiv.org/abs/2303.15105v1)\] introduces a novel quadrangle attention mechanism for vision transformers, improving performance on various vision tasks. These contributions demonstrate the ongoing efforts to enhance the efficiency and effectiveness of Transformer models in different applications.

The category "LayerNorm and Softmax Operation Optimization" focuses on improving the efficiency and performance of key operations in deep learning models, particularly LayerNorm and Softmath, which are critical for training and inference in vision transformers (ViTs). LayerNorm, a normalization technique, operates by applying a composition of linear projection, nonlinear scaling, and affine transformation to input activation vectors, resulting in outputs constrained within the intersection of an (N-1)-dimensional hyperplane and an N-dimensional hyperellipsoid \[[7' 2024-05-07](http://arxiv.org/abs/2405.04134v1)\]. This geometric intuition aids in understanding how LayerNorm affects data distribution and can guide hardware design for efficient implementation. Meanwhile, the Softmax function, widely used for probability distribution in classification tasks, has been optimized through reduced memory access strategies, leading to performance improvements of up to 1.3x for Softmax and 5x for combined Softmax+TopK operations \[[10' 2018-05-08](http://arxiv.org/abs/1805.02867v2)\]. These optimizations are crucial for hardware acceleration, as they reduce the computational and communication overhead between the CPU and accelerator, which is a major bottleneck in ViT implementations. Furthermore, the Mixture of Experts (MoE) framework relies on adaptive softmax gating to dynamically assign tasks to specialized sub-models, and recent studies have analyzed the impact of different softmax variants, such as dense-to-sparse and hierarchical gating, on convergence and parameter estimation \[[12' 2025-03-05](http://arxiv.org/abs/2503.03213v1)\]. These works collectively highlight the importance of optimizing LayerNorm and Softmax operations for both accuracy and efficiency, especially in hardware-constrained environments like FPGAs, where custom arithmetic formats like Microscaling Integer (MXInt) enable high area efficiency and performance \[[1' 2020-09-18](http://arxiv.org/abs/2009.08605v1)\].
