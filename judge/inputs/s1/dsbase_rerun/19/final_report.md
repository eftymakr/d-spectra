## Related Works

Speech-driven facial animation has been a focal point of research in computer vision and machine learning, with a growing emphasis on achieving realistic and expressive lip synchronization. Existing methods often rely on pre-trained self-supervised audio models as encoders to extract meaningful features from speech signals \[[1' 2023-05-31](http://arxiv.org/abs/2305.19556v3)\]. These models, such as HuBERT \[[2' 2022-10-05](http://arxiv.org/abs/2210.02509v1)\], have demonstrated effectiveness in capturing lexical and non-lexical information from audio without the need for extensive lexicons. However, challenges persist in accurately mapping these features to corresponding facial movements, particularly when dealing with phonetically similar syllables that exhibit distinct lip shapes \[[3' 2023-10-11](http://arxiv.org/abs/2310.07236v4)\].

Phonetic context plays a crucial role in lip motion generation, as co-articulation influences the articulation of individual phonemes \[[4' 2023-11-08](http://arxiv.org/abs/2311.04766v2)\]. Early approaches to lip synchronization focused on aligning audio and visual signals using handcrafted features, but recent methods have shifted toward data-driven models that learn cross-modal mappings directly \[[5' 2023-03-09](http://arxiv.org/abs/2303.05416v1)\]. For instance, the work of \[[6' 2023-01-06](http://arxiv.org/abs/2301.02379v2)\] introduced a temporal GAN-based approach that generates videos of talking heads with lip movements synchronized to audio, while \[[7' 2019-06-14](http://arxiv.org/abs/1906.06337v1)\] proposed a system that uses a still image and audio clip to produce subject-independent results. These methods, however, often struggle with fine-grained lip synchronization and natural facial expressions \[[8' 2020-08-23](http://arxiv.org/abs/2008.10004v1)\].

To address these limitations, several studies have explored the use of attention mechanisms to improve the alignment between audio and visual features. The AttnWav2Lip model, for example, incorporates spatial and channel attention modules to focus on the lip region during synthesis \[[9' 2012-09-22](http://arxiv.org/abs/1209.4982v1)\]. Similarly, the work of \[[10' 2018-05-23](http://arxiv.org/abs/1805.09313v4)\] introduced a dual-learning framework that jointly trains audio-driven facial animation and lip reading tasks, leveraging the complementary relationship between facial motion and audio \[[11' 2024-08-03](http://arxiv.org/abs/2408.01826v5)\]. These approaches have contributed to more accurate lip synchronization but still face challenges in capturing subtle facial expressions and emotional nuances \[[12' 2021-12-04](http://arxiv.org/abs/2112.02214v2)\].

Another line of research has focused on improving the expressiveness of facial animations by incorporating emotional and contextual information. For example, \[[13' 2019-12-12](http://arxiv.org/abs/1912.05833v2)\] proposed a method that uses a graph latent diffusion transformer to generate 3D facial animations with greater motion diversity and lip-sync accuracy. Additionally, \[[14' 2023-01-05](http://arxiv.org/abs/2301.02008v2)\] introduced a system that enables emotion-controllable facial animation, allowing for the adjustment of facial expressions based on the intensity and type of emotion conveyed in the speech \[[15' 2022-11-02](http://arxiv.org/abs/2211.00924v2)\]. These methods demonstrate the potential of incorporating emotional and contextual cues to enhance the realism of generated animations.

In the realm of cross-modal alignment techniques for speech-driven facial animation, recent research has focused on improving the synchronization between audio and visual modalities to achieve more natural and expressive results. To address this, methods such as the Context-Aware Lip-Sync (CALS) framework have been proposed, which explicitly leverages phonetic context to generate more spatio-temporally aligned lip movements by mapping each phone to a contextualized lip motion unit \[[1' 2023-05-31](http://arxiv.org/abs/2305.19556v3)\]. Additionally, the use of code query tasks in a finite proxy space has been explored to reduce cross-modal mapping uncertainty and enhance the vividness of generated motions \[[6' 2023-01-06](http://arxiv.org/abs/2301.02379v2)\]. Furthermore, the integration of linguistic and data-driven priors through progressive learning mechanisms has been proposed to improve temporal coherence and audio-visual consistency \[[18' 2024-09-02](http://arxiv.org/abs/2409.01113v1)\]. To overcome these challenges, parallel decoding approaches have been proposed, such as ParaLip, which predicts lip frame durations and generates frames in a non-autoregressive manner, enabling faster and more accurate synthesis \[[25' 2021-07-14](http://arxiv.org/abs/2107.06831v2)\]. Additionally, studies have explored the use of syllables in speech recognition and language modeling, highlighting their advantages over characters and subwords in terms of segmentation efficiency and performance \[[2' 2022-10-05](http://arxiv.org/abs/2210.02509v1)\]. Syllables have been shown to improve language modeling and machine translation, particularly in low-resource scenarios, by providing more meaningful units than characters or morphemes \[[2' 2022-10-05](http://arxiv.org/abs/2210.02509v1)\]. Furthermore, the use of syllables in speech databases has been emphasized for reducing the search space in recognition systems, as demonstrated in the development of speech recognition systems for Indian languages \[[28' 2010-01-13](http://arxiv.org/abs/1001.2263v1)\]. These findings underscore the importance of syllabic units in both speech processing and lip synchronization tasks, offering potential avenues for improving the naturalness and coherence of generated talking face videos.

While recent approaches using autoregressive and diffusion-based models have achieved notable progress, they often suffer from modality inconsistencies, particularly misalignment between audio and mesh, leading to reduced motion diversity and lip-sync accuracy. To address this, we propose GLDiTalker, a novel speech-driven 3D facial animation model based on a Graph Latent Diffusion Transformer. GLDiTalker resolves modality misalignment by diffusing signals within a quantized spatiotemporal latent space. It employs a two-stage training pipeline: the Graph-Enhanced Quantized Space Learning Stage ensures lip-sync accuracy, while the Space-Time Powered Latent Diffusion Stage enhances motion diversity. Together, these stages enable GLDiTalker to generate realistic, temporally stable 3D facial animations.
