## Related Works

Quantitative susceptibility mapping (QSM) has emerged as a powerful tool for characterizing multiple sclerosis (MS) lesions, particularly in distinguishing between paramagnetic rim lesions (PRLs) and non-rim lesions \[[1' 2019-01-17](http://arxiv.org/abs/1901.05733v1)\]. PRLs, surrounded by paramagnetic iron deposits, are an emerging biomarker in MS and are associated with increased disability \[[4' 2023-12-28](http://arxiv.org/abs/2312.16835v2)\]. However, the rarity of PRLs poses a significant challenge for supervised learning methods due to class imbalance \[[1' 2019-01-17](http://arxiv.org/abs/1901.05733v1)\]. Several approaches have been proposed to address this issue, including data augmentation and synthetic data generation.

Synthetic lesion generation has been widely explored in medical imaging to improve model performance in the presence of limited or imbalanced data \[[2' 2024-12-13](http://arxiv.org/abs/2412.10492v2)\]. For example, a U-Net-based model was used to generate synthetic MS lesions on healthy MRI images, which were then used for data augmentation to enhance supervised lesion detection algorithms \[[1' 2019-01-17](http://arxiv.org/abs/1901.05733v1)\]. Similarly, a partial convolution generative adversarial network (GAN) was developed to generate synthetic liver lesions, which improved segmentation performance when combined with real data \[[8' 2022-06-18](http://arxiv.org/abs/2206.09065v2)\]. These methods demonstrate that synthetic data can effectively augment training sets and improve model generalization.

In the context of QSM, a probabilistic dipole inversion method was proposed to estimate susceptibility maps with uncertainty quantification, addressing the lack of ground-truth in clinical settings \[[9' 2020-09-07](http://arxiv.org/abs/2009.04251v6)\]. Another approach, QSM-RimDS, was developed to jointly detect and segment PRLs using T2-weighted FLAIR lesion masks, achieving a mean Dice similarity coefficient (DSC) of 0.57 and improved detection performance compared to other methods \[[2' 2024-12-13](http://arxiv.org/abs/2412.10492v2)\]. These works highlight the importance of accurate segmentation and the challenges associated with PRL detection.

To further improve lesion segmentation, a geometric loss function was introduced to address data imbalance and exploit the geometric properties of MS lesions \[[18' 2020-09-29](http://arxiv.org/abs/2009.13755v1)\]. This approach outperformed traditional region-based and boundary-aware loss functions, demonstrating the potential of incorporating geometric constraints into segmentation models. Additionally, a hybrid cascaded neural network was proposed for liver lesion segmentation, combining 2D and 3D convolutional networks to improve detection accuracy \[[23' 2019-09-11](http://arxiv.org/abs/1909.04797v3)\].

In the field of skin lesion segmentation, a mutual bootstrapping model was introduced to simultaneously perform segmentation and classification, achieving high performance on the ISIC-2017 and PH2 datasets \[[22' 2019-03-08](http://arxiv.org/abs/1903.03313v4)\]. This approach leverages the interdependence between segmentation and classification tasks, demonstrating the benefits of joint learning.
