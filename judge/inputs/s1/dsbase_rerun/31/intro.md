## Related Works

Recent advances in deep learning have enabled significant progress in the field of genomic sequence modeling. While unsupervised pre-training paradigms have been widely applied to DNA pre-training, these approaches often yield suboptimal results due to the limited information contained in pure DNA sequences \[[1' 2024-10-23](http://arxiv.org/abs/2410.17954v2)\]. The functions of DNA sequences are regulated by genomic profiles such as chromatin accessibility, which are not captured by pure sequence pre-training \[[1' 2024-10-23](http://arxiv.org/abs/2410.17954v2)\]. As a result, supervised training for genomic profile prediction has emerged as a more effective alternative \[[1' 2024-10-23](http://arxiv.org/abs/2410.17954v2)\].

Several studies have explored the use of supervised learning for genomic prediction. For instance, \[[5' 2013-01-25](http://arxiv.org/abs/1301.5948v3)\] demonstrated that statistical learning approaches can generate robust and highly accurate predictive models based on genome-wide SNP profiles. These models have been shown to explain a significant portion of disease variance and heritability, with applications in celiac disease \[[5' 2013-01-25](http://arxiv.org/abs/1301.5948v3)\]. Similarly, \[[15' 2013-08-09](http://arxiv.org/abs/1308.2134v1)\] developed a random forest classifier to predict CpG site methylation levels using features such as neighboring CpG site methylation levels, genomic distance, and co-localization with regulatory elements. This approach achieved high prediction accuracy, particularly within CpG islands \[[15' 2013-08-09](http://arxiv.org/abs/1308.2134v1)\].

The use of mixture-of-experts (MoE) architectures has also gained traction in genomic modeling. \[[8' 2022-07-19](http://arxiv.org/abs/2207.09094v1)\] proposed MoEC, a general approach that enables expert layers to learn more diverse and appropriate knowledge by imposing variance-based constraints on the routing stage. This method has been shown to improve performance on machine translation and natural language understanding tasks \[[8' 2022-07-19](http://arxiv.org/abs/2207.09094v1)\]. Additionally, \[[16' 2025-03-26](http://arxiv.org/abs/2503.22731v1)\] introduced MoRE-LLM, a Mixture of Rule Experts guided by a Large Language Model, which combines a data-driven black-box model with knowledge extracted from an LLM to enable domain knowledge-aligned and transparent predictions \[[16' 2025-03-26](http://arxiv.org/abs/2503.22731v1)\].

In the context of self-supervised learning, \[[14' 2022-04-14](http://arxiv.org/abs/2204.06699v1)\] introduced SNP2Vec, a scalable self-supervised pre-training approach for understanding SNP. This method was applied to perform long-sequence genomics modeling and demonstrated superior performance in predicting Alzheimer's disease risk \[[14' 2022-04-14](http://arxiv.org/abs/2204.06699v1)\]. Similarly, \[[20' 2022-03-11](http://arxiv.org/abs/2203.06041v1)\] proposed Embedding Earth, a self-supervised contrastive pre-training method for leveraging the large availability of satellite imagery to improve performance on downstream dense land cover classification tasks \[[20' 2022-03-11](http://arxiv.org/abs/2203.06041v1)\].

The integration of genomic data with other biological data has also been explored. \[[25' 2021-09-30](http://arxiv.org/abs/2109.14781v1)\] reviewed the progress and perspectives for genomic prediction in rice improvement, highlighting the importance of integrating genomic prediction into breeding strategies \[[25' 2021-09-30](http://arxiv.org/abs/2109.14781v1)\].
