## Related Works

Deploying Large Language Models (LLMs) on edge devices has garnered significant attention due to its potential to enhance privacy, reduce latency, and improve resource utilization \[[1' 2024-12-13](http://arxiv.org/abs/2412.12178v2)\]. However, the computational and memory demands of LLMs pose substantial challenges for deployment on resource-constrained edge hardware \[[2' 2025-02-19](http://arxiv.org/abs/2502.15816v1)\]. Prior research has explored various strategies to address these challenges, including model compression, quantization, and hardware acceleration \[[3' 2024-02-22](http://arxiv.org/abs/2402.14800v2)\].

Model compression techniques such as pruning and quantization have been widely studied to reduce the size and computational cost of LLMs \[[4' 2024-06-03](http://arxiv.org/abs/2406.01698v3)\]. For instance, activation sparsity methods have been proposed to achieve up to 50% memory and computing reductions with minimal accuracy degradation \[[1' 2024-12-13](http://arxiv.org/abs/2412.12178v2)\]. These methods are orthogonal to existing compression techniques and can be combined to maximize compression while maintaining performance \[[1' 2024-12-13](http://arxiv.org/abs/2412.12178v2)\]. Similarly, low-rank pruning and factorization approaches have been used to reduce the parameter count of large models while preserving their effectiveness \[[24' 2023-06-25](http://arxiv.org/abs/2306.14152v1)\].

Quantization is another critical technique for deploying LLMs on edge devices, as it reduces both memory usage and computational load \[[6' 2022-05-30](http://arxiv.org/abs/2205.15437v2)\]. Mixed-precision quantization, in particular, has been shown to offer better utilization of hardware that supports different bitwidths \[[6' 2022-05-30](http://arxiv.org/abs/2205.15437v2)\]. Recent work has also emphasized the importance of considering the hardware-specific characteristics when designing quantization strategies \[[7' 2024-10-06](http://arxiv.org/abs/2410.04466v4)\]. For example, automatic mixed-precision quantization (AMED) has demonstrated superior performance in balancing accuracy and hardware efficiency \[[6' 2022-05-30](http://arxiv.org/abs/2205.15437v2)\].

Hardware acceleration plays a crucial role in enabling efficient LLM inference on edge devices \[[8' 2024-10-01](http://arxiv.org/abs/2410.00531v1)\]. FPGA-based accelerators, in particular, have been proposed due to their reconfigurability and ability to achieve high throughput with low power consumption \[[5' 2021-10-01](http://arxiv.org/abs/2110.00478v1)\]. Several studies have focused on designing FPGA accelerators tailored for LLM inference, such as SECDA-LLM, which streamlines the design process and improves performance \[[12' 2024-08-01](http://arxiv.org/abs/2408.00462v1)\]. Additionally, the use of hardware-software co-design methodologies has been highlighted as a promising approach to optimize LLM inference on edge platforms \[[9' 2025-04-07](http://arxiv.org/abs/2505.03745v1)\].

Collaborative inference strategies have also emerged as a viable solution for deploying large LLMs on edge devices with limited resources \[[11' 2025-04-11](http://arxiv.org/abs/2504.08242v1)\]. These approaches leverage multiple edge devices to distribute the computational load, thereby reducing latency and improving scalability. For example, TPI-LLM enables efficient inference of 70B-scale models by introducing a sliding window memory scheduler and optimizing communication bottlenecks \[[8' 2024-10-01](http://arxiv.org/abs/2410.00531v1)\].
