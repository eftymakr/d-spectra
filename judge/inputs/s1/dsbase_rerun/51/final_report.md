## Related Works

Federated learning (FL) has gained significant attention as a privacy-preserving approach to collaborative machine learning, enabling models to be trained across decentralized data sources without exposing raw data \[[1' 2024-01-10](http://arxiv.org/abs/2401.05146v3)\]. This paradigm has been extended to address the "right to be forgotten," leading to the emergence of federated unlearning (FU), which aims to remove the influence of specific clients or data points from the global model \[[2' 2024-12-26](http://arxiv.org/abs/2412.19069v1)\]. The need for FU is driven by both legal requirements, such as the GDPR, and practical concerns like data poisoning and model bias \[[3' 2022-05-25](http://arxiv.org/abs/2205.12709v1)\].

Recent work has focused on developing efficient and verifiable FU methods. For instance, VeriFi introduces a framework that allows clients to verify the unlearning effect locally, ensuring that the global model is updated without compromising privacy \[[4' 2024-01-19](http://arxiv.org/abs/2401.11018v1)\]. This approach involves marking and checking steps to ensure the unlearning is effective and verifiable \[[5' 2024-03-04](http://arxiv.org/abs/2403.02437v3)\]. Similarly, the paper "Communication Efficient and Provable Federated Unlearning" introduces a framework that guarantees exact unlearning with communication efficiency and theoretical guarantees \[[6' 2024-05-28](http://arxiv.org/abs/2405.17878v3)\]. This work also highlights the importance of total variation (TV) stability in achieving fast and accurate unlearning \[[7' 2023-10-30](http://arxiv.org/abs/2310.19218v5)\].

In the context of federated online learning to rank (FOLTR), privacy is maintained by training ranking models across decentralized data sources without sharing raw data \[[8' 2023-06-03](http://arxiv.org/abs/2306.02216v3)\]. However, existing methods often lack robust unlearning capabilities, making it difficult to remove specific client contributions without retraining the entire model \[[9' 2022-11-21](http://arxiv.org/abs/2211.11656v5)\]. The paper "Effective and Secure Federated Online Learning to Rank" addresses this challenge by proposing methods that enhance privacy while ensuring unlearning capabilities \[[10' 2020-03-09](http://arxiv.org/abs/2003.04247v2)\]. It emphasizes the need for unlearning to be both effective and secure, especially in dynamic environments where user preferences change rapidly \[[11' 2024-10-14](http://arxiv.org/abs/2410.10922v4)\].

The concept of unlearning has also been explored in the context of deep neural networks (DNNs), where the goal is to remove the influence of specific data points while maintaining model performance \[[12' 2024-05-23](http://arxiv.org/abs/2405.17462v4)\]. The Information Difference Index (IDI) has been introduced as a novel metric to evaluate strong unlearning, measuring the retained information in intermediate features \[[13' 2022-07-12](http://arxiv.org/abs/2207.05521v3)\]. This approach provides a more comprehensive assessment of unlearning efficacy compared to traditional metrics like accuracy \[[14' 2025-02-28](http://arxiv.org/abs/2502.20709v1)\].

In addition, the paper "Forgettable Federated Linear Learning" proposes a framework that allows for certified data unlearning without requiring additional client communication or storage \[[15' 2024-05-24](http://arxiv.org/abs/2405.15474v2)\]. This method uses pre-trained models to linearly approximate DNNs, enabling efficient and secure unlearning \[[16' 2024-10-13](http://arxiv.org/abs/2410.09947v1)\]. ### <a id='category-federated-unlearning-(fu)-motivation'></a>Federated Unlearning (FU) Motivation: 12 papers found.
The concept of federated unlearning (FU) has emerged as a critical area of research, driven by the need to address the "right to be forgotten" in privacy-sensitive applications like federated learning (FL). This right, mandated by regulations such as GDPR, requires models to allow users to remove their data contributions from the global model \[[1' 2024-01-10](http://arxiv.org/abs/2401.05146v3)\]. However, traditional unlearning methods designed for centralized learning are not directly applicable to FL due to its distributed nature, which introduces challenges such as interactivity, stochasticity, and heterogeneity \[[2' 2024-12-26](http://arxiv.org/abs/2412.19069v1)\]. To address these challenges, several FU approaches have been proposed, each aiming to efficiently remove specific clients' contributions without requiring full retraining \[[3' 2022-05-25](http://arxiv.org/abs/2205.12709v1)\]. For instance, SIFU introduces a method that provides formal unlearning guarantees for both convex and non-convex optimization regimes, while maintaining computational efficiency \[[4' 2024-01-19](http://arxiv.org/abs/2401.11018v1)\]. Additionally, methods like FedEraser and FUSED have been developed to reduce unlearning costs and improve model performance by leveraging historical parameter updates or introducing reversible unlearning mechanisms \[[5' 2024-03-04](http://arxiv.org/abs/2403.02437v3)\]\[[6' 2024-05-28](http://arxiv.org/abs/2405.17878v3)\]. These approaches highlight the ongoing efforts to balance privacy, accuracy, and efficiency in federated unlearning, with a growing emphasis on practical evaluation and benchmarking \[[7' 2023-10-30](http://arxiv.org/abs/2310.19218v5)\].

Particularly, we propose a more efficient and FL-friendly unlearning method, and two more effective and robust non-invasive-verification methods. We extensively evaluate VeriFi on 7 datasets and 4 types of deep learning models. Our analysis establishes important empirical understandings for more trustworthy federated unlearning.»

### <a id='category-challenges-in-existing-unlearning-methods'></a>Challenges in Existing Unlearning Methods: 2 papers found.
The challenges in existing unlearning methods are well-documented, particularly in their inability to effectively balance efficiency with the preservation of critical model properties such as fairness. This limitation highlights a critical gap in the field, as most existing techniques prioritize computational efficiency over the maintenance of fairness and other essential model properties. ### <a id='category-federated-learning-foundations'></a>Federated Learning Foundations: 8 papers found.
Federated learning (FL) has gained significant attention for its ability to enable collaborative model training while preserving data privacy by keeping training data on local devices \[[30' 2020-07-28](http://arxiv.org/abs/2007.14390v5)\]. However, achieving efficient and scalable FL remains challenging due to issues such as client heterogeneity, communication costs, and the need for robust privacy protections \[[28' 2024-09-19](http://arxiv.org/abs/2409.13004v1)\]. Recent research has focused on improving FL frameworks to support large-scale and heterogeneous device environments, with frameworks like Flower offering enhanced capabilities for executing FL experiments on a massive number of clients \[[30' 2020-07-28](http://arxiv.org/abs/2007.14390v5)\]. Additionally, the importance of interpretability in FL has emerged, as it is crucial for model debugging, fairness, and motivating active participation in collaborative learning \[[24' 2023-02-27](http://arxiv.org/abs/2302.13473v2)\]. Privacy threats in FL, including data leakage and poisoning attacks, have also been extensively studied, with strategies such as gradient perturbation proposed to mitigate these risks \[[28' 2024-09-19](http://arxiv.org/abs/2409.13004v1)\]. Furthermore, fairness in FL has become a critical area of research, with new notions such as proportional fairness introduced to ensure equitable performance across diverse clients \[[22' 2022-02-03](http://arxiv.org/abs/2202.01666v5)\]. These advancements highlight the growing complexity and interdisciplinary nature of FL, addressing both technical and ethical challenges in its deployment.

In this work, we address the zero-shot unlearning scenario, whereby an unlearning algorithm must be able to remove data given only a trained model and the data to be forgotten.
