## Related Works

Database transaction isolation has been a focal point of research, with a growing emphasis on the challenges of testing and verifying isolation properties in production systems. Prior work has explored various approaches to ensure correctness under different isolation levels, with a particular focus on the trade-offs between performance and correctness guarantees. For instance, the use of black-box testing to detect isolation bugs has been extensively studied, where large, concurrent transaction workloads are used to stress-test databases and verify whether execution histories adhere to specified isolation levels \[[1' 2025-04-03](http://arxiv.org/abs/2504.02344v1)\]. This approach, however, is often limited by the high computational overhead associated with verifying strong isolation levels such as serializability and snapshot isolation \[[1' 2025-04-03](http://arxiv.org/abs/2504.02344v1)\].

Recent studies have also investigated the complexity of isolation testing, revealing that testing strong isolation is NP-complete, while weak isolation levels can be tested in polynomial time \[[1' 2025-04-03](http://arxiv.org/abs/2504.02344v1)\]. This distinction has enabled the development of more scalable testing tools, although existing testers still suffer from high polynomial complexity, which restricts their applicability to moderate-sized workloads \[[1' 2025-04-03](http://arxiv.org/abs/2504.02344v1)\]. The work in \[[1' 2025-04-03](http://arxiv.org/abs/2504.02344v1)\] highlights the need for more efficient algorithms that can handle large-scale databases, which is where our approach, AWDIT, contributes significantly.

In the context of testing weak isolation levels, several studies have proposed methods to improve the efficiency of isolation checking. For example, the use of mini-transactions to reduce overhead during history generation has been explored as a way to improve performance \[[1' 2025-04-03](http://arxiv.org/abs/2504.02344v1)\]. Additionally, the application of active fuzzing techniques to test and secure cyber-physical systems has demonstrated the value of automated, adaptive testing strategies in identifying vulnerabilities \[[2' 2020-05-28](http://arxiv.org/abs/2005.14124v2)\]. These methods, while not directly related to database isolation, share common principles with our approach in terms of automation and efficiency.

The concept of isolated testing for self-organization algorithms has also been studied, emphasizing the need for automation due to the complexity of these systems \[[4' 2016-06-08](http://arxiv.org/abs/1606.02442v1)\]. This aligns with our focus on developing an automated and efficient testing framework for weak isolation levels. Furthermore, the use of model-based testing to derive test cases for self-organization algorithms has been shown to yield representative results \[[4' 2016-06-08](http://arxiv.org/abs/1606.02442v1)\], which is a principle that informs our approach to test case generation.

In summary, the related works highlight the importance of efficiency and automation in testing database isolation, while also demonstrating the value of adaptive and scalable testing strategies. Our work builds on these insights to provide a novel and efficient solution for weak isolation testing.
