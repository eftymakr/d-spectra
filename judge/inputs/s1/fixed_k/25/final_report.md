## Related Works

The integration of knowledge graphs (KGs) into large language models (LLMs) for claim verification has gained significant attention in recent years. KGs provide structured, semantically rich representations that are well-suited for reasoning, yet most existing verification methods rely on unstructured text corpora, limiting their ability to effectively leverage KGs \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/2306.08302)\]. This limitation is particularly evident in modern LLMs, which, despite their strong reasoning abilities, struggle with multi-step modular pipelines and reasoning over KGs without adaptation \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2312.11539)\]. 

To address these challenges, several approaches have been proposed. For instance, KGLens introduces a Thompson-sampling-inspired framework for measuring the alignment between KGs and LLMs, demonstrating that structured knowledge can be effectively used to probe factual accuracy \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/2306.08302)\]. Similarly, the work in \[[10](http://arxiv.org/abs/2504.14905)\] presents CRAVE, a framework that leverages conflicting rationales generated by LLMs for explainable and accurate claim verification. CRAVE includes modules for evidence retrieval, conflicting perspective reasoning, and a small language model-based judge, enhancing the robustness of claim verification systems.

Another approach, Reasoning-CV, proposes a fine-tuning method to enhance the verification capabilities of LLMs by leveraging the Chain-of-Thought (CoT) paradigm. This method avoids the need for decomposition into sub-claims and instead generates CoT-verification paths directly from the original claim \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/2505.12348)\]. The work in \[[16](http://arxiv.org/abs/2301.08914)\] further explores explainable claim verification using rationalization, introducing ExClaim, which provides a natural language explanation (rationale) to justify the model’s decision-making process. This aligns with the goal of making claim verification systems more transparent and interpretable.

The integration of LLMs with KGs has also been explored in the context of knowledge-enhanced pre-trained language models. For example, KELM proposes a novel framework that incorporates KGs into pre-trained language models (PLMs) using a hierarchical relational-graph-based message passing mechanism, allowing for dynamic knowledge integration \[[7](http://arxiv.org/abs/2109.04223)\]. This approach enables efficient incorporation of world knowledge from KGs into existing language models, improving performance on tasks such as machine reading comprehension.

Additionally, the work in \[[11](http://arxiv.org/abs/2410.12096)\] introduces LangGSL, a robust framework that integrates the strengths of pre-trained language models and graph structure learning models (GSLMs) to jointly enhance both node feature and graph structure learning. This approach filters noise in raw data using LLMs and refines the evolving graph structure using GSLMs, leading to more accurate and robust representations.
