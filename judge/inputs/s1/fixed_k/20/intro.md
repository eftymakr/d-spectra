## Related Works

Plug-and-play (PnP) and deep unrolling approaches have emerged as leading methods for solving inverse problems in imaging, particularly in single-pixel imaging (SPI) \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2311.01216)\]. PnP methods replace the proximal step in iterative algorithms such as ADMM or FISTA with a powerful denoiser, often a pre-trained deep neural network \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/1809.04693)\]. This approach is flexible for varying compression ratios (CRs) but can suffer from limitations in reconstruction accuracy and speed \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/2301.13731)\]. The flexibility of PnP methods is attributed to their ability to incorporate advanced denoisers without requiring explicit knowledge of the regularization function \[[4](http://arxiv.org/abs/2204.13940)\]. However, the convergence and stability of PnP algorithms have been a subject of ongoing research, particularly when using non-symmetric denoisers \[[5](http://arxiv.org/abs/2202.05820)\].

On the other hand, unrolling approaches transform iterative optimization processes into end-to-end trainable neural networks, often leading to better accuracy and faster inference \[[6](http://arxiv.org/abs/2312.16024)\]. These methods, such as those based on the Half-Quadratic Splitting (HQS) or ADMM, typically require fine-tuning or retraining when the compression ratio changes \[[7](http://arxiv.org/abs/2105.05177)\]. This limitation motivates the need for more adaptable and efficient methods that can handle varying CRs without retraining \[[8](http://arxiv.org/abs/2106.07795)\].

Recent works have explored the convergence properties of PnP algorithms, especially when using kernel denoisers \[[9](http://arxiv.org/abs/2402.12872)\]. For instance, it has been shown that PnP-ISTA with symmetric kernel denoisers can achieve linear convergence under mild conditions \[[10](http://arxiv.org/abs/2505.15318)\]. These findings have paved the way for more robust and theoretically grounded PnP methods \[[11](http://arxiv.org/abs/1902.03493)\]. Additionally, the use of learned denoisers has raised concerns about instabilities and hallucinated features, especially when compared to classical denoisers like BM3D \[[12](http://arxiv.org/abs/1811.00120)\]. Addressing these issues is crucial for the reliability of PnP methods in practical applications \[[13](http://arxiv.org/abs/2405.03854)\].

To overcome the limitations of both PnP and unrolling methods, recent research has focused on integrating the strengths of both approaches. For example, the work by \[[14](http://arxiv.org/abs/2410.02423)\] proposes a method that combines PnP with unrolling techniques, enabling flexible handling of varying CRs while maintaining high accuracy and speed. Similarly, \[[15](http://arxiv.org/abs/2012.00887)\] introduces a general proximal trajectory (PT) loss function to train unrolling networks, allowing them to approximate the proximal operator of an ideal explicit restoration regularizer \[[16](http://arxiv.org/abs/2004.03145)\]. These advancements highlight the potential of hybrid approaches in improving the performance of image restoration algorithms \[[17](http://arxiv.org/abs/1912.10557)\].
