## Related Works

Plug-and-play (PnP) and deep unrolling approaches have emerged as leading methods for solving inverse problems in imaging, particularly in single-pixel imaging (SPI) \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2311.01216)\]. PnP methods replace the proximal step in iterative algorithms such as ADMM or FISTA with a powerful denoiser, often a pre-trained deep neural network \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/1809.04693)\]. This approach is flexible for varying compression ratios (CRs) but can suffer from limitations in reconstruction accuracy and speed \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/2301.13731)\]. The flexibility of PnP methods is attributed to their ability to incorporate advanced denoisers without requiring explicit knowledge of the regularization function \[[4](http://arxiv.org/abs/2204.13940)\]. However, the convergence and stability of PnP algorithms have been a subject of ongoing research, particularly when using non-symmetric denoisers \[[5](http://arxiv.org/abs/2202.05820)\].

On the other hand, unrolling approaches transform iterative optimization processes into end-to-end trainable neural networks, often leading to better accuracy and faster inference \[[6](http://arxiv.org/abs/2312.16024)\]. These methods, such as those based on the Half-Quadratic Splitting (HQS) or ADMM, typically require fine-tuning or retraining when the compression ratio changes \[[7](http://arxiv.org/abs/2105.05177)\]. This limitation motivates the need for more adaptable and efficient methods that can handle varying CRs without retraining \[[8](http://arxiv.org/abs/2106.07795)\].

Recent works have explored the convergence properties of PnP algorithms, especially when using kernel denoisers \[[9](http://arxiv.org/abs/2402.12872)\]. For instance, it has been shown that PnP-ISTA with symmetric kernel denoisers can achieve linear convergence under mild conditions \[[10](http://arxiv.org/abs/2505.15318)\]. These findings have paved the way for more robust and theoretically grounded PnP methods \[[11](http://arxiv.org/abs/1902.03493)\]. Additionally, the use of learned denoisers has raised concerns about instabilities and hallucinated features, especially when compared to classical denoisers like BM3D \[[12](http://arxiv.org/abs/1811.00120)\]. Addressing these issues is crucial for the reliability of PnP methods in practical applications \[[13](http://arxiv.org/abs/2405.03854)\].

To overcome the limitations of both PnP and unrolling methods, recent research has focused on integrating the strengths of both approaches. For example, the work by \[[14](http://arxiv.org/abs/2410.02423)\] proposes a method that combines PnP with unrolling techniques, enabling flexible handling of varying CRs while maintaining high accuracy and speed. Similarly, \[[15](http://arxiv.org/abs/2012.00887)\] introduces a general proximal trajectory (PT) loss function to train unrolling networks, allowing them to approximate the proximal operator of an ideal explicit restoration regularizer \[[16](http://arxiv.org/abs/2004.03145)\]. These advancements highlight the potential of hybrid approaches in improving the performance of image restoration algorithms \[[17](http://arxiv.org/abs/1912.10557)\].

### <a id='category-convergence-and-stability-analysis'></a>Convergence and Stability Analysis: 6 papers found.
The convergence and stability analysis of Plug-and-Play (PnP) algorithms has been a focal point in recent research, addressing the theoretical guarantees of these methods which replace traditional proximal operators with deep denoisers \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2311.01216)\]. Early works highlighted that PnP algorithms, such as PnP-DRS and PnP-ISTA, often require restrictive stepsize or regularization parameter conditions for convergence, which can limit their effectiveness in image restoration tasks \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2311.01216)\]. To mitigate this, researchers have proposed relaxed versions of proximal algorithms, such as the PnP-$\alpha$PGD, which allow for a broader range of regularization parameters, enhancing the flexibility and accuracy of image restoration \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/1809.04693)\]. Additionally, studies have focused on the convergence properties of PnP methods using kernel denoisers, demonstrating that symmetric kernel denoisers can lead to global linear convergence under certain conditions \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/2301.13731)\]. Theoretical analyses have also explored the convergence of PnP-ISTA with generic kernel denoisers, proving fixed-point convergence for linear inverse problems like deblurring and inpainting \[[4](http://arxiv.org/abs/2204.13940)\]. Furthermore, the integration of scene-adapted priors into frameworks like ADMM has been shown to improve convergence and performance in specific imaging tasks \[[5](http://arxiv.org/abs/2202.05820)\]. These advancements collectively contribute to a more robust theoretical foundation for PnP methods, enabling their application across a wider range of inverse problems while maintaining convergence guarantees \[[6](http://arxiv.org/abs/2312.16024)\].

### <a id='category-algorithmic-theoretical-foundations'></a>Algorithmic Theoretical Foundations: 9 papers found.
Plug-and-play (PnP) methods have emerged as a powerful framework for solving imaging inverse problems by integrating advanced denoisers into iterative algorithms, offering state-of-the-art performance in various applications \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2311.01216)\]. These methods typically replace the proximal operator in traditional optimization algorithms like ISTA or ADMM with a denoiser, enabling flexible regularization without explicit knowledge of the forward model \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/1809.04693)\]. However, early PnP approaches lacked theoretical guarantees on convergence, limiting their rigorous application \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/2301.13731)\]. Recent work has addressed this by proving that a broader class of linear denoisers can be expressed as proximal maps of convex regularizers, allowing for convergence guarantees even with non-symmetric denoisers \[[4](http://arxiv.org/abs/2204.13940)\]. This theoretical foundation has enabled the development of provable PnP algorithms, such as PnP-ISTA and PnP-ADMM, which exhibit linear convergence under mild conditions \[[5](http://arxiv.org/abs/2202.05820)\]. Additionally, incremental and stochastic variants of PnP algorithms have been introduced to improve scalability and computational efficiency, particularly for large-scale datasets \[[6](http://arxiv.org/abs/2312.16024)\]. These advancements have also been applied to specific imaging tasks, such as image deblurring and compressed sensing MRI, where preconditioning and unrolling techniques have been used to accelerate convergence and enhance performance \[[7](http://arxiv.org/abs/2105.05177)\]. The integration of deep learning with PnP methods has further expanded their applicability, allowing for the use of pre-trained neural networks as denoisers while maintaining theoretical guarantees \[[8](http://arxiv.org/abs/2106.07795)\]. Overall, the PnP framework continues to evolve, combining the flexibility of traditional iterative methods with the power of modern denoisers to achieve both accuracy and efficiency in imaging reconstruction \[[9](http://arxiv.org/abs/2402.12872)\].
