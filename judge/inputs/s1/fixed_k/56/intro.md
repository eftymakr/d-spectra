## Related Works

Cross-Domain Sequential Recommendation (CDSR) has gained significant attention due to its ability to alleviate the cold-start problem and improve recommendation accuracy by leveraging user behavior across multiple domains \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2406.03085)\]. Traditional CDSR approaches typically rely on overlapping users to transfer knowledge between domains, which limits their applicability in real-world scenarios where such users are rare \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/2410.13588)\]. Moreover, these methods often struggle with modeling complex transition patterns from mixed behavior sequences \[[4](http://arxiv.org/abs/2304.03891)\]. Recent studies have explored the integration of Large Language Models (LLMs) into CDSR to enhance semantic understanding and improve cross-domain preference modeling \[[5](http://arxiv.org/abs/2411.19862)\].

Several works have attempted to address the overlap dilemma by utilizing non-overlapping users or leveraging domain-specific knowledge. For instance, \[[6](http://arxiv.org/abs/2503.07761)\] proposes a framework that enables cross-domain recommendations without requiring overlapping users, while \[[7](http://arxiv.org/abs/2209.10163)\] introduces a method that leverages domain-specific knowledge to improve recommendation accuracy. These approaches highlight the potential of non-overlapping cross-domain sequential recommendation (NCSR) and the importance of domain-specific modeling. However, they often face challenges in capturing the intricate relationships between items and users across domains \[[8](http://arxiv.org/abs/2311.04760)\].

In addition to addressing the overlap dilemma, researchers have also focused on improving the modeling of complex transition patterns. \[[9](http://arxiv.org/abs/2304.04218)\] introduces a method that integrates inter-sequence and intra-sequence item interactions to better capture user preferences. Similarly, \[[10](http://arxiv.org/abs/2311.04590)\] proposes a contrastive cross-domain infomax objective to enhance the correlation between single- and cross-domain user representations. These approaches demonstrate the importance of modeling both intra- and inter-sequence interactions for accurate cross-domain recommendations.

The integration of LLMs into CDSR has also been explored in recent works. \[[11](http://arxiv.org/abs/2405.17890)\] investigates the use of LLMs for sequential recommendation, demonstrating their potential in capturing semantic relationships between items and users. \[[12](http://arxiv.org/abs/2502.15694)\] further explores the use of LLMs for cross-domain sequential recommendation by incorporating item image information to better capture visual preferences. These studies highlight the growing interest in leveraging LLMs for CDSR and their potential to improve recommendation accuracy.

Moreover, several works have focused on the challenges of data sparsity and cold-start problems in CDSR. \[[13](http://arxiv.org/abs/2104.08490)\] proposes a dual learning approach that transfers information between domains iteratively to improve recommendation performance. \[[14](http://arxiv.org/abs/2309.08420)\] introduces a federated learning framework that enables cross-domain recommendations while preserving user privacy. These approaches demonstrate the importance of addressing data sparsity and cold-start problems in CDSR.
