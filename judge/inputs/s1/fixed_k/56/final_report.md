## Related Works

Cross-Domain Sequential Recommendation (CDSR) has gained significant attention due to its ability to alleviate the cold-start problem and improve recommendation accuracy by leveraging user behavior across multiple domains \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2406.03085)\]. Traditional CDSR approaches typically rely on overlapping users to transfer knowledge between domains, which limits their applicability in real-world scenarios where such users are rare \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/2410.13588)\]. Moreover, these methods often struggle with modeling complex transition patterns from mixed behavior sequences \[[4](http://arxiv.org/abs/2304.03891)\]. Recent studies have explored the integration of Large Language Models (LLMs) into CDSR to enhance semantic understanding and improve cross-domain preference modeling \[[5](http://arxiv.org/abs/2411.19862)\].

Several works have attempted to address the overlap dilemma by utilizing non-overlapping users or leveraging domain-specific knowledge. For instance, \[[6](http://arxiv.org/abs/2503.07761)\] proposes a framework that enables cross-domain recommendations without requiring overlapping users, while \[[7](http://arxiv.org/abs/2209.10163)\] introduces a method that leverages domain-specific knowledge to improve recommendation accuracy. These approaches highlight the potential of non-overlapping cross-domain sequential recommendation (NCSR) and the importance of domain-specific modeling. However, they often face challenges in capturing the intricate relationships between items and users across domains \[[8](http://arxiv.org/abs/2311.04760)\].

In addition to addressing the overlap dilemma, researchers have also focused on improving the modeling of complex transition patterns. \[[9](http://arxiv.org/abs/2304.04218)\] introduces a method that integrates inter-sequence and intra-sequence item interactions to better capture user preferences. Similarly, \[[10](http://arxiv.org/abs/2311.04590)\] proposes a contrastive cross-domain infomax objective to enhance the correlation between single- and cross-domain user representations. These approaches demonstrate the importance of modeling both intra- and inter-sequence interactions for accurate cross-domain recommendations.

The integration of LLMs into CDSR has also been explored in recent works. \[[11](http://arxiv.org/abs/2405.17890)\] investigates the use of LLMs for sequential recommendation, demonstrating their potential in capturing semantic relationships between items and users. \[[12](http://arxiv.org/abs/2502.15694)\] further explores the use of LLMs for cross-domain sequential recommendation by incorporating item image information to better capture visual preferences. These studies highlight the growing interest in leveraging LLMs for CDSR and their potential to improve recommendation accuracy.

Moreover, several works have focused on the challenges of data sparsity and cold-start problems in CDSR. \[[13](http://arxiv.org/abs/2104.08490)\] proposes a dual learning approach that transfers information between domains iteratively to improve recommendation performance. \[[14](http://arxiv.org/abs/2309.08420)\] introduces a federated learning framework that enables cross-domain recommendations while preserving user privacy. These approaches demonstrate the importance of addressing data sparsity and cold-start problems in CDSR.

### <a id='category-inter-sequence-and-intra-sequence-interaction-modeling'></a>Inter-Sequence and Intra-Sequence Interaction Modeling: 5 papers found.
Cross-domain sequential recommendation (CDSR) focuses on predicting future user interactions by leveraging historical interactions across multiple domains, with a particular emphasis on capturing both intra-sequence and inter-sequence item interactions \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/2401.04971)\]. Existing approaches often rely on single-domain user preferences and transfer modules to infer cross-domain preferences, but these methods are limited by the bottleneck of the transfer module and neglect inter-sequence relationships \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/2401.04971)\]. To address these challenges, recent works have proposed models that simultaneously leverage intra- and inter-sequence interactions to jointly learn single- and cross-domain user preferences \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/2401.04971)\]. For instance, C2DSR employs graph neural networks to mine inter-sequence collaborative relationships and sequential attentive encoders to capture intra-sequence patterns, followed by two training objectives to obtain user representations \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/2401.04971)\]. Similarly, IFCDSR integrates item image information with intra- and inter-sequence interactions using a frozen CLIP model and multiple attention layers to enhance cross-domain interest modeling \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/2410.13588)\]. Additionally, SCSR, which involves shared accounts across domains, has been addressed with graph-based solutions like TiDA-GCN, which incorporates time interval information and domain-aware graph convolutions to improve recommendation accuracy \[[4](http://arxiv.org/abs/2304.03891)\]. Furthermore, RL-ISN introduces a reinforcement learning-based approach to refine domain-specific user representations by focusing on recent behaviors and filtering irrelevant interactions \[[5](http://arxiv.org/abs/2411.19862)\]. These methods collectively highlight the importance of modeling both intra- and inter-sequence interactions to improve cross-domain recommendation performance.

### <a id='category-cold-start-and-data-sparsity-challenges'></a>Cold-Start and Data Sparsity Challenges: 5 papers found.
Cross-domain Sequential Recommendation (CDSR) addresses the data sparsity and cold-start problems in single-domain sequential recommendation (SDSR) by leveraging information from multiple domains \[[4](http://arxiv.org/abs/2304.03891)\]. Existing approaches rely on overlapping users with abundant behaviors to propagate cross-domain information, but this limits their effectiveness in real-world scenarios where most users have sparse or no interactions across domains \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/2410.13588)\]. To overcome these limitations, recent work has proposed frameworks like DDGHM, which integrates dual dynamic graph modeling and hybrid metric training to capture both intra-domain and inter-domain sequential transitions while improving prediction accuracy \[[7](http://arxiv.org/abs/2209.10163)\]. Additionally, the Adaptive Multi-Interest Debiasing (AMID) framework addresses open-world assumptions by adapting to data distribution shifts and reducing biases across domains through a multi-interest information module and a doubly robust estimator \[[10](http://arxiv.org/abs/2311.04590)\]. These methods highlight the challenges of retaining single-domain preferences while integrating cross-domain influence and the need for more robust models in real-world settings. Furthermore, the use of large language models (LLMs) has shown promise in bridging semantic gaps and capturing user preferences through reasoning capabilities, offering a new direction for CDSR \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2406.03085)\].

[Snippet]: «Cross-domain Sequential Recommendation (CSR) which leverages user sequence data from multiple domains has received extensive attention in recent years. However, the existing CSR methods require sharing origin user data across domains, which violates the General Data Protection Regulation (GDPR). Thus, it is necessary to combine federated learning (FL) and CSR to fully utilize knowledge from different domains while preserving data privacy. Nonetheless, the sequence feature heterogeneity across different domains significantly impacts the overall performance of FL. In this paper, we propose FedDCSR, a novel federated cross-domain sequential recommendation framework via disentangled representation learning. Specifically, to address the sequence feature heterogeneity across domains, we introduce an approach called inter-intra domain sequence representation disentanglement (SRD) to disentangle the user sequence features into domain-shared and domain-exclusive features. In addition, we design an intra domain contrastive infomax (CIM) strategy to learn richer domain-exclusive features of users by performing data augmentation on user sequences. Extensive experiments on three real-world scenarios demonstrate that FedDCSR achieves significant improvements over existing baselines.»
