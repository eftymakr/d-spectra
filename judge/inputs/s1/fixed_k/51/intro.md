## Related Works

Federated unlearning has emerged as a critical research area in the context of privacy-preserving machine learning, particularly in response to the "right to be forgotten" regulation \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2412.19069)\]. This concept is especially relevant in federated learning (FL), where models are trained across decentralized data sources without sharing raw data \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/2401.05146)\]. In federated online learning to rank (FOLTR), the challenge of unlearning becomes even more complex due to the continuous nature of model updates and the need to preserve privacy while optimizing search results based on implicit feedback \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/2310.20448)\].

Recent studies have highlighted the limitations of existing unlearning methods, particularly in terms of evaluation practices. Most prior work has relied on single evaluation metrics, which may not comprehensively capture the success of unlearning \[[4](http://arxiv.org/abs/2310.19218)\]. This has led to calls for more rigorous evaluation frameworks that can assess both under-unlearning and over-unlearning scenarios \[[5](http://arxiv.org/abs/2406.03078)\]. Our work builds on these insights by introducing a set of adapted and newly proposed evaluation metrics to rigorously assess the effectiveness of unlearning strategies in FOLTR \[[6](http://arxiv.org/abs/2403.02437)\].

Several approaches have been proposed to enable efficient and secure federated unlearning. For example, FedEraser \[[7](http://arxiv.org/abs/2308.12502)\] introduces a method that reconstructs an unlearned model by leveraging historical parameter updates stored at the central server, significantly reducing the time required for unlearning compared to retraining from scratch. Similarly, the VeriFi framework \[[8](http://arxiv.org/abs/2306.02216)\] introduces a systematic way to verify the unlearning effect, allowing users to independently confirm that their data has been removed from the global model. This is particularly important in FL, where unlearning effects can be canceled out by contributions from other participants \[[9](http://arxiv.org/abs/2012.13891)\].

In addition to verification, there has been growing interest in the efficiency and scalability of unlearning methods. The FedAU framework \[[10](http://arxiv.org/abs/2205.12709)\] incorporates a lightweight auxiliary unlearning module into the learning process, enabling efficient and effective unlearning without requiring additional time-consuming steps. This approach supports unlearning at various levels of granularity, including individual data samples and entire clients \[[11](http://arxiv.org/abs/2405.17462)\]. Another notable contribution is the FATS framework \[[12](http://arxiv.org/abs/2207.05521)\], which introduces a TV-stable FL algorithm that enables exact federated unlearning with communication efficiency and provable guarantees. This framework ensures that the unlearned model is statistically indistinguishable from the one trained without the deleted data \[[13](http://arxiv.org/abs/2405.15474)\].
