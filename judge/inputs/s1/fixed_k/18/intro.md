## Related Works

Anomaly detection has become a critical component in modern industrial systems, particularly in the context of intelligent manufacturing and quality control. Numerous approaches have been proposed to address the challenges of detecting anomalies in time-series data, images, and multimodal settings. However, most existing methods face limitations in terms of computational efficiency, memory usage, and adaptability to real-world deployment scenarios. For instance, traditional deep learning models such as convolutional neural networks (CNNs) often require significant computational resources, making them unsuitable for deployment on resource-constrained embedded devices \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2303.03611)\]. To mitigate this issue, researchers have proposed memory-efficient architectures, such as depthwise separable CNNs, which can reduce model size by up to 90% compared to standard CNNs \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2303.03611)\]. Additionally, techniques like in-place computation and patch-by-patch memory rescheduling have been introduced to further minimize peak memory consumption during inference \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2303.03611)\].

In the realm of industrial anomaly detection, methods such as the tiled ensemble approach have been developed to address the challenge of high-resolution image processing by dividing input images into tiles and training dedicated models for each tile \[[4](http://arxiv.org/abs/2403.04932)\]. This approach allows for efficient memory usage while maintaining high detection accuracy. Furthermore, the use of lightweight models, such as MobileSAM, has gained traction due to their ability to run on edge devices with limited computational resources \[[5](http://arxiv.org/abs/2306.14289)\]. MobileSAM, for example, is more than 60 times smaller than the original SAM and achieves comparable performance, making it suitable for mobile and embedded applications \[[5](http://arxiv.org/abs/2306.14289)\].

Another key area of research involves the use of encoder-decoder architectures for time-series anomaly detection. For example, the EncDec-AD model leverages long short-term memory (LSTM) networks to reconstruct normal time-series behavior and detect anomalies based on reconstruction error \[[6](http://arxiv.org/abs/1607.00148)\]. This approach has been shown to be robust across a variety of time-series types, including periodic, aperiodic, and quasi-periodic data \[[6](http://arxiv.org/abs/1607.00148)\]. Similarly, the use of synthetic data and digital twin systems has been explored to generate training datasets for anomaly detection in industrial settings \[[10](http://arxiv.org/abs/2011.06296)\].

In addition, methods that incorporate multimodal data, such as RGB images and 3D point clouds, have been proposed to improve detection accuracy by leveraging the complementarity of different modalities \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/2405.13571)\]. However, these methods often face challenges in handling incomplete modalities during inference, which is why approaches like cross-modal distillation have been introduced to enable effective training with multimodal data and inference with fewer modalities \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/2405.13571)\].
