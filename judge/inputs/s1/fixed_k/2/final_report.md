## Related Works

Nonverbal communication plays a crucial role in human interaction, encompassing gestures, facial expressions, and body language that convey intent and emotion \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/1811.09362)\]. Existing large language models (LLMs) often lack the capability to effectively integrate these nonverbal elements, thereby limiting their ability to create immersive conversational experiences \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/1811.09362)\]. This gap has spurred recent research efforts aimed at enhancing the multimodal understanding of LLMs through the incorporation of nonverbal cues \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/1811.09362)\].

Several studies have explored the integration of nonverbal signals into AI systems. For instance, \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/2502.16671)\] introduced a dataset called MimeQA, which focuses on nonverbal social interactions through mime videos. This dataset is used to evaluate video large language models (VideoLLMs), revealing their limited accuracy in nonverbal social reasoning tasks \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/2502.16671)\]. Similarly, \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/2410.14050)\] proposed the RAVEN model, which dynamically shifts word representations based on nonverbal cues, achieving competitive performance in multimodal sentiment analysis and emotion recognition \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/2410.14050)\].

The importance of nonverbal cues in educational settings has also been highlighted. \[[4](http://arxiv.org/abs/1712.04314)\] introduced a computational model for analyzing nonverbal social behavior in classrooms, assessing the nonverbal immediacy (NVI) of teachers using RGB videos. This model demonstrated strong correlations with educational outcomes, underscoring the value of nonverbal cues in teaching effectiveness \[[4](http://arxiv.org/abs/1712.04314)\].

In the realm of human-robot interaction, \[[5](http://arxiv.org/abs/2404.07214)\] emphasized the significance of nonverbal cues in fostering natural and accessible interactions. They proposed a series of nonverbal codes that can be translated into design patterns for human-robot interaction (HRI), enhancing the perceived "aliveness" or "social agency" of robots \[[5](http://arxiv.org/abs/2404.07214)\].

Recent advancements in multimodal models have also focused on improving the integration of nonverbal cues. \[[6](http://arxiv.org/abs/2410.21652)\] introduced the VENUS dataset, a large-scale annotated video dataset with time-aligned text, facial expressions, and body language, which serves as a foundation for training models like MARS. This dataset enables the development of models that can understand and generate nonverbal cues alongside text \[[6](http://arxiv.org/abs/2410.21652)\].

This work highlights the potential of nonverbal cues in evaluating and improving human communication abilities.

[Snippet]: «Massive web datasets play a key role in the success of large vision-language models like CLIP and Flamingo. However, the raw web data is noisy, and existing filtering methods to reduce noise often come at the expense of data diversity. Our work focuses on caption quality as one major source of noise, and studies how generated captions can increase the utility of web-scraped datapoints with nondescript text. Through exploring different mixing strategies for raw and generated captions, we outperform the best filtering method proposed by the DataComp benchmark by 2% on ImageNet and 4% on average across 38 tasks, given a candidate pool of 128M image-text pairs. Our best approach is also 2x better at Flickr and MS-COCO retrieval. We then analyze what makes synthetic captions an effective source of text supervision. In experimenting with different image captioning models, we also demonstrate that the performance of a model on standard image captioning benchmarks (e.g., NoCaps CIDEr) is not a reliable indicator of the utility of the captions it generates for multimodal training. Finally, our experiments with using generated captions at DataComp's large scale (1.28B image-text pairs) offer insights into the limitations of synthetic text, as well as the importance of image curation with increasing training data quantity. The synthetic captions used in our experiments are now available on HuggingFace.»
