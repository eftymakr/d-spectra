## Related Works

Semi-supervised learning (SSL) has emerged as a powerful paradigm for improving model performance by leveraging both labeled and unlabeled data. However, traditional SSL methods assume that unlabeled data comes from the same distribution as the labeled data, which is often violated in real-world scenarios where unlabeled data may contain out-of-distribution (OOD) samples, i.e., outliers \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2311.10572)\]. These outliers, typically treated as noise, can significantly degrade SSL performance \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/2303.12091)\]. To address this challenge, several works have explored open-set semi-supervised learning (OSSL), where the goal is to classify inliers and detect outliers simultaneously \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/2308.13168)\].

Early approaches in OSSL focused on outlier detection using binary classifiers, but these methods suffered from scalability issues and were unable to distinguish between different types of uncertainty \[[4](http://arxiv.org/abs/2105.14148)\]. More recent works, such as Adaptive Negative Evidential Deep Learning (ANEDL), have introduced frameworks that quantify uncertainty through evidential deep learning (EDL), enabling more nuanced handling of OOD samples \[[5](http://arxiv.org/abs/2107.08943)\]. Similarly, OpenMatch integrates novelty detection with consistency regularization, improving outlier detection by leveraging one-vs-all (OVA) classifiers \[[6](http://arxiv.org/abs/2405.06979)\]. These methods demonstrate that effective OSSL requires both robust outlier detection and inlier classification.

Another line of research, such as IOMatch, proposes a framework that jointly utilizes inliers and outliers during training, even when distinguishing between them is difficult \[[7](http://arxiv.org/abs/2301.10127)\]. This approach avoids the performance degradation caused by unreliable outlier detectors when labeled data is scarce \[[8](http://arxiv.org/abs/2505.13317)\]. Similarly, OpenCoS leverages self-supervised contrastive learning to identify out-of-class samples in unlabeled data, enabling more effective semi-supervised learning in open-set scenarios \[[9](http://arxiv.org/abs/2403.10658)\]. These methods highlight the importance of explicitly handling OOD data during training.

Recent works have also focused on improving the robustness of SSL by selectively leveraging open-set data. For example, WiseOpen introduces a gradient-variance-based selection mechanism to filter out unfriendly open-set samples, enhancing the model's ability to generalize to in-distribution data \[[10](http://arxiv.org/abs/2201.09196)\]. This aligns with the idea that not all open-set data should be used indiscriminately, as some may negatively impact performance \[[11](http://arxiv.org/abs/2401.14387)\].

In addition to data selection, some methods have focused on improving the learning process itself. For instance, FreeMatch introduces a self-adaptive thresholding mechanism to dynamically adjust the confidence threshold during training, improving performance when labeled data is scarce \[[12](http://arxiv.org/abs/2201.06247)\]. ### <a id='category-open-set-semi-supervised-learning-(ossl)-frameworks'></a>Open-Set Semi-Supervised Learning (OSSL) Frameworks: 12 papers found.
Open-set semi-supervised learning (OSSL) addresses the challenge where unlabeled data may contain outliers from novel categories not present in the labeled set, which can degrade model performance \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2311.10572)\]. Existing methods often rely on outlier detection via binary classifiers or assume that outliers are harmful and should be excluded \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/2303.12091)\]. However, these approaches may suffer from scalability issues and fail to distinguish different types of uncertainty \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/2303.12091)\]. Some methods, such as IOMatch, propose to jointly utilize inliers and outliers by introducing a multi-binary classifier that treats all outliers as a single class, improving performance even with limited labels \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/2308.13168)\]. Others, like OpenMatch, combine FixMatch with novelty detection using one-vs-all classifiers to detect outliers and apply a soft-consistency regularization loss to enhance outlier detection \[[4](http://arxiv.org/abs/2105.14148)\]. Additionally, OpenCoS leverages self-supervised contrastive learning to identify out-of-class samples and uses one-hot and soft-labels for inliers and outliers, respectively, to improve SSL performance \[[5](http://arxiv.org/abs/2107.08943)\]. Meanwhile, WiseOpen introduces a data selection strategy that selectively uses open-set data during training, avoiding the negative impact of unfriendly samples \[[6](http://arxiv.org/abs/2405.06979)\]. A more recent approach, SSB, proposes to incorporate high-confidence pseudo-labeled data and use non-linear transformations to separate inlier and outlier features, leading to improved performance in both classification and outlier detection \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2311.10572)\]. These methods highlight the ongoing efforts to enhance robustness in OSSL by addressing the challenges of outlier detection, data selection, and model generalization \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2311.10572)\]\[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/2303.12091)\]\[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/2308.13168)\]\[[4](http://arxiv.org/abs/2105.14148)\]\[[5](http://arxiv.org/abs/2107.08943)\]\[[6](http://arxiv.org/abs/2405.06979)\].

### <a id='category-self-supervised-contrastive-learning'></a>Self-Supervised Contrastive Learning: 2 papers found.
In the realm of self-supervised contrastive learning, recent research has highlighted the potential of semi-supervised learning (SSL) and pre-trained models in addressing label scarcity. SSL methods, which leverage both labeled and unlabeled data, have shown promising results in various tasks, particularly in image classification \[[14](http://arxiv.org/abs/2503.17057)\]. However, the advent of large foundation models has shifted the focus towards pre-trained models, such as CLIP, which demonstrate high efficiency and strong performance on SSL benchmarks, even under limited supervision \[[8](http://arxiv.org/abs/2505.13317)\]. This has led to a critical question: should researchers prioritize SSL methods or pre-trained models when labeled data is scarce? The results of recent studies suggest that pre-trained models have surpassed SSL in many scenarios, indicating that the era of SSL may have reached its "Waterloo" \[[8](http://arxiv.org/abs/2505.13317)\]. Moreover, the integration of multi-modal large language models (MLLMs) into image classification tasks has revealed both opportunities and challenges, as these models, despite their massive parameter scales, still face performance limitations \[[8](http://arxiv.org/abs/2505.13317)\]. These findings underscore the need for further exploration into the synergy between SSL and pre-trained models, as well as the potential of novel frameworks that can better handle the complexities of real-world data \[[14](http://arxiv.org/abs/2503.17057)\].

### <a id='category-consistency-regularization-approaches'></a>Consistency Regularization Approaches: 8 papers found.
Consistency regularization has emerged as a cornerstone in semi-supervised learning (SSL), aiming to improve model performance by leveraging unlabeled data through mechanisms that enforce prediction consistency \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2311.10572)\]. However, traditional approaches often assume that labeled and unlabeled data share the same distribution, which is frequently violated in real-world scenarios where unlabeled data may contain outliers or out-of-distribution samples \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/2303.12091)\]. To address this, recent works have explored methods to enhance robustness in open-set SSL settings. For instance, InterLUDE introduces a dual-component approach that combines embedding fusion with a novel consistency loss, achieving significant improvements on benchmarks like STL-10 \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/2308.13168)\]. Similarly, Inconsistency Masks (IM) reframes model disagreement as a signal for uncertainty, using ensemble-based masks to filter inconsistent regions and improve segmentation accuracy \[[4](http://arxiv.org/abs/2105.14148)\]. Contrastive regularization has also been proposed to enhance consistency-based methods by clustering unlabeled data and improving feature aggregation, leading to better performance with fewer iterations \[[5](http://arxiv.org/abs/2107.08943)\]. In the context of federated learning, FedMatch tackles semi-supervised learning by introducing inter-client consistency losses and parameter decomposition, outperforming both local and federated baselines \[[6](http://arxiv.org/abs/2405.06979)\]. Additionally, methods like SDA-MVS address distribution-gap ambiguities in multi-view stereo tasks by employing style consistency losses to bridge gaps between labeled and unlabeled data \[[7](http://arxiv.org/abs/2301.10127)\]. These approaches highlight the evolving strategies to refine consistency regularization, addressing challenges such as outliers, distribution mismatches, and limited labeled data \[[8](http://arxiv.org/abs/2505.13317)\].

### <a id='category-outlier-treatment-in-ssl'></a>Outlier Treatment in SSL: 2 papers found.
The category "Outlier Treatment in SSL" explores methods to improve semi-supervised learning (SSL) by addressing the impact of outliers, which are often treated as noise and degrade model performance. Existing approaches typically rely on unsupervised partition-based algorithms like k-means, which struggle with non-convex clusters and fail to incorporate outlier detection effectively \[[15](http://arxiv.org/abs/2208.05561)\]. To overcome these limitations, researchers have proposed density-based algorithms that integrate outlier detection with clustering, such as SSDBCODI, which uses reachability-score, local-density-score, and similarity-score to identify potential outliers and adjust clustering results \[[15](http://arxiv.org/abs/2208.05561)\]. Additionally, challenges in applying deep learning to private datasets, such as domain shift and lack of labels, have led to the development of methods like Tabular Data Adapters (TDA), which generate soft labels for unlabeled data by aligning private datasets with public ones through autoencoders \[[20](http://arxiv.org/abs/2504.20862)\]. These approaches highlight the need for robust outlier treatment in SSL, especially in open-set scenarios where labeled data is limited and outliers are prevalent. By leveraging multiple models with different biases, recent frameworks like Diversify and Conquer (DAC) aim to enhance robustness by exploiting prediction disagreements to detect outliers and improve generalization \[[15](http://arxiv.org/abs/2208.05561)\].
