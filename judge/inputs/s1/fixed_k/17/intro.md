## Related Works

Semi-supervised learning (SSL) has emerged as a powerful paradigm for improving model performance by leveraging both labeled and unlabeled data. However, traditional SSL methods assume that unlabeled data comes from the same distribution as the labeled data, which is often violated in real-world scenarios where unlabeled data may contain out-of-distribution (OOD) samples, i.e., outliers \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2311.10572)\]. These outliers, typically treated as noise, can significantly degrade SSL performance \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/2303.12091)\]. To address this challenge, several works have explored open-set semi-supervised learning (OSSL), where the goal is to classify inliers and detect outliers simultaneously \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/2308.13168)\].

Early approaches in OSSL focused on outlier detection using binary classifiers, but these methods suffered from scalability issues and were unable to distinguish between different types of uncertainty \[[4](http://arxiv.org/abs/2105.14148)\]. More recent works, such as Adaptive Negative Evidential Deep Learning (ANEDL), have introduced frameworks that quantify uncertainty through evidential deep learning (EDL), enabling more nuanced handling of OOD samples \[[5](http://arxiv.org/abs/2107.08943)\]. Similarly, OpenMatch integrates novelty detection with consistency regularization, improving outlier detection by leveraging one-vs-all (OVA) classifiers \[[6](http://arxiv.org/abs/2405.06979)\]. These methods demonstrate that effective OSSL requires both robust outlier detection and inlier classification.

Another line of research, such as IOMatch, proposes a framework that jointly utilizes inliers and outliers during training, even when distinguishing between them is difficult \[[7](http://arxiv.org/abs/2301.10127)\]. This approach avoids the performance degradation caused by unreliable outlier detectors when labeled data is scarce \[[8](http://arxiv.org/abs/2505.13317)\]. Similarly, OpenCoS leverages self-supervised contrastive learning to identify out-of-class samples in unlabeled data, enabling more effective semi-supervised learning in open-set scenarios \[[9](http://arxiv.org/abs/2403.10658)\]. These methods highlight the importance of explicitly handling OOD data during training.

Recent works have also focused on improving the robustness of SSL by selectively leveraging open-set data. For example, WiseOpen introduces a gradient-variance-based selection mechanism to filter out unfriendly open-set samples, enhancing the model's ability to generalize to in-distribution data \[[10](http://arxiv.org/abs/2201.09196)\]. This aligns with the idea that not all open-set data should be used indiscriminately, as some may negatively impact performance \[[11](http://arxiv.org/abs/2401.14387)\].

In addition to data selection, some methods have focused on improving the learning process itself. For instance, FreeMatch introduces a self-adaptive thresholding mechanism to dynamically adjust the confidence threshold during training, improving performance when labeled data is scarce \[[12](http://arxiv.org/abs/2201.06247)\].
