## Related Works

Federated Learning (FL) has emerged as a promising paradigm for collaborative machine learning while preserving data privacy \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2504.01921)\]. However, existing FL approaches face significant challenges due to data heterogeneity and client-side computational constraints \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/2504.11216)\]. Early works such as FedAvg \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/2206.10032)\] and SCAFFOLD \[[4](http://arxiv.org/abs/2011.06393)\] laid the foundation for FL by enabling distributed training without sharing raw data. Despite their success, these methods often fail to account for the heterogeneity of data distribution across clients, leading to suboptimal model performance \[[5](http://arxiv.org/abs/2205.12493)\].

Recent research has focused on addressing data heterogeneity through client selection strategies. For example, FedDiverse \[[6](http://arxiv.org/abs/2207.08187)\] introduces a client selection algorithm that promotes collaboration between clients with complementary data distributions, enhancing model performance while maintaining low communication overhead. Similarly, WAFFLE \[[7](http://arxiv.org/abs/2110.06978)\] leverages stochastic control variates to improve convergence by weighting client contributions based on their proximity to the target model. These methods demonstrate the importance of adaptive client selection in heterogeneous FL settings.

In addition to client selection, several works have explored the use of self-supervised learning in FL to mitigate the lack of labeled data. Hetero-SSFL \[[8](http://arxiv.org/abs/2411.01240)\] proposes a framework that enables self-supervised learning across heterogeneous clients, allowing collaborative representation learning without requiring labeled data. This approach has shown promising results in improving model performance on non-convex objectives. Similarly, FedCor \[[9](http://arxiv.org/abs/2206.05507)\] introduces a correlation-based client selection strategy that significantly improves convergence rates by modeling loss correlations between clients.

Another line of research addresses the issue of data and model heterogeneity in FL. MDH-FL \[[10](http://arxiv.org/abs/2309.14675)\] proposes a method that simultaneously exploits both data and model heterogeneity by using knowledge distillation and symmetric loss to minimize their impact on model performance. This approach has been validated on medical datasets, demonstrating its effectiveness in real-world scenarios. Additionally, FedFN \[[11](http://arxiv.org/abs/2408.09545)\] introduces feature normalization to alleviate data heterogeneity, showing improved performance even when applied to pretrained models.

To further enhance FL performance, several works have explored the integration of differential privacy \[[12](http://arxiv.org/abs/2209.05424)\] and secure aggregation \[[13](http://arxiv.org/abs/2006.12097)\] to ensure data confidentiality. These techniques are crucial for maintaining privacy in FL while ensuring model accuracy. Furthermore, the use of conformal predictions \[[14](http://arxiv.org/abs/2007.06081)\] has been proposed to improve the calibration and interpretability of federated models, addressing concerns related to poor calibration and lack of interpretability in clinical settings.

### <a id='category-client-selection-strategies'></a>Client Selection Strategies: 3 papers found.
Client selection strategies in federated learning (FL) are critical for addressing data and latency heterogeneity among clients, which significantly impact model convergence and performance \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2504.01921)\]. Prior research has focused on mitigating these challenges through various methods, such as optimizing client selection to reduce runtime and improve accuracy \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2504.01921)\]. For instance, FedEntOpt introduces a method that maximizes the entropy of the aggregated label distribution, ensuring diverse data exposure and improving classification accuracy by up to 6% in standard settings \[[8](http://arxiv.org/abs/2411.01240)\]. Similarly, FedCorr employs a correlation-based client selection strategy, leveraging Gaussian Processes (GP) to model loss correlations and enhance convergence rates by 34%–99% on benchmark datasets like FMNIST and CIFAR-10 \[[24](http://arxiv.org/abs/2103.13822)\]. These approaches highlight the importance of adaptive selection mechanisms that account for both data distribution and communication constraints. Additionally, the integration of entropy maximization and correlation modeling demonstrates the potential for combining multiple strategies to further improve efficiency and robustness in real-world FL scenarios \[[8](http://arxiv.org/abs/2411.01240)\]\[[24](http://arxiv.org/abs/2103.13822)\].

### <a id='category-data-and-model-heterogeneity-solutions'></a>Data and Model Heterogeneity Solutions: 14 papers found.
Federated Learning (FL) has emerged as a critical paradigm for collaborative machine learning while preserving data privacy, yet it faces significant challenges due to data and model heterogeneity across clients \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2504.01921)\]. Non-identically distributed (non-IID) data, disjoint classes, and signal multi-modality hinder model convergence and performance \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/2504.11216)\]. To address these issues, researchers have proposed various solutions, such as FedDiverse, which introduces a client selection algorithm to leverage complementary data distributions \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2504.01921)\], and SDA-FL, which uses synthetic data generated by GANs to align client data distributions \[[4](http://arxiv.org/abs/2011.06393)\]. Additionally, methods like WAFFLE employ weighted averaging based on client update distances to improve convergence and accuracy \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/2206.10032)\]. Furthermore, FedCompass introduces a computing power-aware scheduler to handle device heterogeneity, improving both convergence speed and model accuracy \[[5](http://arxiv.org/abs/2205.12493)\]. In parallel, approaches like MDH-FL tackle both data and model heterogeneity by combining knowledge distillation and symmetric loss functions to enhance global model efficiency \[[9](http://arxiv.org/abs/2206.05507)\]. These methods collectively highlight the ongoing efforts to address FL challenges, ensuring robustness, scalability, and performance in real-world heterogeneous environments.

### <a id='category-privacy-preservation-techniques'></a>Privacy Preservation Techniques: 5 papers found.
Privacy preservation techniques in federated learning (FL) have garnered significant attention, particularly in addressing data privacy and communication efficiency. Existing approaches often focus on horizontally partitioned datasets, where clients share the same feature space and can train models independently \[[20](http://arxiv.org/abs/2305.11236)\]. However, vertical FL, where data points are scattered across clients, requires secure gradient exchange and careful privacy considerations to prevent leakage \[[14](http://arxiv.org/abs/2007.06081)\]. Techniques such as perturbed local embedding and secure aggregation have been introduced to enhance privacy and improve performance \[[14](http://arxiv.org/abs/2007.06081)\]. Additionally, differential privacy (DP) has been integrated into FL frameworks to ensure robustness against privacy risks, though it can impact model performance, especially with non-i.i.d. data \[[25](http://arxiv.org/abs/2402.02230)\]. Recent work has also explored individualized privacy settings, allowing clients to choose their preferred privacy levels, which has led to more flexible and efficient FL systems \[[29](http://arxiv.org/abs/2501.17634)\]. These advancements highlight the ongoing efforts to balance privacy and utility in FL, ensuring both security and effectiveness across diverse applications.

systems by strategically selecting the most informative unlabeled data for
annotation, but performing it individually may still be insufficient due to
restricted data diversity and annotation budget. Federated Active Learning
(FAL) addresses this by facilitating collaborative data selection and model
training, while preserving the confidentiality of raw data samples. Yet,
existing FAL methods fail to account for the heterogeneity of data distribution
across clients and the associated fluctuations in global and local model
parameters, adversely affecting model accuracy.
