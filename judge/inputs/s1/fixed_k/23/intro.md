## Related Works

Automatic geometric calibration of radar and camera systems has been an active research area, with several notable approaches proposed in recent years. Early methods often relied on target-based calibration, which required specialized infrastructure and controlled environments \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2307.15264)\]. For instance, a method proposed in \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2307.15264)\] uses a single corner reflector (CR) and iteratively collects radar and camera data to obtain extrinsic calibration, leveraging the perspective-n-point (PnP) problem and RANSAC for robustness. However, these methods are not suitable for real-time or online calibration due to their reliance on external targets \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2307.15264)\].

More recently, targetless calibration methods have gained traction, especially for autonomous driving applications. A method introduced in \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/2309.00787)\] leverages deep learning to extract common features from raw radar data and camera images, enabling online targetless calibration without the need for dedicated calibration targets. This approach implicitly utilizes common features to match identical objects from both data sources, with RANSAC and Levenberg-Marquardt (LM) optimization used for accuracy \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/2309.00787)\]. Similarly, \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/2211.01871)\] presents a spatiotemporal calibration method that does not require specialized infrastructure, using radar velocity measurements to estimate the extrinsic transformation matrix \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/2211.01871)\]. This method is particularly useful for long-term calibration in dynamic environments.

Another approach, introduced in \[[4](http://arxiv.org/abs/2103.07505)\], focuses on continuous-time 3D radar-to-camera extrinsic calibration. This method utilizes radar velocity measurements and does not require specialized radar retroreflectors, making it suitable for real-world deployment \[[4](http://arxiv.org/abs/2103.07505)\]. The method also derives observability properties and demonstrates efficacy through synthetic and real-world experiments \[[4](http://arxiv.org/abs/2103.07505)\].

In the realm of deep learning-based calibration, \[[5](http://arxiv.org/abs/1904.08743)\] proposes a data-driven method for automatic rotational radar-camera calibration without dedicated calibration targets. This approach uses a coarse and fine convolutional neural network, with a boosting-inspired training algorithm to improve performance \[[5](http://arxiv.org/abs/1904.08743)\]. The method is tested on real-world data and shows robustness across different sensor alignments and perspectives \[[5](http://arxiv.org/abs/1904.08743)\].

Furthermore, several studies have explored the use of radar and camera fusion for 3D object detection, which inherently requires accurate calibration. For example, \[[6](http://arxiv.org/abs/2309.15465)\] introduces a novel fusion network and evaluates its performance on two datasets, nuScenes and View-of-Delft, demonstrating that radar-camera fusion significantly outperforms camera-only and radar-only baselines \[[6](http://arxiv.org/abs/2309.15465)\].
