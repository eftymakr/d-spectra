## Related Works

Collaborative filtering (CF) has been a cornerstone in recommendation systems, with recent advancements leveraging graph-based models to better capture user-item interactions \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2401.12590)\]. Graph Convolutional Networks (GCNs) have emerged as a powerful tool for learning graph representations, enabling the modeling of high-order connectivity in user-item graphs \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/2007.03383)\]. However, existing GCN-based CF models often suffer from issues such as over-smoothing, limited expressiveness, and inadequate modeling of complex node dependencies \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/2001.00267)\]. 

Several works have attempted to address these limitations. For instance, NGCF integrates user-item interactions directly into the embedding process by propagating embeddings on the graph, thereby explicitly capturing high-order connectivity \[[4](http://arxiv.org/abs/1905.08108)\]. Similarly, Multi-GCCF explicitly incorporates multiple graphs in the embedding learning process, including user-user and item-item graphs, to better model high-order and proximal information \[[5](http://arxiv.org/abs/2307.04384)\]. These models, however, still struggle with over-smoothing and the inability to capture nuanced preference patterns \[[6](http://arxiv.org/abs/2406.13996)\].

To mitigate over-smoothing, some studies have proposed residual structures and linear models. For example, LR-GCCF introduces a linear model with residual connections to alleviate over-smoothing and improve training efficiency \[[7](http://arxiv.org/abs/2001.10167)\]. Additionally, Adaptive Propagation GCN (AP-GCN) allows nodes to independently decide the number of communication steps, leading to more efficient and accurate results \[[8](http://arxiv.org/abs/1911.10699)\]. Despite these improvements, the ability to capture non-linear and high-frequency components of the graph signal remains a challenge.

Another line of research focuses on improving the expressiveness of graph filters. PolyCF reframes CF as a graph signal processing problem and leverages polynomial graph filters to capture spectral features across multiple eigenspaces \[[9](http://arxiv.org/abs/2203.14748)\]. Similarly, the use of Chebyshev polynomials in graph filters has been explored for tasks such as image denoising \[[10](http://arxiv.org/abs/2107.04755)\]. These methods demonstrate the potential of spectral graph filters in capturing complex patterns in the data.

The integration of contrastive learning with graph-based models has also gained traction. [Snippet]: «Abstract Graph convolutional neural networks (GCNNs) have received much attention recently, owing to their capability in handling graph-structured data. Among the existing GCNNs, many methods can be viewed as instances of a neural message passing motif; features of nodes are passed around their neighbors, aggregated and transformed to produce better nodes’ representations. Nevertheless, these methods seldom use node transition probabilities, a measure that has been found useful in exploring graphs. Furthermore, when the transition probabilities are used, their transition direction is often improperly considered in the feature aggregation step, resulting in an inefficient weighting scheme. In addition, although a great number of GCNN models with increasing level of complexity have been introduced, the GCNNs often suffer from over-fitting when being trained on small graphs. Another issue of the GCNNs is over-smoothing, which tends to make nodes’ representations indistinguishable. This work presents a new method to improve the message passing process based on node transition probabilities by properly considering the transition direction, leading to a better weighting scheme in nodes’ features aggregation compared to the existing counterpart. Moreover, we propose a novel regularization method termed DropNode to address the over-fitting and over-smoothing issues simultaneously. DropNode randomly discards part of a graph, thus it creates multiple deformed versions of the graph, leading to data augmentation regularization effect. Additionally, DropNode lessens the connectivity of the graph, mitigating the effect of over-smoothing in deep GCNNs. Extensive experiments on eight benchmark datasets for node and graph classification tasks demonstrate the effectiveness of the proposed methods in comparison with the state of the art.»
