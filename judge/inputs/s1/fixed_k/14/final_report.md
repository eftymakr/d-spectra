## Related Works

Video super-resolution (VSR) has been a focal point in computer vision, particularly with the advent of deep learning and diffusion models. Early approaches relied on traditional image restoration techniques, often failing to capture the temporal consistency essential for video quality \[[28](http://arxiv.org/abs/1807.07930)\]. More recently, diffusion models have shown promise in generating high-quality, realistic details for VSR tasks \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2312.00853)\]. However, the inherent randomness in diffusion processes can lead to artifacts and inconsistencies, especially when applied to real-world videos with complex degradations \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2312.00853)\].

To address temporal inconsistency, several works have introduced mechanisms to ensure coherence across frames. For instance, MGLD \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2312.00853)\] employs a motion-guided loss to optimize the latent sampling path, ensuring generated HR videos maintain a continuous visual flow. Similarly, StableVSR \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/2311.15908)\] introduces a Temporal Conditioning Module (TCM) that uses Temporal Texture Guidance to synthesize realistic and temporally consistent details. These methods emphasize the importance of temporal alignment and spatial consistency in VSR.

The challenge of handling compressed and low-quality videos has also been explored. DC-VSR \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/2502.03502)\] proposes a novel approach that ensures spatial and temporal consistency by integrating Spatial Attention Propagation (SAP) and Temporal Attention Propagation (TAP) schemes. This method effectively captures spatio-temporal dependencies, leading to high-quality VSR results. Additionally, the work in \[[4](http://arxiv.org/abs/2502.07381)\] introduces a Spatial Degradation-Aware and Temporal Consistent (SDATC) diffusion model, which mitigates spatial distortions and enhances temporal consistency through a distortion control module and a spatio-temporal attention module.

Efforts to improve the efficiency and performance of VSR models have also been made. For example, the Temporal Consistency Learning Network (TCNet) \[[5](http://arxiv.org/abs/2211.01639)\] is designed to enhance the consistency of reconstructed videos by leveraging spatio-temporal stability and self-attention mechanisms. This network demonstrates superior performance on benchmark datasets, highlighting the effectiveness of temporal modeling in VSR.

Another significant contribution is the introduction of recurrent architectures to handle the temporal dependencies in VSR. The Recurrent Residual Network (RRN) \[[6](http://arxiv.org/abs/2008.05765)\] is designed for efficient video super-resolution, utilizing residual learning to stabilize training and improve performance. This approach achieves state-of-the-art results on several benchmarks, showcasing the potential of recurrent models in VSR.

### <a id='category-diffusion-models-in-vsr'></a>Diffusion Models in VSR: 10 papers found.
Recent advancements in diffusion models for video super-resolution (VSR) have focused on addressing the challenges of temporal consistency and perceptual quality, particularly in real-world scenarios with complex degradations. Several studies have introduced novel strategies to enhance the performance of diffusion-based VSR methods. For instance, the motion-guided latent diffusion (MGLD) approach leverages temporal dynamics in low-resolution videos to guide the diffusion process, ensuring content consistency across adjacent frames \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2312.00853)\]. Similarly, the StableVSR method introduces a Temporal Conditioning Module (TCM) with Temporal Texture Guidance, which synthesizes spatially-aligned texture information from adjacent frames to improve temporal consistency \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/2311.15908)\]. Additionally, the SDATC diffusion model incorporates a distortion control module (DCM) and a spatio-temporal attention module (STAM) to mitigate spatial distortions and refine temporal coherence in compressed videos \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/2502.03502)\]. The DOVE framework, an efficient one-step diffusion model, addresses the computational inefficiency of multi-step diffusion by fine-tuning a pretrained video diffusion model with a latent-pixel training strategy \[[8](http://arxiv.org/abs/2410.18137)\]. Furthermore, the STAR approach enhances spatio-temporal quality by integrating local information enhancement and a dynamic frequency loss to improve fidelity and reduce artifacts \[[9](http://arxiv.org/abs/2208.11608)\]. These methods collectively highlight the ongoing efforts to improve the perceptual quality and temporal consistency of diffusion-based VSR, while also addressing the inherent randomness and computational challenges associated with these models.

### <a id='category-spatial-and-temporal-attention'></a>Spatial and Temporal Attention: 3 papers found.
Video super-resolution (VSR) has been a focal point in recent research, with a particular emphasis on achieving spatial and temporal consistency in high-resolution video reconstruction. Existing diffusion-based VSR methods often suffer from spatio-temporal inconsistencies due to the inherent randomness in the diffusion process and tile-based approaches \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/2502.03502)\]. To address these challenges, several studies have introduced novel attention mechanisms to enhance both spatial and temporal coherence. For instance, DC-VSR proposes a Spatial Attention Propagation (SAP) and Temporal Attention Propagation (TAP) scheme, leveraging self-attention to propagate information across spatio-temporal tiles \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/2502.03502)\]. Similarly, the Video Restoration Transformer (VRT) employs a Temporal Reciprocal Self Attention (TRSA) module, which enables joint motion estimation and feature alignment across video clips, along with parallel warping for cross-clip interaction \[[13](http://arxiv.org/abs/2201.12288)\]. These approaches aim to improve the quality of generated videos by better utilizing temporal information from adjacent frames. Additionally, text-to-video diffusion models have also faced challenges in accurately reproducing motion and generating diverse visual variations, prompting the development of the Video Motion Customization (VMC) framework, which introduces a motion distillation objective to preserve low-frequency motion trajectories while reducing noise \[[18](http://arxiv.org/abs/2312.00845)\]. These studies collectively highlight the importance of attention mechanisms in enhancing the temporal consistency and visual quality of video generation and restoration tasks.

### <a id='category-efficiency-and-computational-cost'></a>Efficiency and Computational Cost: 2 papers found.
Efficiency and computational cost are critical considerations in space-time video super-resolution (STSR) methods, as highlighted in prior research. Early approaches typically decomposed the problem into separate video frame interpolation (VFI) and video super-resolution (VSR) tasks, but this two-stage framework often led to suboptimal results due to the interdependence of temporal and spatial processing \[[15](http://arxiv.org/abs/2104.07473)\]. Additionally, these methods often relied on large frame reconstruction modules, resulting in high computational overhead and slow inference times \[[15](http://arxiv.org/abs/2104.07473)\]. To address these limitations, recent work has proposed one-stage frameworks that directly reconstruct high-resolution (HR) slow-motion videos from low-resolution (LR) and low frame rate (LFR) inputs, leveraging feature-level temporal interpolation to capture local motion contexts without explicitly generating intermediate LR frames \[[15](http://arxiv.org/abs/2104.07473)\]. Another approach focused on improving efficiency by first interpolating in the LR space using quadratic modeling, then reusing flowmaps and blending masks in the HR space through bilinear upsampling, followed by refinement via a lightweight network to reduce artifacts \[[22](http://arxiv.org/abs/2104.05778)\]. These methods emphasize the importance of exploiting space-time relationships while maintaining computational efficiency, which aligns with the goal of achieving high-quality results without excessive resource consumption.
