## Related Works

Deep sequential recommendation models have seen significant advancements, yet they often struggle with effectively modeling the complex and varied nature of user behaviors, particularly in handling sequence length variations and capturing diverse interaction patterns \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2502.08132)\]. Traditional approaches, such as RNN- and Transformer-based models, face challenges in efficiently capturing long-range dependencies and maintaining inference efficiency, especially with long behavior sequences \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/2403.03900)\]. For instance, Transformer-based models suffer from quadratic computational complexity due to the attention mechanism, which becomes a major bottleneck for real-time applications \[[10](http://arxiv.org/abs/2501.01242)\]. To address these limitations, several works have explored alternative modeling strategies, including state space models (SSMs) and attention mechanisms, to improve both efficiency and effectiveness.

State space models (SSMs) have emerged as a promising alternative to traditional recurrent and attention-based models, offering linear complexity and efficient hardware-aware designs \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/2403.03900)\]. For example, Mamba, a selective SSM, has been adapted for sequential recommendation, enabling efficient processing of long sequences by leveraging a parallel algorithm \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/2403.03900)\]. Similarly, EchoMamba4Rec integrates bi-directional processing with frequency-domain filtering to enhance the modeling of complex user interactions \[[5](http://arxiv.org/abs/2406.02638)\]. These models demonstrate that SSMs can effectively capture temporal dynamics and provide scalable solutions for sequential recommendation tasks \[[5](http://arxiv.org/abs/2406.02638)\].

Attention mechanisms, particularly self-attention, have also been widely adopted in sequential recommendation systems due to their ability to model long-range dependencies \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2502.08132)\]. However, self-attention mechanisms suffer from the quadratic complexity issue, which limits their applicability to long sequences \[[10](http://arxiv.org/abs/2501.01242)\]. To mitigate this, researchers have proposed linear attention mechanisms, such as LinRec, which theoretically improves efficiency while preserving the learning capabilities of traditional attention mechanisms \[[8](http://arxiv.org/abs/2411.01537)\]. Additionally, HydraRec introduces an efficient attention mechanism inspired by Hydra attention, significantly reducing the computational complexity for longer sequences \[[10](http://arxiv.org/abs/2501.01242)\].

To further enhance the modeling of diverse user preferences and interaction patterns, several works have explored hybrid approaches combining attention and SSMs. For instance, SSD4Rec integrates Mamba with a structured state space duality block to enable efficient and accurate modeling of long-range dependencies \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/2409.01192)\]. Similarly, SS4Rec combines time-aware and relation-aware SSMs to capture both temporal and sequential dependencies in user-item interactions \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2502.08132)\]. These models highlight the potential of hybrid architectures in addressing the limitations of individual approaches.
