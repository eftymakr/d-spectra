## Related Works

Deep sequential recommendation models have seen significant advancements, yet they often struggle with effectively modeling the complex and varied nature of user behaviors, particularly in handling sequence length variations and capturing diverse interaction patterns \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2502.08132)\]. Traditional approaches, such as RNN- and Transformer-based models, face challenges in efficiently capturing long-range dependencies and maintaining inference efficiency, especially with long behavior sequences \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/2403.03900)\]. For instance, Transformer-based models suffer from quadratic computational complexity due to the attention mechanism, which becomes a major bottleneck for real-time applications \[[10](http://arxiv.org/abs/2501.01242)\]. To address these limitations, several works have explored alternative modeling strategies, including state space models (SSMs) and attention mechanisms, to improve both efficiency and effectiveness.

State space models (SSMs) have emerged as a promising alternative to traditional recurrent and attention-based models, offering linear complexity and efficient hardware-aware designs \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/2403.03900)\]. For example, Mamba, a selective SSM, has been adapted for sequential recommendation, enabling efficient processing of long sequences by leveraging a parallel algorithm \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/2403.03900)\]. Similarly, EchoMamba4Rec integrates bi-directional processing with frequency-domain filtering to enhance the modeling of complex user interactions \[[5](http://arxiv.org/abs/2406.02638)\]. These models demonstrate that SSMs can effectively capture temporal dynamics and provide scalable solutions for sequential recommendation tasks \[[5](http://arxiv.org/abs/2406.02638)\].

Attention mechanisms, particularly self-attention, have also been widely adopted in sequential recommendation systems due to their ability to model long-range dependencies \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2502.08132)\]. However, self-attention mechanisms suffer from the quadratic complexity issue, which limits their applicability to long sequences \[[10](http://arxiv.org/abs/2501.01242)\]. To mitigate this, researchers have proposed linear attention mechanisms, such as LinRec, which theoretically improves efficiency while preserving the learning capabilities of traditional attention mechanisms \[[8](http://arxiv.org/abs/2411.01537)\]. Additionally, HydraRec introduces an efficient attention mechanism inspired by Hydra attention, significantly reducing the computational complexity for longer sequences \[[10](http://arxiv.org/abs/2501.01242)\].

To further enhance the modeling of diverse user preferences and interaction patterns, several works have explored hybrid approaches combining attention and SSMs. For instance, SSD4Rec integrates Mamba with a structured state space duality block to enable efficient and accurate modeling of long-range dependencies \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/2409.01192)\]. Similarly, SS4Rec combines time-aware and relation-aware SSMs to capture both temporal and sequential dependencies in user-item interactions \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2502.08132)\]. These models highlight the potential of hybrid architectures in addressing the limitations of individual approaches.

### <a id='category-state-space-models-for-sequential-data'></a>State Space Models for Sequential Data: 5 papers found.
State space models (SSMs) have emerged as a promising approach for sequential recommendation, addressing limitations of traditional methods like RNNs and Transformers. These models excel at capturing dynamic user interests over time, particularly in scenarios with irregular time intervals and long-range dependencies \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2502.08132)\]. For instance, SS4Rec introduces a time-aware SSM to handle irregular intervals and a relation-aware SSM to model contextual dependencies, enabling more accurate and personalized recommendations \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2502.08132)\]. Similarly, Mamba, an SSM-based architecture, has shown potential for efficient sequence modeling, especially in applications with high interaction volumes, such as short video platforms \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/2409.01192)\]. The Mamba4Rec model further leverages selective SSMs to improve both effectiveness and inference efficiency, outperforming attention-based and RNN-based baselines \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/2403.03900)\]. Additionally, EchoMamba4Rec integrates control theory principles into SSMs, using bi-directional processing and frequency-domain filtering to capture complex user interaction patterns \[[4](http://arxiv.org/abs/2105.14060)\]. These models also benefit from hardware-aware designs, allowing for parallel computations and improved scalability \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/2409.01192)\]. Moreover, the application of Mamba to lifelong sequential recommendation has demonstrated significant improvements in training efficiency, reducing time and memory costs by up to 70% and 80%, respectively \[[5](http://arxiv.org/abs/2406.02638)\]. Overall, these works highlight the growing importance of SSMs in sequential recommendation, offering efficient and effective solutions to long-standing challenges in modeling user behavior.

### <a id='category-attention-mechanisms-in-recommendation-systems'></a>Attention Mechanisms in Recommendation Systems: 13 papers found.
Recent advances in attention mechanisms have significantly enhanced sequential recommendation systems by enabling more accurate modeling of user behavior and diverse interaction patterns. Several studies have explored the use of self-attention to capture long-term dependencies and complex relationships between items \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2502.08132)\]. However, traditional self-attention models often suffer from issues such as over-smoothing, where item embeddings become increasingly similar across attention blocks, leading to a loss of information and degraded performance \[[10](http://arxiv.org/abs/2501.01242)\]. To address this, researchers have proposed various improvements, including the introduction of linear attention mechanisms that reduce computational complexity while preserving the effectiveness of attention \[[8](http://arxiv.org/abs/2411.01537)\]. Additionally, some works have focused on incorporating temporal information more effectively, such as using multi-temporal embeddings to capture different patterns in user behavior \[[6](http://arxiv.org/abs/2504.05323)\]. Others have introduced mixture-of-experts (MoE) frameworks to handle diverse behavioral patterns by routing interactions to specialized experts \[[14](http://arxiv.org/abs/2209.07997)\]. Furthermore, there is growing interest in leveraging frequency domain analysis to better capture periodic and high-frequency patterns in user sequences \[[9](http://arxiv.org/abs/2012.03323)\]. These approaches highlight the ongoing efforts to refine attention-based models for sequential recommendation, addressing challenges such as computational efficiency, modeling diversity, and capturing temporal dynamics.

### <a id='category-linear-complexity-in-attention-mechanisms'></a>Linear Complexity in Attention Mechanisms: 1 papers found.
Linear attention mechanisms have emerged as a critical area of research to address the computational inefficiencies of traditional transformer models in sequential recommendation tasks. Unlike standard dot-product attention, which exhibits quadratic complexity in sequence length, linear attention models aim to reduce this complexity to linear in both token and embedding dimensions \[[10](http://arxiv.org/abs/2501.01242)\]. This is particularly important in recommendation systems where user interaction sequences are dynamic and often involve long-tail items, making it challenging to process with conventional attention mechanisms \[[10](http://arxiv.org/abs/2501.01242)\]. Hydra attention, a linear attention model originally proposed for vision transformers, has been adapted for sequential recommendation tasks, leading to the development of HydraRec. HydraRec maintains temporal context while significantly improving the theoretical complexity of attention computation, enabling efficient processing of longer sequences and larger datasets \[[10](http://arxiv.org/abs/2501.01242)\]. This advancement aligns with broader efforts to enhance the scalability of transformer-based models in recommendation systems, where the ability to handle variable-length sequences and diverse user behaviors is crucial. The success of HydraRec highlights the potential of linear attention models to outperform both traditional dot-product attention and other linear transformer-based approaches in sequential recommendation tasks \[[10](http://arxiv.org/abs/2501.01242)\].
