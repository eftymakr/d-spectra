## Related Works

Speech-driven 3D facial animation has been a focal point in computer vision and machine learning research, with a growing body of work addressing the challenges of lip synchronization, facial expression generation, and style control. Most existing methods rely on pre-trained self-supervised audio models as encoders to extract meaningful features from speech signals \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2309.04814)\]. However, these models often fail to capture the nuanced differences between phonetically similar syllables, which can lead to significant coupling in the feature space and an averaging effect in lip motion generation \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/2312.02781)\]. This issue is particularly pronounced in languages with rich phonetic variation, where near-homophone syllables may have distinct lip shapes \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/2408.07005)\]. 

To mitigate this, several works have explored multimodal approaches that integrate visual and textual cues to improve the accuracy of facial animation. For instance, PMMTalk introduces a framework that leverages complementary pseudo multi-modal features to enhance the precision of facial animation by aligning audio, image, and text features at both temporal and semantic levels \[[4](http://arxiv.org/abs/2503.09852)\]. Similarly, Content and Style Aware Audio-Driven Facial Animation proposes a two-stage training approach that first learns styles from audio data and then fine-tunes the model using low-resource audio/3D mesh pairs to control 3D vertex generation \[[5](http://arxiv.org/abs/2112.02214)\]. These methods demonstrate the importance of integrating multiple modalities to achieve more accurate and expressive facial animations.

Another line of research focuses on improving the realism and controllability of facial animations by disentangling style and content representations. StyleSpeaker explicitly models speaking styles based on speaker characteristics and accounts for style biases caused by different speech conditions \[[6](http://arxiv.org/abs/2312.10877)\]. This approach enables more fine-grained control over facial expressions while maintaining synchronization with speech. Similarly, Mimic introduces a framework that disentangles speaking style and content by building two latent spaces for style and content, respectively, and employs four well-designed constraints to ensure the construction of identity-related style and semantic-related content spaces \[[7](http://arxiv.org/abs/2407.12501)\]. These methods highlight the importance of disentanglement in achieving both realism and controllability in facial animation.
