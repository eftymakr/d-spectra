## Related Works

Protein dynamics are essential for understanding biological functions and properties, yet traditional methods for studying these dynamics rely on computationally expensive molecular dynamics (MD) simulations \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2004.13167)\]. Recent advances in generative modeling have introduced more efficient approaches for protein structure prediction and conformation sampling, particularly through denoising diffusion models \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/2411.08154)\]. These models learn distributions over crystallographic structures, enabling accurate and rapid conformational sampling. However, integrating physical supervision into data-driven approaches remains a challenge, as standard energy-based objectives often result in intractable optimization \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/2402.10433)\].

Several studies have explored the use of energy-based models for protein conformation prediction. For instance, an energy-based model (EBM) was proposed to predict protein conformations at the atomic scale, achieving performance comparable to the Rosetta energy function \[[4](http://arxiv.org/abs/1510.05682)\]. Additionally, energy-based graph convolutional networks (EGCN) have been developed to score protein docking models by learning generalized interaction energies from graph data \[[5](http://arxiv.org/abs/1501.03971)\]. These models incorporate physical principles and improve the ranking of near-native docked models \[[6](http://arxiv.org/abs/2403.14088)\].

Efforts to enhance the accuracy of generative models have also focused on incorporating physical simulations. A few-shot conformation sampler was introduced, which integrates molecular dynamics (MD) simulations in a tractable manner to fine-tune generative models \[[7](http://arxiv.org/abs/2307.02170)\]. This approach improves the accuracy of conformation sampling while maintaining computational efficiency \[[8](http://arxiv.org/abs/1912.12476)\]. Similarly, a force-guided SE(3) diffusion model, ConfDiff, was proposed to generate protein conformations with high fidelity by incorporating physical prior knowledge \[[9](http://arxiv.org/abs/2306.01824)\].

Another approach, Structure Language Modeling (SLM), has been introduced as a novel framework for efficient protein conformation generation. SLM uses a discrete variational auto-encoder to encode protein structures into a latent space, followed by conditional language modeling to capture sequence-specific conformation distributions \[[10](http://arxiv.org/abs/1511.08097)\]. This method enables more efficient and interpretable exploration of diverse conformational modes \[[11](http://arxiv.org/abs/2505.16896)\].

The integration of physical principles into generative models has also been explored through the development of EigenFold, a diffusion generative modeling framework that samples a distribution of structures from a given protein sequence \[[12](http://arxiv.org/abs/1404.1556)\]. EigenFold models the structure as a system of harmonic oscillators, naturally inducing a cascading-resolution generative process \[[13](http://arxiv.org/abs/2410.18403)\].
