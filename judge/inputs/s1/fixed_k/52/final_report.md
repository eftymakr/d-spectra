## Related Works

Dense retrieval systems typically rely on pre-trained language models (e.g., BERT, LLaMA) that are fine-tuned using contrastive learning to encode text into dense representations that can be compared via simple similarity operations, such as inner products \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2208.09846)\]. Recent studies have questioned the role of fine-tuning versus pre-training in dense retrievers, arguing that retrieval knowledge is primarily acquired during pre-training, and that fine-tuning may not significantly alter this knowledge \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/2108.05540)\]. This claim was initially tested using a BERT-based encoder with DPR as the representative dense retriever \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/2306.03166)\]. However, this analysis was limited in scope, focusing only on a single encoder architecture and dataset.

This study extends the previous analysis by examining different representation approaches, such as CLS token usage versus mean pooling, and varying backbone architectures, including encoder-only BERT and decoder-only LLaMA \[[4](http://arxiv.org/abs/2104.08253)\]. Additionally, we test the claim on additional datasets, such as MSMARCO and Natural Questions \[[5](http://arxiv.org/abs/2010.02616)\]. Our findings confirm that in DPR tuning, pre-trained knowledge underpins retrieval performance, with fine-tuning primarily adjusting neuron activation rather than reorganizing knowledge \[[6](http://arxiv.org/abs/2208.00635)\]. However, this pattern does not hold universally. For example, in mean-pooled models like Contriever, and decoder-based models like LLaMA, fine-tuning plays a more significant role in shaping retrieval performance \[[7](http://arxiv.org/abs/2312.05934)\].

Several prior works have explored the effectiveness of different pre-training and fine-tuning strategies for dense retrieval. For instance, \[[8](http://arxiv.org/abs/2204.10641)\] proposed a contrastive pre-training approach to learn discriminative autoencoders, while \[[9](http://arxiv.org/abs/2112.09118)\] introduced unsupervised corpus-aware pre-training for dense passage retrieval. These methods aim to improve the quality of dense representations by leveraging contrastive learning during pre-training \[[10](http://arxiv.org/abs/2408.06663)\]. Similarly, \[[11](http://arxiv.org/abs/2307.11077)\] explored the use of relevance-aware contrastive learning to enhance the performance of unsupervised dense retrievers \[[12](http://arxiv.org/abs/2212.07841)\].

The role of fine-tuning in dense retrieval has also been investigated in the context of robustness to typos. \[[13](http://arxiv.org/abs/2303.10464)\] proposed a multi-positive contrastive learning approach to improve the robustness of dense retrievers against misspelled queries, demonstrating that leveraging multiple positive examples during fine-tuning can enhance performance \[[14](http://arxiv.org/abs/2107.07773)\]. ### <a id='category-contrastive-learning-in-pre-training'></a>Contrastive Learning in Pre-training: 7 papers found.
Contrastive learning has emerged as a critical approach in pre-training for dense retrieval, with multiple studies focusing on improving text representation and retrieval effectiveness through contrastive strategies \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2208.09846)\]. These methods aim to enhance the discriminative power of representations by contrasting different versions of input texts, either through masked spans \[[4](http://arxiv.org/abs/2104.08253)\] or word distributions \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2208.09846)\]. For example, one study proposes a contrastive pre-training approach that generates word distributions in a non-autoregressive fashion, pulling together masked versions of a text while pushing away from others, thereby suppressing common words and highlighting representative ones \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2208.09846)\]. Another work introduces a contrastive span prediction task to pre-train the encoder alone, avoiding the bypass effect of the decoder and enabling efficient learning of discriminative text representations \[[4](http://arxiv.org/abs/2104.08253)\]. Additionally, contrastive learning has been applied to unsupervised dense retrievers, demonstrating strong performance on benchmark datasets like BEIR, outperforming traditional term-frequency methods such as BM25 on multiple datasets \[[5](http://arxiv.org/abs/2010.02616)\]. Furthermore, contrastive strategies have been integrated into pre-training architectures like Condenser, where an unsupervised corpus-level contrastive loss is used to warm up the passage embedding space, reducing the need for heavy data engineering \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/2108.05540)\]. These approaches collectively highlight the importance of contrastive learning in enhancing retrieval performance, especially in low-resource or cross-lingual settings \[[5](http://arxiv.org/abs/2010.02616)\]\[[6](http://arxiv.org/abs/2208.00635)\].

These findings highlight the complex interplay between pre-training and fine-tuning, emphasizing the need for careful interpretation of their combined effects on model performance and knowledge acquisition \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2208.09846)\]\[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/2108.05540)\]\[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/2306.03166)\]\[[5](http://arxiv.org/abs/2010.02616)\].
