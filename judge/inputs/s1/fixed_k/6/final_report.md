## Related Works

Narrative summarization, a critical yet challenging task in natural language processing, requires capturing intricate plotlines, character interactions, and thematic coherence from long-form texts such as books, movies, and TV scripts. Existing approaches often struggle with maintaining coherence and accuracy, particularly in long documents \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2504.09071)\]. This section reviews related works that have explored narrative summarization, coherence, and multi-agent systems for long-form text generation.

Several studies have focused on improving the faithfulness and coherence of summaries generated by large language models (LLMs). For instance, \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2504.09071)\] investigates plan-guided summarization for narrative texts, aiming to reduce hallucinations by grounding summaries to the source text. However, their results show that neither plan-based approaches nor higher-level narrative plans significantly improve summary quality or faithfulness. This highlights the difficulty of achieving coherent and faithful summaries from long, complex narratives.

In contrast, \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/2504.07459)\] proposes a novel framework for generating causal graphs from narrative texts, which bridges high-level causality with detailed event-specific relationships. Their method combines LLM-based summarization with an "Expert Index" and a structured prompting process to refine causal graphs. This approach demonstrates superior precision in causal link identification compared to pure LLM-based methods.

The challenge of maintaining coherence in long-form narratives has also been addressed through cognitive writing frameworks. \[[6](http://arxiv.org/abs/2502.12568)\] introduces CogWriter, a training-free framework that equips LLMs with human-like cognitive writing capabilities, including iterative planning, translating, reviewing, and monitoring. This framework improves the quality of long-form text generation by systematically decomposing tasks and maintaining quality through continuous monitoring and revisions.

Multi-agent systems have also been explored for improving the performance of LLMs in complex tasks. \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/2401.07324)\] proposes a multi-LLM agent framework that decomposes capabilities such as task planning, tool invocation, and result summarization into specialized components. Each component is implemented by a single LLM, enabling modular updates and the use of smaller models for specific tasks. This approach outperforms traditional single-LLM methods in tool-use benchmarks.

For long-form text summarization, \[[4](http://arxiv.org/abs/2006.10213)\] introduces SEAL, a Transformer-based model that dynamically extracts input snippets for each output segment. This model achieves state-of-the-art results on long-form summarization tasks and outperforms strong baseline models on a new dataset. The explicit content selection in SEAL also enhances interpretability.

### <a id='category-causal-graph-generation-for-narratives'></a>Causal Graph Generation for Narratives: 3 papers found.
Recent advancements in narrative understanding have focused on generating causal graphs to model the intricate relationships within stories, emphasizing both structural coherence and semantic richness. Researchers have proposed frameworks that integrate linguistic and contextual features to enhance the accuracy of causal link identification, such as the Situation-Task-Action-Consequence (STAC) classification model augmented with an Expert Index \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/2504.07459)\]. These models leverage large language models (LLMs) combined with symbolic reasoning to capture nuanced event relationships, as seen in hierarchical knowledge graph frameworks that organize narrative content across panel, event, and macro-event levels, integrating visual and textual elements \[[10](http://arxiv.org/abs/2506.10008)\]. Additionally, novel graph-based approaches like NarCo have emerged, which model task-agnostic coherence dependencies through fine-grained contextual analysis, enabling downstream tasks such as question answering and timeline reconstruction \[[20](http://arxiv.org/abs/2402.13551)\]. These methods collectively highlight the growing interest in interpretable and structured representations of narrative content, aiming to improve both the quality and usability of automated narrative analysis tools.

### <a id='category-multi-agent-llm-systems'></a>Multi-Agent LLM Systems: 5 papers found.
The category "Multi-Agent LLM Systems" explores the use of multiple Large Language Models (LLMs) working collaboratively to enhance task performance across various domains. These systems are designed to address limitations of single-agent models by decomposing complex tasks into specialized components such as planning, tool invocation, and summarization, with each component handled by a dedicated LLM \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/2401.07324)\]. This modular approach allows for greater flexibility, improved task execution, and the potential use of smaller models for specific capabilities \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/2401.07324)\]. In text classification, multi-agent frameworks like PRINCIPLE-BASED PROMPTING leverage multiple LLMs to generate and refine principles for better classification accuracy, outperforming zero-shot and few-shot prompting methods \[[17](http://arxiv.org/abs/2502.07165)\]. In healthcare, MedAide introduces a multi-agent collaboration framework that enables intent-aware information fusion and coordinated reasoning, addressing challenges such as information redundancy and hallucinations through dynamic intent recognition and role rotation \[[18](http://arxiv.org/abs/2410.12532)\]. Similarly, in financial trading, TradingAgents simulates collaborative dynamics by deploying LLM-powered agents in specialized roles, leading to improved performance metrics such as cumulative returns and Sharpe ratio \[[19](http://arxiv.org/abs/1311.5108)\]. These examples highlight the growing interest in multi-agent LLM systems for complex, multi-step tasks, demonstrating their potential to enhance performance in diverse applications.

Results on LV-HAYSTACK highlight a significant research gap in temporal search capabilities, with current SOTA search methods only achieving 2.1% temporal F1 score on the LongVideoBench subset.
