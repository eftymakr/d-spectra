## Related Works

Narrative summarization, a critical yet challenging task in natural language processing, requires capturing intricate plotlines, character interactions, and thematic coherence from long-form texts such as books, movies, and TV scripts. Existing approaches often struggle with maintaining coherence and accuracy, particularly in long documents \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2504.09071)\]. This section reviews related works that have explored narrative summarization, coherence, and multi-agent systems for long-form text generation.

Several studies have focused on improving the faithfulness and coherence of summaries generated by large language models (LLMs). For instance, \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2504.09071)\] investigates plan-guided summarization for narrative texts, aiming to reduce hallucinations by grounding summaries to the source text. However, their results show that neither plan-based approaches nor higher-level narrative plans significantly improve summary quality or faithfulness. This highlights the difficulty of achieving coherent and faithful summaries from long, complex narratives.

In contrast, \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/2504.07459)\] proposes a novel framework for generating causal graphs from narrative texts, which bridges high-level causality with detailed event-specific relationships. Their method combines LLM-based summarization with an "Expert Index" and a structured prompting process to refine causal graphs. This approach demonstrates superior precision in causal link identification compared to pure LLM-based methods.

The challenge of maintaining coherence in long-form narratives has also been addressed through cognitive writing frameworks. \[[6](http://arxiv.org/abs/2502.12568)\] introduces CogWriter, a training-free framework that equips LLMs with human-like cognitive writing capabilities, including iterative planning, translating, reviewing, and monitoring. This framework improves the quality of long-form text generation by systematically decomposing tasks and maintaining quality through continuous monitoring and revisions.

Multi-agent systems have also been explored for improving the performance of LLMs in complex tasks. \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/2401.07324)\] proposes a multi-LLM agent framework that decomposes capabilities such as task planning, tool invocation, and result summarization into specialized components. Each component is implemented by a single LLM, enabling modular updates and the use of smaller models for specific tasks. This approach outperforms traditional single-LLM methods in tool-use benchmarks.

For long-form text summarization, \[[4](http://arxiv.org/abs/2006.10213)\] introduces SEAL, a Transformer-based model that dynamically extracts input snippets for each output segment. This model achieves state-of-the-art results on long-form summarization tasks and outperforms strong baseline models on a new dataset. The explicit content selection in SEAL also enhances interpretability.
