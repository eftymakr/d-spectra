## Related Works

The field of noisy annotation handling has seen significant advancements, particularly in the context of training machine learning models on datasets with unreliable labels. Early approaches focused on identifying and mitigating the impact of noisy annotations by leveraging statistical properties of the data. For instance, \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2110.10456)\] highlights the challenges of training object detectors on datasets with entangled noises, such as inaccurate bounding boxes and incorrect class labels, and proposes a method to decouple these noises and correct annotations. This work emphasizes the need for robust annotation correction mechanisms, which is a key theme in the broader literature on noisy data.

In the realm of language models, \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/2503.08684)\] investigates the phenomenon of source bias, where PLM-based retrieval models favor low perplexity documents, often generated by large language models, over human-written ones. This bias is attributed to the learned perplexity features used for relevance estimation. The study proposes a causal-inspired debiasing method, CDC, which separates bias effects from overall relevance scores. This work is closely related to the current paper's focus on addressing biases in perplexity-based methods for text generation under noisy annotations.

The problem of noisy labels is also well-studied in the context of deep learning. \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/2406.16966)\] introduces a framework that uses synthetic samples with soft labels to mitigate the impact of noisy labels during training. This approach leverages the early learning phase, where clean samples are learned before mislabeled ones, and proposes a mixing strategy to create synthetic samples. The method also estimates soft targets to improve the accuracy of learned representations. This work aligns with the current paper's emphasis on correcting perplexity estimates through synthesized neighbors.

In the domain of scene graph generation, \[[4](http://arxiv.org/abs/2207.13316)\] addresses the issue of noisy labels by proposing NICEST, a strategy that detects and corrects noisy samples through a combination of clustering and knowledge distillation. This method improves the robustness of scene graph models by generating high-quality samples and designing an effective training strategy. The approach of detecting and correcting noisy samples is also relevant to the current paper's dual debiasing framework.

The current paper builds upon these prior works by introducing a dual debiasing framework that explicitly corrects perplexity estimates using synthesized neighbors. This approach addresses two key sources of bias in perplexity: the annotation itself and the domain-specific knowledge inherent in large language models. The proposed method, which yields a robust Sample Cleanliness Score, is evaluated on various benchmarks and shown to outperform existing approaches in noise detection and ICL performance.

### <a id='category-debiasing-techniques-in-language-models'></a>Debiasing Techniques in Language Models: 6 papers found.
The category "Debiasing Techniques in Language Models" encompasses a range of approaches aimed at mitigating biases in language models, particularly those arising from training data, model architecture, or inference processes. Several studies have identified source bias, where models favor LLM-generated content over human-written documents \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/2503.08684)\], and have proposed methods like Causal Diagnosis and Correction (CDC) to address this by separating bias effects from relevance scores \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/2503.08684)\]. Additionally, dataset bias has been highlighted as a critical issue, where improperly constructed datasets lead to poor generalization, and recent techniques have attempted to counteract this by emphasizing bias-aligned samples and highlighting bias-conflicting ones \[[5](http://arxiv.org/abs/2212.01189)\]. In vision-language models, biases from imbalanced datasets have also been addressed through text-only debiasing frameworks like TOD, which eliminate the need for image data by leveraging text-based training \[[8](http://arxiv.org/abs/2410.09365)\]. Similarly, in abstractive summarization, factual inconsistency has been attributed to language and irrelevancy biases, leading to the development of frameworks like CoFactSum that use counterfactual estimation to reduce these biases \[[4](http://arxiv.org/abs/2207.13316)\]. For pretrained text encoders, social biases have been identified at the sentence level, and methods such as FairFil have been introduced to transform encoder outputs into debiased representations without requiring retraining \[[5](http://arxiv.org/abs/2212.01189)\]. In text-to-3D generation, view inconsistency has been linked to embedded biases in 2D diffusion models, prompting the development of debiasing strategies such as score and prompt debiasing to improve 3D consistency \[[6](http://arxiv.org/abs/2401.14360)\]. These diverse approaches underscore the multifaceted nature of bias in language models and the ongoing efforts to develop robust, effective debiasing techniques across different modalities and tasks.

In this paper, we dig deeper into the representation distributions in the early learning phase and find that, regardless of their noisy labels, learned representations of images from the same category still congregate together. Inspired by it, we propose a framework that trains the model with new synthetic samples to mitigate the impact of noisy labels. Specifically, we propose a mixing strategy to create the synthetic samples by aggregating original samples with their top-K nearest neighbours, wherein the weights are calculated using a mixture model learning from the per-sample loss distribution. To enhance the performance in the presence of extreme label noise, we estimate the soft targets by gradually correcting the noisy labels. Furthermore, we demonstrate that the estimated soft targets yield a more accurate approximation to ground truth labels and the proposed method produces a superior quality of learned representations with more separated and clearly bounded clusters. The extensive experiments in two benchmarks (CIFAR-10 and CIFAR-100) and two larg-scale real-world datasets (Clothing1M and Webvision) demonstrate that our approach outperforms the state-of-the-art methods and robustness of the learned representation.»
