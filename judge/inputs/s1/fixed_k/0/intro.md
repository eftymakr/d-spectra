## Related Works

Economic inequality remains a critical challenge for policymakers, with significant implications for education, healthcare, and social stability \[[10](http://arxiv.org/abs/2303.13994)\]. Traditional approaches, such as the U.S. federal income tax system, aim to reduce inequality but often lack adaptability to heterogeneous taxpayer behavior and irrational decision-making \[[10](http://arxiv.org/abs/2303.13994)\]. While models like the Saez Optimal Taxation framework attempt to dynamically adjust tax policies, they fail to fully account for the complexity of taxpayer heterogeneity and behavioral irrationality \[[10](http://arxiv.org/abs/2303.13994)\]. This paper introduces TaxAgent, a novel integration of large language models (LLMs) with agent-based modeling (ABM) to design adaptive tax policies \[[10](http://arxiv.org/abs/2303.13994)\]. 

Agent-based modeling (ABM) has emerged as a powerful tool in social policy-making and socio-economics, offering a flexible and dynamic approach to understanding and simulating complex systems \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/2504.01242)\]. Unlike traditional analytic methods, ABM can generate large ensembles of scenarios and evaluate adaptive policies, particularly in unpredictable environments \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/2504.01242)\]. This capability is crucial for modeling heterogeneous taxpayer behavior and simulating the impact of policy interventions \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/2504.01242)\]. 

The use of LLMs in policy analysis is a relatively new area, but several studies have explored their potential. For instance, a paper by \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2502.16879)\] introduces a Multi-LLM-Agent-Based (MLAB) framework for economic and public policy analysis, leveraging the inherent variations in analytical capabilities across different LLMs to model agents with diverse cognitive traits. This approach aligns with the TaxAgent framework, which uses LLMs to iteratively optimize tax rates, balancing equity and productivity \[[10](http://arxiv.org/abs/2303.13994)\]. 

Additionally, the integration of reinforcement learning (RL) into ABMs has been explored in the context of policy analysis \[[4](http://arxiv.org/abs/2006.05048)\]. RL models are effective at producing reward-seeking or reward-maximizing behaviors in ABM agents, outperforming traditional heuristic decision-making rules \[[4](http://arxiv.org/abs/2006.05048)\]. This is particularly relevant for the TaxAgent framework, which relies on LLMs to adaptively optimize tax policies based on simulated outcomes \[[10](http://arxiv.org/abs/2303.13994)\]. 

Furthermore, the importance of data-driven ABMs in economic research has been emphasized \[[16](http://arxiv.org/abs/2412.16591)\]. These models, initialized with real-world micro-data and calibrated to empirical time series, offer a stronger alternative to equilibrium models by capturing the dynamic and heterogeneous nature of economic systems \[[16](http://arxiv.org/abs/2412.16591)\]. The TaxAgent framework, which incorporates heterogeneous H-Agents and utilizes LLMs for policy optimization, aligns with this trend \[[10](http://arxiv.org/abs/2303.13994)\].
