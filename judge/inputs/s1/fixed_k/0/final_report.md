Traditional economic models often rely on mechanistic approaches and assume bounded rationality, which limits their ability to capture the adaptive and heterogeneous behaviors of economic agents \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/1804.09284)\]. In contrast, ABMs simulate micro-level interactions among agents to generate macro-level outcomes, offering a more nuanced understanding of policy impacts \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/2504.01242)\]. Recent advancements have integrated machine learning techniques, such as reinforcement learning (RL), into ABMs to improve the accuracy and adaptability of agent decision-making \[[4](http://arxiv.org/abs/2006.05048)\]. For instance, RL models have been used to enhance the performance of ABMs in scenarios like minority games and influenza transmission, demonstrating their ability to produce reward-seeking behaviors \[[4](http://arxiv.org/abs/2006.05048)\]. Additionally, ABMs have been applied to study tax evasion dynamics, incorporating social and behavioral factors through models inspired by statistical mechanics \[[6](http://arxiv.org/abs/1112.0233)\]. These models allow for the exploration of policy interventions under varying enforcement scenarios, providing insights into effective strategies for reducing inequality \[[6](http://arxiv.org/abs/1112.0233)\]. Furthermore, the development of frameworks like ABIDES-Economist and ADAGE has enabled more realistic and adaptive simulations by incorporating heterogeneous agent behaviors and environmental interactions \[[5](http://arxiv.org/abs/2402.09563)\]\[[10](http://arxiv.org/abs/2303.13994)\]. ### <a id='category-reinforcement-learning-in-adaptive-policies'></a>Reinforcement Learning in Adaptive Policies: 3 papers found.
Adaptive policies in pervasive computing and economic systems are increasingly relying on reinforcement learning (RL) to handle complexity and heterogeneity. In pervasive computing, challenges include integrating devices with varying computational resources and dynamically adjusting to environmental changes, which existing systems often address through low-level SLOs \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2502.16879)\]. However, these approaches introduce significant complexity, prompting the use of Active Inference (AIF) to design agents that can balance global system constraints and adapt to changes, achieving up to 98% fulfillment rates in heterogeneous environments \[[7](http://arxiv.org/abs/2410.09099)\]. Similarly, in economic systems, traditional models like the Saez Optimal Taxation fail to account for taxpayer heterogeneity and irrational behavior, necessitating novel approaches such as TaxAgent, which integrates large language models (LLMs) with agent-based modeling to iteratively optimize tax policies \[[10](http://arxiv.org/abs/2303.13994)\]. In robotics and game environments, generalist agents face the challenge of rapid adaptation, with recent work proposing retrieval-based methods and semi-parametric agents like REGENT that achieve strong performance with fewer parameters and training data \[[9](http://arxiv.org/abs/2412.04759)\]. These developments highlight the growing importance of RL in creating adaptive systems that can effectively manage complexity, heterogeneity, and dynamic environments across diverse domains.

### <a id='category-heterogeneous-agent-behavior'></a>Heterogeneous Agent Behavior: 3 papers found.
The category "Heterogeneous Agent Behavior" explores the complexities of agent interactions in various systems, particularly in financial markets and multi-agent task environments. In financial markets, heterogeneous agents exhibit behaviors such as herding, which significantly influence market dynamics \[[14](http://arxiv.org/abs/1403.1574)\]. This behavior is modeled through agent-based frameworks that incorporate both exogenous and endogenous noise, leading to the development of a three-state herding model that captures macroscopic market trends and short-term fluctuations \[[14](http://arxiv.org/abs/1403.1574)\]. The resulting model successfully reproduces empirical data from multiple stock exchanges, highlighting the role of herding in market bubbles and the dominance of behavioral factors over rational decision-making \[[14](http://arxiv.org/abs/1403.1574)\]. In multi-agent task environments, heterogeneous agents with varying capabilities are managed through a novel task grammar that allows for flexible task specification without explicit constraints on agent roles \[[17](http://arxiv.org/abs/2402.00296)\]. This approach enables the automatic synthesis of control policies that ensure task completion while maintaining scalability and adaptability. Additionally, studies on heterogeneous learning agents in asset markets reveal that different learning strategies, such as Bayesian and no-regret learners, perform variably under stochastic conditions, with no-regret methods offering greater robustness against model inaccuracies \[[19](http://arxiv.org/abs/2502.08597)\]. These findings underscore the importance of designing adaptive systems that can accommodate diverse agent behaviors and learning dynamics.
