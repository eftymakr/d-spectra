## Related Works

Recent advancements in Text-to-Speech (TTS) systems have significantly enhanced speech naturalness, but challenges remain in achieving precise prosody control and mispronunciation correction \[[24](http://arxiv.org/abs/1606.09222)\]. Traditional approaches for prosody manipulation often rely on specialized modules or additional training, which limits their ability to perform post-hoc adjustments \[[24](http://arxiv.org/abs/1606.09222)\]. Similarly, conventional mispronunciation correction methods depend on grapheme-to-phoneme (G2P) dictionaries, making them less practical in low-resource settings \[[24](http://arxiv.org/abs/1606.09222)\]. These limitations highlight the need for more flexible and efficient methods for post-hoc TTS editing.

Several studies have explored methods for improving prosody in TTS. For instance, \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2102.08328)\] introduces a context-aware method for text-based speech editing, aiming to reduce prosody mismatches around edited regions. The approach uses neural networks to generate prosody features that are dependent on the surrounding speech, followed by pitch-shift and time-stretching operations. \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/2011.06465)\] proposes a hierarchical prosody modeling approach for non-autoregressive TTS, where phoneme-level prosody is conditioned on word-level features, leading to improved audio quality and naturalness. \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/2211.13533)\] further explores prosody control in spontaneous TTS using neural HMMs, demonstrating the ability to reproduce expressive phenomena in spontaneous speech.

Mispronunciation correction has also been a focus of recent research. \[[25](http://arxiv.org/abs/2303.00171)\] presents a system for detecting and correcting mispronunciations in TTS, using a Siamese network with dynamic time warping (DTW) and triplet loss. This method improves pronunciation accuracy by ~6% over strong baselines. \[[27](http://arxiv.org/abs/2201.10198)\] investigates a hybrid CTC-attention-based approach for detecting mispronunciations in L2 English speakers, showing that end-to-end models can achieve better performance than traditional methods. \[[28](http://arxiv.org/abs/2106.04312)\] introduces a speech BERT model to extract fine-grained prosody information, which is used to improve the naturalness of synthesized speech.

These approaches highlight the growing interest in improving the flexibility and expressiveness of TTS systems, particularly in scenarios where post-hoc adjustments are necessary. Furthermore, recent efforts have explored zero-shot language transfer and subphoneme-level control, showcasing the potential for more versatile and adaptable TTS models \[[18](http://arxiv.org/abs/2408.06827)\]. Overall, these studies underscore the importance of developing more flexible and controllable TTS systems that can adapt to a wide range of linguistic and expressive requirements.
