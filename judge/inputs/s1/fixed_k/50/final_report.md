## Related Works

Existing research has explored the use of knowledge graphs (KGs) to enhance the management and analysis of machine learning (ML) resources. Several studies have focused on the construction and application of KGs in various domains, including e-commerce, life sciences, and industrial systems \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2208.08130)\]\[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/2411.01023)\]\[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/2112.10006)\]. For instance, the work on the Fashion Knowledge Graph (FKG) demonstrates how KGs can be enriched using natural language processing (NLP) and machine learning techniques to capture detailed product attributes and relationships \[[6](http://arxiv.org/abs/2206.01087)\]. Similarly, the Multi-modal UMLS Graph Learning (MMUGL) approach leverages graph neural networks over medical knowledge graphs to improve patient outcome predictions by incorporating multi-modal data and prior medical knowledge \[[4](http://arxiv.org/abs/2307.04461)\].

In the context of e-commerce, the Pre-trained Knowledge Graph Model (PKGM) has been proposed to address the challenges of incomplete and noisy KGs by providing a unified and efficient way to access and utilize knowledge for downstream tasks such as item recommendation and classification \[[7](http://arxiv.org/abs/2203.00964)\]. This aligns with the goals of HuggingKG, which aims to enhance the management of ML resources by capturing structured representations and enabling advanced queries \[[10](http://arxiv.org/abs/2312.16810)\].

Moreover, the integration of large language models (LLMs) with KGs has been explored as a promising direction for improving the interpretability and factual accuracy of LLMs \[[9](http://arxiv.org/abs/2306.08302)\]. This synergy between LLMs and KGs is particularly relevant for the HuggingKG project, as it enables the incorporation of structured knowledge to enhance the understanding and reasoning capabilities of models used for resource management and recommendation.

In the life sciences, the use of KGs has been extensively studied for managing and interpreting complex biological and medical data \[[8](http://arxiv.org/abs/2309.17255)\]. These KGs often require careful construction and maintenance to ensure accuracy and completeness, which is also a critical aspect of HuggingKG. The work on the Industry 4.0 Knowledge Graph (I40KG) highlights the importance of standardization and semantic interoperability in the context of industrial systems, which can inform the design of HuggingKG for ML resource management \[[11](http://arxiv.org/abs/2107.01910)\].

Additionally, the application of KGs in the scholarly domain has been explored to facilitate the extraction and organization of scientific knowledge from research publications \[[5](http://arxiv.org/abs/2011.01103)\]. This is closely related to the goal of HuggingKG, which aims to build a comprehensive and structured representation of ML resources from open-source platforms like Hugging Face.

The challenges of constructing and maintaining large-scale KGs, such as data quality, scalability, and the integration of heterogeneous data sources, have been widely discussed in the literature

The category "Challenges in Large-Scale KG Development" highlights key issues in building and maintaining high-quality knowledge graphs (KGs), such as errors, duplicates, and missing values, which can hinder their utility in real-world applications \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2208.08130)\]. These challenges are compounded by the need for structured representations in domains like machine learning (ML), where existing platforms like Hugging Face lack the ability to support advanced queries and analyses such as tracing model evolution or recommending relevant datasets \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2208.08130)\]. To address these limitations, efforts have been made to construct large-scale KGs, such as HuggingKG, which integrates structured relations and textual attributes from open-source ML resources, enabling more sophisticated tasks like resource recommendation and classification \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2208.08130)\]. Additionally, the reliance on labeled data in ML models has led to research exploring the use of auxiliary information from KGs to reduce the need for extensive labeled samples, particularly in zero-shot and few-shot learning scenarios \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/2411.01023)\]. This trend underscores the growing importance of integrating KGs into ML workflows to enhance performance and adaptability in dynamic environments \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/2411.01023)\]. Furthermore, the development of KGs faces challenges related to data quality, model generalization, and the integration of diverse data sources, which are critical for advancing applications in fields such as Prognostics and Health Management (PHM) \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/2112.10006)\]. These challenges highlight the need for robust curation frameworks and systematic approaches to ensure the reliability and effectiveness of KGs in supporting complex tasks \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2208.08130)\].

### <a id='category-large-language-models-and-kg-synergy'></a>Large Language Models and KG Synergy: 1 papers found.
The integration of large language models (LLMs) and knowledge graphs (KGs) represents a promising direction for enhancing both systems' capabilities in natural language processing and knowledge representation. LLMs, such as ChatGPT and GPT4, offer strong generalization and emergent abilities but are often criticized for their black-box nature and limited factual accuracy \[[9](http://arxiv.org/abs/2306.08302)\]. In contrast, KGs like Wikipedia and Huapu provide structured, explicit representations of factual knowledge, making them valuable for improving the interpretability and inference capabilities of LLMs \[[9](http://arxiv.org/abs/2306.08302)\]. However, KGs face challenges in scalability and dynamic knowledge updating, which limits their ability to capture new or evolving information \[[9](http://arxiv.org/abs/2306.08302)\]. To address these limitations, recent efforts have focused on unifying LLMs and KGs through three complementary frameworks: KG-enhanced LLMs, which integrate KGs during pre-training and inference to improve factual accuracy; LLM-augmented KGs, which leverage LLMs for tasks such as knowledge embedding, completion, and question answering; and synergized LLMs + KGs, where both systems collaborate to enable bidirectional reasoning and mutual enhancement \[[9](http://arxiv.org/abs/2306.08302)\]. These approaches highlight the potential for combining the strengths of LLMs and KGs to achieve more robust and interpretable AI systems.

To achieve this goal, components such as actuators, sensors, and cyber-physical systems along with their data, need to be described; moreover, interoperability conflicts arisen from various semantic representations of these components demand also solutions. To empowering communication in smart factories, a variety of standards and standardization frameworks have been proposed. Standardization frameworks classify, align, and integrate industrial standards according to their purposes and features. Various standardization frameworks have been proposed all over the world by industrial communities, e.g., RAMI4.0 or IICF. While being expressive to categorize existing standards, standardization frameworks may present divergent classifications of the same standard. Mismatches between standard classifications generate semantic interoperability conflicts that negatively impact the effectiveness of communication in smart factories. In this article, we tackle the problem of standard interoperability across different standardization frameworks, and devise a knowledge-driven approach that allows for the description of standards and standardization frameworks into an Industry 4.0 knowledge graph (I40KG). The I40KG integrates more than 200 standards and four standardization frameworks. Additionally, the I40KG has been linked to existing knowledge graphs and an automated reasoning has been implemented to reveal implicit relations between standards as well as mappings across standardization frameworks. We analyze both the number of discovered relations between standards and the accuracy of these relations. Observed results indicate that both reasoning and linking processes enable for increasing the connectivity in the knowledge graph by up to 80%, whilst up to 96% of the relations can be validated. These outcomes suggest that integrating standards and standardization frameworks into the I40KG enables the resolution of semantic interoperability conflicts, empowering the communication in smart factories.»
