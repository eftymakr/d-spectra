## Related Works

Fund allocation in financial domains has attracted significant research interest, with a growing emphasis on integrating forecasting and optimization in a unified framework. Traditional approaches often separate these tasks, leading to goal mismatch and suboptimal results \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2206.03246)\]. For instance, prediction-only methods ignore the optimization aspect, while Predict-then-Optimize (PnO) approaches may suffer from misalignment between forecasted values and the optimization objective \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/2503.06928)\]. To address these limitations, recent works have explored end-to-end frameworks that jointly optimize forecasting and allocation \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/2501.02945)\]. 

Several studies have investigated the use of deep learning models for financial time series forecasting. The Portfolio Transformer (PT) network, for example, directly optimizes the Sharpe ratio without requiring explicit return forecasts, demonstrating superior risk-adjusted performance \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2206.03246)\]. Similarly, the CNN and Transformer hybrid model proposed in \[[5](http://arxiv.org/abs/2304.04912)\] effectively captures both short-term and long-term dependencies, achieving competitive accuracy in intraday stock price forecasting. These models highlight the importance of leveraging attention mechanisms and convolutional layers to improve forecasting accuracy.

In the context of asset allocation, the work in \[[7](http://arxiv.org/abs/2503.11499)\] introduces a novel framework that integrates macroeconomic regime detection with asset allocation, demonstrating improved portfolio performance by adapting to changing economic conditions. This aligns with the idea of incorporating external factors into the forecasting process to enhance decision-making. Additionally, the dynamic asset allocation approach in \[[13](http://arxiv.org/abs/2406.09578)\] leverages asset-specific regime forecasts to improve portfolio construction, showing consistent outperformance across various portfolio models.

The challenge of forecasting financial assets with limited historical data has also been addressed through transfer learning. The work in \[[4](http://arxiv.org/abs/2503.12648)\] proposes a multi-source transfer learning approach, using complementary source data to improve volatility forecasts for new issues and spin-offs. This method demonstrates that integrating external data can significantly enhance forecasting accuracy, even in the early stages of asset trading.

Moreover, the integration of uncertainty quantification into forecasting models has gained traction. The work in \[[11](http://arxiv.org/abs/2407.06390)\] introduces JANET, a framework for constructing conformal prediction regions that provide theoretical guarantees for time series forecasting. This is particularly relevant for financial applications where uncertainty estimation is crucial for risk management. Similarly, the study in \[[15](http://arxiv.org/abs/2307.11494)\] explores self-guiding diffusion models for probabilistic time series forecasting, demonstrating their effectiveness in generating accurate and reliable forecasts.

In summary, the field of financial time series forecasting and allocation has seen significant advancements, with a growing emphasis on end-to-end frameworks, uncertainty quantification, and the integration of external factors. These developments provide a strong foundation for the proposed RTS-PnO framework
