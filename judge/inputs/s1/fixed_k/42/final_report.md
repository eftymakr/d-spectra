## Related Works

The growing adoption of cloud-native applications has led to increased reliance on managed cloud services and containerization technologies such as Kubernetes \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2501.14753)\]. These technologies enable rapid deployment and scalability, but they also introduce new challenges in cost management, particularly in the context of network usage and infrastructure expenses \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/1901.04946)\]. FinOps, an emerging discipline that combines financial management with cloud operations, has gained traction as a means to optimize costs in cloud-based applications \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/2104.02423)\]. While prior research has focused on system-level optimization strategies for resource efficiency in containerized systems \[[4](http://arxiv.org/abs/2307.12479)\], the analysis of network costs in Kubernetes clusters remains underexplored \[[5](http://arxiv.org/abs/2405.12311)\].

Several studies have investigated the use of Kubernetes for orchestrating microservices and managing containerized applications. For instance, Kubernetes has been evaluated as a platform for ensuring availability in microservice architectures, though its default configuration has been found to result in significant service outages in certain scenarios \[[6](http://arxiv.org/abs/2006.15275)\]. Additionally, the performance and availability of Kubernetes have been questioned in edge computing environments due to the limitations of its etcd-based state management \[[7](http://arxiv.org/abs/2207.01222)\]. To address these challenges, researchers have proposed modifications to Kubernetes, such as adopting an eventually consistent approach to improve scalability and performance \[[8](http://arxiv.org/abs/2401.07417)\].

In the context of cost optimization, the FinOps framework has been introduced as a solution for managing cloud spend through budgeting, enforcement of spending limits, and predictive cost estimation using Infrastructure-as-Code \[[9](http://arxiv.org/abs/2301.01178)\]. This aligns with broader efforts to reduce cloud costs, particularly in AI and machine learning workloads, where GPU compute represents a significant portion of technical budgets \[[10](http://arxiv.org/abs/1507.05492)\]. Techniques such as model quantization, GPU instance selection, and inference optimization have been explored to achieve substantial cost savings \[[11](http://arxiv.org/abs/2502.02842)\]. Furthermore, solutions like SpotKube have been developed to optimize microservices deployment costs using Kubernetes and spot pricing, ensuring cost-effectiveness without compromising performance \[[12](http://arxiv.org/abs/2412.15778)\].

The integration of workflow systems with Kubernetes has also been a focus of recent research. For example, KubeAdaptor has been proposed as a docking framework to ensure consistency in task scheduling order between workflow systems and Kubernetes, improving the efficiency of containerized workflows \[[13](http://arxiv.org/abs/1207.2799)\]. Additionally, efforts have been made to extend Kubernetes networking to support large-scale and distributed scenarios by leveraging Segment Routing over IPv6 (SRv6), offering enhanced traffic engineering capabilities \[[14](http://arxiv.org/abs/2105.01939)\].

In summary, the related works highlight the growing interest in optimizing cloud costs, particularly in Kubernetes-based environments, and the need for more sophisticated approaches to manage network and infrastructure expenses. These studies provide a foundation for further research into FinOps

### <a id='category-finops-framework-and-cost-management'></a>FinOps Framework and Cost Management: 1 papers found.
FinOps practices have gained significant attention as enterprises increasingly adopt cloud infrastructure to manage their operations, with a focus on optimizing costs and ensuring accountability in cloud spend \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2501.14753)\]. These practices integrate financial management with cloud operations to address the challenges of achieving holistic cloud spend visibility and cost optimization \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2501.14753)\]. A notable example is the ABACUS framework, which automates budget analysis and cloud usage surveillance by setting budgets, enforcing them through deployment blocking, and issuing alerts when spending thresholds are breached \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2501.14753)\]. Additionally, ABACUS leverages Infrastructure-as-Code to provide engineering teams with cost estimates before cloud resources are deployed, thereby promoting proactive cost management \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2501.14753)\]. These approaches highlight the growing need for tools and methodologies that support financial accountability and operational efficiency in cloud environments. As the field continues to evolve, further research is being proposed to enhance the state of the art in FinOps \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2501.14753)\].

### <a id='category-kubernetes-and-containerization-challenges'></a>Kubernetes and Containerization Challenges: 2 papers found.
The category "Kubernetes and Containerization Challenges" highlights key concerns around the use of Kubernetes in managing containerized microservices, particularly in terms of availability and security. Research indicates that while Kubernetes provides a robust framework for deploying and managing microservices, its default configurations may not always ensure optimal availability, especially in carrier-grade environments \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2501.14753)\]. This is further compounded by the fact that Kubernetes deployments are vulnerable to security risks, as evidenced by incidents such as the 2018 Tesla breach \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/1901.04946)\]. To address these challenges, studies have proposed systematic approaches to enhance security, including the implementation of role-based access control (RBAC), regular application of security patches, and the enforcement of pod and network-specific security policies \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/1901.04946)\]. These practices are essential for mitigating vulnerabilities and ensuring the reliability of Kubernetes-based systems. Additionally, the impact of redundancy on application availability has been explored, with findings suggesting that strategic redundancy can significantly improve the resilience of microservice architectures \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2501.14753)\]. Overall, these studies underscore the need for a balanced approach that leverages Kubernetes' capabilities while addressing its inherent limitations through careful configuration and security practices.

### <a id='category-edge-computing-and-kubernetes-limitations'></a>Edge Computing and Kubernetes Limitations: 3 papers found.
Edge computing and Kubernetes limitations are critical areas of research, particularly as edge environments demand high performance, scalability, and low latency. Kubernetes, while widely adopted for container orchestration, faces challenges in edge deployments due to its reliance on etcd for cluster state management, which introduces latency and throughput issues at scale \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/2104.02423)\]. This limitation is exacerbated by the fact that approximately 30% of Kubernetes requests are write operations, further degrading performance in edge scenarios \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/2104.02423)\]. Additionally, 5G networks, which support edge computing through network slicing and virtualization, still struggle with integration and maturity in private 5G setups, affecting slice isolation and resource allocation \[[11](http://arxiv.org/abs/2502.02842)\]. These challenges highlight the need for alternative approaches, such as decentralized AI systems that can address the limitations of centralized cloud-based models by enabling distributed learning and inference \[[15](http://arxiv.org/abs/2501.05323)\]. The proposed solutions aim to enhance the scalability and performance of edge computing environments while maintaining the necessary levels of availability and security.

### <a id='category-system-level-optimization-strategies'></a>System-Level Optimization Strategies: 4 papers found.
System-Level Optimization Strategies encompass a range of approaches aimed at improving the efficiency and performance of networked systems, with particular emphasis on computational complexity, parallelism, and cost modeling. Community detection in networks, for instance, has been a focal point, with algorithms like label propagation offering linear complexity but still facing challenges in scalability \[[10](http://arxiv.org/abs/1507.05492)\]. To address these limitations, parallel toolkits have been introduced to enhance performance on both distributed and shared memory machines, significantly reducing running time and improving speedup \[[10](http://arxiv.org/abs/1507.05492)\]. Similarly, quantum computing introduces novel paradigms for optimization, leveraging superposition and entanglement to perform computations in parallel, thereby offering potential advantages over classical algorithms for specific tasks \[[12](http://arxiv.org/abs/2412.15778)\]. In infrastructure recovery, discrete optimization problems have been formulated to minimize costs associated with network restoration, considering interdependencies and varying cost functions, with NP-hardness and heuristic solutions being key considerations \[[13](http://arxiv.org/abs/1207.2799)\]. Additionally, file caching strategies have evolved to incorporate rental and zapping costs, with competitive analysis frameworks providing bounds for online algorithms to manage dynamic resource allocation efficiently \[[17](http://arxiv.org/abs/1208.2724)\]. These strategies collectively highlight the interdisciplinary nature of system-level optimization, integrating insights from computer science, operations research, and quantum computing to address real-world challenges in networked and cloud environments.

### <a id='category-network-cost-analysis-in-kubernetes'></a>Network Cost Analysis in Kubernetes: 2 papers found.
Network cost analysis in Kubernetes is an emerging area of research that focuses on understanding and optimizing the financial implications of network usage in containerized environments. As highlighted in the literature, the complexity of analyzing network costs in Kubernetes clusters stems from the dynamic and scalable nature of container orchestration systems, which can lead to significant variations in resource consumption and associated expenses \[[14](http://arxiv.org/abs/2105.01939)\]. Researchers have identified the need for advanced methodologies to evaluate network performance and cost efficiency, particularly given the challenges of accurately measuring and predicting network expenditures in distributed systems \[[16](http://arxiv.org/abs/2002.10992)\]. This has led to the development of various approximating algorithms aimed at improving the efficiency of network cost analysis, with a particular emphasis on the box-covering method, which is used to assess fractality in network structures \[[14](http://arxiv.org/abs/2105.01939)\]. Additionally, the study of migration networks has contributed to the broader understanding of network interactions, offering insights into how network structures can be analyzed and optimized for cost and performance \[[16](http://arxiv.org/abs/2002.10992)\]. These developments underscore the growing interest in integrating network cost analysis with broader cloud management strategies, particularly within the FinOps framework, which seeks to bridge the gap between financial management and cloud operations \[[14](http://arxiv.org/abs/2105.01939)\].
