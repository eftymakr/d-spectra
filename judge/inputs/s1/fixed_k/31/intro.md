## Related Works

Recent advances in deep learning have seen a shift towards self-supervised pre-training paradigms, which have demonstrated success in fields such as natural language processing and computer vision \[[4](http://arxiv.org/abs/2007.00800)\]. In the context of genomics, similar approaches have been explored, with models like GeneBERT \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2110.05231)\] and SNP2Vec \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/2204.06699)\] being introduced to pre-train on genomic data. These models focus on sequence-based representations but often overlook the importance of genomic profiles such as chromatin accessibility, which are crucial for understanding regulatory mechanisms \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2110.05231)\]. 

While self-supervised methods have shown promise, they are limited in their ability to capture the complex interactions between DNA sequences and genomic profiles. This is because pure DNA sequences lack the contextual information necessary for accurate prediction of regulatory functions \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2110.05231)\]. In contrast, supervised training methods, such as those used in Deep Motif \[[15](http://arxiv.org/abs/1605.01133)\], have been applied to classify genomic sequences and extract meaningful motifs. However, these methods often focus on specific tasks and do not generalize well across different genomic profiles and species \[[15](http://arxiv.org/abs/1605.01133)\].

To address these limitations, researchers have explored hybrid approaches that combine both self-supervised and supervised learning. For instance, the work by \[[17](http://arxiv.org/abs/2204.02130)\] introduces SemanticCAP, which leverages a language model to learn contextual features from gene sequences, enhancing the prediction of chromatin accessibility. This approach demonstrates the value of incorporating contextual information into genomic models \[[17](http://arxiv.org/abs/2204.02130)\]. Similarly, the work on featureCounts \[[23](http://arxiv.org/abs/1305.3347)\] highlights the importance of efficient read summarization in genomic analysis, which is a foundational step in many downstream tasks \[[23](http://arxiv.org/abs/1305.3347)\].

Furthermore, the application of self-supervised learning to image and 3D data has also inspired similar approaches in genomics. For example, the work on self-supervised pre-training of 3D point cloud networks \[[20](http://arxiv.org/abs/2211.11801)\] demonstrates how combining image and point cloud modalities can improve performance in tasks where labeled data is scarce. This concept has parallels in genomic data, where integrating multiple modalities such as DNA sequences and epigenomic profiles could enhance predictive power \[[20](http://arxiv.org/abs/2211.11801)\].

This work highlights the importance of network topology in understanding chromatin interactions and how specific epigenomic features can influence these interactions \[[28](http://arxiv.org/abs/1512.00268)\].
