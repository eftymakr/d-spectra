## Related Works

Recent advances in deep learning have seen a shift towards self-supervised pre-training paradigms, which have demonstrated success in fields such as natural language processing and computer vision \[[4](http://arxiv.org/abs/2007.00800)\]. In the context of genomics, similar approaches have been explored, with models like GeneBERT \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2110.05231)\] and SNP2Vec \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/2204.06699)\] being introduced to pre-train on genomic data. These models focus on sequence-based representations but often overlook the importance of genomic profiles such as chromatin accessibility, which are crucial for understanding regulatory mechanisms \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2110.05231)\]. 

While self-supervised methods have shown promise, they are limited in their ability to capture the complex interactions between DNA sequences and genomic profiles. This is because pure DNA sequences lack the contextual information necessary for accurate prediction of regulatory functions \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2110.05231)\]. In contrast, supervised training methods, such as those used in Deep Motif \[[15](http://arxiv.org/abs/1605.01133)\], have been applied to classify genomic sequences and extract meaningful motifs. However, these methods often focus on specific tasks and do not generalize well across different genomic profiles and species \[[15](http://arxiv.org/abs/1605.01133)\].
