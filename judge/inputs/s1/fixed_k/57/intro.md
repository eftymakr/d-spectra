## Related Works

Generative retrieval (GR) has gained significant attention as a promising paradigm in information retrieval (IR), offering end-to-end optimization of retrieval tasks by generating document identifiers (docids) directly from queries \[[4](http://arxiv.org/abs/2304.04171)\]. However, most existing GR models are primarily evaluated on static document collections, with limited studies on their performance in dynamic corpora where document sets evolve continuously \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2305.18952)\]. This section reviews related works that address the challenges of GR in dynamic environments, the design of docids, and the efficiency of retrieval systems.

Several studies have explored the adaptability of GR models to dynamic corpora. For instance, \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2305.18952)\] highlights that GR models, which apply autoregressive language models to IR, demonstrate better adaptability to evolving knowledge compared to Dual Encoders (DE), which are commonly used in retrieval systems. Additionally, \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/2308.14968)\] introduces a continual learning framework for GR, addressing the challenge of incrementally indexing new documents while preserving the ability to answer queries with both previously and newly indexed documents. The proposed Continual-LEarner for generative Retrieval (CLEVER) model employs Incremental Product Quantization for efficient encoding of new documents and a memory-augmented learning mechanism to retain knowledge from previous documents.

The design of docids plays a crucial role in the effectiveness of GR models. While numeric-based docids are often favored for their efficiency, they suffer from poor generalization to unseen documents, likely due to overfitting on the training set \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2305.18952)\]. In contrast, text-based docids, which align better with the semantic knowledge of pre-trained language models, show superior performance in dynamic settings \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2305.18952)\]. This is further supported by \[[6](http://arxiv.org/abs/2305.13859)\], which emphasizes the importance of semantic alignment between docids and language models' pre-trained knowledge for effective retrieval.

Efforts have also been made to improve the efficiency of GR models. For example, \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/2408.02152)\] proposes a few-shot indexing-based GR framework (Few-Shot GR), which eliminates the need for extensive training by prompting a large language model (LLM) to generate docids for all documents in a corpus. This approach significantly reduces training costs while maintaining performance comparable to state-of-the-art methods \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/2408.02152)\].

In addition, \[[4](http://arxiv.org/abs/2304.04171)\] introduces GenRet, a document tokenization learning method that learns to tokenize documents into short discrete representations (docids) via a discrete auto-encoding approach. GenRet enables end-to-end modeling of document retrieval tasks and achieves state-of-the-art results on benchmark datasets \[[4](http://arxiv.org/abs/2304.04171)\].
