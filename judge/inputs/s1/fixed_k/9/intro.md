## Related Works

The detection and segmentation of paramagnetic rim lesions (PRLs) in multiple sclerosis (MS) have been the focus of several recent studies, particularly due to their significance as emerging biomarkers. Traditional methods for identifying PRLs rely on manual segmentation and are time-consuming \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2412.10492)\]. To address this, deep learning approaches have been proposed to automate the detection and segmentation of PRLs. For instance, QSM-RimNet has been developed for PRL detection, but it lacks the capability for accurate rim segmentation \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2412.10492)\]. In contrast, QSM-RimDS has been introduced as a U-Net-based method that not only detects PRLs but also provides rim segmentation, offering better performance and consistency compared to QSM-RimNet \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2412.10492)\]. 

Lesion segmentation in MS has also been explored using various deep learning architectures. A fully convolutional neural network (CNN) has been proposed for segmenting white matter lesions from multi-contrast MRI images, showing significant improvements over existing methods \[[7](http://arxiv.org/abs/1803.09172)\]. Similarly, a multi-branch CNN has been designed for MS lesion segmentation, incorporating multi-scale feature fusion blocks to improve performance \[[28](http://arxiv.org/abs/1811.02942)\]. These methods highlight the importance of architectural design in achieving accurate segmentation.

The challenge of class imbalance, particularly between PRLs and non-PRLs, has also been addressed in recent works. A study introduced a method for generating synthetic PRLs using a generative adversarial network (GAN) to improve classifier performance and address the minority class issue \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2412.10492)\]. This approach has been further extended to include a denoising method that allows training on ambiguous rim cases, thereby improving detection accuracy \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2412.10492)\]. 

In addition to lesion detection, the segmentation of MS lesions has been enhanced using attention mechanisms. A framework with an augmented U-Net architecture and attention mechanisms has been proposed to segment and quantify MS lesions, achieving a high Dice score of 89% \[[13](http://arxiv.org/abs/2304.10790)\]. This approach has demonstrated robustness and generalization ability on new test samples. 

Lesion activity segmentation, which involves detecting new and enlarging lesions between two time points, has also been explored. A two-path CNN with attention-guided interactions has been proposed to improve lesion activity segmentation, outperforming classic approaches \[[6](http://arxiv.org/abs/2008.02001)\]. This method enables effective information exchange between the two time points, leading to improved performance.

Furthermore, the use of synthetic lesions for data augmentation has been shown to improve segmentation performance. A method for generating synthetic lesions using a partial convolution GAN has been proposed, leading to significant improvements in segmentation accuracy
