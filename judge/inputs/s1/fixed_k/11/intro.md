## Related Works

3D human pose estimation has attracted significant research interest due to its applications in human-computer interaction, robotics, and virtual reality. Most existing methods rely on either graph convolutional networks (GCNs) or transformers to model the complex spatial and temporal dependencies in human motion \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2505.01003)\]. GCNs are effective in capturing local joint relationships but often fail to model long-range dependencies and body-wide interactions \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/2405.03055)\]. Transformers, on the other hand, excel at modeling long-range dependencies but may neglect local details crucial for accurate pose estimation \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/1708.01101)\]. 

Several works have attempted to address the limitations of these approaches. For instance, \[[4](http://arxiv.org/abs/2412.19676)\] introduces a multi-hop graph transformer network that combines multi-head self-attention with multi-hop graph convolutional layers to capture both local and global dependencies. This approach enhances the model's ability to handle spatial details and long-range interactions. Similarly, \[[5](http://arxiv.org/abs/2111.00950)\] proposes a method that leverages pyramid structures to improve scale invariance, which is essential for handling variations in human body parts during pose estimation. 

The challenge of capturing long-range dependencies has also been addressed through the use of attention mechanisms. \[[6](http://arxiv.org/abs/2401.16700)\] introduces a spatial-temporal transformer framework that models both intra-frame and inter-frame dependencies, enabling accurate 3D pose estimation from video sequences. Additionally, \[[7](http://arxiv.org/abs/2407.10099)\] proposes a dual-stream model that integrates local features with global dependencies to enhance pose estimation accuracy. This approach uses a module with large kernels to capture fine-grained local dependencies, complementing the global modeling capabilities of the transformer.

Another key challenge is the accurate modeling of body structure and motion continuity. \[[8](http://arxiv.org/abs/2306.12525)\] introduces a hierarchical graph network that uses a denser graph topology to better reflect the human body structure and reduce ambiguity in 2D-to-3D pose estimation. This approach improves the geometric accuracy of the estimated poses by incorporating more detailed spatial relationships. Similarly, \[[9](http://arxiv.org/abs/2003.07516)\] proposes a method that leverages residual connections to preserve important information during deep network training, leading to more accurate and stable pose predictions.

Recent works have also focused on improving the efficiency and scalability of 3D human pose estimation models. \[[10](http://arxiv.org/abs/1810.01616)\] presents a lightweight multi-scale transformer architecture that integrates graph convolution modules with self-attention to capture both local and global dependencies efficiently. This approach reduces computational complexity while maintaining high accuracy. Furthermore, \[[11](http://arxiv.org/abs/2304.14045)\] introduces a method that combines graph convolutional networks with attention mechanisms to better model the spatial and temporal dynamics of human motion.
