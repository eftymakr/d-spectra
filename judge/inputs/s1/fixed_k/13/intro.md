## Related Works

Long-tailed recognition remains a challenging problem in computer vision due to the significant imbalance in class distribution, where a few dominant classes (head classes) contain the majority of the data, while the remaining classes (tail classes) are severely underrepresented \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2403.06151)\]. This imbalance leads to biased feature learning and poor generalization performance on tail classes \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/2306.15925)\]. Several approaches have been proposed to address this issue, primarily focusing on data augmentation, class balancing, and contrastive learning techniques \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/2309.11177)\].

Early methods attempted to tackle the long-tailed problem by using data reweighing or resampling techniques to balance class distribution \[[4](http://arxiv.org/abs/2305.01160)\]. However, these approaches often introduce new imbalances between head and tail classes and fail to capture the rich semantic substructures of the former \[[5](http://arxiv.org/abs/2207.09052)\]. More recently, supervised contrastive learning (SCL) has shown promise in improving representation learning for long-tailed recognition \[[6](http://arxiv.org/abs/2406.02223)\]. SCL pulls positive samples (augmented images and same-class images) together and pushes negative samples apart to optimize the learned embedding \[[7](http://arxiv.org/abs/2211.11938)\]. However, in the context of long-tailed data, treating all positive samples equally leads to biased optimization for intra-category distances \[[8](http://arxiv.org/abs/2111.13998)\]. Additionally, SCL ignores the semantic relationships among negative samples, which can provide meaningful cues for learning \[[9](http://arxiv.org/abs/2410.06109)\].

To address these limitations, several works have proposed modifications to SCL. For instance, Decoupled Contrastive Learning (DCL) decouples the two types of positive samples in SCL and optimizes their relations toward different objectives to alleviate the influence of the imbalanced dataset \[[10](http://arxiv.org/abs/2306.13275)\]. Similarly, Balanced Contrastive Learning (BCL) introduces class-averaging and class-complement strategies to balance the gradient contributions of negative classes and ensure that all classes appear in every mini-batch \[[11](http://arxiv.org/abs/2411.09453)\]. These methods aim to form a regular simplex, which is ideal for representation learning \[[12](http://arxiv.org/abs/2309.07186)\].

Another line of research focuses on subclass balancing, where each head class is clustered into multiple subclasses of similar sizes as the tail classes, enforcing a two-layer class hierarchy during representation learning \[[13](http://arxiv.org/abs/2309.16135)\]. This approach preserves the semantic substructures of head classes while ensuring equal contribution from all instances \[[14](http://arxiv.org/abs/2408.00483)\]. Additionally, methods such as SMCL and TSC have been proposed to improve the uniformity of the feature distribution on the hypersphere, ensuring that minority classes are better represented \[[15](http://arxiv.org/abs/2206.01010)\].
