## Related Works

Multi-Domain Recommendation (MDR) has emerged as a critical research area in recommender systems, aiming to improve recommendation performance by leveraging knowledge across multiple domains \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2201.05973)\]. Traditional approaches often assume a single structure for transferring domain-shared knowledge, which may not be optimal due to the varying relevance and quality of domain-specific information \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2201.05973)\]. This limitation leads to the **Negative Transfer Problem (NTP)**, where unselective knowledge transfer can degrade performance, especially when domains conflict or contain poor-quality data \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2201.05973)\]. 

Several studies have addressed the NTP by focusing on domain adaptation and knowledge transfer. For instance, \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/2311.13188)\] investigates Cross-Domain Sequential Recommendation (CDSR), where the effectiveness of systems depends on the interplay among multiple domains. The paper proposes a framework that assesses the extent of negative transfer and adaptively assigns low weights to prediction losses from problematic domains \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/2311.13188)\]. Similarly, \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/2308.05508)\] introduces a hierarchical hypergraph network for MDR, which captures correlative preferences and eliminates domain discrepancy in item representations \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/2308.05508)\]. This approach uses two hyperedge-based modules to improve user and item representations by exploiting high-order connections \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/2308.05508)\].

Another line of research focuses on **domain-specific adaptation** and **knowledge disentanglement**. For example, \[[4](http://arxiv.org/abs/2410.05877)\] proposes a framework called MDAP, which disentangles user preferences across domains and adaptively combines embeddings to generate a comprehensive user representation \[[4](http://arxiv.org/abs/2410.05877)\]. This model enhances adaptability by allowing feature selection and representation disentanglement. In contrast, \[[5](http://arxiv.org/abs/2211.11191)\] introduces EDDA, a method that disentangles embeddings and aligns domains by leveraging random walks to identify similar user-item pairs \[[5](http://arxiv.org/abs/2211.11191)\]. This improves knowledge transfer, especially in domains with small overlaps \[[5](http://arxiv.org/abs/2211.11191)\].

Additionally, \[[6](http://arxiv.org/abs/2202.12524)\] presents a scalable MDR platform, MAMDR, which addresses domain conflicts and overfitting by using a model-agnostic learning framework. It incorporates Domain Negotiation (DN) and Domain Regularization (DR) strategies to enhance generalizability and scalability \[[6](http://arxiv.org/abs/2202.12524)\]. Similarly, \[[7](http://arxiv.org/abs/2403.17374)\] introduces HAMUR, a hyper-network-based model that dynamically generates domain-specific parameters, reducing parameter interference and improving adaptability \[[7](http://arxiv.org/abs/2403.17374)\]. These approaches highlight the importance of **domain-specific adaptation** and **parameter management** in MDR systems.
