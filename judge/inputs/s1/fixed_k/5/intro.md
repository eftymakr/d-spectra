## Related Works

Instruction-following text embedding has emerged as a critical area of research, aiming to generate dynamic text embeddings that adapt to user instructions by highlighting specific attributes of text. This task is particularly challenging due to the computational overhead of re-encoding entire corpora for each new instruction \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2212.09741)\]. Prior approaches, such as INSTRUCTOR \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2212.09741)\], have attempted to address this by training a single embedder on a multitask mixture of 330 diverse tasks, achieving state-of-the-art performance with an average improvement of 3.4% over previous results \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2212.09741)\]. However, these methods often require extensive training and are not optimized for real-time adaptation. Similarly, InBedder \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/2402.09642)\] proposes a method where instructions are treated as questions about the input text, and embeddings are generated based on the expected answers. This approach significantly improves instruction-following capabilities, particularly when applied to both large language models (LLMs) and smaller encoder-based models \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/2402.09642)\]. 

Another line of research focuses on multimodal transformer models for instruction-following agents, such as the work by \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/2210.13431)\], which introduces a multimodal transformer that encodes both visual observations and language instructions. This model is pre-trained on millions of image-text pairs and natural language text, producing generic cross-modal representations that enable effective instruction-following in vision-based environments \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/2210.13431)\]. However, these models are typically complex and not optimized for lightweight, real-time adaptation. 

In the context of instruction embedding, \[[4](http://arxiv.org/abs/2409.19680)\] introduces the concept of instruction embedding, which aims to capture latent representations of instructions for task identification. The work constructs the Instruction Embedding Benchmark (IEB) and proposes a baseline method, Prompt-based Instruction Embedding (PIE), which improves task category identification accuracy. This aligns with the goal of generating embeddings that are sensitive to instruction semantics \[[4](http://arxiv.org/abs/2409.19680)\]. 

Recent studies have also explored the use of instruction-following in speech recognition \[[18](http://arxiv.org/abs/2309.09843)\], where models are trained to understand and execute free-form text instructions. This enables a wide range of speech recognition tasks without relying on predefined command sets. However, these models often require extensive training and are not optimized for real-time adaptation \[[18](http://arxiv.org/abs/2309.09843)\]. 

The challenge of instruction-following in text-based games has also been explored, with studies showing that reinforcement learning agents often struggle to follow instructions effectively \[[21](http://arxiv.org/abs/2211.04591)\]. These works highlight the need for more robust and efficient methods for instruction-following, which is precisely what GSTransform aims to address \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2212.09741)\].
