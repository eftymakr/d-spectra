## Related Works

Brain tumor segmentation in medical imaging has been a focal point for deep learning research due to its critical role in diagnosis, treatment planning, and prognosis. Early approaches relied on traditional image processing techniques, such as thresholding and region-based segmentation, which were limited in their ability to capture complex tumor structures \[[26](http://arxiv.org/abs/1503.02466)\]. With the advent of deep learning, convolutional neural networks (CNNs) have become the cornerstone of modern segmentation methods, particularly for 3D medical images \[[29](http://arxiv.org/abs/1803.08691)\]. The U-Net architecture, originally designed for biomedical image segmentation, has been widely adopted and modified to address the challenges of 3D data \[[18](http://arxiv.org/abs/2010.13082)\]. Variants such as the 3D U-Net \[[13](http://arxiv.org/abs/1811.02654)\] and nnU-Net \[[25](http://arxiv.org/abs/2011.00848)\] have been developed to handle the high dimensionality and variability of brain tumor data.

The integration of pre-trained models has also been explored to improve performance, especially in the context of limited training data. For instance, the EfficientNet model has been incorporated into 3D segmentation tasks by adapting its 2D structure to handle 3D inputs \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2011.11052)\]. Similarly, transfer learning from 2D classification networks to 3D segmentation tasks has been investigated, with methods such as the Dilated Inception U-Net (DIU-Net) demonstrating improved performance by combining Inception modules with dilated convolutions \[[6](http://arxiv.org/abs/2108.06772)\]. These approaches aim to leverage the rich feature representations learned from large-scale natural image datasets to enhance medical image segmentation.

Recent work has also focused on incorporating anatomical and structural information into segmentation models. For example, the use of brain symmetry has been explored to improve segmentation accuracy by emphasizing asymmetric regions that are more likely to be tumor areas \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/1711.06636)\]. Additionally, multi-modal convolutional neural networks have been proposed to combine information from different MRI sequences, such as T1, T2, and FLAIR, to improve segmentation performance \[[11](http://arxiv.org/abs/1809.06191)\]. The MBDRes-U-Net model, which integrates multi-branch residual blocks and fused attention mechanisms, has shown promising results in reducing computational overhead while maintaining high segmentation accuracy \[[4](http://arxiv.org/abs/2411.01896)\].

The challenge of handling class imbalance and improving generalization has also been addressed through various techniques. For instance, the V-Net model has been used to segment brain tumors with a dice loss layer to address class imbalance \[[13](http://arxiv.org/abs/1811.02654)\]. Furthermore, the use of attention mechanisms, such as in the AGD-Autoencoder model, has been shown to enhance segmentation accuracy by highlighting salient regions in fMRI images \[[7](http://arxiv.org/abs/2107.03323)\]. ### <a id='category-attention-mechanisms-in-segmentation-models'></a>Attention Mechanisms in Segmentation Models: 3 papers found.
Attention mechanisms have been widely explored to improve the accuracy and efficiency of brain tumor segmentation in medical imaging. Several studies have focused on leveraging asymmetry patterns in brain structures to enhance segmentation performance, as healthy brains exhibit high-level symmetry while tumor regions are typically asymmetric \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2011.11052)\]. This insight has led to the development of models that encode brain symmetry into neural networks, enabling more efficient information extraction during training \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2011.11052)\]. Additionally, attention gates have been introduced to highlight salient regions in fMRI images, combining edge detection with attention mechanisms to improve segmentation accuracy and reduce the need for explicit tissue localization \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/1711.06636)\]. These attention-based approaches have demonstrated significant improvements in metrics such as Intersection over Union (IOU), achieving values as high as 0.78 \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/1711.06636)\]. Furthermore, dynamic scale attention mechanisms have been proposed to better integrate multi-scale features, enhancing the ability of models to capture both low-level details and high-level semantic information \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/2402.07008)\]. These methods have been evaluated on challenging datasets such as the BraTS 2020 challenge, where they achieved competitive results, including an average Dice Similarity Coefficient (DSC) of 0.8828 for whole tumor segmentation \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/2402.07008)\]. Overall, attention mechanisms continue to play a crucial role in advancing brain tumor segmentation by improving model efficiency, accuracy, and adaptability to complex medical imaging data.

Furthermore, combining machine-learned features from FCNs with hand-crafted features, such as texton-based features, has been explored to improve segmentation accuracy \[[30](http://arxiv.org/abs/1909.06337)\]. These approaches collectively highlight the importance of multi-modal data fusion in achieving reliable and accurate brain tumor segmentation.
