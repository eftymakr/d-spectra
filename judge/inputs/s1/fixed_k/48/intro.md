## Related Works

Neural retrieval methods, particularly those based on transformer-based pre-trained language models, have significantly advanced multilingual and cross-lingual retrieval capabilities \[[13](http://arxiv.org/abs/2108.06279)\]. These models leverage dense vector representations to capture semantic relationships between queries and documents, offering improvements over traditional sparse retrieval methods \[[14](http://arxiv.org/abs/2211.14876)\]. However, their effectiveness for low-resource, morphologically rich languages such as Amharic remains underexplored due to data scarcity and suboptimal tokenization \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2503.18570)\]. This gap is addressed by recent efforts to adapt retrieval models specifically for Amharic, leveraging pre-trained models like BERT and RoBERTa \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2503.18570)\]. 

Several studies have explored the challenges of dense retrieval for low-resource languages. For example, the work on Amharic named entity recognition highlights the difficulty of handling inflected structures and the need for specialized datasets and models \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/2207.00785)\]. Similarly, the development of the AmQA dataset demonstrates the lack of annotated resources for Amharic QA tasks, which is critical for training effective retrieval systems \[[20](http://arxiv.org/abs/2303.03290)\]. The study on Amharic speech recognition also underscores the importance of fine-tuning strategies and dataset composition for improving accuracy in low-resource settings \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/2503.18485)\]. 

Efforts to improve ASR for Amharic have focused on combining existing datasets with new data to enhance model performance \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/2503.18485)\]. The use of transfer learning and data augmentation techniques has shown promise in overcoming the limitations of small training data \[[26](http://arxiv.org/abs/2505.02463)\]. Additionally, the integration of multilingual BERT and other transformer-based models has enabled cross-lingual retrieval, though challenges persist in low-resource settings \[[17](http://arxiv.org/abs/2204.05814)\]. 

The development of dense retrieval models for Amharic has also been informed by studies on cross-lingual retrieval and the limitations of multilingual models. For instance, the work on the mDPR model highlights the challenges of achieving high performance for extremely low-resource languages like Amharic and Khmer \[[6](http://arxiv.org/abs/2408.11942)\]. The study on the Amharic LLaMA and LLaVA models further demonstrates the potential of fine-tuning large language models for low-resource languages, using data augmentation and translation techniques to expand training data \[[4](http://arxiv.org/abs/2403.06354)\]. 

In addition to dense retrieval, the field has seen advancements in other NLP tasks for Amharic, such as hate speech detection \[[10](http://arxiv.org/abs/2408.03849)\] and parts-of-speech tagging \[[16](http://arxiv.org/abs/2001.03324)\]. These tasks require specialized models and datasets, which are often not available for low-resource languages.
