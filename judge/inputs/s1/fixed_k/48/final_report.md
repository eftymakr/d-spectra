### <a id='category-dense-vs.-sparse-retrieval-methods'></a>Dense vs. Sparse Retrieval Methods: 7 papers found.
The category "Dense vs. Sparse Retrieval Methods" encompasses a range of approaches aimed at improving the effectiveness and efficiency of information retrieval systems. Dense retrieval methods leverage pre-trained language models to generate semantic embeddings for queries and documents, enabling more accurate semantic matching compared to traditional sparse retrieval techniques that rely on lexical signals \[[13](http://arxiv.org/abs/2108.06279)\]. These methods have been further refined into two main families: single representation models, such as ANCE, which use a single embedding (e.g., BERT's [CLS] token) to represent entire passages, and multiple representation models, such as ColBERT, which generate embeddings for each token in a passage \[[13](http://arxiv.org/abs/2108.06279)\]. While single representation models are more efficient in terms of response time and memory usage, multiple representation models have demonstrated superior performance in terms of MAP and MRR@10, particularly for challenging queries that are difficult for BM25 \[[13](http://arxiv.org/abs/2108.06279)\]. Additionally, recent studies have explored hybrid approaches, such as the 'Blended RAG' method, which combines semantic search techniques like dense vector indexes with sparse encoder indexes and hybrid query strategies to improve retrieval accuracy \[[26](http://arxiv.org/abs/2505.02463)\]. Furthermore, techniques like LADR have been introduced to enhance the efficiency of dense retrieval without compromising effectiveness, using lexical retrieval to seed a dense retrieval process that leverages document proximity graphs \[[15](http://arxiv.org/abs/2010.10469)\]. These advancements highlight the ongoing evolution of dense retrieval methods, emphasizing their potential to overcome limitations of traditional sparse retrieval while addressing challenges in low-resource settings through language-specific adaptations \[[27](http://arxiv.org/abs/2201.01614)\].
