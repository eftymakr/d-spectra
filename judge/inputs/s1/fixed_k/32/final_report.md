The importance of identifying critical layers for alignment has also been explored in the context of security. \[[7](http://arxiv.org/abs/2408.17003)\] introduces the concept of "safety layers" in aligned LLMs, which are crucial for distinguishing malicious queries from normal ones. These layers are identified through analysis of input vectors and parameter scaling, and their preservation during fine-tuning is essential for maintaining model security. This work builds on the broader understanding of how layer-specific changes affect model behavior.

In addition to alignment and security, the role of layer dynamics in fine-tuning has been studied in various domains. \[[17](http://arxiv.org/abs/2304.05216)\] investigates how different layers encode lexical, syntactic, and semantic properties of code, showing that the top two layers undergo the most significant changes during fine-tuning. Similarly, \[[19](http://arxiv.org/abs/2401.18028)\] explores how fine-tuning LLMs on diverse data sources can improve their ability to generate a wide range of impacts, suggesting that layer-level changes can influence the diversity and quality of outputs.

These findings underscore the importance of understanding how model activations encode behaviors and how they can be manipulated, which has implications for both security and broader applications requiring precise control over model outputs \[[15](http://arxiv.org/abs/2502.05945)\]. The proposed approaches, such as Safely Partial-Parameter Fine-Tuning (SPPFT), aim to preserve model security during fine-tuning by fixing gradients in safety layers, offering a balance between performance and robustness \[[7](http://arxiv.org/abs/2408.17003)\]. These efforts highlight the ongoing challenges and innovations in securing LLMs against both intentional and unintentional threats.
