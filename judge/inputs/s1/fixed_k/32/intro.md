## Related Works

Recent research has focused on understanding how large language models (LLMs) evolve during fine-tuning and how specific layers contribute to model behavior. Several studies have explored the significance of different layers in LLMs, particularly in the context of alignment and fine-tuning efficiency. For instance, \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2410.17875)\] investigates how alignment primarily affects a model's presentation style rather than its foundational knowledge, suggesting that only certain layers are significantly impacted. This aligns with findings that freezing non-essential layers improves overall model performance, while selectively tuning critical layers enhances fine-tuning efficiency \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2410.17875)\].

The importance of identifying critical layers for alignment has also been explored in the context of security. \[[7](http://arxiv.org/abs/2408.17003)\] introduces the concept of "safety layers" in aligned LLMs, which are crucial for distinguishing malicious queries from normal ones. These layers are identified through analysis of input vectors and parameter scaling, and their preservation during fine-tuning is essential for maintaining model security. This work builds on the broader understanding of how layer-specific changes affect model behavior.

In addition to alignment and security, the role of layer dynamics in fine-tuning has been studied in various domains. \[[17](http://arxiv.org/abs/2304.05216)\] investigates how different layers encode lexical, syntactic, and semantic properties of code, showing that the top two layers undergo the most significant changes during fine-tuning. This insight supports the idea that certain layers are more sensitive to fine-tuning updates and may play a key role in adapting models to new tasks. Similarly, \[[19](http://arxiv.org/abs/2401.18028)\] explores how fine-tuning LLMs on diverse data sources can improve their ability to generate a wide range of impacts, suggesting that layer-level changes can influence the diversity and quality of outputs.

Efforts to improve fine-tuning efficiency have also led to the development of parameter-efficient methods. \[[9](http://arxiv.org/abs/2406.01563)\] introduces LoFiT, a localized fine-tuning approach that modifies only a sparse subset of attention heads, achieving comparable performance to more resource-intensive methods with fewer parameters. This aligns with findings that selective layer or head-level tuning can significantly reduce computational costs while preserving model performance \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2410.17875)\].

Furthermore, the use of contrastive learning during fine-tuning has been explored to improve discriminative representation learning. \[[13](http://arxiv.org/abs/2102.06605)\] proposes Core-tuning, which applies contrastive loss during fine-tuning to reduce intra-class feature scattering and enhance model performance. This approach is particularly relevant for tasks requiring fine-grained discrimination, such as image classification and semantic segmentation.
