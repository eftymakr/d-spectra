## Related Works

Calibration of predictive models has been extensively studied in recent years, with a growing emphasis on aligning model confidence with prediction accuracy. Traditional calibration approaches often rely on predefined transformation functions, such as piece-wise linear functions, which are order-preserving but limited in expressiveness \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/2106.03228)\]. These methods are typically insufficient for complex calibration scenarios where the relationship between confidence and accuracy is non-linear \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/2301.04378)\]. Moreover, such fixed-form functions can lead to significant distortion of original predictions, especially in high-dimensional or complex prediction spaces \[[4](http://arxiv.org/abs/2406.08010)\].

Recent work has explored the use of neural networks for calibration, leveraging their flexibility and modeling power. For instance, the Unconstrained Monotonic Neural Network (UMNN) has been introduced as a universal approximator of continuous monotonic functions, enabling the modeling of diverse and complex calibration behaviors \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/1908.05164)\]. This architecture enforces monotonicity by ensuring the positiveness of the network output, which is crucial for maintaining the relative ordering of predictions while allowing for flexible calibration \[[5](http://arxiv.org/abs/2012.07923)\]. The UMNN has been successfully applied in distributional reinforcement learning, where it enables efficient decoupling of function approximators from probability metrics \[[6](http://arxiv.org/abs/2106.07998)\].

Another line of research focuses on improving the calibration of deep neural networks through novel loss functions. The Smooth Calibration Loss (SCLoss) has been proposed as a method to optimize calibration by enforcing a necessary condition for achieving ideal calibration \[[7](http://arxiv.org/abs/1802.07595)\]. This loss function is designed to work alongside flexible neural networks, such as the UMNN, to ensure that the calibration process is both effective and efficient \[[8](http://arxiv.org/abs/2402.07307)\]. The use of such loss functions has been shown to improve calibration performance in various domains, including image classification and regression tasks \[[9](http://arxiv.org/abs/1911.00104)\].

In addition to loss function design, recent studies have investigated the integration of calibration into ranking systems. The Self-Boosted framework for Calibrated Ranking (SBCR) introduces a calibration module that decouples point loss and ranking loss, allowing for a more optimal trade-off between calibration and ranking accuracy \[[10](http://arxiv.org/abs/2303.10761)\]. This approach has been successfully deployed in large-scale online systems, demonstrating its effectiveness in real-world applications \[[11](http://arxiv.org/abs/2101.04356)\].

The importance of calibration in deep learning models has also been highlighted in the context of large language models (LLMs), where the alignment between model confidence and prediction accuracy is critical for reliable deployment \[[12](http://arxiv.org/abs/2305.18764)\]. Studies have shown that incorporating response agreement and appropriate loss functions can significantly improve calibration performance \[[13](http://arxiv.org/abs/2203.09278)\]. Furthermore, the use of focal loss and AUC surrogate losses has been explored as a means to enhance calibration in LLMs

### <a id='category-monotonic-neural-networks-(umnn)'></a>Monotonic Neural Networks (UMNN): 2 papers found.
Monotonic neural networks, specifically the Unconstrained Monotonic Neural Network (UMNN) architecture, have been explored for their ability to model continuous monotonic functions, which is crucial for tasks requiring invertible transformations and accurate probability modeling \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/1908.05164)\]. Unlike traditional approaches that enforce monotonicity through weight and activation constraints, UMNNs ensure monotonicity by requiring the positivity of the network's output, allowing for greater flexibility and expressiveness \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/1908.05164)\]. This property has been leveraged in autoregressive flows, such as UMNN-MAF, where UMNNs serve as invertible building blocks for density estimation, demonstrating their effectiveness in capturing complex probability distributions \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/1908.05164)\]. Additionally, UMNNs have been applied in distributional reinforcement learning, where they enable the modeling of different representations of the random return distribution, such as probability density functions (PDF), cumulative distribution functions (CDF), and quantile functions (QF) \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/2106.03228)\]. This capability allows for the efficient decoupling of function approximator design from probability metrics, leading to more flexible and accurate learning of return distributions \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/2106.03228)\]. The use of UMNNs in these contexts highlights their potential to improve both density estimation and reinforcement learning by providing a universal approximator for monotonic functions while maintaining the necessary constraints for invertibility and stability.

[Snippet]: «Scale-calibrated ranking systems are ubiquitous in real-world applications nowadays, which pursue accurate ranking quality and calibrated probabilistic predictions simultaneously. For instance, in the advertising ranking system, the predicted click-through rate (CTR) is utilized for ranking and required to be calibrated for the downstream cost-per-click ads bidding. Recently, multi-objective based methods have been wildly adopted as a standard approach for Calibrated Ranking, which incorporates the combination of two loss functions: a pointwise loss that focuses on calibrated absolute values and a ranking loss that emphasizes relative orderings. However, when applied to industrial online applications, existing multi, objective CR approaches still suffer from two crucial limitations First, previous methods need to aggregate the full candidate list within a single mini-batch to compute the ranking loss. Such aggregation strategy violates extensive data shuffling which has long been proven beneficial for preventing overfitting, and thus degrades the training effectiveness. Second, existing multi-objective methods apply the two inherently conflicting loss functions on a single probabilistic prediction, which results in a sub-optimal trade-off between calibration and ranking. To tackle the two limitations, we propose a Self-Boosted framework for Calibrated Ranking (SBCR). In SBCR, the predicted ranking scores by the online deployed model are dumped into context features. With these additional context features, each single item can perceive the overall distribution of scores in the whole ranking list, so that the ranking loss can be constructed without the need for sample aggregation. As the deployed model is a few versions older than the training model, the dumped predictions reveal what was failed to learn and keep boosting the model to correct previously mis-predicted items. Moreover, a calibration module is introduced to decouple the point loss and ranking loss. The two losses are applied before and after the calibration module separately, which elegantly addresses the sub-optimal trade-off problem. We conduct comprehensive experiments on industrial scale datasets and online A/B tests, demonstrating that SBCR can achieve advanced performance on both calibration and ranking. Our method has been deployed on the video search system of Kuaishou, and results in significant performance improvements on CTR and the total amount of time users spend on Kuaishou.»
