Diffusion transformers (DiT) have emerged as a powerful architecture for image and video generation, outperforming traditional diffusion models such as U-Net-based architectures \[[9](http://arxiv.org/abs/2307.01831)\]. However, their large parameter count and computational complexity hinder deployment on edge devices, motivating research into efficient quantization techniques \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2406.02540)\]. Early work on diffusion model quantization, such as Q-Diffusion \[[8](http://arxiv.org/abs/2302.04304)\], identified challenges in maintaining performance during quantization due to the multi-timestep pipeline and bimodal activation distributions. These issues were further explored in Q-DiT \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/2406.17343)\], which introduced methods to handle spatial and temporal variance in DiT models. Q-DiT demonstrated that automatic quantization granularity allocation and sample-wise dynamic activation quantization could reduce FID by 1.09 when quantizing DiT-XL/2 to W6A8 on ImageNet \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/2406.17343)\].

Recent efforts have focused on video-specific quantization challenges, such as the skewness of temporal features and inter-channel disparities in activation distributions \[[7](http://arxiv.org/abs/2407.11585)\]. ### <a id='category-quantization-techniques-for-image-generation'></a>Quantization Techniques for Image Generation: 6 papers found.
Diffusion transformers (DiT) have shown significant advancements in image and video generation, but their large parameter counts and computational complexity hinder deployment on edge devices \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2406.02540)\]. To address these challenges, various quantization techniques have been proposed to reduce memory usage and accelerate inference. Post-Training Quantization (PTQ) is a promising approach, but existing methods often fail to generalize to DiT models due to issues like biased quantization and performance degradation \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/2406.17343)\]. To overcome these limitations, researchers have introduced specialized quantization frameworks tailored for DiT. For example, Q-DiT integrates automatic quantization granularity allocation and sample-wise dynamic activation quantization to handle spatial and temporal variances in DiT models, achieving notable improvements in FID scores \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/2406.17343)\]. Similarly, ViDiT-Q is designed specifically for DiT models, achieving W8A8 and W4A8 quantization with minimal visual quality degradation \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2406.02540)\]. Multi-region quantization (MRQ) and time-grouping quantization (TGQ) have also been proposed to address asymmetric distribution and temporal variation in activations, respectively, with MRQ achieving a 0.29 increase in FID at W8A8 \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/2505.18663)\]. Additionally, TaQ-DiT introduces joint reconstruction and time-variance-aware transformations to improve quantization accuracy, surpassing previous methods when quantizing to W4A8 \[[4](http://arxiv.org/abs/2502.04056)\]. The Hybrid Floating-point Quantization for DiT (HQ-DiT) further enhances efficiency by using 4-bit floating-point precision on both weights and activations, achieving minimal performance impact \[[5](http://arxiv.org/abs/2411.14172)\]. Lastly, a novel PTQ method tailored for diffusion models' multi-timestep pipeline has been developed, enabling 4-bit quantization with a small FID change of at most 2.34 \[[6](http://arxiv.org/abs/2405.19751)\]. These approaches collectively highlight the ongoing efforts to develop effective quantization strategies for DiT models, balancing efficiency with performance.

### <a id='category-temporal-maintenance-distillation'></a>Temporal Maintenance Distillation: 2 papers found.
Temporal Maintenance Distillation (TMD) has emerged as a critical approach for preserving spatiotemporal correlations in video generation models, addressing the challenges of quantization and optimization in video diffusion transformers \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2406.02540)\]. Unlike traditional image-based distillation methods, TMD directly optimizes synthetic videos with respect to a pre-trained model, ensuring that the distillation process maintains temporal dynamics and reduces redundancy \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2406.02540)\]. This is achieved through mechanisms such as temporal saliency-guided filtering, which leverages inter-frame differences to retain informative temporal cues while suppressing frame-level redundancy \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2406.02540)\]. Additionally, TMD introduces a unified framework that disentangles dynamic and static information in videos, first distilling them into still images as static memory and then compensating for motion information using a learnable dynamic memory block \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/2406.17343)\]. These approaches are essential for improving the performance of video generation models under quantization constraints, as they ensure that the unique temporal characteristics of video data are preserved during compression and optimization \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2406.02540)\]\[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/2406.17343)\].
