## Related Works

Diffusion transformers (DiT) have emerged as a powerful architecture for image and video generation, outperforming traditional diffusion models such as U-Net-based architectures \[[9](http://arxiv.org/abs/2307.01831)\]. However, their large parameter count and computational complexity hinder deployment on edge devices, motivating research into efficient quantization techniques \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2406.02540)\]. Early work on diffusion model quantization, such as Q-Diffusion \[[8](http://arxiv.org/abs/2302.04304)\], identified challenges in maintaining performance during quantization due to the multi-timestep pipeline and bimodal activation distributions. These issues were further explored in Q-DiT \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/2406.17343)\], which introduced methods to handle spatial and temporal variance in DiT models. Q-DiT demonstrated that automatic quantization granularity allocation and sample-wise dynamic activation quantization could reduce FID by 1.09 when quantizing DiT-XL/2 to W6A8 on ImageNet \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/2406.17343)\].

Recent efforts have focused on video-specific quantization challenges, such as the skewness of temporal features and inter-channel disparities in activation distributions \[[7](http://arxiv.org/abs/2407.11585)\]. QVD \[[7](http://arxiv.org/abs/2407.11585)\] was the first PTQ strategy tailored for video diffusion models, introducing High Temporal Discriminability Quantization (HTDQ) and Scattered Channel Range Integration (SCRI) to improve quantization coverage and temporal guidance. This work achieved near-lossless performance degradation on W8A8, outperforming existing methods by 205.12 in FVD \[[7](http://arxiv.org/abs/2407.11585)\]. Similarly, DVD-Quant \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/2505.18663)\] proposed a data-free quantization framework for Video DiTs, achieving a 2× speedup while maintaining visual fidelity, and enabling W4A4 PTQ without quality loss \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/2505.18663)\].

Efforts to improve quantization efficiency have also included time-aware approaches. TQ-DiT \[[5](http://arxiv.org/abs/2411.14172)\] addressed the issue of non-convergence during reconstruction by introducing a joint reconstruction method and time-variance-aware static transformations for Post-GELU activations. This led to significant performance improvements at W4A8, surpassing previous quantization methods \[[5](http://arxiv.org/abs/2411.14172)\]. Similarly, TaQ-DiT \[[5](http://arxiv.org/abs/2411.14172)\] introduced joint reconstruction and time-variance-aware transformations, achieving superior results when quantizing DiTs to W4A8 \[[5](http://arxiv.org/abs/2411.14172)\].

In addition to quantization, other approaches have focused on improving the efficiency of diffusion models for video generation. For instance, DSV \[[14](http://arxiv.org/abs/2502.07590)\] leveraged dynamic attention sparsity to accelerate video DiT training, achieving up to 3.02× higher training throughput while maintaining model quality \[[14](http://arxiv.org/abs/2502.07590)\].
