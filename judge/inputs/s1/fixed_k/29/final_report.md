## Related Works

Maximum Inner Product Search (MIPS) has been extensively studied in recent years due to its critical role in machine learning and information retrieval, especially in high-dimensional settings \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2503.06882)\]. Early approaches to MIPS either relied solely on inner product similarity, which can lead to local optima and redundant computations \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2503.06882)\], or reduced the problem to Euclidean Nearest Neighbor Search (NNS) through space projection, which often results in topology distortion and information loss \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2503.06882)\]. This paper argues that there is no inherent binary opposition between inner product and Euclidean metrics and that combining both can significantly enhance MIPS performance \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2503.06882)\].

Several works have explored approximate MIPS using graph-based methods. The inner-product navigable small world graph (ip-NSW) represents a state-of-the-art method for approximate MIPS, achieving an order of magnitude speedup over existing baselines \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/1909.13459)\]. However, ip-NSW suffers from a strong norm bias, where large-norm items dominate the search process, potentially missing relevant smaller-norm vectors \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/1909.13459)\]. To address this, ip-NSW+ was proposed, which introduces an angular proximity graph to improve search robustness across different data distributions \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/1909.13459)\].

Another approach, BanditMIPS, presents a randomized algorithm with complexity independent of the dimensionality, offering significant speedups in high-dimensional settings \[[4](http://arxiv.org/abs/2212.07551)\]. It uses adaptive sampling inspired by multi-armed bandits to estimate inner products efficiently \[[4](http://arxiv.org/abs/2212.07551)\]. Similarly, the work by \[[12](http://arxiv.org/abs/1812.06360)\] introduces a bandit-based approach that allows users to control the suboptimality of results with theoretical guarantees, without requiring any preprocessing \[[12](http://arxiv.org/abs/1812.06360)\].

Locality Sensitive Hashing (LSH) has also been widely used for approximate MIPS \[[11](http://arxiv.org/abs/1405.5869)\]. However, the original LSH framework was found insufficient for solving MIPS, prompting the development of Asymmetric LSH (ALSH), which converts the problem into approximate near neighbor search \[[11](http://arxiv.org/abs/1405.5869)\]. NORM-RANGING LSH further improves upon this by addressing the normalization issue in real datasets, leading to lower query time complexity \[[17](http://arxiv.org/abs/1809.08782)\].

Clustering-based approaches, such as spherical $k$-means, have also been explored for approximate MIPS \[[10](http://arxiv.org/abs/1507.05910)\]. These methods partition vectors into clusters and only search a subset of clusters during retrieval, reducing computational overhead \[[10](http://arxiv.org/abs/1507.05910)\]. The work by \[[18](http://arxiv.org/abs/2301.10622)\] extends this to streaming sparse vectors, proposing an algorithm called Sinnamon that efficiently retrieves top-k results while trading off memory, latency, and accuracy \[[18](http://arxiv.org/abs/2301.10622)\].

Recent work introduces novel frameworks that equate MIPS with NNS without requiring space transformation, enabling the use of advanced graph-based indices and edge pruning strategies to reduce redundant computations \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2503.06882)\]. ### <a id='category-mips-problem-definition-and-challenges'></a>MIPS Problem Definition and Challenges: 7 papers found.
Maximum Inner Product Search (MIPS) has been a focal point in machine learning and information retrieval, particularly for high-dimensional data applications \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2503.06882)\]. Existing approaches often rely solely on inner product (IP) similarity, which can lead to local optima and redundant computations \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2503.06882)\], or reduce MIPS to nearest neighbor search under the Euclidean metric, causing topology distortion and information loss \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2503.06882)\]. However, recent works challenge this dichotomy, arguing that IP and Euclidean metrics are not inherently opposed and that integrating both can enhance MIPS performance \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2503.06882)\]. For instance, some studies propose budgeted MIPS settings, allowing control over the trade-off between search efficiency and quality \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/1610.03317)\]. Others explore sampling-based methods like wedge and diamond sampling, which offer efficiency but may suffer from linear costs and performance degradation with negative inputs \[[6](http://arxiv.org/abs/1908.08656)\]. Additionally, diversity-aware approaches have been introduced to balance relevance and diversity in search results, leveraging techniques such as Maximal Marginal Relevance (MMR) \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/1909.13459)\]. Furthermore, methods like c-Approximate Maximum Inner Product (c-AMIP) search have been developed to improve efficiency by relaxing accuracy guarantees and using lightweight indexing structures \[[4](http://arxiv.org/abs/2212.07551)\]. Another line of work addresses reverse MIPS, where the goal is to find user vectors with maximum inner product relative to a query vector, using efficient indexing strategies \[[5](http://arxiv.org/abs/2309.09013)\]. These diverse approaches highlight the evolving landscape of MIPS research, emphasizing the need for flexible, adaptive, and efficient solutions tailored to specific application needs.

The category "Bandit-Based Approximate MIPS Methods" focuses on developing efficient and accurate algorithms for Maximum Inner Product Search (MIPS) in high-dimensional settings. Another study presents an algorithm that eliminates the need for preprocessing and enables users to control the suboptimality of approximate results with theoretical guarantees, framing MIPS as a Best Arm Identification problem \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/1610.03317)\]. These approaches highlight the growing interest in bandit-based methods for MIPS, emphasizing their potential to enhance performance while maintaining flexibility and adaptability in real-world applications.

The use of dynamic pruning, inspired by the clustering paradigm, also enables the development of novel indexing structures that improve retrieval performance for general sparse vectors \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2503.06882)\]. ### <a id='category-locality-sensitive-hashing-(lsh)-variants'></a>Locality Sensitive Hashing (LSH) Variants: 4 papers found.
The category "Locality Sensitive Hashing (LSH) Variants" focuses on advancements in hashing algorithms for efficient Maximum Inner Product Search (MIPS), a critical task in high-dimensional data applications. Early approaches to MIPS faced challenges due to the inherent difficulty of directly applying standard LSH frameworks, as they were not designed for inner product similarity \[[11](http://arxiv.org/abs/1405.5869)\]. To address this, asymmetric hashing schemes were introduced, transforming MIPS into an approximate near neighbor search problem, enabling sublinear time algorithms \[[14](http://arxiv.org/abs/1410.5410)\]. These methods leverage mathematical insights to convert inner products into problems solvable with hashing, offering both theoretical guarantees and practical efficiency \[[11](http://arxiv.org/abs/1405.5869)\]. However, real-world datasets often exhibit long-tailed 2-norm distributions, which can degrade performance in existing methods like SIMPLE-LSH \[[17](http://arxiv.org/abs/1809.08782)\]. To mitigate this, NORM-RANGING LSH was proposed, partitioning datasets into sub-datasets to reduce normalization issues and improve query efficiency \[[17](http://arxiv.org/abs/1809.08782)\]. Additionally, the RkMIPS problem, which involves finding top-k user vectors with high inner product scores, was tackled with novel hashing schemes such as Shifting-aware Asymmetric Hashing (SAH), which introduces shifting-invariant transformations and blocking strategies for faster computation \[[22](http://arxiv.org/abs/2211.12751)\]. These developments highlight the evolving landscape of LSH variants, emphasizing asymmetric transformations, dataset partitioning, and hybrid approaches to enhance MIPS performance while maintaining theoretical rigor.
