## Related Works

Neural retrieval models have shown significant promise in web search tasks, particularly when trained on large-scale document collections like ClueWeb22 \[[14](http://arxiv.org/abs/2204.05862)\]. However, the training of these models often requires substantial amounts of labeled query-document pairs, which are costly to obtain \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2305.18290)\]. Recent advances in large language models have enabled the generation of synthetic queries, offering a scalable alternative to manual labeling \[[14](http://arxiv.org/abs/2204.05862)\]. Despite this, synthetic queries often vary in quality, leading to suboptimal downstream retrieval performance \[[14](http://arxiv.org/abs/2204.05862)\]. Existing methods typically filter out noisy query-document pairs based on signals from an external re-ranker \[[14](http://arxiv.org/abs/2204.05862)\]. In contrast, we propose a framework that leverages Direct Preference Optimization (DPO) to integrate ranking signals into the query generation process, aiming to directly optimize the model towards generating high-quality queries that maximize downstream retrieval effectiveness \[[14](http://arxiv.org/abs/2204.05862)\].

DPO is a novel parameterization of the reward model in reinforcement learning from human feedback (RLHF), enabling the extraction of the corresponding optimal policy in closed form \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2305.18290)\]. This allows solving the standard RLHF problem with only a simple classification loss, resulting in a stable, performant, and computationally lightweight algorithm \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2305.18290)\]. DPO has been shown to fine-tune large-scale language models to align with human preferences as well as or better than existing methods \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2305.18290)\]. Notably, it outperforms PPO-based RLHF in controlling the sentiment of generations and matches or improves response quality in summarization and single-turn dialogue \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2305.18290)\].

In the context of information retrieval, several works have explored the use of deep neural networks for ranking and retrieval tasks. For instance, the Deep Relevance Matching Model (DRMM) was proposed for ad-hoc retrieval, addressing the challenge of relevance matching by incorporating exact matching signals, query term importance, and diverse matching requirements \[[6](http://arxiv.org/abs/1711.08611)\]. DRMM employs a joint deep architecture at the query term level, using matching histogram mapping, a feed forward matching network, and a term gating network to effectively handle these factors \[[6](http://arxiv.org/abs/1711.08611)\]. Experimental results on benchmark collections showed that DRMM significantly outperformed existing retrieval models and deep matching models \[[6](http://arxiv.org/abs/1711.08611)\].

Similarly, the Neural Corpus Indexer (NCI) aims to unify training and indexing stages in document retrieval, improving recall performance through a prefix-aware weight-adaptive decoder architecture \[[11](http://arxiv.org/abs/2206.02743)\]. NCI leverages techniques such as query generation, semantic document identifiers, and consistency-based regularization to achieve superior performance on academic benchmarks \[[11](http://arxiv.org/abs/2206.02743)\].

### <a id='category-direct-preference-optimization-(dpo)-framework'></a>Direct Preference Optimization (DPO) Framework: 1 papers found.
Answer:  
The Direct Preference Optimization (DPO) framework represents a significant advancement in aligning large language models with human preferences by simplifying the fine-tuning process. Unlike traditional methods that rely on complex and unstable procedures such as reinforcement learning from human feedback (RLHF), which involves training a reward model and then using reinforcement learning to maximize this reward, DPO introduces a new parameterization of the reward model that allows for the extraction of the optimal policy in closed form \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2305.18290)\]. This approach eliminates the need for sampling from the large model during fine-tuning and reduces the reliance on extensive hyperparameter tuning, making the process more stable and computationally efficient \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2305.18290)\]. Experimental results demonstrate that DPO achieves performance comparable to or better than existing methods, particularly in controlling the sentiment of generated text and improving response quality in tasks such as summarization and single-turn dialogue \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2305.18290)\]. By directly optimizing the model to generate high-quality queries that maximize downstream retrieval effectiveness, DPO offers a more integrated and efficient solution for tasks requiring both query generation and retrieval performance \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2305.18290)\].

### <a id='category-neural-corpus-indexer-(nci)-for-document-retrieval'></a>Neural Corpus Indexer (NCI) for Document Retrieval: 1 papers found.
Answer:  
The Neural Corpus Indexer (NCI) represents a significant advancement in document retrieval by integrating training and indexing stages within a single end-to-end deep neural network \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2305.18290)\]. Unlike traditional index-retrieve paradigms, which are difficult to optimize for final retrieval targets, NCI employs a sequence-to-sequence architecture to directly generate relevant document identifiers for a given query \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2305.18290)\]. This approach is supported by a prefix-aware weight-adaptive decoder, which enhances recall performance through tailored techniques such as query generation, semantic document identifiers, and consistency-based regularization \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2305.18290)\]. Empirical results on academic benchmarks demonstrate that NCI outperforms existing methods, achieving notable improvements in Recall@1 and R-Precision metrics \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2305.18290)\]. These findings align with broader trends in neural retrieval, where models are increasingly designed to address the limitations of synthetic training data and improve downstream performance through more sophisticated integration of ranking signals \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/1707.06347)\].
