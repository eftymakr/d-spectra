## Related Works

Diffusion models have become a cornerstone in generative modeling, particularly for image and video generation, due to their ability to produce high-quality, diverse outputs \[[15](http://arxiv.org/abs/2106.08295)\]. However, their computational and memory demands hinder their deployment on resource-constrained devices \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2302.04304)\]. To address this, post-training quantization (PTQ) has emerged as a promising technique to reduce model size and accelerate inference without retraining \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/2211.15736)\]. PTQ has been successfully applied to diffusion models, with methods such as Q-Diffusion \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2302.04304)\], PTQ4DM \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/2211.15736)\], and PTQD \[[4](http://arxiv.org/abs/2305.10657)\] achieving 4-bit and 8-bit quantization while maintaining performance. These methods address the unique challenges of diffusion models, such as the changing output distributions over time steps and bimodal activation patterns \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2302.04304)\]. 

Despite these advances, quantization of diffusion transformers (DiTs) remains a non-trivial task. DiTs, which replace traditional U-Net architectures with transformers, offer scalability and flexibility but come with high parameter counts and computational costs \[[8](http://arxiv.org/abs/2212.09748)\]. Existing PTQ methods, such as PTQ4DiT \[[5](http://arxiv.org/abs/2405.16005)\], Q-DiT \[[9](http://arxiv.org/abs/2406.17343)\], and HQ-DiT \[[22](http://arxiv.org/abs/2405.19751)\], have made strides in quantizing DiTs to 4-bit and 8-bit precision. These approaches tackle challenges like spatial and temporal variance in activations and weights, using techniques such as channel-wise salience balancing and dynamic activation quantization. For example, PTQ4DiT \[[5](http://arxiv.org/abs/2405.16005)\] introduces Channel-wise Salience Balancing (CSB) and Spearmen’s ρ-guided Salience Calibration (SSC) to improve quantization accuracy, while Q-DiT \[[9](http://arxiv.org/abs/2406.17343)\] integrates automatic quantization granularity allocation and sample-wise dynamic activation quantization. 

In the realm of video generation, diffusion models face additional challenges due to the temporal coherence required in video sequences \[[11](http://arxiv.org/abs/2204.03458)\]. Video diffusion models, such as Stable Video Diffusion \[[17](http://arxiv.org/abs/2311.15127)\] and VideoLDM \[[18](http://arxiv.org/abs/2304.08818)\], have been developed to generate high-resolution, temporally consistent videos. However, these models still suffer from high computational demands, making quantization essential for deployment on edge devices. Recent work on video diffusion models has explored quantization techniques, but they often lack the specificity needed for video tasks. For instance, PTQD \[[4](http://arxiv.org/abs/2305.10657)\] and Temporal Dynamic Quantization \[[10](http://arxiv.org/abs/2306.02316)\] have been proposed to address the temporal variation in activation, improving output quality by dynamically adjusting quantization intervals based on time steps.
