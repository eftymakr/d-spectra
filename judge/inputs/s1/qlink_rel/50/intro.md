## Related Works

The integration of knowledge graphs (KGs) with pre-trained language models (PLMs) has emerged as a critical research direction in natural language processing (NLP). Early work focused on improving link prediction in KGs using embedding-based approaches such as TransE \[[10](http://arxiv.org/abs/1412.6575)\], which models relations as translations between entities in a semantic space. However, these models often fail to capture complex relational patterns \[[11](http://arxiv.org/abs/1902.10197)\]. Subsequent models like TransH \[[10](http://arxiv.org/abs/1412.6575)\] and TransR \[[7](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/994afdf0db0cb0456f4f76468380822c2f532726)\] attempted to address this by introducing separate spaces for entities and relations, allowing for more nuanced modeling of entity relationships. These approaches laid the foundation for more sophisticated embedding techniques, such as DistMult \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/1703.06103)\], which was later improved by R-GCNs \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/1703.06103)\] through graph-based message passing. R-GCNs demonstrated significant improvements in link prediction tasks, achieving a 29.8% improvement on the FB15k-237 dataset over decoder-only baselines \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/1703.06103)\].

The limitations of traditional KG embedding methods have also been addressed through the development of convolutional models such as ConvE \[[6](http://arxiv.org/abs/1707.01476)\], which outperformed existing models in terms of parameter efficiency and performance on standard benchmarks. ConvE's ability to model high-indegree nodes effectively made it particularly suitable for large-scale knowledge graphs like Freebase and YAGO3 \[[6](http://arxiv.org/abs/1707.01476)\]. These advancements in KG embedding have been complemented by the rise of pre-trained language models (PLMs), which excel at capturing contextual and semantic information from unstructured text. However, PLMs often struggle with factual accuracy, as they are black-box models that lack structured knowledge \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2306.08302)\].

To bridge this gap, researchers have proposed unified models that integrate PLMs with KGs. KEPLER \[[4](http://arxiv.org/abs/1911.06136)\], for instance, combines knowledge embedding with pre-trained language representation, allowing for better integration of factual knowledge into PLMs. This model uses PLMs to encode textual entity descriptions and jointly optimizes knowledge embedding and language modeling objectives, achieving state-of-the-art results on various NLP tasks. Similarly, the QA-GNN model \[[5](http://arxiv.org/abs/2104.06378)\] leverages both LMs and KGs for question answering, using relevance scoring and joint reasoning to improve performance on tasks like CommonsenseQA and OpenBookQA.

Conceptual frameworks such as the unification of LLMs and KGs \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2306.08302)\] have also been proposed to enable bidirectional reasoning, where both models complement each other to enhance understanding and inference.
