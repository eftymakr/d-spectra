## Related Works

Anomaly detection in industrial settings has garnered significant attention due to its critical role in quality control and defect identification. A wide range of methods have been proposed to address the challenges of unsupervised and one-class learning scenarios, where only normal data is available during training \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/2011.08785)\]. Early approaches relied on handcrafted features and classical computer vision techniques, but recent advances have leveraged deep learning to improve detection accuracy and localization capabilities \[[7](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/3aa681914a7da79f7d7293f51a058eefe61c8bb7)\].

Several methods have focused on improving the efficiency of anomaly detection models to enable deployment on resource-constrained embedded devices. For instance, PatchCore \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/2011.08785)\] introduced a memory bank of representative patches to enhance detection performance while maintaining competitive inference times. Similarly, CFLOW-AD \[[4](http://arxiv.org/abs/2107.12571)\] proposed a real-time model based on conditional normalizing flows, achieving a 10× reduction in computational complexity compared to prior state-of-the-art methods. These approaches highlight the importance of balancing performance with computational efficiency.

Another line of research has explored the use of generative models for anomaly detection, such as convolutional autoencoders and generative adversarial networks (GANs) \[[7](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/3aa681914a7da79f7d7293f51a058eefe61c8bb7)\]. However, these models often struggle with reconstructing anomalies accurately, leading to reduced detection performance. To address this, methods like RIAD \[[14](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/2c89b183df320c3ef698989bdc5d1d4731c4d65d)\] have been introduced, which cast anomaly detection as a self-supervised reconstruction-by-inpainting problem, improving detection capabilities by focusing on partial image reconstruction.

The MVTec AD dataset \[[7](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/3aa681914a7da79f7d7293f51a058eefe61c8bb7)\] has become a standard benchmark for evaluating anomaly detection algorithms, offering a comprehensive collection of real-world anomalies such as scratches, dents, and contaminations. It provides pixel-precise ground truth annotations, enabling precise evaluation of both detection and localization performance. Similarly, the MVTec 3D-AD dataset \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/2112.09045)\] extends this work to 3D data, addressing the challenges of geometric anomalies in industrial inspection.

Recent advances have also focused on multi-class and multi-modal anomaly detection. For example, UniAD \[[9](http://arxiv.org/abs/2206.03687)\] proposed a unified framework for detecting anomalies across multiple classes, overcoming the limitations of training separate models for each object category. The method incorporates improvements such as layer-wise query decoding and neighbor masked attention to prevent shortcut learning. Additionally, M3DM \[[10](http://arxiv.org/abs/2303.00601)\] introduced a hybrid fusion approach for multimodal anomaly detection, combining 3D point clouds and RGB images to improve detection precision.

Several works have also explored self-supervised learning for anomaly detection, such as CutPaste \[[11](http://arxiv.org/abs/2104.04015)\], which uses data augmentation strategies to learn robust representations without requiring anomalous data.
