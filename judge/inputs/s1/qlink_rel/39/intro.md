## Related Works

De novo genome assembly has been a focal point of research in bioinformatics, driven by the advent of next-generation sequencing (NGS) technologies \[[9](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/d206ac8572f7480e5d1e92620060a39a8c3fd3ce)\]. These technologies, such as Illumina and Solexa, generate massive volumes of short reads, necessitating efficient and accurate assembly methods \[[1](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/7279acd9c61a8de019ea2d55d23124dfbb6b5269)\]. Early approaches relied on overlap-layout-consensus (OLC) methods or de Bruijn graph-based algorithms, each with distinct trade-offs in terms of computational efficiency and accuracy \[[1](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/7279acd9c61a8de019ea2d55d23124dfbb6b5269)\]. For instance, string-based assemblers were found to be superior in software implementation, while graph-based assemblers like SOAPdenovo were complex to configure \[[1](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/7279acd9c61a8de019ea2d55d23124dfbb6b5269)\]. The challenge of assembling large genomes from short reads has been addressed by tools like SHARCGS, which demonstrated high accuracy and speed in assembling short-read data \[[11](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/fbd59e17f72c3aedb3cd06af2e50a91526ccd81a)\]. Similarly, SSAKE was developed to handle millions of short sequences by leveraging prefix trees for overlap detection \[[13](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/dbb41f80dc43d139761a863eb7d6da773a726261)\].

Long-read technologies, such as PacBio and Oxford Nanopore, have further transformed de novo assembly by enabling the reconstruction of reference-quality genomes \[[3](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/2b59fd18ada769ef76fb7fcc7382f063791abfab)\]. However, these technologies come with high error rates, which complicate the assembly of complex repeats and haplotypes \[[3](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/2b59fd18ada769ef76fb7fcc7382f063791abfab)\]. Canu, a successor to Celera Assembler, was specifically designed to handle noisy single-molecule sequences by introducing adaptive overlapping strategies and sparse graph construction to avoid collapsing diverged repeats \[[3](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/2b59fd18ada769ef76fb7fcc7382f063791abfab)\]. Similarly, BELLA was developed to compute overlaps for long reads using a probabilistic model and reliable k-mers to reduce spurious alignments \[[10](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/f27decfab0389e2003e7ca66260b34eb3c1b8827)\].

The computational challenges of de novo assembly, including memory constraints and irregular data patterns, have prompted the development of parallel and distributed assemblers \[[14](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/fe8d86bc5520b5049104ff58ee7e980982816ebf)\]. SWAP-Assembler, for example, scales to thousands of cores and achieves high-quality assemblies with good N50 sizes and low error rates \[[14](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/fe8d86bc5520b5049104ff58ee7e980982816ebf)\]. Similarly, the diBELLA 2D pipeline introduced distributed-memory parallel algorithms for overlap detection and layout simplification, achieving near-linear scaling and significant runtime reductions \[[15](http://arxiv.org/abs/2010.10055)\]. These advancements highlight the ongoing efforts to improve scalability and performance in genome assembly.

Despite these advances, the field still faces challenges in terms of computational efficiency and memory usage. The Assemblathon 2 competition evaluated multiple assemblers across three vertebrate species, revealing significant variability in performance and output quality \[[16](http://arxiv.org/abs/1301.5406)\]. This underscores the need for more robust and adaptable assembly methods that can handle the complexity of different genome types and sequencing technologies \[[16](http://arxiv.org/abs/1301.5406)\].
