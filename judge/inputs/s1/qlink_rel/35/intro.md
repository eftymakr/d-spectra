## Related Works

Protein dynamics and conformational sampling are essential for understanding biological function and have traditionally relied on computationally expensive molecular dynamics (MD) simulations \[[1](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/21a4afa74caf78e28880b7fe4c30a09a5f9c14e3)\]. These simulations, while accurate, are often infeasible for large systems due to their high computational cost \[[1](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/21a4afa74caf78e28880b7fe4c30a09a5f9c14e3)\]. Recent advances in generative modeling, particularly denoising diffusion models, have enabled efficient and accurate protein structure prediction and conformation sampling by learning distributions over crystallographic structures \[[1](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/21a4afa74caf78e28880b7fe4c30a09a5f9c14e3)\]. However, integrating physical supervision into these data-driven approaches remains challenging, as standard energy-based objectives often lead to intractable optimization \[[1](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/21a4afa74caf78e28880b7fe4c30a09a5f9c14e3)\].

Several studies have explored the use of machine learning for protein structure prediction and conformational sampling. For instance, AlphaFold has demonstrated the ability to predict protein structures with atomic accuracy even in cases where no similar structure is known \[[2](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/dc32a984b651256a8ec282be52310e6bd33d9815)\]. Similarly, Boltzmann generators have been developed to sample equilibrium states of many-body systems using deep learning and statistical mechanics \[[5](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/2eb1ed9d4baf548391d8220c0a8a3d0be79845ed)\]. These models learn to generate unbiased equilibrium samples of representative condensed-matter systems and proteins, overcoming rare event sampling problems \[[5](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/2eb1ed9d4baf548391d8220c0a8a3d0be79845ed)\].

Diffusion models have also been applied to protein conformation generation. EigenFold, for example, is a diffusion-based generative model that samples a distribution of structures from a given protein sequence, achieving a median TMScore of 0.84 \[[4](http://arxiv.org/abs/2304.02198)\]. Similarly, ConfDiff, a force-guided SE(3) diffusion model, generates protein conformations with rich diversity while preserving high fidelity \[[10](http://arxiv.org/abs/2403.14088)\]. These models incorporate physical prior knowledge to guide the generation process, ensuring that sampled conformations are physically plausible \[[10](http://arxiv.org/abs/2403.14088)\].

In addition to diffusion models, other approaches have been developed for protein conformation sampling. Str2Str is a score-based framework for zero-shot protein conformation sampling that leverages an amortized denoising score matching objective trained on general crystal structures \[[8](http://arxiv.org/abs/2306.03117)\]. This method outperforms previous state-of-the-art generative structure prediction models and is significantly faster than long MD simulations \[[8](http://arxiv.org/abs/2306.03117)\]. Similarly, the SE(3) diffusion model, FrameDiff, has been used for protein backbone generation, demonstrating the ability to generate designable monomers up to 500 amino acids without relying on a pretrained protein structure prediction network \[[17](http://arxiv.org/abs/2302.02277)\].

The integration of physical supervision into generative models has also been explored in the context of protein design. ProteinMPNN is a deep learning-based protein sequence design method that has shown outstanding performance in both in silico and experimental tests \[[11](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/98926d43356e87c22c82efc132dcaaac1ff40ebe)\].
