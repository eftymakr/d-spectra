## Related Works

Zero-Knowledge Proofs (ZKPs) have garnered significant attention for their ability to enable secure and private computation, allowing a prover to demonstrate the truth of a statement without revealing additional information \[[9](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/f056118f41849a3688634e1f2fc73108fc660435)\]. This capability has led to their adoption in various domains, including blockchain technologies, verifiable machine learning, and electronic voting \[[10](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/b179cf70666c9f67fc8c330be3a55a5dd2266c2e)\]. However, the computational complexity of the proving process has hindered their widespread adoption \[[1](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/e149d93531d8c07cf43f6cae327a4795144d1304)\]. Recent efforts have focused on accelerating the key primitives of ZKP protocols using hardware, particularly GPUs and ASICs \[[5](http://arxiv.org/abs/2408.05890)\]. 

Several works have explored methods to improve the performance of ZKP systems. GZKP introduces a GPU-accelerated approach that optimizes polynomial computation and multi-scalar multiplication, achieving up to 48.1× speedup on standard benchmarks \[[1](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/e149d93531d8c07cf43f6cae327a4795144d1304)\]. Similarly, PipeZK employs a pipelined architecture with two subsystems to handle large polynomial computations and multi-scalar multiplications, achieving 10× speedup on cryptographic benchmarks and 5× on Zcash \[[2](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/6ee84674b7c426849c3da56cb278c08eb7a903cf)\]. cuZK further enhances performance by proposing a new parallel multi-scalar multiplication algorithm, achieving up to 4.86× speedup on a cryptocurrency application \[[6](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/e4a4608e8e35379e63b9df5bda1b7599ee8ab9ac)\]. 

The challenge of generating proofs with small sizes and universal setup has also been addressed. For instance, zkCNN enables efficient zero-knowledge proofs for convolutional neural networks, supporting large models like VGG16 with a proof generation time of 88.3 seconds and a proof size of 341 kilobytes \[[3](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/6336149196f02d0eb6c1a89bc6b662e4422bd794)\]. Similarly, the work on zero-knowledge proofs for decision tree predictions demonstrates the feasibility of applying ZKPs to machine learning models, achieving a 250-second proof generation time for a model with 23 levels and 1,029 nodes \[[8](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/3becc2935d1f52c82ffd7216c28bc86f5e393208)\]. 

Despite these advancements, many existing ZKP protocols require a trusted setup for each application or generate larger proof sizes with higher verification costs, limiting their applicability in scenarios with numerous verifiers or strict verification time constraints \[[1](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/e149d93531d8c07cf43f6cae327a4795144d1304)\]. This work addresses these limitations by introducing zkSpeed, an accelerator for HyperPlonk, a ZKP protocol that supports one-time, universal setup and small proof sizes \[[1](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/e149d93531d8c07cf43f6cae327a4795144d1304)\]. By accelerating the entire protocol, including SumCheck and Multi-scalar Multiplications (MSMs), zkSpeed achieves geometric mean speedups of 801× over CPU baselines \[[1](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/e149d93531d8c07cf43f6cae327a4795144d1304)\].
