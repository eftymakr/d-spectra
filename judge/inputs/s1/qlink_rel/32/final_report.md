## Related Works

Recent advancements in large language models (LLMs) have emphasized the importance of understanding how feature representations evolve across layers, as this knowledge is critical for improving interpretability and robustness \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2307.09288)\]. Several studies have explored the role of specific layers in LLMs, often focusing on how fine-tuning affects model behavior \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/2109.01652)\]. However, most of these efforts rely on data-dependent analyses of fine-tuned models, which limits their applicability to post-hoc settings \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/2305.18290)\]. In contrast, this work introduces a data-oblivious approach to identify intrinsic critical layers in pre-fine-tuned LLMs by analyzing representation dynamics via Centered Kernel Alignment (CKA) \[[4](http://arxiv.org/abs/1910.10683)\]. This method allows for a more general understanding of how layers contribute to model behavior before any fine-tuning occurs.

The idea of fine-tuning as a means to improve model performance has been extensively studied. For example, instruction tuning has been shown to significantly improve zero-shot performance on unseen tasks by training models on a collection of tasks described via natural language instructions \[[5](http://arxiv.org/abs/2302.13971)\]. Similarly, reinforcement learning from human feedback (RLHF) has been used to align language models with user intent, as demonstrated by the development of InstructGPT \[[6](http://arxiv.org/abs/2309.02144)\]. This approach involves collecting human feedback on model outputs and using it to fine-tune the model, resulting in improvements in truthfulness and reductions in toxic output generation \[[7](http://arxiv.org/abs/2106.09685)\].

Another important line of work explores parameter-efficient fine-tuning methods, which aim to reduce the computational and memory costs associated with full fine-tuning. Techniques such as LoRA (Low-Rank Adaptation) inject trainable rank decomposition matrices into each layer of the Transformer architecture, significantly reducing the number of trainable parameters while maintaining model quality \[[8](http://arxiv.org/abs/2407.21783)\]. Similarly, BitFit proposes modifying only the bias-terms of the model during fine-tuning, which can be competitive with full fine-tuning in some cases \[[9](http://arxiv.org/abs/2106.10199)\]. These methods are particularly useful when deploying large models on resource-constrained devices or when the number of downstream tasks is large \[[10](http://arxiv.org/abs/1810.04805)\].

### <a id='category-fine-tuning-impact-on-model-behavior'></a>Fine-Tuning Impact on Model Behavior: 2 papers found.
The fine-tuning of large language models (LLMs) has been shown to significantly impact their behavior and performance, particularly in terms of reasoning capabilities and alignment with desired outcomes \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2307.09288)\]. Recent studies highlight that fine-tuning LLMs on data that includes chain-of-thought (COT) reasoning can enhance their reasoning abilities, but this process may also introduce challenges such as assessment misalignment, where models tend to assign higher scores to subpar COTs \[[6](http://arxiv.org/abs/2309.02144)\]. To address this, researchers have proposed alignment fine-tuning (AFT) as a paradigm that involves three key steps: fine-tuning with COT data, generating and categorizing multiple COT responses, and calibrating scores using a constraint alignment loss that ensures positive responses outperform negative ones while keeping negative scores within a reasonable range \[[6](http://arxiv.org/abs/2309.02144)\]. This approach not only improves reasoning accuracy but also prevents model degradation. Additionally, the AFT framework can be adapted to ranking scenarios, and it has been compared to other ranking-based alignment methods like DPO and PRO, revealing that the constraint component is crucial for performance \[[6](http://arxiv.org/abs/2309.02144)\]. These findings underscore the importance of carefully designed fine-tuning strategies to enhance model behavior and ensure alignment with human values.

### <a id='category-reinforcement-learning-from-human-feedback-(rlhf)'></a>Reinforcement Learning from Human Feedback (RLHF): 3 papers found.
The category "Reinforcement Learning from Human Feedback (RLHF)" encompasses methods aimed at aligning large language models with human preferences through iterative feedback loops. These approaches typically involve training a reward model based on human-labeled data and then fine-tuning the language model to maximize this reward, often using reinforcement learning. This process is complex and unstable, requiring careful tuning and sampling from the model during training \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/2305.18290)\]. To address these challenges, researchers have proposed alternatives such as Direct Preference Optimization (DPO), which simplifies the RLHF process by enabling closed-form policy extraction using a simple classification loss, thus eliminating the need for extensive sampling or hyperparameter tuning \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/2305.18290)\]. Additionally, prior work has demonstrated that aligning models with human intent through RLHF can significantly improve performance on various tasks, including summarization and dialogue, while reducing toxic outputs and enhancing truthfulness \[[20](http://arxiv.org/abs/2203.02155)\]. Furthermore, iterative online training strategies have been explored, where preference models and RL policies are continuously updated with fresh human feedback, leading to more robust and adaptable models \[[18](http://arxiv.org/abs/2204.05862)\]. These advancements highlight the growing importance of RLHF in improving the alignment and usability of large language models.

### <a id='category-parameter-efficient-fine-tuning-methods'></a>Parameter-Efficient Fine-Tuning Methods: 6 papers found.
Parameter-efficient fine-tuning methods have emerged as a critical area of research in natural language processing, particularly for adapting large pre-trained models to downstream tasks without retraining all parameters. These methods aim to reduce computational costs and storage requirements while maintaining model performance. For instance, Low-Rank Adaptation (LoRA) introduces trainable rank decomposition matrices into each Transformer layer, significantly reducing the number of trainable parameters and GPU memory usage compared to full fine-tuning \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2307.09288)\]. Similarly, BitFit focuses on modifying only the bias terms of the model, demonstrating competitiveness with full fine-tuning, especially with small-to-medium datasets \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/2109.01652)\]. Prefix-tuning offers another lightweight alternative by optimizing task-specific vectors, referred to as prefixes, which are appended to input sequences, allowing the model to attend to these virtual tokens during generation \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/2305.18290)\]. Adapter modules, which add only a few trainable parameters per task, enable parameter sharing across tasks and maintain high performance, as evidenced by their effectiveness in transferring BERT to multiple text classification tasks \[[4](http://arxiv.org/abs/1910.10683)\]. Additionally, recent work has sought to unify these methods under a common framework, identifying key design dimensions such as the function to compute modifications and the position to apply them, leading to more efficient and effective parameter-efficient fine-tuning strategies \[[5](http://arxiv.org/abs/2302.13971)\]. These approaches collectively highlight the growing interest in reducing the resource demands of fine-tuning while preserving model capabilities.
