## Related Works

Generative retrieval has emerged as a promising paradigm in information retrieval, shifting from traditional index-based methods to end-to-end neural models that directly generate document identifiers \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2204.10628)\]. This approach leverages the power of large language models to encode both queries and documents, enabling seamless integration with generative models \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/2305.11841)\]. Early work on autoregressive search engines proposed using ngrams as document identifiers, allowing autoregressive models to generate and score distinctive ngrams, which are then mapped to full passages \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2204.10628)\]. This method demonstrated significant improvements over traditional retrieval systems on the KILT benchmark \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2204.10628)\].

The Differentiable Search Index (DSI) represents a key advancement in this area, encoding all corpus information within a single transformer model, mapping queries directly to relevant document IDs \[[12](http://arxiv.org/abs/2202.06991)\]. This design simplifies the retrieval process and enables end-to-end optimization. However, early DSI models faced challenges in handling long documents and short queries, leading to data distribution mismatches \[[11](http://arxiv.org/abs/2206.10128)\]. To address this, DSI-QG was introduced, which generates potential queries during indexing, allowing the model to better align with retrieval queries \[[11](http://arxiv.org/abs/2206.10128)\].

Generative retrieval has also been explored in the context of open-domain question answering, where dense passage retrieval methods outperform traditional sparse models \[[4](http://arxiv.org/abs/2004.04906)\]. These methods use dual-encoder frameworks to learn dense representations of questions and passages, leading to significant improvements in retrieval accuracy \[[4](http://arxiv.org/abs/2004.04906)\]. Similarly, retrieval-augmented generation (RAG) combines pre-trained models with dense vector indexes to enhance the quality of generated answers \[[5](http://arxiv.org/abs/2005.11401)\]. Blended RAG further improves accuracy by integrating semantic search techniques and hybrid query strategies \[[14](http://arxiv.org/abs/2404.07220)\].

Recent work has focused on improving the scalability of generative retrieval to large-scale corpora. The ANCE framework addresses training bottlenecks by using approximate nearest neighbor indices to generate more representative negative samples \[[8](http://arxiv.org/abs/2007.00808)\]. This leads to better performance compared to traditional dense and sparse retrieval baselines \[[8](http://arxiv.org/abs/2007.00808)\]. Similarly, the RIPOR framework introduces a novel optimization strategy for generative retrieval, significantly improving performance on large-scale benchmarks like MS MARCO \[[10](http://arxiv.org/abs/2311.09134)\].

The generalization capabilities of generative retrieval on out-of-distribution corpora have also been a focus of recent research. The SEAL model, which uses ngrams as identifiers, demonstrated strong performance on the KILT benchmark while maintaining a lighter memory footprint \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2204.10628)\].
