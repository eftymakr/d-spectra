## Related Works

The growing adoption of cloud-native applications has led to increased interest in optimizing both resource usage and financial costs. Several studies have explored the use of containerization technologies, such as Docker and Kubernetes, to improve deployment efficiency and scalability \[[5](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/b5634b15badc73f3cda72b960fd51ef03ac1265d)\]. Kubernetes, in particular, has emerged as a critical platform for managing containerized workloads, offering features like automated scaling, deployment, and monitoring \[[5](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/b5634b15badc73f3cda72b960fd51ef03ac1265d)\]. However, the financial implications of running such applications, particularly in terms of network costs, remain underexplored in the literature.

Prior work has investigated cost optimization strategies for cloud-based applications, with a focus on traditional cloud pricing models and resource allocation techniques \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/2307.12479)\]. These studies highlight the importance of strategic resource management to reduce unnecessary expenditures. More recently, the field of FinOps has gained traction, combining financial management with cloud operations to optimize costs in cloud-based applications \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2501.14753)\]. FinOps practices emphasize budgeting, cost transparency, and accountability, which are essential for managing cloud spend effectively.

In the context of Kubernetes, several studies have examined security and operational challenges, such as role-based access control (RBAC) and patch management \[[4](http://arxiv.org/abs/2006.15275)\]. These practices are crucial for maintaining secure and efficient Kubernetes deployments. Additionally, the integration of Kubernetes with workflow systems has been explored, with solutions like KubeAdaptor addressing scheduling inconsistencies between workflow systems and Kubernetes \[[6](http://arxiv.org/abs/2207.01222)\]. These advancements demonstrate the evolving capabilities of Kubernetes in supporting complex workloads.

The use of containers for scientific workflows has also been studied, with tools like Pachyderm providing a data management layer that enhances reproducibility and scalability \[[7](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/51e2cba5a013cbd11f2ab760a4e3906cfff44780)\]. These systems leverage Kubernetes for orchestration, highlighting the platform’s versatility in supporting diverse applications. Furthermore, the importance of reproducibility in scientific research has been emphasized, with containerization technologies like Docker offering a solution to ensure consistent execution environments \[[13](http://arxiv.org/abs/1410.0846)\].

While existing research has focused on system-level optimization strategies for cost and resource efficiency in containerized systems, the specific analysis of network costs in Kubernetes clusters remains underexplored. This paper addresses this gap by investigating the network usage and cost implications of containerized applications running on Kubernetes clusters. By combining measurement analysis, experimentation, and cost modelling, this study aims to provide actionable insights into network cost optimization for cloud-native applications.
