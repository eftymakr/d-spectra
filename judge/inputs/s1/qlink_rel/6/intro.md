## Related Works

Narrative summarization, which involves condensing long-form texts such as books, movies, and TV scripts into coherent and informative summaries, has been a challenging task for existing language models (LMs) due to the complexity of capturing intricate plotlines, character interactions, and thematic coherence \[[19](http://arxiv.org/abs/2301.09790)\]. Early approaches to text summarization focused on extractive methods, which simply rephrase or select sentences from the source text \[[21](http://arxiv.org/abs/1509.00685)\]. However, these methods often fail to capture the abstract and coherent nature of narratives \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/1912.08777)\]. More recent work has shifted toward abstractive summarization, where models generate new content that captures the essence of the original text \[[5](http://arxiv.org/abs/1602.06023)\]. 

Several studies have explored the use of sequence-to-sequence models for abstractive summarization, with notable contributions from models like the Pointer-Generator Network, which addresses issues of repetition and inaccuracy by allowing the model to either generate new words or copy from the source text \[[4](http://arxiv.org/abs/1704.04368)\]. Similarly, the Hierarchical Neural Story Generation model introduces a structured approach to story generation by first generating a premise and then expanding it into a full passage, demonstrating improvements over non-hierarchical models \[[9](http://arxiv.org/abs/1805.04833)\]. 

Pre-training strategies have also played a crucial role in improving summarization performance. The PEGASUS model, for instance, pre-trains large Transformer-based encoder-decoder models on massive text corpora using a novel self-supervised objective where important sentences are masked and generated from the remaining content \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/1912.08777)\]. This approach has led to state-of-the-art results across multiple domains, including news, science, and stories \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/1912.08777)\]. Similarly, BART, a denoising autoencoder, has shown strong performance in both generation and comprehension tasks by corrupting text and learning to reconstruct it \[[12](http://arxiv.org/abs/1910.13461)\]. 

Despite these advances, challenges remain, particularly in handling long documents and ensuring coherence. The Longformer model addresses the issue of processing long sequences by introducing an attention mechanism that scales linearly with sequence length, enabling efficient handling of long documents \[[10](http://arxiv.org/abs/2004.05150)\]. Additionally, the BookSum dataset has been introduced to address the lack of long-form narrative data, providing a collection of multi-sentence summaries for books, plays, and stories \[[8](http://arxiv.org/abs/2105.08209)\]. 

Recent work has also focused on improving the quality of generated summaries through iterative refinement and coherence evaluation. The Self-Refine approach improves initial outputs from LLMs through iterative feedback and refinement, achieving improvements of up to 20% in task performance \[[18](http://arxiv.org/abs/2303.17651)\].
