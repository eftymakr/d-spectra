## Related Works

Sensor calibration is a critical step in autonomous systems that integrate heterogeneous sensors such as radar, camera, and LiDAR. Prior work has focused on both offline and online calibration methods, with varying degrees of success in addressing the challenges posed by sensor sparsity and measurement uncertainty. For instance, \[[1](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/0955326c67a947b5fac5fbae7238d04fc9adc251)\] presents a novel open-source tool for extrinsic calibration of radar, camera, and LiDAR, emphasizing joint calibration across multiple sensors. However, this method requires a calibration target and does not address the issue of online calibration during system operation. Similarly, \[[2](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/4d4dbbcf27d991aeab43dafb82a105bb5512b799)\] introduces a continuous-time approach for 3D radar-to-camera extrinsic calibration, which does not rely on specialized radar retroreflectors but still requires a controlled environment for calibration. 

Online calibration methods have also been explored, particularly in the context of moving object tracking. \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/2103.07505)\] proposes an online calibration method based on detection and tracking of moving objects, which leverages existing perception pipelines to provide a resource-efficient solution. This approach is particularly relevant for long-term system reliability, as it avoids the need for dedicated calibration targets. However, it primarily focuses on radar and LiDAR and does not address the specific challenges of radar-camera calibration. 

In the context of radar-camera calibration, \[[4](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/c80265fa719eb85c8a56a6b36c74b99b793f39b2)\] presents a data-driven method for automatic rotational radar-camera calibration without dedicated calibration targets. This method uses a coarse and fine convolutional neural network to achieve precise and robust sensor registration. While this approach is promising, it is limited to rotational calibration and does not address the full six degrees of freedom (6-DoF) calibration required for accurate sensor fusion. 

Another notable work is \[[5](http://arxiv.org/abs/1904.08743)\], which introduces a middle-fusion approach for radar and camera sensor fusion, focusing on 3D object detection. This method uses a center point detection network and a frustum-based association method to align radar and camera data. While this work improves object detection performance, it does not directly address the calibration problem between radar and camera. 

The nuScenes dataset \[[6](http://arxiv.org/abs/2011.04841)\] provides a comprehensive benchmark for autonomous driving, including synchronized data from multiple sensors such as cameras, LiDAR, and radar. This dataset has been used in various studies to evaluate sensor fusion and calibration methods. The results show that some methods are not appropriate for camera-radar calibration, while others provide similar accuracy.

In this paper, we address the problem of extrinsic calibration of a radar–LiDAR–camera sensor system. This problem is primarily challenging due to sparse informativeness of radar measurements. Namely, radars cannot extract rich structural information about the environment, while their lack of elevation resolution, that is nevertheless accompanied by substantial elevation field of view, introduces uncertainty in the origin of the measurements. We propose a novel calibration method which involves a special target design and two-step optimization procedure to solve the aforementioned challenges. First step of the optimization is minimization of a reprojection error based on an introduced point–circle geometric constraint. Since the first step is not able to provide reliable estimates of all the six extrinsic parameters, we introduce a second step to refine the subset of parameters with high uncertainty. We exploit a pattern discovered in the radar cross section estimation that is correlated to the missing elevation angle. Additionally, we carry out identifiability analysis based on the Fisher Information Matrix to show minimal requirements on the dataset and to verify the method through simulations. We test the calibration method on a variety of sensor configurations and address the problem of radar vertical misalignment. In the end, we show via extensive experiment analysis that the proposed method is able to reliably estimate all the six parameters of the extrinsic calibration.»

### <a id='category-multi-sensor-fusion-approaches'></a>Multi-Sensor Fusion Approaches: 9 papers found.
The related works in multi-sensor fusion for autonomous driving primarily focus on improving radar-camera integration through advanced data association and feature-level fusion techniques. Early approaches relied on rule-based methods for radar-camera association, which often struggled with noisy and sparse radar data and depth ambiguity from 2D bounding boxes \[[8](http://arxiv.org/abs/2103.07825)\]. More recent studies have shifted toward deep learning-based methods to enhance feature-level interaction and global reasoning, such as the proposed deep representation learning approach that achieves a 92.2% F1 score, surpassing traditional rule-based methods by 11.6% \[[8](http://arxiv.org/abs/2103.07825)\]. Additionally, cross-supervised learning strategies, such as RODNet, have been introduced to leverage camera-radar fusion without extensive annotation efforts, achieving 86% average precision and 88% average recall in object detection \[[9](http://arxiv.org/abs/2102.05150)\]. Other works, like CenterFusion, employ a middle-fusion approach to combine radar and camera data, improving the nuScenes Detection Score (NDS) by over 12% \[[1](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/0955326c67a947b5fac5fbae7238d04fc9adc251)\]. Similarly, CameraRadarFusion Net (CRF-Net) automatically determines the optimal fusion level within network layers and introduces a training strategy inspired by Dropout to enhance performance \[[10](http://arxiv.org/abs/2005.07431)\]. The use of multi-view radar-camera fusion, such as MVFusion, has also gained traction, achieving state-of-the-art results with semantic-aligned radar features and cross-modal interaction \[[13](http://arxiv.org/abs/2302.10511)\]. These methods collectively highlight the importance of addressing data association, feature alignment, and robustness in challenging environments, paving the way for more reliable and accurate perception systems in autonomous driving.

The Related Works section of the paper focuses on benchmark datasets for calibration, which are crucial for evaluating radar and camera system calibration methods. Existing datasets, such as nuScenes \[[2](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/4d4dbbcf27d991aeab43dafb82a105bb5512b799)\], provide a comprehensive set of sensor data including radar, lidar, and cameras, enabling the development and testing of multi-sensor fusion algorithms. nuScenes includes 3D bounding boxes for 23 classes and offers detailed annotations, making it a valuable resource for calibration tasks \[[2](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/4d4dbbcf27d991aeab43dafb82a105bb5512b799)\]. Similarly, the RADDet dataset \[[1](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/0955326c67a947b5fac5fbae7238d04fc9adc251)\] introduces radar data in the form of Range-Azimuth-Doppler tensors, along with bounding boxes and category labels, specifically designed for radar-based object detection. This dataset addresses the lack of public radar data and supports the training of deep learning models for radar perception \[[1](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/0955326c67a947b5fac5fbae7238d04fc9adc251)\]. The View-of-Delft (VoD) dataset \[[4](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/c80265fa719eb85c8a56a6b36c74b99b793f39b2)\] further expands the scope by including synchronized LiDAR, camera, and radar data, allowing for comparative studies between different sensor modalities. It contains a large number of annotated 3D bounding boxes, facilitating the evaluation of object detection algorithms in complex urban environments \[[4](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/c80265fa719eb85c8a56a6b36c74b99b793f39b2)\]. Additionally, the automotive radar dataset presented in \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/2103.07505)\] provides point-wise annotations from over four hours of driving, enabling the development of machine learning-based radar perception algorithms focused on moving road users. These datasets collectively highlight the importance of diverse and annotated sensor data in advancing the field of autonomous vehicle perception and calibration.
