## Related Works

Differential privacy (DP) has become the de facto standard for ensuring privacy in data analysis \[[16](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/e4ce10063cd25447dcde75c2d9ce327446ced952)\]. However, its implementation is vulnerable to malicious behaviors by servers, particularly in distributed settings \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/1811.12469)\]. For instance, servers may sample noise from incorrect distributions or generate correlated noise while appearing to follow established protocols \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/1811.12469)\]. This issue has been explored in several works that examine the robustness of DP mechanisms against adversarial manipulation \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/1909.09630)\]. 

Local differential privacy (LDP) is a variant of DP where users perturb their data before sending it to the data collector, ensuring privacy even if the collector is untrusted \[[5](http://arxiv.org/abs/1712.01524)\]. LDP has been widely studied and deployed in industry settings, such as telemetry data collection \[[5](http://arxiv.org/abs/1712.01524)\]. However, LDP protocols are susceptible to manipulation attacks, where adversaries can alter their inputs to skew the results \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/1909.09630)\]. For example, data poisoning attacks can inject fake users to manipulate the output of LDP protocols \[[6](http://arxiv.org/abs/1911.02046)\]. These attacks highlight the need for additional verification mechanisms to ensure the correctness of the execution of DP protocols \[[20](http://arxiv.org/abs/2104.06569)\].

To address these issues, recent work has proposed verifiable differential privacy (VDP) frameworks that incorporate additional verification steps to ensure that the execution of DP mechanisms is faithful \[[20](http://arxiv.org/abs/2104.06569)\]. This idea is closely related to the concept of verifiable distributed differential privacy (VDDP), which we introduce in this paper. VDDP extends the notion of DP by incorporating verification mechanisms that allow a third party (verifier) to confirm that the data collector has followed the protocol correctly \[[20](http://arxiv.org/abs/2104.06569)\]. This approach is inspired by the use of zero-knowledge proofs (ZKPs) in cryptographic protocols, which allow one party to prove the validity of a statement without revealing any additional information \[[20](http://arxiv.org/abs/2104.06569)\]. While ZKPs are sufficient for achieving DP under verifiability requirements, they are not necessary, as our work demonstrates \[[20](http://arxiv.org/abs/2104.06569)\].

Several LDP protocols have been proposed to improve the utility of privacy-preserving data collection. For example, RAPPOR uses randomized response to collect statistics on sensitive data while preserving privacy \[[19](http://arxiv.org/abs/1407.6981)\]. Similarly, the Discrete Gaussian mechanism has been introduced to address the practical challenges of using continuous distributions in DP \[[10](http://arxiv.org/abs/2004.00010)\]. These mechanisms have been evaluated for their accuracy and privacy guarantees, showing that they can achieve comparable or even better performance than existing methods \[[10](http://arxiv.org/abs/2004.00010)\].
