## Related Works

The problem of learning from noisy labels has been extensively studied in the context of deep learning. Early approaches focused on modifying network architectures or estimating true labels from noisy ones \[[5](http://arxiv.org/abs/1712.09482)\]. For instance, \[[5](http://arxiv.org/abs/1712.09482)\] proposed a framework that formulates the problem using an undirected graphical model, representing the relationship between noisy and clean labels. This approach is trained in a semi-supervised setting and regularizes training using auxiliary sources of information. Similarly, \[[12](http://arxiv.org/abs/1803.11364)\] introduced a joint optimization framework that simultaneously learns DNN parameters and estimates true labels, alternating updates between the network and the label estimates. This method was tested on noisy CIFAR-10 and Clothing1M datasets, showing significant improvements over existing methods.

Another line of research focuses on loss correction techniques to make deep neural networks robust to label noise. \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/1609.03683)\] presented a theoretically grounded approach that uses matrix inversion and multiplication to correct losses, assuming knowledge of class-dependent label noise probabilities. They also demonstrated how these probabilities can be estimated using a recent noise estimation technique. This method was evaluated on multiple datasets, including MNIST, IMDB, CIFAR-10, and a large-scale clothing image dataset. \[[10](http://arxiv.org/abs/1805.07836)\] further generalized this idea by proposing a family of noise-robust loss functions, including a generalization of mean absolute error (MAE) and categorical cross entropy (CCE). These losses were tested on CIFAR-10, CIFAR-100, and FASHION-MNIST datasets, showing improved performance under noisy label conditions.

### <a id='category-noise-robustness-in-deep-learning'></a>Noise Robustness in Deep Learning: 11 papers found.
The category "Noise Robustness in Deep Learning" encompasses a variety of approaches aimed at improving the performance of deep neural networks in the presence of noisy or corrupted labels. Several studies have explored methods to mitigate the adverse effects of label noise, such as Co-teaching, which involves training two neural networks to teach each other by selecting and communicating clean data \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/1804.06872)\]. Another approach focuses on loss correction, proposing techniques that are agnostic to network architecture and domain, relying on matrix inversion and multiplication to adjust for class-dependent label noise \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/1609.03683)\]. Additionally, some research emphasizes the use of graphical models to represent the relationship between noisy and clean labels, enabling semi-supervised learning and improving the robustness of models trained on noisy datasets \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/1706.00038)\]. Meta-learning algorithms have also been introduced to dynamically assign weights to training examples based on gradient directions, offering a data-efficient way to handle label noise without requiring extensive hyperparameter tuning \[[4](http://arxiv.org/abs/1803.09050)\]. Theoretical work has further explored noise-tolerant loss functions, such as mean absolute error, which can enable standard backpropagation to learn accurate classifiers even under label noise \[[5](http://arxiv.org/abs/1712.09482)\]. In practical applications, unified distillation frameworks have been proposed to leverage side information, such as small clean datasets and knowledge graphs, to reduce the risk of learning from noisy labels \[[6](http://arxiv.org/abs/1908.06112)\]. Moreover, methods that utilize trusted clean data to correct label noise have been shown to significantly improve performance across various tasks, including vision and natural language processing \[[8](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/5d6ae67e569f974360b107060c23cbb8a13b0687)\]. The importance of robustness to label noise has also been highlighted in the context of large-scale datasets, where label corruption can arise from non-expert annotations or adversarial attacks \[[9](http://arxiv.org/abs/1802.05300)\]. Overall, these approaches reflect a growing body of research dedicated to enhancing the reliability and generalization of deep learning models in real-world scenarios where label noise is prevalent.

[Snippet]: «Training accurate deep neural networks (DNNs) in the presence of noisy labels is an important and challenging task. Though a number of approaches have been proposed for learning with noisy labels, many open issues remain. In this paper, we show that DNN learning with Cross Entropy (CE) exhibits overfitting to noisy labels on some classes (``easy" classes), but more surprisingly, it also suffers from significant under learning on some other classes (``hard" classes). Intuitively, CE requires an extra term to facilitate learning of hard classes, and more importantly, this term should be noise tolerant, so as to avoid overfitting to noisy labels. Inspired by the symmetric KL-divergence, we propose the approach of Symmetric cross entropy Learning (SL), boosting CE symmetrically with a noise robust counterpart Reverse Cross Entropy (RCE). Our proposed SL approach simultaneously addresses both the under learning and overfitting problem of CE in the presence of noisy labels. We provide a theoretical analysis of SL and also empirically show, on a range of benchmark and real-world datasets, that SL outperforms state-of-the-art methods. We also show that SL can be easily incorporated into existing methods in order to further enhance their performance.»
