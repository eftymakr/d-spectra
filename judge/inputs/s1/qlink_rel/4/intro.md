## Related Works

The problem of learning from noisy labels has been extensively studied in the context of deep learning. Early approaches focused on modifying network architectures or estimating true labels from noisy ones \[[5](http://arxiv.org/abs/1712.09482)\]. For instance, \[[5](http://arxiv.org/abs/1712.09482)\] proposed a framework that formulates the problem using an undirected graphical model, representing the relationship between noisy and clean labels. This approach is trained in a semi-supervised setting and regularizes training using auxiliary sources of information. Similarly, \[[12](http://arxiv.org/abs/1803.11364)\] introduced a joint optimization framework that simultaneously learns DNN parameters and estimates true labels, alternating updates between the network and the label estimates. This method was tested on noisy CIFAR-10 and Clothing1M datasets, showing significant improvements over existing methods.

Another line of research focuses on loss correction techniques to make deep neural networks robust to label noise. \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/1609.03683)\] presented a theoretically grounded approach that uses matrix inversion and multiplication to correct losses, assuming knowledge of class-dependent label noise probabilities. They also demonstrated how these probabilities can be estimated using a recent noise estimation technique. This method was evaluated on multiple datasets, including MNIST, IMDB, CIFAR-10, and a large-scale clothing image dataset. \[[10](http://arxiv.org/abs/1805.07836)\] further generalized this idea by proposing a family of noise-robust loss functions, including a generalization of mean absolute error (MAE) and categorical cross entropy (CCE). These losses were tested on CIFAR-10, CIFAR-100, and FASHION-MNIST datasets, showing improved performance under noisy label conditions.

More recently, \[[6](http://arxiv.org/abs/1908.06112)\] introduced Symmetric Cross Entropy (SL), which combines Cross Entropy (CE) with Reverse Cross Entropy (RCE) to address both overfitting to noisy labels and underlearning of hard examples. This approach was evaluated on a range of benchmark and real-world datasets, demonstrating superior performance compared to state-of-the-art methods. \[[14](http://arxiv.org/abs/2002.07394)\] proposed DivideMix, a framework that leverages semi-supervised learning techniques to dynamically divide training data into clean and noisy subsets, improving robustness to label noise. This method was tested on multiple benchmark datasets, showing significant improvements over existing methods.

Additionally, \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/1804.06872)\] proposed Co-teaching, a paradigm where two deep neural networks are trained simultaneously, allowing them to teach each other by selecting and communicating clean data.
