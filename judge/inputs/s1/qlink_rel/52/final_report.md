## Related Works

Dense retrievers have gained significant traction in information retrieval due to their ability to encode text into dense vector representations that can be efficiently compared using shallow similarity operations such as inner product \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2108.05540)\]. These models typically rely on pre-trained language models (e.g., BERT, LLaMA) that are fine-tuned using contrastive learning to align queries and passages in a shared embedding space \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/2104.08253)\]. Recent studies have questioned the role of fine-tuning versus pre-training in dense retrievers, arguing that retrieval knowledge is primarily acquired during pre-training, and that fine-tuning only adjusts neuron activations rather than reorganizing knowledge \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/1907.11692)\]. This claim was initially tested using a BERT-based encoder with the Dense Passage Retriever (DPR) as a representative model \[[4](http://arxiv.org/abs/1810.04805)\]. However, the generalizability of this finding has been limited due to the narrow scope of the original study \[[5](http://arxiv.org/abs/2112.09118)\].

To address this limitation, we extend the analysis by evaluating different representation approaches, such as CLS token usage versus mean pooling, and varying backbone architectures, including encoder-only BERT and decoder-only LLaMA \[[6](http://arxiv.org/abs/2007.00808)\]. Additionally, we test the claim on multiple datasets, including MSMARCO and Natural Questions, to assess its robustness \[[7](http://arxiv.org/abs/2002.08909)\]. Our results confirm that pre-trained knowledge underpins retrieval performance in DPR tuning, with fine-tuning primarily adjusting neuron activation rather than reorganizing knowledge \[[8](http://arxiv.org/abs/2104.06967)\]. However, this pattern does not universally apply to all models, such as mean-pooled Contriever and decoder-based LLaMA models, which exhibit different behavior \[[9](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/5be217c678916543884c354654263f27c0a6bd9f)\].

The importance of pre-training in dense retrievers has also been explored in the context of contrastive learning. For instance, the coCondenser model introduces an unsupervised corpus-level contrastive loss to warm up the passage embedding space, reducing the need for heavy data engineering and large batch training \[[10](http://arxiv.org/abs/2010.08191)\]. Similarly, the Condenser architecture is designed to pre-train dense encoders by conditioning LM prediction on dense representations, leading to improved performance on various text retrieval tasks \[[11](http://arxiv.org/abs/2002.03932)\]. These approaches highlight the significance of pre-training in shaping the quality of dense representations, which is critical for effective retrieval \[[12](http://arxiv.org/abs/1906.08237)\].
