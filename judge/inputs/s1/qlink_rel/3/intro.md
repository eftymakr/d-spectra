## Related Works

Recent advancements in Text-to-Speech (TTS) systems have significantly enhanced speech naturalness, but challenges remain in achieving precise prosody control and mispronunciation correction \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2110.02952)\]. Traditional approaches for prosody manipulation often rely on specialized modules or additional training, which limits their capacity for post-hoc adjustments \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2110.02952)\]. Similarly, conventional mispronunciation correction methods depend on grapheme-to-phoneme dictionaries, making them less practical in low-resource settings \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2110.02952)\]. This section reviews related work on TTS, prosody modeling, and mispronunciation correction, highlighting their limitations and how they relate to the proposed method.

Prosody modeling is a critical component in modern TTS frameworks, as it enables control over the style of synthesized utterances \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/2011.06465)\]. By explicitly providing prosody features to the TTS model, the style of the output can be controlled \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/2011.06465)\]. However, predicting natural and reasonable prosody at inference time remains challenging \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/2011.06465)\]. Several works have proposed hierarchical architectures to improve prosody modeling, such as conditioning phoneme-level prosody features on word-level prosody features \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/2011.06465)\]. These methods have shown improvements in audio quality and prosody naturalness \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/2011.06465)\]. Additionally, hierarchical prosody modeling has been explored in non-autoregressive TTS models, where the model is conditioned on both coarse and fine-grained acoustic speech features to learn a latent prosody space \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2110.02952)\]. This approach enables more intuitive and meaningful control over prosody dimensions \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2110.02952)\].

Non-autoregressive TTS models, such as FastSpeech and Fastpitch, have been developed to improve inference speed while maintaining high-quality speech synthesis \[[6](http://arxiv.org/abs/2006.06873)\]. FastSpeech 2, for instance, directly trains the model with ground-truth targets instead of simplified outputs from a teacher model, leading to improved voice quality \[[6](http://arxiv.org/abs/2006.06873)\]. Fastpitch, on the other hand, conditions the model on fundamental frequency contours, allowing for more expressive and semantically aligned speech \[[6](http://arxiv.org/abs/2006.06873)\]. These models demonstrate the potential of non-autoregressive approaches for efficient and high-quality TTS synthesis.
