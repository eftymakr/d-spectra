## Related Works

Nonverbal communication plays a crucial role in human interaction, encompassing gestures, facial expressions, and body language that convey intent and emotion \[[25](http://arxiv.org/abs/1612.00837)\]. However, existing large language models (LLMs) have limited capabilities in incorporating these nonverbal elements, thereby constraining their ability to create immersive conversational experiences \[[25](http://arxiv.org/abs/1612.00837)\]. This section reviews related works that address multimodal understanding, nonverbal communication, and the integration of visual and textual modalities in language models.

Multimodal language models have been extensively studied, with a focus on integrating multiple modalities such as text, images, and audio \[[14](http://arxiv.org/abs/1705.09406)\]. Early approaches often relied on late fusion strategies, where features from different modalities were combined at the final stage \[[14](http://arxiv.org/abs/1705.09406)\]. More recent works, such as the Multimodal Transformer (MulT), have introduced end-to-end architectures that handle non-aligned multimodal time-series data by using directional pairwise crossmodal attention \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/1906.00295)\]. This approach enables the model to capture interactions between modalities across different time steps, improving performance on tasks such as sentiment analysis and visual question answering \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/1906.00295)\].

The importance of nonverbal cues in human communication has also been explored in the context of sentiment analysis. For example, the Tensor Fusion Network (TFN) models both intra-modality and inter-modality dynamics, achieving state-of-the-art results in multimodal sentiment analysis \[[11](http://arxiv.org/abs/1707.07250)\]. Similarly, the LSTM-based model proposed in \[[16](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/75d17e8fa5165a849ebe5f0475bdf77bf0b6be74)\] captures contextual information from utterances in videos, improving performance by 5-10% over existing methods \[[16](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/75d17e8fa5165a849ebe5f0475bdf77bf0b6be74)\]. These studies highlight the need for models that can effectively integrate nonverbal cues with textual information.

Recent advances in multimodal models have also focused on training strategies that enable models to understand and generate nonverbal signals. For instance, the Visual Genome dataset provides dense annotations of objects, attributes, and relationships within images, enabling models to learn about interactions between objects and their visual context \[[8](http://arxiv.org/abs/1602.07332)\]. This is critical for tasks such as visual question answering, where understanding the spatial and relational context is essential \[[8](http://arxiv.org/abs/1602.07332)\].

The integration of nonverbal cues into language models has also been explored through instruction tuning. For example, LLaVA, a large multimodal model, is trained using instruction-following data generated by GPT-4, enabling it to perform tasks such as visual question answering and image captioning \[[7](http://arxiv.org/abs/2304.08485)\].
