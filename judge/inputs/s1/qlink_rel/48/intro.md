## Related Works

Recent advances in neural information retrieval (NIR) have been driven by transformer-based pre-trained language models, which have shown significant improvements in multilingual and cross-lingual retrieval \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/1911.02116)\]. These models, such as BERT \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/1810.04805)\], RoBERTa \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/1907.11692)\], and XLM-R \[[4](http://arxiv.org/abs/2004.04906)\], have been extensively studied for their ability to capture contextual semantics and improve performance on a wide range of tasks. However, their effectiveness for low-resource, morphologically rich languages like Amharic remains underexplored due to data scarcity and suboptimal tokenization \[[5](http://arxiv.org/abs/2103.11811)\]. 

The challenge of handling low-resource languages has motivated research into language-specific adaptations. For instance, XLM-R \[[4](http://arxiv.org/abs/2004.04906)\] demonstrates that pretraining multilingual models at scale can lead to significant performance gains, especially for low-resource languages. Similarly, RoBERTa \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/1907.11692)\] has been shown to outperform BERT \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/1810.04805)\] in various tasks by introducing more effective training strategies. These models have also been adapted for specific languages, such as Amharic, where the availability of pre-trained models is limited \[[6](http://arxiv.org/abs/1512.03385)\]. 

Efforts to improve retrieval performance in low-resource settings have also focused on dense retrieval methods. Dense retrieval models, such as ColBERT \[[11](http://arxiv.org/abs/2004.12832)\] and Dense Passage Retrieval \[[12](http://arxiv.org/abs/2001.03324)\], leverage neural embeddings to compute similarity between queries and documents. These approaches have been shown to outperform traditional sparse retrieval methods like BM25 \[[13](http://arxiv.org/abs/2005.14165)\] in many cases. However, they often require extensive training data and careful tuning to achieve optimal performance, which is challenging for low-resource languages \[[14](http://arxiv.org/abs/2104.08663)\].

In the context of Amharic, several studies have explored the development of language-specific models. For example, MasakhaNER \[[15](http://arxiv.org/abs/1908.10084)\] introduces a dataset for named entity recognition in ten African languages, including Amharic, and evaluates state-of-the-art methods for this task. Similarly, the work on Amharic Parts-of-speech Tagging \[[16](http://arxiv.org/abs/2106.09685)\] highlights the importance of language-specific adaptations and the challenges posed by morphological complexity. These studies underscore the need for tailored approaches to address the unique characteristics of Amharic.

The proposed RoBERTa-Base-Amharic-Embed model \[[17](http://arxiv.org/abs/1103.0398)\] addresses these challenges by leveraging pre-trained RoBERTa backbones and fine-tuning them on Amharic-specific data. This approach allows the model to capture the morphological richness of Amharic while maintaining efficiency.
