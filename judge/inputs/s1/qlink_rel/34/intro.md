## Related Works

Time series forecasting has been a central challenge in various domains, including finance, energy, and weather prediction. Traditional approaches such as autoregressive models and Gaussian processes have been widely used but often struggle with capturing complex temporal dependencies and handling long-term forecasting \[[12](http://arxiv.org/abs/1703.07015)\]. Recent advances in deep learning have led to the development of more sophisticated models that leverage neural networks to better model temporal dynamics \[[6](http://arxiv.org/abs/2004.13408)\]. 

Several studies have explored the use of Transformers for time series forecasting, given their ability to capture long-range dependencies \[[10](http://arxiv.org/abs/2202.07125)\]. However, Transformers face challenges in terms of computational efficiency and memory usage, especially for long sequences \[[19](http://arxiv.org/abs/2012.07436)\]. To address these issues, researchers have proposed variants such as the Informer, which introduces a ProbSparse self-attention mechanism to reduce time and memory complexity while maintaining performance \[[19](http://arxiv.org/abs/2012.07436)\]. Similarly, the Autoformer employs an Auto-Correlation mechanism to improve the efficiency and accuracy of long-term forecasting \[[9](http://arxiv.org/abs/2106.13008)\]. 

Another line of research focuses on the interpretability of time series models. The Temporal Fusion Transformer (TFT) was introduced to provide interpretable insights into temporal dynamics, combining recurrent layers with self-attention mechanisms to learn both local and long-term dependencies \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/1912.09363)\]. This approach has been shown to improve forecasting accuracy and provide meaningful insights into model behavior \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/1912.09363)\]. 

In addition to forecasting, there has been growing interest in using pre-trained models for time series analysis. The Chronos framework, for instance, pre-trains Transformer-based models on a large collection of time series data, enabling zero-shot performance on new datasets \[[4](http://arxiv.org/abs/2403.07815)\]. Similarly, the Time-LLM framework reprograms large language models (LLMs) for time series forecasting, aligning the modalities of time series data and natural language \[[16](http://arxiv.org/abs/2310.01728)\]. These approaches demonstrate the potential of pre-trained models in improving forecasting accuracy across diverse domains. 

However, the effectiveness of LLMs for time series forecasting remains a topic of debate. Some studies have found that replacing the LLM component with a basic attention layer does not degrade forecasting performance, and in some cases, results in improvement \[[20](http://arxiv.org/abs/2406.16964)\]. Furthermore, LLMs may not effectively capture sequential dependencies in time series, and their performance can be limited by the way they tokenize numerical data \[[20](http://arxiv.org/abs/2406.16964)\].
