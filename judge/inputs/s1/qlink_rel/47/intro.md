## Related Works

Multi-Domain Recommendation (MDR) has emerged as a promising approach to enhance recommendation performance by leveraging knowledge transfer across different domains \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2103.01696)\]. Traditional MDR methods often assume a uniform transfer strategy, which may not be suitable for all scenarios due to varying domain characteristics and potential conflicts \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/1910.05189)\]. For instance, methods like CoNet \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/1804.06769)\] and DDTCDR \[[4](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/2b031c7170c9dccbe506f3d3a1d6104f65e835ae)\] have explored deep transfer learning to capture complex user-item interactions, but they typically treat all domains equally without considering domain-specific variations. This can lead to the Negative Transfer Problem (NTP), where unselective knowledge transfer degrades performance \[[5](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/d5630291a4ba3f6266c77e6d00920502f986afa6)\].

To address this, several works have attempted to model domain relationships more effectively. The work in \[[6](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/24a473700894dbfab0bfd9dc3d542acfeef66bca)\] introduces a framework for dual-target cross-domain recommendation, where both the source and target domains are simultaneously improved. This approach emphasizes the importance of bidirectional knowledge transfer and the need for adaptive embedding strategies. Similarly, the paper \[[7](http://arxiv.org/abs/1203.3535)\] proposes a Bi-directional Transfer Graph Collaborative Filtering Network (BiTGCF), which leverages common users as bridges for knowledge transfer and integrates domain-specific and common features during the process. These methods highlight the necessity of modeling domain relationships and the potential of bidirectional transfer.

Another key contribution is the work in \[[8](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/7807d924fa46caec7b706b98156962caf8458d59)\], which introduces the Preference Propagation GraphNet (PPGN). This model constructs a Cross-Domain Preference Matrix (CDPM) to capture interactions across domains and uses a propagation layer to model how user preferences spread in the graph. PPGN simplifies cross-domain recommendation into a unified multi-task model, which can be optimized more efficiently than previous methods. This approach aligns with the idea of dynamic domain selection, as it implicitly models domain relationships through graph-based propagation.

The paper \[[9](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/cb919954e93726aff490458bd61d6b3d8155fbd7)\] introduces a unified framework for cross-domain and cross-system recommendations, which aims to improve performance across multiple datasets simultaneously. This work proposes a graph embedding and attention-based approach to combine embeddings from different domains, emphasizing the importance of personalized training strategies to avoid negative transfer. This aligns with the concept of dynamic domain selection, as it allows for adaptive model adjustments based on domain characteristics.

In addition, the work \[[10](http://arxiv.org/abs/2108.07976)\] presents a multi-sparse-domain collaborative recommendation model (MSDCR) that treats all domains as sparse and focuses on capturing domain-specific and domain-invariant preferences. This approach uses adversarial training to preserve domain-specific preferences while allowing for knowledge transfer, which is a key consideration in the design of the Similar Domain Selection Principle (SDSP) proposed in this paper
