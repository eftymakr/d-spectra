## Related Works

Multi-token prediction (MTP) has emerged as an alternative to the traditional next-token prediction (NTP) objective in language model pre-training. While NTP focuses on predicting one token at a time, MTP extends this by predicting multiple future tokens simultaneously, often using multiple independent output heads that share a common model trunk \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2404.19737)\]. This approach has been shown to improve downstream performance, inference speed, and training efficiency, particularly for large models \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2404.19737)\]. However, prior work has demonstrated that smaller language models (SLMs) struggle with the MTP objective, often failing to capture the necessary complexity \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2404.19737)\]. 

Several studies have explored the limitations of NTP. For instance, \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/2403.06963)\] highlights that the assumption that teacher-forcing learns an accurate next-token predictor is often flawed, leading to failure in certain tasks. This issue is exacerbated in simple planning tasks, where both Transformer and Mamba architectures fail to learn accurate predictions \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/2403.06963)\]. To address this, \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/2403.06963)\] proposes a teacherless training approach that predicts multiple tokens in advance, offering a potential solution to these failures. 

The benefits of MTP have also been explored in the context of inference speed. \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2404.19737)\] reports that models trained with 4-token prediction can be up to 3 times faster at inference, even with large batch sizes. Similarly, \[[8](http://arxiv.org/abs/2211.17192)\] introduces speculative decoding, an algorithm that enables faster inference by generating multiple tokens in parallel without changing the output distribution. This method accelerates existing models without retraining or architectural changes, achieving a 2X-3X speedup on T5-XXL \[[8](http://arxiv.org/abs/2211.17192)\]. 

In addition to inference speed, MTP has been linked to improved reasoning capabilities. \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2404.19737)\] notes that multi-token prediction is favorable for the development of induction heads and algorithmic reasoning capabilities, as it encourages the model to plan for future tokens rather than relying on local correlations \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2404.19737)\]. This aligns with \[[10](http://arxiv.org/abs/2001.04063)\], which introduces ProphetNet, a model that predicts future n-grams during pre-training. By planning for multiple tokens at once, ProphetNet avoids overfitting to strong local correlations and achieves state-of-the-art results on summarization and question generation tasks \[[10](http://arxiv.org/abs/2001.04063)\].
