## Related Works

Federated unlearning has emerged as a critical research area in response to the "right to be forgotten" legislation, which mandates that users can request the removal of their data from machine learning models \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2203.07320)\]. This has spurred the development of unlearning techniques that aim to remove the influence of specific data points or clients from a trained model without requiring full retraining \[[2](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/eadeffdec9fac8fd7f9aea732ca410eb082b7dcf)\]. Existing approaches vary in their methods and efficiency, with some focusing on retraining from scratch \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/2310.20448)\], while others propose more efficient strategies that leverage historical parameter updates or model calibration \[[4](http://arxiv.org/abs/2207.05521)\].

Several studies have explored federated unlearning in the context of privacy-preserving machine learning. For example, \[[5](http://arxiv.org/abs/2201.09441)\] introduces a method that uses knowledge distillation to remove a client's contribution without requiring access to their data. This approach is particularly useful in scenarios where clients are unwilling or unable to share their training data. Similarly, \[[6](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/39da2d48f2132b919405e3bb6df6ece44ac6e15a)\] proposes a technique that eliminates the influence of a client by subtracting accumulated historical updates from the model, enabling efficient unlearning without retraining. This method is especially relevant in federated learning settings where data is distributed across multiple clients.

Another line of research focuses on the challenges of verifying the effectiveness of unlearning. \[[7](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/06edb1f6eadb6c141d720fcca4c854578dc5d670)\] introduces a framework called VeriFi, which allows for systematic analysis of federated unlearning and quantifies its impact. This is crucial because unlearning itself may not be sufficient to implement the right to be forgotten unless the effect can be independently verified \[[8](http://arxiv.org/abs/2205.12709)\]. VeriFi enables clients to verify the unlearning effect through local checks, ensuring that the model no longer retains the influence of the revoked data \[[9](http://arxiv.org/abs/2209.02299)\].

Efforts have also been made to improve the efficiency of federated unlearning. \[[10](http://arxiv.org/abs/2401.05146)\] proposes a method that reduces the computational overhead by using a lightweight auxiliary unlearning module integrated into the learning process. This approach eliminates the need for additional time-consuming steps, making it well-suited for federated learning environments. ### <a id='category-federated-unlearning-techniques'></a>Federated Unlearning Techniques: 19 papers found.
The category "Federated Unlearning Techniques" explores the challenges and solutions for enabling data removal from federated learning (FL) models, driven by the "right to be forgotten" and privacy concerns. Existing unlearning methods in centralized machine learning are not directly applicable to FL due to its distributed nature and lack of global data access \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/2310.20448)\]. To address this, researchers have proposed various approaches, such as FedEraser, which leverages historical parameter updates to reconstruct an unlearned model without requiring full retraining \[[2](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/eadeffdec9fac8fd7f9aea732ca410eb082b7dcf)\]. Similarly, methods like FRU (Federated Recommendation Unlearning) use rollback mechanisms and calibration of historical updates to efficiently remove user contributions while maintaining model performance \[[9](http://arxiv.org/abs/2209.02299)\]. Other works, such as the Bayesian Federated Unlearning (BFU) approach, introduce adaptive unlearning rates and parameter self-sharing to balance forgetting and retaining model knowledge \[[11](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/a52c3ec7325e81bd87fdf8d74bf9acfd1e1ffe5f)\]. Additionally, the concept of verifiable federated unlearning has emerged, with frameworks like VeriFi enabling users to verify the effectiveness of unlearning through marker-based checks and performance evaluation \[[7](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/06edb1f6eadb6c141d720fcca4c854578dc5d670)\]. These techniques aim to improve both the efficiency and reliability of unlearning in FL, ensuring compliance with legal and ethical standards while maintaining model utility \[[10](http://arxiv.org/abs/2401.05146)\]. The field is still evolving, with ongoing research focusing on reducing computational overhead, enhancing unlearning accuracy, and ensuring robustness against malicious attacks \[[16](http://arxiv.org/abs/2401.11018)\].

### <a id='category-privacy-preserving-unlearning-methods'></a>Privacy-Preserving Unlearning Methods: 6 papers found.
The category "Privacy-Preserving Unlearning Methods" explores the challenges and solutions in ensuring that machine learning models can effectively "forget" specific data samples while maintaining privacy and performance. This is particularly critical in federated learning (FL) systems, where data remains decentralized, and the "right to be forgotten" is increasingly mandated by legislation \[[23](http://arxiv.org/abs/2005.02205)\]. Existing unlearning methods often rely on retraining-based approaches, which are not feasible in FL due to privacy and computational constraints \[[11](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/a52c3ec7325e81bd87fdf8d74bf9acfd1e1ffe5f)\]. Additionally, many strategies are limited to convex optimization problems and fail to handle complex models like neural networks \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2203.07320)\]. To address these issues, researchers have proposed methods such as FedRecovery, which leverages differential privacy to remove the influence of specific clients without retraining, ensuring statistical indistinguishability between the unlearned and retrained models \[[7](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/06edb1f6eadb6c141d720fcca4c854578dc5d670)\]. However, unlearning can inadvertently introduce privacy risks, such as membership inference attacks that exploit differences between model versions to infer whether a data sample was part of the training set \[[23](http://arxiv.org/abs/2005.02205)\]. To mitigate these risks, new frameworks like Starfish have been introduced, using secure computation techniques to preserve privacy during unlearning \[[5](http://arxiv.org/abs/2201.09441)\]. These efforts highlight the ongoing tension between achieving effective unlearning and safeguarding user privacy in decentralized learning environments.
