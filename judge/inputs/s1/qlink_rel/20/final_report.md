## Related Works

Deep-unrolling and plug-and-play (PnP) approaches have become the de-facto standard solvers for single-pixel imaging (SPI) inverse problems \[[30](http://arxiv.org/abs/2002.09611)\]. PnP approaches are iterative algorithms where regularization is implicitly performed by an off-the-shelf deep denoiser \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/1903.08616)\]. These methods are flexible for varying compression ratios (CRs) but are limited in reconstruction accuracy and speed \[[30](http://arxiv.org/abs/2002.09611)\]. Conversely, unrolling approaches are multi-stage neural networks where a truncated iterative optimization process is transformed into an end-to-end trainable network \[[28](http://arxiv.org/abs/1712.02862)\]. These typically achieve better accuracy with faster inference but require fine-tuning or even retraining when CR changes \[[30](http://arxiv.org/abs/2002.09611)\].

The integration of deep learning with traditional iterative algorithms has been a focal point in recent research. Plug-and-play methods, which integrate advanced denoisers into proximal algorithms, have shown state-of-the-art performance in various imaging applications \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/1809.04693)\]. These methods leverage pre-trained denoisers, making them suitable for scenarios with limited training data \[[4](http://arxiv.org/abs/1905.05406)\]. However, the theoretical convergence of PnP algorithms has been a challenge, especially with non-convex regularization \[[6](http://arxiv.org/abs/2201.13256)\]. Recent work has addressed this by proposing techniques such as real spectral normalization to ensure denoisers satisfy Lipschitz conditions \[[4](http://arxiv.org/abs/1905.05406)\].

Unrolling methods, on the other hand, have been explored for their ability to capture the structure of iterative algorithms in deep networks \[[28](http://arxiv.org/abs/1712.02862)\]. These methods often require retraining when the problem or noise characteristics change \[[30](http://arxiv.org/abs/2002.09611)\]. However, some approaches have attempted to make these networks more adaptable by using modular designs that allow for varying CRs without retraining \[[28](http://arxiv.org/abs/1712.02862)\]. For instance, the MoDL framework uses a model-based deep learning architecture that explicitly accounts for the forward model, reducing the need for extensive training data \[[28](http://arxiv.org/abs/1712.02862)\].

The convergence of PnP algorithms has also been a subject of recent study. Several works have established convergence guarantees under specific conditions, such as the use of Lipschitz continuous denoisers \[[4](http://arxiv.org/abs/1905.05406)\]. Additionally, the use of gradient descent steps to train denoisers has been proposed to ensure convergence \[[15](http://arxiv.org/abs/2110.03220)\]. These approaches have been validated on various tasks, including deblurring, super-resolution, and inpainting \[[15](http://arxiv.org/abs/2110.03220)\].

### <a id='category-convergence-analysis-of-pnp-algorithms'></a>Convergence Analysis of PnP Algorithms: 10 papers found.
Plug-and-play (PnP) methods have emerged as a powerful framework for solving imaging inverse problems by integrating advanced denoisers into iterative algorithms such as ADMM or proximal gradient methods \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/1809.04693)\]. These methods replace traditional proximal operators with denoising operations, enabling the use of pre-trained denoisers like BM3D or deep learning-based models, even when data for end-to-end training is limited \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/1704.03488)\]. However, the theoretical analysis of PnP algorithms has been limited, with most convergence results assuming nonexpansive denoisers or strongly convex data-fidelity terms \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/1903.08616)\]. Recent work has addressed this gap by establishing convergence guarantees for PnP-ISTA and PnP-ADMM under certain Lipschitz conditions on the denoisers \[[4](http://arxiv.org/abs/1905.05406)\]. Additionally, researchers have proposed techniques such as real spectral normalization to train deep denoisers that satisfy these conditions \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/1704.03488)\]. The convergence of PnP algorithms has also been extended to non-convex settings, where the denoiser is modeled as a gradient descent step on a functional parameterized by a deep neural network, ensuring convergence to stationary points of an explicit global functional \[[6](http://arxiv.org/abs/2201.13256)\]. These advancements have enabled PnP methods to achieve state-of-the-art performance in applications such as image deblurring, super-resolution, and inpainting \[[6](http://arxiv.org/abs/2201.13256)\]. Furthermore, the flexibility of PnP methods allows them to handle varying compression ratios without requiring retraining, making them suitable for a wide range of imaging tasks \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/1809.04693)\]. The convergence of PnP algorithms has been further validated through numerical experiments, demonstrating their effectiveness in both theoretical and practical settings \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/1809.04693)\].

### <a id='category-pnp-approaches-and-their-limitations'></a>PnP Approaches and Their Limitations: 10 papers found.
The category "PnP Approaches and Their Limitations" highlights the growing use of plug-and-play (PnP) methods in image restoration and inverse problems, particularly in MRI and single-pixel imaging. PnP methods leverage pre-trained deep neural networks as denoisers to implicitly encode image priors, enabling flexible and effective solutions to inverse problems \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/1903.08616)\]. However, these methods face challenges such as computational inefficiency and scalability issues, especially when dealing with large-scale datasets \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/1704.03488)\]. Recent work has addressed these limitations by proposing incremental variants of PnP algorithms, such as PnP-ADMM, which improve scalability and convergence properties \[[5](http://arxiv.org/abs/2006.03224)\]. Additionally, the integration of deep convolutional neural networks (CNNs) into PnP frameworks has significantly enhanced performance, as demonstrated in tasks like deblurring, super-resolution, and demosaicing \[[8](http://arxiv.org/abs/2008.13751)\]. Despite these advances, theoretical gaps remain, particularly in characterizing the asymptotic behavior of PnP algorithms and ensuring their convergence under nonexpansive assumptions \[[5](http://arxiv.org/abs/2006.03224)\]. To address these issues, researchers have introduced frameworks like Regularization by Denoising (RED) and Consensus Equilibrium (CE), which provide more flexible and powerful alternatives to traditional PnP approaches by incorporating advanced regularization strategies and equilibrium-based formulations \[[6](http://arxiv.org/abs/2201.13256)\]\[[9](http://arxiv.org/abs/1611.02862)\]. Furthermore, the development of tuning-free PnP algorithms has enabled automatic parameter adjustment, improving the adaptability of these methods across varying imaging conditions \[[10](http://arxiv.org/abs/1611.03679)\]. Overall, while PnP methods have achieved state-of-the-art results, ongoing research continues to refine their theoretical foundations and practical implementations.
