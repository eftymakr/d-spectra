## Related Works

Deep-unrolling and plug-and-play (PnP) approaches have become the de-facto standard solvers for single-pixel imaging (SPI) inverse problems \[[30](http://arxiv.org/abs/2002.09611)\]. PnP approaches are iterative algorithms where regularization is implicitly performed by an off-the-shelf deep denoiser \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/1903.08616)\]. These methods are flexible for varying compression ratios (CRs) but are limited in reconstruction accuracy and speed \[[30](http://arxiv.org/abs/2002.09611)\]. Conversely, unrolling approaches are multi-stage neural networks where a truncated iterative optimization process is transformed into an end-to-end trainable network \[[28](http://arxiv.org/abs/1712.02862)\]. These typically achieve better accuracy with faster inference but require fine-tuning or even retraining when CR changes \[[30](http://arxiv.org/abs/2002.09611)\].

The integration of deep learning with traditional iterative algorithms has been a focal point in recent research. Plug-and-play methods, which integrate advanced denoisers into proximal algorithms, have shown state-of-the-art performance in various imaging applications \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/1809.04693)\]. These methods leverage pre-trained denoisers, making them suitable for scenarios with limited training data \[[4](http://arxiv.org/abs/1905.05406)\]. However, the theoretical convergence of PnP algorithms has been a challenge, especially with non-convex regularization \[[6](http://arxiv.org/abs/2201.13256)\]. Recent work has addressed this by proposing techniques such as real spectral normalization to ensure denoisers satisfy Lipschitz conditions \[[4](http://arxiv.org/abs/1905.05406)\].

Unrolling methods, on the other hand, have been explored for their ability to capture the structure of iterative algorithms in deep networks \[[28](http://arxiv.org/abs/1712.02862)\]. These methods often require retraining when the problem or noise characteristics change \[[30](http://arxiv.org/abs/2002.09611)\]. However, some approaches have attempted to make these networks more adaptable by using modular designs that allow for varying CRs without retraining \[[28](http://arxiv.org/abs/1712.02862)\]. For instance, the MoDL framework uses a model-based deep learning architecture that explicitly accounts for the forward model, reducing the need for extensive training data \[[28](http://arxiv.org/abs/1712.02862)\].

The convergence of PnP algorithms has also been a subject of recent study. Several works have established convergence guarantees under specific conditions, such as the use of Lipschitz continuous denoisers \[[4](http://arxiv.org/abs/1905.05406)\]. Additionally, the use of gradient descent steps to train denoisers has been proposed to ensure convergence \[[15](http://arxiv.org/abs/2110.03220)\]. These approaches have been validated on various tasks, including deblurring, super-resolution, and inpainting \[[15](http://arxiv.org/abs/2110.03220)\].
