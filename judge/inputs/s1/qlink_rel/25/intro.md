## Related Works

The integration of knowledge graphs (KGs) into large language models (LLMs) to enhance reasoning capabilities has gained significant attention in recent years. While LLMs have demonstrated strong zero-shot reasoning abilities \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/2205.11916)\], they often struggle with multi-step reasoning and leveraging structured knowledge from KGs without adaptation \[[18](http://arxiv.org/abs/2212.10511)\]. This limitation highlights the need for frameworks that can effectively combine the strengths of LLMs with the rich, semantically structured information provided by KGs.

Several approaches have explored the use of KGs for fact-checking and claim verification. For instance, the work by \[[10](http://arxiv.org/abs/1905.07129)\] introduces ERNIE, a model that integrates both textual corpora and KGs to enhance language representation, achieving improvements on knowledge-driven tasks. Similarly, \[[19](http://arxiv.org/abs/2004.14974)\] presents SciFact, a dataset and baseline models for scientific claim verification, demonstrating the importance of evidence-based reasoning. However, these methods primarily rely on unstructured text corpora, limiting their ability to fully leverage the structured knowledge available in KGs \[[19](http://arxiv.org/abs/2004.14974)\].

To address these limitations, \[[17](http://arxiv.org/abs/2312.10997)\] reviews Retrieval-Augmented Generation (RAG) as a promising approach, combining LLMs with external databases to enhance accuracy and credibility for knowledge-intensive tasks. This aligns with the idea of using KGs to provide additional context and structured knowledge. Additionally, \[[18](http://arxiv.org/abs/2212.10511)\] investigates the effectiveness of parametric and non-parametric memories in LLMs, finding that retrieval augmentation significantly improves performance on less popular factual knowledge, which is critical for claim verification.

The concept of using KGs for multi-document question answering (MD-QA) has also been explored. \[[8](http://arxiv.org/abs/2308.11730)\] proposes Knowledge Graph Prompting (KGP), which constructs a graph over multiple documents to guide LLMs in MD-QA. This approach highlights the potential of KGs in structuring and organizing information for more accurate and interpretable reasoning. Furthermore, \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2005.11401)\] explores RAG models that combine pre-trained seq2seq models with dense vector indexes of Wikipedia, demonstrating the effectiveness of integrating external knowledge into LLMs for open-domain QA tasks.

In the realm of fact-checking, \[[15](http://arxiv.org/abs/2010.09926)\] presents an explainable fact-checking framework for public health claims, emphasizing the need for transparency and justification in verification processes. \[[20](http://arxiv.org/abs/2310.05253)\] introduces FOLK, a reasoning framework that leverages LLMs to translate claims into first-order logic (FOL) clauses and perform knowledge-grounded reasoning without relying on annotated evidence.
