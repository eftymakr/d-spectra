## Related Works

Vision Transformers (ViTs) have emerged as a powerful alternative to convolutional neural networks (CNNs) in computer vision tasks, leveraging the transformer architecture to effectively capture global context \[[6](http://arxiv.org/abs/2010.11929)\]. However, the deployment of ViTs on hardware platforms, especially FPGAs, remains challenging due to the complex arithmetic operations such as Softmax and LayerNorm, which are sensitive to model accuracy and difficult to map onto efficient hardware with low precision \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2208.05163)\]. Prior work has primarily focused on optimizing matrix multiplication operations on hardware while keeping these complex operations on the CPU, resulting in suboptimal performance due to communication overhead between the CPU and accelerator \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2208.05163)\].

Several studies have explored hardware acceleration for ViTs. Auto-ViT-Acc introduces an FPGA-aware automatic acceleration framework based on mixed-scheme quantization, achieving a 5.6x improvement in frame rate with only a 0.71% accuracy drop \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2208.05163)\]. VAQF proposes a fully automatic framework for low-bit ViT acceleration on FPGAs, enabling real-time execution with 8-bit or 6-bit activation quantization \[[4](http://arxiv.org/abs/2201.06618)\]. Similarly, HeatViT introduces hardware-efficient adaptive token pruning and 8-bit fixed-point quantization, achieving significant computation reduction while maintaining high accuracy \[[7](http://arxiv.org/abs/2211.08110)\]. These approaches, however, primarily focus on quantization and pruning without fully mapping all operations onto hardware.

The design of efficient hardware accelerators for ViTs has also been addressed by other works. CvT introduces convolutions into ViTs to improve performance and efficiency, combining the strengths of CNNs and ViTs \[[8](http://arxiv.org/abs/2103.15808)\]. MobileViT further combines CNNs and ViTs to create a lightweight, mobile-friendly architecture for vision tasks \[[13](http://arxiv.org/abs/2110.02178)\]. Swin Transformer introduces a hierarchical design with shifted windows, enabling efficient processing of high-resolution images \[[15](http://arxiv.org/abs/2103.14030)\]. These models demonstrate the potential of hybrid architectures but do not fully address the challenge of mapping all operations onto hardware for optimal performance.

Recent efforts have also focused on reducing the computational cost of ViTs. Patch Slimming identifies and removes redundant patches in a top-down manner, reducing FLOPs by over 45% without significant accuracy loss \[[9](http://arxiv.org/abs/2106.02852)\]. T2T-ViT improves the tokenization process, leading to a more efficient model with reduced parameter count and MACs \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/2101.11986)\]. These methods highlight the importance of efficient model design but do not directly address the hardware acceleration challenge.

### <a id='category-fpga-based-acceleration-frameworks'></a>FPGA-Based Acceleration Frameworks: 3 papers found.
Vision Transformer (ViT) hardware acceleration has been a focal point in recent research, with several frameworks aiming to address the challenges posed by ViTs' complex architecture and high computational demands. Existing approaches often rely on FPGA-based accelerators that focus on parallelizing matrix multiplication operations while offloading complex operations like Softmax and LayerNorm to the CPU, leading to suboptimal performance due to CPU-accelerator communication overhead \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2208.05163)\]. To overcome this, recent works have explored fully FPGA-based acceleration frameworks that map all ViT operations onto hardware, improving both performance and efficiency. For instance, one framework introduces a mixed-scheme quantization method to enhance accuracy while maintaining high frame rates, achieving up to 5.6x speedup on the ImageNet dataset with minimal accuracy loss \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2208.05163)\]. Another work proposes VAQF, an automatic quantization framework that integrates binary weights and low-precision activations, enabling real-time execution of ViTs on FPGAs by satisfying frame rate targets with 8-bit or 6-bit activation quantization \[[4](http://arxiv.org/abs/2201.06618)\]. Additionally, HeatViT introduces an image-adaptive token pruning technique combined with 8-bit fixed-point quantization and polynomial approximations to reduce computation while preserving accuracy, achieving significant speedups on various ViT models \[[7](http://arxiv.org/abs/2211.08110)\]. These approaches collectively highlight the trend toward fully hardware-aware ViT acceleration, emphasizing the importance of quantization strategies, efficient hardware design, and the integration of complex operations directly onto FPGAs to minimize communication overhead and improve overall performance.

### <a id='category-vision-transformer-(vit)-overview-and-comparison-to-cnns'></a>Vision Transformer (ViT) Overview and Comparison to CNNs: 6 papers found.
Vision Transformer (ViT) models have been extensively studied for their ability to capture global context in image classification tasks, demonstrating strong performance compared to traditional convolutional neural networks (CNNs) \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/2210.09573)\]. However, ViTs face challenges in hardware acceleration due to complex arithmetic operations like Softmax and LayerNorm, which are difficult to map onto low-precision hardware \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2208.05163)\]. To address this, researchers have proposed various improvements, such as T2T-ViT, which introduces a Tokens-to-Token transformation to better model local structures and reduce computational overhead \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2208.05163)\]. Additionally, LeViT combines attention-based architectures with principles from CNNs, optimizing the speed-accuracy trade-off for fast inference \[[5](http://arxiv.org/abs/2104.01136)\]. Similarly, CrossViT enhances ViT by using dual-branch transformers to process image patches of different sizes, improving feature representation through cross-attention mechanisms \[[4](http://arxiv.org/abs/2201.06618)\]. The Pyramid Vision Transformer (PVT) further extends ViT's applicability to dense prediction tasks by enabling high-resolution outputs and efficient computation through a progressive shrinking pyramid \[[5](http://arxiv.org/abs/2104.01136)\]. Meanwhile, PiT incorporates CNN-inspired spatial dimension reduction into ViT, improving model capability and generalization \[[6](http://arxiv.org/abs/2010.11929)\]. These advancements highlight ongoing efforts to refine ViT architectures, enhance their efficiency, and expand their applicability to diverse vision tasks.

[Snippet]: «Vision Transformers (ViTs) have achieved state-of-the-art performance on various vision tasks. However, ViTs’ self-attention module is still arguably a major bottleneck, limiting their achievable hardware efficiency and more extensive applications to resource constrained platforms. Meanwhile, existing accelerators dedicated to NLP Transformers are not optimal for ViTs. This is because there is a large difference between ViTs and Transformers for natural language processing (NLP) tasks: ViTs have a relatively fixed number of input tokens, whose attention maps can be pruned by up to 90% even with fixed sparse patterns, without severely hurting the model accuracy (e.g., <=1.5% under 90% pruning ratio); while NLP Transformers need to handle input sequences of varying numbers of tokens and rely on on-the-fly predictions of dynamic sparse attention patterns for each input to achieve a decent sparsity (e.g., >=50%). To this end, we propose a dedicated algorithm and accelerator co-design framework dubbed ViTCoD for accelerating ViTs. Specifically, on the algorithm level, ViTCoD prunes and polarizes the attention maps to have either denser or sparser fixed patterns for regularizing two levels of workloads without hurting the accuracy, largely reducing the attention computations while leaving room for alleviating the remaining dominant data movements; on top of that, we further integrate a lightweight and learnable auto-encoder module to enable trading the dominant high-cost data movements for lower-cost computations. On the hardware level, we develop a dedicated accelerator to simultaneously coordinate the aforementioned enforced denser and sparser workloads for boosted hardware utilization, while integrating on-chip encoder and decoder engines to leverage ViTCoD’s algorithm pipeline for much reduced data movements. Extensive experiments and ablation studies validate that ViTCoD largely reduces the dominant data movement costs, achieving speedups of up to 235.3×, 142.9×, 86.0×, 10.1×, and 6.8× over general computing platforms CPUs, EdgeGPUs, GPUs, and prior-art Transformer accelerators SpAtten and Sanger under an attention sparsity of 90%, respectively. ### <a id='category-quantization-and-pruning-techniques-for-vits'></a>Quantization and Pruning Techniques for ViTs: 2 papers found.
Vision Transformers (ViTs) have emerged as a powerful architecture for computer vision tasks, leveraging the transformer's ability to capture global context \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2208.05163)\]. However, deploying ViTs on edge devices remains challenging due to their high computational and storage demands \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2208.05163)\]. Quantization techniques have been proposed to reduce model complexity and enable efficient inference, but traditional methods often struggle with non-linear operations such as Softmax and LayerNorm, which are critical for ViT performance \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2208.05163)\]. Recent work has focused on developing integer-only quantization schemes to eliminate floating-point operations, improving both efficiency and hardware compatibility \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2208.05163)\]. For instance, I-ViT introduces integer-only arithmetic methods like Shiftmax and ShiftGELU to approximate non-linear operations, achieving comparable accuracy to full-precision models \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2208.05163)\]. Similarly, FQ-ViT addresses inter-channel variation in LayerNorm inputs and proposes Log-Int-Softmax (LIS) to sustain performance with 4-bit quantization \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/2101.11986)\]. These approaches highlight the growing interest in quantization techniques tailored for ViTs, aiming to balance accuracy with computational efficiency \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2208.05163)\]\[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/2101.11986)\].
