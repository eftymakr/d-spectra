## Related Works

Maximum Inner Product Search (MIPS) has been a central challenge in machine learning and information retrieval, particularly in high-dimensional data applications \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/1610.03317)\]. Existing approaches to MIPS typically fall into two categories: those that rely solely on inner product (IP) similarity, which can suffer from local optima and redundant computations, and those that reduce the MIPS problem to nearest neighbor search under the Euclidean metric through space projection, which may lead to topology destruction and information loss \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/1610.03317)\]. However, recent works have demonstrated that the two paradigms are not inherently mutually exclusive, and that integrating IP and Euclidean metrics in indexing and search algorithms can significantly enhance MIPS performance \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/1610.03317)\].

A greedy approach for budgeted MIPS has been proposed, which yields superior performance compared to state-of-the-art approaches \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/1610.03317)\]. This method, called Greedy-MIPS, is designed to handle budgeted MIPS by carefully studying the problem structure and provides a simple yet effective solution \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/1610.03317)\]. Additionally, asymmetric LSH (ALSH) has been introduced as a provably sublinear time algorithm for approximate MIPS, enabling efficient sublinear hashing schemes for MIPS by converting inner products into near neighbor search problems \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/1405.5869)\].

The problem of efficiently finding the best match for a query in a given set with respect to the inner product has also been explored using branch-and-bound algorithms and novel data structures like the cone tree \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/1202.6101)\]. These methods have shown significant improvements in query time over naive search techniques, with some cases exhibiting up to five orders of magnitude improvement \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/1202.6101)\]. Furthermore, the use of proximity graphs for MIPS has gained traction, with the inner-product navigable small world graph (ip-NSW) achieving an order of magnitude speedup over the fastest baseline \[[4](http://arxiv.org/abs/1909.13459)\].

Norm-Ranging LSH (NORM-RANGING LSH) has been proposed to address the performance issues of SIMPLE-LSH by partitioning datasets into sub-datasets and building hash indexes independently, which reduces the number of probes required for the same recall \[[5](http://arxiv.org/abs/1809.08782)\]. Similarly, quantization-based approaches have been introduced to enable fast approximate MIPS by quantizing database vectors in multiple subspaces and approximating inner products as sums of inner products with subspace quantizers \[[6](http://arxiv.org/abs/1509.01469)\].

### <a id='category-inner-product-similarity-methods'></a>Inner Product Similarity Methods: 10 papers found.
The category "Inner Product Similarity Methods" encompasses a range of approaches aimed at improving the efficiency and accuracy of Maximum Inner Product Search (MIPS), a critical task in high-dimensional data applications. Early work focused on developing branch-and-bound algorithms and dual-tree structures to address the computational challenges of exact MIPS \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/1202.6101)\]. More recently, researchers have explored approximate methods, such as quantization-based approaches that minimize inner product quantization error by decomposing it into norm and direction components \[[10](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/d8aad5d9c2c336c550ca9621b73c7688f184d50b)\]. These methods leverage the observation that norm errors have a more significant impact on inner product accuracy than direction errors. Additionally, asymmetric transformations have been proposed to convert MIPS into more tractable problems like approximate nearest neighbor search or cosine similarity search, enabling the use of techniques such as Locality-Sensitive Hashing (LSH) \[[6](http://arxiv.org/abs/1509.01469)\]\[[7](http://arxiv.org/abs/1410.5410)\]. However, these transformations often introduce distortion errors, prompting further innovations like the Homocentric Hypersphere partition (H2-ALSH) which reduces distortion through query normalization and efficient pruning \[[6](http://arxiv.org/abs/1509.01469)\]. Furthermore, hardware-efficient brute-force approaches, such as blocked matrix multiply (BMM), and novel indexing structures like MAX-IMUS, have been developed to improve performance by leveraging hardware capabilities and pruning techniques \[[9](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/0b2790c2c1e23e5535421696e1ec763bbc651bbd)\]. These methods highlight the ongoing efforts to balance computational efficiency with search accuracy in MIPS, often through hybrid strategies that integrate insights from both inner product and Euclidean metrics \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/1202.6101)\]\[[6](http://arxiv.org/abs/1509.01469)\]\[[9](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/0b2790c2c1e23e5535421696e1ec763bbc651bbd)\].

### <a id='category-graph-based-search-algorithms'></a>Graph-Based Search Algorithms: 3 papers found.
Graph-based search algorithms have emerged as a powerful approach for efficient Maximum Inner Product Search (MIPS), particularly in high-dimensional data applications \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/1202.6101)\]. These methods leverage graph structures to approximate the inner product similarity, offering significant speedups over traditional approaches \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/1610.03317)\]. The inner-product navigable small world graph (ip-NSW) is a state-of-the-art method that achieves an order of magnitude speedup by exploiting the norm bias in MIPS, where large norm items dominate the search process \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/1610.03317)\]. However, ip-NSW's performance is further enhanced by the introduction of the ip-NSW+ algorithm, which combines angular and inner-product proximity graphs to improve search efficiency and robustness \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/1610.03317)\]. Similarly, the norm adjusted proximity graph (NAPG) addresses the challenge of constructing meaningful graph connections by incorporating adjusting factors based on sampled data, leading to more effective inner product search \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/1202.6101)\]. These advancements highlight the growing importance of graph-based methods in MIPS, offering competitive performance and adaptability across diverse data distributions \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/1610.03317)\]\[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/1202.6101)\].

### <a id='category-efficient-indexing-strategies'></a>Efficient Indexing Strategies: 2 papers found.
Efficient indexing strategies for Maximum Inner Product Search (MIPS) have been a focal point in recent research, particularly due to the challenges posed by high-dimensional data and the need for scalable solutions. One notable approach is SIMPLE-LSH, which has been considered state-of-the-art for MIPS but suffers from performance degradation due to long-tailed 2-norm distributions in real datasets \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/1610.03317)\]. To address this, NORM-RANGING LSH was proposed, introducing dataset partitioning to mitigate the normalization issue and achieve lower query time complexity under mild conditions \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/1610.03317)\]. Additionally, the problem of high index space consumption and excessive disk page accesses in LSH-based methods has been tackled by a lightweight iDistance index, which uses 2-stable random projections to reduce dimensionality while maintaining accuracy guarantees for c-Approximate Maximum Inner Product (c-AMIP) search \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/1405.5869)\]. These methods highlight the ongoing efforts to balance efficiency and accuracy in MIPS, with recent advances focusing on adaptive indexing and probabilistic guarantees.
