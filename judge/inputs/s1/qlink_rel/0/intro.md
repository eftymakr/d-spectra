## Related Works

Traditional approaches to addressing economic inequality, such as the U.S. federal income tax system, rely on static rules that lack adaptability to changing economic conditions and taxpayer behaviors \[[15](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/9230b3c0d79aeac2eef4742addaa5001f4842bde)\]. While models like the Saez Optimal Taxation framework attempt to dynamically adjust tax policies, they often fail to account for the heterogeneity of taxpayers and irrational decision-making patterns \[[15](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/9230b3c0d79aeac2eef4742addaa5001f4842bde)\]. This limitation highlights the need for more sophisticated models that can simulate complex human behaviors and adapt to evolving economic scenarios.

Agent-based modeling (ABM) has emerged as a promising tool for simulating economic systems by representing agents (e.g., households, firms) with heterogeneous characteristics and decision-making rules \[[19](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/236be94b54e930786a9ef21a8f0957853537d65f)\]. These models allow for the exploration of emergent phenomena and the evaluation of policy interventions in multi-agent environments \[[12](http://arxiv.org/abs/2006.05048)\]. However, most ABM approaches rely on heuristic decision rules or regression-based behavioral models, which may not fully capture the complexity of human decision-making \[[12](http://arxiv.org/abs/2006.05048)\]. Recent work has explored the use of reinforcement learning (RL) to improve the accuracy and adaptability of agent behavioral models in policy-focused ABMs \[[12](http://arxiv.org/abs/2006.05048)\].

Large language models (LLMs) have also shown potential in enhancing the reasoning and decision-making capabilities of AI systems \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2309.02144)\]. For instance, fine-tuning LLMs on chain-of-thought (COT) reasoning data has been shown to improve their reasoning abilities, although they often suffer from assessment misalignment, where they assign higher scores to subpar COTs \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2309.02144)\]. To address this, researchers have proposed alignment fine-tuning (AFT) methods that incorporate constraint alignment loss to ensure high-quality reasoning outputs \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2309.02144)\]. Additionally, the ReAct framework synergizes reasoning and acting in LLMs by generating reasoning traces and task-specific actions in an interleaved manner, improving interpretability and performance on tasks like question answering and fact verification \[[8](http://arxiv.org/abs/2210.03629)\].

The integration of LLMs with ABMs, as proposed in the TaxAgent model, represents a novel approach to designing adaptive tax policies. This integration allows for the simulation of heterogeneous taxpayer behaviors while enabling the government agent to iteratively optimize tax rates using LLMs \[[15](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/9230b3c0d79aeac2eef4742addaa5001f4842bde)\]. This approach builds on earlier work that explored the use of RL for agent behavioral models in policy simulations \[[12](http://arxiv.org/abs/2006.05048)\], and aligns with broader efforts to enhance the reasoning and decision-making capabilities of AI systems through techniques like few-shot learning and in-context learning \[[10](http://arxiv.org/abs/2005.14165)\].
