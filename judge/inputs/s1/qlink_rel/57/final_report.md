## Related Works

Generative retrieval (GR) has recently emerged as a promising paradigm in information retrieval (IR), shifting the focus from traditional index-retrieve pipelines to end-to-end document retrieval through sequence-to-sequence models \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2308.14968)\]. Unlike conventional methods that rely on precomputed indices, GR models directly generate document identifiers (docids) for a given query, enabling more flexible and semantically rich retrieval \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/2206.02743)\]. However, most existing GR models are primarily evaluated on static document collections, with limited exploration of their performance on dynamic corpora where new documents are continuously added \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/2202.06991)\]. This paper addresses this gap by systematically evaluating various GR approaches over dynamic corpora and identifying key design considerations for improving performance in such settings.

Several recent works have explored the use of GR models for document retrieval. For instance, the Differentiable Search Index (DSI) encodes all documents into a single model, allowing direct query-to-docid generation without the need for external indices \[[4](http://arxiv.org/abs/2206.10128)\]. Similarly, the Neural Corpus Indexer (NCI) proposes a sequence-to-sequence network that generates docids directly for a query, achieving significant improvements in recall performance \[[5](http://arxiv.org/abs/2304.04171)\]. The DSI framework has also been extended with query generation techniques, such as DSI-QG, which addresses data distribution mismatches between indexing and retrieval phases \[[6](http://arxiv.org/abs/2208.07652)\]. These approaches highlight the potential of GR models to simplify the retrieval process while maintaining high performance.

However, the effectiveness of GR models in dynamic settings remains underexplored. The paper under review demonstrates that GR models with text-based docids show superior generalization to unseen documents, particularly when the docid design is fine-grained \[[7](http://arxiv.org/abs/2305.11841)\]. This aligns with findings from the GenRet method, which learns to tokenize documents into short discrete representations (docids) via a discrete auto-encoding approach, achieving state-of-the-art results on benchmark datasets \[[8](http://arxiv.org/abs/2311.09134)\]. In contrast, numeric-based docids, while efficient, suffer from performance degradation in dynamic settings due to their tendency to overfit to the initial document set \[[9](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/6682a3679a17d090830635f1c420126d20eae54a)\]. ### <a id='category-generative-retrieval-(gr)-overview'></a>Generative Retrieval (GR) Overview: 9 papers found.
Generative retrieval (GR) has gained significant attention as a novel paradigm in information retrieval, shifting from traditional index-based methods to end-to-end models that generate document identifiers (docids) directly \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2308.14968)\]. Unlike conventional approaches that rely on static document collections, GR models face challenges in dynamic environments where new documents are continuously added \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2308.14968)\]. This has led to the development of continual learning frameworks, such as CLEVER, which enable incremental indexing and memory-augmented learning to preserve performance on both old and new documents \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2308.14968)\]. Additionally, GR models like GenRet introduce discrete auto-encoding techniques to learn semantic docids, improving retrieval accuracy and generalization to unseen documents \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/2206.02743)\]. The importance of fine-grained docid design is further emphasized, as models with text-based docids demonstrate superior performance over dynamic corpora, leveraging semantic alignment with pre-trained language models and lexical diversity \[[9](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/6682a3679a17d090830635f1c420126d20eae54a)\]. In contrast, numeric-based docids, while efficient, suffer from overfitting and poor generalization due to their strong dependency on initial training data \[[9](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/6682a3679a17d090830635f1c420126d20eae54a)\]. To address these limitations, researchers have proposed hybrid approaches, such as the multi-docid design, which combines the strengths of both text-based and numeric-based docids to achieve better performance in dynamic settings without requiring additional retraining \[[9](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/6682a3679a17d090830635f1c420126d20eae54a)\]. These advancements highlight the evolving landscape of GR, emphasizing the need for models that balance efficiency with adaptability in real-world search engines \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2308.14968)\]\[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/2206.02743)\]\[[9](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/6682a3679a17d090830635f1c420126d20eae54a)\].

Unlike traditional retrieval architectures where index and retrieval are two different and separate components, DSI uses a single transformer model to perform both indexing and retrieval. In this paper, we identify and tackle an important issue of current DSI models: the data distribution mismatch that occurs between the DSI indexing and retrieval processes. The presence of these queries at indexing allows the DSI models to connect a document identifier to a set of queries, hence mitigating data distribution mismatches present between the indexing and the retrieval phases. Empirical results on popular mono-lingual and cross-lingual passage retrieval datasets show that DSI-QG significantly outperforms the original DSI model.»

### <a id='category-fine-grained-docid-design'></a>Fine-Grained DocID Design: 4 papers found.
The category "Fine-Grained DocID Design" explores advancements in document identifier (docid) generation for generative retrieval models, emphasizing the importance of semantic alignment, fine-grained representation, and adaptability to dynamic corpora. Recent approaches, such as Neural Optimized VOcabularial (NOVO) docids, focus on generating unique n-gram sets that can be optimized through training to better capture document semantics and relationships \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2308.14968)\]. Similarly, the Differentiable Search Index (DSI) paradigm introduces a sequence-to-sequence model that directly maps queries to document identifiers, parameterizing traditional index-retrieval pipelines within a single neural model \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/2206.02743)\]. This approach addresses the limitations of static docids by enabling the model to learn associations between documents and their identifiers during training. Another method, PAG, introduces a set-based and sequential identifier design, combining lexical tokens with relevance-based representations to improve retrieval performance \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/2202.06991)\]. These methods highlight the shift from static, numeric-based identifiers to more flexible, semantically rich docid designs that enhance generalization over dynamic corpora. Furthermore, research on autoregressive generation of document identifiers, such as in SEAL, demonstrates the effectiveness of using all ngrams in a passage as potential identifiers, allowing for more interpretable and efficient retrieval \[[4](http://arxiv.org/abs/2206.10128)\]. These studies collectively underscore the importance of fine-grained docid design in improving retrieval performance, especially in evolving document collections, while balancing efficiency and semantic richness \[[5](http://arxiv.org/abs/2304.04171)\].

[Snippet]: «Differentiable Search Indices (DSIs) encode a corpus of documents in model parameters and use the same model to answer user queries directly. Despite the strong performance of DSI models, deploying them in situations where the corpus changes over time is computationally expensive because reindexing the corpus requires re-training the model. In this work, we introduce DSI++, a continual learning challenge for DSI to incrementally index new documents while being able to answer queries related to both previously and newly indexed documents. Across different model scales and document identifier representations, we show that continual indexing of new documents leads to considerable forgetting of previously indexed documents. We also hypothesize and verify that the model experiences forgetting events during training, leading to unstable learning. To mitigate these issues, we investigate two approaches. The first focuses on modifying the training dynamics. Flatter minima implicitly alleviate forgetting, so we optimize for flatter loss basins and show that the model stably memorizes more documents ($+12\%$). Next, we introduce a generative memory to sample pseudo-queries for documents and supplement them during continual indexing to prevent forgetting for the retrieval task. Extensive experiments on novel continual indexing benchmarks based on Natural Questions (NQ) and MS MARCO demonstrate that our proposed solution mitigates forgetting significantly. Concretely, it improves the average Hits@10 by $+21.1\%$ over competitive baselines for NQ and requires $6$ times fewer model updates compared to re-training the DSI model for incrementally indexing five corpora in a sequence.»
