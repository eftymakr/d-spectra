## Related Works

Generative retrieval (GR) has recently emerged as a promising paradigm in information retrieval (IR), shifting the focus from traditional index-retrieve pipelines to end-to-end document retrieval through sequence-to-sequence models \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2308.14968)\]. Unlike conventional methods that rely on precomputed indices, GR models directly generate document identifiers (docids) for a given query, enabling more flexible and semantically rich retrieval \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/2206.02743)\]. However, most existing GR models are primarily evaluated on static document collections, with limited exploration of their performance on dynamic corpora where new documents are continuously added \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/2202.06991)\]. This paper addresses this gap by systematically evaluating various GR approaches over dynamic corpora and identifying key design considerations for improving performance in such settings.

Several recent works have explored the use of GR models for document retrieval. For instance, the Differentiable Search Index (DSI) encodes all documents into a single model, allowing direct query-to-docid generation without the need for external indices \[[4](http://arxiv.org/abs/2206.10128)\]. Similarly, the Neural Corpus Indexer (NCI) proposes a sequence-to-sequence network that generates docids directly for a query, achieving significant improvements in recall performance \[[5](http://arxiv.org/abs/2304.04171)\]. The DSI framework has also been extended with query generation techniques, such as DSI-QG, which addresses data distribution mismatches between indexing and retrieval phases \[[6](http://arxiv.org/abs/2208.07652)\]. These approaches highlight the potential of GR models to simplify the retrieval process while maintaining high performance.

However, the effectiveness of GR models in dynamic settings remains underexplored. The paper under review demonstrates that GR models with text-based docids show superior generalization to unseen documents, particularly when the docid design is fine-grained \[[7](http://arxiv.org/abs/2305.11841)\]. This aligns with findings from the GenRet method, which learns to tokenize documents into short discrete representations (docids) via a discrete auto-encoding approach, achieving state-of-the-art results on benchmark datasets \[[8](http://arxiv.org/abs/2311.09134)\]. In contrast, numeric-based docids, while efficient, suffer from performance degradation in dynamic settings due to their tendency to overfit to the initial document set \[[9](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/6682a3679a17d090830635f1c420126d20eae54a)\]. This issue is further exacerbated in cross-lingual retrieval scenarios, where document and query texts are in different languages \[[10](http://arxiv.org/abs/2208.09257)\].

To address these challenges, the paper proposes a novel multi-docid design that combines the efficiency of numeric-based docids with the effectiveness of text-based docids, achieving improved performance without requiring additional retraining \[[11](http://arxiv.org/abs/2212.09744)\].
