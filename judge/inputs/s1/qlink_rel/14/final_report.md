## Related Works

Video super-resolution (VSR) has been a focal point in computer vision, aiming to enhance the spatial resolution of low-resolution (LR) videos to produce high-resolution (HR) counterparts while maintaining temporal consistency \[[1](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/c6a8ff4d9dcdf9e5d82310b847af999ed6655f65)\]. Early approaches relied on explicit motion estimation and compensation to align frames \[[1](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/c6a8ff4d9dcdf9e5d82310b847af999ed6655f65)\], but these methods were computationally intensive and prone to inaccuracies \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/1801.04590)\]. More recent methods have leveraged deep learning, particularly convolutional neural networks (CNNs), to improve performance \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/2201.12288)\]. However, these methods often require complex motion compensation strategies, which can introduce artifacts and increase computational costs \[[4](http://arxiv.org/abs/1611.05250)\].

The advent of recurrent neural networks (RNNs) has enabled more efficient temporal modeling in VSR by leveraging hidden states to propagate information across frames \[[5](http://arxiv.org/abs/1812.02898)\]. For instance, the BasicVSR framework introduced bidirectional propagation and feature alignment to exploit spatiotemporal information effectively \[[6](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/755227d7ea41b1d90df31f411f9ecbc998c20d26)\]. This approach significantly improved the temporal consistency of the output while reducing computational overhead \[[7](http://arxiv.org/abs/1909.08080)\]. Building on this, BasicVSR++ further enhanced performance by introducing second-order grid propagation and flow-guided deformable alignment, achieving a 0.82 dB improvement in PSNR over BasicVSR \[[8](http://arxiv.org/abs/1905.02716)\].

Transformers have also been explored for VSR, offering parallel processing capabilities for sequence modeling \[[9](http://arxiv.org/abs/1707.02921)\]. However, traditional Transformer architectures with fully connected self-attention layers lack the ability to exploit spatial-temporal locality effectively \[[10](http://arxiv.org/abs/2111.12704)\]. To address this, the Video Super-Resolution Transformer (VSR-Transformer) introduced a spatial-temporal convolutional self-attention layer and a bidirectional optical flow-based feed-forward layer to improve feature alignment and correlation discovery \[[11](http://arxiv.org/abs/2007.10595)\]. These innovations enabled better performance on benchmark datasets \[[12](http://arxiv.org/abs/2008.05765)\].

Diffusion models have emerged as a powerful tool for image and video restoration due to their ability to generate high-quality details \[[13](http://arxiv.org/abs/2007.11803)\]. However, their inherent randomness poses challenges for VSR, particularly in ensuring temporal consistency \[[14](http://arxiv.org/abs/2008.00455)\]. To mitigate this, the Upscale-A-Video framework introduced a text-guided latent diffusion model that ensures temporal coherence through local and global mechanisms \[[15](http://arxiv.org/abs/1501.00092)\]. This approach allows for greater flexibility in balancing restoration and generation while maintaining visual realism \[[16](http://arxiv.org/abs/2104.13371)\].

Recent efforts have focused on improving the robustness of VSR models in real-world scenarios, where degradation processes are complex and unpredictable \[[17](http://arxiv.org/abs/1511.04587)\]. The RealVSR dataset was introduced to bridge the gap between synthetic and real-world VSR tasks by capturing LR-HR video pairs using a multi-camera system \[[18](http://arxiv.org/abs/2206.02146)\].

### <a id='category-deep-learning-approaches-in-vsr'></a>Deep Learning Approaches in VSR: 15 papers found.
Video super-resolution (VSR) has seen significant advancements through deep learning approaches, focusing on improving temporal consistency, reducing artifacts, and leveraging spatio-temporal information effectively. Early methods relied heavily on motion estimation and compensation, which often introduced errors and computational overhead \[[1](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/c6a8ff4d9dcdf9e5d82310b847af999ed6655f65)\]. More recent works, such as the Video Restoration Transformer (VRT), have introduced parallel frame prediction capabilities using temporal reciprocal self attention (TRSA) and parallel warping, enabling cross-clip interactions and improved feature fusion \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/1801.04590)\]. Similarly, the Temporally-Deformable Alignment Network (TDAN) addresses temporal alignment challenges by aligning reference and supporting frames at the feature level without optical flow computation, thereby reducing artifacts and improving alignment accuracy \[[4](http://arxiv.org/abs/1611.05250)\]. The EDVR framework further enhances VSR by introducing a Pyramid, Cascading, and Deformable (PCD) alignment module and a Temporal and Spatial Attention (TSA) fusion module, which effectively handle large motions and diverse frame characteristics \[[6](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/755227d7ea41b1d90df31f411f9ecbc998c20d26)\]. Additionally, the Progressive Fusion Network (PFN) and Recurrent Residual Network (RRN) have demonstrated efficiency and effectiveness in temporal modeling, with PFN improving performance through progressive fusion strategies and RRN stabilizing training using residual learning \[[5](http://arxiv.org/abs/1812.02898)\]\[[9](http://arxiv.org/abs/1707.02921)\]. The BasicVSR framework simplifies VSR pipelines by focusing on essential components like propagation, alignment, aggregation, and upscaling, achieving competitive results with a lightweight design \[[13](http://arxiv.org/abs/2007.11803)\]. These methods collectively highlight the shift from traditional motion-based approaches to more efficient and accurate deep learning models that incorporate attention mechanisms, deformable convolutions, and self-supervised learning to enhance VSR performance.

### <a id='category-recurrent-neural-networks-in-vsr'></a>Recurrent Neural Networks in VSR: 8 papers found.
Recent advances in video super-resolution (VSR) have focused on leveraging recurrent neural networks (RNNs) to improve temporal consistency and computational efficiency. Traditional methods often process each frame independently, leading to inconsistencies and high computational costs \[[1](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/c6a8ff4d9dcdf9e5d82310b847af999ed6655f65)\]. To address these limitations, researchers have proposed recurrent frameworks that utilize hidden states to propagate temporal information across frames, enabling more coherent and efficient super-resolution \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/1801.04590)\]. For instance, the proposed frame-recurrent video super-resolution framework reduces computational overhead by warping only one image per step and assimilates multiple previous frames without additional cost \[[1](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/c6a8ff4d9dcdf9e5d82310b847af999ed6655f65)\]. Similarly, the RLSP algorithm introduces high-dimensional latent states to implicitly propagate temporal information, achieving significant speed improvements over existing methods \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/1801.04590)\]. Other works, such as BasicVSR, have enhanced recurrent structures with improved propagation and alignment techniques, leading to better performance under similar computational constraints \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/2201.12288)\]. Additionally, the RVRT model integrates parallel processing within a globally recurrent framework, balancing model size, effectiveness, and efficiency by leveraging both local and global temporal dependencies \[[4](http://arxiv.org/abs/1611.05250)\]. These approaches highlight the growing trend of using RNNs to model long-term temporal dependencies in VSR, offering a more robust and efficient solution compared to traditional methods \[[5](http://arxiv.org/abs/1812.02898)\].

[Category]: «Transformers for VSR and Their Limitations»
[Snippet]: «Video super-resolution (VSR), with the aim to restore a high-resolution video from its corresponding low-resolution version, is a spatial-temporal sequence prediction problem. Recently, Transformer has been gaining popularity due to its parallel computing ability for sequence-to-sequence modeling. Thus, it seems to be straightforward to apply the vision Transformer to solve VSR. However, the typical block design of Transformer with a fully connected self-attention layer and a token-wise feed-forward layer does not fit well for VSR due to the following two reasons. First, the fully connected self-attention layer neglects to exploit the data locality because this layer relies on linear layers to compute attention maps. Second, the token-wise feed-forward layer lacks the feature alignment which is important for VSR since this layer independently processes each of the input token embeddings without any interaction among them. In this paper, we make the first attempt to adapt Transformer for VSR. Specifically, to tackle the first issue, we present a spatial-temporal convolutional self-attention layer with a theoretical understanding to exploit the locality information. For the second issue, we design a bidirectional optical flow-based feed-forward layer to discover the correlations across different video frames and also align features. Extensive experiments on several benchmark datasets demonstrate the effectiveness of our proposed method. The code will be available at https://github.com/caojiezhang/VSR-Transformer.»
