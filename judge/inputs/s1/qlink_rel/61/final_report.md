## Related Works

Personalized outfit generation has been a focal point in recent research, driven by the need to provide users with fashion recommendations that are both compatible and tailored to their preferences. Early works focused on outfit compatibility and item similarity, often using rule-based systems or simple machine learning models \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/1803.09196)\]. However, these approaches lacked the ability to capture complex stylistic relationships and user-specific preferences. 

The introduction of deep learning models has significantly advanced the field. For instance, \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/1803.09196)\] proposed a method to learn item compatibility by considering the types of items and their stylistic relationships, achieving improvements over state-of-the-art methods. Similarly, \[[10](http://arxiv.org/abs/1902.08009)\] introduced a graph-based approach to model outfit compatibility, representing outfits as subgraphs and using node-wise graph neural networks to better capture interactions between items. This work demonstrated superior performance in compatibility prediction and fill-in-the-blank tasks.

Recent studies have also explored the use of neural networks for outfit generation and recommendation. \[[19](http://arxiv.org/abs/2204.04812)\] introduced OutfitTransformer, which uses self-attention mechanisms to learn effective outfit-level representations, enabling compatibility prediction and complementary item retrieval. This model was trained using a set-wise outfit ranking loss, which improved retrieval performance. Additionally, \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/1905.01866)\] proposed a Personalized Outfit Generation (POG) model that connects user preferences with a Transformer architecture, achieving strong results in both compatibility and personalization metrics. The POG model was deployed on a large-scale platform, demonstrating its industrial applicability.
