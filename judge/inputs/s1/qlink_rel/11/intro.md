## Related Works

3D human pose estimation has attracted significant research interest due to its applications in action recognition, human-computer interaction, and augmented reality. Early methods primarily relied on 2D pose estimation followed by 3D reconstruction, often using regression or optimization techniques \[[25](http://arxiv.org/abs/1611.07828)\]. However, these approaches suffer from depth ambiguities and self-occlusions, which limit their accuracy \[[1](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/50167a9e6bdc1f64a58fe8a3c990105fec0bd2bc)\]. Recent advances have focused on leveraging deep learning to directly estimate 3D poses from 2D inputs or video sequences \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/1705.03098)\]. 

Several works have explored the use of graph-based models to exploit spatial and temporal relationships in 3D pose estimation. For instance, \[[1](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/50167a9e6bdc1f64a58fe8a3c990105fec0bd2bc)\] proposed a graph convolutional network (GCN) to model the human skeleton, incorporating domain knowledge about body configurations. This approach improved performance by learning multi-scale features and capturing spatial dependencies. Similarly, \[[12](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/442928cd95d7bca15800b29ce2e2f8db5461bd58)\] introduced a modulated GCN that allows for more flexible modeling of body part relationships by adjusting graph structures and feature transformations. This was further extended in \[[26](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/2170b81eaac4095f5948b7922f29200a25db9b2b)\], where high-order graph convolutions were used to capture long-range dependencies between body joints, improving robustness to occlusions and depth ambiguities.

Temporal consistency is another key challenge in 3D pose estimation, particularly when dealing with video sequences. \[[9](http://arxiv.org/abs/1711.08585)\] addressed this by designing a sequence-to-sequence network with LSTM units to model temporal smoothness, achieving a 12.2% improvement on the Human3.6M dataset. \[[27](http://arxiv.org/abs/1811.11742)\] further advanced this by using dilated temporal convolutions and semi-supervised training with back-projection, reducing the mean per-joint position error by 11% on Human3.6M. Additionally, \[[17](http://arxiv.org/abs/2103.14304)\] proposed a Strided Transformer that effectively aggregated long-range information from 2D pose sequences into a single 3D pose, achieving state-of-the-art results with fewer parameters.

Attention mechanisms have also been widely adopted to improve the modeling of spatial and temporal dependencies. \[[5](http://arxiv.org/abs/2103.10455)\] introduced a spatial-temporal transformer to capture joint relationships within and across frames, achieving competitive performance on standard benchmarks. \[[13](http://arxiv.org/abs/2111.12707)\] proposed a multi-hypothesis transformer (MHFormer) that explicitly models multiple plausible pose hypotheses, improving accuracy by 3% on Human3.6M. Similarly, \[[14](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/22e305b7b66bdedf379fa678802cb46e8bb546b1)\] designed a graph-oriented transformer (GraFormer) that incorporated graph convolution layers to better model joint relationships, achieving superior performance with fewer parameters.
