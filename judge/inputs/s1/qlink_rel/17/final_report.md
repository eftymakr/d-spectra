## Related Works

Semi-supervised learning (SSL) has emerged as a powerful paradigm for leveraging unlabeled data to improve model performance when labeled data is scarce \[[1](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/f3df804a7eef204b0c4fc4b81166dec9b4abc073)\]. Traditional SSL methods assume that labeled and unlabeled data share the same class distribution, a strong assumption that is often violated in real-world scenarios \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/2001.07685)\]. In practice, unlabeled data may contain out-of-distribution (OOD) samples, i.e., outliers, which are typically treated as noise and can significantly degrade SSL performance \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/2007.11330)\]. To address this limitation, numerous approaches have been proposed to handle open-set SSL, where the presence of OOD samples is explicitly considered \[[4](http://arxiv.org/abs/2105.14148)\].

One line of work focuses on filtering out OOD samples before applying SSL techniques. For example, OpenMatch \[[5](http://arxiv.org/abs/2109.00650)\] introduces an open-set soft-consistency regularization loss that enhances the smoothness of the OVA-classifier with respect to input transformations, improving outlier detection. Similarly, Multi-Task Curriculum Framework \[[6](http://arxiv.org/abs/1905.02249)\] proposes a joint optimization framework that alternates between updating network parameters and OOD scores, enabling the model to learn from both in-distribution (ID) and OOD samples. These methods aim to improve performance by explicitly handling OOD data, but they often require additional computational overhead or complex architectures \[[7](http://arxiv.org/abs/2108.05617)\].

Another approach is to incorporate OOD samples directly into the training process while mitigating their negative impact. For instance, Trash to Treasure \[[8](http://arxiv.org/abs/1610.02242)\] proposes a warm-up training phase that leverages all unlabeled data, including OOD samples, to learn discriminative features. It then introduces a cross-modal matching strategy to detect OOD samples, aligning the feature space with the core classification task. This method demonstrates that OOD data can be effectively utilized to enhance feature learning without significant performance degradation \[[9](http://arxiv.org/abs/2205.07246)\].

Recent methods have also focused on improving the robustness of SSL models in the presence of OOD samples. For example, OpenCoS \[[10](http://arxiv.org/abs/2107.08943)\] utilizes self-supervised contrastive learning to identify OOD samples and employs one-hot pseudo-labels and soft-labels for inlier and OOD data, respectively. This allows the model to handle open-set scenarios more effectively \[[11](http://arxiv.org/abs/1606.04586)\]. ### <a id='category-open-set-ssl-frameworks'></a>Open-Set SSL Frameworks: 6 papers found.
Open-set semi-supervised learning (SSL) addresses the challenge where unlabeled data may contain out-of-distribution (OOD) samples, which are not present in the labeled training set. Traditional SSL methods assume that both labeled and unlabeled data share the same class distribution, but this assumption is often violated in real-world scenarios, leading to performance degradation due to the presence of outliers \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/2007.11330)\]. To mitigate this, several frameworks have been proposed. For instance, a multi-task curriculum learning approach was introduced to jointly optimize OOD detection and SSL, where OOD samples are identified based on their probability of belonging to out-of-distribution classes, and only in-distribution samples are used for training \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/2007.11330)\]. Similarly, OpenCoS leverages self-supervised contrastive learning to identify OOD samples in unlabeled data and uses one-hot pseudo-labels for in-distribution samples while applying soft-labels for OOD samples, thereby improving classification performance \[[10](http://arxiv.org/abs/2107.08943)\]. Another framework, ORCA, introduces an uncertainty adaptive margin mechanism to address the class distribution mismatch between labeled and unlabeled data, enabling the model to handle novel classes at test time \[[12](http://arxiv.org/abs/2102.03526)\]. IOMatch further improves upon existing methods by jointly utilizing inliers and outliers, even when distinguishing between them is challenging, through a multi-binary classifier and unified open-set pseudo-labels \[[15](http://arxiv.org/abs/2308.13168)\]. Additionally, the SSB framework enhances open-set SSL by incorporating high-confidence pseudo-labeled data and utilizing non-linear transformations to separate inlier and outlier features, along with pseudo-negative mining to boost outlier detection \[[21](http://arxiv.org/abs/2311.10572)\]. These approaches collectively highlight the evolving strategies to address open-set SSL, emphasizing the importance of robust outlier detection and the integration of both inlier and outlier data for improved performance.

[Snippet]: «This paper presents SimCLR: a simple framework for contrastive learning of visual representations. We simplify recently proposed contrastive self-supervised learning algorithms without requiring specialized architectures or a memory bank. In order to understand what enables the contrastive prediction tasks to learn useful representations, we systematically study the major components of our framework. We show that (1) composition of data augmentations plays a critical role in defining effective predictive tasks, (2) introducing a learnable nonlinear transformation between the representation and the contrastive loss substantially improves the quality of the learned representations, and (3) contrastive learning benefits from larger batch sizes and more training steps compared to supervised learning. By combining these findings, we are able to considerably outperform previous methods for self-supervised and semi-supervised learning on ImageNet. A linear classifier trained on self-supervised representations learned by SimCLR achieves 76.5% top-1 accuracy, which is a 7% relative improvement over previous state-of-the-art, matching the performance of a supervised ResNet-50. When fine-tuned on only 1% of the labels, we achieve 85.8% top-5 accuracy, outperforming AlexNet with 100X fewer labels.»
