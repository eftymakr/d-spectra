## Related Works

Zero-Knowledge Proofs (ZKPs) have emerged as a critical cryptographic primitive for privacy-preserving computation, enabling a prover to demonstrate the truth of a statement without revealing additional information \[[10](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/9ccf7b6cb32cf89752a35bd910555adac54773e0)\]. This has led to their adoption in blockchain technologies, verifiable machine learning, and secure voting systems \[[6](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/65f1e83a53c5ce9dee04042e0dd4191a451df2b8)\]. However, the computational complexity of the proving process has hindered widespread adoption \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2408.05890)\]. Recent efforts have focused on accelerating the key primitives of ZKP protocols using hardware accelerators, particularly GPUs and ASICs \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2408.05890)\]\[[3](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/e149d93531d8c07cf43f6cae327a4795144d1304)\]\[[5](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/e4a4608e8e35379e63b9df5bda1b7599ee8ab9ac)\]. 

Several works have explored the acceleration of critical operations such as the Number Theoretic Transform (NTT) and Multi-scalar Multiplication (MSM), which are essential for proof generation \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2408.05890)\]. For instance, SZKP introduces an ASIC-based accelerator that achieves speedups of over 400×, 3×, and 12× over CPU, ASIC, and GPU implementations, respectively \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2408.05890)\]. Similarly, PipeZK proposes a pipelined architecture with two subsystems to handle large polynomial computations and MSMs, achieving 10× and 5× speedups on cryptographic benchmarks and cryptocurrency applications like Zcash \[[2](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/6ee84674b7c426849c3da56cb278c08eb7a903cf)\]. GZKP, on the other hand, leverages GPU acceleration for ZKP systems, achieving up to 48.1× and 17.6× speedups over state-of-the-art systems \[[3](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/e149d93531d8c07cf43f6cae327a4795144d1304)\]. 

cuZK further improves GPU-based acceleration by introducing a new parallel MSM algorithm that achieves nearly perfect linear speedup over the Pippenger algorithm, along with parallelized matrix-vector multiplication (MUL) and optimized data transfer mechanisms \[[5](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/e4a4608e8e35379e63b9df5bda1b7599ee8ab9ac)\]. This results in significant performance gains, with cuZK achieving up to 4.86× speedup on standard benchmarks and 2.18× on a cryptocurrency application like Filecoin \[[5](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/e4a4608e8e35379e63b9df5bda1b7599ee8ab9ac)\]. 

In addition to hardware accelerators, there are also software-based approaches and libraries that aim to simplify the implementation of ZKPs. For example, zksk is a Python library that enables the definition and computation of sigma protocols, allowing for the composition of smaller proofs into larger ones \[[8](http://arxiv.org/abs/1911.02459)\]. This makes it easier for developers to construct complex ZKP systems without requiring deep cryptographic expertise \[[8](http://arxiv.org/abs/1911.02459)\].

### <a id='category-zero-knowledge-proof-acceleration'></a>Zero Knowledge Proof Acceleration: 5 papers found.
Zero Knowledge Proof Acceleration has been a focal point in improving the efficiency of cryptographic protocols, particularly for applications requiring privacy and verifiability, such as blockchain and verifiable machine learning. Prior works have targeted the computational bottlenecks in proof generation, which often involve complex operations like Number Theoretic Transform (NTT) and Multi-scalar Multiplication (MSM) \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2408.05890)\]. For instance, SZKP introduced an ASIC-based accelerator that achieved significant speedups over CPU, ASIC, and GPU implementations by leveraging structured dataflows for both NTT and MSM \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2408.05890)\]. Similarly, PipeZK proposed a pipelined accelerator with two subsystems, one for decomposing large kernels into smaller tasks and another for dynamic work dispatch, achieving up to 10x speedup on cryptographic benchmarks \[[2](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/6ee84674b7c426849c3da56cb278c08eb7a903cf)\]. GZKP further advanced GPU acceleration by optimizing memory access patterns and introducing a new parallelization strategy for multi-scalar multiplication, achieving up to 48.1× speedup on standard benchmarks \[[3](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/e149d93531d8c07cf43f6cae327a4795144d1304)\]. cuZK focused on optimizing GPU implementations of zkSNARKs, introducing a new parallel MSM algorithm and reducing CPU-GPU data transfer latency, resulting in up to 4.86x speedup on benchmarks \[[5](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/e4a4608e8e35379e63b9df5bda1b7599ee8ab9ac)\]. Additionally, zkCNN demonstrated the application of ZKPs to convolutional neural networks, enabling efficient verification of machine learning predictions without leaking model details, with a 1264× speedup over existing schemes \[[7](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/6336149196f02d0eb6c1a89bc6b662e4422bd794)\]. These works collectively highlight the importance of hardware acceleration in overcoming the performance limitations of ZKPs, particularly in real-world applications where speed and efficiency are critical.

### <a id='category-others'></a>Others: 3 papers found.
Zero-knowledge proofs (ZKPs) have emerged as a critical tool in privacy-preserving systems, enabling parties to verify the truth of a statement without revealing additional information \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2408.05890)\]. This concept is particularly valuable in blockchain environments, where ZKPs can be used to validate transactions without exposing sensitive data such as account balances or addresses \[[2](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/6ee84674b7c426849c3da56cb278c08eb7a903cf)\]. For instance, ZKPs can allow users to prove they have sufficient funds for a transaction without disclosing the exact amount \[[2](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/6ee84674b7c426849c3da56cb278c08eb7a903cf)\]. The implementation of ZKPs, however, remains challenging due to their computational complexity, which has led to the development of specialized libraries and frameworks to simplify their use \[[3](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/e149d93531d8c07cf43f6cae327a4795144d1304)\]. These tools, such as zksk, provide a domain-specific language for defining sigma protocols, making it easier for developers to construct and combine zero-knowledge proofs without requiring expertise in cryptographic languages \[[3](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/e149d93531d8c07cf43f6cae327a4795144d1304)\]. Additionally, recent advancements have focused on improving the efficiency of ZKP protocols, such as HyperPlonk, which supports universal setup and small proof sizes, making them suitable for public verifiable systems \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2408.05890)\]. Efforts to accelerate these protocols using hardware, such as GPUs and ASICs, have also been explored to reduce verification time and improve scalability \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2408.05890)\]. Despite these advances, challenges remain, including the trade-off between proof size and verification efficiency, which continues to be a focus of research \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2408.05890)\].
