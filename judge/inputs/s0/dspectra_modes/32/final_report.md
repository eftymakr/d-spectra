Recent research has explored the internal mechanisms of large language models (LLMs), particularly focusing on how feature representations evolve across layers. A significant body of work has investigated the role of specific layers in performing particular functions or behaviors, often through data-dependent analyses of fine-tuned models \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2309.02144)\]. However, these approaches are typically limited to post-hoc settings, making them less useful for understanding the intrinsic properties of pre-fine-tuned models \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2309.02144)\]. In contrast, our work introduces a data-oblivious approach that identifies critical layers in pre-fine-tuned LLMs by analyzing representation dynamics using Centered Kernel Alignment (CKA) \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2309.02144)\]. This method allows for a more systematic and generalizable understanding of how layers contribute to model behavior.

Several studies have examined the impact of fine-tuning on LLMs, particularly in terms of how it affects model performance and robustness \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/1802.05365)\].
