## Related Works

Sequential recommendation systems aim to predict the next item a user will interact with, based on their historical behavior. These systems have evolved significantly, incorporating advanced neural network architectures to better capture sequential patterns and user preferences. Early approaches relied on Markov Chains (MCs) \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/1808.09781)\], which assume that a user's next action depends only on their last (or few) actions. While effective in sparse datasets, MCs lack the ability to model long-term dependencies. Recurrent Neural Networks (RNNs) \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/1808.09781)\] were introduced to address this limitation, allowing for the modeling of longer-term semantics. However, RNNs often suffer from vanishing gradient problems and are computationally expensive \[[12](http://arxiv.org/abs/1904.06690)\]. 

To overcome these limitations, self-attention based models such as SASRec \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/1808.09781)\] have emerged. SASRec leverages self-attention to capture long-term dependencies while maintaining efficiency by focusing on relevant items. This approach balances the strengths of MCs and RNNs, offering a more flexible and efficient framework for sequential modeling. However, most self-attention models rely on simple positional encodings and ignore the temporal dynamics of user interactions \[[9](http://arxiv.org/abs/2008.08273)\]. 

Recent works have attempted to incorporate time-aware mechanisms into self-attention models. For example, TiSASRec \[[1](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/9c7a455b1e48d01a99e58e884a6c1acb75074ad0)\] explicitly models time intervals between interactions, enhancing the model's ability to capture temporal patterns. Similarly, MEANTIME \[[9](http://arxiv.org/abs/2008.08273)\] introduces multi-temporal embeddings to better represent the time-aware nature of user behavior. These models recognize that sequential recommendation differs from language modeling in that timestamp information is available and should be leveraged to extract additional contextual information. 

In addition to temporal modeling, recent studies have explored the use of hierarchical attention networks \[[4](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/0b42168f22713c48d8b0ce3bc8f680d347184d88)\] and feature-level self-attention \[[16](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/63555b4f363ee72dd8d49d8deca68760542c0cd2)\] to better capture diverse interaction patterns. Hierarchical attention networks \[[4](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/0b42168f22713c48d8b0ce3bc8f680d347184d88)\] integrate both long-term and short-term preferences, while feature-level self-attention \[[16](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/63555b4f363ee72dd8d49d8deca68760542c0cd2)\] considers both item-level and feature-level sequences to extract richer sequential patterns. These approaches highlight the importance of modeling both item transitions and feature transitions to improve recommendation accuracy.

Another key challenge in sequential recommendation is the sparsity of item-to-item transitions \[[7](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/455e11bde14967fc76d87d4649d821d2f27c3055)\]. To address this, Category-aware Collaborative Sequential Recommendation \[[7](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/455e11bde14967fc76d87d4649d821d2f27c3055)\] leverages item categories to enhance dependency modeling and collaborative learning across users. This approach improves the robustness of recommendation systems in sparse scenarios by incorporating category information into the modeling process.

### <a id='category-sequential-recommendation-models'></a>Sequential Recommendation Models: 6 papers found.
Sequential recommendation models have evolved to better capture the dynamic nature of user interactions, with recent approaches focusing on modeling both temporal and sequential patterns more effectively. Traditional methods such as Markov Chains (MCs) and Recurrent Neural Networks (RNNs) have been widely used, but they often assume rigidly ordered sequences without considering the time intervals between interactions \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/1808.09781)\]. This limitation has led to the development of self-attention based models like SASRec, which allow for more flexible modeling of long-term semantics while still focusing on relevant recent actions \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/1808.09781)\]. Similarly, models such as TiSASRec explicitly incorporate time intervals into their framework, improving next-item prediction by considering both absolute positions and time gaps between interactions \[[1](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/9c7a455b1e48d01a99e58e884a6c1acb75074ad0)\]. Additionally, hierarchical attention networks have been introduced to better capture evolving user preferences by distinguishing between long-term and short-term behaviors \[[4](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/0b42168f22713c48d8b0ce3bc8f680d347184d88)\]. Another approach, Caser, uses convolutional filters to extract local features from sequences, enabling the model to capture both general preferences and sequential patterns \[[6](http://arxiv.org/abs/1809.07426)\]. More recently, bidirectional models like BERT4Rec have been proposed to overcome the limitations of unidirectional architectures by allowing items to be influenced by both left and right contexts during training \[[12](http://arxiv.org/abs/1904.06690)\]. Furthermore, state-space models (SSMs) have been adapted for continuous-time modeling, addressing irregular time intervals in user interactions by integrating time-aware and relation-aware components \[[14](http://arxiv.org/abs/2502.08132)\]. These advancements highlight the ongoing efforts to improve sequential recommendation by incorporating more nuanced representations of user behavior and interaction patterns.

### <a id='category-sequential-recommendation-methods'></a>Sequential Recommendation Methods: 4 papers found.
Recent advances in sequential recommendation have focused on improving the modeling of user behavior through deep learning techniques, with a particular emphasis on addressing data sparsity and capturing complex interaction patterns \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/2008.07873)\]. Many existing models rely on item prediction loss to learn representations, which can lead to suboptimal performance due to overemphasis on final outcomes and limited integration of contextual information \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/2008.07873)\]. To overcome these limitations, researchers have proposed self-supervised learning approaches that utilize mutual information maximization (MIM) to enhance data representations by capturing correlations between attributes, items, subsequences, and sequences \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/2008.07873)\]. Additionally, there has been a growing interest in developing transferable sequence representation learning methods that leverage item descriptions to create universal representations applicable across different recommendation scenarios \[[5](http://arxiv.org/abs/2206.05941)\]. These approaches often employ contrastive pre-training tasks and lightweight encoding architectures to enable efficient adaptation to new domains \[[5](http://arxiv.org/abs/2206.05941)\]. Another line of research has focused on modeling both global and local aspects of user preferences, incorporating hierarchical self-attention mechanisms to better capture temporal dynamics and fine-grained interactions \[[11](http://arxiv.org/abs/2208.04760)\]. Furthermore, some studies have emphasized the importance of considering feature-level transitions in addition to item-level sequences, arguing that explicit and implicit feature patterns can significantly improve the accuracy of sequential recommendations \[[16](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/63555b4f363ee72dd8d49d8deca68760542c0cd2)\]. These developments highlight the ongoing efforts to enhance sequential recommendation models by addressing key challenges such as data sparsity, transferability, and the modeling of diverse user behaviors.

### <a id='category-sequential-recommendation-techniques'></a>Sequential Recommendation Techniques: 2 papers found.
Sequential recommendation techniques have seen significant advancements, particularly with the integration of self-attention mechanisms and collaborative modeling strategies. Recent approaches leverage self-attention to capture sequential dependencies, yet they often struggle with modeling temporal dynamics and handling sparsity in item transitions \[[9](http://arxiv.org/abs/2008.08273)\]. To address these challenges, researchers have proposed methods that incorporate item categories to enhance dependency modeling and collaborative learning. For instance, a Category-aware Collaborative Sequential Recommender utilizes item categories to organize user actions and improve in-category transition patterns through self-attention \[[7](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/455e11bde14967fc76d87d4649d821d2f27c3055)\]. Additionally, the use of temporal embeddings has been introduced to better exploit timestamp information and reduce information bottlenecks, as seen in the MEANTIME model \[[9](http://arxiv.org/abs/2008.08273)\]. These innovations highlight the importance of both temporal and categorical information in improving the accuracy and robustness of sequential recommendation systems.

### <a id='category-others'></a>Others: 4 papers found.
The related works in sequential recommendation focus on improving user representation learning and handling the challenges of sparse and noisy user behavior data. Recent approaches, such as CauseRec \[[8](http://arxiv.org/abs/2109.05261)\], propose counterfactual data distribution modeling to learn robust user representations that are less sensitive to noisy behaviors. Similarly, CoSeRec \[[13](http://arxiv.org/abs/2108.06479)\] applies contrastive self-supervised learning to enhance model performance against sparse and noisy data by leveraging item correlations for high-quality view generation. Additionally, Time-LSTM \[[15](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/e1b93001202afd674cc3542cdb5599bff0d10157)\] introduces a novel LSTM variant with time gates to better capture time intervals between user actions, improving the modeling of both short-term and long-term interests. These methods collectively address key limitations in traditional sequential recommendation models, such as the inability to handle variable-length sequences and capture diverse interaction patterns. The proposed STAR-Rec \[[1](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/9c7a455b1e48d01a99e58e884a6c1acb75074ad0)\] builds upon these advancements by integrating preference-aware attention and state-space modeling within a sequence-level mixture-of-experts framework, further enhancing the ability to adapt to diverse user behaviors and varying sequence lengths.
