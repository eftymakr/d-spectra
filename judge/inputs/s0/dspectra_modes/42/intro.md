## Related Works

The growing adoption of cloud-native technologies has led to an increased focus on optimizing both operational and financial aspects of cloud infrastructure. Prior research has explored system-level optimization strategies for cost and resource efficiency in containerized systems \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2006.15275)\]. However, the analysis of network costs in Kubernetes clusters remains underexplored \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2006.15275)\]. This paper contributes to the emerging FinOps discipline by addressing the financial and operational aspects of managing network costs in cloud-native environments \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2006.15275)\].

Several studies have examined the security and operational challenges of Kubernetes deployments. For instance, \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/2501.14753)\] highlights the importance of role-based access control (RBAC) and the application of security patches to mitigate vulnerabilities. Additionally, \[[3](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/61020665f9134e56009b6e10606fc21348f59d53)\] identifies 11 categories of security misconfigurations in Kubernetes manifests, emphasizing the need for security-focused code reviews and static analysis during development. These findings underscore the broader context of security and operational challenges in Kubernetes environments \[[3](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/61020665f9134e56009b6e10606fc21348f59d53)\].

The integration of workflow systems with Kubernetes has also been a focus of recent research. \[[4](http://arxiv.org/abs/2207.01222)\] introduces KubeAdaptor, a docking framework that ensures consistency in task scheduling order between workflow systems and Kubernetes. This work demonstrates how Kubernetes can be adapted to better support workflow execution, which is relevant to the resource and scheduling challenges discussed in this paper \[[4](http://arxiv.org/abs/2207.01222)\].

In the realm of cost optimization, \[[5](http://arxiv.org/abs/2307.12479)\] presents ABACUS, a FinOps service that sets budgets, enforces them through deployment blocking, and alerts teams when spending thresholds are exceeded. This aligns with the goals of this paper, which aims to provide actionable insights into network cost optimization for Kubernetes clusters \[[5](http://arxiv.org/abs/2307.12479)\]. Additionally, \[[6](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/b0fa8fd1b1638f4afad41956b65a15a9096e309f)\] discusses the importance of Infrastructure-as-Code (IaC) in cloud cost management, which is relevant to the broader FinOps practices explored in this study \[[6](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/b0fa8fd1b1638f4afad41956b65a15a9096e309f)\].

The use of Helm charts for Kubernetes applications has also been studied. \[[7](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/18a720c86ed6e166c26f7d39490bfd11b93ea3ec)\] highlights the security risks associated with outdated container images and the importance of maintaining up-to-date versions. This is critical for ensuring both security and cost efficiency in Kubernetes deployments \[[7](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/18a720c86ed6e166c26f7d39490bfd11b93ea3ec)\]. Furthermore, \[[8](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/00f68d85141a6981a05bb57b9e84610b2ef61941)\] emphasizes the need for real-time monitoring and logging solutions to detect and mitigate misconfigurations, which is essential for maintaining both security and cost control in Kubernetes environments \[[8](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/00f68d85141a6981a05bb57b9e84610b2ef61941)\].

Research on Kubernetes scheduling has also contributed to the understanding of resource allocation challenges. \[[9](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/3c2ac07d840d148085dc50dfa5bb9aebd33fabc9)\] presents a taxonomy of Kubernetes scheduling approaches, highlighting the trade-offs between different Quality of Service (QoS) parameters. This is relevant to the resource management strategies discussed in this paper \[[9](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/3c2ac07d840d148085dc50dfa5bb9aebd33fabc9)\].
