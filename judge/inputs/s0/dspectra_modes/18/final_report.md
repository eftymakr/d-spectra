## Related Works

Anomaly detection has become a critical component in modern industrial settings, particularly in the context of intelligent manufacturing, where maintaining quality control is essential \[[11](http://arxiv.org/abs/2106.08265)\]. Traditional approaches often rely on threshold-based methods or supervised learning, which are limited by the lack of labeled data and the complexity of real-world scenarios \[[9](http://arxiv.org/abs/1901.04997)\]. Recent advances in deep learning have enabled more robust and flexible anomaly detection methods, particularly for image-based and time-series data \[[5](http://arxiv.org/abs/2301.11514)\]\[[10](http://arxiv.org/abs/2007.10088)\].

Several studies have focused on improving the efficiency and performance of anomaly detection models for deployment in resource-constrained environments. For instance, \[[11](http://arxiv.org/abs/2106.08265)\] proposed PatchCore, a method that uses a maximally representative memory bank of nominal patch-features to achieve state-of-the-art performance for both detection and localization, with competitive inference times. This is particularly relevant for embedded systems where computational resources are limited. Similarly, \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/1607.00148)\] introduced an LSTM-based Encoder-Decoder scheme for anomaly detection, which is effective for both predictable and unpredictable time-series data. This approach leverages reconstruction errors to identify anomalies, demonstrating robustness across different types of time-series data.

In the realm of image-based anomaly detection, \[[8](http://arxiv.org/abs/2005.14140)\] proposed a method that leverages deep feature representations from pre-trained models, such as those trained on ImageNet, to detect anomalies in a transfer learning setting. This approach fits a multivariate Gaussian to deep features and uses the Mahalanobis distance as an anomaly score, achieving high performance on the MVTec dataset. Another study, \[[13](http://arxiv.org/abs/2310.07716)\], introduced the PAD dataset and benchmark for pose-agnostic anomaly detection, addressing the limitations of existing datasets that assume fixed object poses during training and testing. This work also proposed a novel method, OmniposeAD, specifically designed for pose-agnostic anomaly detection.

For multivariate time series, \[[6](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/440d248d148f7e36dad232e48f1c5c1cbc556d86)\] proposed OmniAnomaly, a stochastic recurrent neural network that captures normal patterns by learning robust representations and uses reconstruction probabilities to determine anomalies. This model is particularly effective for industrial devices such as servers and spacecrafts, where multivariate time series are common. Additionally, \[[9](http://arxiv.org/abs/1901.04997)\] introduced MAD-GAN, an unsupervised multivariate anomaly detection method based on Generative Adversarial Networks (GANs), which captures latent interactions among variables and uses a novel anomaly score for detection.

### <a id='category-anomaly-detection-methods'></a>Anomaly Detection Methods: 6 papers found.
In the realm of anomaly detection, recent approaches have focused on improving efficiency and adaptability for real-world applications, particularly in industrial settings. Several methods have been proposed to address the challenges of detecting anomalies in time-series data, attributed networks, and images, often with constraints on computational resources \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/1607.00148)\]. For instance, EncDec-AD employs a Long Short Term Memory (LSTM) based encoder-decoder architecture to reconstruct normal time-series behavior and detect anomalies through reconstruction error, demonstrating robustness across predictable, unpredictable, and short/long time-series \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/1607.00148)\]. Similarly, AnomalyDAE introduces a dual autoencoder framework that jointly learns node and attribute embeddings, capturing complex interactions between network structure and node attributes, and using reconstruction errors for anomaly detection \[[4](http://arxiv.org/abs/2002.03665)\]. In image-based anomaly detection, methods like CFLOW-AD and the Gaussian-based approach leverage conditional normalizing flows and multivariate Gaussian models, respectively, to achieve high accuracy and real-time performance, with CFLOW-AD outperforming prior methods in both detection and localization tasks \[[7](http://arxiv.org/abs/2107.12571)\]\[[8](http://arxiv.org/abs/2005.14140)\]. PatchCore, another notable approach, utilizes a maximally representative memory bank of nominal patch features to achieve state-of-the-art performance on the MVTec-AD benchmark, with significant improvements in AUROC scores \[[11](http://arxiv.org/abs/2106.08265)\]. Additionally, a 3D dataset has been introduced for unsupervised anomaly detection and localization, highlighting the need for models that can detect geometric deviations in manufactured products trained only on anomaly-free data \[[12](http://arxiv.org/abs/2112.09045)\]. These methods collectively emphasize the importance of adaptability, computational efficiency, and robustness in anomaly detection, especially for deployment in resource-constrained environments like those found in SMEs.

In this work, we provide the first systematic analysis of such schemes, and introduce a taxonomy of properties that are verified by those detection systems. We then present a novel general framework to generate adversarial spoofing signals that violate physical properties of the system, and use the framework to analyze four anomaly detectors published at top security conferences. We find that three of those detectors are susceptible to a number of adversarial manipulations (e.g., spoofing with precomputed patterns), which we call Synthetic Sensor Spoofing and one is resilient against our attacks. Our attacks reduce the Recall (True Positive Rate) of the attacked schemes making them not able to correctly detect anomalies. Thus, the vulnerabilities we discovered in the anomaly detectors show that (despite an original good detection performance), those detectors are not able to reliably learn physical properties of the system. ### <a id='category-others'></a>Others: 2 papers found.
In the realm of anomaly detection, recent advancements in deep learning have demonstrated significant improvements over traditional shallow methods, particularly in handling large and complex datasets \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/1906.02694)\]. While most semi-supervised approaches focus on utilizing labeled normal samples, few effectively leverage labeled anomalies, and existing deep methods often remain domain-specific \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/1906.02694)\]. This gap is addressed by Deep SAD, an end-to-end deep methodology that introduces an information-theoretic framework based on the entropy difference between normal and anomalous latent distributions \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/1906.02694)\]. Additionally, unsupervised methods have gained traction, especially in scenarios where failure modes are complex or environmental changes introduce unpredictability \[[10](http://arxiv.org/abs/2007.10088)\]. One such method involves creating negative samples from positive observations and training classifiers to distinguish between them, leveraging the Contraction Principle to establish robust decision boundaries \[[10](http://arxiv.org/abs/2007.10088)\]. These approaches have been validated on synthetic and real-world datasets, achieving competitive performance and scalability, as evidenced by their deployment in large-scale industrial settings \[[10](http://arxiv.org/abs/2007.10088)\]. The integration of such techniques highlights the evolving landscape of anomaly detection, emphasizing the need for adaptable and efficient models suitable for resource-constrained environments \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/1906.02694)\].

### <a id='category-industrial-anomaly-detection-research'></a>Industrial Anomaly Detection Research: 2 papers found.
Recent advancements in deep learning have significantly impacted industrial image anomaly detection (IAD), with a growing focus on developing efficient and effective models for real-world applications \[[5](http://arxiv.org/abs/2301.11514)\]. Researchers have explored various neural network architectures, levels of supervision, and loss functions to improve detection accuracy and adaptability \[[5](http://arxiv.org/abs/2301.11514)\]. However, existing datasets often assume pose-aligned training and testing samples, which is unrealistic in industrial settings where anomalies can occur in any region and objects may appear in different poses \[[13](http://arxiv.org/abs/2310.07716)\]. To address these limitations, the Multi-pose Anomaly Detection (MAD) dataset and Pose-agnostic Anomaly Detection (PAD) benchmark were introduced, offering a more realistic and diverse set of scenarios for evaluating anomaly detection methods \[[13](http://arxiv.org/abs/2310.07716)\]. These efforts have led to the development of novel approaches like OmniposeAD, which is specifically designed for pose-agnostic anomaly detection \[[13](http://arxiv.org/abs/2310.07716)\]. Additionally, there is a growing emphasis on deploying lightweight models on embedded devices, as seen in the KairosAD approach, which leverages the Mobile Segment Anything Model (MobileSAM) to achieve high performance with reduced computational demands \[[5](http://arxiv.org/abs/2301.11514)\]. This trend highlights the importance of balancing model complexity with practical deployment requirements in industrial settings \[[5](http://arxiv.org/abs/2301.11514)\].

### <a id='category-anomaly-detection-in-time-series-and-networks'></a>Anomaly Detection in Time Series and Networks: 2 papers found.
In the realm of anomaly detection, recent research has focused on improving the efficiency and effectiveness of methods for complex systems such as smart buildings, factories, and industrial networks \[[9](http://arxiv.org/abs/1901.04997)\]. Traditional threshold-based approaches are limited by their inability to adapt to dynamic system behaviors, while supervised learning methods face challenges due to the lack of labeled data \[[9](http://arxiv.org/abs/1901.04997)\]. To address these limitations, unsupervised machine learning techniques have been explored, with a particular emphasis on capturing spatial-temporal correlations and interactions among multiple variables \[[9](http://arxiv.org/abs/1901.04997)\]. For instance, Generative Adversarial Networks (GANs) have been leveraged to develop frameworks like MAD-GAN, which simultaneously analyze multivariate time series data and detect anomalies through a novel DR-score metric \[[9](http://arxiv.org/abs/1901.04997)\]. Similarly, in attributed networks, deep learning models have been proposed to integrate both topological structures and node attributes for more accurate anomaly detection, using graph convolutional networks (GCN) and autoencoders to capture structural and attribute-based reconstruction errors \[[14](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/bcfdbb6b8911272139170ef4b24e31d9145093e7)\]. These approaches highlight the growing trend of utilizing advanced machine learning techniques to enhance anomaly detection in real-world systems, particularly in environments where data is high-dimensional and unlabeled \[[9](http://arxiv.org/abs/1901.04997)\]\[[14](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/bcfdbb6b8911272139170ef4b24e31d9145093e7)\].
