## Related Works

Generative retrieval (GR) has recently gained significant attention as a novel paradigm in information retrieval (IR), offering a direct mapping from queries to relevant document identifiers (docids) without the need for traditional indexing or reranking \[[15](http://arxiv.org/abs/2406.01197)\]. This approach leverages sequence-to-sequence models to generate docids, enabling end-to-end learning of retrieval tasks \[[15](http://arxiv.org/abs/2406.01197)\]. However, most existing GR models are primarily evaluated on static document collections, with limited exploration of their performance in dynamic corpora where documents are continuously added \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2308.14968)\]. This section reviews related works that address the challenges of GR in dynamic settings, docid design, and model efficiency.

Several studies have explored the use of text-based docids in GR, which are often generated as natural language sequences such as titles or n-grams \[[3](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/6682a3679a17d090830635f1c420126d20eae54a)\]. These docids benefit from the pre-trained knowledge of language models, allowing for better semantic alignment and fine-grained representation of documents \[[16](http://arxiv.org/abs/2409.18409)\]. In contrast, numeric-based docids, which are typically fixed identifiers assigned during preprocessing, are more efficient but suffer from poor generalization to unseen documents \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2308.14968)\]. This is attributed to their tendency to overfit to the initial document set, leading to suboptimal performance in dynamic environments \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2308.14968)\]. 

The importance of docid design in GR has been highlighted in several works. For instance, \[[3](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/6682a3679a17d090830635f1c420126d20eae54a)\] proposes NOVO docids, which are learnable and interpretable n-gram sets that can be optimized during training to capture semantic relationships between documents. Similarly, \[[13](http://arxiv.org/abs/2305.13859)\] introduces Term-Set Generation (TSGen), which uses a set of terms as docids to improve retrieval accuracy by avoiding token pruning errors. These approaches emphasize the need for flexible and semantically rich docid representations that can adapt to evolving document collections.

Efforts to improve the scalability of GR to large-scale corpora have also been explored. \[[10](http://arxiv.org/abs/2305.11841)\] investigates how generative retrieval scales to millions of passages, finding that synthetic queries are crucial for indexing, while existing architectural modifications are often ineffective when considering computational costs. \[[14](http://arxiv.org/abs/2311.09134)\] proposes RIPOR, an optimization framework that enhances the accuracy of relevance estimation during sequential docid generation, achieving significant improvements on standard benchmarks. These studies underscore the challenges of deploying GR in real-world scenarios with large and dynamic document collections.

### <a id='category-generative-retrieval-research'></a>Generative Retrieval Research: 6 papers found.
Generative retrieval (GR) has gained significant attention in information retrieval (IR) for its ability to directly predict document identifiers (docids) using parametric models, enabling end-to-end retrieval without relying on traditional indexes \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2308.14968)\]. However, most existing GR models are evaluated on static document collections, limiting their applicability to dynamic corpora where new documents are continuously added \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2308.14968)\]. Recent works have addressed this challenge by proposing incremental indexing techniques, such as Incremental Product Quantization, which allows for efficient encoding of new documents while maintaining retrieval performance \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2308.14968)\]. Additionally, memory-augmented learning mechanisms have been introduced to prevent catastrophic forgetting and maintain connections between old and new documents \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2308.14968)\]. The design of docids plays a crucial role in GR performance, with text-based docids showing superior generalization to unseen documents due to their semantic alignment with language models' pretrained knowledge and fine-grained design \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2308.14968)\]. In contrast, numeric-based docids, while efficient, suffer from performance degradation in dynamic settings due to overfitting on the initial document set \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2308.14968)\]. Research has also explored the use of optimized docids, such as NOVO docids, which are generated through training to better capture semantic relationships and improve retrieval effectiveness \[[3](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/6682a3679a17d090830635f1c420126d20eae54a)\]. Furthermore, methods like GenRet and Ultron have been developed to learn semantic docids through end-to-end training, demonstrating improved performance on benchmark datasets \[[3](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/6682a3679a17d090830635f1c420126d20eae54a)\]\[[4](http://arxiv.org/abs/2304.04171)\]. These advancements highlight the ongoing efforts to enhance GR models for both static and dynamic environments, emphasizing the importance of docid design and training strategies \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2308.14968)\]\[[3](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/6682a3679a17d090830635f1c420126d20eae54a)\]\[[4](http://arxiv.org/abs/2304.04171)\].

### <a id='category-generative-retrieval-models'></a>Generative Retrieval Models: 2 papers found.
Generative retrieval (GR) has gained significant traction in information retrieval (IR) as a paradigm that integrates query-document matching within a unified model, often using sequence-to-sequence frameworks to directly generate relevant document identifiers (docids) \[[12](http://arxiv.org/abs/2403.12499)\]. Unlike traditional index-retrieve-rank pipelines, GR models consolidate corpus knowledge into a single model, enabling more flexible and adaptive retrieval \[[2](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/5beaeee1e27007bd12967480bb9c379cebd15e6e)\]. However, existing GR models, particularly those using text-based docids, have shown superior generalization to unseen documents and better performance over dynamic corpora, where document collections evolve continuously \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2308.14968)\]. This advantage is attributed to the fine-grained design of text-based docids, which aligns better with the semantic understanding of language models and supports high lexical diversity \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2308.14968)\]. In contrast, numeric-based docids, while efficient, suffer from performance degradation in dynamic settings due to their tendency to overfit the initial training data \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2308.14968)\]. Recent advances have focused on improving GR through listwise optimization strategies that consider the ranked list of docids during training, rather than treating each docid independently \[[12](http://arxiv.org/abs/2403.12499)\]. These developments highlight the ongoing efforts to enhance GR models for real-world applications, particularly in scenarios requiring adaptability to changing document collections \[[2](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/5beaeee1e27007bd12967480bb9c379cebd15e6e)\].

### <a id='category-others'></a>Others: 6 papers found.
Generative retrieval (GR) has gained significant attention as an alternative to traditional retrieval paradigms, focusing on directly generating document identifiers (DocIDs) from queries without the need for explicit indexing \[[15](http://arxiv.org/abs/2406.01197)\]. Existing GR methods often rely on training-based indexing, which fine-tunes models to memorize associations between queries and DocIDs, but this approach suffers from high training costs and limited adaptability to dynamic corpora \[[7](http://arxiv.org/abs/2408.02152)\]. To address these limitations, researchers have proposed few-shot indexing-based frameworks, such as Few-Shot GR, which prompt large language models (LLMs) to generate DocIDs without additional training, enabling efficient and adaptable retrieval \[[7](http://arxiv.org/abs/2408.02152)\]. Additionally, the use of pre-trained models in GR systems, such as DynamicRetriever, allows for dynamic and updatable document semantic mapping, parameterizing static indexes within the model \[[9](http://arxiv.org/abs/2203.00537)\]. However, GR models with numeric-based DocIDs often underperform in dynamic environments due to overfitting on the initial document set, highlighting the importance of semantic alignment and fine-grained DocID design \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2308.14968)\]. Recent work, such as Term-Set Generation (TSGen), has introduced novel DocID strategies using term sets instead of sequences, improving robustness and decoding efficiency by allowing permutation-invariant generation \[[13](http://arxiv.org/abs/2305.13859)\]. These advancements underscore the evolving landscape of GR, emphasizing the need for more efficient, adaptable, and scalable methods to handle dynamic document collections \[[15](http://arxiv.org/abs/2406.01197)\].

### <a id='category-document-retrieval-innovations'></a>Document Retrieval Innovations: 3 papers found.
Generative retrieval (GR) has emerged as a promising paradigm in information retrieval (IR), offering a novel approach to document retrieval by directly generating relevant document identifiers (DocIDs) for queries \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2308.14968)\]. Unlike traditional retrieval methods that rely on preprocessed document identifiers, GR leverages an encoder-decoder architecture to dynamically produce DocIDs, enhancing retrieval performance and adaptability \[[2](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/5beaeee1e27007bd12967480bb9c379cebd15e6e)\]. However, existing GR models are often evaluated on static document collections, limiting their applicability to dynamic corpora where document sets evolve over time \[[3](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/6682a3679a17d090830635f1c420126d20eae54a)\]. Recent studies have explored the generalization of GR models over dynamic corpora, revealing that text-based DocID designs exhibit superior performance compared to numeric-based ones, particularly in handling unseen documents \[[4](http://arxiv.org/abs/2304.04171)\]. This is attributed to the fine-grained semantic alignment of text-based DocIDs with language models' pretrained knowledge, as well as their high lexical diversity \[[5](http://arxiv.org/abs/2305.18952)\]. Additionally, the effectiveness of GR is further enhanced by integrating generative retrieval with closed-book generation and retrieval-augmented generation (RAG), as demonstrated by models like CorpusLM \[[6](http://arxiv.org/abs/2208.09257)\]. To address the limitations of current GR approaches, researchers have proposed frameworks such as GR$^2$, which introduces multi-graded constrained contrastive training to improve relevance ranking and document distinctness \[[7](http://arxiv.org/abs/2408.02152)\]. Furthermore, fully end-to-end retrieval paradigms, such as Auto Search Indexer (ASI), have been developed to automatically learn optimal DocIDs for both existing and new documents, enabling more flexible and scalable retrieval systems \[[8](http://arxiv.org/abs/2402.01176)\]. These advancements highlight the ongoing evolution of GR, emphasizing the need for models that can adapt to dynamic environments while maintaining efficiency and accuracy \[[9](http://arxiv.org/abs/2203.00537)\].
