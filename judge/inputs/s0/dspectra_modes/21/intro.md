## Related Works

Video frame interpolation has been extensively studied for its ability to enhance temporal resolution of video sequences, enabling applications such as slow-motion playback, video up-conversion, and improved visual quality. Early methods relied on optical flow estimation and warping techniques \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/1712.00080)\], but recent advances have shifted toward deep learning approaches that directly learn the mapping from input frames to intermediate frames \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/1812.01210)\]. These methods often combine motion estimation and image synthesis in a single network, reducing error propagation and improving performance \[[3](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/089f2072c95c244f3b7f4df404562bb4afe24448)\]. For example, Super SloMo proposes an end-to-end convolutional neural network that jointly models motion interpretation and occlusion reasoning, using bi-directional optical flow and visibility maps to generate high-quality intermediate frames \[[4](http://arxiv.org/abs/1711.07566)\]. Similarly, Zoom-In-To-Check introduces an instance-level supervision mechanism that leverages high-resolution object details to refine interpolated frames \[[5](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/a285a442317f24068f7cc262531976e82a5c41d6)\]. 

High-resolution video frame interpolation remains a challenging task due to the computational demands of handling large-scale motion and maintaining visual coherence \[[6](http://arxiv.org/abs/1906.05928)\]. IM-Net addresses this by formulating motion estimation as a classification problem and using an efficient architecture with multi-scale losses, achieving state-of-the-art results on high-resolution datasets \[[7](http://arxiv.org/abs/1604.00449)\]. The method also emphasizes real-time performance, with inference times under 33 milliseconds on HD resolution \[[8](http://arxiv.org/abs/2007.11762)\]. Another approach, Video Frame Interpolation via Adaptive Separable Convolution, reduces parameter count by using local separable convolutions, enabling efficient and high-quality interpolation \[[9](http://arxiv.org/abs/2111.13817)\]. This method also incorporates perceptual loss to ensure visually pleasing results \[[10](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/23c9b2b3d315dd81563f57bbec8fda28d53700a6)\].

Unsupervised learning has also gained traction, particularly for scenarios where high frame-rate training data is scarce \[[11](http://arxiv.org/abs/1506.06825)\]. Cycle consistency-based methods, such as Unsupervised Video Interpolation Using Cycle Consistency, enable the synthesis of high frame-rate videos from low frame-rate inputs by minimizing cycle reconstruction errors \[[12](http://arxiv.org/abs/1708.01692)\]. These methods can be further enhanced with pseudo-supervised loss terms that align interpolated frames with pre-trained models, improving adaptation to new domains \[[13](http://arxiv.org/abs/2201.10075)\]. Additionally, methods like Time Lens leverage event-based cameras to capture dense motion signals, enabling more accurate motion estimation in dynamic scenes \[[14](http://arxiv.org/abs/1703.07514)\]. These techniques often combine event-based and frame-based information to reduce artifacts and improve performance \[[15](http://arxiv.org/abs/2004.00779)\].

Recent works have also focused on lightweight and efficient architectures for real-time applications \[[16](http://arxiv.org/abs/1603.06041)\]. For instance, the Video Frame Interpolation Transformer introduces self-attention mechanisms to capture long-range dependencies, while local attention strategies reduce computational overhead \[[17](http://arxiv.org/abs/2203.17191)\].
