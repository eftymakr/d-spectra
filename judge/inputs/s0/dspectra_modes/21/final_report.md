## Related Works

Video frame interpolation has been extensively studied for its ability to enhance temporal resolution of video sequences, enabling applications such as slow-motion playback, video up-conversion, and improved visual quality. Early methods relied on optical flow estimation and warping techniques \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/1712.00080)\], but recent advances have shifted toward deep learning approaches that directly learn the mapping from input frames to intermediate frames \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/1812.01210)\]. These methods often combine motion estimation and image synthesis in a single network, reducing error propagation and improving performance \[[3](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/089f2072c95c244f3b7f4df404562bb4afe24448)\]. For example, Super SloMo proposes an end-to-end convolutional neural network that jointly models motion interpretation and occlusion reasoning, using bi-directional optical flow and visibility maps to generate high-quality intermediate frames \[[4](http://arxiv.org/abs/1711.07566)\]. Similarly, Zoom-In-To-Check introduces an instance-level supervision mechanism that leverages high-resolution object details to refine interpolated frames \[[5](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/a285a442317f24068f7cc262531976e82a5c41d6)\]. 

High-resolution video frame interpolation remains a challenging task due to the computational demands of handling large-scale motion and maintaining visual coherence \[[6](http://arxiv.org/abs/1906.05928)\]. IM-Net addresses this by formulating motion estimation as a classification problem and using an efficient architecture with multi-scale losses, achieving state-of-the-art results on high-resolution datasets \[[7](http://arxiv.org/abs/1604.00449)\]. The method also emphasizes real-time performance, with inference times under 33 milliseconds on HD resolution \[[8](http://arxiv.org/abs/2007.11762)\]. Another approach, Video Frame Interpolation via Adaptive Separable Convolution, reduces parameter count by using local separable convolutions, enabling efficient and high-quality interpolation \[[9](http://arxiv.org/abs/2111.13817)\]. This method also incorporates perceptual loss to ensure visually pleasing results \[[10](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/23c9b2b3d315dd81563f57bbec8fda28d53700a6)\].

Unsupervised learning has also gained traction, particularly for scenarios where high frame-rate training data is scarce \[[11](http://arxiv.org/abs/1506.06825)\]. Cycle consistency-based methods, such as Unsupervised Video Interpolation Using Cycle Consistency, enable the synthesis of high frame-rate videos from low frame-rate inputs by minimizing cycle reconstruction errors \[[12](http://arxiv.org/abs/1708.01692)\]. These methods can be further enhanced with pseudo-supervised loss terms that align interpolated frames with pre-trained models, improving adaptation to new domains \[[13](http://arxiv.org/abs/2201.10075)\]. Additionally, methods like Time Lens leverage event-based cameras to capture dense motion signals, enabling more accurate motion estimation in dynamic scenes \[[14](http://arxiv.org/abs/1703.07514)\]. These techniques often combine event-based and frame-based information to reduce artifacts and improve performance \[[15](http://arxiv.org/abs/2004.00779)\].

Recent works have also focused on lightweight and efficient architectures for real-time applications \[[16](http://arxiv.org/abs/1603.06041)\]. For instance, the Video Frame Interpolation Transformer introduces self-attention mechanisms to capture long-range dependencies, while local attention strategies reduce computational overhead \[[17](http://arxiv.org/abs/2203.17191)\]. ### <a id='category-video-frame-interpolation-techniques'></a>Video Frame Interpolation Techniques: 16 papers found.
Video frame interpolation techniques have seen significant advancements, particularly with the integration of deep learning approaches. These methods aim to generate intermediate frames between consecutive video frames to create temporally coherent sequences \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/1712.00080)\]. Traditional approaches often rely on optical flow estimation, but recent works have moved toward end-to-end neural networks that can handle complex motion and occlusion more effectively \[[3](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/089f2072c95c244f3b7f4df404562bb4afe24448)\]. For instance, some methods utilize U-Net architectures to estimate optical flow and refine intermediate frames, while others introduce novel architectures like Transformers to capture long-range dependencies and content-aware aggregation \[[7](http://arxiv.org/abs/1604.00449)\]. Additionally, there are efforts to improve efficiency and scalability, such as using local separable convolutions to reduce computational demands \[[12](http://arxiv.org/abs/1708.01692)\]. Furthermore, unsupervised learning techniques have been proposed to synthesize high frame rate videos from low frame rate inputs, leveraging cycle consistency and pseudo-supervised losses \[[6](http://arxiv.org/abs/1906.05928)\]. These innovations collectively enhance the quality and efficiency of video frame interpolation, making it more suitable for real-time applications and diverse scenarios \[[8](http://arxiv.org/abs/2007.11762)\].

### <a id='category-others'></a>Others: 11 papers found.
Video frame interpolation techniques have seen significant advancements, particularly in their ability to synthesize high-quality intermediate frames from existing video sequences. These methods often rely on sophisticated architectures that combine motion estimation, image synthesis, and learning-based approaches to improve performance. For instance, some methods use flow-based warping modules to generate intermediate frames by estimating optical flow and then refine them with image synthesis modules \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/1712.00080)\]. Others introduce lightweight models that reduce computational resources while maintaining high performance, such as methods that utilize instance-level supervision to refine object details during training \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/1812.01210)\]. Additionally, there are approaches that leverage event-based cameras to capture dense motion signals, enabling more accurate motion-aware synthesis without requiring high frame-rate videos \[[4](http://arxiv.org/abs/1711.07566)\]. Furthermore, some works focus on improving the efficiency of frame interpolation by integrating auxiliary features like depth and normals, which help in better motion estimation and occlusion handling \[[6](http://arxiv.org/abs/1906.05928)\]. These techniques collectively address the challenges of motion accuracy, computational efficiency, and visual quality in video frame interpolation, contributing to the broader field of neural rendering and computer vision.

### <a id='category-video-processing-techniques'></a>Video Processing Techniques: 3 papers found.
Video frame interpolation techniques have seen significant advancements, particularly with the integration of deep learning approaches. These methods aim to enhance temporal resolution by synthesizing intermediate frames between existing ones, which is crucial for applications such as video editing and 3D scene reconstruction \[[13](http://arxiv.org/abs/2201.10075)\]. However, traditional neural network-based methods often face challenges in terms of computational efficiency and performance at high resolutions \[[13](http://arxiv.org/abs/2201.10075)\]. Recent research has explored alternative strategies, such as splatting-based synthesis, which offers faster processing times and maintains high-quality results, especially in multi-frame interpolation scenarios \[[13](http://arxiv.org/abs/2201.10075)\]. Additionally, unsupervised learning approaches have been developed to address frame interpolation by leveraging temporal coherence in video sequences, allowing for the training of Convolutional Neural Networks (CNNs) without explicit supervision \[[16](http://arxiv.org/abs/1603.06041)\]. These models can then be used to infer inter-frame correspondences, enhancing the overall performance of image matching tasks \[[16](http://arxiv.org/abs/1603.06041)\]. Furthermore, deep learning methods for single image super-resolution (SR) have demonstrated the potential of CNNs to learn end-to-end mappings between low- and high-resolution images, achieving state-of-the-art results while maintaining a lightweight structure \[[29](http://arxiv.org/abs/1501.00092)\]. These developments highlight the growing importance of deep learning in video processing, offering new possibilities for improving the quality and efficiency of frame interpolation and related tasks.
