## Related Works

Prosody modeling has been a central challenge in text-to-speech (TTS) systems, as it directly impacts the naturalness and expressiveness of synthesized speech. Early TTS systems often relied on handcrafted rules or statistical models to generate prosody, but these approaches lacked the flexibility and adaptability needed for modern applications \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2011.06465)\]. Recent advances in neural TTS have enabled more natural speech synthesis, but the control over prosody remains limited, especially in post-hoc scenarios \[[14](http://arxiv.org/abs/2206.14643)\]. For instance, many TTS systems implicitly encode prosody within acoustic features, making it difficult to alter without retraining \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/1909.10302)\]. This limitation is particularly evident in systems that lack explicit prosody labeling in their training data \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/1909.10302)\]. 

Several works have attempted to improve prosody control by introducing explicit prosody modeling. For example, Hierarchical Prosody Modeling for Non-Autoregressive Speech Synthesis proposes a hierarchical architecture where phoneme-level prosody features are conditioned on word-level features, leading to improved audio quality and prosody naturalness \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2011.06465)\]. Similarly, the work on Deep Voice 3 demonstrates how fully-convolutional attention-based models can scale to large datasets, improving both training efficiency and audio quality \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/1710.07654)\]. However, these models still face challenges in fine-grained prosody control, especially when dealing with multi-sentence inputs \[[14](http://arxiv.org/abs/2206.14643)\].

To address these limitations, researchers have explored the use of pre-trained language models to enhance TTS systems with richer semantic and syntactic information. This approach is further extended in Improving Prosody Modelling with Cross-Utterance BERT Embeddings, where cross-utterance context vectors are used to improve prosody generation for multi-sentence TTS \[[10](http://arxiv.org/abs/2011.05161)\].
