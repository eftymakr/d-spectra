## Related Works

Diffusion models have become a cornerstone in generative AI, particularly for image and video synthesis due to their ability to iteratively refine outputs based on learned patterns \[[21](http://arxiv.org/abs/2105.05233)\]. However, the computational intensity of these models, especially diffusion transformers (DiT), poses challenges for deployment on resource-constrained devices \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2406.02540)\]. Prior work has focused on reducing the computational footprint of diffusion models through quantization, which involves reducing the bit-width of model parameters to lower memory usage and increase inference speed \[[25](http://arxiv.org/abs/2305.10657)\]. 

Several approaches have been proposed for quantizing diffusion models. For instance, Q-Diffusion introduces a novel PTQ method tailored to the unique multi-timestep pipeline of diffusion models, addressing the changing output distributions of noise estimation networks over time steps \[[5](http://arxiv.org/abs/2302.04304)\]. Similarly, PTQ4DiT identifies challenges in DiT quantization, such as salient channels with extreme magnitudes and temporal variability in activation distributions, and proposes solutions like Channel-wise Salience Balancing (CSB) and Spearmen's ρ-guided Salience Calibration (SSC) \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/2405.16005)\]. These methods aim to maintain high fidelity in image and video generation while enabling 4-bit quantization \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/2405.16005)\].

In the context of video generation, QVD introduces the first PTQ strategy specifically for video diffusion models, addressing the skewness of temporal features and inter-channel disparities in activation \[[20](http://arxiv.org/abs/2407.11585)\]. This work emphasizes the importance of preserving temporal consistency and spatial coherence in video generation, which is critical for maintaining visual quality \[[20](http://arxiv.org/abs/2407.11585)\]. Similarly, ViDiT-Q is designed for DiT models, achieving W8A8 and W4A8 quantization with minimal degradation in visual quality and metrics \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2406.02540)\]. The method also implements efficient GPU kernels to achieve significant memory savings and latency improvements \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2406.02540)\].

The challenge of maintaining spatiotemporal coherence during quantization has also been addressed in other works. For example, Q-DiT introduces automatic quantization granularity allocation and sample-wise dynamic activation quantization to handle the spatial and temporal variance in DiTs \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/2406.17343)\]. This approach improves the fidelity of image and video generation, particularly under challenging quantization settings like W4A8 \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/2406.17343)\]. Additionally, Temporal Dynamic Quantization for Diffusion Models dynamically adjusts the quantization interval based on time step information, improving output quality without increasing computational overhead during inference \[[9](http://arxiv.org/abs/2306.02316)\].

### <a id='category-diffusionmodelquantizationtechniques'></a>DiffusionModelQuantizationTechniques: 10 papers found.
Diffusion models, particularly Diffusion Transformers (DiTs), have shown significant advancements in image and video generation, yet their deployment on edge devices is hindered by high computational and memory costs \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2406.02540)\]. To address this, post-training quantization (PTQ) has emerged as a promising technique to reduce model size and accelerate inference without retraining \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/2406.17343)\]. However, existing PTQ methods face challenges when applied to DiTs due to their unique architectural properties, such as spatial and temporal variance in weights and activations \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/2405.16005)\]. Several works have focused on improving quantization for DiTs, proposing methods like ViDiT-Q \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2406.02540)\], Q-DiT \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/2406.17343)\], and PTQ4DiT \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/2405.16005)\], which aim to handle the specific challenges of DiT quantization. These approaches introduce techniques such as dynamic activation quantization, salience balancing, and channel-wise calibration to maintain performance at low bit-widths \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/2405.16005)\]. Additionally, methods like HQ-DiT \[[12](http://arxiv.org/abs/2405.19751)\] leverage 4-bit floating-point precision to minimize quantization error, while QVD \[[20](http://arxiv.org/abs/2407.11585)\] extends PTQ to video diffusion models by addressing temporal and inter-channel disparities. These efforts highlight the ongoing research into efficient quantization techniques tailored for DiTs, aiming to enable real-time deployment without compromising visual quality \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2406.02540)\]\[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/2406.17343)\]\[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/2405.16005)\]\[[12](http://arxiv.org/abs/2405.19751)\]\[[20](http://arxiv.org/abs/2407.11585)\].

### <a id='category-others'></a>Others: 5 papers found.
Video generation has seen significant advancements with the introduction of diffusion transformers (DiTs), which have demonstrated exceptional performance in generating high-quality videos \[[29](http://arxiv.org/abs/2411.02397)\]. However, these models often suffer from high computational complexity and a large number of parameters, limiting their deployment on edge devices \[[29](http://arxiv.org/abs/2411.02397)\]. To address these challenges, researchers have explored quantization techniques to reduce storage requirements and accelerate inference \[[29](http://arxiv.org/abs/2411.02397)\]. For instance, Token-aware Quantization Estimator (TQE) has been proposed to compensate for quantization errors in both token and feature dimensions, ensuring that the integrity of video content is maintained \[[29](http://arxiv.org/abs/2411.02397)\]. Additionally, Temporal Maintenance Distillation (TMD) has been introduced to preserve spatiotemporal correlations between frames, allowing for more efficient optimization of each frame in the context of the entire video \[[29](http://arxiv.org/abs/2411.02397)\]. These approaches are part of a broader trend in video generation research that seeks to improve efficiency without compromising quality, as seen in other works such as the Video Motion Customization (VMC) framework, which focuses on adapting temporal attention layers for motion customization \[[4](http://arxiv.org/abs/2312.00845)\], and Latte, a Latent Diffusion Transformer that models video distribution in the latent space with efficient variants for handling spatio-temporal tokens \[[11](http://arxiv.org/abs/2401.03048)\]. Furthermore, the development of DiT-3D, a Diffusion Transformer for 3D shape generation, highlights the adaptability of Transformer architectures to different modalities, including video \[[17](http://arxiv.org/abs/2307.01831)\]. These efforts collectively contribute to the ongoing evolution of video generation models, emphasizing both quality and efficiency.

### <a id='category-diffusion-models-for-text-to-image-and-video'></a>Diffusion Models for Text to Image and Video: 7 papers found.
Diffusion models have seen significant advancements in text-to-image and video generation, with various approaches addressing challenges such as computational efficiency, semantic alignment, and image/video quality. For text-to-image generation, VQ-Diffusion leverages a vector quantized variational autoencoder (VQ-VAE) combined with a conditional Denoising Diffusion Probabilistic Model (DDPM), enabling efficient and high-quality image synthesis while reducing error accumulation through a mask-and-replace strategy \[[6](http://arxiv.org/abs/2111.14822)\]. Similarly, eDiff-I introduces an ensemble of diffusion models specialized for different synthesis stages, improving text alignment and visual quality by utilizing diverse embeddings like T5, CLIP text, and CLIP image embeddings \[[14](http://arxiv.org/abs/2211.01324)\]. In video generation, CogVideoX employs a 3D Variational Autoencoder (VAE) and an expert transformer with adaptive LayerNorm to enhance text-video alignment and generate coherent, long-duration videos \[[8](http://arxiv.org/abs/2408.06072)\]. Additionally, cascaded diffusion models, such as those used in I2VGen-XL, improve resolution and semantic accuracy by sequentially upsampling images and incorporating conditioning augmentation to prevent error compounding \[[28](http://arxiv.org/abs/2311.04145)\]. To further optimize performance, methods like classifier-free guided diffusion models have been distilled into faster inference models, reducing sampling steps while maintaining high fidelity \[[19](http://arxiv.org/abs/2210.03142)\]. These developments highlight the ongoing efforts to enhance both the quality and efficiency of diffusion-based generative models across modalities.

### <a id='category-video-generation-techniques'></a>Video Generation Techniques: 5 papers found.
Video generation techniques have seen significant advancements, particularly with the integration of diffusion models and transformers. Recent works such as Stable Video Diffusion \[[15](http://arxiv.org/abs/2311.15127)\] and MagicVideo \[[16](http://arxiv.org/abs/2211.11018)\] have demonstrated the potential of latent diffusion models (LDMs) in generating high-resolution, high-quality videos from text or images. These models leverage a low-dimensional latent space to reduce computational demands while maintaining visual fidelity. For instance, MagicVideo introduces a 3D U-Net architecture and a novel VideoVAE auto-encoder to enhance video synthesis efficiency and quality \[[16](http://arxiv.org/abs/2211.11018)\]. Similarly, Video LDM \[[26](http://arxiv.org/abs/2304.08818)\] extends the LDM paradigm to video generation by introducing a temporal dimension and finetuning on encoded video sequences, achieving state-of-the-art performance on real-world driving data. Meanwhile, research on quantization for video generation, such as Q-VDiT, addresses the challenges of parameter reduction and computational efficiency, proposing methods like Token-aware Quantization Estimator (TQE) and Temporal Maintenance Distillation (TMD) to preserve spatiotemporal coherence \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2406.02540)\]. Additionally, models like Customize-A-Video \[[23](http://arxiv.org/abs/2402.14780)\] focus on motion customization, using low-rank adaptation (LoRA) and appearance absorbers to adapt pre-trained models to new motion patterns. These developments highlight the ongoing efforts to improve both the quality and efficiency of video generation systems.

### <a id='category-video-generation-and-editing-techniques'></a>Video Generation and Editing Techniques: 3 papers found.
Document 1 discusses a framework that leverages text-to-image diffusion models for text-driven video editing, emphasizing the preservation of spatial layout and motion while adhering to the target text \[[18](http://arxiv.org/abs/2307.10373)\]. This approach utilizes diffusion features and inter-frame correspondences to ensure consistency without requiring additional training \[[18](http://arxiv.org/abs/2307.10373)\]. This tokenizer not only improves image and video generation but also excels in video compression and action recognition tasks \[[27](http://arxiv.org/abs/2310.05737)\]. Document 3 presents Flow Vid, a consistent video-to-video synthesis framework that integrates spatial conditions and temporal optical flow to maintain temporal consistency across video frames \[[30](http://arxiv.org/abs/2312.17681)\]. This method allows for flexible edits using existing image-to-image models and achieves high-quality results with improved efficiency compared to prior methods \[[30](http://arxiv.org/abs/2312.17681)\]. These works collectively highlight advancements in video generation and editing, focusing on improving quality, efficiency, and flexibility while addressing challenges such as temporal consistency and model adaptability.
