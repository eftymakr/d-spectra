## Related Works

Large language models (LLMs) have seen significant advancements in recent years, particularly in their ability to perform complex tasks through instruction tuning and fine-tuning. Instruction tuning, which involves training models on a collection of tasks described via natural language instructions, has been shown to improve zero-shot performance on unseen tasks \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/2109.01652)\]. This approach has been successfully applied to a wide range of models, including PaLM and T5, leading to substantial improvements in benchmark performance \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2210.11416)\]. For example, FLAN, an instruction-tuned model, outperforms zero-shot GPT-3 on 20 out of 25 tasks \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/2109.01652)\]. Similarly, instruction tuning has been extended to multimodal settings, where models like LLaVA are trained using generated instruction data to enhance visual and language understanding \[[8](http://arxiv.org/abs/2304.08485)\].

The effectiveness of instruction tuning is further supported by studies that highlight the importance of diverse instruction datasets and the use of chain-of-thought (CoT) and program-of-thought (PoT) rationales in improving mathematical reasoning \[[11](http://arxiv.org/abs/2309.05653)\]. MAmmoTH, a series of models trained on a hybrid instruction dataset, demonstrates significant gains in mathematical reasoning tasks, achieving performance that surpasses existing open-source models and even competes with GPT-4 \[[11](http://arxiv.org/abs/2309.05653)\]. These findings underscore the importance of diverse and structured instruction data in enhancing model capabilities.

Efforts to scale LLMs have also been a focal point in recent research. The PaLM model, with 540 billion parameters, demonstrates the benefits of scaling in few-shot learning, achieving state-of-the-art results on various benchmarks \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/2204.02311)\]. Additionally, the training of large models is often constrained by memory and computational limitations, leading to the development of efficient training methods such as model parallelism \[[5](http://arxiv.org/abs/1909.08053)\]. Megatron-LM, for instance, enables the training of models with up to 8.3 billion parameters using model parallelism, demonstrating the feasibility of training very large models \[[5](http://arxiv.org/abs/1909.08053)\].

In the context of inference, techniques such as "windowing" and "row-column bundling" have been introduced to optimize the use of limited memory, allowing models up to twice the size of available DRAM to be run efficiently \[[4](http://arxiv.org/abs/2312.11514)\]. These methods are crucial for deploying LLMs on devices with constrained resources.

The evaluation of LLMs has also evolved, with benchmarks like GLUE and MATH being used to assess model performance across various tasks \[[7](http://arxiv.org/abs/1804.07461)\]\[[9](http://arxiv.org/abs/2103.03874)\]. ### <a id='category-large-language-models-research'></a>Large Language Models Research: 6 papers found.
The category "Large Language Models Research" encompasses a range of studies focused on improving the performance, scalability, and usability of large language models (LLMs). Research has shown that instruction finetuning, where models are trained on tasks phrased as natural language instructions, significantly enhances zero-shot and few-shot learning capabilities, leading to improved performance on a variety of benchmarks such as MMLU, BBH, and TyDiQA \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2210.11416)\]. This approach has been successfully applied to models like Flan-PaLM 540B, which outperforms previous versions by a large margin when fine-tuned on a large number of tasks \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2210.11416)\]. Similarly, instruction-tuned models like FLAN demonstrate substantial improvements over both unmodified models and even large-scale models like GPT-3, particularly in tasks such as ANLI, RTE, and StoryCloze \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/2109.01652)\]. Additionally, the development of models like PaLM 540B highlights the benefits of scaling in terms of parameter count and training data, with improvements observed across multiple tasks, including multilingual and code generation \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/2204.02311)\]. The introduction of Codex, a model fine-tuned on GitHub code, further demonstrates the potential of specialized fine-tuning for specific applications, such as Python code writing \[[10](http://arxiv.org/abs/2107.03374)\]. These studies collectively emphasize the importance of training strategies, model scale, and task diversity in advancing the capabilities of LLMs.

### <a id='category-large-language-model-research'></a>Large Language Model Research: 4 papers found.
The category "Large Language Model Research" encompasses a range of advancements and challenges in training, evaluation, and deployment of large-scale language models. Research has shown that training very large transformer models, such as those with billions of parameters, is feasible through efficient intra-layer model parallelism, enabling convergence on up to 8.3 billion parameter models using 512 GPUs \[[5](http://arxiv.org/abs/1909.08053)\]. These models have demonstrated state-of-the-art performance on various benchmarks, including WikiText103, LAMBADA, and RACE, with significant improvements over existing models \[[5](http://arxiv.org/abs/1909.08053)\]. Additionally, efforts to improve generalization and robustness have led to the development of benchmarks like GLUE, which evaluates models across nine diverse NLU tasks and emphasizes the importance of transfer learning and efficient knowledge representation \[[7](http://arxiv.org/abs/1804.07461)\]. Furthermore, recent work has explored instruction tuning for multimodal models, such as LLaVA, which leverages language-only models to generate multimodal instruction-following data, achieving competitive results on tasks like Science QA \[[8](http://arxiv.org/abs/2304.08485)\]. Finally, the challenge of deploying LLMs at scale has prompted the development of tools like LLM-Pilot, which characterizes and predicts inference performance across different hardware configurations, optimizing cost and efficiency \[[12](http://arxiv.org/abs/2410.02425)\]. These contributions highlight the ongoing evolution of LLM research, addressing both technical and practical challenges in model training, evaluation, and deployment.

In this paper, we show an avenue for creating large amounts of instruction data with varying levels of complexity using LLM instead of humans. Starting with an initial set of instructions, we use our proposed Evol-Instruct to rewrite them step by step into more complex instructions.
