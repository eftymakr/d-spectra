## Related Works

Nonverbal communication plays a crucial role in human interaction, encompassing gestures, facial expressions, and body language that convey intent and emotion \[[11](http://arxiv.org/abs/1811.09362)\]. Existing large language models (LLMs) have primarily focused on textual information, often neglecting the integration of nonverbal cues, which limits their ability to create immersive conversational experiences \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2306.05424)\]. This gap has motivated recent efforts to develop multimodal models that can understand and generate both textual and nonverbal information \[[11](http://arxiv.org/abs/1811.09362)\]. 

Several works have explored the integration of vision and language. ViLBERT \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/1908.02265)\] extends BERT to a multi-modal two-stream model, processing visual and textual inputs in separate streams that interact through co-attentional transformer layers. This approach enables the model to learn task-agnostic representations of image content and natural language, achieving state-of-the-art results on multiple vision-and-language tasks. Similarly, PaLM-E \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/2303.03378)\] is an embodied multimodal language model that incorporates real-world continuous sensor modalities, enabling it to perform tasks such as robotic manipulation planning, visual question answering, and captioning. These models demonstrate the potential of combining vision and language for more comprehensive understanding.

The challenge of aligning perception with language models has also been addressed in recent work. Kosmos-1 \[[8](http://arxiv.org/abs/2302.14045)\] is a Multimodal Large Language Model (MLLM) that can perceive general modalities, learn in context, and follow instructions. It is trained on web-scale multimodal corpora, including arbitrarily interleaved text and images, image-caption pairs, and text data. Kosmos-1 achieves impressive performance on language understanding, generation, and perception-language tasks, including image captioning and visual question answering. This work highlights the importance of integrating perception and language for more robust AI systems.

The integration of nonverbal behaviors into language models has also been explored. The RAVEN model \[[11](http://arxiv.org/abs/1811.09362)\] dynamically adjusts word representations based on nonverbal cues such as vocal patterns and facial expressions. By modeling the fine-grained structure of nonverbal subword sequences, RAVEN captures the dynamic nature of speaker intentions, achieving competitive performance on multimodal sentiment analysis and emotion recognition tasks. This approach underscores the necessity of incorporating nonverbal contexts when modeling human language.

In addition, the development of large-scale multimodal datasets has been crucial for training and evaluating these models. The LAMM-Dataset \[[5](http://arxiv.org/abs/2306.06687)\] covers almost all high-level vision tasks for 2D and 3D vision, providing a comprehensive benchmark for multi-modal large language models

### <a id='category-video-and-language-model-alignment'></a>Video and Language Model Alignment: 2 papers found.
Video-based conversation models have emerged as a critical area of research in aligning language and visual modalities, with recent works focusing on developing models that can understand and generate detailed conversations about videos. Video-ChatGPT \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2306.05424)\] introduces a multimodal model that merges a video-adapted visual encoder with a large language model (LLM), enabling the system to comprehend and produce rich dialogue about video content. This model is trained on a large dataset of 100,000 video-instruction pairs, created through a scalable and robust manual and semi-automated pipeline. Additionally, the work presents a quantitative evaluation framework to assess the performance of video-based dialogue models. Similarly, Kosmos-1 \[[8](http://arxiv.org/abs/2302.14045)\] is a Multimodal Large Language Model (MLLM) that can perceive various modalities, including text and images, and perform tasks such as image captioning, visual question answering, and even OCR-free NLP directly from document images. Kosmos-1 is trained on web-scale multimodal corpora and can operate in zero-shot and few-shot settings, demonstrating strong performance across language understanding, generation, and vision tasks. These models highlight the growing interest in integrating nonverbal cues and visual understanding into conversational AI, aligning with the goals of systems like MARS, which aim to incorporate nonverbal elements such as facial expressions and body language into multimodal dialogue \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2306.05424)\].

### <a id='category-others'></a>Others: 5 papers found.
The Related Works section for the paper abstract on multimodal language models and nonverbal communication should focus on existing research that addresses the integration of nonverbal cues with language models. Several studies have explored multimodal understanding and generation, with a particular emphasis on vision-language models (VLMs) that aim to bridge the gap between textual and visual information \[[9](http://arxiv.org/abs/2404.07214)\]. For instance, ViLBERT \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/1908.02265)\] extends the BERT architecture to a multi-modal two-stream model, enabling joint representations of image content and natural language through co-attentional transformer layers. This approach has shown significant improvements across various vision-and-language tasks, such as visual question answering and image retrieval. Similarly, the Multi-attention Recurrent Network (MARN) \[[6](http://arxiv.org/abs/1802.00923)\] introduces a novel neural architecture for understanding human communication, leveraging multi-attention blocks and a hybrid memory system to capture interactions between modalities over time. Another relevant work, the Recurrent Attended Variation Embedding Network (RAVEN) \[[11](http://arxiv.org/abs/1811.09362)\], focuses on modeling nonverbal behaviors by dynamically shifting word representations based on accompanying visual and acoustic cues, achieving competitive performance in multimodal sentiment analysis. These models highlight the growing interest in developing systems that can effectively process and generate both verbal and nonverbal signals, aligning with the goals of the proposed MARS model. Additionally, the broader field of VLMs has seen advancements in categorizing models based on their capabilities, such as those that process multimodal inputs to generate unimodal outputs \[[9](http://arxiv.org/abs/2404.07214)\]. These contributions underscore the importance of integrating nonverbal cues into language models to enhance their understanding and generation of human-like communication.

### <a id='category-natural-language-understanding-benchmarks'></a>Natural Language Understanding Benchmarks: 3 papers found.
The category "Natural Language Understanding Benchmarks" encompasses efforts to develop models capable of general, flexible, and robust language understanding, extending beyond specific tasks \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/2303.03378)\]. Recent works highlight the need for benchmarks that evaluate models across diverse linguistic tasks and domains, such as the General Language Understanding Evaluation (GLUE), which includes nine NLU tasks and emphasizes the ability to transfer knowledge across tasks \[[7](http://arxiv.org/abs/1804.07461)\]. This aligns with the broader goal of creating models that can handle complex linguistic phenomena and generalize to new contexts \[[10](http://arxiv.org/abs/1802.05365)\]. Additionally, the development of large language models (LLMs) has shown promise in achieving artificial general intelligence, with recent advancements focusing on extending these models to handle multimodal data, such as point clouds and nonverbal cues, to enhance understanding in real-world scenarios \[[5](http://arxiv.org/abs/2306.06687)\]. These contributions underscore the importance of creating comprehensive benchmarks and datasets that support the evaluation and improvement of models in both traditional and emerging modalities \[[5](http://arxiv.org/abs/2306.06687)\]\[[7](http://arxiv.org/abs/1804.07461)\]\[[10](http://arxiv.org/abs/1802.05365)\].
