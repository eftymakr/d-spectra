## Related Works

Time series forecasting has been extensively studied due to its critical role in various domains, including finance, energy, and traffic management. Early approaches relied on autoregressive models and Gaussian processes, which often struggled with capturing complex temporal dependencies and long-term patterns \[[5](http://arxiv.org/abs/1703.07015)\]. More recently, deep learning models such as Long-Short-Term Memory (LSTM) networks and Gated Recurrent Units (GRUs) have been proposed to better model sequential data \[[15](http://arxiv.org/abs/2403.07815)\]. However, these models still face challenges in capturing global dependencies and handling long-term forecasting tasks effectively \[[17](http://arxiv.org/abs/2310.06625)\].

Transformers have emerged as a powerful alternative due to their ability to model long-range dependencies through self-attention mechanisms \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/1907.00235)\]. However, the canonical Transformer architecture suffers from two major limitations: (1) locality-agnostic behavior, which makes the model insensitive to local context and prone to anomalies in time series, and (2) a quadratic memory complexity with respect to sequence length, which limits their applicability to long time series \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/1907.00235)\]. To address these issues, several works have proposed modifications to the Transformer architecture. For instance, LogSparse Transformer reduces the memory cost to $O(L(\log L)^2)$, enabling efficient modeling of long time series \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/1907.00235)\]. Similarly, Convolutional Self-Attention incorporates causal convolutions to better capture local context within the attention mechanism \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/1907.00235)\].

In addition to architectural improvements, recent works have focused on improving the interpretability and performance of time series forecasting models. The Temporal Fusion Transformer (TFT) combines recurrent layers for local processing with self-attention layers for learning long-term dependencies, enabling interpretable insights into temporal dynamics \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/1912.09363)\]. Similarly, the Temporal Pattern Attention (TPA) model introduces a novel attention mechanism that captures temporal patterns across multiple time steps, leading to state-of-the-art performance in multivariate forecasting tasks \[[4](http://arxiv.org/abs/1809.04206)\].

For financial time series forecasting, the DeepAR model leverages autoregressive recurrent networks to produce probabilistic forecasts, demonstrating significant accuracy improvements over classical methods \[[14](http://arxiv.org/abs/1704.04110)\]. More recently, the Chronos framework has been introduced as a pretrained probabilistic time series model, achieving competitive performance on both training and zero-shot tasks \[[15](http://arxiv.org/abs/2403.07815)\]. Additionally, the iTransformer model proposes an inverted Transformer architecture that improves forecasting accuracy for series with larger lookback windows by applying attention and feed-forward networks on inverted dimensions \[[17](http://arxiv.org/abs/2310.06625)\].

The proposed RTS-PnO framework builds upon these advancements by introducing a risk-aware approach that addresses the limitations of prediction-only and Predict-then-Optimize methods.

### <a id='category-time-series-forecasting-models'></a>Time Series Forecasting Models: 15 papers found.
Time series forecasting models have seen significant advancements, particularly in addressing the challenges of long-term dependencies, non-stationarity, and computational efficiency. Traditional methods such as Autoregressive models and Gaussian Processes often struggle with complex, non-linear interdependencies and mixed patterns in real-world data \[[4](http://arxiv.org/abs/1809.04206)\]. To overcome these limitations, researchers have proposed novel architectures like the Long- and Short-term Time-series network (LSTNet), which integrates Convolutional Neural Networks (CNN) and Recurrent Neural Networks (RNN) to capture both short-term local patterns and long-term trends \[[5](http://arxiv.org/abs/1703.07015)\]. Similarly, the PatchTST model introduces a patching design that segments time series into subseries-level patches, reducing computational complexity while retaining local semantic information \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/2211.14730)\]. Transformers, despite their global-range modeling capabilities, face issues with non-stationary data and memory bottlenecks, which have been addressed by models like Non-stationary Transformers, incorporating modules for series stationarization and de-stationary attention \[[6](http://arxiv.org/abs/2205.14415)\]. Additionally, the iTransformer model reimagines the Transformer architecture by inverting dimensions to better handle multivariate time series and arbitrary lookback windows \[[17](http://arxiv.org/abs/2310.06625)\]. These innovations highlight the ongoing efforts to improve forecasting accuracy, computational efficiency, and adaptability to diverse time series tasks.

### <a id='category-others'></a>Others: 6 papers found.
The category "Others" encompasses a variety of advanced time-series forecasting methodologies that address the limitations of traditional approaches and enhance predictive accuracy and interpretability. These methods include the Temporal Fusion Transformer (TFT), which integrates attention-based mechanisms with recurrent layers to capture both local and long-term temporal dependencies, offering interpretable insights into temporal dynamics \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/1912.09363)\]. Additionally, the SegRNN model introduces strategies like segment-wise iterations and parallel multistep forecasting to reduce computational overhead while improving forecast accuracy, particularly in resource-constrained IoT environments \[[8](http://arxiv.org/abs/2308.11200)\]. The CoST framework leverages contrastive learning to disentangle seasonal and trend components, achieving significant improvements in forecasting performance across multivariate benchmarks \[[10](http://arxiv.org/abs/2202.01575)\]. Meanwhile, the StemGNN model enhances multivariate time-series forecasting by jointly capturing inter-series correlations and temporal dependencies in the spectral domain, eliminating the need for pre-defined priors \[[12](http://arxiv.org/abs/2103.07719)\]. Furthermore, hybrid models combine the data-driven flexibility of deep learning with the uncertainty estimation capabilities of classical models, offering a balanced approach to scalability and reliability \[[18](http://arxiv.org/abs/1905.12417)\]. Lastly, TSDiff introduces an unconditional diffusion model that enables task-agnostic generation for time series, supporting forecasting, refinement, and synthetic data generation without altering the training procedure \[[21](http://arxiv.org/abs/2307.11494)\]. These approaches collectively highlight the evolving landscape of time-series forecasting, emphasizing adaptability, efficiency, and interpretability.
