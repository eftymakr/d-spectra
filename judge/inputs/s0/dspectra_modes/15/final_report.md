## Related Works

Brain tumor segmentation is a critical task in medical imaging, with significant implications for diagnosis, treatment planning, and prognosis. Several approaches have been proposed to address this challenge, leveraging deep learning models such as U-Net, 3D CNNs, and more recently, transformer-based architectures. Early works focused on 2D U-Net architectures, which were adapted for 3D MRI data by introducing 3D convolutions and modifications to the encoder-decoder structure \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/1812.01049)\]. These models demonstrated promising results but faced limitations in computational efficiency and handling of class imbalance \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/1812.01049)\].

The introduction of 3D CNNs allowed for better modeling of spatial relationships in MRI data, but they often required substantial computational resources due to the high dimensionality of 3D volumes \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/2001.02040)\]. To mitigate this, researchers explored efficient architectures, such as the 11-layer multi-scale 3D CNN combined with a 3D fully connected CRF for post-processing, which improved performance while maintaining computational efficiency \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/1603.05959)\]. This approach was evaluated on challenging tasks like brain tumor and stroke segmentation, achieving top rankings on public benchmarks \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/1603.05959)\].

Another significant advancement was the development of the nnU-Net framework, which is self-adapting and designed to handle diverse medical imaging tasks with minimal manual tuning \[[4](http://arxiv.org/abs/2210.13336)\]. This framework demonstrated high performance across multiple segmentation challenges, achieving the highest mean dice scores in several tasks \[[4](http://arxiv.org/abs/2210.13336)\]. However, it still required extensive training and underutilized pre-trained weights, which limited its applicability in resource-constrained settings \[[4](http://arxiv.org/abs/2210.13336)\].

To address these limitations, researchers proposed integrating pre-trained weights from ImageNet into the U-Net framework, which improved performance and reduced training time \[[5](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/420f3f1078a6d8e0696572c032877079286051c6)\]. This approach was further extended by incorporating axial-coronal-sagittal convolutions, which helped in capturing multi-orientation features critical for accurate segmentation \[[5](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/420f3f1078a6d8e0696572c032877079286051c6)\]. These strategies were validated on the BraTS dataset, showing improved efficiency and performance \[[5](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/420f3f1078a6d8e0696572c032877079286051c6)\].

Additionally, joint classification and segmentation models were explored, where pre-trained encoders from a glioma grade classification task were leveraged to enhance segmentation performance, especially for challenging tumor labels \[[6](http://arxiv.org/abs/2011.11052)\]. This approach demonstrated that transfer learning from related tasks could improve segmentation accuracy, particularly in cases with limited training data \[[6](http://arxiv.org/abs/2011.11052)\].

These approaches highlight the ongoing efforts to develop more efficient and accurate segmentation methods, often leveraging pre-trained models and advanced training strategies to enhance performance \[[13](http://arxiv.org/abs/1802.10508)\].

### <a id='category-brain-tumor-segmentation-methods'></a>Brain Tumor Segmentation Methods: 9 papers found.
The related works in brain tumor segmentation focus on improving the accuracy and efficiency of 3D MRI segmentation using deep learning techniques. Early approaches relied on 2D networks, which had limited ability to capture 3D context, while 3D networks, though more accurate, required significant computational resources \[[10](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/b9c3631120c171570b94f946273b98c32505fc9a)\]. To address these challenges, researchers have explored hybrid architectures, such as 2.5D networks, which balance memory consumption and receptive field size, along with test-time augmentation to improve segmentation accuracy and provide uncertainty information \[[10](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/b9c3631120c171570b94f946273b98c32505fc9a)\]. Additionally, the use of pre-trained models, such as EfficientNet and ImageNet, has been investigated to transfer 2D knowledge to the 3D domain, reducing training time and improving performance \[[6](http://arxiv.org/abs/2011.11052)\]. More recently, transformer-based models, such as Swin UNETR, have been introduced to better capture long-range dependencies in 3D medical images, achieving state-of-the-art results on BraTS challenges \[[12](http://arxiv.org/abs/2201.01266)\]. Furthermore, lightweight architectures, like HDC-Net, have been developed to reduce computational complexity while maintaining high accuracy, making them suitable for clinical applications \[[20](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/9bf1c55d5441b29acf35c2193342723187dbe896)\]. These advancements highlight the ongoing efforts to enhance both the efficiency and effectiveness of brain tumor segmentation methods.

### <a id='category-medical-image-segmentation-techniques'></a>Medical Image Segmentation Techniques: 8 papers found.
The Related Works section for the paper's category on medical image segmentation techniques highlights the evolution and advancements in brain tumor segmentation using deep learning models. Traditional manual segmentation methods are time-consuming and highly dependent on operator expertise \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/2001.02040)\]. In response, fully automatic segmentation methods, such as U-Net-based deep convolutional networks, have been proposed to improve efficiency and accuracy \[[7](http://arxiv.org/abs/1705.03820)\]. U-Net has become a standard benchmark in medical image segmentation due to its encoder-decoder architecture and skip connections, which enable multi-contextual feature extraction \[[14](http://arxiv.org/abs/2010.13082)\]. However, U-Net's performance can be limited by insufficient feature utilization, prompting modifications such as densely connected blocks and residual inception blocks to enhance feature reusability and global-local information extraction \[[14](http://arxiv.org/abs/2010.13082)\]. Additionally, the nnU-Net framework has been introduced as a self-adapting, robust approach that minimizes manual adjustments and achieves high mean dice scores across multiple medical segmentation tasks \[[22](http://arxiv.org/abs/1809.10486)\]. Furthermore, ensemble methods combining 3D CNNs and U-Net have been explored to improve segmentation accuracy by leveraging complementary strengths of different architectures \[[24](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/281ede296764b95c329a7093e3b40b0b0c3ddee8)\]. The integration of pre-trained weights from ImageNet and strategies for transferring 2D pre-trained models to the 3D domain have also been investigated to reduce training time and improve model efficiency \[[16](http://arxiv.org/abs/2110.03352)\]. These approaches collectively address the challenges of brain tumor segmentation, including variability in tumor appearance, similarity with normal tissue, and the need for accurate delineation of tumor sub-regions \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/1603.05959)\].
