## Related Works

Neural retrieval methods using transformer-based pre-trained language models have significantly advanced multilingual and cross-lingual retrieval \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/1907.11692)\]. However, their effectiveness for low-resource, morphologically rich languages such as Amharic remains underexplored due to data scarcity and suboptimal tokenization \[[26](http://arxiv.org/abs/2503.18570)\]. This gap is addressed by introducing Amharic-specific dense retrieval models based on pre-trained Amharic BERT and RoBERTa backbones \[[26](http://arxiv.org/abs/2503.18570)\]. These models demonstrate notable improvements over existing multilingual baselines, such as Arctic Embed 2.0 \[[26](http://arxiv.org/abs/2503.18570)\].

The RoBERTa-Base-Amharic-Embed model (110M parameters) achieves a 17.6% relative improvement in MRR@10 and a 9.86% gain in Recall@10 over the strongest multilingual baseline \[[26](http://arxiv.org/abs/2503.18570)\]. This performance is attributed to the fine-tuning of pre-trained models on Amharic-specific data, which better captures the linguistic nuances of the language \[[26](http://arxiv.org/abs/2503.18570)\]. More compact variants, such as RoBERTa-Medium-Amharic-Embed (42M), remain competitive while being over 13x smaller, highlighting the trade-off between model size and performance \[[26](http://arxiv.org/abs/2503.18570)\].

Additionally, a ColBERT-based late interaction retrieval model is trained, achieving the highest MRR@10 score (0.843) among all evaluated models \[[26](http://arxiv.org/abs/2503.18570)\]. This model leverages late interaction to model fine-grained similarity between queries and documents, which is particularly beneficial for languages with complex morphological structures \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/2004.12832)\]. The effectiveness of late interaction models has been previously demonstrated in passage retrieval tasks, where they outperform traditional dense retrieval models \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/2004.12832)\].

The proposed models are benchmarked against both sparse and dense retrieval baselines to systematically assess retrieval effectiveness in Amharic \[[26](http://arxiv.org/abs/2503.18570)\]. This evaluation highlights key challenges in low-resource settings, such as the lack of sufficient training data and the need for language-specific adaptation \[[26](http://arxiv.org/abs/2503.18570)\]. These challenges are not unique to Amharic; similar issues have been observed in other low-resource languages \[[26](http://arxiv.org/abs/2503.18570)\].

To foster future research in low-resource IR, the dataset, codebase, and trained models are publicly released \[[26](http://arxiv.org/abs/2503.18570)\]. This release aligns with broader efforts to improve cross-lingual retrieval, such as the development of multilingual models like XLM-R \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/1911.02116)\] and the use of adapters for parameter-efficient zero-shot transfer \[[21](http://arxiv.org/abs/2212.10448)\].
