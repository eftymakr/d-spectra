## Related Works

Narrative summarization, the task of condensing long-form texts such as books, movies, and TV scripts, has been a challenging area for existing large language models (LLMs) due to the complexity of capturing intricate plotlines, character interactions, and thematic coherence \[[14](http://arxiv.org/abs/2311.10537)\]. While LLMs have shown significant progress in language understanding and generation, their ability to handle structured, long-form content remains limited \[[12](http://arxiv.org/abs/2005.14165)\]. This challenge is compounded by the need for coherent and high-quality summaries that maintain the original narrative's integrity \[[15](http://arxiv.org/abs/2210.03629)\].

Several approaches have been proposed to enhance LLMs' performance in summarization tasks. For instance, \[[12](http://arxiv.org/abs/2005.14165)\] highlights that while LLMs can perform well on various NLP tasks, their few-shot learning capabilities are still limited, especially on tasks requiring on-the-fly reasoning or domain adaptation. This limitation is also observed in \[[15](http://arxiv.org/abs/2210.03629)\], where the authors emphasize the difficulty of generating accurate summaries of complex narratives without fine-tuning. 

To address these challenges, recent works have explored multi-agent frameworks that leverage the strengths of multiple LLMs to improve summarization quality. For example, \[[14](http://arxiv.org/abs/2311.10537)\] introduces MedAgents, a framework that uses multiple LLMs in a collaborative setting to enhance medical reasoning, demonstrating the potential of multi-agent systems in complex domains. Similarly, \[[18](http://arxiv.org/abs/2407.06567)\] presents FinCon, a multi-agent LLM framework tailored for financial decision-making, which uses a manager-analyst communication hierarchy to improve decision-making outcomes. These frameworks suggest that multi-agent systems can effectively handle complex tasks by leveraging structured communication and collaboration.

In the context of narrative summarization, \[[15](http://arxiv.org/abs/2210.03629)\] explores the use of reasoning and acting in LLMs, proposing the ReAct framework that interleaves reasoning traces with task-specific actions. This approach improves human interpretability and trustworthiness, which is crucial for generating coherent summaries. Additionally, \[[13](http://arxiv.org/abs/2002.04235)\] introduces Learning Structured Communication (LSC), a framework that learns hierarchical communication structures for multi-agent reinforcement learning, which could be adapted for narrative summarization by enabling structured information exchange between agents.

The proposed NexusSum framework builds on these ideas by introducing a structured, sequential pipeline for narrative summarization without requiring fine-tuning.
