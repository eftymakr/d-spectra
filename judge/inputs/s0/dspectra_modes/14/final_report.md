## Related Works

Video super-resolution (VSR) has been a focal point in computer vision, with recent advances leveraging deep learning to address the challenges of temporal consistency and artifact reduction in high-resolution video generation. Early methods, such as those based on single-image super-resolution (SR), often failed to capture temporal dependencies, leading to inconsistent results across frames \[[7](http://arxiv.org/abs/1501.00092)\]. To address this, researchers introduced multi-frame SR approaches that incorporated motion estimation and compensation, such as optical flow-based methods \[[6](http://arxiv.org/abs/1611.05250)\]. However, these approaches were often computationally intensive and prone to errors due to inaccurate motion estimation \[[16](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/755227d7ea41b1d90df31f411f9ecbc998c20d26)\].

More recently, deep learning has enabled significant improvements in VSR. The introduction of deep convolutional neural networks (CNNs) for SR, such as the Enhanced Deep Residual Networks (EDSR) \[[9](http://arxiv.org/abs/1707.02921)\], demonstrated that residual learning could enhance performance by allowing deeper networks to train more effectively. These models were later extended to video restoration tasks, where temporal information from multiple frames was exploited using sliding window strategies or recurrent architectures \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/2201.12288)\]. However, these methods often suffered from high computational costs and limited temporal coherence \[[12](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/a2c50f93f6d0892918e0ff653750d198ccab48d0)\].

To further improve temporal consistency, researchers proposed recurrent architectures, such as the Bidirectional Recurrent Convolutional Network (BRCN) \[[12](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/a2c50f93f6d0892918e0ff653750d198ccab48d0)\], which modeled long-term dependencies using convolutional operations. This approach reduced parameter counts and improved efficiency while maintaining performance. Similarly, the Frame-Recurrent Video Super-Resolution (FR-VSR) framework \[[8](http://arxiv.org/abs/1801.04590)\] used previously inferred high-resolution (HR) estimates to super-resolve subsequent frames, leading to more temporally consistent results. This method also reduced computational costs by warping only one image per step \[[8](http://arxiv.org/abs/1801.04590)\].

Another line of research focused on incorporating attention mechanisms into VSR models. The Video Super-Resolution Transformer (VSR-Transformer) \[[19](http://arxiv.org/abs/2106.06847)\] introduced spatial-temporal convolutional self-attention layers to exploit locality and feature alignment, improving performance on benchmark datasets. Similarly, the Temporal Group Attention (TGA) method \[[20](http://arxiv.org/abs/2007.10595)\] divided input sequences into groups based on frame rates and used attention modules to integrate complementary information, achieving favorable performance against state-of-the-art methods \[[20](http://arxiv.org/abs/2007.10595)\].

In addition to attention-based models, diffusion-based approaches have gained traction for their ability to generate high-quality, temporally consistent videos. The Stable Video Diffusion (SVD) model \[[4](http://arxiv.org/abs/2311.15127)\] demonstrated that latent diffusion models could be adapted for video generation by introducing temporal layers and finetuning on high-quality datasets. ### <a id='category-video-super-resolution-techniques'></a>Video Super-Resolution Techniques: 13 papers found.
Video super-resolution (VSR) has evolved significantly, with recent approaches focusing on improving temporal consistency, computational efficiency, and handling complex motion patterns. Early methods relied heavily on motion estimation and compensation, which often introduced artifacts or increased computational costs \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/1511.04587)\]. More recent techniques, such as the Video Restoration Transformer (VRT), utilize self-attention mechanisms and parallel frame prediction to better exploit temporal information across multiple video frames, achieving superior performance in tasks like video super-resolution and deblurring \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/2201.12288)\]. Similarly, the spatio-temporal sub-pixel convolution networks introduced in \[[6](http://arxiv.org/abs/1611.05250)\] leverage early fusion, slow fusion, and 3D convolutions to enhance reconstruction accuracy while maintaining real-time speed. These networks also incorporate a fast multi-resolution spatial transformer module for efficient motion compensation. Another notable approach is the end-to-end trainable frame-recurrent video super-resolution framework \[[8](http://arxiv.org/abs/1801.04590)\], which reduces computational costs by warping only one image per step and encourages temporally consistent results through recurrent modeling. Additionally, the one-stage space-time video super-resolution framework \[[10](http://arxiv.org/abs/2002.11616)\] directly synthesizes high-resolution slow-motion videos from low-frame-rate, low-resolution inputs, improving both speed and quality compared to two-stage methods. The bidirectional recurrent convolutional network \[[12](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/a2c50f93f6d0892918e0ff653750d198ccab48d0)\] further enhances efficiency by using weight-sharing convolutional connections and 3D feedforward convolutions to model spatio-temporal patterns effectively. Finally, the progressive fusion network \[[16](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/755227d7ea41b1d90df31f411f9ecbc998c20d26)\] introduces an improved non-local operation to avoid complex motion estimation and compensation, achieving better efficiency and performance. These advancements collectively highlight the shift towards more efficient, accurate, and temporally consistent VSR methods that leverage deep learning and novel architectural designs.

### <a id='category-others'></a>Others: 2 papers found.
In the realm of video super-resolution (VSR), recent approaches have focused on mitigating artifacts and improving consistency across frames. For instance, recurrent VSR methods are known to propagate artifacts over time, making them more vulnerable than image super-resolution techniques \[[22](http://arxiv.org/abs/2407.13987)\]. To address this, researchers have explored attention mechanisms, such as spatial and channel attention, with channel attention showing less sensitivity to artifacts but leading to feature redundancy \[[22](http://arxiv.org/abs/2407.13987)\]. In contrast, methods like TimeSformer utilize self-attention over space and time, enabling spatiotemporal feature learning without convolutional operations \[[27](http://arxiv.org/abs/2102.05095)\]. This approach, which employs "divided attention" by separately applying spatial and temporal attention within each block, achieves state-of-the-art results on action recognition benchmarks \[[27](http://arxiv.org/abs/2102.05095)\]. Additionally, the proposed RealViformer framework, which is based on channel attention, surpasses existing methods by reducing artifacts and improving efficiency \[[22](http://arxiv.org/abs/2407.13987)\]. These developments highlight the ongoing efforts to enhance VSR performance through advanced attention mechanisms and self-supervised learning strategies.
