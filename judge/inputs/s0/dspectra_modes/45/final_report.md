Opinion mining has gained significant traction in recent years due to the increasing availability of user-generated content, particularly in app reviews \[[19](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/695886bf2b7bf90e98e98f630b26dabcdbe78247)\]. While most studies focus on sentiment polarity classification (positive, negative, neutral), the analysis of fine-grained emotions in app reviews remains underexplored \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2005.00547)\]. This gap is addressed in this work by adapting Plutchik’s emotion taxonomy to the context of app reviews and developing a structured annotation framework \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2005.00547)\]. 

Several studies have explored sentiment analysis in app reviews, highlighting the importance of user feedback for improving software quality and user satisfaction \[[7](http://arxiv.org/abs/1505.04657)\]. For instance, the MARK framework proposes a keyword-based approach for semi-automated review analysis, enabling developers to filter and summarize relevant feedback \[[7](http://arxiv.org/abs/1505.04657)\]. Similarly, AR-Miner presents a computational framework for mining informative reviews, grouping them using topic modeling and prioritizing them based on their relevance \[[12](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/ff921a209cc9e1c2120669d3d81e627ca5e53572)\]. These approaches, however, typically focus on sentiment polarity rather than fine-grained emotion classification \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2005.00547)\].

The GoEmotions dataset, which contains 58,000 manually annotated Reddit comments labeled for 27 emotion categories, represents one of the largest manually annotated datasets for fine-grained emotion analysis \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2005.00547)\]. The related works in sentiment analysis focus on the challenges of fine-grained emotion classification, particularly in domains such as social media, literature, and app reviews. Recent efforts have aimed to expand emotion typologies to include more detailed and context-sensitive labels, such as those found in poetry, where the focus is on the reader's emotional experience rather than the author's intent \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/1703.02256)\]. The use of advanced models, such as BERT-based approaches, has also shown promise in capturing complex emotional patterns, although challenges remain in achieving high accuracy for mixed and subtle emotions \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/1703.02256)\]. The findings from these studies align with the current work, which addresses the underexplored area of fine-grained emotion analysis in app reviews by adapting existing emotion taxonomies and developing a structured annotation framework \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2005.00547)\].

### <a id='category-affect-recognition-in-text-and-models'></a>Affect Recognition in Text and Models: 4 papers found.
The category "Affect Recognition in Text and Models" encompasses a range of research efforts aimed at understanding and modeling emotional content in text. However, challenges persist due to the complexity of language, the prevalence of non-standard expressions, and the lack of paralinguistic cues such as tone or stress \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2005.00547)\]. To address these challenges, researchers have explored the development of emotion lexicons, leveraging crowdsourcing to generate large, high-quality word-emotion associations \[[3](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/33c95f4d13958e65e676a9cd64bd4d8df0fdb44d)\]. These efforts have been complemented by the creation of large annotated datasets, which are crucial for training and evaluating models capable of fine-grained emotion detection \[[4](http://arxiv.org/abs/2005.11882)\]. Recent work has also focused on aligning language models with user intent through fine-tuning with human feedback, leading to improvements in truthfulness and reduction of toxic outputs while maintaining performance on standard NLP tasks \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/1703.02256)\]. Additionally, studies have highlighted the importance of structured annotation frameworks and iterative human annotation processes in defining clear guidelines for emotion classification, particularly in specialized domains such as app reviews \[[5](http://arxiv.org/abs/2003.07723)\]. These contributions collectively advance the field by addressing both the technical and practical challenges of affect recognition in text.
