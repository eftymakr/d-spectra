## Related Works

De novo genome assembly has been a focal point of computational biology research, driven by the need to reconstruct genomes from short, error-prone reads without a reference. Early approaches such as SOAPdenovo \[[1](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/f8d1052e2c9d1cdd63093427ccc9169e848d777d)\] laid the groundwork for de novo assembly, but they faced limitations in handling large genomes and repeat regions. Subsequent improvements like SOAPdenovo2 \[[1](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/f8d1052e2c9d1cdd63093427ccc9169e848d777d)\] introduced memory-efficient algorithms and enhanced scaffold construction, significantly improving coverage and accuracy. However, these methods still struggled with memory-bound behavior and irregular data patterns \[[1](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/f8d1052e2c9d1cdd63093427ccc9169e848d777d)\]. 

The development of de Bruijn graph-based assemblers, such as those used in the SHARCGS algorithm \[[14](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/fbd59e17f72c3aedb3cd06af2e50a91526ccd81a)\], enabled faster and more accurate assembly of short reads, particularly for bacterial and eukaryotic genomes. These tools demonstrated high accuracy and scalability, with SHARCGS achieving N50 sizes greater than 20 kbp for Drosophila and Arabidopsis \[[14](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/fbd59e17f72c3aedb3cd06af2e50a91526ccd81a)\]. Despite these advancements, the computational demands of de novo assembly remain high, especially for large genomes. 

Recent work has focused on optimizing assembly pipelines for extreme-scale data. For instance, the HipMER assembler \[[19](http://arxiv.org/abs/1705.11147)\] leverages efficient parallelization to handle large genomes, such as the human genome and hexaploid wheat, on supercomputers. Similarly, the SWAP-Assembler \[[20](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/fe8d86bc5520b5049104ff58ee7e980982816ebf)\] introduced a scalable framework for processing massive datasets using thousands of cores, achieving high-quality contigs with good N50 sizes and low error rates. These tools highlight the importance of parallel processing and memory management in handling the computational complexity of de novo assembly.

The challenge of memory usage and computational efficiency has also been addressed in distributed-memory systems. The diBELLA 2D pipeline \[[5](http://arxiv.org/abs/2010.10055)\] introduced new algorithms for overlap detection and transitive reduction, achieving near-linear scaling and significant performance improvements. These methods are critical for handling the large datasets generated by next-generation sequencing technologies. 

Moreover, the use of probabilistic and heuristic methods has been explored to improve assembly accuracy. For example, the MinHash Alignment Process (MHAP) \[[15](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/618d82a202bb0408e388eba250c66c8a90700c81)\] enables the assembly of long reads with high accuracy by using locality-sensitive hashing. This approach has been successfully applied to assemble large eukaryotic genomes, including Saccharomyces cerevisiae and Drosophila melanogaster, with high continuity and accuracy.

Genome assembly algorithms have evolved significantly to address the challenges of de novo assembly, particularly with the advent of long-read sequencing technologies and the need for efficient, scalable solutions \[[3](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/91c964330beeed2c5c875d18633793a548a987cf)\]. Early approaches, such as SHARCGS, focused on short-read assembly with high accuracy and speed, demonstrating the feasibility of de novo assembly for eukaryotic genomes \[[14](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/fbd59e17f72c3aedb3cd06af2e50a91526ccd81a)\]. More recent advancements, like Canu and MinHash Alignment Process (MHAP), have specifically targeted the issues of error rates and repeat resolution in long-read sequencing, improving assembly continuity and accuracy \[[7](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/2b59fd18ada769ef76fb7fcc7382f063791abfab)\]\[[15](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/618d82a202bb0408e388eba250c66c8a90700c81)\]. These algorithms leverage probabilistic methods and adaptive strategies to handle noisy data, enabling the reconstruction of high-quality genomes \[[15](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/618d82a202bb0408e388eba250c66c8a90700c81)\]. Additionally, distributed-memory parallel algorithms, such as those implemented in diBELLA 2D, have been developed to address the computational intensity of large-scale genome assembly, achieving near-linear scaling and significant runtime reductions \[[5](http://arxiv.org/abs/2010.10055)\]. The use of entropy-compressed data structures and succinct graph representations has also contributed to more efficient memory usage and better handling of sequencing errors \[[11](http://arxiv.org/abs/1008.2555)\]. These developments highlight the ongoing efforts to enhance both the accuracy and scalability of de novo assembly algorithms, addressing the computational and memory challenges inherent in the process \[[3](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/91c964330beeed2c5c875d18633793a548a987cf)\]\[[5](http://arxiv.org/abs/2010.10055)\]\[[7](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/2b59fd18ada769ef76fb7fcc7382f063791abfab)\]\[[11](http://arxiv.org/abs/1008.2555)\]\[[14](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/fbd59e17f72c3aedb3cd06af2e50a91526ccd81a)\]\[[15](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/618d82a202bb0408e388eba250c66c8a90700c81)\].

### <a id='category-others'></a>Others: 8 papers found.
The category "Others" encompasses a range of research addressing the challenges and advancements in de novo genome assembly, particularly in the context of computational efficiency, accuracy, and scalability. Several studies highlight the limitations of current short-read sequencing technologies, which often result in fragmented and incomplete assemblies due to their inability to resolve complex repeats and structural variations \[[2](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/c6e319a023f932d6d2ff8897c2c91e56e872db8e)\]. To address these issues, researchers have explored alternative approaches, such as long-read sequencing and hybrid methods, which offer improved contiguity and accuracy \[[1](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/f8d1052e2c9d1cdd63093427ccc9169e848d777d)\]. Additionally, the computational demands of de novo assembly have led to the development of distributed and parallel processing frameworks, such as SWAP-Assembler, which can handle massive datasets efficiently and scale up to thousands of cores \[[5](http://arxiv.org/abs/2010.10055)\]. These tools demonstrate significant improvements in assembly quality, with high N50 values and reduced error rates \[[5](http://arxiv.org/abs/2010.10055)\]. Furthermore, the integration of genetic linkage maps with de novo assemblies has been proposed as a method to resolve misassemblies and anchor chromosome-scale sequences, enhancing the accuracy of genome reconstruction \[[8](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/d7827222e6d751e13b1fe74b97ce413a037abfaf)\]. The use of k-mer frequency analysis has also emerged as an assembly-independent method for estimating genomic characteristics like genome size and repeat content, providing valuable insights without the need for a reference genome \[[4](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/f5cfb387a6bd8a1cb7bdf07562fadb9a351e37cd)\]. Finally, recent studies have explored the potential of quantum computing and optimization techniques to address the computational complexity of genome assembly, offering promising avenues for future advancements \[[7](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/2b59fd18ada769ef76fb7fcc7382f063791abfab)\]. These contributions collectively underscore the ongoing efforts to improve the efficiency, accuracy, and scalability of de novo genome assembly methods.

[Snippet]: «BackgroundThe current revolution in genomics has been made possible by software tools called genome assemblers, which stitch together DNA fragments “read” by sequencing machines into complete or nearly complete genome sequences. Despite decades of research in this field and the development of dozens of genome assemblers, assessing and comparing the quality of assembled genome sequences still relies on the availability of independently determined standards, such as manually curated genome sequences, or independently produced mapping data. These “gold standards” can be expensive to produce and may only cover a small fraction of the genome, which limits their applicability to newly generated genome sequences. Here we introduce a de novo probabilistic measure of assembly quality which allows for an objective comparison of multiple assemblies generated from the same set of reads. We define the quality of a sequence produced by an assembler as the conditional probability of observing the sequenced reads from the assembled sequence. A key property of our metric is that the true genome sequence maximizes the score, unlike other commonly used metrics.ResultsWe demonstrate that our de novo score can be computed quickly and accurately in a practical setting even for large datasets, by estimating the score from a relatively small sample of the reads. To demonstrate the benefits of our score, we measure the quality of the assemblies generated in the GAGE and Assemblathon 1 assembly “bake-offs” with our metric. Even without knowledge of the true reference sequence, our de novo metric closely matches the reference-based evaluation metrics used in the studies and outperforms other de novo metrics traditionally used to measure assembly quality (such as N50). Finally, we highlight the application of our score to optimize assembly parameters used in genome assemblers, which enables better assemblies to be produced, even without prior knowledge of the genome being assembled.ConclusionLikelihood-based measures, such as ours proposed here, will become the new standard for de novo assembly evaluation.»
