## Related Works

Maximum Inner Product Search (MIPS) has been a central challenge in machine learning and information retrieval, particularly in high-dimensional data applications \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/1405.5869)\]. Existing approaches to MIPS typically fall into two categories: those that rely solely on inner product (IP) similarity, which can suffer from local optima and redundant computations, and those that reduce MIPS to nearest neighbor search under the Euclidean metric via space projection, which can lead to topology destruction and information loss \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/1405.5869)\]. However, recent studies have shown that these two paradigms are not inherently mutually exclusive \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/1405.5869)\]. Instead, integrating IP and Euclidean metrics in the design of indexing and search algorithms can significantly enhance MIPS performance \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/1405.5869)\].

Asymmetric LSH (ALSH) was introduced as the first provably sublinear time algorithm for approximate MIPS, leveraging asymmetric transformations to convert the problem into approximate near neighbor search \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/1509.01469)\]. This approach was later improved by proposing a different transformation that converted MIPS into approximate cosine similarity search using signed random projections \[[4](http://arxiv.org/abs/1410.5410)\]. These developments laid the foundation for efficient hashing-based MIPS solutions \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/1509.01469)\]\[[4](http://arxiv.org/abs/1410.5410)\].

Quantization-based methods have also emerged as a promising alternative to LSH for fast approximate MIPS. These methods quantize database vectors into multiple subspaces using learned codebooks, approximating inner products as the sum of inner products with subspace quantizers \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/1610.03317)\]. This approach avoids the need for augmenting vectors in a higher-dimensional space, and further improves accuracy when small sample queries are available during training \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/1610.03317)\].

Graph-based approaches have shown strong empirical advantages in real-world MIPS tasks. The inner-product navigable small world graph (ip-NSW) represents a state-of-the-art method for approximate MIPS, achieving an order of magnitude speedup over baselines \[[5](http://arxiv.org/abs/1909.13459)\]. However, its performance is closely tied to the norm bias in the MIPS problem, where large norm items are more likely to be the result of MIPS \[[5](http://arxiv.org/abs/1909.13459)\]. To address this, ip-NSW+ was proposed, which introduces an additional angular proximity graph to improve search efficiency and robustness \[[5](http://arxiv.org/abs/1909.13459)\].

Clustering-based methods have also been explored for approximate MIPS, with spherical $k$-means adapted to solve the problem \[[6](http://arxiv.org/abs/1507.05910)\]. These methods compare favorably with LSH-based approaches in terms of accuracy, particularly for large search spaces \[[6](http://arxiv.org/abs/1507.05910)\]. Similarly, norm-adjusted proximity graphs (NAPG) have been proposed to improve graph-based indexing for inner product search by adjusting factors estimated on sampled data \[[7](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/25454f456c514219c9d6eeb42200ebbf378db9ca)\].
