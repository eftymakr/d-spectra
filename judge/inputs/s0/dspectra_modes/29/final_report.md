## Related Works

Maximum Inner Product Search (MIPS) has been a central challenge in machine learning and information retrieval, particularly in high-dimensional data applications \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/1405.5869)\]. Existing approaches to MIPS typically fall into two categories: those that rely solely on inner product (IP) similarity, which can suffer from local optima and redundant computations, and those that reduce MIPS to nearest neighbor search under the Euclidean metric via space projection, which can lead to topology destruction and information loss \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/1405.5869)\]. However, recent studies have shown that these two paradigms are not inherently mutually exclusive \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/1405.5869)\]. Instead, integrating IP and Euclidean metrics in the design of indexing and search algorithms can significantly enhance MIPS performance \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/1405.5869)\].

Asymmetric LSH (ALSH) was introduced as the first provably sublinear time algorithm for approximate MIPS, leveraging asymmetric transformations to convert the problem into approximate near neighbor search \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/1509.01469)\]. This approach was later improved by proposing a different transformation that converted MIPS into approximate cosine similarity search using signed random projections \[[4](http://arxiv.org/abs/1410.5410)\]. These developments laid the foundation for efficient hashing-based MIPS solutions \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/1509.01469)\]\[[4](http://arxiv.org/abs/1410.5410)\].

Quantization-based methods have also emerged as a promising alternative to LSH for fast approximate MIPS. These methods quantize database vectors into multiple subspaces using learned codebooks, approximating inner products as the sum of inner products with subspace quantizers \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/1610.03317)\]. This approach avoids the need for augmenting vectors in a higher-dimensional space, and further improves accuracy when small sample queries are available during training \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/1610.03317)\].

Graph-based approaches have shown strong empirical advantages in real-world MIPS tasks. The inner-product navigable small world graph (ip-NSW) represents a state-of-the-art method for approximate MIPS, achieving an order of magnitude speedup over baselines \[[5](http://arxiv.org/abs/1909.13459)\]. However, its performance is closely tied to the norm bias in the MIPS problem, where large norm items are more likely to be the result of MIPS \[[5](http://arxiv.org/abs/1909.13459)\]. To address this, ip-NSW+ was proposed, which introduces an additional angular proximity graph to improve search efficiency and robustness \[[5](http://arxiv.org/abs/1909.13459)\].

Clustering-based methods have also been explored for approximate MIPS, with spherical $k$-means adapted to solve the problem \[[6](http://arxiv.org/abs/1507.05910)\]. These methods compare favorably with LSH-based approaches in terms of accuracy, particularly for large search spaces \[[6](http://arxiv.org/abs/1507.05910)\]. Similarly, norm-adjusted proximity graphs (NAPG) have been proposed to improve graph-based indexing for inner product search by adjusting factors estimated on sampled data \[[7](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/25454f456c514219c9d6eeb42200ebbf378db9ca)\].

Maximum Inner Product Search (MIPS) has been a focal point in machine learning and information retrieval, particularly for high-dimensional data applications \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/1405.5869)\]. Early approaches either relied solely on inner product similarity, which faced issues with local optima and redundant computations, or reduced MIPS to nearest neighbor search under the Euclidean metric, leading to topology distortion and information loss \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/1405.5869)\]. However, recent works have explored asymmetric transformations to convert MIPS into near neighbor search problems, enabling efficient sublinear time algorithms \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/1405.5869)\]. For instance, the extended asymmetric LSH (ALSH) framework allows for hashing schemes that handle un-normalized inner products, addressing the limitations of traditional LSH \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/1405.5869)\]. Additionally, some studies have proposed alternative transformations, such as converting MIPS into cosine similarity search, which can be efficiently solved using signed random projections \[[4](http://arxiv.org/abs/1410.5410)\]. These methods have shown significant improvements in both theoretical guarantees and practical performance. Furthermore, budgeted MIPS approaches have emerged, where algorithms like Greedy-MIPS and wedge-based methods aim to balance search efficiency and quality by incorporating computational constraints \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/1610.03317)\]\[[14](http://arxiv.org/abs/1908.08656)\]. The development of novel indexing structures, such as the Metric-Amphibious Graph (MAG), has also contributed to improving MIPS performance by leveraging graph-based search strategies and adaptive navigation techniques \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/1405.5869)\]. These advancements highlight the ongoing efforts to enhance MIPS through diverse algorithmic and theoretical innovations.

[Snippet]: «We propose a quantization based approach for fast approximate Maximum Inner Product Search (MIPS). Each database vector is quantized in multiple subspaces via a set of codebooks, learned directly by minimizing the inner product quantization error. Then, the inner product of a query to a database vector is approximated as the sum of inner products with the subspace quantizers. Different from recently proposed LSH approaches to MIPS, the database vectors and queries do not need to be augmented in a higher dimensional feature space. We also provide a theoretical analysis of the proposed approach, consisting of the concentration results under mild assumptions. Furthermore, if a small sample of example queries is given at the training time, we propose a modified codebook learning procedure which further improves the accuracy. Experimental results on a variety of datasets including those arising from deep neural networks show that the proposed approach significantly outperforms the existing state-of-the-art.»

[Snippet]: «The inner-product navigable small world graph (ip-NSW) represents the state-of-the-art method for approximate maximum inner product search (MIPS) and it can achieve an order of magnitude speedup over the fastest baseline. However, to date it is still unclear where its exceptional performance comes from. In this paper, we show that there is a strong norm bias in the MIPS problem, which means that the large norm items are very likely to become the result of MIPS. Then we explain the good performance of ip-NSW as matching the norm bias of the MIPS problem — large norm items have big in-degrees in the ip-NSW proximity graph and a walk on the graph spends the majority of computation on these items, thus effectively avoids unnecessary computation on small norm items. Furthermore, we propose the ip-NSW+ algorithm, which improves ip-NSW by introducing an additional angular proximity graph. Search is first conducted on the angular graph to find the angular neighbors of a query and then the MIPS neighbors of these angular neighbors are used to initialize the candidate pool for search on the inner-product proximity graph. Experiment results show that ip-NSW+ consistently and significantly outperforms ip-NSW and provides more robust performance under different data distributions.»

[Snippet]: «Locality Sensitive Hashing (LSH) techniques have recently become a popular solution for solving the approximate Maximum Inner Product Search (MIPS) problem, which arises in many situations and have in particular been used as a speed-up for the training of large neural probabilistic language models. In this paper we propose a new approach for solving approximate MIPS based on a variant of the $k$-means algorithm. We suggest using spherical $k$-means which is an algorithm that can efficiently be used to solve the approximate Maximum Cosine Similarity Search (MCSS), and basing ourselves on previous work by Shrivastava and Li we show how it can be adapted for approximate MIPS. Our new method compares favorably with LSH-based methods on a simple recall rate test, by providing a more accurate set of candidates for the maximum inner product. The proposed method is thus likely to benefit the wide range of problems with very large search spaces where a robust approximate MIPS heuristic could be of interest, such as for providing a high quality short list of candidate words to speed up the training of neural probabilistic language models.»
