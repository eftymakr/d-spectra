## Related Works

Neural retrieval models have shown significant promise in improving Web search performance by learning dense representations of queries and documents for semantic matching \[[17](http://arxiv.org/abs/2102.11903)\]. However, training these models typically requires a large amount of labeled query-document pairs, which are expensive to obtain \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2305.18290)\]. Recent work has explored the use of synthetic queries generated by large language models as a scalable alternative to labeled data \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2305.18290)\]. While this approach offers cost savings, synthetic queries often vary in quality, leading to suboptimal retrieval performance \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2305.18290)\]. 

Existing methods typically filter out noisy query-document pairs using signals from an external re-ranker \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2305.18290)\]. This approach, however, introduces additional complexity and may not fully address the issue of query quality during training. In contrast, our work proposes a framework that integrates ranking signals directly into the query generation process using Direct Preference Optimization (DPO) \[[9](http://arxiv.org/abs/2402.13228)\]. This approach allows for the direct optimization of query generation to maximize downstream retrieval effectiveness, without relying on external re-rankers.

DPO has been shown to improve the performance of large language models on various downstream tasks, including reasoning, summarization, and alignment \[[9](http://arxiv.org/abs/2402.13228)\]. By modeling the relative probability of picking one response over another, DPO enables the fine-tuning of models to align with human preferences more effectively than traditional reinforcement learning methods \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2305.18290)\]. This is particularly relevant for our work, as we aim to generate high-quality synthetic queries that maximize retrieval effectiveness.

In addition to DPO, several other approaches have been proposed to improve the quality of synthetic queries. For instance, the work of \[[18](http://arxiv.org/abs/2010.08191)\] introduces RocketQA, an optimized training approach for dense passage retrieval that addresses challenges such as the discrepancy between training and inference, the existence of unlabeled positives, and limited training data. RocketQA incorporates strategies such as cross-batch negatives, denoised hard negatives, and data augmentation to improve retrieval performance \[[18](http://arxiv.org/abs/2010.08191)\]. While RocketQA focuses on passage retrieval, its strategies for improving query-document pair quality are relevant to our work.

Another line of research explores the use of weak supervision for training neural ranking models \[[5](http://arxiv.org/abs/1704.08803)\]. This approach leverages automatically generated labels, such as those from BM25, to train models without requiring extensive human annotation \[[5](http://arxiv.org/abs/1704.08803)\]. This is particularly useful when labeled data is scarce, as is often the case with synthetic queries. However, weak supervision may not always capture the nuances of query-document relevance as effectively as human-labeled data \[[5](http://arxiv.org/abs/1704.08803)\].

### <a id='category-ad-hoc-retrieval-models'></a>Ad-hoc Retrieval Models: 5 papers found.
Ad-hoc retrieval models have seen significant advancements through the integration of deep learning techniques, particularly in addressing the challenges of relevance matching and leveraging weakly supervised signals. Recent studies highlight the limitations of traditional models like BM25, which rely on exact term matching, and demonstrate that deep neural networks can offer superior performance when properly designed \[[5](http://arxiv.org/abs/1704.08803)\]. For instance, the Direct Preference Optimization (DPO) framework introduces a novel parameterization of the reward model in reinforcement learning from human feedback, enabling closed-form policy extraction and simplifying the fine-tuning process \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2305.18290)\]. This approach not only improves stability and computational efficiency but also enhances the control over generated outputs, such as sentiment and response quality \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2305.18290)\]. Additionally, models like the Deep Relevance Matching Model (DRMM) focus on addressing the unique aspects of ad-hoc retrieval by incorporating joint architectures that handle exact matching signals, query term importance, and diverse matching requirements \[[6](http://arxiv.org/abs/1711.08611)\]. Similarly, the "duet" model combines local and distributed representations to enhance ranking performance, outperforming both traditional and other neural models \[[11](http://arxiv.org/abs/1610.08136)\]. These developments underscore the potential of deep learning in ad-hoc retrieval, especially when combined with weak supervision and innovative training strategies.

### <a id='category-dense-retrieval-and-question-answering'></a>Dense Retrieval and Question Answering: 5 papers found.
In the realm of dense retrieval and question answering, recent advancements have focused on improving retrieval effectiveness through innovative methods such as document expansion and the use of dense embeddings \[[12](http://arxiv.org/abs/2004.04906)\]. One approach involves predicting queries that a document can answer and expanding the document with these predicted queries using a sequence-to-sequence model, which has shown promising results when combined with re-ranking components \[[4](http://arxiv.org/abs/1904.08375)\]. Additionally, sparse representations have been explored for their efficiency and ability to maintain exact term matching, with models like SPLADE achieving competitive performance against dense approaches \[[10](http://arxiv.org/abs/2109.10086)\]. The shift towards dense retrieval has also been driven by the need for more efficient and effective ranking mechanisms, with methods such as hard negative sampling and dynamic training strategies being proposed to enhance model stability and performance \[[14](http://arxiv.org/abs/2104.08051)\]. Furthermore, the use of synthetic queries generated by large language models has gained traction as a scalable alternative to labeled data, though challenges remain in ensuring the quality of these synthetic queries \[[18](http://arxiv.org/abs/2010.08191)\]. These developments highlight the ongoing efforts to refine retrieval systems for better accuracy and efficiency in open-domain question answering tasks.

### <a id='category-reinforcement-learning-optimization'></a>Reinforcement Learning Optimization: 2 papers found.
Direct Preference Optimization (DPO) has emerged as a powerful technique for improving the performance of large language models (LLMs) on downstream tasks such as reasoning, summarization, and alignment \[[9](http://arxiv.org/abs/2402.13228)\]. DPO works by using pairs of preferred and dispreferred data to model the relative probability of selecting one response over another, which allows for more effective fine-tuning \[[9](http://arxiv.org/abs/2402.13228)\]. However, the standard DPO loss can sometimes lead to a reduction in the model's likelihood of preferred examples, especially when the edit distance between completions is low \[[9](http://arxiv.org/abs/2402.13228)\]. To address this issue, researchers have proposed DPO-Positive (DPOP), a new loss function and training procedure that avoids this failure mode and outperforms DPO and other fine-tuning methods across various datasets and tasks \[[9](http://arxiv.org/abs/2402.13228)\]. Additionally, DPOP has been shown to improve performance on benchmarks independent of the fine-tuning data, such as MT-Bench \[[9](http://arxiv.org/abs/2402.13228)\]. These advancements highlight the potential of DPO and its variants in enhancing the effectiveness of LLMs in real-world applications.
