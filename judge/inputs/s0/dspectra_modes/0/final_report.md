## Related Works

Economic inequality remains a critical challenge with significant implications for education, healthcare, and social stability \[[10](http://arxiv.org/abs/2311.17252)\]. Traditional systems, such as the U.S. federal income tax, aim to reduce inequality but often lack adaptability to dynamic economic conditions \[[10](http://arxiv.org/abs/2311.17252)\]. Recent approaches, such as the Saez Optimal Taxation model, attempt to address this by dynamically adjusting tax policies \[[10](http://arxiv.org/abs/2311.17252)\]. However, these models often fail to account for taxpayer heterogeneity and irrational behavior, limiting their effectiveness in real-world applications \[[10](http://arxiv.org/abs/2311.17252)\]. 

Agent-based modeling (ABM) has been increasingly used to simulate complex economic systems and policy interventions \[[7](http://arxiv.org/abs/2006.05048)\]. ABMs allow for the exploration of emergent behaviors and the evaluation of policy impacts through the interaction of heterogeneous agents \[[7](http://arxiv.org/abs/2006.05048)\]. For instance, studies have shown that ABMs can effectively model economic inequality and the effects of various policy interventions \[[9](http://arxiv.org/abs/1804.09284)\]. However, many existing ABMs rely on heuristic decision-making rules or regressions trained on historical data, which may not fully capture the adaptive and rational behavior of economic agents \[[7](http://arxiv.org/abs/2006.05048)\]. 

Recent advancements in large language models (LLMs) have opened new possibilities for integrating sophisticated reasoning and adaptive decision-making into economic models \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2309.02144)\]. LLMs have demonstrated the ability to generate reasoning traces and task-specific actions, enhancing their utility in complex decision-making scenarios \[[4](http://arxiv.org/abs/2210.03629)\]. The ReAct framework, which synergizes reasoning and acting in LLMs, has shown promise in improving task-solving trajectories and human interpretability \[[4](http://arxiv.org/abs/2210.03629)\]. Similarly, the Toolformer model enables LLMs to self-teach the use of external tools, achieving improved performance on a range of tasks without sacrificing core language modeling abilities \[[6](http://arxiv.org/abs/2302.04761)\]. 

The integration of LLMs into ABMs, as proposed in the TaxAgent model, represents a novel approach to designing adaptive tax policies \[[10](http://arxiv.org/abs/2311.17252)\]. By leveraging LLMs to iteratively optimize tax rates, TaxAgent balances equity and productivity, achieving superior equity-efficiency trade-offs compared to traditional models \[[10](http://arxiv.org/abs/2311.17252)\]. This approach aligns with broader efforts to enhance the capabilities of LLMs in economic modeling, such as the use of LLMs to simulate and predict economic outcomes \[[12](http://arxiv.org/abs/2405.11357)\].
