## Related Works

Vision Transformers (ViTs) have gained significant traction in computer vision due to their ability to capture global context effectively \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2208.05163)\]. However, their deployment on hardware, particularly FPGAs, remains challenging due to the complexity of their arithmetic operations, such as Softmax and LayerNorm, which are sensitive to model accuracy and difficult to map onto efficient hardware with low precision \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2208.05163)\]. Existing methods often exploit parallelism in matrix multiplication operations while keeping these complex operations on the CPU, resulting in suboptimal performance due to communication overhead between the CPU and accelerator \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2208.05163)\]. This section reviews related works that address these challenges through hardware acceleration, quantization, and efficient design strategies.

Several studies have explored hardware acceleration for ViTs. Auto-ViT-Acc proposes an FPGA-aware automatic acceleration framework based on mixed-scheme quantization, achieving a 5.6x improvement in frame rate with minimal accuracy loss \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/2211.08110)\]. HeatViT introduces a hardware-efficient token pruning framework that reduces computation by up to 65.3% while maintaining high accuracy \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/2210.09573)\]. ViTCoD focuses on algorithm and accelerator co-design, achieving up to 235.3× speedup by reducing dominant data movement costs \[[4](http://arxiv.org/abs/2302.09108)\]. ViTA presents a configurable hardware accelerator for edge deployment, achieving nearly 90% hardware utilization efficiency and a power consumption of 0.88W \[[5](http://arxiv.org/abs/2205.11397)\]. SuperViT reduces computational costs while improving accuracy, achieving a 2× reduction in FLOPs with a 0.2% accuracy gain \[[6](http://arxiv.org/abs/2211.05109)\]. VITALITY unifies low-rank and sparse approximation for ViT acceleration, achieving 3× faster inference with comparable accuracy \[[7](http://arxiv.org/abs/2103.15358)\]. 

Efficient ViT architectures have also been explored. Multi-Scale Vision Longformer enhances ViT for high-resolution image encoding using linear complexity attention mechanisms \[[8](http://arxiv.org/abs/2101.11986)\]. Tokens-to-Token ViT improves training efficiency by structuring image tokens progressively, reducing parameter count and MACs by half \[[9](http://arxiv.org/abs/2103.14899)\]. CrossViT introduces cross-attention for multi-scale feature fusion, achieving a 2% accuracy improvement over DeiT \[[10](http://arxiv.org/abs/2201.06618)\]. VAQF proposes a fully automatic framework for low-bit ViT acceleration on FPGAs, achieving 24 FPS with 8-bit activation quantization \[[11](http://arxiv.org/abs/2403.20230)\]. ### <a id='category-vision-transformer-acceleration-techniques'></a>Vision Transformer Acceleration Techniques: 10 papers found.
Vision Transformer (ViT) acceleration has been a focal point in recent research, addressing the computational and memory challenges inherent in ViTs. Existing approaches often rely on hardware acceleration for matrix multiplication while offloading complex operations like Softmax and LayerNorm to the CPU, leading to communication overhead and suboptimal performance \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2208.05163)\]. To mitigate this, several works have proposed hardware-aware acceleration frameworks. For instance, an FPGA-based framework utilizing mixed-scheme quantization has been introduced, achieving a 5.6x improvement in frame rate with minimal accuracy loss \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2208.05163)\]. Similarly, the HeatViT framework employs token pruning and 8-bit quantization to enhance hardware efficiency, achieving up to 65.3% computation reduction while maintaining high accuracy \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/2211.08110)\]. Another study, ViTCoD, integrates algorithmic and hardware co-design, enabling significant speedups by reducing data movement costs, with results up to 235.3x over CPUs under 90% attention sparsity \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/2210.09573)\]. The ViTA accelerator targets resource-constrained edge devices, employing head-level pipelining and inter-layer optimizations to achieve nearly 90% hardware utilization and low power consumption \[[4](http://arxiv.org/abs/2302.09108)\]. Additionally, the T2T-ViT model improves ViT efficiency by reducing parameters and MACs, achieving better performance than ResNets and MobileNets \[[6](http://arxiv.org/abs/2211.05109)\]. The CrossViT approach introduces a dual-branch transformer with efficient token fusion, outperforming DeiT by 2% with a moderate increase in FLOPs \[[7](http://arxiv.org/abs/2103.15358)\]. The EfficientViT architecture combines convolutional and transformer layers, and an FPGA-based accelerator is proposed to fully exploit its benefits, achieving high throughput and energy efficiency \[[11](http://arxiv.org/abs/2403.20230)\]. Lastly, the LeViT model optimizes the speed-accuracy trade-off by integrating attention-based principles and positional bias, demonstrating superior performance on ImageNet \[[13](http://arxiv.org/abs/2104.01136)\]. These studies collectively highlight the importance of hardware-aware design and efficient arithmetic formats in advancing ViT deployment on resource-constrained platforms.

Vision Transformer (ViT) models have gained significant attention for their ability to capture global context in computer vision tasks, but their deployment on resource-constrained devices remains challenging due to high computational complexity and memory requirements \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2208.05163)\]. To address these limitations, several approaches have been proposed to improve the efficiency of ViT inference. For instance, VITALITY introduces a codesigned algorithm-hardware framework that combines low-rank and sparse attention components to reduce the quadratic cost of attention matrices, achieving a 3× speedup and 3× energy efficiency improvement \[[6](http://arxiv.org/abs/2211.05109)\]. ### <a id='category-vision-transformer-efficiency-techniques'></a>Vision Transformer Efficiency Techniques: 3 papers found.
Vision Transformer (ViT) efficiency techniques have garnered significant attention due to the computational demands of these models. Researchers have explored various strategies to reduce both training and inference costs while maintaining model accuracy. One approach involves integrating sparsity into ViTs through dynamic subnetwork training, which allows for the joint optimization of model parameters and connectivity during training, resulting in a sparse network with reduced computational overhead \[[12](http://arxiv.org/abs/2106.04533)\]. Another method, the coarse-to-fine vision transformer (CF-ViT), addresses the issue of redundant computations by splitting images into coarse patches first and refining the representation only when necessary, achieving substantial reductions in FLOPs without compromising performance \[[14](http://arxiv.org/abs/2203.03821)\]. Additionally, dynamic token configuration has been proposed to adaptively determine the number of patches based on image complexity, allowing for efficient inference by terminating early when a confident prediction is achieved \[[18](http://arxiv.org/abs/2105.15075)\]. These techniques collectively highlight the ongoing efforts to enhance the efficiency of ViTs through architectural and algorithmic innovations.
