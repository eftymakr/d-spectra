## Related Works

The growing adoption of cloud-native applications has led to increased interest in optimizing both resource usage and financial expenditures. Several studies have explored the use of container orchestration platforms like Kubernetes for managing cloud infrastructure \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2501.14753)\]. For instance, Kubernetes has been widely adopted for its ability to automate deployment, scaling, and management of containerized applications, offering flexibility and efficiency in modern cloud environments \[[2](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/b5634b15badc73f3cda72b960fd51ef03ac1265d)\]. However, while much research has focused on system-level optimizations for resource efficiency, the analysis of network costs in Kubernetes clusters remains underexplored \[[3](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/18a720c86ed6e166c26f7d39490bfd11b93ea3ec)\].

FinOps, an emerging discipline that combines financial management with cloud operations, has gained traction as organizations seek to optimize cloud costs \[[4](http://arxiv.org/abs/2307.12479)\]. This approach emphasizes the importance of aligning financial goals with operational practices to achieve cost efficiency \[[5](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/8eabcc47a99622184fc35b731992f10ff9f19444)\]. ABACUS, a FinOps solution, has been proposed to help enterprises manage cloud costs by setting budgets, enforcing them through deployment controls, and providing alerts when spending thresholds are exceeded \[[6](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/0f4ac27e56efd714e3425bd9784eb76c7cd8c2a9)\]. This aligns with the broader goal of FinOps to bring financial accountability to cloud spend \[[7](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/14353c56e5a55a919fcad86b22ec0dc357171dbb)\].

In addition to FinOps, there is significant research on Kubernetes itself. Kubernetes has been shown to improve the availability of applications through automated healing and failure recovery mechanisms \[[8](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/93b2353e44ec2cee51ea432602539fc096e75e0e)\]. However, the default configuration of Kubernetes may not always ensure high availability, as demonstrated by experiments showing that service outages can be higher than expected \[[9](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/4d4080e1a2e26fe1ead433741602ab6eccc22b72)\]. This highlights the need for further research into improving the reliability of Kubernetes-based systems \[[10](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/7c82fc45da1617fdd95fe1b03a2ebdaa6bae23d2)\].

The use of Infrastructure-as-Code (IaC) has also been explored as a means to improve cost management and deployment efficiency \[[11](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/da743c6e2fd08e2a04377bf7979f370753f39abe)\]. IaC allows for the automated provisioning of infrastructure, reducing manual errors and increasing consistency \[[12](http://arxiv.org/abs/2006.15275)\]. This aligns with the FinOps philosophy of ensuring accountability and transparency in cloud spend \[[13](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/f125b540d7453eb58d38f933588f4b80c80959f2)\].

Furthermore, the security of Kubernetes deployments has been a growing concern. Several studies have identified vulnerabilities in Kubernetes manifests and Helm Charts, emphasizing the need for robust security practices such as role-based access control (RBAC) and regular patching \[[14](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/c5d1825555ad1c38501d67496e2cdc251d2d6de4)\]. These findings underscore the importance of security in the broader context of cloud cost optimization \[[15](http://arxiv.org/abs/2207.01222)\].

In summary, while there is substantial research on Kubernetes and FinOps, the specific focus on network costs in Kubernetes clusters remains limited. This paper contributes to the field by addressing this gap, providing actionable insights into network cost optimization for containerized applications \[[16](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/8e853411a33651b57251f5f2bac46b774a180607)\].
