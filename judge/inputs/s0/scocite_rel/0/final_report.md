## Related Works

Taxation and economic inequality have been extensively studied in both theoretical and applied economics. Traditional models of income taxation, such as the U.S. federal income tax, aim to reduce inequality but often lack adaptability to heterogeneous taxpayer behavior and irrational decision-making \[[13](http://arxiv.org/abs/2103.16977)\]. The Saez Optimal Taxation model, for instance, provides a dynamic framework for tax policy but fails to account for the complexities of taxpayer heterogeneity and behavioral irrationality \[[13](http://arxiv.org/abs/2103.16977)\]. This limitation has motivated the development of more sophisticated models that integrate behavioral economics and agent-based modeling to better reflect real-world tax compliance and evasion dynamics.

Agent-based models (ABMs) have been widely used to simulate tax evasion and compliance behavior. These models often incorporate social interactions, heterogeneous agent behavior, and enforcement mechanisms to capture the complexity of tax systems \[[12](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/a065b1629ff85e2a0db928bd8ce115b2f5e2a80f)\]. For example, studies have shown that tax evasion can be influenced by social conformity, fairness, and trust in governmental institutions \[[12](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/a065b1629ff85e2a0db928bd8ce115b2f5e2a80f)\]. The Ising model has also been applied to study tax evasion dynamics, where agents' behaviors are influenced by their neighbors, and enforcement mechanisms such as audits can significantly reduce evasion \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/0805.0998)\]. Similarly, the majority-vote model has been used to analyze and control tax evasion fluctuations, showing that even minimal audit rates can have a substantial impact on compliance \[[11](http://arxiv.org/abs/0910.1964)\].

In addition to behavioral models, reinforcement learning techniques have been explored to improve the adaptability of tax policies. Deep reinforcement learning (DRL) has been applied to solve heterogeneous general equilibrium economic models, enabling the incorporation of diverse economic agents and their interactions \[[13](http://arxiv.org/abs/2103.16977)\]. This approach allows for more flexible and data-driven policy evaluation, as agents learn to optimize their behavior in response to changing economic conditions \[[13](http://arxiv.org/abs/2103.16977)\]. Furthermore, multi-agent reinforcement learning has been used to model complex interactions between taxpayers and tax authorities, with applications in both cooperative and competitive environments \[[16](http://arxiv.org/abs/1706.02275)\].

The integration of large language models (LLMs) with ABMs, as proposed in the TaxAgent framework, represents a novel approach to designing adaptive tax policies. This integration allows for the dynamic optimization of tax rates based on real-time data and behavioral insights, offering a more equitable and efficient alternative to traditional taxation systems \[[13](http://arxiv.org/abs/2103.16977)\]. By leveraging the strengths of both ABMs and LLMs, TaxAgent addresses the limitations of existing models by incorporating heterogeneous agent behavior and rational decision-making \[[13](http://arxiv.org/abs/2103.16977)\].

### <a id='category-agent-based-models-for-tax-compliance-and-evasion'></a>Agent-Based Models for Tax Compliance and Evasion: 11 papers found.
Agent-based models have been extensively used to study tax compliance and evasion, incorporating behavioral aspects and enforcement mechanisms to simulate real-world dynamics. Researchers have adapted models like the Ising model to capture the collective behavior of taxpayers, where tax evasion can be influenced by social interactions and trust in governmental institutions \[[9](http://arxiv.org/abs/0806.0344)\]. These models often introduce an external magnetic field to represent tax morale, which explains variations in evasion rates across countries \[[9](http://arxiv.org/abs/0806.0344)\]. Additionally, studies show that even minimal audit rates can significantly reduce tax evasion, highlighting the effectiveness of enforcement mechanisms in curbing non-compliance \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/0805.0998)\]. The integration of punishment as a deterrent has also been explored, where evaders caught during audits are incentivized to behave honestly for a certain period, leading to long-term compliance improvements \[[4](http://arxiv.org/abs/0801.2980)\]. Furthermore, simulations on different network structures, such as Barabasi–Albert and Voronoi–Delaunay, demonstrate that the robustness of these models is not limited to specific topologies, making them versatile tools for policy analysis \[[9](http://arxiv.org/abs/0806.0344)\]. The use of agent-based Monte-Carlo simulations, such as the majority-vote model with noise, has also been applied to study tax evasion dynamics, showing that the Zaklan model is robust across various network structures and dynamic regimes \[[10](http://arxiv.org/abs/1711.00832)\]. These findings underscore the potential of agent-based models to provide insights into tax behavior and inform policy design.

### <a id='category-reinforcement-learning-for-economic-policy-optimization'></a>Reinforcement Learning for Economic Policy Optimization: 3 papers found.
The category "Reinforcement Learning for Economic Policy Optimization" encompasses research that applies deep reinforcement learning (DRL) to economic models, aiming to improve policy-making through adaptive and data-driven approaches. Several studies explore how DRL can be used to model complex economic systems, where agents learn optimal strategies through interaction with their environment. For instance, one paper introduces a method to solve dynamic stochastic general equilibrium models using DRL, where agents represented by deep neural networks learn to solve their optimization problems without prior knowledge of the environment \[[14](http://arxiv.org/abs/2104.09368)\]. This approach is contrasted with traditional adaptive learning methods, which struggle to handle policy regimes effectively. Another study focuses on multi-agent reinforcement learning (MARL), highlighting challenges such as non-stationarity and variance in policy gradients, and proposes actor-critic methods that account for other agents' policies to enable complex coordination \[[16](http://arxiv.org/abs/1706.02275)\]. Additionally, research on MARL introduces metrics like joint-policy correlation to assess overfitting in policy learning and develops scalable algorithms that generalize across different environments \[[10](http://arxiv.org/abs/1711.00832)\]. These works collectively demonstrate the potential of DRL to enhance economic policy by enabling more flexible and adaptive decision-making processes.
