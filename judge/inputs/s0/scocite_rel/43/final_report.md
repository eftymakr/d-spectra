## Related Works

Large language models (LLMs) have been extensively studied for their ability to perform a wide range of tasks with minimal task-specific training data, a concept known as few-shot learning \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2005.14165)\]. Early work demonstrated that pre-training on large corpora and fine-tuning on specific tasks can achieve strong performance on various NLP benchmarks \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2005.14165)\]. However, the need for task-specific datasets of thousands or tens of thousands of examples remains a challenge \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2005.14165)\]. Recent advancements, such as GPT-3, have shown that scaling up language models significantly improves few-shot performance, sometimes even reaching competitiveness with prior state-of-the-art fine-tuning approaches \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2005.14165)\]. GPT-3, with 175 billion parameters, is applied without any gradient updates or fine-tuning, relying solely on text interaction to perform tasks such as translation, question-answering, and arithmetic \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2005.14165)\].

Instruction tuning has emerged as a promising approach to improve zero-shot and few-shot learning abilities \[[11](http://arxiv.org/abs/2109.01652)\]. Instruction tuning involves finetuning language models on a collection of tasks described via instructions, which has been shown to substantially improve zero-shot performance on unseen tasks \[[11](http://arxiv.org/abs/2109.01652)\]. This method has been applied to large models, such as FLAN, which outperforms GPT-3 on several benchmarks \[[11](http://arxiv.org/abs/2109.01652)\]. Similarly, instruction tuning has been explored with scaling in mind, leading to models like Flan-PaLM 540B, which outperforms previous models on various benchmarks \[[8](http://arxiv.org/abs/2210.11416)\]. These findings suggest that instruction tuning can significantly enhance the generalization capabilities of LLMs.

The development of large-scale models such as PaLM, with 540 billion parameters, has further demonstrated the benefits of scaling in few-shot learning \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/2204.02311)\]. PaLM achieves state-of-the-art results on hundreds of language understanding and generation benchmarks, outperforming finetuned state-of-the-art models on multi-step reasoning tasks \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/2204.02311)\]. Additionally, PaLM exhibits strong capabilities in multilingual tasks and source code generation \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/2204.02311)\]. These results highlight the importance of model scale in improving performance across a wide range of tasks.

The challenge of efficiently finetuning large models has also been addressed with techniques like QLoRA, which enables efficient finetuning of quantized LLMs \[[10](http://arxiv.org/abs/2305.14314)\]. ### <a id='category-large-language-model-scaling-studies'></a>Large Language Model Scaling Studies: 5 papers found.
The category "Large Language Model Scaling Studies" encompasses a range of research efforts focused on the development and evaluation of large language models (LLMs) through increased scale, with a particular emphasis on few-shot learning and performance across diverse tasks. These studies highlight that scaling up LLMs significantly enhances their ability to perform tasks with minimal or no fine-tuning, often achieving results comparable to or surpassing traditional fine-tuning approaches \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2005.14165)\]. For instance, GPT-3, with 175 billion parameters, demonstrates strong performance on translation, question-answering, and reasoning tasks without any gradient updates \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2005.14165)\]. Similarly, PaLM, a 540-billion parameter model, achieves state-of-the-art few-shot learning results on numerous benchmarks, including multi-step reasoning tasks and multilingual tasks \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/2204.02311)\]. Llama 2, a series of models ranging from 7 billion to 70 billion parameters, also shows competitive performance, particularly in dialogue and safety-focused applications \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/2307.09288)\]. Furthermore, GPT-4, a multimodal model, exhibits human-level performance on professional and academic benchmarks, such as passing a simulated bar exam \[[4](http://arxiv.org/abs/2304.12244)\]. Recent advancements like Llama 3, with up to 405 billion parameters, continue this trend, offering comparable quality to leading models like GPT-4 while supporting multilingual, coding, and reasoning capabilities \[[5](http://arxiv.org/abs/2303.08774)\]. These studies collectively underscore the importance of model scale in improving few-shot learning capabilities and task adaptability, while also addressing challenges such as bias, toxicity, and ethical considerations \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/2204.02311)\]\[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/2307.09288)\].

### <a id='category-instruction-tuning-approaches'></a>Instruction Tuning Approaches: 4 papers found.
Instruction Tuning Approaches have been widely explored to enhance the performance and generalization of large language models (LLMs). Several studies have demonstrated that fine-tuning models on instruction-based datasets significantly improves their ability to handle diverse tasks, both in zero-shot and few-shot settings \[[4](http://arxiv.org/abs/2304.12244)\]. For example, Evol-Instruct introduces an iterative method to generate complex instructions using LLMs, which are then used to fine-tune models like LLaMA, resulting in WizardLM, a model that outperforms human-created instructions and even surpasses ChatGPT in certain aspects \[[4](http://arxiv.org/abs/2304.12244)\]. Similarly, MAmmoTH leverages a hybrid of chain-of-thought (CoT) and program-of-thought (PoT) rationales in its training data, leading to substantial improvements in mathematical reasoning tasks, with models achieving accuracy levels that exceed existing open-source models \[[7](http://arxiv.org/abs/2309.05653)\]. The effectiveness of instruction tuning is further supported by studies showing that increasing the number of tasks and model size during fine-tuning leads to better performance across various benchmarks, such as MMLU and BBH \[[8](http://arxiv.org/abs/2210.11416)\]. Additionally, instruction tuning has been shown to enhance zero-shot learning capabilities, with FLAN demonstrating superior performance compared to both unmodified models and GPT-3, even when using fewer training examples \[[11](http://arxiv.org/abs/2109.01652)\]. These findings collectively highlight the importance of instruction tuning in improving the versatility and effectiveness of LLMs across a wide range of applications.

### <a id='category-benchmarking-and-evaluation-protocols'></a>Benchmarking and Evaluation Protocols: 4 papers found.
The category "Benchmarking and Evaluation Protocols" encompasses a range of studies focused on developing and refining methods to assess the capabilities of large language models (LLMs). These works highlight the limitations of existing benchmarks and propose new approaches to more accurately reflect human preferences and model performance. For instance, one study explores the use of strong LLMs as judges to evaluate chat assistants on open-ended questions, identifying biases such as position, verbosity, and self-enhancement, as well as limitations in reasoning ability, and proposes mitigation strategies \[[6](http://arxiv.org/abs/2306.05685)\]. Another study systematically reviews advancements in LLM evaluation methods, emphasizing the need for benchmarks that assess core language capabilities, reasoning, and safety, while also addressing challenges in model alignment and efficient scaling \[[9](http://arxiv.org/abs/2303.18223)\]. Additionally, the introduction of specialized datasets like GSM8K aims to diagnose failures in multi-step mathematical reasoning and improve model performance through verification techniques \[[12](http://arxiv.org/abs/2110.14168)\]. Similarly, the development of Codex and its evaluation on tasks like code generation demonstrates the importance of benchmarking in assessing functional correctness and the effectiveness of strategies such as repeated sampling \[[15](http://arxiv.org/abs/2107.03374)\]. These contributions collectively underscore the ongoing efforts to create more robust, scalable, and human-aligned evaluation protocols for LLMs.

QLoRA backpropagates gradients through a frozen, 4-bit quantized pretrained language model into Low Rank Adapters~(LoRA). Our best model family, which we name Guanaco, outperforms all previous openly released models on the Vicuna benchmark, reaching 99.3% of the performance level of ChatGPT while only requiring 23 hours of finetuning on a single GPU. QLoRA introduces a number of innovations to save memory without sacrificing performance: (a) 4-bit NormalFloat (NF4), a new data type that is information theoretically optimal for normally distributed weights (b) double quantization to reduce the average memory footprint by quantizing the quantization constants, and (c) paged optimizers to manage memory spikes. We use QLoRA to finetune more than 1,000 models, providing a detailed analysis of instruction following and chatbot performance across 8 instruction datasets, multiple model types (LLaMA, T5), and model scales that would be infeasible to run with regular finetuning (e.g. 33B and 65B parameter models). Our results show that QLoRA finetuning on a small high-quality dataset leads to state-of-the-art results, even when using smaller models than the previous SoTA. We provide a detailed analysis of chatbot performance based on both human and GPT-4 evaluations showing that GPT-4 evaluations are a cheap and reasonable alternative to human evaluation. Furthermore, we find that current chatbot benchmarks are not trustworthy to accurately evaluate the performance levels of chatbots. A lemon-picked analysis demonstrates where Guanaco fails compared to ChatGPT. We release all of our models and code, including CUDA kernels for 4-bit training.»
