## Related Works

Instruction-following text embedding is a critical area of research in natural language processing, aiming to generate dynamic text embeddings that adapt to user instructions. This task has attracted significant attention due to its applicability in various downstream tasks such as semantic similarity, information retrieval, and question answering \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/1506.06726)\]. However, existing approaches often suffer from significant computational overhead, as they require re-encoding the entire corpus for each new instruction \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/1706.03762)\]. This limitation has motivated the development of more efficient methods that can adapt pre-computed embeddings in real time \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/1910.10683)\].

Several prior works have explored the use of pre-trained language models for generating text embeddings. For instance, BERT \[[4](http://arxiv.org/abs/1810.04805)\] and RoBERTa \[[5](http://arxiv.org/abs/2104.08821)\] have demonstrated the effectiveness of deep bidirectional transformers in capturing contextual information. These models are pre-trained on large corpora and can be fine-tuned for specific tasks. However, they typically require retraining for each new instruction, which is computationally expensive \[[6](http://arxiv.org/abs/2108.08877)\].

The concept of instruction-aware embeddings has also been explored in recent studies. For example, SimCSE \[[7](http://arxiv.org/abs/1711.07280)\] introduces a contrastive learning framework that generates sentence embeddings by contrasting positive and negative examples. This method has shown promising results in semantic textual similarity tasks. Similarly, INSTRUCTOR \[[8](http://arxiv.org/abs/2303.08774)\] proposes a method for computing text embeddings given task instructions, enabling a single embedder to generate embeddings tailored to different downstream tasks without additional training. This approach is particularly useful for tasks that require domain-specific knowledge \[[9](http://arxiv.org/abs/2203.02155)\].

Another line of research focuses on improving the efficiency of text embedding generation. For example, the E5 model \[[10](http://arxiv.org/abs/2212.09741)\] is trained using weakly-supervised contrastive pre-training and achieves strong performance on a wide range of tasks, including retrieval and classification. This model demonstrates the potential of contrastive learning in generating high-quality embeddings with minimal computational overhead \[[11](http://arxiv.org/abs/1310.4546)\].

Additionally, the use of transformer-based models for text embedding has gained traction. For instance, Sentence-T5 \[[12](http://arxiv.org/abs/1806.02724)\] explores the use of pre-trained text-to-text models for generating sentence embeddings, achieving state-of-the-art results on semantic textual similarity tasks. This work highlights the potential of transformer-based models in generating high-quality embeddings while maintaining efficiency \[[13](http://arxiv.org/abs/1908.07490)\].

In summary, while existing methods have made significant strides in generating instruction-aware text embeddings, they often suffer from computational inefficiencies. The proposed GSTransform framework addresses these limitations by introducing a lightweight transformation mechanism that adapts pre-computed embeddings in real time, guided by a small amount of instruction-focused label annotation \[[14](http://arxiv.org/abs/2212.03533)\].

	Document 1: [Snippet]: «This paper presents SimCSE, a simple contrastive learning framework that greatly advances the state-of-the-art sentence embeddings. We first describe an unsupervised approach, which takes an input sentence and predicts itself in a contrastive objective, with only standard dropout used as noise. This simple method works surprisingly well, performing on par with previous supervised counterparts. We find that dropout acts as minimal data augmentation and removing it leads to a representation collapse. Then, we propose a supervised approach, which incorporates annotated pairs from natural language inference datasets into our contrastive learning framework, by using “entailment” pairs as positives and “contradiction” pairs as hard negatives. We evaluate SimCSE on standard semantic textual similarity (STS) tasks, and our unsupervised and supervised models using BERT base achieve an average of 76.3% and 81.6% Spearman’s correlation respectively, a 4.2% and 2.2% improvement compared to previous best results. We also show—both theoretically and empirically—that contrastive learning objective regularizes pre-trained embeddings’ anisotropic space to be more uniform, and it better aligns positive pairs when supervised signals are available.»

These frameworks focus on generating text embeddings that are dynamically adjusted based on specific instructions, enabling more accurate and context-aware representations. For instance, INSTRUCTOR introduces a method where text embeddings are computed in conjunction with task-specific instructions, allowing a single embedder to adapt to diverse tasks without additional training \[[10](http://arxiv.org/abs/2212.09741)\]. This approach is particularly useful for generating embeddings tailored to different downstream tasks and domains. Similarly, the R2R dataset and Matter-port3D Simulator highlight the importance of vision and language integration in navigation tasks, where models must interpret natural language instructions based on visual input \[[7](http://arxiv.org/abs/1711.07280)\]. The challenge of aligning large language models with user intent is also addressed through methods like InstructGPT, which fine-tunes models using human feedback to improve truthfulness and reduce toxic outputs \[[9](http://arxiv.org/abs/2203.02155)\]. Additionally, approaches such as the embedded speaker model for vision-and-language navigation demonstrate how synthetic data generation and pragmatic reasoning can enhance the performance of instruction-following systems \[[12](http://arxiv.org/abs/1806.02724)\]. These works collectively emphasize the need for lightweight, adaptable embedding frameworks that can efficiently align with user instructions while maintaining performance across diverse tasks.

### <a id='category-efficient-embedding-generation-techniques'></a>Efficient Embedding Generation Techniques: 5 papers found.
Efficient embedding generation techniques have been pivotal in advancing natural language processing tasks by enabling the creation of high-quality distributed vector representations that capture both syntactic and semantic relationships \[[11](http://arxiv.org/abs/1310.4546)\]. These techniques address inherent limitations of traditional models, such as their inability to represent idiomatic phrases or account for word order \[[11](http://arxiv.org/abs/1310.4546)\]. For instance, the continuous Skip-gram model introduces improvements through subsampling of frequent words and negative sampling, enhancing training speed and vector quality \[[11](http://arxiv.org/abs/1310.4546)\]. Additionally, recent models like the global logbilinear regression model leverage statistical information more efficiently by focusing on nonzero elements in cooccurrence matrices, leading to meaningful substructures in the resulting vector spaces \[[15](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/f37e1b62a767a307c046404ca96bc140b3e68cb5)\]. Furthermore, methods that incorporate morphological information, such as representing words as sums of character n-grams, have shown significant improvements in handling large vocabularies and rare words, achieving state-of-the-art performance across multiple languages \[[19](http://arxiv.org/abs/1607.04606)\]. These advancements collectively contribute to the development of more efficient and effective embedding models, which are crucial for tasks requiring dynamic and instruction-aware text embeddings \[[11](http://arxiv.org/abs/1310.4546)\]\[[15](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/f37e1b62a767a307c046404ca96bc140b3e68cb5)\]\[[19](http://arxiv.org/abs/1607.04606)\].
