## Related Works

Maximum Inner Product Search (MIPS) has been a focal point in machine learning and information retrieval, particularly for high-dimensional data. Existing approaches to MIPS can be broadly categorized into two paradigms: those that rely solely on inner product (IP) similarity and those that reduce the problem to nearest neighbor search under the Euclidean metric via space projection. The former often faces challenges such as local optima and redundant computations \[[13](http://arxiv.org/abs/1610.03317)\]. The latter, while leveraging well-established Euclidean-based techniques, may suffer from topology destruction and information loss during the projection process \[[17](http://arxiv.org/abs/1509.01469)\]. 

Despite these divergent approaches, recent studies argue that there is no inherent binary opposition between IP and Euclidean metrics. Instead, the integration of both can lead to significant performance improvements \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/1405.5869)\]. This perspective aligns with the findings of several works that explore the theoretical and empirical connections between these two metrics from the MIPS perspective \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/1405.5869)\]. 

Graph-based methods have emerged as a promising direction for efficient MIPS. The inner-product navigable small world graph (ip-NSW) represents a state-of-the-art method for approximate MIPS, achieving an order of magnitude speedup over existing baselines \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/1909.13459)\]. However, the exceptional performance of ip-NSW is attributed to its ability to align with the norm bias of the MIPS problem, where large norm items dominate the search process \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/1909.13459)\]. This insight has led to the development of ip-NSW+, which introduces an additional angular proximity graph to improve search efficiency and robustness \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/1909.13459)\].

Efforts to improve the efficiency of MIPS have also included the use of hashing techniques. Asymmetric LSH (ALSH) was introduced as the first provably sublinear time algorithm for approximate MIPS, leveraging asymmetric transformations to convert the problem into approximate near neighbor search \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/1405.5869)\]. Later works, such as NORM-RANGING LSH, further refined these hashing approaches by addressing the normalization issues caused by long-tailed distributions \[[6](http://arxiv.org/abs/1809.08782)\]. 

Another approach to improving MIPS involves the use of quantization techniques. The Quantization based Fast Inner Product Search method proposes a novel quantization approach that minimizes the inner product quantization error, leading to improved performance on various datasets \[[17](http://arxiv.org/abs/1509.01469)\]. This work also highlights the importance of decomposing quantization error into norm and direction components, as norm errors have a more significant impact on inner product search \[[19](http://arxiv.org/abs/1911.04654)\].

### <a id='category-hashing-techniques-for-mips'></a>Hashing Techniques for MIPS: 3 papers found.
The category "Hashing Techniques for MIPS" explores advanced methods for efficiently solving the Maximum Inner Product Search (MIPS) problem, which is critical in high-dimensional data applications. Early work introduced the first provably sublinear time algorithm for MIPS, leveraging asymmetric transformations to convert the problem into approximate near neighbor search, enabling efficient hashing schemes \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/1405.5869)\]. This approach extended the Locality Sensitive Hashing (LSH) framework to accommodate asymmetric hashing, offering a theoretically grounded solution \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/1405.5869)\]. Subsequent research, such as SIMPLE-LSH, further advanced the field by providing state-of-the-art hashing algorithms for MIPS, though it faced challenges with long-tailed 2-norm distributions in real datasets \[[6](http://arxiv.org/abs/1809.08782)\]. To address this, NORM-RANGING LSH was proposed, partitioning datasets into sub-datasets to mitigate normalization issues and improve query efficiency \[[6](http://arxiv.org/abs/1809.08782)\]. Additionally, alternative transformations were explored, converting MIPS into cosine similarity search using signed random projections, which theoretically outperformed earlier methods \[[18](http://arxiv.org/abs/1410.5410)\]. These developments highlight the ongoing evolution of hashing techniques for MIPS, emphasizing the importance of asymmetric transformations and dataset-specific optimizations to enhance performance and adaptability.

### <a id='category-graph-based-mips-methods'></a>Graph-Based MIPS Methods: 5 papers found.
Graph-based methods have emerged as a powerful approach for solving the Maximum Inner Product Search (MIPS) problem, offering empirical advantages over hash-based or quantization-based solutions \[[2](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/25454f456c514219c9d6eeb42200ebbf378db9ca)\]. These methods construct a proximity graph where nodes represent data points, and edges encode relationships that facilitate efficient inner product search \[[4](http://arxiv.org/abs/1202.6101)\]. The inner-product navigable small world graph (ip-NSW) represents a state-of-the-art method for approximate MIPS, achieving significant speedups by leveraging norm bias, where large norm items are prioritized due to their higher likelihood of being relevant \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/1909.13459)\]. However, ip-NSW's performance can be further improved by incorporating an angular proximity graph, leading to the ip-NSW+ algorithm, which first identifies angular neighbors and then refines the search on the inner-product graph \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/1909.13459)\]. Additionally, reinforcement learning and imitation learning have been combined to train agents for automated MIPS search, especially when ground truths are unavailable \[[4](http://arxiv.org/abs/1202.6101)\]. The norm adjusted proximity graph (NAPG) also contributes to this field by adjusting factors based on sampled data to enhance the selection of meaningful connections during graph construction \[[2](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/25454f456c514219c9d6eeb42200ebbf378db9ca)\]. These advancements highlight the growing importance of graph-based strategies in addressing the challenges of MIPS, particularly in high-dimensional and large-scale applications \[[4](http://arxiv.org/abs/1202.6101)\].

### <a id='category-mips-problem-definition-and-challenges'></a>MIPS Problem Definition and Challenges: 5 papers found.
The MIPS problem has been extensively studied due to its critical role in machine learning and information retrieval, particularly in high-dimensional data applications \[[13](http://arxiv.org/abs/1610.03317)\]. Early approaches focused on either using inner-product similarity directly, which can lead to local optima and redundant computations \[[13](http://arxiv.org/abs/1610.03317)\], or transforming the problem into nearest neighbor search under the Euclidean metric through space projection, which often results in topology distortion and information loss \[[13](http://arxiv.org/abs/1610.03317)\]. However, recent research has highlighted the potential of combining inner-product and Euclidean metrics within indexing and search algorithms to enhance performance \[[13](http://arxiv.org/abs/1610.03317)\]. For instance, some studies have proposed branch-and-bound algorithms based on tree data structures to efficiently find the best match with respect to inner-product \[[4](http://arxiv.org/abs/1202.6101)\], while others have introduced dual-tree algorithms for handling multiple queries \[[4](http://arxiv.org/abs/1202.6101)\]. Additionally, there has been work on approximate methods that aim to balance search efficiency and quality, such as c-Approximate Maximum Inner Product (c-AMIP) search, which uses lightweight indexing and probabilistic guarantees to improve efficiency \[[8](http://arxiv.org/abs/2104.04406)\]. Another line of research has focused on developing algorithms with controllable trade-offs between search efficiency and quality, such as the Greedy-MIPS algorithm, which allows users to specify a computational budget and achieves significant speedups while maintaining high search accuracy \[[13](http://arxiv.org/abs/1610.03317)\]. These approaches collectively highlight the ongoing efforts to address the challenges of MIPS, emphasizing the need for flexible and efficient solutions that can adapt to different data topologies and application requirements \[[13](http://arxiv.org/abs/1610.03317)\].
