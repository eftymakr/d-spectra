## Related Works

Recent advancements in large language models (LLMs) have emphasized the importance of understanding how feature representations evolve across layers, as this knowledge is critical for improving interpretability and robustness \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2307.09288)\]. While prior work has identified critical layers linked to specific functions or behaviors, these approaches often rely on data-dependent analyses of fine-tuned models, limiting their applicability to post-hoc settings \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/2109.01652)\]. In contrast, this paper introduces a data-oblivious approach that identifies intrinsic critical layers in pre-fine-tuned LLMs by analyzing representation dynamics using Centered Kernel Alignment (CKA) \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/2309.02144)\]. This method enables the identification of layers that undergo significant shifts in representation space, which are also most affected during fine-tuning—a pattern that holds consistently across tasks for a given model \[[4](http://arxiv.org/abs/2305.18290)\].

The spectral analysis of these representation shifts reveals that they are driven by changes in the top principal components, which encode semantic transitions from rationales to conclusions \[[5](http://arxiv.org/abs/2106.09685)\]. This insight is particularly relevant to the broader field of transfer learning, where pre-trained models are adapted to downstream tasks through fine-tuning \[[6](http://arxiv.org/abs/2012.13255)\]. For instance, the effectiveness of transfer learning has been explored through a unified framework that converts every language problem into a text-to-text format, enabling state-of-the-art results across multiple benchmarks \[[7](http://arxiv.org/abs/2407.21783)\]. Similarly, the success of instruction tuning—fine-tuning models on tasks described via natural language instructions—has been shown to significantly improve zero-shot performance on unseen tasks \[[8](http://arxiv.org/abs/2302.13971)\].

This paper also applies its findings to two practical scenarios: efficient domain adaptation and backdoor defense. ### <a id='category-parameter-efficient-fine-tuning-methods'></a>Parameter-Efficient Fine-Tuning Methods: 5 papers found.
Parameter-efficient fine-tuning methods have emerged as a critical area of research in natural language processing, particularly for adapting large pre-trained models to downstream tasks without retraining all parameters \[[5](http://arxiv.org/abs/2106.09685)\]. These methods aim to reduce computational costs and resource requirements while maintaining model performance. For instance, Low-Rank Adaptation (LoRA) introduces trainable rank decomposition matrices into the Transformer architecture, significantly reducing the number of trainable parameters and GPU memory usage compared to full fine-tuning \[[5](http://arxiv.org/abs/2106.09685)\]. Similarly, intrinsic dimension analysis suggests that pre-trained models operate in a low-dimensional subspace, enabling effective fine-tuning with only a small subset of parameters \[[6](http://arxiv.org/abs/2012.13255)\]. BitFit, another method, focuses on modifying only the bias terms of the model, achieving competitive or superior performance compared to full fine-tuning, especially with small-to-medium datasets \[[13](http://arxiv.org/abs/2106.10199)\]. Additionally, Compacter combines ideas from adapters, low-rank optimization, and hypercomplex multiplication layers to fine-tune models with a minimal number of trainable parameters while maintaining strong performance on benchmarks like GLUE and SuperGLUE \[[21](http://arxiv.org/abs/2106.04647)\]. These approaches highlight the potential of parameter-efficient methods to enhance both the practicality and effectiveness of fine-tuning large language models.

### <a id='category-efficient-domain-adaptation-techniques'></a>Efficient Domain Adaptation Techniques: 2 papers found.
Efficient domain adaptation techniques focus on optimizing the use of computational resources and memory during the fine-tuning of large language models (LLMs) to achieve better performance with limited data. Research has shown that the cross-entropy loss scales as a power-law with model size, dataset size, and compute used for training, with trends spanning over seven orders of magnitude \[[12](http://arxiv.org/abs/2001.08361)\]. This implies that larger models are more sample-efficient, allowing for optimal compute allocation by training very large models on modest data and stopping before convergence \[[12](http://arxiv.org/abs/2001.08361)\]. Additionally, managing the key-value (KV) cache efficiently is crucial for high-throughput serving of LLMs, as inefficient memory management can lead to significant waste and limit batch size \[[16](http://arxiv.org/abs/2309.06180)\]. To address this, PagedAttention was introduced, inspired by virtual memory and paging techniques, enabling near-zero waste in KV cache memory and flexible sharing across requests \[[16](http://arxiv.org/abs/2309.06180)\]. These advancements contribute to more efficient and scalable deployment of LLMs in real-world applications.
