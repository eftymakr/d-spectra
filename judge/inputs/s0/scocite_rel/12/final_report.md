## Related Works

Event cameras, which capture scene changes asynchronously on a per-pixel basis, have gained significant attention for their high temporal resolution and ability to operate in challenging lighting conditions \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/1906.07165)\]. However, the high bandwidth, memory, and computational demands of event streams have led to the development of various event downsampling techniques \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/1904.08405)\]. Traditional approaches often rely on fixed heuristics or threshold-based strategies, which limit their adaptability to different applications and environments \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/1804.01310)\]. These methods typically fail to account for the dynamic nature of event streams and the varying importance of individual events \[[4](http://arxiv.org/abs/1812.08156)\].

To address these limitations, recent work has explored probabilistic frameworks for modeling event importance. For instance, some studies have proposed using hand-crafted priors to reconstruct intensity images from event streams \[[5](http://arxiv.org/abs/1802.06898)\]. Others have attempted to synthesize color images from color event streams by learning from simulated data \[[6](http://arxiv.org/abs/1709.06310)\]. These approaches, however, often rely on strong assumptions about the imaging process and the statistics of natural images \[[7](http://arxiv.org/abs/1912.03095)\]. Additionally, while some research has focused on learning-based techniques for processing event data, such as optical flow and depth estimation \[[8](http://arxiv.org/abs/1801.10202)\], these methods typically require large amounts of labeled data or specialized architectures \[[9](http://arxiv.org/abs/1904.08298)\].

A key challenge in event processing is the need for efficient and adaptive sampling strategies. Some works have explored unsupervised learning for motion information from event streams, using input representations such as discretized volumes to maintain temporal distribution \[[10](http://arxiv.org/abs/1803.04523)\]. Others have attempted to apply self-supervised learning techniques to estimate optical flow from event data, using grayscale images as supervisory signals \[[11](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/47cd947b70d5c6d90a5ecf0a76a6199fd2541175)\]. These methods, while promising, often lack the flexibility and adaptability required for real-time applications .

To overcome these limitations, we propose a probabilistic framework, POLED, that models event importance through an event-importance probability density function (ePDF), which can be arbitrarily defined and adapted to different applications . Our approach operates in an online setting, estimating event importance on-the-fly from raw event streams, enabling scene-specific adaptation . Additionally, we introduce zero-shot event downsampling, where downsampled events must remain usable for models trained on the original event stream, without task-specific adaptation . This is a critical advancement, as it allows for the development of more generalizable and adaptable event processing systems .

### <a id='category-learning-based-event-processing'></a>Learning-Based Event Processing: 7 papers found.
Event cameras, which capture asynchronous brightness changes per-pixel, offer significant advantages over traditional frame-based cameras, including high temporal resolution, high dynamic range, and no motion blur \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/1906.07165)\]. However, processing event data remains challenging due to its unconventional format, requiring specialized algorithms or adaptation of existing computer vision techniques \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/1904.08405)\]. Recent work has focused on learning-based approaches to reconstruct intensity images or videos from event streams, enabling the use of off-the-shelf vision algorithms on event data \[[6](http://arxiv.org/abs/1709.06310)\]. For instance, a recurrent neural network trained on simulated event data has demonstrated superior image quality compared to state-of-the-art methods, with performance improvements of over 20% \[[6](http://arxiv.org/abs/1709.06310)\]. Additionally, synthetic event data generated from conventional video datasets has been used to train models for tasks like object recognition and semantic segmentation, showing robustness in challenging lighting conditions \[[7](http://arxiv.org/abs/1912.03095)\]. Furthermore, tools like v2e have been developed to generate realistic synthetic event data, improving generalization when transferred to real-world scenarios \[[11](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/47cd947b70d5c6d90a5ecf0a76a6199fd2541175)\]. These advancements highlight the growing interest in learning-based event processing, which aims to unlock the full potential of event cameras for a wide range of applications \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/1904.08405)\].

### <a id='category-color-image-synthesis-from-events'></a>Color Image Synthesis from Events: 1 papers found.
Context: 
	Document 1: [Snippet]: «Event cameras are bioinspired vision sensors that output pixel-level brightness changes instead of standard intensity frames. These cameras do not suffer from motion blur and have a very high dynamic range, which enables them to provide reliable visual information during high-speed motions or in scenes characterized by high dynamic range. However, event cameras output only little information when the amount of motion is limited, such as in the case of almost still motion. Conversely, standard cameras provide instant and rich information about the environment most of the time (in low-speed and good lighting scenarios), but they fail severely in case of fast motions, or difficult lighting such as high dynamic range or low light scenes. In this letter, we present the first state estimation pipeline that leverages the complementary advantages of these two sensors by fusing in a tightly coupled manner events, standard frames, and inertial measurements. We show on the publicly available Event Camera Dataset that our hybrid pipeline leads to an accuracy improvement of 130% over event-only pipelines, and 85% over standard-frames-only visual-inertial systems, while still being computationally tractable. Furthermore, we use our pipeline to demonstrate—to the best of our knowledge—the first autonomous quadrotor flight using an event camera for state estimation, unlocking flight scenarios that were not reachable with traditional visual-inertial odometry, such as low-light environments and high dynamic range scenes. ### <a id='category-event-camera-overview'></a>Event Camera Overview: 1 papers found.
Context: 
	Document 1: [Snippet]: «Event-based cameras are a new passive sensing modality with a number of benefits over traditional cameras, including extremely low latency, asynchronous data acquisition, high dynamic range, and very low power consumption. There has been a lot of recent interest and development in applying algorithms to use the events to perform a variety of three-dimensional perception tasks, such as feature tracking, visual odometry, and stereo depth estimation. However, there currently lacks the wealth of labeled data that exists for traditional cameras to be used for both testing and development. In this letter, we present a large dataset with a synchronized stereo pair event based camera system, carried on a handheld rig, flown by a hexacopter, driven on top of a car, and mounted on a motorcycle, in a variety of different illumination levels and environments. From each camera, we provide the event stream, grayscale images, and inertial measurement unit (IMU) readings. In addition, we utilize a combination of IMU, a rigidly mounted lidar system, indoor and outdoor motion capture, and GPS to provide accurate pose and depth images for each camera at up to 100 Hz. For comparison, we also provide synchronized grayscale images and IMU readings from a frame-based stereo camera system.»
