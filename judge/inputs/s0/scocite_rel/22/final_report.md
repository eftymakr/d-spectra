## Related Works

Unsupervised domain adaptation (UDA) for semantic segmentation has attracted significant research interest due to its ability to transfer knowledge from labeled source data to unlabeled target data \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/1910.13049)\]. Traditional UDA methods often assume that the category settings between source and target domains are known \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/2004.07703)\], which is not always the case in real-world scenarios. This assumption can lead to performance degradation when private classes—categories present in one domain but not the other—exist \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/1909.13589)\]. To address this limitation, several approaches have been proposed to handle domain adaptation without prior knowledge of category settings.

One line of research focuses on improving feature alignment between domains. For instance, \[[4](http://arxiv.org/abs/1904.01886)\] introduces a category anchor-guided approach that explicitly enforces category-aware feature alignment to learn shared discriminative features and classifiers simultaneously. This method uses category-wise centroids as guided anchors to identify active features in the target domain and assign pseudo-labels \[[4](http://arxiv.org/abs/1904.01886)\]. Similarly, \[[5](http://arxiv.org/abs/1904.10620)\] proposes a two-step self-supervised domain adaptation approach to minimize both inter-domain and intra-domain gaps. The first step involves inter-domain adaptation, while the second step focuses on reducing the intra-domain gap by leveraging self-supervised learning from an easy to hard subdomain \[[5](http://arxiv.org/abs/1904.10620)\].

Another approach leverages adversarial learning to align the marginal distributions between source and target domains. \[[6](http://arxiv.org/abs/2004.05498)\] introduces a method that swaps the low-frequency spectrum of one domain with the other to reduce domain discrepancy. This method is simple and achieves state-of-the-art performance without requiring adversarial optimization \[[6](http://arxiv.org/abs/2004.05498)\]. In contrast, \[[7](http://arxiv.org/abs/1809.09478)\] proposes a category-level adversarial network that enforces local semantic consistency during global alignment. This approach reduces the weight of adversarial loss for well-aligned categories while increasing the adversarial force for poorly aligned ones \[[7](http://arxiv.org/abs/1809.09478)\].

### <a id='category-category-aware-feature-alignment'></a>Category-Aware Feature Alignment: 2 papers found.
Category-aware feature alignment has emerged as a critical approach in unsupervised domain adaptation (UDA) for semantic segmentation, addressing the limitations of traditional methods that assume known category settings between source and target domains \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/1910.13049)\]. Unlike global alignment strategies that focus on matching marginal distributions, category-aware methods emphasize local semantic consistency by aligning features at the class level \[[7](http://arxiv.org/abs/1809.09478)\]. This is achieved through mechanisms such as category-wise centroid alignment, where source domain features are used as anchors to guide feature learning in the target domain \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/1910.13049)\]. These anchors help assign pseudo-labels and enforce intra-category feature clustering while promoting inter-category separation \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/1910.13049)\]. Additionally, adaptive adversarial losses are employed to adjust the alignment strength based on category-level consistency, ensuring that well-aligned features are prioritized while improving the alignment of misaligned ones \[[7](http://arxiv.org/abs/1809.09478)\]. Such approaches mitigate the risk of incorrect mappings during global alignment, thereby enhancing the robustness of domain adaptation in real-world scenarios where category settings may not be fully known \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/1910.13049)\]. The integration of co-training strategies further refines the alignment process by iteratively improving feature consistency across domains \[[7](http://arxiv.org/abs/1809.09478)\]. These advancements highlight the growing importance of category-aware feature alignment in achieving effective and robust domain adaptation for semantic segmentation tasks.

Furthermore, methods such as maximum squares loss have been introduced to balance training gradients and mitigate the dominance of easy-to-transfer samples in the target domain \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/1909.13589)\]. These techniques collectively aim to enhance the quality of pseudo-labels and improve the generalization of models across domains.

In the context of pseudo-label refinement strategies for unsupervised domain adaptation (UDA) in semantic segmentation, several approaches have been proposed to address the challenges posed by domain shift and noisy pseudo-labels. Furthermore, methods like DACS have been proposed to address domain shift by mixing images from both domains along with their corresponding labels and pseudo-labels, enabling more robust training \[[8](http://arxiv.org/abs/2007.08702)\]. These strategies collectively aim to enhance the generalization of models across domains while maintaining efficiency and scalability \[[19](http://arxiv.org/abs/2008.12197)\].
