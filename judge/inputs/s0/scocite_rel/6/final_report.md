## Related Works

Narrative summarization, particularly for long-form texts such as books, movies, and TV scripts, remains a challenging task for existing large language models (LLMs). This is due to the need to capture intricate plotlines, character interactions, and thematic coherence, which are often lost in traditional summarization approaches \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/1908.08960)\]. Several works have explored methods to improve the quality and coherence of generated summaries, often focusing on pre-training strategies, evaluation metrics, and model architectures \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/1910.10683)\].

Early approaches to text summarization primarily relied on statistical methods, such as extractive techniques that selected key sentences from the source text. However, these methods often failed to capture the nuanced structure of narratives \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/1409.0473)\]. More recently, neural text summarization models have been introduced, which aim to generate more coherent and contextually relevant summaries \[[4](http://arxiv.org/abs/2308.00352)\]. Despite these advances, many models still struggle with maintaining factual correctness and thematic consistency, especially when dealing with long-form narratives \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/1908.08960)\].

The use of transfer learning has also been explored as a means to improve summarization performance. For example, the Unified Text-to-Text Transformer framework has demonstrated the effectiveness of pre-training on diverse tasks and fine-tuning on specific ones, leading to state-of-the-art results on various benchmarks \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/1910.10683)\]. This approach has been further refined with the introduction of the "Colossal Clean Crawled Corpus," which helps improve the quality of pre-training data \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/1910.10683)\].

In addition to pre-training and fine-tuning, several studies have investigated the use of multi-agent systems to enhance the performance of LLMs in complex tasks. For instance, MetaGPT introduces a framework that incorporates human workflows into LLM-based multi-agent collaboration, allowing for more structured and accurate outputs \[[4](http://arxiv.org/abs/2308.00352)\]. Similarly, Toolformer demonstrates how LLMs can be trained to use external tools, improving their ability to handle tasks that require factual knowledge or arithmetic reasoning \[[10](http://arxiv.org/abs/2302.04761)\].

The importance of evaluation metrics in summarization has also been highlighted. Traditional metrics such as ROUGE have been criticized for their weak correlation with human judgment and for not accounting for important characteristics such as factual correctness \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/1908.08960)\]. This has led to the development of more sophisticated evaluation frameworks, including BERTScore, which measures the similarity between generated summaries and reference texts using BERT embeddings \[[15](http://arxiv.org/abs/1810.04805)\].
