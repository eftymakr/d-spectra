## Related Works

Table discovery and semantic parsing have seen significant advancements in recent years, driven by the increasing availability of structured and semi-structured data. Existing approaches primarily focus on retrieving tables based on keyword queries or predefined schemas, often requiring manual filtering of large result sets \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/1802.06159)\]. This limitation highlights the need for more sophisticated methods that can integrate natural language (NL) requirements with tabular data to refine search results. Our proposed task, NL-conditional table discovery (nlcTD), aligns with this need by enabling users to combine a query table with NL conditions to improve retrieval accuracy \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2005.08314)\].

Semantic parsing over structured data has been explored in several works. For instance, TaBERT \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2005.08314)\] is a pretrained language model that jointly learns representations for natural language sentences and structured tables, achieving state-of-the-art results on semantic parsing benchmarks like WikiTableQuestions. This model is trained on a large corpus of 26 million tables and their English contexts, demonstrating its effectiveness in understanding both free-form text and structured data. Similarly, the work on compositional semantic parsing \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/1508.00305)\] addresses the challenge of answering complex questions on semi-structured tables by introducing a logical-form driven parsing algorithm guided by strong typing constraints. This approach improves upon natural baselines and is evaluated on a new dataset of 22,033 complex questions.

Ad hoc table retrieval has also been addressed through semantic similarity methods \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/1802.06159)\]. This work introduces a method for semantic matching between queries and tables by representing both in multiple semantic spaces and using various similarity measures. The approach is evaluated on a purpose-built test collection based on Wikipedia tables, showing significant improvements over state-of-the-art baselines. These methods rely on supervised learning models trained on semantic representations, which can be extended to incorporate NL conditions as in our nlcTD task.

The nlcTables benchmark dataset \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2005.08314)\] is designed to address the limitations of existing table discovery methods by including diverse queries with NL-only, union, join, and fuzzy conditions. The dataset comprises 627 queries, 22,080 candidate tables, and 21,200 relevance annotations, providing a comprehensive evaluation framework for advanced techniques. Our evaluation of six state-of-the-art methods on this dataset reveals substantial performance gaps, emphasizing the need for more robust approaches to handle the complexity of NL-conditional table discovery.

In summary, while existing methods have made progress in semantic parsing and ad hoc table retrieval, the integration of NL conditions with tabular data remains a challenging and underexplored area. Our work addresses this gap
