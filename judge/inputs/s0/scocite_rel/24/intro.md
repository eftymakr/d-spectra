## Related Works

Diffusion models have gained significant traction in image and video generation due to their ability to produce high-quality outputs \[[24](http://arxiv.org/abs/2310.00426)\]. However, their deployment on edge devices is hindered by high computational demands and memory usage \[[24](http://arxiv.org/abs/2310.00426)\]. To address these challenges, various quantization techniques have been proposed for diffusion models. Q-Diffusion \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2302.04304)\] introduces a post-training quantization (PTQ) method tailored for diffusion models, targeting the multi-timestep pipeline and model architecture. It identifies key challenges such as changing output distributions over time steps and bimodal activation distributions, and proposes timestep-aware calibration and split shortcut quantization to mitigate these issues. This method successfully quantizes full-precision models to 4-bit while maintaining comparable performance \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2302.04304)\].

Diffusion Transformers (DiTs), an alternative to traditional U-Net-based diffusion models, have shown superior scalability and performance in image and video generation \[[8](http://arxiv.org/abs/2212.09748)\]. Despite their advantages, DiTs face challenges in deployment due to their high parameter count and computational complexity \[[11](http://arxiv.org/abs/2406.17343)\]. PTQ4DiT \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/2405.16005)\] is a PTQ method specifically designed for DiTs, addressing challenges such as salient channels with extreme magnitudes and temporal variability in activation distributions. It introduces Channel-wise Salience Balancing (CSB) and Spearmen's ρ-guided Salience Calibration (SSC) to improve quantization accuracy. PTQ4DiT successfully quantizes DiTs to 8-bit precision (W8A8) and further enables 4-bit weight precision (W4A8) for the first time \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/2405.16005)\].

For video generation, diffusion models face additional challenges due to the need for temporal coherence and high-resolution outputs \[[22](http://arxiv.org/abs/2204.03458)\]. Latte \[[14](http://arxiv.org/abs/2401.03048)\] is a latent diffusion transformer designed for video generation, incorporating spatio-temporal tokens and efficient variants to model video distribution in the latent space. It achieves state-of-the-art performance on standard video generation datasets \[[14](http://arxiv.org/abs/2401.03048)\]. Similarly, Stable Video Diffusion \[[27](http://arxiv.org/abs/2311.15127)\] extends latent diffusion models to video generation, emphasizing the importance of well-curated pretraining datasets for generating high-quality videos. It demonstrates the necessity of a strong base model for downstream tasks like image-to-video generation \[[27](http://arxiv.org/abs/2311.15127)\].

Quantization methods for diffusion models often struggle with maintaining performance at low bit-widths due to the unique properties of diffusion models, such as temporal variation in activation \[[7](http://arxiv.org/abs/2306.02316)\]. PTQD \[[5](http://arxiv.org/abs/2305.10657)\] introduces a unified formulation for quantization noise and diffusion perturbed noise, addressing issues like mean deviation and variance schedule
