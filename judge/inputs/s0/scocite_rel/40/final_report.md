## Related Works

Zero-Knowledge Proofs (ZKPs) have garnered significant attention for their ability to enable secure and private computation, allowing a prover to demonstrate the truth of a statement without revealing additional information \[[9](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/6bcd2606c8f0099a82bd03e520659058a5b12e51)\]. This capability has led to their adoption in various domains, including blockchain technologies, verifiable machine learning, and electronic voting \[[6](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/3becc2935d1f52c82ffd7216c28bc86f5e393208)\]. However, the computational complexity of the proving process has hindered their widespread adoption \[[1](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/e149d93531d8c07cf43f6cae327a4795144d1304)\]. Recent research has focused on accelerating the key primitives of ZKP protocols using hardware accelerators such as GPUs and ASICs \[[10](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/6336149196f02d0eb6c1a89bc6b662e4422bd794)\]. 

Several works have proposed GPU-accelerated ZKP systems to improve performance. GZKP, for instance, achieves significant speedups by optimizing memory access patterns and eliminating costly external shuffles \[[1](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/e149d93531d8c07cf43f6cae327a4795144d1304)\]. Similarly, PipeZK introduces a pipelined architecture with two subsystems to handle polynomial computations and multi-scalar multiplications on elliptic curves, achieving up to 10x speedup on cryptographic benchmarks \[[2](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/6ee84674b7c426849c3da56cb278c08eb7a903cf)\]. cuZK further improves performance by introducing a faster parallel multi-scalar multiplication algorithm and optimizing data transfer between CPU and GPU \[[4](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/e4a4608e8e35379e63b9df5bda1b7599ee8ab9ac)\]. These accelerators demonstrate the potential of hardware-based optimizations to reduce the overhead of ZKP generation.

Despite these advancements, existing accelerators often face limitations. Some require a trusted setup for each application, which limits their reusability \[[1](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/e149d93531d8c07cf43f6cae327a4795144d1304)\]. Others generate larger proof sizes or have higher verification costs, which can be problematic in scenarios with numerous verifiers or strict time constraints \[[1](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/e149d93531d8c07cf43f6cae327a4795144d1304)\]. For example, the Groth16 zkSNARK protocol, widely used in cryptocurrencies, suffers from high overhead due to its proof generation step, which involves large-scale matrix-vector multiplication, number-theoretic transforms, and multi-scalar multiplication \[[4](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/e4a4608e8e35379e63b9df5bda1b7599ee8ab9ac)\]. 

To address these challenges, recent works have focused on developing more efficient and scalable ZKP protocols. For instance, zkCNN introduces a zero-knowledge proof scheme for convolutional neural networks (CNNs), enabling the verification of model predictions without leaking information about the model itself \[[10](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/6336149196f02d0eb6c1a89bc6b662e4422bd794)\]. Similarly, protocols for zero-knowledge decision tree predictions and accuracy have been proposed, allowing models to prove their correctness on public datasets \[[6](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/3becc2935d1f52c82ffd7216c28bc86f5e393208)\]. These developments highlight the growing interest in applying ZKPs to machine learning and other data-intensive applications.

Hardware acceleration for zero-knowledge proof (ZKP) primitives has emerged as a critical area of research to address the performance bottlenecks in ZKP systems, particularly in proof generation \[[1](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/e149d93531d8c07cf43f6cae327a4795144d1304)\]. Traditional ZKP implementations suffer from high computational overhead due to intensive operations such as polynomial computations over large finite fields and multi-scalar multiplications on elliptic curves \[[2](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/6ee84674b7c426849c3da56cb278c08eb7a903cf)\]. To mitigate these challenges, several works have proposed hardware accelerators tailored for ZKP workloads. For instance, GZKP leverages GPU acceleration to optimize memory access patterns and eliminate costly external shuffles, achieving significant speedups in both standard benchmarks and real-world applications \[[1](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/e149d93531d8c07cf43f6cae327a4795144d1304)\]. Similarly, PipeZK introduces a pipelined architecture with two subsystems designed to handle large polynomial computations and multi-scalar multiplications efficiently, resulting in up to 10x speedup on cryptographic benchmarks and 5x on Zcash \[[2](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/6ee84674b7c426849c3da56cb278c08eb7a903cf)\]. cuZK further advances GPU-based acceleration by implementing parallelized algorithms for multi-scalar multiplication, matrix-vector multiplication, and number-theoretic transforms, while minimizing CPU-GPU data transfer latency \[[4](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/e4a4608e8e35379e63b9df5bda1b7599ee8ab9ac)\]. These accelerators collectively highlight the growing focus on optimizing ZKP performance through specialized hardware, enabling broader adoption in privacy-preserving applications such as blockchain and verifiable computing.

### <a id='category-efficient-zkp-protocols-for-machine-learning'></a>Efficient ZKP Protocols for Machine Learning: 2 papers found.
Efficient ZKP Protocols for Machine Learning focus on enabling secure and private verification of machine learning models without exposing model details. These protocols allow model owners to prove predictions or accuracy on datasets without leaking sensitive information, addressing concerns around reproducibility and security in machine learning \[[6](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/3becc2935d1f52c82ffd7216c28bc86f5e393208)\]. For instance, decision tree models can be verified using zero knowledge proofs, where a model with 23 levels and 1,029 nodes can generate a proof in 250 seconds with a proof size of around 287 kilobytes \[[6](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/3becc2935d1f52c82ffd7216c28bc86f5e393208)\]. Similarly, for convolutional neural networks (CNNs), schemes like zkCNN have been proposed, supporting large models such as VGG16 with 15 million parameters and 16 layers. These schemes achieve high efficiency, with proof generation taking only 88.3 seconds and proof sizes of 341 kilobytes \[[10](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/6336149196f02d0eb6c1a89bc6b662e4422bd794)\]. The underlying protocols, such as the sumcheck protocol for fast Fourier transforms and convolutions, enable faster computations and reduce verification times, making these ZKP approaches more practical for real-world applications \[[10](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/6336149196f02d0eb6c1a89bc6b662e4422bd794)\]. These developments are part of broader efforts to enhance the usability of ZKPs in machine learning, ensuring both privacy and verifiability \[[6](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/3becc2935d1f52c82ffd7216c28bc86f5e393208)\].
