## Related Works

Brain tumor segmentation from 3D MRI scans has been a focal point in medical image analysis, with numerous approaches proposed to improve segmentation accuracy and efficiency. Early methods relied on manual delineation, which is time-consuming and subject to human error \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/1810.11654)\]. To automate this process, deep learning models, particularly convolutional neural networks (CNNs), have become the dominant approach \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/1505.04597)\]. The U-Net architecture, introduced in 2015, has been widely adopted for biomedical image segmentation due to its symmetric encoder-decoder structure and ability to capture contextual information \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/1505.04597)\]. Variants of U-Net, such as 3D U-Net, have been specifically tailored for 3D MRI data, addressing the challenges of volumetric segmentation \[[21](http://arxiv.org/abs/1606.06650)\]. 

Several studies have explored the use of pre-trained models to reduce training time and improve performance. For example, the use of pre-trained weights from ImageNet has been shown to enhance feature learning in 2D CNNs, and similar strategies have been adapted for 3D medical image segmentation \[[22](http://arxiv.org/abs/2110.03352)\]. However, transferring 2D pre-trained models to the 3D domain remains a challenge, as it requires careful preservation of learned relationships and feature representations \[[22](http://arxiv.org/abs/2110.03352)\]. This has led to the development of specialized strategies for 3D CNNs, such as the use of axial-coronal-sagittal convolutions, which help maintain spatial coherence in 3D data \[[22](http://arxiv.org/abs/2110.03352)\]. 

The BraTS challenge has served as a benchmark for evaluating brain tumor segmentation methods, with various approaches achieving state-of-the-art results. For instance, the 3D U-Net architecture has been modified with dense training schemes and dual pathway designs to improve performance on multi-channel MRI data \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/1603.05959)\]. Additionally, the use of conditional random fields (CRFs) for post-processing has been effective in reducing false positives and improving segmentation accuracy \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/1603.05959)\]. 

Recent advancements have also focused on improving computational efficiency while maintaining high accuracy. For example, the nnU-Net framework has been designed to self-adapt to different segmentation tasks, reducing the need for manual hyperparameter tuning \[[18](http://arxiv.org/abs/1809.10486)\]. This has led to improved performance on the BraTS challenge, with nnU-Net achieving top rankings in several datasets \[[17](http://arxiv.org/abs/2011.00848)\]. Furthermore, the integration of attention mechanisms and residual connections has been shown to enhance the ability of models to capture both local and global features, leading to better segmentation outcomes \[[24](http://arxiv.org/abs/2010.13082)\].

The related works in brain tumor segmentation primarily focus on improving segmentation accuracy through advanced neural network architectures and efficient training strategies. Early methods relied on manual segmentation, which was time-consuming and error-prone \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/1810.11654)\]. To address these limitations, deep learning approaches, particularly convolutional neural networks (CNNs), have become the standard due to their ability to capture complex spatial patterns in 3D MRI data \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/1505.04597)\]. ### <a id='category-attention-and-residual-mechanisms'></a>Attention and Residual Mechanisms: 2 papers found.
Attention and residual mechanisms have been widely explored in medical image segmentation to improve accuracy and efficiency. These mechanisms allow models to better capture contextual information and preserve important features during the segmentation process. For instance, residual connections enable the network to learn more complex mappings by mitigating the vanishing gradient problem, while attention mechanisms help focus on relevant regions of the image \[[25](http://arxiv.org/abs/1811.01328)\]. In the context of brain tumor segmentation, a cascade of fully convolutional neural networks with residual connections and multi-scale predictions has been proposed to segment multi-modal MR images into background and three hierarchical regions: whole tumor, tumor core, and enhancing tumor core \[[6](http://arxiv.org/abs/1709.00382)\]. Similarly, in liver tumor segmentation, a 3D hybrid residual attention-aware segmentation method (RA-UNet) has been introduced, integrating attention residual modules to adaptively adjust attention-aware features for more accurate tumor detection \[[25](http://arxiv.org/abs/1811.01328)\]. These approaches highlight the effectiveness of combining attention and residual mechanisms to enhance segmentation performance in medical imaging tasks.

### <a id='category-nnu-net-framework'></a>nnU-Net Framework: 3 papers found.
The nnU-Net framework, built upon the foundational U-Net architecture, has emerged as a robust and adaptable solution for medical image segmentation tasks \[[18](http://arxiv.org/abs/1809.10486)\]. Initially introduced to address the variability in network design and preprocessing, nnU-Net simplifies the training process by minimizing manual adjustments and focusing on performance and generalizability \[[18](http://arxiv.org/abs/1809.10486)\]. This framework has been successfully applied to challenges such as the BraTS 2020 competition, where it achieved state-of-the-art results with Dice scores of 88.95, 85.06, and 82.03 for whole tumor, tumor core, and enhancing tumor, respectively, alongside HD95 values of 8.498, 17.337, and 17.805 \[[17](http://arxiv.org/abs/2011.00848)\]. The framework's adaptability is further demonstrated through its performance in the Medical Segmentation Decathlon, where it achieved the highest mean dice scores across all classes and seven phase 1 tasks \[[18](http://arxiv.org/abs/1809.10486)\]. These achievements highlight the effectiveness of nnU-Net in handling diverse medical imaging tasks, making it a valuable baseline for further innovations in brain tumor segmentation \[[17](http://arxiv.org/abs/2011.00848)\].
