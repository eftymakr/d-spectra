## Related Works

Deep learning has emerged as a powerful paradigm for solving inverse problems in imaging, with two prominent approaches being deep-unrolling and plug-and-play (PnP) methods. PnP approaches, which integrate off-the-shelf deep denoisers into iterative algorithms, offer flexibility in handling varying compression ratios (CRs) \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/1704.03488)\]. However, they often suffer from limitations in reconstruction accuracy and speed \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/1704.03488)\]. On the other hand, unrolling methods transform truncated iterative optimization processes into end-to-end trainable networks, typically achieving better accuracy and faster inference \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/1704.03488)\]. Nevertheless, these methods often require retraining when CR changes \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/1704.03488)\]. 

The integration of PnP and unrolling approaches has been explored in several works. For instance, the use of denoising networks as implicit image priors in variational methods has been studied, enabling the replacement of proximal operators with neural networks \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/1912.10557)\]. This approach allows for the use of fixed denoisers across different inverse problems, reducing the need for problem-specific training \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/1912.10557)\]. Similarly, algorithm unrolling has been used to connect iterative algorithms like sparse coding to neural network architectures, offering interpretable and efficient deep learning models \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/1508.02848)\]. This technique has been further extended to image deblurring, where an iterative algorithm is unrolled to construct a neural network \[[4](http://arxiv.org/abs/1902.05399)\].

The concept of unrolling has also been applied to other imaging tasks, such as image super-resolution and compressive sensing. For example, the Learned Primal-Dual algorithm unrolls a proximal primal-dual optimization method, replacing proximal operators with convolutional neural networks \[[5](http://arxiv.org/abs/1704.03264)\]. This approach is trained end-to-end and does not rely on any initial reconstruction, making it suitable for low-dose computed tomography \[[5](http://arxiv.org/abs/1704.03264)\]. Similarly, ISTA-Net, inspired by the Iterative Shrinkage-Thresholding Algorithm (ISTA), has been proposed for compressive sensing (CS) reconstruction, combining the structure insights of traditional optimization-based methods with the speed of network-based approaches \[[6](http://arxiv.org/abs/1712.02862)\].

In addition to unrolling, the use of deep denoisers as priors in model-based optimization methods has been investigated. For example, the TNRD (Trainable Nonlinear Reaction Diffusion) framework learns all parameters, including filters and influence functions, from training data, leading to state-of-the-art performance in image restoration tasks \[[7](http://arxiv.org/abs/1611.03679)\]. The D-AMP algorithm, which mimics the behavior of the denoising-based approximate message passing (D-AMP) algorithm, has also been developed, offering improved accuracy and run time

### <a id='category-unrolling-method-foundations'></a>Unrolling Method Foundations: 9 papers found.
The concept of algorithm unrolling has gained significant traction in bridging the gap between traditional iterative algorithms and deep neural networks, particularly in the context of inverse problems in imaging and signal processing \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/1912.10557)\]. This approach involves unrolling the iterations of an optimization algorithm into a deep network architecture, allowing for the integration of domain-specific knowledge while maintaining the interpretability and efficiency of iterative methods \[[5](http://arxiv.org/abs/1704.03264)\]. For instance, unrolling the half-quadratic splitting (HQS) or alternating direction method of multipliers (ADMM) algorithms has been shown to yield deep networks that can be trained end-to-end, thereby achieving high performance and flexibility in handling varying problem conditions \[[8](http://arxiv.org/abs/2003.10428)\]. Such networks, like the proposed ProxUnroll framework, are capable of adapting to different compression ratios without requiring retraining, a key advantage over CR-specific unrolling networks \[[25](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/2cdc9bbde0f847094582b212f980fa4dbc48950d)\]. Additionally, the use of a general proximal trajectory (PT) loss function enables the training of these networks to approximate the proximal operator of an ideal explicit restoration regularizer, further enhancing their performance \[[25](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/2cdc9bbde0f847094582b212f980fa4dbc48950d)\]. This unrolling technique has been successfully applied to tasks such as image deblurring, compressive sensing, and tomographic reconstruction, demonstrating its versatility and effectiveness \[[7](http://arxiv.org/abs/1611.03679)\]. The ability to combine the strengths of both model-based and learning-based approaches through unrolling has led to significant improvements in accuracy and speed, making it a promising direction for future research \[[10](http://arxiv.org/abs/1409.2574)\].

### <a id='category-image-restoration-frameworks'></a>Image Restoration Frameworks: 7 papers found.
Image restoration frameworks have seen significant advancements with the integration of deep learning techniques, particularly in addressing challenges such as denoising, super-resolution, and deblurring. Recent approaches, such as Trainable Nonlinear Reaction Diffusion (TNRD), leverage dynamic models with time-dependent parameters to enhance performance across various restoration tasks \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/1508.02848)\]. Similarly, discriminative models like DnCNN have demonstrated superior results by incorporating residual learning and batch normalization, enabling blind Gaussian denoising and multi-task handling \[[9](http://arxiv.org/abs/1608.03981)\]. For deblurring, methods like Scale-recurrent Networks (SRN-DeblurNet) employ a "coarse-to-fine" strategy, achieving better quality results with simpler architectures \[[16](http://arxiv.org/abs/1802.01770)\]. Additionally, multi-scale convolutional neural networks have been proposed to handle complex motion blurs in dynamic scenes, supported by large-scale datasets with ground truth \[[20](http://arxiv.org/abs/1612.02177)\]. These developments highlight the shift towards end-to-end trainable models that balance flexibility and efficiency, often leveraging GPU acceleration for faster inference \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/1508.02848)\]\[[9](http://arxiv.org/abs/1608.03981)\]\[[16](http://arxiv.org/abs/1802.01770)\].
