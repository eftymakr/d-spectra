## Related Works

Unsupervised pre-training has emerged as a powerful paradigm for learning general-purpose representations from raw data. This approach has been widely applied in computer vision and natural language processing, with notable works such as Contrastive Predictive Coding \[[8](http://arxiv.org/abs/1905.09272)\], which leverages autoregressive models to learn latent representations by predicting future samples \[[8](http://arxiv.org/abs/1905.09272)\]. Similarly, SimCLR \[[12](http://arxiv.org/abs/2002.05709)\] presents a simple framework for contrastive learning of visual representations, demonstrating that composition of data augmentations and learnable nonlinear transformations significantly improve representation quality \[[12](http://arxiv.org/abs/2002.05709)\]. These methods have shown strong performance across various domains, including speech, images, and text \[[8](http://arxiv.org/abs/1905.09272)\]. However, these approaches often rely on large amounts of unlabeled data and may not capture the complex relationships between different genomic profiles \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/1807.03748)\].

In the field of genomics, unsupervised learning has also been explored. For example, the work by \[[25](http://arxiv.org/abs/1603.09246)\] proposes an unsupervised learning approach to build features suitable for object detection and classification by solving jigsaw puzzles of natural images. This method pre-trains a context-free network (CFN) on a large dataset and transfers the features to other tasks through fine-tuning. While this approach is effective for visual tasks, it does not directly address the challenges of learning representations from genomic data, which are regulated by complex genomic profiles such as chromatin accessibility \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/1807.03748)\].

Recent advances in self-supervised learning have also shown promise for visual tasks. For instance, the paper by \[[20](http://arxiv.org/abs/2104.14294)\] explores the properties of self-supervised Vision Transformers (ViT), finding that these models can learn semantic segmentation information and achieve strong performance in k-NN classification tasks. The paper also highlights the importance of momentum encoder and multi-crop training in improving the performance of self-supervised ViTs \[[20](http://arxiv.org/abs/2104.14294)\]. These findings suggest that self-supervised learning can be effective for visual tasks, but they do not directly address the challenges of learning representations from genomic data, which require capturing the relationships between DNA sequences and genomic profiles \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/1807.03748)\].

In addition to these methods, there are several approaches that focus on semi-supervised learning. For example, the paper by \[[21](http://arxiv.org/abs/1606.04586)\] proposes a regularization method that leverages stochastic transformations and perturbations to improve the performance of semi-supervised learning with convolutional neural networks.
