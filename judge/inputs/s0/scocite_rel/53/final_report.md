## Related Works

Sequential recommendation systems aim to predict the next item a user will interact with, based on their historical behavior. These systems have evolved significantly, incorporating advanced neural network architectures to better capture sequential patterns and user preferences. Early approaches, such as Markov Chains (MCs), assumed that the next action depends only on the last few actions, while Recurrent Neural Networks (RNNs) introduced the ability to model longer-term dependencies \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/1808.09781)\]. However, RNNs often suffer from computational inefficiency and difficulty in capturing long-range dependencies \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/1808.09781)\]. More recently, self-attention mechanisms have gained popularity for their ability to dynamically weigh the importance of different items in a sequence \[[5](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/9c7a455b1e48d01a99e58e884a6c1acb75074ad0)\]. 

The use of self-attention in sequential recommendation was further refined with models like SASRec, which combines the flexibility of attention with the efficiency of MC-based methods by focusing on relevant items in the history \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/1808.09781)\]. Similarly, TiSASRec extends this idea by incorporating time intervals between interactions, allowing for more nuanced modeling of temporal dynamics \[[5](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/9c7a455b1e48d01a99e58e884a6c1acb75074ad0)\]. These models demonstrate the importance of temporal awareness in capturing user behavior accurately.

Convolutional approaches, such as Caser, have also been introduced to model sequential patterns by treating item sequences as images in the time and latent spaces \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/1809.07426)\]. This enables the extraction of local features, which can be useful for capturing both general preferences and sequential patterns. However, these models may not fully address the challenges of modeling long-range dependencies or capturing diverse interaction patterns \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/1809.07426)\].

The introduction of bidirectional models, such as BERT4Rec, has further advanced the field by allowing the model to consider both left and right context of an item, thereby improving the representation of user behavior \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/1904.06690)\]. This bidirectional approach helps in capturing more comprehensive user preferences, but it introduces challenges related to training efficiency and information leakage \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/1904.06690)\]. To mitigate these issues, the Cloze objective is used to train the model by predicting masked items based on their surrounding context \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/1904.06690)\].

In addition to attention-based and convolutional models, there has been growing interest in self-supervised learning for sequential recommendation. S3-Rec, for instance, leverages mutual information maximization to enhance the learning of correlations among item attributes and subsequences \[[4](http://arxiv.org/abs/2008.07873)\]. This approach is particularly beneficial in scenarios with limited training data, as it enables the model to learn meaningful representations without explicit supervision \[[4](http://arxiv.org/abs/2008.07873)\].

Another notable advancement is the use of graph neural networks (GNNs) in session-based recommendation, where session sequences are modeled as graph-structured data to capture complex item transitions

### <a id='category-attention-based-sequential-recommendation'></a>Attention-Based Sequential Recommendation: 4 papers found.
Attention-based sequential recommendation methods have gained significant traction in recent years due to their ability to model complex user behavior patterns effectively. These approaches leverage attention mechanisms to capture both short-term and long-term dependencies in user interaction sequences, allowing for more nuanced recommendations \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/1808.09781)\]. For instance, SASRec introduces a self-attention based model that balances the strengths of Markov Chains and Recurrent Neural Networks by focusing on relevant actions in the user's history \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/1808.09781)\]. Similarly, NARM employs a hybrid encoder with an attention mechanism to identify the user's main purpose within a session, enhancing the accuracy of recommendations \[[6](http://arxiv.org/abs/1711.04725)\]. The FDSA model further advances this field by incorporating feature-level sequences, enabling the extraction of full sequential patterns by considering both item-level and feature-level transitions \[[8](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/63555b4f363ee72dd8d49d8deca68760542c0cd2)\]. Additionally, hierarchical attention networks have been proposed to address the dynamic nature of user preferences by integrating both long-term and short-term behaviors \[[15](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/0b42168f22713c48d8b0ce3bc8f680d347184d88)\]. These methods collectively highlight the importance of attention mechanisms in capturing diverse interaction patterns and adapting to varying sequence lengths, which aligns with the challenges addressed by STAR-Rec \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/1808.09781)\].

### <a id='category-convolutional-approaches-for-sequences'></a>Convolutional Approaches for Sequences: 2 papers found.
Convolutional approaches for sequences have been increasingly applied to session-based next item recommendation, where user interaction sequences are modeled using convolutional neural networks (CNNs) \[[13](http://arxiv.org/abs/1808.05163)\]. In this framework, sequences of past items are embedded into a 2D latent matrix, treated as an image, and processed with convolutional and pooling operations to capture local patterns \[[13](http://arxiv.org/abs/1808.05163)\]. This approach enables the extraction of both short- and long-range dependencies, improving the model's ability to handle variable-length sequences \[[13](http://arxiv.org/abs/1808.05163)\]. Additionally, recent work has introduced stacked holed convolutional layers to expand the receptive field without relying on pooling, allowing for more efficient feature learning \[[13](http://arxiv.org/abs/1808.05163)\]. The integration of residual blocks further enhances model performance by facilitating deeper network training \[[13](http://arxiv.org/abs/1808.05163)\]. These methods have demonstrated superior accuracy compared to traditional approaches, particularly in scenarios involving long user interaction sequences \[[13](http://arxiv.org/abs/1808.05163)\]. Furthermore, convolutional sequence embedding models, such as Caser, have been proposed to capture sequential patterns by embedding item sequences into a 2D space and applying convolutional filters to learn local features \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/1809.07426)\]. These models provide a unified framework for capturing both general preferences and sequential dependencies, achieving state-of-the-art results on various evaluation metrics \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/1809.07426)\]. The combination of convolutional techniques with attention mechanisms and state-space modeling has shown promise in addressing the challenges of sequence modeling in recommendation systems \[[13](http://arxiv.org/abs/1808.05163)\].

### <a id='category-self-supervised-learning-in-recommendations'></a>Self-Supervised Learning in Recommendations: 3 papers found.
Recent advancements in self-supervised learning have significantly impacted sequential recommendation models, addressing key limitations such as data sparsity and representation degeneration. For instance, S3-Rec introduces a self-supervised framework that leverages mutual information maximization to capture correlations among item attributes, subsequences, and sequences, enhancing data representations through pre-training \[[4](http://arxiv.org/abs/2008.07873)\]. Similarly, DuoRec tackles the issue of anisotropic item embedding distributions by employing contrastive regularization, which reshapes sequence representations while preserving semantic consistency \[[10](http://arxiv.org/abs/2110.05730)\]. These methods highlight the importance of improving data representations through auxiliary objectives and regularization techniques. Additionally, PeterRec extends the concept of inductive transfer learning to recommender systems, enabling parameter-efficient adaptation to downstream tasks by maintaining pre-trained parameters during fine-tuning \[[12](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/2ca1abd015c72e7c04d63fa653b0a27d9592fc02)\]. These works collectively emphasize the role of self-supervised learning in improving the robustness and adaptability of sequential recommendation models, particularly in scenarios with limited data or diverse user behaviors.

Extensive empirical studies show the features of TiSASRec under different settings and compare the performance of self-attention with different positional encodings. Furthermore, experimental results show that our method outperforms various state-of-the-art sequential models on both sparse and dense datasets and different evaluation metrics.»

### <a id='category-evaluation-metrics-and-datasets'></a>Evaluation Metrics and Datasets: 3 papers found.
The related works in sequential recommendation systems focus on addressing the challenges of modeling dynamic user preferences and handling noisy logged data. Recent studies have explored the use of deep neural networks such as RNN, CNN, and Transformer to capture sequential patterns in user behavior \[[7](http://arxiv.org/abs/2202.13556)\]. However, these models often struggle with overfitting due to the inherent noise in logged data \[[7](http://arxiv.org/abs/2202.13556)\]. To mitigate this, researchers have drawn inspiration from signal processing techniques, such as filtering algorithms, to attenuate noise in the frequency domain, leading to improved performance \[[7](http://arxiv.org/abs/2202.13556)\]. Additionally, the complexity of modeling sequential interactions has led to the development of specialized architectures, including all-MLP models with learnable filters, which offer lower time complexity and adaptive noise reduction \[[7](http://arxiv.org/abs/2202.13556)\]. Furthermore, the evolution of user preferences and item popularity over time has prompted the integration of high-level visual features and evolving trends in recommendation models, particularly in domains like fashion \[[16](http://arxiv.org/abs/1602.01585)\]. These approaches highlight the ongoing efforts to enhance the accuracy and adaptability of sequential recommendation systems by leveraging advanced modeling techniques and domain-specific insights.
