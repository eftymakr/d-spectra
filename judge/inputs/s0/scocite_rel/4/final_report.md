## Related Works

The problem of learning from noisy labels has been extensively studied in recent years, with a focus on developing robust training strategies for deep neural networks. Early approaches often treated label noise as statistical outliers and employed techniques such as importance re-weighting and bootstrapping to mitigate its effects \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/1703.02391)\]. However, these methods typically assume that noisy labels are independent of the true labels, which is not always the case in real-world scenarios \[[10](http://arxiv.org/abs/1712.09482)\]. 

More recent work has explored the use of loss functions that are inherently robust to label noise. For example, the mean absolute error (MAE) loss has been proposed as a noise-robust alternative to the categorical cross entropy (CCE) loss \[[14](http://arxiv.org/abs/1805.07836)\]. While MAE shows promise, it can perform poorly with deep neural networks on challenging datasets \[[14](http://arxiv.org/abs/1805.07836)\]. To address this, generalized cross entropy loss functions have been introduced, offering a theoretically grounded approach that generalizes both MAE and CCE \[[14](http://arxiv.org/abs/1805.07836)\].

Another line of research has focused on leveraging auxiliary information, such as clean datasets or label relations in knowledge graphs, to improve learning from noisy labels \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/1703.02391)\]. This approach, known as distillation, uses side information to "hedge the risk" of learning from noisy labels \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/1703.02391)\]. Additionally, some methods have explored the use of symmetric cross entropy learning (SL), which combines cross entropy with a noise-robust counterpart, reverse cross entropy (RCE), to address both overfitting and under-learning issues \[[4](http://arxiv.org/abs/1908.06112)\].

The problem of label noise is also closely related to the broader issue of robustness in deep learning. Several studies have investigated the memorization effects of deep neural networks, showing that they tend to first memorize clean labels before those with noise \[[7](http://arxiv.org/abs/1804.06872)\]. This insight has led to the development of methods such as Co-teaching, where two neural networks are trained simultaneously and learn from each other \[[7](http://arxiv.org/abs/1804.06872)\]. Variants of this approach, such as Co-teaching+, have been proposed to address convergence issues and improve robustness \[[11](http://arxiv.org/abs/1901.04215)\].

In addition to these methods, there has been growing interest in semi-supervised learning as a way to reduce the reliance on clean labels. For example, DivideMix leverages semi-supervised learning techniques to dynamically divide training data into labeled and unlabeled sets, improving performance on noisy datasets \[[8](http://arxiv.org/abs/2002.07394)\]. Similarly, L_DMI introduces an information-theoretic loss function that is provably robust to instance-independent label noise, without requiring auxiliary information \[[12](http://arxiv.org/abs/1909.03388)\].

The category "Semi-Supervised Learning for Noisy Data" encompasses approaches aimed at improving deep learning models when trained on datasets with noisy or unreliable labels. These methods often leverage semi-supervised learning techniques to exploit both labeled and unlabeled data, addressing the challenge of label noise. For instance, one approach formulates the problem using an undirected graphical model that captures the relationship between noisy and clean labels, enabling tractable inference over latent clean labels while regularizing training with auxiliary information \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/1706.00038)\]. Another method, DivideMix, introduces a framework that dynamically partitions training data into clean and noisy subsets using a mixture model, and trains the model in a semi-supervised manner by simultaneously training two diverged networks to mitigate confirmation bias \[[8](http://arxiv.org/abs/2002.07394)\]. Additionally, the method enhances the MixMatch strategy through label co-refinement and co-guessing, improving performance on benchmark datasets \[[8](http://arxiv.org/abs/2002.07394)\]. These techniques highlight the growing interest in robust learning strategies that can effectively handle noisy data without requiring extensive manual annotation.

### <a id='category-label-noise-mitigation-techniques'></a>Label Noise Mitigation Techniques: 7 papers found.
Label Noise Mitigation Techniques encompass a variety of approaches aimed at improving the robustness of deep learning models in the presence of noisy or incorrect labels. Existing methods often rely on human supervision, which is time-consuming and not scalable, or on automated techniques that may lack effectiveness \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/1706.00038)\]. To address these challenges, researchers have proposed frameworks that leverage joint neural embedding networks, such as CleanNet, which requires only a fraction of manually verified labels to infer label noise patterns and transfer this knowledge to other classes \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/1706.00038)\]. Another approach involves loss correction procedures that account for class-dependent label noise, using matrix inversion and multiplication to adjust for corruption probabilities, with techniques for estimating these probabilities adapted from noise estimation methods \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/1711.07131)\]. Additionally, dimensionality-driven learning strategies have been introduced to monitor and adapt the loss function based on the dimensionality of deep representation subspaces, enabling models to tolerate significant proportions of noisy labels \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/1703.02391)\]. Meta-learning algorithms have also been developed to assign weights to training examples based on gradient directions, avoiding the need for additional hyperparameter tuning and improving performance on corrupted label problems \[[4](http://arxiv.org/abs/1908.06112)\]. Furthermore, instance-dependent label noise, which is more prevalent in real-world datasets, has been addressed through second-order statistical methods that capture induced imbalances and allow the application of existing class-dependent noise mitigation techniques \[[5](http://arxiv.org/abs/1609.03683)\]. These diverse strategies highlight the ongoing efforts to enhance model robustness under label noise, with a focus on scalability, effectiveness, and adaptability to different noise patterns.

The category "Robust Loss Functions for Noisy Labels" focuses on developing loss functions that can effectively handle label noise in deep learning tasks. Existing approaches often modify network architectures or estimate true labels from noisy ones, but an alternative approach is to design loss functions that are inherently noise-tolerant \[[10](http://arxiv.org/abs/1712.09482)\]. For instance, the mean absolute error (MAE) loss has been proposed as a noise-robust alternative to categorical cross entropy (CCE), although it can still perform poorly on challenging datasets \[[14](http://arxiv.org/abs/1805.07836)\]. To address these limitations, researchers have introduced theoretically grounded loss functions that generalize both MAE and CCE, offering better performance across various noisy label scenarios \[[14](http://arxiv.org/abs/1805.07836)\]. Additionally, symmetric cross entropy learning (SL) has been proposed to address both overfitting to noisy labels and under learning in deep neural networks by combining cross entropy with a noise-robust reverse cross entropy (RCE) term \[[4](http://arxiv.org/abs/1908.06112)\]. These advancements highlight the ongoing efforts to improve robustness in deep learning under label noise, with a growing emphasis on loss function design \[[10](http://arxiv.org/abs/1712.09482)\].

	Document 1: [Snippet]: «Deep learning with noisy labels is practically challenging, as the capacity of deep models is so high that they can totally memorize these noisy labels sooner or later during training. Nonetheless, recent studies on the memorization effects of deep neural networks show that they would first memorize training data of clean labels and then those of noisy labels. Therefore in this paper, we propose a new deep learning paradigm called Co-teaching for combating with noisy labels. Namely, we train two deep neural networks simultaneously, and let them teach each other given every mini-batch: firstly, each network feeds forward all data and selects some data of possibly clean labels; secondly, two networks communicate with each other what data in this mini-batch should be used for training; finally, each network back propagates the data selected by its peer network and updates itself. Empirical results on noisy versions of MNIST, CIFAR-10 and CIFAR-100 demonstrate that Co-teaching is much superior to the state-of-the-art methods in the robustness of trained deep models.»
