## Related Works

Vision Transformers (ViTs) have gained significant attention for their ability to capture global context in computer vision tasks, surpassing traditional convolutional neural networks (CNNs) in certain scenarios \[[4](http://arxiv.org/abs/2010.11929)\]. However, the deployment of ViTs on hardware, especially FPGAs, remains challenging due to the complexity of arithmetic operations such as Softmax and LayerNorm, which are sensitive to precision and difficult to map onto efficient hardware \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2208.05163)\]. Prior works have primarily focused on optimizing matrix multiplication operations on hardware, while keeping complex operations on the CPU, resulting in suboptimal performance due to communication overhead between the CPU and accelerator \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2208.05163)\]. 

Several studies have explored quantization techniques to reduce the computational and memory footprint of ViTs for efficient inference. For example, Auto-ViT-Acc proposes an FPGA-aware acceleration framework with mixed-scheme quantization, achieving higher accuracy than state-of-the-art quantization methods under the same bit-width \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2208.05163)\]. Similarly, VAQF introduces a fully automatic software-hardware co-design framework for low-bit ViTs, enabling real-time execution on FPGAs with minimal compilation time \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/2201.06618)\]. These approaches highlight the potential of quantization in reducing computational demands while preserving accuracy. However, they often rely on manual tuning or limited automation, which can hinder scalability and adaptability.

To address the limitations of existing methods, ViTCoD introduces a dedicated algorithm and accelerator co-design framework that prunes and polarizes attention maps to reduce dominant data movement costs, achieving significant speedups over general computing platforms \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/2210.09573)\]. Additionally, FQ-ViT presents a post-training quantization method for fully quantized ViTs, mitigating performance degradation through systematic techniques like Power-of-Two Factor (PTF) and Log-Int-Softmax (LIS) \[[9](http://arxiv.org/abs/2111.13824)\]. These methods demonstrate the feasibility of quantizing ViTs with minimal accuracy loss, but they often focus on specific aspects of compression without a unified approach.

Recent efforts have also explored structural modifications to ViTs for improved efficiency. For instance, T2T-ViT introduces a Tokens-to-Token transformation to progressively structurize images into tokens, reducing parameter count and MACs while improving performance \[[6](http://arxiv.org/abs/2101.11986)\]. Similarly, CvT integrates convolutions into ViTs to leverage the strengths of both architectures, achieving state-of-the-art performance with fewer parameters \[[13](http://arxiv.org/abs/2103.15808)\]. These approaches demonstrate the potential of hybrid architectures in balancing accuracy and efficiency.

Early ViT models applied attention mechanisms to sequences of image patches, demonstrating strong performance on image classification tasks while requiring fewer computational resources than CNNs \[[4](http://arxiv.org/abs/2010.11929)\]. However, challenges remained in handling the high complexity of natural images, where patch granularity was insufficient for capturing features across different scales and locations \[[7](http://arxiv.org/abs/2103.00112)\]. Additionally, the Swin Transformer introduced a hierarchical design with shifted windowing, allowing efficient modeling of visual entities at various scales while maintaining linear computational complexity \[[17](http://arxiv.org/abs/2103.14030)\]. These advancements highlight the evolving capabilities of ViT models, demonstrating their potential as general-purpose vision backbones with competitive performance across diverse tasks \[[17](http://arxiv.org/abs/2103.14030)\].

These hybrid models demonstrate that combining CNNs and ViTs can yield significant improvements in efficiency and performance across a wide range of computer vision applications.
