## Related Works

Recent advancements in Text-to-Speech (TTS) systems have significantly enhanced speech naturalness, but challenges remain in achieving precise prosody control and mispronunciation correction \[[25](http://arxiv.org/abs/2206.14643)\]. Traditional approaches for prosody manipulation often rely on specialized modules or additional training, which limits their ability to perform post-hoc adjustments \[[25](http://arxiv.org/abs/2206.14643)\]. Similarly, conventional mispronunciation correction methods depend on grapheme-to-phoneme dictionaries, which are impractical in low-resource settings \[[25](http://arxiv.org/abs/2206.14643)\]. This section reviews related works that address these challenges, focusing on prosody control, mispronunciation correction, and model-agnostic post-hoc editing techniques.

Prosody control in TTS has been extensively studied, with several models introducing methods to adjust pitch, duration, and energy \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/1811.02122)\]. For instance, FastSpeech 2 introduces conditional inputs such as pitch, energy, and duration to improve the one-to-many mapping problem in TTS \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/2006.04558)\]. Fastpitch, a fully-parallel TTS model, conditions on fundamental frequency contours to generate more expressive speech \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/2006.06873)\]. These models demonstrate that incorporating prosody features can enhance the expressiveness and naturalness of synthesized speech. However, they often require careful training and may not support post-hoc adjustments \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/2006.06873)\].

Mispronunciation correction in TTS typically relies on grapheme-to-phoneme (G2P) rules, which are limited in low-resource scenarios \[[25](http://arxiv.org/abs/2206.14643)\]. Some works have explored using neural networks to predict phonemes from text, but these methods often lack the flexibility to correct mispronunciations during inference \[[25](http://arxiv.org/abs/2206.14643)\]. Recent advances in controllable TTS have shown that incorporating prosody features can improve pronunciation accuracy \[[16](http://arxiv.org/abs/2008.03388)\]. For example, the Controllable Neural Prosody Synthesis model allows users to input prosody constraints for specific time frames and generates the remaining speech from text and contextual prosody \[[16](http://arxiv.org/abs/2008.03388)\]. This approach enables more flexible and accurate pronunciation correction.

Model-agnostic post-hoc editing techniques have also gained attention. For example, the Hierarchical Prosody Modeling and Control in Non-Autoregressive Parallel Neural TTS model introduces a latent prosody space that allows for fine-grained control over speaking style and expressiveness \[[5](http://arxiv.org/abs/2110.02952)\]. This model is trained hierarchically on both coarse and fine-grained acoustic features, enabling sentence-wise control over prosody \[[5](http://arxiv.org/abs/2110.02952)\]. Similarly, the Hierarchical Generative Modeling for Controllable Speech Synthesis paper proposes a hierarchical latent variable model that enables disentangled control over attributes such as speaking style and accent \[[10](http://arxiv.org/abs/1810.07217)\].

### <a id='category-low-resource-scenario-challenges'></a>Low-Resource Scenario Challenges: 4 papers found.
In the context of low-resource scenario challenges, recent advancements in text-to-speech (TTS) systems have focused on improving performance with limited data. For instance, Deep Voice 3 demonstrates a fully-convolutional attention-based TTS system that achieves state-of-the-art naturalness while training ten times faster than previous models \[[23](http://arxiv.org/abs/1710.07654)\]. This system is trained on over eight hundred hours of audio from more than two thousand speakers, highlighting its scalability \[[23](http://arxiv.org/abs/1710.07654)\]. Similarly, the LibriTTS corpus, designed for TTS, provides 585 hours of speech data at 24kHz from 2,456 speakers, addressing limitations of earlier corpora like LibriSpeech \[[27](http://arxiv.org/abs/1904.02882)\]. Additionally, techniques such as speaker embedding augmentation enable multi-speaker TTS with high-quality synthesis using minimal data per speaker, as demonstrated by Deep Voice 2 and Tacotron improvements \[[26](http://arxiv.org/abs/1705.08947)\]. These developments underscore the importance of efficient training, scalable data, and robust performance in low-resource settings.
