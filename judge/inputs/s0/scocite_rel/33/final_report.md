## Related Works

Rehearsal-based methods have emerged as a prominent approach to address catastrophic forgetting in continual learning (CL) by storing and training on a subset of past data alongside new data in current tasks \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/1612.00796)\]. This strategy has been widely adopted due to its effectiveness in mitigating forgetting, particularly in scenarios where data is not available for replay \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/1705.08690)\]. However, the optimality of concurrent rehearsal remains an open question. Recent work has explored alternative strategies, such as sequential rehearsal, inspired by human learning, where revisiting tasks helps mitigate forgetting \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/1811.11682)\]. 

Theoretical analyses of rehearsal-based CL in overparameterized linear models have shown that sequential rehearsal can outperform concurrent rehearsal when tasks are less similar \[[4](http://arxiv.org/abs/1902.10486)\]. This finding aligns with empirical studies that demonstrate the benefits of selective experience replay, where experiences are stored in a long-term memory and selectively retrieved for replay \[[5](http://arxiv.org/abs/1801.01423)\]. For instance, distribution matching has been shown to successfully prevent catastrophic forgetting, and is consistently the best approach on all domains tested \[[6](http://arxiv.org/abs/1606.09282)\]. Similarly, methods like REMIND, which use compressed representations for replay, have demonstrated superior performance in preventing forgetting while maintaining computational efficiency \[[7](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/90e04f3ae23ca7df5f59b11453341e3db943b6f4)\].

The concept of hybrid rehearsal, which combines concurrent and sequential strategies, has also been explored. This approach trains similar tasks concurrently and revisits dissimilar tasks sequentially, aiming to balance the benefits of both strategies \[[8](http://arxiv.org/abs/1909.08383)\]. The hybrid approach has been shown to outperform standard concurrent rehearsal in both theoretical analysis and empirical experiments with deep neural networks \[[9](http://arxiv.org/abs/1904.07734)\]. This aligns with findings that selective experience replay, when suitable selection algorithms are employed, can prevent catastrophic forgetting \[[10](http://arxiv.org/abs/1708.02072)\].

Several studies have also investigated the role of memory mechanisms in continual learning. For example, the Gradient Episodic Memory (GEM) method has been proposed to alleviate forgetting while allowing beneficial transfer of knowledge to previous tasks \[[11](http://arxiv.org/abs/1312.6211)\]. Similarly, the Learning without Forgetting method enables the addition of new capabilities to a Convolutional Neural Network (CNN) without requiring access to the training data for its existing capabilities \[[12](http://arxiv.org/abs/1706.08840)\]. These approaches highlight the importance of memory mechanisms in mitigating catastrophic forgetting.

In addition, the use of generative models for replay has been explored, such as Deep Generative Replay, which leverages a dual model architecture to sample and interleave training data for previous tasks \[[13](http://arxiv.org/abs/1810.11910)\]. ### <a id='category-catastrophic-forgetting-mitigation'></a>Catastrophic Forgetting Mitigation: 24 papers found.
The category "Catastrophic Forgetting Mitigation" encompasses a wide range of approaches aimed at enabling neural networks to learn sequentially without forgetting previously acquired knowledge. A key challenge in continual learning is catastrophic forgetting, where training on new tasks degrades performance on old ones \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/1612.00796)\]. Various strategies have been proposed to address this issue, including regularization-based methods such as Elastic Weight Consolidation (EWC), which protects important weights for previous tasks \[[7](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/90e04f3ae23ca7df5f59b11453341e3db943b6f4)\]. Experience replay, where past data is stored and retrained alongside new data, has also shown promise, with methods like REMIND using compressed representations to improve efficiency \[[23](http://arxiv.org/abs/1910.02509)\]. Task-based hard attention mechanisms have been introduced to preserve previous tasks' information while allowing current task learning \[[5](http://arxiv.org/abs/1801.01423)\]. Additionally, methods like Learning without Forgetting enable networks to retain original capabilities when new tasks are added without access to old data \[[6](http://arxiv.org/abs/1606.09282)\]. Theoretical analyses have further explored the effectiveness of different rehearsal strategies, such as concurrent versus sequential rehearsal, with sequential rehearsal showing advantages when tasks are less similar \[[18](http://arxiv.org/abs/1906.00695)\]. These approaches highlight the ongoing efforts to develop robust and efficient mechanisms for continual learning, with a focus on balancing stability and plasticity in neural networks \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/1612.00796)\].

### <a id='category-selectivity-in-experience-replay'></a>Selectivity in Experience Replay: 3 papers found.
In the context of selectivity in experience replay, several approaches have been proposed to enhance the effectiveness of replay mechanisms in continual learning. These methods aim to address the issue of catastrophic forgetting by selectively storing and sampling experiences that are most critical for maintaining performance across tasks. For instance, one approach focuses on retrieving samples that are most interfered with during parameter updates, thereby reducing forgetting and improving performance \[[15](http://arxiv.org/abs/1908.04742)\]. Another study explores strategies for selecting experiences based on factors such as surprise, reward, or coverage of the state space, with distribution matching showing consistent success in preventing catastrophic forgetting \[[20](http://arxiv.org/abs/1802.10269)\]. Additionally, a method has been introduced that formulates sample selection as a constraint reduction problem, emphasizing the diversity of samples in the replay buffer to better approximate the feasible region of the original constraints \[[24](http://arxiv.org/abs/1903.08671)\]. These works collectively highlight the importance of selective experience replay in improving continual learning outcomes, offering various strategies to optimize the selection process.
