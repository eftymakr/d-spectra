## Related Works

Modern databases often adopt weak isolation levels to achieve high availability and performance, sacrificing some consistency guarantees for scalability \[[12](http://arxiv.org/abs/1302.0309)\]. This trade-off has led to the emergence of isolation bugs, where databases fail to meet their promised isolation guarantees \[[2](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/cbe191bda20907994d45bc835d20446968370add)\]. Black-box testing has become a prominent technique for detecting these bugs by verifying whether transaction histories adhere to prescribed isolation levels \[[2](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/cbe191bda20907994d45bc835d20446968370add)\]. However, testing large-scale workloads remains challenging due to the computational complexity of existing isolation checkers \[[15](http://arxiv.org/abs/1806.08416)\].

Several works have explored the complexity of checking transactional consistency. For instance, it has been shown that consistency models like read committed, read atomic, and causal consistency are polynomial-time checkable, while prefix consistency and snapshot isolation are NP-complete in general \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/1908.04509)\]. This distinction is important because it influences the design of efficient checkers for weak isolation levels. Additionally, algorithms have been developed that are polynomial-time under certain parameter constraints, such as the number of sessions, which helps in scaling to larger workloads \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/1908.04509)\].

Efforts to improve the efficiency of isolation checking have led to the development of specialized tools. For example, Viper is a fast snapshot isolation checker that is sound, complete, and transparent to users and databases. It leverages BC-polygraphs to represent transaction dependencies and introduces heuristic pruning to accelerate checking \[[4](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/9f96a48d2bc0e77004b32960495685be6c301c63)\]. Similarly, PolySI is a black-box checker for snapshot isolation that uses generalized polygraphs and an SMT solver to detect violations efficiently, reproducing known anomalies and identifying new ones in production databases \[[7](http://arxiv.org/abs/2301.07313)\]. These tools demonstrate the feasibility of checking weak isolation levels in polynomial time, which is a significant improvement over earlier approaches \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/1908.04509)\].

Another line of research focuses on debugging anomalies caused by weak isolation levels. IsoDiff is a tool designed to help developers identify and eliminate false positives in anomaly detection by using correlation detection and timing relationship checks. It also incorporates a feedback mechanism that allows developers to refine the analysis based on their insights \[[5](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/405ae0ebf74cfbf5f2edec62e2966827dcdc9bef)\]. This approach helps balance computation time with the coverage of anomalies, making it practical for real-world applications \[[5](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/405ae0ebf74cfbf5f2edec62e2966827dcdc9bef)\].

Elle is another checker that infers isolation anomalies from experimental observations. It constructs an Adya-style dependency graph between transactions and provides concise explanations of detected anomalies. However, it lacks the efficiency and completeness of more recent tools like PolySI and Viper \[[6](http://arxiv.org/abs/2003.10554)\]. The need for efficient and complete checkers has driven the development of tools like AWDIT, which is designed to test weak isolation levels in polynomial time and is provably optimal \[[15](http://arxiv.org/abs/1806.08416)\].

### <a id='category-tools-for-detecting-isolation-anomalies'></a>Tools for Detecting Isolation Anomalies: 6 papers found.
The category "Tools for Detecting Isolation Anomalies" encompasses a range of approaches aimed at identifying violations of transactional isolation guarantees in databases. These tools are critical for ensuring correctness in systems that sacrifice strong isolation for performance and availability, as seen in modern distributed databases \[[1](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/615ff2896b52bee2cbfd64ced324106a29b73397)\]. Existing checkers, such as Elle, attempt to detect anomalies by analyzing transaction histories and inferring dependency graphs, but they often struggle with predicate operations and real-world workloads \[[6](http://arxiv.org/abs/2003.10554)\]. More recently, Emme was introduced as a white-box checker that leverages version certificate recovery and expected serialization order to efficiently validate isolation levels, offering significant speed improvements over Elle \[[10](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/04178561c9d8cd42904ed0a836e08e55b84703cf)\]. Additionally, static analysis techniques have been proposed to detect non-serializable behaviors in causally-consistent databases by combining graph-based methods with SMT solvers, enhancing precision and providing concrete counterexamples \[[13](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/322ca3c1911230a84aab81b0f5ff8c2b543c56f7)\]. Another approach focuses on bounded serializability violations under weak consistency models, using First-Order Logic (FOL) encodings and SMT solvers to automate the detection of anomalies in transactional programs \[[15](http://arxiv.org/abs/1806.08416)\]. These tools collectively highlight the ongoing efforts to improve the efficiency, accuracy, and applicability of isolation anomaly detection in both traditional and distributed database systems.
