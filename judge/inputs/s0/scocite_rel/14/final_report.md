## Related Works

Video super-resolution (VSR) has been a focal point in computer vision, with recent advances leveraging deep learning to address the challenges of temporal consistency and artifact reduction. Early approaches relied on optical flow and motion compensation to align frames, but these methods often introduced artifacts due to inaccuracies in flow estimation \[[8](http://arxiv.org/abs/1812.02898)\]. More recently, recurrent architectures have been proposed to model temporal dependencies, such as the frame-recurrent video super-resolution framework that reduces computational cost by warping only one image per step \[[7](http://arxiv.org/abs/1801.04590)\]. These methods, however, still face limitations in handling large motions and maintaining temporal consistency.

Deep learning-based VSR methods have also explored the use of convolutional neural networks (CNNs) to exploit spatio-temporal information. For instance, the Progressive Fusion Video Super-Resolution Network introduces a novel framework that effectively fuses temporal information while avoiding complex motion estimation procedures \[[1](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/755227d7ea41b1d90df31f411f9ecbc998c20d26)\]. Similarly, the Enhanced Deformable Convolutional Networks (EDVR) address large motion alignment and frame fusion challenges by using a Pyramid, Cascading, and Deformable (PCD) alignment module and a Temporal and Spatial Attention (TSA) fusion module \[[5](http://arxiv.org/abs/1905.02716)\]. These approaches demonstrate improved performance on benchmark datasets, often outperforming previous methods by significant margins.

The use of recurrent neural networks (RNNs) has also gained traction in VSR, particularly through the introduction of bidirectional recurrent convolutional networks that model long-term temporal dependencies more effectively than traditional RNNs \[[21](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/a2c50f93f6d0892918e0ff653750d198ccab48d0)\]. These models reduce computational complexity by using weight-sharing convolutional connections and capturing spatio-temporal patterns through 3D feedforward convolutions. Additionally, the Recurrent Back-Projection Network (RBPN) integrates spatial and temporal contexts from continuous video frames, offering a more refined approach to multi-frame super-resolution \[[23](http://arxiv.org/abs/1903.10128)\].

Recent work has also focused on diffusion models for video generation and super-resolution, aiming to reduce artifacts and improve temporal consistency. The Video Diffusion Models (VDMs) extend standard image diffusion architectures to handle video data, enabling joint training from image and video data to reduce gradient variance and speed up optimization \[[19](http://arxiv.org/abs/2204.03458)\]. Similarly, the Upscale-A-Video framework introduces a text-guided latent diffusion model that ensures temporal coherence through locally and globally consistent mechanisms \[[18](http://arxiv.org/abs/2312.06640)\]. These models leverage the diffusion paradigm to offer greater flexibility in texture creation and noise level adjustment, balancing fidelity and quality.

Additionally, the MuCAN network introduces temporal multi-correspondence aggregation and cross-scale nonlocal-correspondence aggregation to exploit self-similarity across frames \[[14](http://arxiv.org/abs/2007.11803)\].
