## Related Works

The rapid growth of open-source machine learning (ML) resources has significantly impacted information retrieval (IR) research, as noted in the paper’s abstract. Existing platforms like Hugging Face, while central to the ML community, lack structured representations that enable advanced querying and analysis \[[6](http://arxiv.org/abs/2405.13058)\]. This limitation hinders tasks such as tracing model evolution and recommending relevant datasets \[[6](http://arxiv.org/abs/2405.13058)\]. Several studies have explored the challenges and opportunities in managing and analyzing ML resources, particularly within the Hugging Face ecosystem.

Prior work has examined the carbon footprint of ML models hosted on Hugging Face, highlighting the need for transparent and standardized reporting practices \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2305.11164)\]. This study found that only a small proportion of models report carbon emissions, and there is a lack of consistency in how these emissions are measured and documented \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2305.11164)\]. These findings align with broader concerns about the environmental impact of large-scale ML models \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2305.11164)\]. Additionally, the study proposed classifications for carbon-efficient model development, emphasizing the importance of sustainability in the ML community \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2305.11164)\].

Another line of research has focused on the documentation of datasets on Hugging Face, revealing that while dataset cards are increasingly used, they are often incomplete or lack critical information \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/2401.13822)\]. The study found that the completeness of dataset cards correlates with the popularity of the dataset, and that certain sections, such as "Considerations for Using the Data," are underdeveloped \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/2401.13822)\]. These findings underscore the importance of improving dataset documentation to enhance reproducibility and transparency in ML research \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/2401.13822)\].

The reuse of pre-trained models (PTMs) on Hugging Face has also been extensively studied, with researchers identifying key challenges such as the lack of provenance, reproducibility, and portability \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/2303.02552)\]. These challenges hinder the effective reuse of models and highlight the need for better infrastructure and standardization in model registries \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/2303.02552)\]. Additionally, studies have shown that PTMs have a higher turnover rate compared to traditional software, indicating a more dynamic and rapidly evolving ecosystem \[[5](http://arxiv.org/abs/2406.08205)\]. This rapid evolution is further supported by the observation that model documentation and usage patterns have not kept pace with the growth of the platform \[[7](http://arxiv.org/abs/2401.13177)\].

Moreover, the social and collaborative aspects of the Hugging Face Hub have been analyzed, revealing a core-periphery structure in the community, with a small group of prolific developers and a majority of isolated contributors \[[6](http://arxiv.org/abs/2405.13058)\]. This structure has implications for the dissemination and adoption of models, as the most popular models are often developed by a small number of companies or individuals \[[6](http://arxiv.org/abs/2405.13058)\].
