## Related Works

Anomaly detection in industrial settings has garnered significant attention due to its critical role in quality control and operational efficiency. A wide range of methods have been proposed to address the challenges of detecting anomalies in images and time series data, particularly in scenarios where labeled anomaly data is scarce or unavailable \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/2011.08785)\]. Early approaches relied on handcrafted features and classical computer vision techniques, but recent advances have leveraged deep learning architectures to improve detection accuracy and localization capabilities \[[4](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/3aa681914a7da79f7d7293f51a058eefe61c8bb7)\].

Several works have focused on unsupervised anomaly detection, where models are trained solely on normal data. For instance, PatchCore \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2106.08265)\] introduces a method that uses a maximally representative memory bank of nominal patch features to achieve state-of-the-art performance on the MVTec AD benchmark, achieving an AUROC score of up to 99.6%. Similarly, CFLOW-AD \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/2107.12571)\] proposes a real-time model based on conditional normalizing flows, which is faster and smaller than prior state-of-the-art methods by a factor of 10×. PaDiM \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/2011.08785)\] further improves on this by using a probabilistic representation of the normal class through multivariate Gaussian distributions and exploiting correlations between different semantic levels of the CNN. These methods have demonstrated strong performance on benchmark datasets such as MVTec AD and STC \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/2011.08785)\].

In addition to image-based anomaly detection, there has been growing interest in time series anomaly detection for industrial control systems. For example, \[[5](http://arxiv.org/abs/1911.04831)\] proposes a sequence-to-sequence neural network approach that detects anomalies in operational data of industrial control systems (ICSs), achieving high detection rates on the SWaT dataset. Another study \[[10](http://arxiv.org/abs/1806.08110)\] explores the use of 1D convolutional networks for anomaly detection in ICSs, showing that they outperform recurrent networks in this setting due to their simplicity and efficiency. These methods are particularly relevant for real-time monitoring and fault detection in industrial environments.

Recent works have also explored the use of multimodal data for anomaly detection. For instance, \[[9](http://arxiv.org/abs/2303.00601)\] introduces a hybrid fusion approach that combines 3D point clouds and RGB images to improve detection performance. The proposed method, M3DM, uses unsupervised feature fusion and decision layer fusion to enhance the interaction between different modalities, leading to improved detection and segmentation accuracy on the MVTec-3D AD dataset.

### <a id='category-anomaly-detection-in-industrial-settings'></a>Anomaly Detection in Industrial Settings: 6 papers found.
In the realm of anomaly detection in industrial settings, recent research has focused on developing efficient and accurate methods for both image-based and time-series data. For image-based anomaly detection, PaDiM \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/2011.08785)\] introduces a one-class learning framework that leverages pretrained CNNs and multivariate Gaussian distributions to model normal patterns, achieving state-of-the-art results on MVTec AD and STC datasets. Similarly, Multi-3D-Memory (M3DM) \[[9](http://arxiv.org/abs/2303.00601)\] extends this work to multimodal settings, integrating 3D point clouds and RGB images through hybrid fusion schemes and point feature alignment, demonstrating superior performance on the MVTec-3D AD dataset. In contrast, for operational data in industrial control systems (ICSs), sequence-to-sequence neural networks have been employed to detect anomalies using only normal datasets, with notable success on the SWaT dataset \[[5](http://arxiv.org/abs/1911.04831)\]. Additionally, 1D convolutional networks have shown promise in detecting cyber attacks on ICSs, outperforming recurrent networks in terms of simplicity and speed \[[10](http://arxiv.org/abs/1806.08110)\]. Meanwhile, OmniAnomaly \[[12](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/440d248d148f7e36dad232e48f1c5c1cbc556d86)\] addresses multivariate time series anomalies by employing stochastic recurrent neural networks with techniques like planar normalizing flow and stochastic variable connections, achieving high F1-Score and interpretation accuracy across diverse industrial datasets. These approaches collectively highlight the growing trend toward more efficient, adaptable, and interpretable anomaly detection systems tailored for real-world industrial applications.

### <a id='category-supervised-vs.-unsupervised-anomaly-detection-methods'></a>Supervised vs. Unsupervised Anomaly Detection Methods: 1 papers found.
Context: 
	Document 1: [Snippet]: «Unsupervised anomaly detection and localization is crucial to the practical application when collecting and labeling sufficient anomaly data is infeasible. Most existing representation-based approaches extract normal image features with a deep convolutional neural network and characterize the corresponding distribution through non-parametric distribution estimation methods. The anomaly score is calculated by measuring the distance between the feature of the test image and the estimated distribution. However, current methods can not effectively map image features to a tractable base distribution and ignore the relationship between local and global features which are important to identify anomalies. To this end, we propose FastFlow implemented with 2D normalizing flows and use it as the probability distribution estimator. Our FastFlow can be used as a plug-in module with arbitrary deep feature extractors such as ResNet and vision transformer for unsupervised anomaly detection and localization. In training phase, FastFlow learns to transform the input visual feature into a tractable distribution and obtains the likelihood to recognize anomalies in inference phase. Extensive experimental results on the MVTec AD dataset show that FastFlow surpasses previous state-of-the-art methods in terms of accuracy and inference efficiency with various backbone networks. Our approach achieves 99.4% AUC in anomaly detection with high inference efficiency.»
