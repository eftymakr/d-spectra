## Related Works

The integration of knowledge graphs (KGs) with large language models (LLMs) to enhance reasoning capabilities has gained significant attention in recent years. While LLMs have demonstrated strong reasoning abilities, they often struggle with structured knowledge retrieval and multi-step reasoning tasks \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2306.08302)\]. KGs, on the other hand, provide structured, semantically rich representations that are well-suited for reasoning \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2306.08302)\]. However, most existing verification methods rely on unstructured text corpora, limiting their ability to effectively leverage KGs \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2306.08302)\]. 

Several studies have explored the use of KGs to improve the performance of LLMs. For instance, the QA-GNN model leverages LLMs to estimate the importance of KG nodes and performs joint reasoning over the QA context and KG \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/2104.06378)\]. This approach enhances the interpretability and accuracy of the reasoning process. Similarly, the RAG framework combines pre-trained parametric models with non-parametric memory, such as Wikipedia, to improve the factual accuracy of generated responses \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/2005.11401)\]. These approaches demonstrate the potential of integrating KGs with LLMs for knowledge-intensive tasks.

The concept of using KGs to enhance LLMs has also been explored in the context of multi-document question answering (MD-QA). The Knowledge Graph Prompting (KGP) method constructs a knowledge graph over multiple documents and uses an LLM-based graph traversal agent to gather supporting passages for MD-QA \[[6](http://arxiv.org/abs/2308.11730)\]. This approach improves the accuracy and efficiency of the reasoning process by leveraging the structured knowledge from KGs. 

Another line of research focuses on improving the zero-shot reasoning capabilities of LLMs. The Zero-shot-CoT method demonstrates that adding a simple prompt such as "Let's think step by step" significantly improves the performance of LLMs on diverse reasoning tasks \[[4](http://arxiv.org/abs/2205.11916)\]. This finding highlights the potential of LLMs to perform complex reasoning without the need for extensive training or fine-tuning. 

The challenge of hallucination in LLMs has also been addressed through retrieval-augmented generation (RAG) approaches. The LLM-Augmenter system augments LLMs with external knowledge and iteratively revises prompts to improve the factuality of responses \[[12](http://arxiv.org/abs/2302.12813)\]. This approach reduces hallucinations without sacrificing the fluency and informativeness of the responses. 

In addition, the use of KGs for commonsense reasoning has been explored. The KagNet model utilizes external commonsense knowledge graphs to perform explainable inferences \[[17](http://arxiv.org/abs/1909.02151)\]. This approach enhances the transparency and interpretability of the reasoning process

### <a id='category-retrieval-augmented-generation-approaches'></a>Retrieval-Augmented Generation Approaches: 5 papers found.
The category "Retrieval-Augmented Generation Approaches" focuses on integrating external knowledge sources with pre-trained language models to enhance their reasoning and factual accuracy, particularly for knowledge-intensive tasks. Retrieval-augmented generation (RAG) models combine parametric memory, such as pre-trained sequence-to-sequence models, with non-parametric memory, like dense vector indexes of external databases, to improve performance \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/2005.11401)\]. This approach allows models to access up-to-date and domain-specific information, mitigating issues such as hallucinations and outdated knowledge \[[5](http://arxiv.org/abs/2312.10997)\]. Research has shown that RAG models generate more specific, diverse, and factual language compared to parametric-only models, especially in open-domain question-answering tasks \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/2005.11401)\]. Additionally, retrieval augmentation significantly improves performance on less popular factual knowledge, as demonstrated through large-scale knowledge probing experiments on datasets like PopQA and EntityQuestions \[[9](http://arxiv.org/abs/2212.10511)\]. The effectiveness of RAG is further supported by systems like LLM-Augmenter, which integrates external knowledge and iteratively refines prompts to enhance factuality without compromising fluency \[[12](http://arxiv.org/abs/2302.12813)\]. These findings highlight the importance of retrieval mechanisms in overcoming the limitations of purely parametric models, particularly in tasks requiring structured reasoning and access to external knowledge \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/2005.11401)\]\[[5](http://arxiv.org/abs/2312.10997)\]\[[9](http://arxiv.org/abs/2212.10511)\].
