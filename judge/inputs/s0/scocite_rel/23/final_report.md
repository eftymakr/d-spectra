## Related Works

Automatic calibration of heterogeneous sensors such as radar and camera is a critical step in autonomous systems to ensure accurate perception and robust data fusion. Several approaches have been proposed in recent years to address the challenges of sensor calibration, particularly in the context of radar-camera systems. Early methods often relied on dedicated calibration targets, which limited their applicability in real-world scenarios \[[5](http://arxiv.org/abs/1904.08743)\]. More recent work has focused on targetless calibration techniques, which are essential for practical deployment in dynamic environments.

A notable approach is the automatic targetless extrinsic calibration of multiple 3D LiDAR and radar sensors, which requires only a short driving distance and does not depend on overlapping fields of view \[[2](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/0e88fc24ae7694d0b1a2fe78322936fc568bc7d2)\]. This method estimates the 6-DoF pose of each sensor by minimizing point-to-plane distances between scans and using radial velocity errors for radar sensors. Another method, proposed for radar-camera calibration without dedicated targets, employs a coarse and fine convolutional neural network to achieve precise sensor registration \[[5](http://arxiv.org/abs/1904.08743)\]. This approach is particularly useful in scenarios where manual calibration is impractical.

In addition to targetless calibration, some methods focus on improving the robustness of the calibration process against environmental uncertainties. A continuous-time approach for 3D radar-to-camera extrinsic calibration utilizes radar velocity measurements and does not require specialized radar retroreflectors \[[6](http://arxiv.org/abs/2103.07505)\]. This method is especially relevant for autonomous vehicles operating in adverse weather conditions where traditional calibration targets may be unavailable.

The problem of sparse radar data and height uncertainty is also addressed in several works. A method that combines radar and camera data using a dual-perspective representation, which includes both frontal and bird's-eye views, has been proposed to reduce the impact of height uncertainty \[[1](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/0955326c67a947b5fac5fbae7238d04fc9adc251)\]. This approach uses a selective fusion mechanism to integrate reliable features from both perspectives. Additionally, a multi-modal cross-attention mechanism is employed to find location correspondences through cross-modal matching \[[1](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/0955326c67a947b5fac5fbae7238d04fc9adc251)\].

For online calibration, a method based on moving object tracking has been proposed, which uses detection and tracking of moving objects to perform continuous calibration without requiring ego-motion estimation \[[7](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/c80265fa719eb85c8a56a6b36c74b99b793f39b2)\]. This approach is particularly useful for long-term reliability in autonomous systems. Another method leverages Gaussian processes for spatiotemporal calibration based on moving target tracking, achieving high accuracy in time delay estimation \[[9](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/90d807f4e4999443ce9910ba451675f3b9ce4ba0)\].

In the context of sensor fusion, several studies have explored the integration of radar and camera data for object detection and localization. A feature-level fusion framework, RVDet, has been proposed to align radar and camera features in the bird's-eye view domain, improving the accuracy of object detection

Additionally, we carry out identifiability analysis based on the Fisher Information Matrix to show minimal requirements on the dataset and to verify the method through simulations. We test the calibration method on a variety of sensor configurations and address the problem of radar vertical misalignment. In the end, we show via extensive experiment analysis that the proposed method is able to reliably estimate all the six parameters of the extrinsic calibration.»

Recent advancements in radar-camera calibration have focused on improving accuracy and robustness, particularly in challenging environments where traditional calibration methods fall short. Several studies have explored automatic calibration techniques that eliminate the need for dedicated calibration targets, leveraging data-driven approaches and deep learning models \[[2](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/0e88fc24ae7694d0b1a2fe78322936fc568bc7d2)\]. For instance, one approach employs a coarse and fine convolutional neural network to achieve precise and robust sensor registration, demonstrating generalization across different sensor alignments \[[5](http://arxiv.org/abs/1904.08743)\]. Another method introduces a continuous-time 3D radar-to-camera extrinsic calibration algorithm that utilizes radar velocity measurements and does not require specialized retroreflectors, enhancing practical applicability \[[6](http://arxiv.org/abs/2103.07505)\]. Additionally, researchers have addressed the calibration of low-cost 3D millimeter-wave radars, which are increasingly replacing 2D counterparts, by developing algorithms that rely on radar velocity and avoid environmental constraints \[[3](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/4d4dbbcf27d991aeab43dafb82a105bb5512b799)\]. The importance of accurate calibration for sensor fusion is further emphasized by studies that highlight the necessity of rigid-body transforms for reliable data association and perception \[[12](http://arxiv.org/abs/2103.07825)\]. Some works have also explored cross-supervised learning strategies, where camera-radar fused algorithms are used to label radar data, enabling the creation of large-scale datasets for training deep learning models \[[17](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/29e931b751ac8cc3e7050aef007709ffbd566059)\]. These efforts collectively underscore the growing interest in automated and robust calibration methods that can operate in real-world conditions with minimal human intervention.

Contrary to currently common online approach of using ego-motion estimation, we propose an online calibration method based on detection and tracking of moving objects. Our motivation comes from the practical perspective that many perception sensors of an autonomous system are part of the pipeline for detection and tracking of moving objects. Although much progress has been made, we still observe two problems: First, the spatial alignment among multi-modal data is intractable when involving multiple radar and camera sensors. Second, most existing works are based on object-level fusion, which inevitably has information loss leading to a performance degradation. To this end, we propose a feature-level fusion detection framework based on multiple radars and cameras, termed as the RVDet. We first establish an occupancy grid map by using 4 corner radars and extract radar features in the bird's eye view(BEV). Meantime, the image features of 4 fish-eye cameras are obtained using a pretraining vision detection model. Then, an adaptive projection network is employed to transform all the 4 image features to the BEV domain and integrate them to a dense spatial feature map aligned with the radar feature. Last, the carefully aligned multi-modal feature maps are jointly sent to a deep fusion network to predict final fused detection results. Experiments show that both object detection and positioning performance achieve significant gains by the proposed method in a custom dataset.»

	Document 1: [Snippet]: «Robust and reliable perception of autonomous systems often relies on fusion of heterogeneous sensors, which poses great challenges for multisensor calibration. In this article, we propose a method for multisensor calibration based on Gaussian processes (GPs) estimated moving target trajectories, resulting with spatiotemporal calibration. Unlike competing approaches, the proposed method is characterized by the following: first, joint multisensor on-manifold spatiotemporal optimization framework, second, batch state estimation and interpolation using GPs, and, third, computational efficiency with O(n) complexity. It only requires that all sensors can track the same target. The method is validated in simulation and real-world experiments on the following five different multisensor setups: first, hardware triggered stereo camera, second, camera and motion capture system, third, camera and automotive radar, fourth, camera and rotating 3-D lidar, and, fifth, camera, 3-D lidar, and the motion capture system. The method estimates time delays with the accuracy up to a fraction of the fastest sensor sampling time, outperforming a state-of-the-art ego-motion method.
