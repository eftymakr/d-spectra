## Related Works

Memory bandwidth regulation and cache partitioning have been widely studied for achieving predictable timing in real-time computing systems \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2310.02959)\]. These techniques are often combined with partitioned scheduling to manage task execution times, which are highly sensitive to allocated resources \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/1809.05921)\]. Prior work has focused on optimizing the co-allocation of tasks and resources to cores, as task execution times strongly depend on available resources \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/2410.14003)\]. For example, \[[4](http://arxiv.org/abs/2302.11236)\] presents a hybrid multi-layer design space exploration technique to co-optimize cache partitioning and task scheduling, improving real-time schedulability by up to 233.6% for highly cache-sensitive tasks. This work explores the interplay between cache partitioning and schedulability by interleaving three optimization layers, including a breadth-first search for cache partitioning and a first-fit heuristic for task allocation \[[4](http://arxiv.org/abs/2302.11236)\]. Additionally, \[[5](http://arxiv.org/abs/1210.4053)\] introduces a joint cache partition and job assignment problem, proposing a constant approximation algorithm and showing that the problem is harder than the ordered unrelated machines scheduling problem \[[5](http://arxiv.org/abs/1210.4053)\].

Recent research has also addressed the challenge of managing shared resources such as cache and memory bandwidth in real-time systems. \[[6](http://arxiv.org/abs/2501.08484)\] proposes a co-design of resource allocation and deadline decomposition using generative profiling, enabling more accurate execution models for tasks and improving schedulability. This work emphasizes the importance of considering the relationship between multicore resources and execution time in task modeling \[[6](http://arxiv.org/abs/2501.08484)\]. Similarly, \[[7](http://arxiv.org/abs/2102.11528)\] introduces a coordinated management approach for cache partitioning, bandwidth partitioning, and prefetch throttling, showing that managing these resources together can significantly improve performance during workload consolidation \[[7](http://arxiv.org/abs/2102.11528)\]. The proposed approach, CBP, improves performance by 11% compared to state-of-the-art techniques and by 50% compared to a baseline without coordinated resource management \[[7](http://arxiv.org/abs/2102.11528)\].

In addition to resource management, several studies have explored scheduling strategies for real-time systems. \[[8](http://arxiv.org/abs/1312.4509)\] presents a cache-aware static scheduling approach for hard real-time multicore systems, minimizing L1 data cache misses using communication affinities \[[8](http://arxiv.org/abs/1312.4509)\]. \[[9](http://arxiv.org/abs/1809.02892)\] investigates the synchronization challenges in multiprocessor systems and proposes a dependency-graph approach for frame-based real-time tasks, achieving speedup factors between 2 and 3 under semi-partitioned scheduling \[[9](http://arxiv.org/abs/1809.02892)\]. Furthermore, \[[10](http://arxiv.org/abs/1607.07763)\] introduces energy-efficient real-time scheduling for heterogeneous multiprocessors, proposing three mathematical optimization formulations that solve the scheduling problem for real-time tasksets with hard constraints \[[10](http://arxiv.org/abs/1607.07763)\].

[Snippet]: «Cache partitioning techniques have been successfully adopted to mitigate interference among concurrently executing real-time tasks on multi-core processors. Considering that the execution time of a cache-sensitive task strongly depends on the cache available for it to use, co-optimizing cache partitioning and task allocation improves the system's schedulability. In this paper, we propose a hybrid multi-layer design space exploration technique to solve this multi-resource management problem. We explore the interplay between cache partitioning and schedulability by systematically interleaving three optimization layers, viz., (i) in the outer layer, we perform a breadth-first search combined with proactive pruning for cache partitioning; (ii) in the middle layer, we exploit a first-fit heuristic for allocating tasks to cores; and (iii) in the inner layer, we use the well-known recurrence relation for the schedulability analysis of non-preemptive fixed-priority (NP-FP) tasks in a uniprocessor setting. Although our focus is on NP-FP scheduling, we evaluate the flexibility of our framework in supporting different scheduling policies (NP-EDF, P-EDF) by plugging in appropriate analysis methods in the inner layer. Experiments show that, compared to the state-of-the-art techniques, the proposed framework can improve the real-time schedulability of NP-FP task sets by an average of 15.2% with a maximum improvement of 233.6% (when tasks are highly cache-sensitive) and a minimum of 1.6% (when cache sensitivity is low). For such task sets, we found that clustering similar-period (or mutually compatible) tasks often leads to higher schedulability (on average 7.6 %) than clustering by cache sensitivity. In our evaluation, the framework also achieves good results for preemptive and dynamic-priority scheduling policies.»

[Snippet]: «Multicore shared cache processors pose a challenge for designers of embedded systems who try to achieve minimal and predictable execution time of workloads consisting of several jobs. To address this challenge the cache is statically partitioned among the cores and the jobs are assigned to the cores so as to minimize the makespan. Several heuristic algorithms have been proposed that jointly decide how to partition the cache among the cores and assign the jobs. We initiate a theoretical study of this problem which we call the joint cache partition and job assignment problem. By a careful analysis of the possible cache partitions we obtain a constant approximation algorithm for this problem. For some practical special cases we obtain a 2-approximation algorithm, and show how to improve the approximation factor even further by allowing the algorithm to use additional cache. We also study possible improvements that can be obtained by allowing dynamic cache partitions and dynamic job assignments. We define a natural restriction of the well known scheduling problem on unrelated machines in which machines are ordered by "strength". We call this restriction the ordered unrelated machines scheduling problem. We show that our joint cache partition and job assignment problem is harder than this scheduling problem. The ordered unrelated machines scheduling problem is of independent interest and we give a polynomial time algorithm for certain natural workloads.»
