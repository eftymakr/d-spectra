## Related Works

Collaborative filtering (CF) has been a cornerstone in recommendation systems, with recent advancements leveraging graph-based models to better capture user-item interactions \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2401.12590)\]. Early CF methods, such as matrix factorization, relied on explicit user-item ratings, while later approaches incorporated deep learning and graph neural networks (GNNs) to model complex relationships \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/2007.03383)\]. Graph Convolutional Networks (GCNs) have emerged as a powerful tool for CF, enabling the aggregation of neighborhood information to generate more informative embeddings \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/2001.00267)\]. However, these models often suffer from issues such as over-smoothing and limited expressiveness due to their linear or fixed-frequency filtering mechanisms \[[4](http://arxiv.org/abs/1905.08108)\].

Several works have attempted to address these limitations. For instance, NGCF integrates user-item interactions directly into the embedding process by propagating embeddings on the bipartite graph, thereby capturing high-order connectivity \[[5](http://arxiv.org/abs/2311.03316)\]. Similarly, Multi-GCCF explicitly incorporates multiple graphs (user-user, item-item, and user-item) to model richer interaction patterns \[[6](http://arxiv.org/abs/2307.04384)\]. RGCF improves upon traditional GCN-based models by introducing a refined graph convolution structure and a fine-tuned normalization to better encode popularity features \[[7](http://arxiv.org/abs/2406.13996)\]. These methods, however, still rely on linear or fixed-frequency filters, which may not fully exploit the spectrum of graph signals \[[8](http://arxiv.org/abs/2001.10167)\].

To overcome these limitations, researchers have explored more flexible graph filters. PolyCF proposes a polynomial-based graph signal filter that captures spectral features across multiple eigenspaces, enabling the recovery of missing interactions \[[9](http://arxiv.org/abs/1911.10699)\]. The work in \[[10](http://arxiv.org/abs/2203.14748)\] further extends this idea by designing universal IIR graph filters using Butterworth, Chebyshev, and elliptic functions, achieving more accurate frequency responses than traditional polynomial-based filters \[[11](http://arxiv.org/abs/2107.04755)\]. AutoGCN introduces an automatic graph convolutional filter that dynamically adjusts its bandwidth, capturing both low and high-frequency components of graph signals \[[12](http://arxiv.org/abs/2004.14734)\]. These methods highlight the importance of spectral filtering in improving the expressiveness and robustness of CF models.

Despite these advancements, many existing models still face challenges in handling large-scale and sparse data. For example, the work in \[[13](http://arxiv.org/abs/2501.16888)\] introduces a residual network structure to alleviate over-smoothing in GCN-based CF models, while \[[14](http://arxiv.org/abs/2404.05962)\] proposes a linear residual GCN to improve scalability and efficiency. Additionally, \[[15](http://arxiv.org/abs/2412.01376)\] investigates the use of graph filters with adaptive propagation, allowing each node to independently decide the number of communication steps, thereby improving model performance \[[16](http://arxiv.org/abs/2412.01378)\]. These approaches demonstrate the potential of adaptive and flexible graph filtering in enhancing CF

Graph Neural Networks (GNNs) have become a cornerstone in collaborative filtering (CF) for recommendation systems, offering advanced methods to model complex user-item interactions \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2401.12590)\]. Traditional approaches, such as matrix factorization, often fail to fully utilize the rich structure of user-item graphs, leading to suboptimal embeddings that do not capture high-order connectivity or nuanced preference patterns \[[4](http://arxiv.org/abs/1905.08108)\]. To address these limitations, several studies have introduced novel GNN-based frameworks. For instance, PolyCF reframes CF as a graph signal processing problem, leveraging polynomial graph filters to capture spectral features across multiple eigenspaces and approximate optimal polynomial responses for missing interaction recovery \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2401.12590)\]. Multi-Graph Convolution Collaborative Filtering (Multi-GCCF) further enhances modeling by incorporating multiple graphs—user-user, item-item, and user-item—to better capture proximal and high-order information \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/2001.00267)\]. NGCF explicitly integrates user-item graph structures into the embedding process, enabling explicit modeling of high-order connectivity and collaborative signals \[[4](http://arxiv.org/abs/1905.08108)\]. Additionally, methods like Simple Contrastive Collaborative Filtering (SCCF) combine contrastive learning with graph convolution to enhance high-order connectivity modeling while avoiding oversmoothing \[[7](http://arxiv.org/abs/2406.13996)\]. The work on Neural Causal Graph Collaborative Filtering (NCGCF) integrates causal modeling with GNNs to capture complex causal relations, improving recommendation accuracy \[[6](http://arxiv.org/abs/2307.04384)\]. These advancements highlight the growing trend of leveraging GNNs to enhance CF by better modeling collaborative signals, high-order interactions, and structural properties of user-item graphs.
