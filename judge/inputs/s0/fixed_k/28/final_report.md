## Related Works

Active learning (AL) has been widely studied for reducing annotation costs by selecting the most informative samples for labeling \[[18](http://arxiv.org/abs/2302.08893)\]. However, performing AL in isolation can lead to suboptimal results due to limited data diversity and constrained annotation budgets \[[18](http://arxiv.org/abs/2302.08893)\]. Federated Active Learning (FAL) extends AL to distributed settings, enabling collaborative data selection and model training while preserving data privacy \[[18](http://arxiv.org/abs/2302.08893)\]. Despite these advances, existing FAL methods often overlook the heterogeneity of data distribution across clients and the resulting fluctuations in global and local model parameters, which can degrade model accuracy \[[18](http://arxiv.org/abs/2302.08893)\].

### <a id='category-federated-learning-(fl)-challenges'></a>Federated Learning (FL) Challenges: 9 papers found.
Federated Learning (FL) faces significant challenges due to data and model heterogeneity, which hinder convergence and accuracy. Existing approaches often address either data heterogeneity, such as non-i.i.d. data distributions \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2504.01921)\], or model heterogeneity, such as varying computational capabilities across clients \[[12](http://arxiv.org/abs/2309.14675)\], but few tackle both simultaneously. For instance, data heterogeneity leads to inconsistent local model updates, while model heterogeneity introduces delays and staleness in asynchronous settings \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/2206.10032)\]. Recent works have attempted to mitigate these issues through client selection strategies \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2504.01921)\], communication compression \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/2206.10032)\], and adaptive scheduling \[[12](http://arxiv.org/abs/2309.14675)\], yet they often fail to handle real-world scenarios where both data and model heterogeneity coexist. Additionally, the integration of new clients into FL systems introduces further complexity, as their diverse data distributions and computational capabilities can destabilize training \[[13](http://arxiv.org/abs/2408.09545)\]. To address these challenges, novel methods have been proposed, such as FedCompass, which introduces a computing power-aware scheduler to balance workloads and reduce staleness \[[12](http://arxiv.org/abs/2309.14675)\], and MDH-FL, which simultaneously tackles data and model heterogeneity through knowledge distillation and symmetric loss functions \[[19](http://arxiv.org/abs/2308.00155)\]. These approaches highlight the growing need for robust FL frameworks that can adapt to heterogeneous environments while maintaining model performance and efficiency.
