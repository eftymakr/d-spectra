## Related Works

Representation learning is a cornerstone of modern recommender systems, enabling efficient user-item matching through vectorized embeddings \[[10](http://arxiv.org/abs/1502.04163)\]. However, the computational and memory costs of these embeddings can be prohibitive for real-time applications. To address this, binarization techniques have emerged as a promising approach, converting dense embeddings into compact binary representations \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/1803.09065)\]. This strategy not only reduces memory usage but also accelerates inference, making it suitable for resource-constrained environments \[[16](http://arxiv.org/abs/1611.01600)\]. 

Existing binarization methods primarily focus on numerical quantization, often at the expense of information retention \[[16](http://arxiv.org/abs/1611.01600)\]. This leads to performance degradation, particularly in collaborative filtering tasks where high-order interactions between users and items are crucial \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/1906.01829)\]. To mitigate this, recent works have explored graph-based approaches that integrate user-item interactions into the embedding process \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/1905.08108)\]. For example, Neural Graph Collaborative Filtering (NGCF) explicitly models high-order connectivity by propagating embeddings through a bipartite graph, enhancing the collaborative signal \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/1905.08108)\]. Similarly, Multi-Graph Convolution Collaborative Filtering (Multi-GCCF) integrates user-user and item-item graphs alongside the user-item graph to capture more comprehensive relational patterns \[[4](http://arxiv.org/abs/2001.00267)\]. These methods demonstrate that leveraging graph structures can significantly improve the quality of embeddings \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/1905.08108)\]\[[4](http://arxiv.org/abs/2001.00267)\].

Another line of research focuses on the efficiency of recommendation models. Binarized Collaborative Filtering with Distilling Graph Convolutional Networks (Binarized GCN) introduces a framework that distills ranking information from a GCN into a binarized model, improving efficiency while preserving performance \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/1906.01829)\]. This approach converts binary constrained optimization into a continuous problem, allowing for easier optimization using standard solvers \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/1906.01829)\]. Similarly, Graph-less Collaborative Filtering (SimRec) addresses the limitations of graph neural networks (GNNs), such as over-smoothing and poor scalability, by combining knowledge distillation and contrastive learning \[[8](http://arxiv.org/abs/2303.08537)\]. These techniques highlight the potential of hybrid approaches that balance model efficiency with recommendation accuracy \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/1906.01829)\]\[[8](http://arxiv.org/abs/2303.08537)\].

In addition to efficiency, fairness in recommendation systems has gained attention. ### <a id='category-graph-based-collaborative-filtering'></a>Graph-Based Collaborative Filtering: 8 papers found.
Graph-based collaborative filtering has emerged as a powerful approach in recommender systems, leveraging user-item interaction data to learn rich representations. Recent works focus on integrating graph structures into embedding learning to capture high-order connectivity and collaborative signals explicitly \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/1905.08108)\]. For instance, NGCF introduces a framework that propagates embeddings on a bipartite user-item graph, enabling the modeling of complex relationships \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/1905.08108)\]. Similarly, Multi-GCCF extends this idea by incorporating multiple graphs, including user-user and item-item interactions, to enhance the embedding process \[[4](http://arxiv.org/abs/2001.00267)\]. These models address the limitations of traditional methods that often neglect collaborative signals during embedding generation \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/1905.08108)\]. Additionally, efforts have been made to improve efficiency, such as through binarized collaborative filtering, where binary representations are used to reduce computational overhead \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/1906.01829)\]. However, binary codes can lead to information loss during training, prompting the development of continuous optimization frameworks that maintain model performance \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/1906.01829)\]. Furthermore, the use of graph neural networks (GNNs) has shown promise in capturing latent patterns, though challenges like over-smoothing and scalability remain \[[8](http://arxiv.org/abs/2303.08537)\]. To address these, SimRec integrates knowledge distillation and contrastive learning to enhance representation quality while maintaining efficiency \[[8](http://arxiv.org/abs/2303.08537)\]. These advancements collectively highlight the evolving landscape of graph-based collaborative filtering, emphasizing the importance of graph structure integration, efficient representation learning, and robust handling of implicit feedback \[[6](http://arxiv.org/abs/1809.06120)\]\[[9](http://arxiv.org/abs/1803.03502)\].

### <a id='category-information-loss-mitigation'></a>Information Loss Mitigation: 2 papers found.
Context: 
	Document 1: [Category]: «Information Loss Mitigation»
[Snippet]: «An approach to distributed machine learning is to train models on local datasets and aggregate these models into a single, stronger model. A popular instance of this form of parallelization is federated learning, where the nodes periodically send their local models to a coordinator that aggregates them and redistributes the aggregation back to continue training with it. The most frequently used form of aggregation is averaging the model parameters, e.g., the weights of a neural network. However, due to the non-convexity of the loss surface of neural networks, averaging can lead to detrimental effects and it remains an open question under which conditions averaging is beneficial. In this paper, we study this problem from the perspective of information theory: We measure the mutual information between representation and inputs as well as representation and labels in local models and compare it to the respective information contained in the representation of the averaged model. Our empirical results confirm previous observations about the practical usefulness of averaging for neural networks, even if local dataset distributions vary strongly. Furthermore, we obtain more insights about the impact of the aggregation frequency on the information flow and thus on the success of distributed learning. These insights will be helpful both in improving the current synchronization process and in further understanding the effects of model aggregation.»
