## Related Works

3D human pose estimation has attracted significant research interest due to its applications in human-computer interaction, robotics, and virtual reality. Most existing methods rely on either convolutional neural networks (CNNs) \[[27](http://arxiv.org/abs/1803.04775)\] or transformer architectures \[[22](http://arxiv.org/abs/2103.10455)\] to model spatial and temporal dependencies. However, these approaches often struggle with capturing long-range dependencies between body parts, which are crucial for accurate 3D pose estimation \[[29](http://arxiv.org/abs/1612.06524)\]. 

A number of works have explored graph-based models to represent the human skeleton, leveraging the inherent structure of the body. For instance, \[[25](http://arxiv.org/abs/2111.11927)\] proposed a hierarchical graph network to better capture the complex relationships between joints by using a denser graph topology. This approach helps mitigate the 2D-to-3D ambiguity problem that arises from sparse skeletal representations \[[25](http://arxiv.org/abs/2111.11927)\]. Similarly, \[[28](http://arxiv.org/abs/2107.07797)\] introduced a directed graph convolutional network to explicitly model the hierarchical relationships among joints, which improves the modeling of articulated human skeletons \[[28](http://arxiv.org/abs/2107.07797)\]. 

Transformers have also been increasingly adopted for 3D human pose estimation due to their ability to model long-range dependencies \[[22](http://arxiv.org/abs/2103.10455)\]. However, most transformer-based methods focus on global attention mechanisms, which may not be sufficient for capturing fine-grained local details \[[4](http://arxiv.org/abs/2412.19676)\]. To address this, \[[4](http://arxiv.org/abs/2412.19676)\] proposed a dual-stream model that integrates local features with global dependencies, enabling more comprehensive understanding of human motion. This approach enhances the ability to capture both local and global dependencies, which is essential for accurate pose estimation \[[4](http://arxiv.org/abs/2412.19676)\]. 

Another challenge in 3D human pose estimation is the need to balance computational efficiency with accuracy. Many methods prioritize accuracy, resulting in large network sizes and high computational complexity \[[8](http://arxiv.org/abs/2301.13403)\]. To address this, \[[8](http://arxiv.org/abs/2301.13403)\] introduced a modular multi-stage lightweight graph-based transformer network that prioritizes computational efficiency without sacrificing reconstruction accuracy. This approach is particularly beneficial for real-world applications where computational resources are limited \[[8](http://arxiv.org/abs/2301.13403)\]. 

Additionally, some works have focused on improving the modeling of spatiotemporal dependencies. For example, \[[7](http://arxiv.org/abs/2407.10099)\] introduced a multi-hop graph transformer network that leverages multi-head self-attention and multi-hop graph convolutional layers to capture both local and global dependencies. This approach enhances the model's ability to handle long-range interactions and spatial details required for accurate joint localization \[[7](http://arxiv.org/abs/2407.10099)\].
