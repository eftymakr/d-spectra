## Related Works

Open-vocabulary object detection (OVD) has gained significant attention in recent years due to its ability to detect objects from an unrestricted set of categories, including those unseen during training \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2211.14843)\]. Early approaches relied on expensive grounding annotations or distilling classification-oriented vision models \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2211.14843)\]. However, these methods often struggled with fine-grained object-language alignment and lacked scalability \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2211.14843)\]. 

Several works have explored the use of vision-language models (VLMs) to enable open-vocabulary detection. For instance, VLDet proposes a framework that directly learns from image-text pair data by formulating object-language alignment as a set matching problem \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2211.14843)\]. Similarly, the work by \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/2205.06160)\] introduces a two-stage training approach that first uses location-guided image-caption matching to learn class labels for both novel and known classes in a weakly-supervised manner \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/2205.06160)\]. 

The challenge of aligning visual and textual representations has also been addressed through contrastive learning. For example, \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/2211.12914)\] introduces a framework that integrates pretrained 2D and 3D vision foundation models to reduce dependence on 3D supervision \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/2211.12914)\]. This is further extended in \[[4](http://arxiv.org/abs/2205.06230)\], where a standard Vision Transformer architecture with minimal modifications, contrastive image-text pre-training, and end-to-end detection fine-tuning is proposed \[[4](http://arxiv.org/abs/2205.06230)\]. 

Another key challenge in OVD is the imbalance between base and novel categories. To address this, \[[5](http://arxiv.org/abs/2503.14862)\] proposes a method that uses a simple language model to detect novel objects, showing that it fits better than large contextualized language models \[[5](http://arxiv.org/abs/2503.14862)\]. Additionally, \[[6](http://arxiv.org/abs/2411.16833)\] introduces a consistency-regularization technique to better exploit image-caption pair information \[[6](http://arxiv.org/abs/2411.16833)\]. 

The problem of visual-textual misalignment and long-tailed category imbalance has also been tackled through multimodal question answering. MQADet, for instance, enhances existing open-vocabulary detectors by leveraging the cross-modal reasoning capabilities of multimodal large language models (MLLMs) \[[7](http://arxiv.org/abs/2502.16486)\]. This approach allows for seamless integration with pre-trained object detectors without requiring additional training or fine-tuning \[[7](http://arxiv.org/abs/2502.16486)\].

This is complemented by a class-wise backdoor adjustment to reinforce predictions on novel categories \[[8](http://arxiv.org/abs/2304.00788)\].

### <a id='category-open-vocabulary-object-detection-frameworks'></a>Open-vocabulary object detection frameworks: 18 papers found.
Open-vocabulary object detection (OVD) has emerged as a critical research direction, enabling models to detect objects defined by arbitrary textual descriptions, thus reducing the reliance on costly labeled data \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2211.14843)\]. Recent works have focused on improving the alignment between image regions and language, often leveraging vision-language pre-training models like CLIP \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/2211.12914)\]. For instance, VLDet directly learns from image-text pairs by formulating object-language alignment as a set matching problem, achieving strong performance on COCO and LVIS benchmarks \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2211.14843)\]. Similarly, methods like locov employ a two-stage training approach, combining weakly-supervised image-caption matching with known class annotations to enhance detection of novel objects \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/2205.06160)\]. The use of contrastive learning and pseudo-labeling has also been explored to bridge the gap between image and text modalities, as seen in methods such as Object2Scene and L3Det, which enrich 3D scene datasets with novel object vocabulary \[[17](http://arxiv.org/abs/2309.09456)\]. Furthermore, approaches like DVDet introduce hierarchical textual descriptors and conditional context prompts to enable precise region-text alignment, improving detection accuracy \[[23](http://arxiv.org/abs/2402.04630)\]. Despite these advances, challenges remain, such as handling out-of-distribution objects and ensuring robustness across diverse domains \[[5](http://arxiv.org/abs/2503.14862)\]. To address these, frameworks like OpenSight integrate 2D-3D geometric priors and cross-modal alignment to improve generic object perception in LiDAR-based settings \[[13](http://arxiv.org/abs/2312.08876)\]. Additionally, methods such as CastDet and OWLv2 demonstrate the effectiveness of self-training and large-scale data augmentation in scaling open-vocabulary detection to new categories \[[12](http://arxiv.org/abs/2212.12130)\]\[[21](http://arxiv.org/abs/2306.09683)\]. Overall, these works highlight the growing trend of using vision-language models and weak supervision to expand object detection capabilities beyond closed-set assumptions.
