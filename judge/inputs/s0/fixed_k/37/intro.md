## Related Works

Vision Transformers (ViTs) have emerged as a powerful alternative to convolutional neural networks (CNNs) in computer vision tasks, leveraging self-attention mechanisms to capture global context \[[7](http://arxiv.org/abs/2503.02891)\]. However, their deployment on resource-constrained hardware remains challenging due to their high computational and memory demands \[[7](http://arxiv.org/abs/2503.02891)\]. Existing methods often rely on CPU for complex operations such as Softmax and LayerNorm, leading to suboptimal performance due to communication overhead between CPU and accelerator \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2211.08110)\]. This section reviews related works on ViT acceleration and model compression, focusing on hardware-aware design and algorithmic optimizations.

Several studies have explored hardware-efficient ViT acceleration. HeatViT introduces a hardware-efficient token pruning framework that dynamically identifies and consolidates non-informative tokens, achieving significant computation reduction while maintaining high accuracy \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2211.08110)\]. The work also employs 8-bit fixed-point quantization and polynomial approximations to improve hardware efficiency \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2211.08110)\]. Similarly, CHOSEN proposes a software-hardware co-design framework that optimizes ViT inference on FPGAs through multi-kernel design, approximate non-linear functions, and efficient use of logic blocks \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/2407.12736)\]. ViTA, another hardware accelerator, targets resource-constrained edge devices with a head-level pipeline and inter-layer MLP optimizations, achieving nearly 90% hardware utilization efficiency \[[4](http://arxiv.org/abs/2302.09108)\].

Model quantization has also been extensively studied for ViT acceleration. ViTCoD introduces a co-design framework that combines algorithmic and hardware-level optimizations, including attention map pruning and a lightweight auto-encoder module to reduce data movement costs \[[5](http://arxiv.org/abs/2210.09573)\]. This approach achieves significant speedups over general computing platforms and prior-art accelerators \[[5](http://arxiv.org/abs/2210.09573)\]. SuperViT further reduces computational costs by training a single model capable of handling varying input sizes and token keeping rates, improving hardware efficiency for inference \[[6](http://arxiv.org/abs/2205.11397)\]. VITALITY unifies low-rank and sparse approximations for ViT acceleration, achieving 3× faster and 3× more energy-efficient inference under comparable accuracy \[[8](http://arxiv.org/abs/2211.05109)\].

Evo-ViT introduces a slow-fast token evolution approach that maintains spatial structure and information flow, significantly reducing computational cost while maintaining performance \[[9](http://arxiv.org/abs/2108.01390)\]. The work on sparse hardware accelerators for spike-driven transformers also highlights the importance of exploiting sparsity in low-power hardware implementations \[[10](http://arxiv.org/abs/2501.07825)\]. Attention Guided CAM provides visualization methods for ViT, enhancing interpretability by leveraging self-attention scores \[[11](http://arxiv.org/abs/2402.04563)\]. Surface Vision Transformers extend ViT to non-Euclidean geometries, demonstrating the versatility of self-attention in modeling long-range associations \[[12](http://arxiv.org/abs/2205.15836)\].
