## Related Works

Anomaly detection has gained significant attention in recent years due to its critical role in quality control and fault diagnosis in industrial settings. Several approaches have been proposed to address the challenges of deploying anomaly detection models on resource-constrained embedded devices, particularly in the context of industrial IoT and smart manufacturing. For instance, TinyAD \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2303.03611)\] introduces a memory-efficient framework for deploying convolutional neural networks (CNNs) on microcontroller units (MCUs), reducing peak memory consumption by 2–5× while maintaining negligible computation overhead. This is particularly relevant for real-time anomaly detection in embedded systems. Similarly, the tiled ensemble approach in \[[4](http://arxiv.org/abs/2403.04932)\] reduces memory consumption by dividing images into tiles and training dedicated models for each tile, enabling high-resolution anomaly detection within GPU memory constraints.

In addition to memory efficiency, the ability to handle incomplete or multimodal data is crucial for practical anomaly detection. CMDIAD \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/2405.13571)\] proposes a cross-modal distillation framework that enables Multi-modal Training, Few-modal Inference (MTFI), allowing models to leverage incomplete multimodal information during inference. This is especially useful in industrial settings where only a subset of samples can be inspected using all available modalities. Furthermore, the use of synthetic data and digital twin systems has been explored for training anomaly detection models when real-world labeled anomalies are scarce. For example, \[[9](http://arxiv.org/abs/2011.06296)\] introduces weakly supervised approaches using Digital Twin systems to generate synthetic datasets, which are then used for training anomaly detection models with minimal labeled data.

Another important aspect is the integration of deep learning models with real-time processing capabilities. The LSTM-based Encoder-Decoder (EncDec-AD) \[[5](http://arxiv.org/abs/1607.00148)\] is designed to detect anomalies in time-series data by learning to reconstruct normal behavior and using reconstruction error as a detection metric. This approach is effective for both predictable and unpredictable time-series, making it suitable for a wide range of industrial applications. Similarly, the use of vision foundation models for semantic anomaly detection \[[8](http://arxiv.org/abs/2505.07998)\] leverages the semantic priors of state-of-the-art vision models to detect contextually invalid visual patterns, which is critical for autonomous systems.

[Snippet]: «Monitoring and detecting abnormal events in cyber-physical systems is crucial to industrial production. With the prevalent deployment of the industrial Internet of Things (IIoTs), an enormous amount of time-series data is collected to facilitate machine learning models for anomaly detection, and it is of the utmost importance to directly deploy the trained models on the IIoT devices. However, it is most challenging to deploy complex deep learning models such as convolutional neural networks (CNNs) on these memory-constrained IIoT devices embedded with microcontrollers (MCUs). To alleviate the memory constraints of MCUs, we propose a novel framework named Tiny Anomaly Detection (TinyAD) to efficiently facilitate onboard inference of CNNs for real-time anomaly detection. First, we conduct a comprehensive analysis of depthwise separable CNNs and regular CNNs for anomaly detection and find that the depthwise separable convolution operation can reduce the model size by 50%–90% compared with the traditional CNNs. Then, to reduce the peak memory consumption of CNNs, we explore two complementary strategies, 1) in-place; and 2) patch-by-patch memory rescheduling, and integrate them into a unified framework. The in-place method decreases the peak memory of the depthwise convolution by sparing a temporary buffer to transfer the activation results, while the patch-by-patch method further reduces the peak memory of layer-wise execution by slicing the input data into corresponding receptive fields and executing in order. Furthermore, by adjusting the dimension of convolution filters, these strategies apply to both univariate time series and multidomain time series features. Extensive experiments on real-world industrial datasets show that our framework can reduce peak memory consumption by 2–5× with negligible computation overhead.»

### <a id='category-multi-modal-data-handling-in-anomaly-detection'></a>Multi-modal Data Handling in Anomaly Detection: 4 papers found.
In the context of multi-modal data handling in anomaly detection, recent studies have emphasized the importance of leveraging the redundancy and complementarity among different data modalities to enhance classification and segmentation accuracy, particularly in industrial settings \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/2405.13571)\]. For instance, in the production of Li-ion batteries and composite materials, existing quality control processes integrate rapid in-line inspections, such as optical and infrared imaging, with more time-consuming near-line techniques like industrial CT and electron microscopy \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/2405.13571)\]. However, due to cost and time constraints, only a subset of samples is typically inspected using all methods, while others rely on fewer modalities \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/2405.13571)\]. This has led to the development of frameworks like CMDIAD, which introduces a Cross-Modal Distillation approach to enable Multi-modal Training, Few-modal Inference (MTFI) pipelines, allowing models to effectively utilize incomplete multimodal data during inference \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/2405.13571)\]. Similarly, in radar imaging, the introduction of SAR imagery anomaly detection (SARIAD) provides a comprehensive suite of algorithms and datasets for benchmarking anomaly detection methods on SAR imagery, enhancing reproducibility and standardization in this domain \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/2504.08115)\]. In the medical field, the lack of standardized evaluation frameworks has hindered progress, prompting the creation of benchmark datasets that include multiple image modalities and a wide range of anomaly detection methods, facilitating more rigorous and fair comparisons \[[10](http://arxiv.org/abs/2404.04518)\]. Additionally, for 3D object anomaly detection, challenges such as computational demands have been addressed through innovative frameworks like SplatPose, which offers improved training and inference speeds while maintaining high detection performance \[[11](http://arxiv.org/abs/2404.06832)\]. These developments highlight the ongoing efforts to improve the efficiency, accuracy, and practical applicability of anomaly detection systems across various domains.
