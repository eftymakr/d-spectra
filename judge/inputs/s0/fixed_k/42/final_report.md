## Related Works

The growing adoption of cloud-native applications has led to increased reliance on managed cloud services and containerization technologies such as Kubernetes \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2501.14753)\]. These technologies enable rapid deployment and scalability, but they also introduce new challenges in cost management, particularly in the context of network usage and expenditures \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/1901.04946)\]. FinOps, an emerging discipline that integrates financial management with cloud operations, has gained traction as a framework for optimizing cloud costs \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/2104.02423)\]. However, prior research has primarily focused on system-level optimization strategies for resource efficiency, with limited attention given to network cost analysis in Kubernetes clusters \[[4](http://arxiv.org/abs/2307.12479)\].

Several studies have explored cost optimization techniques for cloud environments. For instance, ABACUS presents a FinOps solution that sets budgets, enforces them through blocking new deployments, and alerts teams when spending thresholds are breached \[[5](http://arxiv.org/abs/2405.12311)\]. This approach aligns with the broader goal of FinOps to bring accountability to cloud spend \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2501.14753)\]. Additionally, the use of Infrastructure-as-Code (IaC) is highlighted as a best practice to estimate deployment costs before resource allocation \[[5](http://arxiv.org/abs/2405.12311)\]. These strategies are essential for managing costs in cloud-native environments but do not directly address network cost implications.

In the context of Kubernetes, several works have examined the challenges of managing microservices and ensuring availability \[[6](http://arxiv.org/abs/2006.15275)\]. Kubernetes, while effective for orchestrating containerized microservices, faces limitations in terms of performance and availability when deployed at the edge \[[7](http://arxiv.org/abs/2207.01222)\]. These limitations are exacerbated by the use of etcd, which stores cluster state and can introduce latency and reduced throughput at scale \[[7](http://arxiv.org/abs/2207.01222)\]. To address these issues, researchers have proposed alternative consistency models, such as eventually consistent approaches, to improve performance and scalability \[[7](http://arxiv.org/abs/2207.01222)\].

The deployment of microservices on public clouds also raises concerns about cost optimization. SpotKube, a Kubernetes-based solution, employs a genetic algorithm to dynamically scale clusters and optimize costs using spot pricing \[[8](http://arxiv.org/abs/2401.07417)\]. This approach helps reduce expenses while maintaining performance, demonstrating the potential of intelligent orchestration in cost management \[[8](http://arxiv.org/abs/2401.07417)\]. Similarly, the integration of Kubernetes with cloud platforms like AWS highlights the need for strategies that balance cost efficiency with system availability \[[9](http://arxiv.org/abs/2301.01178)\].

Network cost analysis in Kubernetes clusters is a critical area that remains underexplored. While some studies have examined the performance and availability of Kubernetes in various deployment scenarios \[[6](http://arxiv.org/abs/2006.15275)\]\[[7](http://arxiv.org/abs/2207.01222)\], there is a lack of comprehensive research on the financial implications of network usage in containerized applications \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/1901.04946)\]. This gap highlights the need for methodologies that combine measurement analysis, experimentation, and cost modeling to provide actionable insights into network cost optimization \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/1901.04946)\].

### <a id='category-kubernetes-performance-and-availability-issues'></a>Kubernetes Performance and Availability Issues: 2 papers found.
The category "Kubernetes Performance and Availability Issues" highlights critical challenges in deploying microservice-based applications using Kubernetes, particularly in terms of availability and performance. Research indicates that while Kubernetes provides a robust framework for managing containerized microservices, its default configurations may not be sufficient for ensuring high availability in certain scenarios \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2501.14753)\]. For instance, studies have shown that Kubernetes can experience significant service outages under specific conditions, raising concerns about its reliability in carrier-grade environments \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2501.14753)\]. Additionally, the use of etcd, a strongly consistent key-value store, for storing cluster state can lead to increased write request latency and reduced throughput as cluster sizes grow, which negatively impacts availability and performance, especially in edge computing environments \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/1901.04946)\]. To address these limitations, researchers have proposed alternative approaches, such as adopting an eventually consistent model, which can enhance performance and scalability without compromising the essential functionalities of Kubernetes \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/1901.04946)\]. These findings underscore the need for further investigation into optimizing Kubernetes for high availability and performance in diverse deployment environments.

### <a id='category-cloud-native-technologies-and-kubernetes-adoption'></a>Cloud-Native Technologies and Kubernetes Adoption: 2 papers found.
Kubernetes has emerged as a leading platform for orchestrating containerized applications, enabling organizations to achieve rapid deployment and scalable management of their services \[[9](http://arxiv.org/abs/2301.01178)\]. Its adoption is widespread, with companies like IBM, Capital One, and Adidas leveraging it to enhance deployment frequency and operational efficiency \[[6](http://arxiv.org/abs/2006.15275)\]. However, Kubernetes deployments are not without challenges, particularly in terms of security. Vulnerabilities in Kubernetes configurations have led to incidents such as the 2018 Tesla breach, highlighting the need for robust security practices \[[6](http://arxiv.org/abs/2006.15275)\]. To address these concerns, researchers have identified key security measures, including role-based access control (RBAC), regular patching, and network-specific policies, which are essential for mitigating risks in Kubernetes environments \[[6](http://arxiv.org/abs/2006.15275)\]. Additionally, advancements in Kubernetes networking, such as the integration of SRv6 through plugins like Calico-VPP, offer enhanced performance and features for large-scale, distributed deployments \[[9](http://arxiv.org/abs/2301.01178)\]. These developments underscore the ongoing evolution of Kubernetes, balancing its operational benefits with the imperative for security and scalability.

### <a id='category-integration-of-kubernetes-with-cloud-platforms'></a>Integration of Kubernetes with Cloud Platforms: 1 papers found.
The integration of Kubernetes with cloud platforms has become a critical area of research, particularly as workflow systems increasingly adopt containerization for scalability and efficiency \[[7](http://arxiv.org/abs/2207.01222)\]. However, a key challenge arises from the inherent scheduling mechanisms of Kubernetes, which do not align with the optimized task execution order typically employed by workflow systems \[[7](http://arxiv.org/abs/2207.01222)\]. This misalignment can lead to significant performance degradation and inefficiencies in workflow execution \[[7](http://arxiv.org/abs/2207.01222)\]. To address this issue, researchers have proposed solutions such as KubeAdaptor, a cloud-native workflow engine designed to bridge the gap between workflow systems and Kubernetes by ensuring consistency in task scheduling order \[[7](http://arxiv.org/abs/2207.01222)\]. This approach not only enhances the execution efficiency of workflows on Kubernetes but also supports the broader goal of achieving seamless integration between traditional workflow systems and modern cloud-native infrastructures \[[7](http://arxiv.org/abs/2207.01222)\]. Such efforts are essential for advancing the capabilities of cloud-native environments and supporting the growing demand for efficient and scalable containerized workflows \[[7](http://arxiv.org/abs/2207.01222)\].

### <a id='category-system-level-optimization-strategies'></a>System-Level Optimization Strategies: 5 papers found.
System-Level Optimization Strategies encompass a range of approaches aimed at improving the efficiency and performance of networked systems, with particular emphasis on community detection, resource allocation, and cost optimization. Community detection algorithms, such as the label propagation algorithm, are essential for identifying dense subgraphs in networks, yet they face challenges in scalability and computational cost, prompting the need for parallel implementations \[[10](http://arxiv.org/abs/1507.05492)\]. In the context of 5G networks, resource allocation mechanisms are critical for ensuring service isolation and low latency, especially in private networks where integration with edge/cloud platforms remains a challenge \[[11](http://arxiv.org/abs/2502.02842)\]. Quantum computing introduces novel paradigms for optimization, leveraging superposition to perform computations in parallel and offering potential advantages over classical algorithms for specific tasks \[[12](http://arxiv.org/abs/2412.15778)\]. Additionally, network recovery strategies must account for interdependencies between nodes, requiring discrete optimization techniques to minimize recovery costs and improve resilience \[[13](http://arxiv.org/abs/1207.2799)\]. In energy systems, profit-maximizing strategies for energy storage involve dynamic decision-making based on cost functions and market conditions, highlighting the importance of localized, time-sensitive algorithms \[[18](http://arxiv.org/abs/1412.0829)\]. These strategies collectively underscore the interdisciplinary nature of system-level optimization, addressing both technical and economic challenges in modern computing and networking environments.
