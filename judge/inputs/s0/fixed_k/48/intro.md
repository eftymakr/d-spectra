## Related Works

Neural retrieval methods using transformer-based pre-trained language models have significantly advanced multilingual and cross-lingual retrieval \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2503.18570)\]. However, their effectiveness for low-resource, morphologically rich languages such as Amharic remains underexplored due to data scarcity and suboptimal tokenization \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/2207.00785)\]. This challenge is further compounded by the lack of large-scale annotated datasets and the difficulty in adapting pre-trained models to these languages \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/2503.18485)\]. 

Several studies have attempted to address these limitations by exploring fine-tuning strategies and dataset composition for improving ASR in low-resource languages \[[4](http://arxiv.org/abs/2403.06354)\]. For example, the work on fine-tuning Whisper for Amharic highlights the importance of combining existing datasets with newly collected data to enhance model performance \[[5](http://arxiv.org/abs/2212.10448)\]. Similarly, the development of the Amharic LLaMA and LLaVA models demonstrates the potential of using open-source translation models to augment training data and improve language representation \[[6](http://arxiv.org/abs/2412.12997)\].

In the context of named entity recognition, the ANEC dataset and associated systems have shown that deep learning approaches, including RoBERTa and bidirectional long short-term memory (LSTM) with conditional random fields (CRF), can achieve high performance \[[7](http://arxiv.org/abs/2408.11942)\]. This work also emphasizes the importance of addressing class imbalance through techniques like Synthetic Minority Over-sampling Technique (SMOTE) \[[8](http://arxiv.org/abs/2201.08471)\]. 

Cross-lingual dense retrieval has also been explored in the context of low-resource languages, with studies such as the one on the mDPR model showing that while it achieves success on multilingual benchmarks, performance can be limited for extremely low-resource languages like Amharic and Khmer \[[9](http://arxiv.org/abs/2307.10814)\]. The paper on ColBERT-X further demonstrates that using XLM-RoBERTa as an encoder can improve cross-language retrieval by leveraging machine-translated data \[[10](http://arxiv.org/abs/2501.05260)\].

The challenge of data scarcity has also been addressed in other domains, such as speech emotion recognition, where cross-lingual and multilingual training strategies have been shown to improve performance when resources for a language are scarce \[[11](http://arxiv.org/abs/2108.06279)\]. Similarly, in the field of plagiarism detection, the use of BERT embeddings in conjunction with traditional features like TF-IDF has been explored to enhance detection accuracy for low-resource languages like Marathi \[[12](http://arxiv.org/abs/2211.14876)\].

Recent advancements in dense retrieval have also focused on improving efficiency and effectiveness, with techniques such as Learning to Retrieve (LTRe) showing significant improvements in both performance and training speed \[[13](http://arxiv.org/abs/2010.10469)\].
