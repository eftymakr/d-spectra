## Related Works

Cross-domain sequential recommendation (CDSR) has gained significant attention in recent years due to its ability to leverage user behavior across multiple domains to improve recommendation performance. Early approaches primarily relied on domain-specific models and transfer learning techniques, often assuming overlapping users or items between domains \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/2304.03891)\]. However, these methods typically struggled with data sparsity and cold-start problems, especially when dealing with non-overlapping domains \[[12](http://arxiv.org/abs/2304.04218)\]. 

Recent advances have focused on integrating domain-specific information into the self-attention mechanism of transformer-based models, which is a core component of most sequential recommendation systems \[[25](http://arxiv.org/abs/1911.06478)\]. For example, some works have introduced domain-aware modules to enhance the modeling of cross-domain interactions \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/2405.12473)\]. However, these approaches often overlook the inherent power of the self-attention mechanism itself, which is capable of learning complex correlations between items in a sequence \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2401.04971)\]. 

To address this, several studies have proposed methods that explicitly enhance the self-attention mechanism to better capture cross-domain dependencies. For instance, \[[19](http://arxiv.org/abs/2304.09184)\] introduced a frequency-enhanced hybrid attention network that leverages the frequency domain to capture both low- and high-frequency information, improving the model's ability to distinguish periodic patterns in user behavior. Similarly, \[[20](http://arxiv.org/abs/2211.01297)\] proposed a self-attentive model based on cheap causal convolutions, which efficiently captures local context information while reducing computational overhead. 

Another line of research has focused on the challenges of non-overlapping cross-domain scenarios, where users and items are entirely distinct between domains \[[12](http://arxiv.org/abs/2304.04218)\]. In such cases, traditional methods that rely on overlapping users or items are ineffective. To overcome this, \[[12](http://arxiv.org/abs/2304.04218)\] proposed a prompt learning-based framework that learns domain-invariant and domain-specific representations without requiring explicit alignment. This approach is particularly useful in scenarios where content information is unavailable \[[12](http://arxiv.org/abs/2304.04218)\]. 

Additionally, \[[22](http://arxiv.org/abs/2302.03221)\] introduced a lightweight external attention-enhanced graph convolutional network (LEA-GCN) to address the computational inefficiencies of existing methods while maintaining accuracy. This framework uses a dual-channel external attention mechanism to efficiently capture correlations among items, making it suitable for large-scale applications.

This is especially relevant in scenarios where data privacy regulations such as GDPR are in place \[[16](http://arxiv.org/abs/2309.08420)\].

### <a id='category-cross-domain-attention-optimization'></a>Cross-Domain Attention Optimization: 15 papers found.
Cross-domain sequential recommendation (CDSR) has emerged as a critical research direction in recommendation systems, aiming to leverage sequential interactions across multiple domains to improve prediction accuracy \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2401.04971)\]. Existing approaches often rely on self-attention mechanisms within transformer architectures, with additional domain-specific modules to enhance performance \[[30](http://arxiv.org/abs/2012.00485)\]. However, these methods typically overlook the potential of the self-attention module itself to learn cross-domain correlations \[[30](http://arxiv.org/abs/2012.00485)\]. Recent works have explored various strategies to address this gap, such as integrating graph neural networks to capture inter-sequence relationships \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/2304.03891)\], employing multi-interest information modules to adapt to open-world environments \[[4](http://arxiv.org/abs/2311.04590)\], and leveraging large language models to extract semantic item relationships \[[7](http://arxiv.org/abs/2504.18383)\]. Additionally, some studies focus on aligning item representations across domains to improve sequential modeling \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/2405.12473)\], while others propose hybrid metric learning to alleviate data sparsity \[[6](http://arxiv.org/abs/2209.10163)\]. A notable approach, C2DSR, jointly learns intra- and inter-sequence item interactions to capture precise user preferences \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/2304.03891)\], and another, AMID, introduces adaptive multi-interest debiasing to handle open-world assumptions \[[4](http://arxiv.org/abs/2311.04590)\]. Furthermore, methods like IFCDSR incorporate visual information through frozen CLIP models to enrich item embeddings \[[13](http://arxiv.org/abs/2502.15694)\], and HAF-VT integrates visual and textual data using hierarchical attention mechanisms \[[26](http://arxiv.org/abs/2504.15085)\]. These diverse techniques highlight the ongoing efforts to enhance cross-domain sequential recommendation by addressing challenges such as data sparsity, domain alignment, and model adaptability.

### <a id='category-self-attention-mechanism-enhancement'></a>Self-Attention Mechanism Enhancement: 7 papers found.
The category "Self-Attention Mechanism Enhancement" focuses on improving the self-attention mechanism in sequential recommendation models to better capture complex item relationships and temporal dynamics. Researchers have identified limitations in traditional self-attention, such as oversmoothing, localization-deficit, and insufficient modeling of high-frequency information, which hinder the ability to learn high-order dependencies and handle uncertainty in user behavior \[[10](http://arxiv.org/abs/2410.21048)\]\[[11](http://arxiv.org/abs/2312.10325)\]\[[17](http://arxiv.org/abs/2209.07997)\]. To address these issues, several approaches have been proposed, including Attention Weight Refinement (AWRSR), which refines attention distributions by emphasizing attention weights \[[10](http://arxiv.org/abs/2410.21048)\]; Beyond Self-Attention for Sequential Recommendation (BSARec), which mitigates oversmoothing using Fourier transforms to integrate low and high-frequency information \[[11](http://arxiv.org/abs/2312.10325)\]; and STOchastic Self-Attention (STOSA), which incorporates uncertainty through stochastic Gaussian distributions and Wasserstein attention to enhance collaborative transitivity \[[15](http://arxiv.org/abs/2201.06035)\]. Additionally, Frequency Enhanced Hybrid Attention Network (FEARec) shifts the perspective to the frequency domain to explicitly learn both low- and high-frequency information and capture periodic patterns \[[19](http://arxiv.org/abs/2304.09184)\]. Relation-Aware Kernelized Self-Attention (RKSA) introduces a probabilistic model to enhance relation-awareness in self-attention, leveraging co-occurrence and item characteristics \[[25](http://arxiv.org/abs/1911.06478)\]. Finally, TemProxRec integrates contrastive learning and self-attention to consider both vertical and horizontal temporal proximities, improving temporal context modeling \[[27](http://arxiv.org/abs/2402.09784)\]. These studies collectively highlight the importance of enhancing self-attention to better model sequential behaviors and improve recommendation performance.

[Snippet]: «Modeling user sequential behaviors has recently attracted increasing attention in the recommendation domain. Existing methods mostly assume coherent preference in the same sequence. However, user personalities are volatile and easily changed, and there can be multiple mixed preferences underlying user behaviors. To solve this problem, in this paper, we propose a novel sequential recommender model via decomposing and modeling user independent preferences. To achieve this goal, we highlight three practical challenges considering the inconsistent, evolving and uneven nature of the user behaviors. For overcoming these challenges in a unified framework, we introduce a reinforcement learning module to simulate the evolution of user preference. More specifically, the action aims to allocate each item into a sub-sequence or create a new one according to how the previous items are decomposed as well as the time interval between successive behaviors. The reward is associated with the final loss of the learning objective, aiming to generate sub-sequences which can better fit the training data. We conduct extensive experiments based on eight real-world datasets across different domains. Comparing with the state-of-the-art methods, empirical studies manifest that our model can on average improve the performance by about 9.68%, 12.4%, 8.56% and 7.13% on the metrics of Precision, Recall, NDCG and MRR, respectively.»
