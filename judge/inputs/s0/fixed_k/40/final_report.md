## Related Works

Zero-Knowledge Proofs (ZKPs) have garnered significant attention for their ability to enable secure and private computation, allowing a prover to demonstrate the truth of a statement without revealing additional information \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2408.05890)\]. This capability has led to their application in blockchain technologies, verifiable machine learning, and electronic voting \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2408.05890)\]. However, the computational complexity of the proving process has hindered widespread adoption \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2408.05890)\]. Recent efforts have focused on accelerating the key primitives of ZKP protocols using hardware, particularly GPUs and ASICs \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2408.05890)\]. For instance, SZKP presents a scalable accelerator framework that leverages structured dataflows for both Number Theoretic Transform (NTT) and Multi-scalar Multiplication (MSM) operations, achieving speedups of over 400×, 3×, and 12× over CPU, ASIC, and GPU implementations \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/2412.12481)\]. Similarly, if-ZKP introduces an FPGA-based architecture for accelerating the multi-scalar multiplication (MSM) operation in zk-SNARKs, achieving 110x-150x speedup over software for the BLS12-381 and BN128 elliptic curves \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/2206.13350)\]. These works highlight the importance of optimizing the computationally intensive parts of ZKP protocols to enable practical deployment.

Despite these advancements, existing protocols often face challenges such as requiring a trusted setup for each application or generating larger proof sizes with higher verification costs, which limits their applicability in scenarios with numerous verifiers or strict verification time constraints \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2408.05890)\]. For example, some protocols rely on complex cryptographic constructions that necessitate a trusted setup, which can be a security risk \[[4](http://arxiv.org/abs/2307.00521)\]. Others generate proofs that are too large or require excessive computation, making them unsuitable for real-time or resource-constrained environments \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2408.05890)\]. To address these limitations, recent work has focused on developing ZKP protocols that support universal setup and small proof sizes, such as HyperPlonk, which enables one-time, universal setup and small proof sizes for typical ZKP applications in publicly verifiable, consensus-based systems \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2408.05890)\]. This work presents an accelerator, zkSpeed, for HyperPlonk, which accelerates the entire protocol, including the SumCheck and Multi-scalar Multiplication (MSM) primitives, using a full-chip architecture with 366.46 mm² and 2 TB/s of bandwidth, achieving geometric mean speedups of 801× over CPU baselines \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2408.05890)\].

### <a id='category-hardware-acceleration-for-zkp-primitives'></a>Hardware Acceleration for ZKP Primitives: 2 papers found.
Hardware acceleration for zero-knowledge proof (ZKP) primitives has emerged as a critical area of research to address the computational inefficiencies inherent in ZKP generation. Key primitives such as the Number Theoretic Transform (NTT) and Multi-scalar Multiplication (MSM) are particularly resource-intensive, with NTTs involving irregular memory access patterns and MSMs requiring data-dependent memory accesses \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2408.05890)\]. Prior efforts have explored GPU implementations and custom RTL designs, but these approaches often struggle with the complex dataflow patterns required for efficient computation \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2408.05890)\]. To overcome these limitations, SZKP was introduced as the first ASIC to accelerate the entire proof generation process on-chip, leveraging structured dataflows for both NTTs and MSMs \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2408.05890)\]. This approach achieves significant speedups, with conservative full-proof improvements of over 400×, 3×, and 12× compared to CPU, ASIC, and GPU implementations, respectively \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2408.05890)\]. Similarly, the need for efficient cryptographic hashing in ZKP protocols has led to the development of ZK-friendly hash functions like MiMC, which offer a simple algebraic structure suitable for ZKP applications \[[10](http://arxiv.org/abs/2411.06350)\]. While software implementations of MiMC have shown performance gains, hardware acceleration remains essential for practical deployment, especially in resource-constrained environments \[[10](http://arxiv.org/abs/2411.06350)\]. The AMAZE framework exemplifies this by providing a highly optimized hardware implementation of MiMC, achieving over 13× speedup compared to standard CPU implementations \[[10](http://arxiv.org/abs/2411.06350)\]. These advancements highlight the growing importance of hardware acceleration in enabling the widespread adoption of ZKP technologies.

### <a id='category-zkp-protocol-overview-and-applications'></a>ZKP Protocol Overview and Applications: 6 papers found.
Zero-knowledge proofs (ZKPs) have emerged as a critical tool for privacy-preserving computations, enabling parties to verify the correctness of statements without disclosing underlying data \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/2206.13350)\]. This concept is particularly relevant in blockchain technologies, where ZKPs can ensure transaction validity without exposing sensitive information \[[7](http://arxiv.org/abs/2012.10452)\]. Recent advancements have focused on improving the efficiency of ZKP protocols, especially in terms of computational complexity and proof size \[[9](http://arxiv.org/abs/2301.02161)\]. For instance, the zkFi protocol offers a modular approach to integrating ZKPs into multi-chain systems, allowing developers to handle transactional assets without requiring deep expertise in zero-knowledge proofs \[[4](http://arxiv.org/abs/2307.00521)\]. Additionally, the zksk library provides a Python-based framework for defining and computing sigma protocols, simplifying the implementation of ZKPs through a domain-specific language \[[5](http://arxiv.org/abs/1911.02459)\]. Meanwhile, protocols like Crêpe address complex problems such as regular expression equivalence, demonstrating the potential of ZKPs in tackling PSPACE-complete tasks \[[6](http://arxiv.org/abs/2504.01198)\]. These developments highlight the growing interest in ZKPs for both theoretical and practical applications, with ongoing efforts to enhance their scalability and usability across diverse domains.
