## Related Works

Zero-Knowledge Proofs (ZKPs) have garnered significant attention for their ability to enable secure and private computation, allowing a prover to demonstrate the truth of a statement without revealing additional information \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2408.05890)\]. This capability has led to their application in blockchain technologies, verifiable machine learning, and electronic voting \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2408.05890)\]. However, the computational complexity of the proving process has hindered widespread adoption \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2408.05890)\]. Recent efforts have focused on accelerating the key primitives of ZKP protocols using hardware, particularly GPUs and ASICs \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2408.05890)\]. For instance, SZKP presents a scalable accelerator framework that leverages structured dataflows for both Number Theoretic Transform (NTT) and Multi-scalar Multiplication (MSM) operations, achieving speedups of over 400×, 3×, and 12× over CPU, ASIC, and GPU implementations \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/2412.12481)\]. Similarly, if-ZKP introduces an FPGA-based architecture for accelerating the multi-scalar multiplication (MSM) operation in zk-SNARKs, achieving 110x-150x speedup over software for the BLS12-381 and BN128 elliptic curves \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/2206.13350)\]. These works highlight the importance of optimizing the computationally intensive parts of ZKP protocols to enable practical deployment.

Despite these advancements, existing protocols often face challenges such as requiring a trusted setup for each application or generating larger proof sizes with higher verification costs, which limits their applicability in scenarios with numerous verifiers or strict verification time constraints \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2408.05890)\]. For example, some protocols rely on complex cryptographic constructions that necessitate a trusted setup, which can be a security risk \[[4](http://arxiv.org/abs/2307.00521)\]. Others generate proofs that are too large or require excessive computation, making them unsuitable for real-time or resource-constrained environments \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2408.05890)\]. To address these limitations, recent work has focused on developing ZKP protocols that support universal setup and small proof sizes, such as HyperPlonk, which enables one-time, universal setup and small proof sizes for typical ZKP applications in publicly verifiable, consensus-based systems \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2408.05890)\]. This work presents an accelerator, zkSpeed, for HyperPlonk, which accelerates the entire protocol, including the SumCheck and Multi-scalar Multiplication (MSM) primitives, using a full-chip architecture with 366.46 mm² and 2 TB/s of bandwidth, achieving geometric mean speedups of 801× over CPU baselines \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2408.05890)\].
