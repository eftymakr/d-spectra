## Related Works

Differential privacy (DP) has been widely recognized as a de facto standard for ensuring data privacy in statistical databases \[[19](http://arxiv.org/abs/0803.3946)\]. However, its implementation in distributed settings is vulnerable to malicious behaviors by servers, such as sampling noise from incorrect distributions or generating correlated noise while appearing to follow established protocols \[[20](http://arxiv.org/abs/2209.15091)\]. This issue has been addressed in several works. For instance, \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2406.18940)\] highlights the vulnerability of Local Differential Privacy (LDP) to malicious clients who can manipulate inputs or outputs to skew results. To mitigate this, \[[4](http://arxiv.org/abs/2104.06569)\] proposes verifiable randomization mechanisms that allow data collectors to verify the correctness of randomization applied by data providers, preventing output manipulation attacks. Similarly, \[[15](http://arxiv.org/abs/2504.20926)\] introduces a verifiable LDP mechanism that prevents both input and output manipulation attacks, requiring only one-time interaction between client and server. These works align with our focus on ensuring verifiability in distributed DP systems.

Verifiable Differential Privacy (VDP) has also been explored in the context of secure multi-party computation (MPC) and distributed settings. \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/2409.10667)\] introduces interactive proofs for differential privacy, requiring a publisher to provide a zero-knowledge proof that validates the correctness of DP outputs. This approach ensures that the randomness used in DP is faithfully generated without revealing it. \[[10](http://arxiv.org/abs/2202.10209)\] extends this idea by proposing verifiable randomized response mechanisms that allow for the verification of the correctness of randomized responses, enhancing the reliability of DP outputs in distributed environments. These approaches are closely related to our proposed Verifiable Distributed Differential Privacy (VDDP) framework, which incorporates verification mechanisms to ensure the correct execution of DP protocols.

In the context of verifiable LDP, \[[14](http://arxiv.org/abs/1212.1186)\] presents a mechanism that allows for the verification of the correctness of DP outputs while maintaining high utility. This is achieved through the use of zero-knowledge proofs that validate the randomness used in the DP process. Similarly, \[[17](http://arxiv.org/abs/2504.17523)\] introduces a mechanism that applies randomization to indexes rather than values, ensuring rigorous LDP guarantees while preserving utility. These methods are relevant to our work as they demonstrate the feasibility of verifiable DP mechanisms that balance privacy and utility.

The shuffle model with LDP provides an additional layer of privacy, by disconnecting the link between clients and the aggregator. We show that all schemes are highly practical, with client run times of less than 2 seconds, and server run times of 5-7 milliseconds per client.»

### <a id='category-differential-privacy-(dp)-fundamentals'></a>Differential Privacy (DP) Fundamentals: 8 papers found.
The category "Differential Privacy (DP) Fundamentals" encompasses foundational research on differential privacy, including its models, challenges, and applications. It highlights the central and local models of DP, where the former relies on a trusted server for data aggregation, while the latter requires individuals to add noise, often at the cost of reduced utility \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/2409.10667)\]. The distributed model, which combines elements of both, leverages Secure Multi-party Computation (MPC) to achieve utility comparable to the central model while preventing information leakage under specific security assumptions \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/2409.10667)\]. Challenges in implementing DP in distributed settings include efficiently sampling noise with MPC, with existing protocols varying in security assumptions and performance \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/2409.10667)\]. Additionally, the integration of DP in Natural Language Processing (NLP) has been explored, with mechanisms like word-level Metric Local Differential Privacy (MLDP) offering privacy through word-by-word perturbations, though they face utility loss and computational costs \[[5](http://arxiv.org/abs/2405.01678)\]. The privacy-utility tradeoff is also a key theme, with studies showing that symmetric DP mechanisms are optimal for certain source distribution sets, while asymmetric mechanisms are better suited for others \[[7](http://arxiv.org/abs/1601.06426)\]. Furthermore, research has examined the impact of DP on model security, revealing that differentially private neural networks may be more vulnerable to adversarial attacks than non-private versions, with factors like gradient clipping and noise addition influencing robustness \[[13](http://arxiv.org/abs/2012.07828)\]. Lastly, applications of DP in decentralized systems, such as matrix factorization for recommendations, have been addressed with novel approaches like Heterogeneous Differentially Private Matrix Factorization (HDPMF), which balances privacy and accuracy through innovative mechanisms \[[16](http://arxiv.org/abs/2212.00306)\].

### <a id='category-local-differential-privacy-(ldp)-verification'></a>Local Differential Privacy (LDP) Verification: 8 papers found.
Local Differential Privacy (LDP) has been extensively studied for its ability to protect user privacy while maintaining data utility in distributed settings. Recent works have focused on improving the efficiency and accuracy of LDP mechanisms, particularly in scenarios involving graph data, categorical distributions, and location-based services. For instance, the DPRR algorithm \[[10](http://arxiv.org/abs/2202.10209)\] introduces a novel approach to preserve graph structure while ensuring edge-level LDP, using randomized response and strategic sampling. Similarly, the BRR mechanism \[[15](http://arxiv.org/abs/2504.20926)\] enhances utility by adaptively dividing items based on their utility rankings, achieving significant error reduction. In the context of location-based services, the L-SRR framework \[[20](http://arxiv.org/abs/2209.15091)\] proposes a staircase randomized response mechanism that improves utility for applications like traffic density estimation. Additionally, the CRIAD approach \[[17](http://arxiv.org/abs/2504.17523)\] offers a scalable solution for subset counting queries by randomizing indexes rather than values, thereby minimizing utility loss. These advancements highlight the ongoing efforts to refine LDP mechanisms for diverse applications, balancing privacy guarantees with data utility.

[Snippet]: «Over recent decades, machine learning has significantly advanced network communication, enabling improved decision-making, user behavior analysis, and fault detection. Simultaneously, the growth of communication networks has facilitated the efficient collection of large-scale training data. Traditional centralized machine learning, however, requires collecting data from users, raising significant concerns about privacy and security. Decentralized approaches, where participants exchange computation results instead of raw private data, mitigate these risks but introduce challenges related to trust and verifiability. A critical issue arises: How can one ensure the integrity and validity of computation results shared by other participants? Existing survey articles predominantly address security and privacy concerns in decentralized machine learning, whereas this survey uniquely highlights the emerging issue of verifiability. Recognizing the critical role of zero-knowledge proofs in ensuring verifiability, we present a comprehensive review of Zero-Knowledge Proof-based Verifiable Machine Learning (ZKP-VML). To clarify the research problem, we present a definition of ZKP-VML consisting of four algorithms and several key security properties. In addition, we provide an overview of the current research landscape by systematically organizing the research timeline and categorizing existing schemes based on their security properties. Furthermore, through an in-depth analysis of each existing scheme, we summarize their technical contributions and optimization strategies, aiming to uncover common design principles underlying ZKP-VML schemes. Building on the reviews and analysis presented, we identify current research challenges and suggest future research directions. To the best of our knowledge, this is the most comprehensive survey to date on verifiable decentralized machine learning and ZKP-VML.»
