## Related Works

The rapid growth of open-source machine learning (ML) resources has significantly impacted information retrieval (IR) research, as noted in the paper’s abstract \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2502.04484)\]. However, existing platforms like Hugging Face lack structured representations, which limits advanced querying and analysis \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2502.04484)\]. This section reviews related works that address similar challenges, focusing on dataset documentation, model evolution, and knowledge graph construction.

Several studies have examined the documentation and structure of datasets on Hugging Face. For instance, a large-scale analysis of dataset cards revealed that while some sections are well-populated, others such as "Considerations for Using the Data" are underdeveloped \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/2401.13822)\]. This highlights a need for more comprehensive documentation, which aligns with the goals of HuggingKG in capturing rich textual attributes \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2502.04484)\]. Additionally, the study found that dataset popularity correlates with documentation completeness \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/2401.13822)\], suggesting that more popular datasets may benefit from better documentation practices.

The evolution and maintenance of ML models on Hugging Face have also been explored. A repository mining study analyzed over 380,000 models, revealing trends in ML domains, framework usage, and maintenance practices \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/2311.13380)\]. The study introduced a classification system for model maintenance, categorizing commit messages into corrective, perfective, and adaptive types \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/2311.13380)\]. These findings support the importance of structured metadata in tracking model evolution, a key aspect of HuggingKG \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2502.04484)\].

In the context of carbon footprint and sustainability, a study analyzed 1,417 ML models on Hugging Face, finding that only a small proportion report carbon emissions \[[4](http://arxiv.org/abs/2305.11164)\]. The study also identified correlations between carbon emissions and model size, dataset size, and application domains \[[4](http://arxiv.org/abs/2305.11164)\]. These findings underscore the need for transparent reporting practices, which HuggingKG aims to address through its structured representation of resources \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2502.04484)\].

The development of Hugging Face as a platform for empirical studies has also been evaluated. A qualitative and quantitative analysis confirmed that Hugging Face offers sufficient features and diverse data for empirical studies on AI-related projects \[[5](http://arxiv.org/abs/2307.14841)\]. This supports the use of Hugging Face as a source for constructing knowledge graphs like HuggingKG \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2502.04484)\].

### <a id='category-open-source-community-contributions'></a>Open-Source Community Contributions: 3 papers found.
The category "Open-Source Community Contributions" highlights the dynamics of collaboration and diversity within open-source AI development, particularly on platforms like Hugging Face (HF). Research indicates that activity on the HF Hub is highly imbalanced, with a small percentage of repositories accounting for the majority of downloads, and many models having zero downloads \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2502.04484)\]. This pattern aligns with Pareto distributions observed in open-source software development, suggesting that a few core contributors drive much of the innovation \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2502.04484)\]. Additionally, gender diversity remains a significant issue, with women being underrepresented in both organizational roles and code contributions, which may affect team dynamics and innovation \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/2401.13822)\]. The community exhibits a core-periphery structure, where a core group of prolific developers collaborates with a majority of isolated contributors, and collaboration is marked by high reciprocity among active participants \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2502.04484)\]. Furthermore, the development of large-scale datasets, such as WebFace42M, has driven advancements in face recognition, though challenges remain in managing the computational demands of such datasets \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/2311.13380)\]. These findings underscore the need for more equitable participation and efficient resource management in open-source AI ecosystems.
