## Related Works

Maximum Inner Product Search (MIPS) has been extensively studied in recent years due to its critical role in machine learning and information retrieval, especially in high-dimensional settings \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2503.06882)\]. Traditional approaches to MIPS either rely solely on inner product similarity, which can lead to local optima and redundant computations \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2503.06882)\], or reduce the problem to Euclidean Nearest Neighbor Search (NNS) through space projection, which often results in topology distortion and information loss \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2503.06882)\]. While these paradigms have been explored separately, recent work suggests that the dichotomy between inner product and Euclidean metrics is not inherent and that integrating both can enhance MIPS performance \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2503.06882)\].

Several methods have been proposed to address MIPS. For instance, the ip-NSW algorithm, an advanced graph-based approach, achieves significant speedup over existing baselines by leveraging the norm bias in the MIPS problem \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/1909.13459)\]. It effectively avoids unnecessary computations on small norm vectors by focusing on large norm items \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/1909.13459)\]. Building on this, ip-NSW+ further improves performance by introducing an angular proximity graph to refine the search process \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/1909.13459)\]. Similarly, the BanditMIPS algorithm presents a randomized approach with complexity independent of dimensionality, offering a 20× speedup on real-world datasets \[[4](http://arxiv.org/abs/2212.07551)\].

Another line of research explores budgeted MIPS, where the goal is to find the best approximate top-k results under a computational budget. The Greedy-MIPS algorithm, which balances search efficiency and quality, outperforms naive approaches by 200× on large-scale datasets \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/1610.03317)\]. Additionally, wedge sampling has been proposed as a method for budgeted top-k MIPS, demonstrating competitive performance compared to diamond sampling \[[6](http://arxiv.org/abs/1908.08656)\]. These approaches highlight the growing interest in adaptive and efficient MIPS algorithms that can handle large-scale and high-dimensional data.

In the realm of approximate MIPS, quantization-based methods have also been explored. For example, the proposed quantization approach for MIPS uses multiple subspaces to approximate inner products, avoiding the need for higher-dimensional feature space augmentation \[[7](http://arxiv.org/abs/1509.01469)\]. This method has shown superior performance on datasets arising from deep neural networks \[[7](http://arxiv.org/abs/1509.01469)\]. Furthermore, clustering-based approaches, such as those using spherical $k$-means, have been introduced to address approximate MIPS by efficiently partitioning data and reducing the search space \[[10](http://arxiv.org/abs/1507.05910)\].

The category "Search Algorithm Design for MIPS" encompasses several approaches aimed at improving the efficiency and effectiveness of Maximum Inner Product Search (MIPS) in high-dimensional settings. Existing methods often face challenges such as computational inefficiency or loss of information when reducing MIPS to Euclidean nearest neighbor search \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/1610.03317)\]. Recent works like Greedy-MIPS introduce a novel algorithm that allows for budgeted search by balancing efficiency and quality, achieving significant speedups while maintaining high precision \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2503.06882)\]. Similarly, BanditMIPS leverages randomized sampling and multi-armed bandit strategies to reduce dependency on dimensionality, offering a constant-time complexity independent of the input dimension \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/1610.03317)\]. These methods highlight the need for adaptive and flexible search strategies. Additionally, diversity-aware approaches like D$k$MIPS extend beyond traditional relevance metrics by incorporating diversity objectives, ensuring a balanced trade-off between relevance and variety in search results \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/1909.13459)\]. These advancements collectively emphasize the importance of tailored algorithms that can adapt to different data characteristics and application requirements.

### <a id='category-approximate-mips-methods'></a>Approximate MIPS Methods: 11 papers found.
The category "Approximate MIPS Methods" encompasses a range of techniques aimed at efficiently solving the Maximum Inner Product Search problem, which is critical in high-dimensional machine learning and information retrieval. These methods address the computational challenges of exact MIPS by leveraging approximation strategies, such as clustering, hashing, and dimensionality reduction. For instance, clustering-based approaches partition vectors into clusters and search only a fraction of them, offering efficiency for sparse vectors \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2503.06882)\]. Hashing techniques, like Locality Sensitive Hashing (LSH), enable fast approximate MIPS by mapping vectors into hash tables, though they often require careful handling of data distributions \[[5](http://arxiv.org/abs/2309.09013)\]. Dimensionality reduction methods, such as those using random projections or codebook learning, approximate inner products by transforming vectors into lower-dimensional spaces, reducing computational overhead \[[7](http://arxiv.org/abs/1509.01469)\]. Additionally, some methods convert MIPS into cosine similarity or near neighbor search problems, leveraging asymmetric transformations or signed random projections for improved accuracy \[[14](http://arxiv.org/abs/1410.5410)\]. Budgeted settings further refine these methods by limiting computational operations, with wedge sampling showing competitive performance over diamond sampling \[[6](http://arxiv.org/abs/1908.08656)\]. Theoretical guarantees and practical implementations, such as the proposed c-AMIP with lightweight indexing and the Metric-Amphibious Graph (MAG) index, highlight ongoing efforts to balance efficiency, accuracy, and adaptability in approximate MIPS \[[9](http://arxiv.org/abs/2104.04406)\]\[[15](http://arxiv.org/abs/2012.11098)\]. These approaches collectively demonstrate the diversity and innovation in addressing the challenges of high-dimensional inner product search.

[Snippet]: «The MIPS (maximum inner product search), which finds the item with the highest inner product with a given query user, is an essential problem in the recommendation field. It is usual that e-commerce companies face situations where they want to promote and sell new or discounted items. In these situations, we have to consider a question: who are interested in the items and how to find them? This paper answers this question by addressing a new problem called reverse maximum inner product search (reverse MIPS). Given a query vector and two sets of vectors (user vectors and item vectors), the problem of reverse MIPS finds a set of user vectors whose inner product with the query vector is the maximum among the query and item vectors. Although the importance of this problem is clear, its straightforward implementation incurs a computationally expensive cost. We therefore propose Simpfer, a simple, fast, and exact algorithm for reverse MIPS. In an offline phase, Simpfer builds a simple index that maintains a lower-bound of the maximum inner product. By exploiting this index, Simpfer judges whether the query vector can have the maximum inner product or not, for a given user vector, in a constant time. Besides, our index enables filtering user vectors, which cannot have the maximum inner product with the query vector, in a batch. We theoretically demonstrate that Simpfer outperforms baselines employing state-of-the-art MIPS techniques. Furthermore, our extensive experiments on real datasets show that Simpfer is about 500–8000 times faster than the baselines.»
