## Related Works

Narrative summarization remains a challenging task for large language models (LLMs), particularly when dealing with long-form texts such as books, movies, and TV scripts, which require capturing intricate plotlines, character interactions, and thematic coherence \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2504.09071)\]. Prior work has explored various approaches to improve summarization quality, including plan-guided methods that aim to reduce hallucinations by grounding summaries to the source text \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2504.09071)\]. However, these approaches often fail to significantly improve summary quality or faithfulness, especially in complex narrative domains \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2504.09071)\]. 

Several studies have investigated the use of multi-agent systems to enhance task performance. For instance, a multi-LLM agent framework has been proposed to decompose capabilities such as task planning, tool invocation, and result summarization into separate components, each implemented by a dedicated LLM \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/2401.07324)\]. This modular approach enables individual updates and the potential use of smaller models for specific tasks, improving overall system efficiency \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/2401.07324)\]. Similarly, a framework for generating causal graphs from narrative texts has been introduced, combining LLM-based summarization with linguistically informed features to improve causal link identification \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/2504.07459)\]. This method demonstrates superior precision in identifying relationships between events and characters compared to pure LLM-based approaches \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/2504.07459)\].

In the domain of dialogue and narrative understanding, several works have focused on extracting coherent storylines from large text corpora. Narrative Trails, for example, uses semantic-level information from deep learning models to build a sparse coherence graph and extract narratives that maximize the minimum coherence of the storylines \[[5](http://arxiv.org/abs/2503.15681)\]. This method is scalable and generalizable across different narrative contexts \[[5](http://arxiv.org/abs/2503.15681)\]. Additionally, a framework for structured semantic understanding of visual narratives has been proposed, using hierarchical knowledge graphs to model narrative content across panel, event, and macro-event levels \[[4](http://arxiv.org/abs/2506.10008)\]. This approach emphasizes transparency and enables structured inference aligned with cognitive theories of event segmentation \[[4](http://arxiv.org/abs/2506.10008)\].

Another line of research has focused on improving dialogue description generation, where the goal is to describe the object or action of a conversation rather than maintaining the natural flow of dialogue \[[6](http://arxiv.org/abs/1811.00185)\]. This task has been addressed using neural attentive models that exploit interactions between utterances from different speakers, achieving more accurate and descriptive results \[[6](http://arxiv.org/abs/1811.00185)\]. 

In summary, while existing methods have made progress in narrative understanding and summarization, they often lack scalability, interpretability, or structured coherence. The introduction of multi-agent LLM frameworks, such as NexusSum, represents a promising direction for improving narrative summarization by leveraging structured pipelines and preprocessing techniques \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2504.09071)\]. ### <a id='category-plan-guided-summarization-methods'></a>Plan-Guided Summarization Methods: 1 papers found.
Plan-guided summarization methods aim to enhance the faithfulness of summaries generated by small language models (SLMs) by aligning them with the source text through fine-grained details like dates or named entities \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2504.09071)\]. These approaches typically rely on a structured plan to guide the summarization process, ensuring that generated content is grounded in the original material. However, recent research has shown that even with such planning, hallucinations can still occur, particularly in complex narrative texts \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2504.09071)\]. This is because the plans themselves may contain inaccuracies, leading to summaries that are no more faithful than those produced without planning \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2504.09071)\]. The challenge of summarizing long, intricate narratives—such as books, movies, and TV scripts—has motivated the development of alternative frameworks, such as multi-agent LLM systems that process text through structured pipelines without requiring fine-tuning \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2504.09071)\]. These systems often incorporate innovations like dialogue-to-description transformation and hierarchical summarization pipelines to improve coherence and output quality \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2504.09071)\]. While plan-guided methods have been explored for their potential to improve summarization accuracy, the findings suggest that they may not be sufficient for handling the complexity of narrative texts, highlighting the need for alternative approaches \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2504.09071)\].

### <a id='category-multi-agent-llm-frameworks'></a>Multi-Agent LLM Frameworks: 8 papers found.
The category "Multi-Agent LLM Frameworks" explores the use of multiple LLMs working collaboratively to enhance task performance across various domains. These frameworks typically decompose complex tasks into specialized roles, such as planning, tool invocation, and summarization, allowing each LLM to focus on a specific capability \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/2401.07324)\]. This modular approach improves task execution by enabling individual updates and the use of smaller models for specific functions \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/2401.07324)\]. For instance, in healthcare, MedAide introduces a multi-agent collaboration framework that enables intent-aware information fusion and coordinated reasoning, reducing hallucinations and improving clinical decision-making \[[9](http://arxiv.org/abs/2410.12532)\]. Similarly, in finance, TradingAgents proposes a stock trading framework where LLM-powered agents assume specialized roles like fundamental analysts and traders, simulating collaborative dynamics seen in real-world trading firms \[[11](http://arxiv.org/abs/2412.20138)\]. In text classification, PRINCIPLE-BASED PROMPTING leverages multiple agents to generate and refine principles for better classification performance, outperforming zero-shot and few-shot prompting methods \[[8](http://arxiv.org/abs/2502.07165)\]. These frameworks also emphasize the importance of coordination mechanisms, such as payload referencing and dynamic role rotation, to enhance communication and task completion \[[6](http://arxiv.org/abs/1811.00185)\]. Additionally, in feature engineering, TCTO employs a collaborative multi-agent reinforcement learning approach to automate feature transformations through graph-driven path optimization, improving the efficiency and effectiveness of downstream machine learning tasks \[[18](http://arxiv.org/abs/2504.17355)\]. Overall, these works highlight the potential of multi-agent LLM frameworks to address complex tasks by leveraging distributed expertise and adaptive collaboration strategies.
