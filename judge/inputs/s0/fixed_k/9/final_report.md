## Related Works

Quantitative susceptibility mapping (QSM) has emerged as a powerful tool for characterizing pathologies in multiple sclerosis (MS), particularly for distinguishing between rim and non-rim lesions \[[20](http://arxiv.org/abs/2211.04764)\]. Rim lesions, surrounded by paramagnetic iron deposits, are an emerging biomarker for MS and are associated with increased disability \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/2312.16835)\]. However, manual identification and segmentation of rim lesions on QSM images are time-consuming and prone to inter-rater variability \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2412.10492)\]. Deep learning approaches have been proposed to automate this process, but existing methods often lack the ability to provide accurate rim segmentation for quantitative analysis \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2412.10492)\]. 

Several studies have focused on improving lesion segmentation in MS using deep learning techniques. For example, QSM-RimNet was introduced to detect PRLs but lacks rim segmentation capabilities \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2412.10492)\]. In contrast, QSM-RimDS was developed as a U-Net-based method that provides joint PRL detection and rim segmentation using T2-weighted FLAIR lesion masks \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2412.10492)\]. This method demonstrated a mean Dice similarity coefficient (DSC) of 0.57 with moderate to high agreement against expert segmentation \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2412.10492)\]. Additionally, QSM-RimDS showed improved detection performance with a mean area under the curve (AUC) of 0.754, surpassing QSM-RimNet's AUC of 0.514 \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2412.10492)\]. 

The challenge of class imbalance in rim lesion detection has also been addressed in the literature. RimSet, a method combining unsupervised segmentation and radiomic measurements, achieved a DSC of 78.7% against ground truth and demonstrated superior performance in detecting rim+ lesions \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/2312.16835)\]. Similarly, the use of synthetic data to address class imbalance has been explored. A study proposed a foreground-based generative method to create synthetic lesions on healthy images, which improved segmentation performance when used as data augmentation \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/2208.02135)\]. This approach was validated on MS brain images and showed better results than traditional data augmentation methods \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/2208.02135)\]. 

In addition to lesion detection, methods have been developed to segment and quantify lesion activity over time. Attention-guided two-path CNNs have been proposed to segment lesion activity between two time points, showing improved performance over classic methods \[[4](http://arxiv.org/abs/2008.02001)\]. These models utilize attention mechanisms to suppress old, irrelevant lesions and improve lesion-wise detection accuracy \[[4](http://arxiv.org/abs/2008.02001)\]. Similarly, a 4D deep learning approach was introduced to process a history of MRI volumes, enabling better performance in detecting new and enlarging lesions \[[18](http://arxiv.org/abs/2004.09216)\].

### <a id='category-quantitative-susceptibility-mapping-(qsm)-techniques'></a>Quantitative Susceptibility Mapping (QSM) Techniques: 3 papers found.
Quantitative Susceptibility Mapping (QSM) has emerged as a critical tool for detecting and characterizing pathologies involving magnetic susceptibility variations, such as iron and calcium deposits, which are associated with neurological disorders like multiple sclerosis, Parkinson's disease, and Alzheimer's \[[20](http://arxiv.org/abs/2211.04764)\]. QSM reconstructs susceptibility maps from MRI phase data, enabling the identification of subtle changes in tissue properties that correlate with disease progression \[[28](http://arxiv.org/abs/2008.05024)\]. However, high-resolution QSM, while beneficial for detailed analysis, often suffers from reduced signal-to-noise ratio (SNR), which can hinder diagnostic accuracy \[[8](http://arxiv.org/abs/2404.19309)\]. To address this, advanced denoising techniques such as Marchenko-Pastur Principal Component Analysis (MP-PCA) have been applied to enhance the quality of T2* and R2* maps, leading to improved SNR and more reliable susceptibility estimates \[[8](http://arxiv.org/abs/2404.19309)\]. Additionally, deep learning approaches, including Learned Proximal Convolutional Neural Networks (LP-CNN), have been introduced to solve the ill-posed dipole inversion problem in QSM, offering state-of-the-art reconstruction results while maintaining interpretability \[[28](http://arxiv.org/abs/2008.05024)\]. These innovations are particularly relevant for the detection of paramagnetic rim lesions (PRLs) in multiple sclerosis, where susceptibility maps provide essential contrast to differentiate between lesion types \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2412.10492)\]. The integration of synthetic data generation and denoising techniques further enhances classifier performance in imbalanced datasets, improving the detection and segmentation of rare PRLs \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2412.10492)\].
