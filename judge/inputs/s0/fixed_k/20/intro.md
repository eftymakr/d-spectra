## Related Works

Deep-unrolling and plug-and-play (PnP) approaches have emerged as leading methods for solving inverse problems in imaging, particularly in single-pixel imaging (SPI) \[[24](http://arxiv.org/abs/2310.16869)\]. PnP methods are iterative algorithms that implicitly perform regularization using pre-trained deep denoisers \[[4](http://arxiv.org/abs/2410.02423)\]. These methods are flexible for varying compression ratios (CRs) but often suffer from limitations in reconstruction accuracy and speed \[[24](http://arxiv.org/abs/2310.16869)\]. On the other hand, unrolling approaches, which transform truncated iterative optimization processes into end-to-end trainable neural networks, typically achieve better accuracy and faster inference \[[24](http://arxiv.org/abs/2310.16869)\]. However, they require fine-tuning or retraining when CR changes \[[24](http://arxiv.org/abs/2310.16869)\].

Several studies have explored the use of unrolling for image restoration and reconstruction. For instance, the Deep Unrolling for Blind Deblurring (DUBLID) method was proposed to connect iterative algorithms like total-variation regularization to neural network architectures, achieving significant performance gains while maintaining interpretability \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/1902.03493)\]. Similarly, the Dynamic Proximal Unrolling Network (DPUNet) was introduced to handle multiple compressive imaging modalities under varying sampling ratios and noise levels using a single trained model \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2107.11007)\]. This approach dynamically adjusts parameters at inference time to adapt to different imaging settings \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2107.11007)\].

The convergence properties of unrolled networks have also been a focus of research. For example, the Convergent Unrolled Network based on the Half-Quadratic Splitting (HQS) algorithm was developed to maintain the convergence properties of the underlying iterative algorithm while improving performance \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/2402.12872)\]. This work demonstrated that the proposed unrolled network converges as the number of layers increases, and it was validated on benchmark datasets \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/2402.12872)\].

In addition, the use of proximal operators in unrolling has been explored. The Generalized Deep Learning-based Proximal Gradient Descent for MR Reconstruction proposed a network that incorporates the forward model into the reconstruction process, improving generalizability across different MR acquisition settings \[[7](http://arxiv.org/abs/2211.16881)\]. Similarly, the Proximal Alternating Direction Network was introduced to ensure global convergence of the unrolled deep model, even when task information is partially available \[[8](http://arxiv.org/abs/1711.07653)\].

The integration of deep learning with traditional iterative algorithms has also been applied to other imaging tasks. For example, the DUS-Net method proposed a novel soft thresholding with channel attention (AST) operator to improve dynamic MR imaging by learning thresholds for each channel \[[11](http://arxiv.org/abs/2307.09818)\]. The JotlasNet method further advanced dynamic MRI reconstruction by jointly utilizing tensor low-rank and attention-based sparse priors \[[17](http://arxiv.org/abs/2502.11749)\].
