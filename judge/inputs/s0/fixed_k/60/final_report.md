## Related Works

Calibration of predictive models has been a central topic in machine learning, particularly for ensuring that predicted probabilities reflect the true likelihood of events. Traditional approaches to calibration often rely on predefined transformation functions, such as Platt Scaling or isotonic regression, which are order-preserving but limited in expressiveness \[[13](http://arxiv.org/abs/1710.08901)\]. These methods typically assume a fixed functional form, such as piece-wise linear, which may not be sufficient for complex calibration tasks \[[13](http://arxiv.org/abs/1710.08901)\]. In contrast, recent work has explored the use of neural networks to learn more flexible and expressive calibration functions \[[11](http://arxiv.org/abs/2203.09278)\]. 

The use of monotonic neural networks for calibration has been explored in several studies. For example, the Unconstrained Monotonic Neural Network (UMNN) has been proposed as a flexible architecture that can learn arbitrary monotonic functions while preserving the ordering of predictions \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/1908.05164)\]. This property is crucial for calibration, as it ensures that the calibrated predictions maintain the relative order of the original outputs. The UMNN has been applied in distributional reinforcement learning, where it enables the efficient modeling of different representations of the random return distribution \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/2106.03228)\]. This work highlights the potential of monotonic neural networks to improve the flexibility and expressiveness of calibration methods.

In the context of ranking systems, the importance of both calibration and ranking quality has been emphasized. The Self-Boosted framework for Calibrated Ranking (SBCR) addresses the limitations of existing multi-objective methods by introducing a calibration module that decouples the point loss and ranking loss \[[4](http://arxiv.org/abs/2406.08010)\]. This approach allows for a more optimal trade-off between calibration and ranking performance. Additionally, the work on improving model calibration with accuracy versus uncertainty optimization introduces a differentiable accuracy versus uncertainty calibration (AvUC) loss function, which enables models to learn well-calibrated uncertainties while maintaining high accuracy \[[5](http://arxiv.org/abs/2012.07923)\]. This method has been shown to outperform existing calibration techniques on large-scale image classification tasks under distributional shift.

Furthermore, the concept of self-calibrating conformal prediction has been introduced, combining Venn-Abers calibration with conformal prediction to deliver both calibrated point predictions and prediction intervals with finite-sample validity \[[7](http://arxiv.org/abs/2402.07307)\]. This approach extends the original Venn-Abers procedure to regression, allowing for the construction of conditionally valid prediction intervals based on calibrated predictions. The results show that model calibration can significantly improve interval efficiency.

### <a id='category-monotonic-neural-networks-for-calibration'></a>Monotonic Neural Networks for Calibration: 2 papers found.
Monotonic neural networks have emerged as a promising approach for modeling invertible transformations and improving the calibration of predictions, particularly in ranking tasks where both relative order and absolute accuracy are important \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/1908.05164)\]. These networks enforce monotonicity by ensuring the positivity of the network's output, allowing for flexible and powerful function approximation while maintaining invertibility \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/1908.05164)\]. This property has been leveraged in the development of the Unconstrained Monotonic Neural Network (UMNN) architecture, which has been applied to density estimation and variational inference \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/1908.05164)\]. In the context of reinforcement learning, UMNNs have also been used to model the complete probability distribution of the random return, enabling the efficient decoupling of the function approximator from the probability metric \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/2106.03228)\]. This has led to the creation of the Unconstrained Monotonic Deep Q-Network (UMDQN), a novel distributional RL algorithm capable of learning multiple representations of the return distribution, including the probability density function (PDF), cumulative distribution function (CDF), and quantile function (QF) \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/2106.03228)\]. The use of UMNNs in these applications highlights their versatility and effectiveness in scenarios requiring monotonic transformations and accurate calibration of predictions. The flexibility of UMNNs, combined with the introduction of a novel loss function such as the Smooth Calibration Loss (SCLoss), has further enhanced their performance in complex calibration tasks \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/1908.05164)\].

[Snippet]: «According to the Probability Ranking Principle (PRP), ranking documents in decreasing order of their probability of relevance leads to an optimal document ranking for ad-hoc retrieval. The PRP holds when two conditions are met: [C1] the models are well calibrated, and, [C2] the probabilities of relevance are reported with certainty. We know however that deep neural networks (DNNs) are often not well calibrated and have several sources of uncertainty, and thus [C1] and [C2] might not be satisfied by neural rankers. Given the success of neural Learning to Rank (LTR) approaches—and here, especially BERT-based approaches—we first analyze under which circumstances deterministic neural rankers are calibrated for conversational search problems. Then, motivated by our findings we use two techniques to model the uncertainty of neural rankers leading to the proposed stochastic rankers, which output a predictive distribution of relevance as opposed to point estimates. Our experimental results on the ad-hoc retrieval task of conversation response ranking reveal that (i) BERT-based rankers are not robustly calibrated and that stochastic BERT-based rankers yield better calibration; and (ii) uncertainty estimation is beneficial for both risk-aware neural ranking, i.e. taking into account the uncertainty when ranking documents, and for predicting unanswerable conversational contexts.»
