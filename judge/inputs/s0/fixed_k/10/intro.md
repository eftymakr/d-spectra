## Related Works

Multi-token prediction (MTP) has emerged as a promising pre-training objective for language models, offering potential improvements in downstream performance, inference speed, and training efficiency compared to traditional next-token prediction (NTP) \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/2404.19737)\]. Unlike NTP, which predicts one token at a time, MTP predicts multiple future tokens simultaneously using multiple prediction heads \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/2404.19737)\]. This approach has been shown to enhance generative capabilities, particularly for large models, by enabling more efficient and effective training strategies \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/2404.19737)\]. However, smaller language models (SLMs) have struggled to fully leverage the benefits of MTP, highlighting the need for specialized training strategies \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2505.17505)\].

Curriculum learning has been explored as a means to address these challenges. Curriculum learning involves structuring the training process to gradually increase task complexity, which has been successfully applied in computer vision and machine translation \[[7](http://arxiv.org/abs/2108.02170)\]. In the context of MTP, two curriculum learning variants have been proposed: a forward curriculum, which transitions from NTP to MTP, and a reverse curriculum, which does the opposite \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2505.17505)\]. The forward curriculum enables SLMs to better leverage the MTP objective during pre-training, improving downstream NTP performance and generative output quality while retaining the benefits of self-speculative decoding \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2505.17505)\]. In contrast, the reverse curriculum achieves stronger NTP performance and output quality but fails to provide any self-speculative decoding benefits \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2505.17505)\].

The benefits of MTP have also been demonstrated in other domains. For instance, multi-token prediction has been shown to improve the development of induction heads and algorithmic reasoning capabilities in small models \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/2404.19737)\]. Additionally, models trained with multi-token prediction can achieve up to 3 times faster inference speeds, even with large batch sizes \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/2404.19737)\]. This efficiency gain is attributed to the ability of MTP to reduce the number of forward passes required during inference \[[10](http://arxiv.org/abs/2405.00888)\].

Furthermore, multi-token prediction has been extended to other modalities, such as vision-language pre-training, where it has been used to improve feature extraction and model architecture design \[[6](http://arxiv.org/abs/2202.09061)\]. In speech processing, prompting techniques have been adapted to leverage pre-trained language models for tasks such as speech classification and generation \[[12](http://arxiv.org/abs/2408.13040)\]. These methods demonstrate the versatility of multi-token prediction across different domains.

In addition, curriculum learning has been applied to reinforcement learning, where it has been used to shape exploration by presenting agents with increasingly complex tasks \[[9](http://arxiv.org/abs/1901.11478)\]. This approach has been shown to improve initial performance, reduce suboptimal actions, and discover better policies \[[9](http://arxiv.org/abs/1901.11478)\].
