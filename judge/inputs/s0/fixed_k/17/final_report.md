## Related Works

Semi-supervised learning (SSL) has emerged as a powerful paradigm for improving model generalization by leveraging unlabeled data alongside a small set of labeled samples \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2311.10572)\]. However, conventional SSL methods assume that both labeled and unlabeled data share the same class distribution, which is often violated in practice due to the presence of outliers—unlabeled data from unknown classes \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/2303.12091)\]. These outliers are typically treated as noise, which significantly degrades SSL performance \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/2401.07551)\]. To address this challenge, numerous open-set SSL approaches have been proposed, aiming to distinguish between inliers and outliers while maintaining robust classification performance \[[4](http://arxiv.org/abs/2308.13168)\].

Early methods, such as FixMatch, primarily rely on consistency regularization, enforcing consistency between predictions for labeled and unlabeled data \[[5](http://arxiv.org/abs/2105.14148)\]. However, these methods struggle in open-set scenarios where unlabeled data contain out-of-distribution samples \[[6](http://arxiv.org/abs/2107.08943)\]. To mitigate this, methods like OpenMatch integrate novelty detection using one-vs-all classifiers to identify inliers and outliers \[[7](http://arxiv.org/abs/2405.06979)\]. Similarly, OpenCoS leverages self-supervised contrastive learning to identify out-of-class samples and utilize them effectively in training \[[8](http://arxiv.org/abs/2002.06815)\]. These approaches demonstrate that handling open-set data is critical for improving SSL performance in real-world settings \[[9](http://arxiv.org/abs/2301.10127)\].

More recently, methods such as SSB and ANEDL have introduced advanced strategies to enhance open-set SSL. SSB incorporates pseudo-negative mining and non-linear transformations to separate inlier and outlier features, improving both classification and outlier detection \[[10](http://arxiv.org/abs/2407.05370)\]. ANEDL, on the other hand, introduces adaptive negative optimization, tailoring the model to unlabeled data containing both inliers and outliers \[[11](http://arxiv.org/abs/2207.13856)\]. These methods highlight the importance of designing models that can effectively handle the presence of outliers while maintaining classification accuracy \[[12](http://arxiv.org/abs/2403.10658)\].

In addition to outlier detection, some works focus on improving robustness by jointly utilizing inliers and outliers during training. IOMatch, for instance, proposes a framework that jointly utilizes both types of data, avoiding the performance degradation caused by unreliable outlier detection \[[13](http://arxiv.org/abs/2201.09196)\]. Similarly, OpenMatch introduces an open-set soft-consistency regularization loss to enhance the smoothness of the OVA-classifier, improving outlier detection \[[14](http://arxiv.org/abs/1711.07476)\]. These methods underscore the value of leveraging all available data, even when distinguishing between inliers and outliers is challenging \[[15](http://arxiv.org/abs/2201.06247)\].
