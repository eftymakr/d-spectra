## Related Works

Conversational recommendation systems (CRSs) have gained significant attention for their ability to engage users in multi-turn dialogues to infer preferences and provide personalized recommendations \[[16](http://arxiv.org/abs/1806.03277)\]. However, capturing user preferences from conversations remains a fundamental challenge due to the complexity and multifaceted nature of user intent \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2304.13874)\]. Existing approaches often rely on handcrafted reward functions or fail to effectively model the dynamic and evolving nature of user preferences \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/2310.20109)\]. Additionally, frequent user involvement in the interaction process can lead to a degraded user experience \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2304.13874)\].

To address these limitations, several works have explored the use of simulated users and reinforcement learning to improve the efficiency and effectiveness of CRSs. For instance, \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2304.13874)\] proposes a user simulator-based framework for multi-turn interactions with mixed-initiative conversational search systems, demonstrating that user feedback can significantly improve retrieval performance. Similarly, \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/2310.20109)\] introduces a multi-objective intrinsic reward learning approach for CRSs, where intrinsic rewards are learned from user interactions to guide policy optimization. This aligns with our approach of using a generative reward model to simulate user feedback and improve recommendation accuracy.

Another key challenge in CRSs is the integration of user preferences with external knowledge. \[[14](http://arxiv.org/abs/2112.08140)\] proposes a model that leverages item metadata and semantic alignment with dialog context to enhance recommendation quality. This work highlights the importance of incorporating contextual information in recommendation systems, which is also central to our approach. Additionally, \[[10](http://arxiv.org/abs/2412.02415)\] introduces a Transformer-based sequential modeling approach to capture sequential dependencies in conversations, further demonstrating the need for modeling temporal and contextual patterns in CRSs.

To ensure seamless integration of feedback mechanisms, \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2304.13874)\] and \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/2310.20109)\] both emphasize the importance of designing feedback actions that align with the conversational flow. Our work extends this by introducing two types of feedback actions—generative item scoring and attribute-based item critique—that provide both coarse-grained and fine-grained feedback. These actions are unified into an instruction-based format, enabling the development of a unified simulated user through instruction tuning \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2304.13874)\].

Efficiency and effectiveness in multi-turn interactions are also critical considerations. \[[16](http://arxiv.org/abs/1806.03277)\] proposes a deep reinforcement learning framework that integrates dialog systems and recommender systems to optimize a per-session utility function. This approach is similar to our method, which uses beam search to balance effectiveness and efficiency during interaction. Furthermore, \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2304.13874)\] shows that increasing the number of feedback rounds leads to consistent improvements in retrieval performance, supporting the value of iterative feedback mechanisms in CRSs.
