## Related Works

The integration of knowledge graphs (KGs) with large language models (LLMs) to enhance reasoning capabilities in claim verification has gained significant attention in recent research. While KGs offer structured, semantically rich representations suitable for reasoning, most existing claim verification methods rely on unstructured text corpora, limiting their ability to effectively leverage KGs \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2406.06621)\]. This limitation is particularly evident in the inability of modern LLMs to perform multi-step modular pipelines and reason over KGs without adaptation \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2406.06621)\]. 

Several works have explored the use of KGs to improve the accuracy and explainability of claim verification. For example, the FOLK approach leverages first-order logic (FOL) to guide reasoning over knowledge-grounded question-and-answer pairs, enabling the verification of complex claims without the need for annotated evidence \[[9](http://arxiv.org/abs/2310.05253)\]. Similarly, the CRAVE framework utilizes conflicting rationales generated by LLMs to provide explainable and accurate claim verification, incorporating evidence retrieval and judgment modules \[[10](http://arxiv.org/abs/2504.14905)\]. These methods highlight the potential of combining structured knowledge from KGs with the reasoning capabilities of LLMs.

In addition, the use of KGs to enhance LLMs has been explored in various contexts. The KELM framework proposes a knowledge-aware language model framework based on fine-tuning, enabling the integration of KGs into pre-trained language models to improve machine reading comprehension \[[7](http://arxiv.org/abs/2109.04223)\]. The LangGSL framework further integrates LLMs with graph structure learning models to jointly enhance both node feature and graph structure learning, demonstrating robustness against noise \[[11](http://arxiv.org/abs/2410.12096)\]. These approaches underscore the complementary strengths of LLMs and KGs in reasoning tasks.

Moreover, the use of KGs for zero-shot and few-shot learning has been extensively studied. The KG-HTC framework integrates KGs with LLMs to address the challenges of hierarchical text classification, particularly in large label spaces and long-tail distributions \[[15](http://arxiv.org/abs/2505.05583)\]. The FlexKBQA framework leverages LLMs as program translators to address the few-shot KBQA task, demonstrating the flexibility of combining structured knowledge with LLMs \[[21](http://arxiv.org/abs/2308.12060)\]. These works highlight the versatility of integrating KGs with LLMs across different tasks.

The Validator framework computes confidence scores for KG triples by comparing features across different knowledge sources, ensuring semantic correctness \[[17](http://arxiv.org/abs/2104.12622)\].
