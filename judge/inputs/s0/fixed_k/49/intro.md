## Related Works

Neural retrieval models have shown significant promise in Web search, particularly in their ability to learn from large-scale document collections and generate relevant results \[[16](http://arxiv.org/abs/2403.13468)\]. However, training these models typically requires substantial amounts of labeled query-document pairs, which are expensive to obtain \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2412.15453)\]. With the availability of Web document collections like ClueWeb22, synthetic queries generated by large language models have emerged as a scalable alternative to labeled data \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2412.15453)\]. Despite this, synthetic training queries often vary in quality, leading to suboptimal downstream retrieval performance \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2412.15453)\]. 

Existing methods typically filter out noisy query-document pairs based on signals from an external re-ranker \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2412.15453)\]. This approach, while effective in some cases, introduces additional complexity and may not fully address the underlying issues of query quality \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2412.15453)\]. In contrast, we propose a framework that leverages Direct Preference Optimization (DPO) to integrate ranking signals directly into the query generation process, aiming to optimize the model towards generating high-quality queries that maximize downstream retrieval effectiveness \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2412.15453)\]. 

DPO has been widely adopted for preference alignment of Large Language Models (LLMs) due to its simplicity and effectiveness \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/2410.04350)\]. However, DPO is derived as a bandit problem in which the whole response is treated as a single arm, ignoring the importance differences between tokens, which may affect optimization efficiency \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/2410.04350)\]. To address this, TIS-DPO was proposed, which assigns importance weights to each token based on its reward, enabling more efficient and accurate optimization \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/2410.04350)\]. This approach aligns with our framework's goal of improving query quality by incorporating ranking signals directly into the generation process.

Additionally, the use of synthetic queries for training retrieval models has been explored in various contexts. For example, synthetic query generation for privacy-preserving deep retrieval systems using differentially private language models has been proposed to ensure query privacy while maintaining retrieval quality \[[6](http://arxiv.org/abs/2305.05973)\]. This work highlights the importance of generating high-quality synthetic queries, which is a key focus of our framework.

Furthermore, the challenge of improving retrievability in search systems has been addressed through methods like CtrlQGen, which generates diverse synthetic queries to reduce retrievability bias \[[12](http://arxiv.org/abs/2303.11648)\]. This approach is relevant to our work as it also focuses on enhancing the quality of synthetic queries used for training.

In summary, while existing methods rely on external re-rankers to filter noisy query-document pairs, our framework integrates ranking signals directly into the query generation process using DPO, aiming to improve the quality of synthetic queries and, consequently, downstream retrieval performance \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2412.15453)\].
