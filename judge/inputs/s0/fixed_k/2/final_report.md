## Related Works

Nonverbal communication plays a crucial role in human interaction, encompassing gestures, facial expressions, and body language that convey intent and emotion \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/1811.09362)\]. Existing large language models (LLMs) often lack the ability to effectively integrate these nonverbal elements, limiting their capacity to create immersive conversational experiences \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/1811.09362)\]. This limitation has been noted in several studies that emphasize the importance of nonverbal cues in social and emotional understanding \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/2502.16671)\]. For instance, \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/1712.04314)\] highlights the dynamic nature of nonverbal behaviors and their impact on the interpretation of language, suggesting that models should account for these variations to better align with human communication patterns.

Recent efforts have aimed to address this gap by incorporating nonverbal data into AI models. \[[4](http://arxiv.org/abs/2404.07214)\] introduces MimeQA, a dataset of mime videos designed to evaluate nonverbal social reasoning capabilities. This dataset has revealed that current video large language models (VideoLLMs) struggle with nonverbal understanding, achieving only 20-30% accuracy, while humans score 86% \[[4](http://arxiv.org/abs/2404.07214)\]. This underscores the need for models that can effectively interpret and generate nonverbal cues.

In addition to datasets, several studies have explored the integration of nonverbal cues into AI systems. \[[5](http://arxiv.org/abs/2410.21652)\] discusses the use of audio and haptics to represent nonverbal behaviors in virtual reality (VR) for blind and low vision individuals, demonstrating the effectiveness of such cues in supporting conversations \[[5](http://arxiv.org/abs/2410.21652)\]. Similarly, \[[6](http://arxiv.org/abs/2104.02841)\] presents a model that learns triadic belief dynamics from videos, incorporating nonverbal cues such as gaze, human poses, and gestures to infer agents’ mental states \[[6](http://arxiv.org/abs/2104.02841)\].

The integration of nonverbal data into AI models has also been explored in the context of multimodal communication. \[[7](http://arxiv.org/abs/2407.17209)\] introduces a computational model for analyzing nonverbal social behavior in educational settings, using multimodal cues such as facial expressions and spatial dynamics to assess nonverbal immediacy \[[7](http://arxiv.org/abs/2407.17209)\]. This work highlights the potential of nonverbal cues in educational contexts, suggesting that they can provide valuable insights into teaching effectiveness.

Furthermore, \[[8](http://arxiv.org/abs/2207.10574)\] surveys the use of nonverbal cues in co-located human-human interaction analysis, emphasizing the importance of these cues in understanding social traits, roles, and interaction dynamics \[[8](http://arxiv.org/abs/2207.10574)\]. These advancements align with the broader goal of creating more immersive conversational experiences by incorporating nonverbal cues, as seen in the development of models like MARS, which leverages a large-scale dataset of annotated videos to train on both text and nonverbal representations \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/1712.04314)\].
