## Related Works

Generative retrieval (GR) has gained significant attention as a promising paradigm in information retrieval (IR), offering end-to-end modeling of retrieval tasks by generating document identifiers (docids) directly from queries \[[4](http://arxiv.org/abs/2304.04171)\]. However, most existing GR models are primarily evaluated on static document collections, with limited studies on their performance in dynamic corpora where document sets evolve continuously \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2305.18952)\]. This section reviews related works that address the challenges of GR in dynamic environments, the design of docids, and the efficiency-performance trade-offs in retrieval systems.

Early works in GR focused on static document collections, assuming a fixed set of documents during training and inference \[[4](http://arxiv.org/abs/2304.04171)\]. However, in practical scenarios, document collections are often dynamic, requiring models to adapt to new documents while preserving retrieval accuracy \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/2308.14968)\]. This challenge has led to the development of continual learning approaches for GR, such as the CLEVER model, which enables incremental indexing of new documents while maintaining query performance \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/2308.14968)\]. These methods aim to address the limitations of static indexing, which is computationally expensive and less adaptable to evolving corpora \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/2408.02152)\].

The design of docids plays a critical role in GR performance. Traditional approaches use fixed rule-based docids, such as document titles or clustered BERT embeddings, which often fail to capture semantic information effectively \[[4](http://arxiv.org/abs/2304.04171)\]. Recent work proposes GenRet, a method that learns to tokenize documents into short discrete representations (docids) using a discrete auto-encoding approach, enabling fully end-to-end modeling of retrieval tasks \[[4](http://arxiv.org/abs/2304.04171)\]. This approach improves semantic alignment and generalization to unseen documents, as observed in experiments on benchmark datasets \[[4](http://arxiv.org/abs/2304.04171)\].

The use of text-based docids has shown superior generalization to unseen documents compared to numeric-based docids, which are more efficient but less effective in dynamic settings \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2305.18952)\]. This is attributed to the fine-grained design of text-based docids, which aligns better with the semantic knowledge of large language models (LLMs) \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2305.18952)\]. In contrast, numeric-based docids tend to overfit to the initial document set, leading to performance degradation in dynamic corpora \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2305.18952)\]. This overfitting issue is further exacerbated by the lack of lexical diversity in numeric-based docids, which limits their adaptability to new documents \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2305.18952)\].

To address these limitations, recent work proposes a multi-docid design that combines the efficiency of numeric-based docids with the effectiveness of text-based docids \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2305.18952)\]. This hybrid approach aims to improve retrieval performance in dynamic environments without requiring additional retraining \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2305.18952)\]. ### <a id='category-empirical-evidence-for-gr-in-dynamic-settings'></a>Empirical Evidence for GR in Dynamic Settings: 5 papers found.
In the context of dynamic information retrieval, Generative Retrieval (GR) has been shown to offer significant advantages over traditional methods like Dual Encoders (DE) and BM25, particularly in adapting to evolving document collections \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2305.18952)\]. GR models, which leverage autoregressive language models, demonstrate better adaptability to changing knowledge, with performance improvements of 4–11% on the StreamingQA benchmark \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2305.18952)\]. This adaptability is attributed to their ability to incorporate temporal information and maintain robustness in dynamic settings \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2305.18952)\]. Additionally, GR models with text-based docids exhibit superior generalization to unseen documents and perform well in dynamic corpora, surpassing BM25 and even matching dense retrieval methods \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2305.18952)\]. The effectiveness of text-based docids is linked to their semantic alignment with pretrained language models, fine-grained design, and high lexical diversity \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2305.18952)\]. In contrast, numeric-based docids, while efficient, suffer from performance degradation in dynamic settings due to overfitting on the initial document set \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2305.18952)\]. This highlights the trade-off between efficiency and adaptability in GR models. Furthermore, recent work has explored dynamic retrieval frameworks, such as the Tree of Reviews (ToR), which address challenges in multi-hop question answering by dynamically managing retrieval paths and reducing error propagation \[[14](http://arxiv.org/abs/2404.14464)\]. These studies collectively underscore the importance of docid design and dynamic adaptation in improving GR performance in real-world, evolving document collections.

### <a id='category-generative-retrieval-(gr)-methodology'></a>Generative Retrieval (GR) Methodology: 7 papers found.
Generative retrieval (GR) has been increasingly explored as an alternative to traditional retrieval methods, with recent works focusing on improving efficiency and adaptability to dynamic corpora \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/2408.02152)\]. Existing GR methods often rely on training-based indexing, which fine-tunes models to memorize associations between queries and document identifiers (docids), but this approach is costly and limits adaptability \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/2408.02152)\]. To address these limitations, few-shot indexing-based GR frameworks have been proposed, enabling retrieval without training by prompting large language models (LLMs) to generate docids for a corpus \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/2408.02152)\]. Additionally, methods like GenRet introduce end-to-end learning for document tokenization, generating semantic docids through auto-encoding frameworks, which improves performance on unseen documents \[[4](http://arxiv.org/abs/2304.04171)\]. Another approach, direct document relevance optimization (DDRO), tackles token-level misalignment by aligning docid generation with document-level relevance through pairwise ranking, outperforming reinforcement learning-based methods \[[5](http://arxiv.org/abs/2504.05181)\]. Furthermore, research has highlighted the importance of docid design, showing that text-based docids, which align better with LLMs' pretrained knowledge and offer fine-grained representation, generally outperform numeric-based docids in dynamic settings \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/2408.02152)\]. These findings underscore the ongoing efforts to enhance GR methods for real-world applications, particularly in handling evolving document collections \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/2408.02152)\].

### <a id='category-docid-design-and-performance'></a>DocID Design and Performance: 1 papers found.
Context: 
	Document 1: [Category]: «DocID Design and Performance»
[Snippet]: «Recently, generative retrieval has emerged as a promising alternative to the traditional retrieval paradigms. It assigns each document a unique identifier, known as the DocID, and employs a generative model to directly generate the relevant DocID for the input query. A common choice for the DocID is one or several natural language sequences, e.g. the title, synthetic queries, or n-grams, so that the pre-trained knowledge of the generative model can be effectively utilized. However, a sequence is generated token by token, where only the most likely candidates are kept and the rest are pruned at each decoding step, thus, retrieval fails if any token within the relevant DocID is falsely pruned. What's worse, during decoding, the model can only perceive preceding tokens in the DocID while being blind to subsequent ones, hence is prone to make such errors. To address this problem, we present a novel framework for generative retrieval, dubbed Term-Set Generation (TSGen). Instead of sequences, we use a set of terms as the DocID. The terms are selected based on learned weights from relevance signals, so that they concisely summarize the document's semantics and distinguish it from others. On top of the term-set DocID, we propose a permutation-invariant decoding algorithm, with which the term set can be generated in any permutation yet will always lead to the corresponding document. Remarkably, TSGen perceives all valid terms rather than only the preceding ones at each decoding step. Given the constant decoding space, it can make more reliable decisions due to the broader perspective. TSGen is also resilient to errors: the relevant DocID will not be falsely pruned as long as the decoded term belongs to it. Moreover, TSGen can explore the optimal decoding permutation of the term set on its own, which further improves the likelihood of generating the relevant DocID. Lastly, we design an iterative optimization procedure to incentivize the model to generate the relevant term set in its favorable permutation. We conduct extensive experiments on popular benchmarks of generative retrieval, which validate the effectiveness, the generalizability, the scalability, and the efficiency of TSGen.»
