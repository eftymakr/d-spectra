## Related Works

Deploying large language models (LLMs) on edge devices has garnered significant attention due to its potential to enable real-time, privacy-preserving AI applications \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2505.03745)\]. However, the resource constraints of edge devices, including limited storage, memory, and power, pose substantial challenges to deploying LLM-powered applications \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2505.03745)\]. Prior work has focused on optimizing both the algorithmic and hardware aspects of LLM deployment to address these limitations. 

Several studies have explored algorithmic optimizations for LLM inference, such as pruning, quantization, and activation sparsity \[[4](http://arxiv.org/abs/2412.12178)\]. For instance, activation sparsity methods have been shown to reduce memory and computational requirements while maintaining model accuracy \[[4](http://arxiv.org/abs/2412.12178)\]. These techniques are particularly beneficial for edge deployment, as they allow for significant reductions in model size and inference latency \[[4](http://arxiv.org/abs/2412.12178)\]. Additionally, hardware-aware pruning methods have been proposed to further enhance the efficiency of LLMs on resource-constrained devices \[[14](http://arxiv.org/abs/2408.14055)\]. 

On the hardware front, FPGA-based accelerators have emerged as a promising solution for LLM inference due to their reconfigurability and energy efficiency \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/2408.00462)\]. For example, the AccLLM framework integrates algorithmic optimizations with a dedicated FPGA-based accelerator, achieving a 4.07× improvement in energy efficiency compared to state-of-the-art approaches \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2505.03745)\]. Similarly, the SECDA-LLM platform streamlines the design of FPGA-based LLM accelerators by leveraging the SECDA methodology, resulting in a 11× reduction in latency for the TinyLlama model \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/2408.00462)\]. These hardware solutions demonstrate the potential of FPGA-based accelerators to enable efficient LLM inference on edge devices.

Moreover, the integration of hardware and software co-design methodologies has been emphasized in recent work to further optimize LLM deployment \[[12](http://arxiv.org/abs/2110.00478)\]. The SECDA framework, for instance, combines SystemC simulation with hardware execution to reduce design time and improve the performance of DNN accelerators on edge devices \[[12](http://arxiv.org/abs/2110.00478)\]. This approach enables faster development cycles and more efficient resource utilization, which are critical for deploying LLMs on edge platforms.
