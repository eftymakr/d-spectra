## Related Works

The field of in-context learning (ICL) has seen significant advancements, particularly in the context of large language models (LLMs) and their ability to leverage annotated corpora for task execution \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2110.10456)\]. However, the reliance on high-quality demonstrations has exposed critical limitations when dealing with noisy annotations. Existing approaches often assume that noisy samples yield higher perplexity than clean ones, using local perplexity rankings to detect anomalies \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/2012.11854)\]. This assumption, however, becomes invalid under high noise ratios, where many demonstrations are flawed, leading to unreliable detection \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/2406.16966)\].

Several studies have addressed the issue of noisy annotations in deep learning models. For instance, \[[4](http://arxiv.org/abs/2207.13316)\] explores the challenges of training models with noisy labels, emphasizing the need for robust methods to correct such errors. Similarly, \[[5](http://arxiv.org/abs/2312.12275)\] proposes a framework for mitigating noisy supervision by generating synthetic samples with soft labels, which helps in improving generalization performance. These approaches, while effective in some settings, often fail to account for the complex interplay between annotation quality and model behavior \[[6](http://arxiv.org/abs/2212.01189)\].

In the context of scene graph generation, \[[7](http://arxiv.org/abs/1407.5155)\] highlights the prevalence of noisy ground-truth annotations and proposes NICEST, a method that detects and corrects noisy samples to improve model performance. This work aligns with our focus on improving the reliability of demonstration data by addressing annotation noise. Additionally, \[[8](http://arxiv.org/abs/2401.14360)\] investigates the impact of noisy labels on dataset bias and proposes a method that combines denoising with debiasing to enhance model robustness. This approach is particularly relevant to our dual debiasing framework, which aims to correct both annotation and model-related biases \[[9](http://arxiv.org/abs/1809.04019)\].

The use of synthetic data to mitigate label noise has also been explored in other domains. For example, \[[10](http://arxiv.org/abs/2410.09365)\] introduces a method for generating synthetic samples with soft labels to reduce the impact of noisy annotations, which is similar to our approach of using synthesized neighbors to correct perplexity estimates \[[11](http://arxiv.org/abs/2305.10736)\]. Furthermore, \[[12](http://arxiv.org/abs/2106.07704)\] discusses the challenges of training models on noisy datasets and proposes a method that leverages the early learning phase to identify and correct noisy samples \[[13](http://arxiv.org/abs/2412.04727)\].
