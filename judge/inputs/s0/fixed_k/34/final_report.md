## Related Works

Fund allocation in financial domains has garnered significant attention due to its critical role in investment decision-making. Traditional approaches such as prediction-only or predict-then-optimize (PnO) methods often suffer from goal mismatch, as they do not jointly optimize forecasting and allocation \[[10](http://arxiv.org/abs/2307.08853)\]. Additionally, the integration of state-of-the-art time series forecasting models introduces additional uncertainty into the predicted results \[[10](http://arxiv.org/abs/2307.08853)\]. To address these challenges, this work introduces the Risk-aware Time-Series Predict-and-Allocate (RTS-PnO) framework, which is designed to be agnostic to forecasting models and incorporates adaptive uncertainty calibration \[[10](http://arxiv.org/abs/2307.08853)\].

Several studies have explored the use of advanced time series forecasting models in financial applications. For instance, \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/2304.04912)\] introduced FinTSBridge, an evaluation suite that connects cutting-edge time series models with financial asset pricing. This work constructed three financial datasets, validated over ten forecasting models, and developed new metrics to assess model performance in financial contexts. Similarly, \[[13](http://arxiv.org/abs/2311.13247)\] proposed a projected nonlinear state-space model for forecasting time series signals, emphasizing the importance of capturing nonlinear dynamics from noisy data. These models are essential for accurate forecasting, which is a prerequisite for effective fund allocation.

Uncertainty calibration is another critical aspect of financial forecasting. \[[15](http://arxiv.org/abs/2012.10988)\] addressed the problem of uncertainty calibration, showing that existing post-hoc calibration methods yield highly over-confident predictions under domain shift. They introduced a perturbation-based strategy to improve calibration under domain drift. Similarly, \[[16](http://arxiv.org/abs/2410.14630)\] investigated the regularization of learnable embeddings for time series forecasting, demonstrating that regularization methods can enhance model performance by preventing co-adaptation between local and global parameters. These findings are relevant to the adaptive forecasting uncertainty calibration feature of RTS-PnO.

Transfer learning has also been applied to financial forecasting, particularly in scenarios with limited historical data. \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/2503.12648)\] proposed a multi-source transfer learning approach for volatility forecasting of new issues and spin-offs, leveraging complementary source data to improve forecasting accuracy. This approach aligns with the agnostic nature of RTS-PnO, which does not rely on specific forecasting models but instead integrates uncertainty calibration and objective alignment.

In addition to forecasting, the integration of deep learning models in financial applications has been explored. \[[12](http://arxiv.org/abs/2401.03006)\] surveyed the use of diffusion models in time-series forecasting, highlighting their state-of-the-art performance in generative modeling tasks. \[[11](http://arxiv.org/abs/2307.11494)\] introduced TSDiff, an unconditional diffusion model for time series, demonstrating its effectiveness in forecasting, refinement, and synthetic data generation.
