## Related Works

Diffusion transformers (DiT) have emerged as a powerful architecture for image and video generation, demonstrating superior performance compared to traditional diffusion models \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2406.02540)\]. However, their large parameter counts and computational complexity pose significant challenges for deployment on resource-constrained devices \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2406.02540)\]. To address these limitations, various quantization techniques have been proposed to reduce memory usage and improve inference speed \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/2406.17343)\]. 

Early efforts in quantizing diffusion models focused on image generation tasks, where techniques like post-training quantization (PTQ) have been applied with varying degrees of success \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/2505.18663)\]. However, these methods often fail to generalize to video generation due to the additional complexity of temporal dependencies \[[4](http://arxiv.org/abs/2502.04056)\]. For instance, ViDiT-Q \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2406.02540)\] was specifically designed for DiT models, achieving W8A8 quantization with minimal degradation in visual quality. Similarly, Q-DiT \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/2406.17343)\] introduced techniques to handle spatial and temporal variance in DiT weights and activations, achieving significant improvements in FID scores. 

In the context of video generation, several works have explored quantization strategies tailored for video diffusion models. QVD \[[5](http://arxiv.org/abs/2411.14172)\] was the first PTQ strategy designed for video diffusion models, addressing the challenges of temporal feature skewness and inter-channel activation disparities. It introduced High Temporal Discriminability Quantization (HTDQ) and Scattered Channel Range Integration (SCRI) to improve quantization accuracy. Similarly, DVD-Quant \[[6](http://arxiv.org/abs/2405.19751)\] proposed a data-free quantization framework for Video DiTs, achieving a 2× speedup while maintaining visual fidelity. 

Another line of research has focused on time-aware quantization techniques to handle the temporal dynamics of DiT models. TQ-DiT \[[7](http://arxiv.org/abs/2407.11585)\] introduced a joint reconstruction method to address non-convergence issues during quantization and proposed time-variance-aware static transformations to improve quantization accuracy. This method achieved superior performance at W4A8 compared to previous approaches. Similarly, Q-Diffusion \[[8](http://arxiv.org/abs/2302.04304)\] introduced a novel PTQ method specifically tailored for diffusion models, addressing the challenges of changing output distributions and bimodal activation distributions. 

Recent works have also explored hybrid quantization approaches for DiT models. HQ-DiT \[[9](http://arxiv.org/abs/2307.01831)\] introduced FP4 hybrid quantization, achieving 4-bit quantization with minimal performance degradation. This approach utilized a clipping range selection mechanism to align with the data distribution within DiT, reducing quantization error.
