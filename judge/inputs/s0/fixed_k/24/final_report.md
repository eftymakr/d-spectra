## Related Works

Diffusion transformers (DiT) have emerged as a powerful architecture for image and video generation, demonstrating superior performance compared to traditional diffusion models \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2406.02540)\]. However, their large parameter counts and computational complexity pose significant challenges for deployment on resource-constrained devices \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2406.02540)\]. To address these limitations, various quantization techniques have been proposed to reduce memory usage and improve inference speed \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/2406.17343)\]. 

Early efforts in quantizing diffusion models focused on image generation tasks, where techniques like post-training quantization (PTQ) have been applied with varying degrees of success \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/2505.18663)\]. However, these methods often fail to generalize to video generation due to the additional complexity of temporal dependencies \[[4](http://arxiv.org/abs/2502.04056)\]. For instance, ViDiT-Q \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2406.02540)\] was specifically designed for DiT models, achieving W8A8 quantization with minimal degradation in visual quality. Similarly, Q-DiT \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/2406.17343)\] introduced techniques to handle spatial and temporal variance in DiT weights and activations, achieving significant improvements in FID scores. 

In the context of video generation, several works have explored quantization strategies tailored for video diffusion models. ### <a id='category-hybrid-quantization-strategies'></a>Hybrid Quantization Strategies: 2 papers found.
Hybrid quantization strategies have emerged as a critical area of research to address the computational and storage limitations of large models like Diffusion Transformers (DiTs) \[[6](http://arxiv.org/abs/2405.19751)\]. These strategies aim to reduce the bit-width of model parameters and activations while maintaining performance, particularly on resource-constrained devices \[[12](http://arxiv.org/abs/2307.00331)\]. For instance, the Hybrid Floating-point Quantization for DiT (HQ-DiT) employs 4-bit floating-point (FP) precision for both weights and activations, leveraging a clipping range selection mechanism to minimize quantization error \[[6](http://arxiv.org/abs/2405.19751)\]. This approach is more aligned with the data distribution within DiTs compared to fixed-point quantization methods like INT8 \[[6](http://arxiv.org/abs/2405.19751)\]. Additionally, the variation-aware quantization scheme proposed in another study identifies challenges in low-bit quantization of transformers, such as module quantization sensitivities, outliers in weight and activation distributions, and dynamic parameter fluctuations, which can destabilize quantization-aware training (QAT) \[[12](http://arxiv.org/abs/2307.00331)\]. To mitigate these issues, the study introduces techniques to stabilize training and improve performance across vision and language transformers \[[12](http://arxiv.org/abs/2307.00331)\]. These efforts highlight the growing interest in hybrid quantization strategies that balance efficiency with accuracy, especially for complex models like DiTs.
