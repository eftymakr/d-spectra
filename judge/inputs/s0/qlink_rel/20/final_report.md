## Related Works

Deep learning has emerged as a powerful paradigm for solving inverse problems in imaging, particularly in single-pixel imaging (SPI) where the goal is to reconstruct high-quality images from compressed measurements. Traditional approaches to SPI, such as iterative algorithms, often rely on hand-crafted priors and are computationally intensive \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/1912.10557)\]. In contrast, deep learning methods, especially those based on deep unrolling and plug-and-play (PnP) strategies, have gained significant traction due to their flexibility and performance \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/1704.03488)\].

PnP approaches are a class of iterative algorithms where the regularization is implicitly performed by an off-the-shelf deep denoiser \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/1611.03679)\]. These methods are flexible for varying compression ratios (CRs) but suffer from limitations in reconstruction accuracy and speed \[[4](http://arxiv.org/abs/1712.02862)\]. For instance, the use of denoisers as implicit regularization has been explored in various contexts, including image deblurring and denoising \[[5](http://arxiv.org/abs/1508.02848)\]. However, the performance of these methods can be constrained by the quality of the denoiser and the specific problem at hand \[[6](http://arxiv.org/abs/1902.05399)\].

On the other hand, unrolling approaches transform a truncated iterative optimization process into an end-to-end trainable neural network \[[7](http://arxiv.org/abs/2003.10428)\]. These methods typically achieve better accuracy and faster inference but require fine-tuning or retraining when the compression ratio changes \[[8](http://arxiv.org/abs/1704.03264)\]. For example, the unrolling of iterative algorithms such as ISTA and FISTA has been used to create deep networks that can be trained end-to-end \[[9](http://arxiv.org/abs/1704.06625)\]. The success of these methods is often attributed to their ability to incorporate domain-specific knowledge into the network architecture \[[10](http://arxiv.org/abs/1707.06474)\].

To address the limitations of both PnP and unrolling approaches, recent work has focused on integrating the strengths of both paradigms. This has led to the development of hybrid methods that combine the flexibility of PnP with the accuracy of unrolling \[[11](http://arxiv.org/abs/1608.03981)\]. For instance, the use of learned proximal operators to replace traditional proximal steps in iterative algorithms has been proposed to improve the performance of unrolled networks \[[12](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/b954a94af132294a4a4e2efce15027758a535742)\]. These methods leverage the power of deep learning to learn the proximal operator, which can then be used to enforce data consistency during the reconstruction process \[[13](http://arxiv.org/abs/1505.04597)\].

Furthermore, the design of efficient and interpretable deep networks has been a key focus in recent research. Techniques such as algorithm unrolling have been used to create networks that mimic the behavior of traditional iterative algorithms while maintaining the benefits of deep learning \[[14](http://arxiv.org/abs/1409.2574)\]. ### <a id='category-end-to-end-training-and-optimization'></a>End-to-End Training and Optimization: 12 papers found.
End-to-end training and optimization have been pivotal in advancing deep learning approaches for inverse problems in imaging, such as image restoration, super-resolution, and tomographic reconstruction \[[4](http://arxiv.org/abs/1712.02862)\]. These methods typically involve unrolling iterative optimization algorithms, such as conjugate gradients or proximal methods, into neural network architectures, allowing for end-to-end learning \[[4](http://arxiv.org/abs/1712.02862)\]. This approach enables the network to adapt to the forward model, improving performance over pre-trained denoisers \[[4](http://arxiv.org/abs/1712.02862)\]. For instance, the Learned Primal-Dual algorithm unrolls a proximal primal-dual optimization method, replacing proximal operators with convolutional neural networks, and is trained end-to-end without requiring an initial reconstruction \[[10](http://arxiv.org/abs/1707.06474)\]. Similarly, the TNRD framework employs dynamic nonlinear reaction diffusion models, where all parameters are learned simultaneously from training data, leading to highly efficient and effective image restoration \[[5](http://arxiv.org/abs/1508.02848)\]. The decoupling of the number of iterations from network complexity also allows for reduced training data and faster convergence \[[4](http://arxiv.org/abs/1712.02862)\]. Moreover, residual learning and batch normalization have been utilized to accelerate training and enhance performance in denoising tasks \[[11](http://arxiv.org/abs/1608.03981)\]. These techniques have been further refined in deep unfolding networks, which combine the flexibility of model-based methods with the efficiency of learning-based approaches \[[7](http://arxiv.org/abs/2003.10428)\]. The success of these methods highlights the importance of end-to-end training in achieving state-of-the-art results while maintaining computational efficiency \[[4](http://arxiv.org/abs/1712.02862)\]\[[5](http://arxiv.org/abs/1508.02848)\]\[[7](http://arxiv.org/abs/2003.10428)\]\[[10](http://arxiv.org/abs/1707.06474)\].

	Document 1: [Snippet]: «Plug-and-play priors (PnP) is a powerful framework for regularizing imaging inverse problems by using advanced denoisers within an iterative algorithm. Recent experimental evidence suggests that PnP algorithms achieve the state-of-the-art performance in a range of imaging applications. In this paper, we introduce a new online PnP algorithm based on the proximal gradient method (PGM). The proposed algorithm uses only a subset of measurements at every iteration, which makes it scalable to very large datasets. We present a new theoretical convergence analysis, for both batch and online variants of PnP-PGM, for denoisers that do not necessarily correspond to proximal operators. We also present simulations illustrating the applicability of the algorithm to image reconstruction in diffraction tomography. The results in this paper have the potential to expand the applicability of the PnP framework to very large datasets.»

	Document 2: [Snippet]: «We present a new image reconstruction method that replaces the projector in a projected gradient descent (PGD) with a convolutional neural network (CNN). Recently, CNNs trained as image-to-image regressors have been successfully used to solve inverse problems in imaging. However, unlike existing iterative image reconstruction algorithms, these CNN-based approaches usually lack a feedback mechanism to enforce that the reconstructed image is consistent with the measurements. We propose a relaxed version of PGD wherein gradient descent enforces measurement consistency, while a CNN recursively projects the solution closer to the space of desired reconstruction images. We show that this algorithm is guaranteed to converge and, under certain conditions, converges to a local minimum of a non-convex inverse problem. Finally, we propose a simple scheme to train the CNN to act like a projector. Our experiments on sparse-view computed-tomography reconstruction show an improvement over total variation-based regularization, dictionary learning, and a state-of-the-art deep learning-based direct reconstruction technique.»
