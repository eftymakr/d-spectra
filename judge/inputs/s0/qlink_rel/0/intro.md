## Related Works

Traditional approaches to modeling economic inequality and taxation have relied on static frameworks that fail to capture the complexity of heterogeneous taxpayer behavior and dynamic policy adjustments. For instance, the U.S. federal income tax system, while effective in reducing inequality, lacks adaptability to changing economic conditions and individual behaviors \[[15](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/0fba9c1bee4a5bda0d5dbf01f97ef5a8e212833e)\]. Similarly, models like the Saez Optimal Taxation framework, which dynamically adjusts tax rates, do not adequately address irrational taxpayer behavior or heterogeneity in income distribution \[[15](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/0fba9c1bee4a5bda0d5dbf01f97ef5a8e212833e)\]. These limitations highlight the need for more flexible and data-driven policy design.

Agent-based modeling (ABM) has been increasingly used to simulate economic systems, particularly in the context of tax evasion and social behavior. For example, the Ising model has been adapted to study tax evasion dynamics, where agents (taxpayers) interact with their environment and enforcement mechanisms to either evade or comply with tax laws \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/0805.0998)\]. This approach allows for the simulation of how different enforcement strategies, such as audits, can influence taxpayer behavior over time \[[4](http://arxiv.org/abs/0801.2980)\]. Additionally, the multi-agent-based approach to tax morale has shown that external factors, such as trust in governmental institutions, can significantly affect the prevalence of tax evasion \[[7](http://arxiv.org/abs/0806.0344)\]. These models provide a foundation for understanding how policy interventions can shape economic outcomes.

Recent advancements in large language models (LLMs) have also contributed to the field of economic modeling. LLMs, when fine-tuned on reasoning tasks, can generate structured thought processes that improve their ability to make complex decisions \[[6](http://arxiv.org/abs/2210.03629)\]. This capability is particularly relevant for policy design, where models must balance multiple objectives, such as equity and productivity. The ReAct framework, which synergizes reasoning and acting in LLMs, has demonstrated effectiveness in tasks requiring both logical deduction and external information retrieval \[[6](http://arxiv.org/abs/2210.03629)\]. These techniques can be integrated into ABMs to enhance the decision-making capabilities of policy agents.

Furthermore, reinforcement learning (RL) has been explored as a method to improve the behavioral models of agents in ABMs. RL-based agents can learn to maximize rewards, making them suitable for simulating economic agents that respond to policy changes \[[9](http://arxiv.org/abs/2006.05048)\]. This approach has been applied to policy-relevant ABMs, such as those modeling influenza transmission and minority games, demonstrating the potential of RL to improve the realism and adaptability of economic simulations \[[9](http://arxiv.org/abs/2006.05048)\].
