## Related Works

Diffusion models have become a cornerstone in generative modeling, particularly for image and video synthesis, due to their ability to produce high-quality outputs through iterative noise estimation \[[13](http://arxiv.org/abs/2105.05233)\]. However, their computational demands and memory usage hinder deployment on edge devices, motivating research into quantization techniques to reduce model size and accelerate inference \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2302.04304)\]. Post-training quantization (PTQ) has emerged as a promising solution, allowing for model compression without retraining \[[15](http://arxiv.org/abs/2106.08295)\]. Despite its advantages, PTQ faces challenges when applied to diffusion transformers (DiTs), which are increasingly used in video generation due to their scalability and performance \[[8](http://arxiv.org/abs/2212.09748)\]. 

Existing quantization methods for image generation models often fail to generalize to video generation tasks due to two primary issues: the loss of information during quantization and the misalignment between optimization objectives and the unique requirements of video generation \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2302.04304)\]. For instance, quantization noise can accumulate over time steps, leading to a low signal-to-noise ratio (SNR) and degraded video quality \[[4](http://arxiv.org/abs/2305.10657)\]. Additionally, the temporal and spatial correlations in video data are not well captured by existing quantization strategies \[[25](http://arxiv.org/abs/2405.19751)\].

To address these challenges, several works have explored PTQ for DiTs. Q-DiT \[[25](http://arxiv.org/abs/2405.19751)\] introduces a novel approach that integrates automatic quantization granularity allocation and sample-wise dynamic activation quantization, achieving significant improvements in image and video generation. Similarly, PTQ4DiT \[[5](http://arxiv.org/abs/2405.16005)\] proposes Channel-wise Salience Balancing (CSB) and Spearmen's $\rho$-guided Salience Calibration (SSC) to handle the unique characteristics of DiTs, enabling 8-bit and even 4-bit quantization with minimal performance degradation. These methods demonstrate that with careful design, PTQ can be effective for DiTs, although they often require additional calibration and optimization steps \[[25](http://arxiv.org/abs/2405.19751)\].

Another line of research focuses on the application of PTQ to diffusion models in general. Q-Diffusion \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2302.04304)\] and PTQ4DM \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/2211.15736)\] propose methods tailored to the multi-timestep pipeline of diffusion models, addressing the changing output distributions over time steps. These approaches emphasize the importance of calibration and quantization-aware strategies to maintain performance. Similarly, PTQD \[[4](http://arxiv.org/abs/2305.10657)\] introduces a unified formulation for quantization noise and diffusion perturbed noise, enabling more accurate quantization of diffusion models at lower bit-widths.
