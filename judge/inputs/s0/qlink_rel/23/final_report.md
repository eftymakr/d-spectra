## Related Works

Sensor calibration is a critical step in multi-sensor fusion for autonomous driving systems, ensuring accurate alignment between different modalities such as radar, camera, and LiDAR. Prior work has focused on extrinsic calibration methods for radar-camera systems, often relying on calibration targets or specialized hardware \[[1](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/4d4dbbcf27d991aeab43dafb82a105bb5512b799)\]. However, these methods are not suitable for online automatic calibration, which is essential for long-term system reliability \[[6](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/c80265fa719eb85c8a56a6b36c74b99b793f39b2)\]. Recent efforts have explored data-driven approaches to overcome the limitations of traditional calibration techniques \[[7](http://arxiv.org/abs/1904.08743)\]. For example, \[[1](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/4d4dbbcf27d991aeab43dafb82a105bb5512b799)\] presents a novel open-source tool for extrinsic calibration of radar, camera, and LiDAR, emphasizing joint calibration of all three modalities. While this method improves calibration accuracy, it still requires manual intervention and is not suitable for real-time applications \[[1](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/4d4dbbcf27d991aeab43dafb82a105bb5512b799)\].

Online calibration methods have also been proposed to address the challenge of maintaining sensor alignment during system operation \[[6](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/c80265fa719eb85c8a56a6b36c74b99b793f39b2)\]. In \[[6](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/c80265fa719eb85c8a56a6b36c74b99b793f39b2)\], an online calibration method based on moving object tracking is introduced, leveraging detection and tracking information to estimate sensor parameters. This approach is resource-efficient and suitable for long-term reliability. However, it relies on ego-motion estimation and may not be robust under varying environmental conditions \[[6](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/c80265fa719eb85c8a56a6b36c74b99b793f39b2)\]. Another study \[[7](http://arxiv.org/abs/1904.08743)\] proposes a data-driven method for automatic rotational radar-camera calibration without dedicated calibration targets, using a coarse and fine convolutional neural network. This method demonstrates robustness and generalization capabilities, but it is limited to rotational calibration and does not address full 6DoF calibration \[[7](http://arxiv.org/abs/1904.08743)\].

The use of deep learning for sensor fusion has gained traction in recent years, enabling more accurate and robust perception systems \[[13](http://arxiv.org/abs/2209.06535)\]. For instance, \[[13](http://arxiv.org/abs/2209.06535)\] introduces a novel early fusion approach that exploits both spatial and contextual properties of camera and radar data for 3D object detection. This method outperforms late fusion strategies by effectively integrating information from both modalities. Similarly, \[[14](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/a94f8a34a9c4fce90727f62d652f2420fd1021a2)\] proposes a feature-level fusion detection framework that aligns radar and camera features in the bird's-eye view (BEV) domain, achieving significant improvements in object detection and positioning performance. However, these methods focus on object detection rather than calibration, which is a distinct challenge \[[14](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/a94f8a34a9c4fce90727f62d652f2420fd1021a2)\].

Unlike currently available offerings, our tool facilitates joint extrinsic calibration of all three sensing modalities on multiple measurements. Furthermore, our calibration target design extends existing work to obtain simultaneous measurements for all these modalities. We study how various factors of the calibration procedure affect the outcome on real multi-modal measurements of the target. Three different configurations of the optimization criterion are considered, namely using error terms for a minimal amount of sensor pairs, or using terms for all sensor pairs with additional loop closure constraints, or by adding terms for structure estimation in a probabilistic model. The experiments further evaluate how the number of calibration boards affect calibration performance, and robustness against different levels of zero mean Gaussian noise. Our results show that all configurations achieve good results for lidar to camera errors and that fully connected pose estimation shows the best performance for lidar to radar errors when more than five board locations are used.»

### <a id='category-radar-camera-fusion-techniques'></a>Radar-Camera Fusion Techniques: 11 papers found.
The related works in radar-camera fusion techniques focus on improving the accuracy and robustness of sensor data integration for autonomous driving systems. Early approaches relied on 2D radar sensors and required specialized retroreflectors for calibration \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/2103.07505)\]. However, recent advancements have shifted toward 3D millimetre-wavelength radars, which offer better performance in adverse weather conditions \[[1](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/4d4dbbcf27d991aeab43dafb82a105bb5512b799)\]. Several studies have proposed fusion frameworks that combine radar and camera data to enhance object detection and tracking. For example, Camera Radar Net (CRN) generates a semantically rich bird’s-eye-view (BEV) feature map by transforming perspective image features into BEV using sparse radar points, and employs multi-modal deformable attention to address spatial misalignment \[[4](http://arxiv.org/abs/2304.00670)\]. Similarly, CenterFusion uses a center point detection network and a frustum-based method to associate radar detections with object centers, improving depth and velocity estimation \[[5](http://arxiv.org/abs/2011.04841)\]. Other methods, such as CRF-Net, fuse camera and projected radar data within network layers, learning the optimal fusion level for detection \[[8](http://arxiv.org/abs/2005.07431)\]. MVFusion introduces semantic-aligned radar features and a radar-guided fusion transformer to enhance cross-modal information interaction \[[9](http://arxiv.org/abs/2302.10511)\]. RODNet, on the other hand, detects objects from radar RF images using a cross-supervised approach with a camera-radar fusion strategy, achieving high precision in various driving conditions \[[11](http://arxiv.org/abs/2102.05150)\]. Additionally, studies like the one proposing an early fusion approach have demonstrated that exploiting spatial and contextual properties of both modalities can lead to state-of-the-art performance \[[13](http://arxiv.org/abs/2209.06535)\]. These methods collectively highlight the importance of accurate calibration, semantic alignment, and robust fusion strategies in improving the reliability of radar-camera systems for autonomous driving \[[12](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/fa7f4199640500d48c0671181ea6a8dce3141123)\].

	Document 1: [Snippet]: «The goal of perception for autonomous vehicles is to extract semantic representations from multiple sensors and fuse these representations into a single "bird's-eye-view" coordinate frame for consumption by motion planning. We propose a new end-to-end architecture that directly extracts a bird's-eye-view representation of a scene given image data from an arbitrary number of cameras. The core idea behind our approach is to "lift" each image individually into a frustum of features for each camera, then "splat" all frustums into a rasterized bird's-eye-view grid. By training on the entire camera rig, we provide evidence that our model is able to learn not only how to represent images but how to fuse predictions from all cameras into a single cohesive representation of the scene while being robust to calibration error. On standard bird's-eye-view tasks such as object segmentation and map segmentation, our model outperforms all baselines and prior work. In pursuit of the goal of learning dense representations for motion planning, we show that the representations inferred by our model enable interpretable end-to-end motion planning by "shooting" template trajectories into a bird's-eye-view cost map output by our network.»
