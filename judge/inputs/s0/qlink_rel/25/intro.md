## Related Works

The integration of knowledge graphs (KGs) with large language models (LLMs) to enhance reasoning capabilities in claim verification has gained significant attention in recent research. While LLMs have demonstrated strong reasoning abilities, their performance on complex, multi-step reasoning tasks remains limited, particularly when compared to structured knowledge sources such as KGs \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2306.08302)\]. KGs, on the other hand, provide rich, semantically grounded representations that are well-suited for reasoning but are often underutilized in verification tasks due to the reliance on unstructured text corpora \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2306.08302)\]. 

Several studies have explored the use of KGs to improve LLM reasoning. For instance, the QA-GNN model introduces a joint reasoning framework that combines LLMs and KGs by estimating the relevance of KG nodes relative to a question and performing graph-based message passing to update representations \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/2104.06378)\]. Similarly, the KGP method constructs a knowledge graph over multiple documents to guide LLMs in multi-document question answering, leveraging semantic and structural relationships to improve reasoning \[[5](http://arxiv.org/abs/2308.11730)\]. These approaches highlight the potential of integrating KGs with LLMs but often focus on specific tasks or limited reasoning pipelines \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/2104.06378)\]\[[5](http://arxiv.org/abs/2308.11730)\].

Retrieval-augmented generation (RAG) has also been proposed as a way to enhance LLMs with external knowledge. RAG models combine pre-trained LLMs with non-parametric memory, such as Wikipedia, to improve accuracy and reduce hallucinations in knowledge-intensive tasks \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/2005.11401)\]. The LLM-Augmenter system further builds on this idea by incorporating external databases and iterative feedback mechanisms to refine LLM outputs, reducing hallucinations without sacrificing fluency \[[7](http://arxiv.org/abs/2302.12813)\]. These methods demonstrate that external knowledge can significantly improve the reliability of LLM outputs, especially in domains requiring factual accuracy \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/2005.11401)\]\[[7](http://arxiv.org/abs/2302.12813)\].

The ability of LLMs to perform zero-shot reasoning has also been explored. Zero-shot-CoT prompting, for example, enables LLMs to generate step-by-step reasoning without the need for task-specific examples, achieving notable improvements on arithmetic and symbolic reasoning tasks \[[4](http://arxiv.org/abs/2205.11916)\]. However, LLMs still struggle with multi-step modular reasoning and reasoning over KGs without adaptation \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2306.08302)\]. This limitation underscores the need for frameworks that can effectively bridge the gap between LLMs and structured knowledge sources \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2306.08302)\].
