## Related Works

Personalized outfit generation has been a focal point in recent research, driven by the increasing demand for fashion recommendation systems that can produce both compatible and personalized outfits. Early approaches primarily focused on outfit compatibility and item-level similarity, often relying on rule-based systems or simple machine learning models \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/1905.01866)\]. However, these methods lacked the ability to capture complex user preferences and the nuanced relationships between fashion items.

Several studies have explored the use of deep learning techniques to improve outfit compatibility and personalization. For instance, \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/1608.03016)\] proposed a system that scores fashion outfit candidates based on appearances and metadata, leveraging outfit popularity on fashion websites to supervise the scoring component. This approach achieved an AUC of 85% for the scoring component and an accuracy of 77% for a constrained composition task. Similarly, \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/1807.03133)\] introduced a model that uses bidirectional LSTM and autoencoder to extract style information from outfits, enabling more flexible and interpretable outfit generation. The model outperformed baseline methods in fashion item prediction and style extraction tasks.

The importance of user preferences in outfit recommendation has also been emphasized. \[[4](http://arxiv.org/abs/1803.09196)\] introduced a method to learn type-aware embeddings for fashion compatibility, capturing both item similarity and compatibility across different types. This approach improved compatibility prediction by 3–5% over state-of-the-art methods. \[[5](http://arxiv.org/abs/1712.02662)\] further extended this idea by proposing a hierarchical fashion graph network that models relationships among users, items, and outfits simultaneously, achieving significant improvements over existing compatibility matching models and outfit recommenders.

In addition to compatibility, personalization has been a key focus. \[[6](http://arxiv.org/abs/2005.12566)\] developed a system that automatically creates capsule wardrobes by selecting a minimal set of items that maximize mix-and-match possibilities, incorporating submodular objective functions to capture visual compatibility, versatility, and user-specific preferences. \[[7](http://arxiv.org/abs/1711.02231)\] introduced a framework that uses generative image models to create new clothing items based on user preferences, demonstrating the potential of generative models in fashion recommendation.

Recent works have also explored the use of attention mechanisms and transformer architectures to enhance outfit generation and recommendation. \[[8](http://arxiv.org/abs/1806.08977)\] proposed a model that uses a transformer architecture to generate personalized outfits, leveraging large-scale datasets to improve compatibility and personalization. \[[9](http://arxiv.org/abs/1906.07273)\] introduced a method that generates outfits based on text queries, using multimodal embeddings to measure item-query coherence and compatibility between fashion items. This approach yielded compatibility prediction results that met or exceeded the state of the art.
