## Related Works

Video frame interpolation has been a focal point in video processing research, with numerous methods proposed to synthesize intermediate frames between existing ones. Traditional approaches typically involve two stages: motion estimation and pixel synthesis \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/1703.07514)\]. However, these methods often suffer from computational inefficiency and poor performance in the presence of complex motion or occlusion \[[18](http://arxiv.org/abs/1803.10967)\]. Recent advances have shifted towards end-to-end deep learning approaches that combine motion estimation and synthesis into a single process \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/1703.07514)\]. For instance, Video Frame Interpolation via Adaptive Separable Convolution \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/1703.07514)\] introduces a method that uses local separable convolutions to reduce parameter count and improve efficiency. Similarly, Super SloMo \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/1712.00080)\] proposes an end-to-end convolutional neural network for multi-frame interpolation, leveraging bi-directional optical flow and visibility maps to handle motion boundaries and occlusions effectively.

The use of optical flow remains a common strategy in video interpolation, but its limitations in handling large motion and occlusion have led to alternative approaches. For example, Depth-Aware Video Frame Interpolation \[[5](http://arxiv.org/abs/1904.00830)\] explicitly detects occlusion by incorporating depth information, which improves the quality of interpolated frames. Additionally, the work in \[[25](http://arxiv.org/abs/2007.11762)\] introduces a temporally adaptive multi-frame interpolator that handles complex motion segments using an advanced motion model and a pyramidal style network. This method is particularly effective for generating temporally consistent frames in high-resolution videos.

Another significant development is the introduction of perceptual losses for training deep neural networks, which helps produce visually pleasing results \[[29](http://arxiv.org/abs/1603.08155)\]. This concept has been applied to video frame interpolation, as seen in the work of \[[7](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/bb4a9650ca3946c70a7e92007cc61dc0dfd75522)\], where a channel attention mechanism is used to replace optical flow computation, leading to improved performance in challenging scenarios. Similarly, the use of adversarial training and cycle consistency loss has been explored in \[[20](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/380f46cd24a15271036ff49e9e0d91f39f89d8b4)\], where the cycle consistency loss enhances interpolation results by ensuring that synthesized frames can reconstruct the input frames with high quality.

Furthermore, the integration of context-aware synthesis has been proposed to improve the quality of interpolated frames. End-to-end deep learning approaches have emerged as a promising solution for video frame interpolation, addressing the limitations of traditional methods that rely on optical flow estimation and separate motion estimation and pixel synthesis steps \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/1708.01692)\]. Recent works propose to merge these steps into a single convolutional process, using spatially adaptive kernels that capture both motion and pixel synthesis information \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/1703.07514)\]. This approach reduces computational complexity and error propagation, particularly in challenging scenarios with large motion or occlusion \[[7](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/bb4a9650ca3946c70a7e92007cc61dc0dfd75522)\]. Others introduce novel architectures, such as those incorporating channel attention mechanisms and feature reshaping operations like PixelShuffle, to enhance motion-aware synthesis at the pixel level \[[7](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/bb4a9650ca3946c70a7e92007cc61dc0dfd75522)\]. These models are trained end-to-end using widely available video data without requiring ground-truth optical flow annotations, enabling high-quality frame interpolation while maintaining efficiency \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/1703.07514)\]. Additionally, some approaches focus on optimizing the training process by leveraging deep neural networks to learn complex upscaling filters directly from LR feature maps, improving both speed and performance \[[26](http://arxiv.org/abs/1609.05158)\]. Overall, these methods demonstrate the potential of end-to-end learning in achieving robust and high-quality video frame interpolation across diverse scenarios.
