## Related Works

Multi-token prediction (MTP) has emerged as a promising pre-training objective for language models, offering potential improvements in downstream performance, inference speed, and training efficiency \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/2404.19737)\]. Unlike traditional next-token prediction (NTP), which predicts one token at a time, MTP predicts the next $k$ tokens simultaneously using multiple prediction heads \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/2404.19737)\]. This approach has been shown to enhance generative capabilities, particularly for large models, by enabling more efficient training and faster inference \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/2404.19737)\]. However, prior work has demonstrated that smaller language models (SLMs) face challenges in adapting to the MTP objective \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/2404.19737)\].

Several studies have explored alternative training strategies to address these limitations. For instance, curriculum learning has been proposed as a method to gradually increase the complexity of the training objective \[[9](http://arxiv.org/abs/1506.03099)\]. This approach has been applied in sequence prediction tasks, where models are first trained on simpler tasks before progressing to more complex ones \[[9](http://arxiv.org/abs/1506.03099)\]. Similarly, the use of process supervision, which provides feedback at each reasoning step, has been shown to improve the performance of models on complex tasks such as mathematical reasoning \[[4](http://arxiv.org/abs/2305.20050)\]. This method, however, requires extensive manual annotation, which can be costly and impractical for large-scale training \[[4](http://arxiv.org/abs/2305.20050)\].

Another related approach is speculative decoding, which enables faster inference by generating multiple tokens in parallel \[[10](http://arxiv.org/abs/2211.17192)\]. This technique leverages the fact that some subtasks within language modeling can be approximated by more efficient models, allowing for parallel computation without compromising the final output \[[10](http://arxiv.org/abs/2211.17192)\]. While speculative decoding has been successfully applied to large models, its benefits for smaller models remain underexplored \[[10](http://arxiv.org/abs/2211.17192)\].

The concept of reverse curriculum learning, which gradually decreases the complexity of the training objective from MTP to NTP, has also been explored \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/2404.19737)\]. This approach has shown promise in improving the performance of SLMs on NTP tasks, although it does not provide the same benefits as self-speculative decoding \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/2404.19737)\]. In contrast, the forward curriculum, which increases complexity from NTP to MTP, enables SLMs to better leverage the MTP objective during pre-training, resulting in improved downstream performance and generative output quality \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/2404.19737)\].

Additionally, the use of large-scale pre-training datasets has been shown to significantly impact model performance \[[7](http://arxiv.org/abs/2203.15556)\].
