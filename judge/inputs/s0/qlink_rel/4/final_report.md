## Related Works

Label noise is a pervasive issue in deep learning, particularly when training with large-scale datasets that may contain incorrect annotations. Several approaches have been proposed to address this challenge, each with distinct methodologies and theoretical foundations. Early works focused on modifying network architectures or estimating true labels from noisy ones \[[6](http://arxiv.org/abs/1712.09482)\]. For instance, \[[6](http://arxiv.org/abs/1712.09482)\] introduced robust loss functions that are inherently tolerant to label noise, showing that mean absolute error (MAE) is inherently robust to label noise, and that standard back propagation is sufficient to learn the true classifier even under label noise. This work laid the groundwork for later studies that explored loss correction and noise-tolerant training strategies.

More recently, researchers have explored loss correction approaches that are agnostic to both application domain and network architecture \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/1609.03683)\]. These methods rely on estimating the probability of each class being corrupted into another, and they use a matrix inversion and multiplication to correct the loss. \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/1609.03683)\] also demonstrated that when ReLU is the only non-linearity, the loss curvature is immune to class-dependent label noise. This theoretical grounding has inspired subsequent works that aim to improve robustness against label noise in various settings.

Another line of research focuses on co-teaching, where two neural networks are trained simultaneously, and they teach each other by selecting data with possibly clean labels \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/1804.06872)\]. This approach mitigates the problem of deep models memorizing noisy labels by allowing the networks to communicate and refine their training data. The Co-teaching method has been shown to be effective in improving robustness against noisy labels, particularly in datasets like MNIST, CIFAR-10, and CIFAR-100.

In addition to co-teaching, there has been significant interest in distillation-based approaches that leverage side information, such as a small clean dataset or label relations in a knowledge graph, to improve robustness against noisy labels \[[4](http://arxiv.org/abs/1703.02391)\]. These methods aim to "hedge the risk" of learning from noisy labels by using auxiliary information to guide the training process. \[[4](http://arxiv.org/abs/1703.02391)\] also introduced new benchmark datasets in domains such as Sports, Species, and Artifacts to evaluate the effectiveness of these methods in practical settings.

### <a id='category-label-noise-in-deep-learning'></a>Label Noise in Deep Learning: 10 papers found.
The category "Label Noise in Deep Learning" encompasses a variety of approaches aimed at improving the robustness of deep neural networks in the presence of noisy labels. Several studies focus on developing frameworks that can effectively train models using datasets with high proportions of incorrect labels. For example, one paper proposes a novel framework that formulates the problem using an undirected graphical model to represent the relationship between noisy and clean labels, enabling tractable inference and regularization during training \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/1706.00038)\]. Another work introduces a theoretically grounded approach to train deep neural networks under class-dependent label noise, proposing loss correction procedures that rely on estimating class corruption probabilities \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/1609.03683)\]. Additionally, a dimensionality-driven learning strategy has been developed to monitor the deep representation subspace during training and adapt the loss function accordingly, showing high tolerance to noisy labels \[[8](http://arxiv.org/abs/1806.02612)\]. Meta-learning algorithms have also been introduced to learn example weights based on gradient directions, avoiding the need for additional hyperparameter tuning \[[9](http://arxiv.org/abs/1803.09050)\]. Furthermore, the Symmetric cross entropy Learning (SL) approach has been proposed to address both under learning and overfitting issues caused by noisy labels by combining cross entropy with a noise-robust reverse cross entropy \[[10](http://arxiv.org/abs/1908.06112)\]. These methods collectively highlight the ongoing efforts to mitigate the negative impact of label noise and improve model performance in real-world scenarios where noisy labels are prevalent.

### <a id='category-co-teaching-approaches'></a>Co-teaching Approaches: 2 papers found.

	Document 1: [Snippet]: «Deep learning with noisy labels is practically challenging, as the capacity of deep models is so high that they can totally memorize these noisy labels sooner or later during training. Nonetheless, recent studies on the memorization effects of deep neural networks show that they would first memorize training data of clean labels and then those of noisy labels. Therefore in this paper, we propose a new deep learning paradigm called Co-teaching for combating with noisy labels. Namely, we train two deep neural networks simultaneously, and let them teach each other given every mini-batch: firstly, each network feeds forward all data and selects some data of possibly clean labels; secondly, two networks communicate with each other what data in this mini-batch should be used for training; finally, each network back propagates the data selected by its peer network and updates itself. Empirical results on noisy versions of MNIST, CIFAR-10 and CIFAR-100 demonstrate that Co-teaching is much superior to the state-of-the-art methods in the robustness of trained deep models.»

### <a id='category-robust-loss-functions'></a>Robust Loss Functions: 2 papers found.
In the context of robust loss functions for deep learning under label noise, recent research has focused on developing loss functions that are inherently tolerant to noisy labels. For binary classification, theoretical results have established conditions for loss functions that ensure robustness to label noise, and these findings have been extended to multiclass settings \[[6](http://arxiv.org/abs/1712.09482)\]. The mean absolute error (MAE) loss has been identified as a noise-robust alternative to the commonly used categorical cross entropy (CCE) loss, as it can yield better performance in noisy label scenarios \[[15](http://arxiv.org/abs/1805.07836)\]. However, MAE has also been shown to perform poorly with deep neural networks on challenging datasets, prompting the development of more generalized noise-robust loss functions that combine the strengths of both MAE and CCE \[[15](http://arxiv.org/abs/1805.07836)\]. These new loss functions are designed to be compatible with existing deep learning architectures and algorithms, offering improved performance across a range of noisy label scenarios \[[15](http://arxiv.org/abs/1805.07836)\]. The theoretical grounding of these loss functions provides a solid foundation for their application in practical deep learning tasks where label noise is prevalent.
