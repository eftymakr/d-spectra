## Related Works

Video super-resolution (VSR) has been a focal point in computer vision, with recent advances leveraging deep learning to address the challenges of temporal coherence and detail preservation \[[1](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/c6a8ff4d9dcdf9e5d82310b847af999ed6655f65)\]. Early methods often relied on explicit motion estimation and compensation, which were computationally intensive and prone to errors \[[1](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/c6a8ff4d9dcdf9e5d82310b847af999ed6655f65)\]. For instance, the Real-Time Video Super-Resolution with Spatio-Temporal Networks and Motion Compensation \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/1611.05250)\] introduced a method that combined early fusion, slow fusion, and 3D convolutions to exploit temporal redundancies while maintaining real-time performance. However, these approaches often struggled with computational efficiency and temporal consistency \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/1611.05250)\].

More recently, recurrent architectures have emerged as a promising solution for VSR, enabling the model to assimilate information from multiple frames without increasing computational demands \[[4](http://arxiv.org/abs/1801.04590)\]. The Frame-Recurrent Video Super-Resolution \[[4](http://arxiv.org/abs/1801.04590)\] proposed an end-to-end trainable framework that uses previously inferred high-resolution (HR) estimates to super-resolve subsequent frames, naturally encouraging temporal consistency. This approach significantly reduced computational costs compared to sliding window methods \[[4](http://arxiv.org/abs/1801.04590)\]. Similarly, the Video Super-Resolution via Bidirectional Recurrent Convolutional Networks \[[17](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/a2c50f93f6d0892918e0ff653750d198ccab48d0)\] introduced a fully convolutional RNN that reduced the number of parameters and modeled temporal dependencies at a finer level, achieving faster performance than traditional multi-frame SR methods \[[17](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/a2c50f93f6d0892918e0ff653750d198ccab48d0)\].

Transformers have also been explored for VSR, although their direct application faced challenges due to the lack of spatial-temporal locality exploitation and feature alignment \[[20](http://arxiv.org/abs/2106.06847)\]. The Video Super-Resolution Transformer \[[20](http://arxiv.org/abs/2106.06847)\] addressed these issues by introducing a spatial-temporal convolutional self-attention layer and a bidirectional optical flow-based feed-forward layer, enhancing feature alignment and correlation discovery across video frames \[[20](http://arxiv.org/abs/2106.06847)\]. This work demonstrated the effectiveness of Transformers in VSR, achieving competitive results on benchmark datasets \[[20](http://arxiv.org/abs/2106.06847)\].

Diffusion models have gained traction for image and video generation due to their ability to produce high-quality outputs with controllable guidance \[[5](http://arxiv.org/abs/2112.10752)\]. However, applying diffusion models to video generation posed challenges in maintaining temporal coherence and reducing computational costs \[[24](http://arxiv.org/abs/2304.08818)\]. The Align Your Latents: High-Resolution Video Synthesis with Latent Diffusion Models \[[24](http://arxiv.org/abs/2304.08818)\] proposed a method to train diffusion models in a compressed latent space, enabling efficient video generation. This approach was further extended to handle high-resolution video synthesis, achieving state-of-the-art performance on real-world datasets \[[24](http://arxiv.org/abs/2304.08818)\].

Video super-resolution (VSR) has seen significant advancements with the integration of deep learning techniques, focusing on improving both the quality and efficiency of high-resolution video generation. Traditional methods often rely on motion estimation and compensation, which can introduce artifacts or computational overhead \[[1](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/c6a8ff4d9dcdf9e5d82310b847af999ed6655f65)\]. Recent approaches, such as spatio-temporal sub-pixel convolution networks, have effectively exploited temporal redundancies, enhancing reconstruction accuracy while maintaining real-time performance through efficient architectures like 3D convolutions and fast spatial transformers \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/1611.05250)\]. Similarly, the Temporally-Deformable Alignment Network (TDAN) addresses temporal alignment challenges by dynamically predicting sampling kernel offsets at the feature level, avoiding optical flow computation and reducing artifacts \[[6](http://arxiv.org/abs/1812.02898)\]. Another notable contribution is the progressive fusion network, which improves spatio-temporal information utilization and reduces computational costs compared to direct fusion or 3D convolution strategies \[[8](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/755227d7ea41b1d90df31f411f9ecbc998c20d26)\]. The BasicVSR framework further enhances recurrent structures by introducing second-order grid propagation and flow-guided deformable alignment, achieving significant improvements in PSNR over previous methods \[[12](http://arxiv.org/abs/2104.13371)\]. Additionally, the Multi-Correspondence Aggregation Network (MuCAN) leverages temporal multi-correspondence and cross-scale nonlocal operations to exploit self-similarity across frames, leading to state-of-the-art results on benchmark datasets \[[15](http://arxiv.org/abs/2007.11803)\]. These methods collectively highlight the shift towards more efficient and accurate VSR techniques that minimize explicit motion compensation and better utilize temporal information \[[16](http://arxiv.org/abs/2012.02181)\].

	Document 1: [Snippet]: «Recent advances in video super-resolution have shown that convolutional neural networks combined with motion compensation are able to merge information from multiple low-resolution (LR) frames to generate high-quality images. ### <a id='category-training-strategies-for-vsr'></a>Training Strategies for VSR: 5 papers found.
Training strategies for video super-resolution (VSR) have seen significant advancements through the integration of deep learning techniques, with a focus on improving both accuracy and training stability \[[7](http://arxiv.org/abs/1707.02921)\]. Researchers have explored the use of very deep convolutional networks, such as those inspired by VGG-net, which demonstrate improved accuracy with increased depth, although this comes at the cost of slower convergence \[[11](http://arxiv.org/abs/1511.04587)\]. To address this, training procedures have been refined, including the use of high learning rates combined with gradient clipping to enable faster training \[[11](http://arxiv.org/abs/1511.04587)\]. Additionally, residual learning techniques have been employed to enhance performance by removing unnecessary modules and stabilizing the training process \[[7](http://arxiv.org/abs/1707.02921)\]. The development of very deep residual channel attention networks (RCAN) further illustrates this trend, as they incorporate residual in residual (RIR) structures and channel attention mechanisms to improve the representation of high-frequency information while bypassing low-frequency details through skip connections \[[21](http://arxiv.org/abs/1807.02758)\]. Similarly, the LapSRN approach leverages a progressive reconstruction strategy using Laplacian pyramids, allowing for efficient upsampling and reducing computational complexity without the need for bicubic interpolation \[[28](http://arxiv.org/abs/1704.03915)\]. These strategies collectively highlight the ongoing efforts to enhance VSR performance through advanced network architectures and optimized training methods.
