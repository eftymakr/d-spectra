## Related Works

Time series forecasting has been a central challenge in various domains, including finance, energy, and logistics. Recent advances in deep learning have led to the development of numerous models tailored for time series analysis. Traditional approaches, such as autoregressive models and Gaussian processes, often struggle with capturing complex temporal dependencies and long-term patterns \[[4](http://arxiv.org/abs/1703.07015)\]. To address these limitations, several deep learning-based methods have been proposed. For instance, the LSTNet framework combines convolutional neural networks (CNNs) and recurrent neural networks (RNNs) to extract both short-term and long-term patterns \[[4](http://arxiv.org/abs/1703.07015)\]. Similarly, the Temporal Fusion Transformer (TFT) integrates recurrent layers with self-attention mechanisms to learn interpretable temporal relationships \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/1912.09363)\]. These models have demonstrated significant improvements over classical methods in various applications.

Transformers have also gained traction in time series forecasting due to their ability to capture long-range dependencies \[[9](http://arxiv.org/abs/2202.07125)\]. However, the use of Transformers in this domain has been challenged by issues such as quadratic time complexity and memory bottlenecks \[[5](http://arxiv.org/abs/2205.13504)\]. To mitigate these challenges, several variants have been proposed. For example, the Informer model introduces a ProbSparse self-attention mechanism that reduces computational complexity while maintaining performance \[[17](http://arxiv.org/abs/2012.07436)\]. Similarly, the Autoformer incorporates an Auto-Correlation mechanism to improve efficiency and accuracy in long-term forecasting \[[12](http://arxiv.org/abs/2106.13008)\]. The PatchTST model, on the other hand, employs a patch-based design to reduce attention map complexity and enable longer history attention \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2211.14730)\]. These innovations highlight the ongoing efforts to enhance Transformer-based models for time series tasks.

In addition to Transformers, other architectures have been developed for time series forecasting. The DeepAR model, based on autoregressive recurrent networks, provides probabilistic forecasts and has shown accuracy improvements over state-of-the-art methods \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/1704.04110)\]. The N-BEATS architecture, which uses deep residual blocks, has also demonstrated state-of-the-art performance on diverse datasets \[[13](http://arxiv.org/abs/1905.10437)\]. Furthermore, the Spatio-Temporal Graph Convolutional Network (STGCN) has been introduced to capture spatio-temporal dependencies in traffic forecasting \[[15](http://arxiv.org/abs/1709.04875)\]. These models illustrate the diversity of approaches in time series forecasting, ranging from purely neural network-based methods to hybrid models that combine classical statistical techniques with deep learning.

In the context of financial applications, the challenge of accurate forecasting is compounded by the need for uncertainty quantification and robust allocation strategies. ### <a id='category-transformer-based-forecasting-innovations'></a>Transformer-Based Forecasting Innovations: 9 papers found.
The related works in Transformer-based forecasting innovations focus on addressing the limitations of traditional models in capturing long-range dependencies and handling large-scale time series data. Several studies have introduced novel architectures to improve forecasting accuracy and efficiency. For instance, PatchTST proposes a segmentation approach to retain local semantic information and reduce computational complexity \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2211.14730)\]. Similarly, the Temporal Fusion Transformer (TFT) combines recurrent layers with self-attention mechanisms to enhance interpretability and performance in multi-horizon forecasting \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/1912.09363)\]. However, the use of Transformers in time series modeling faces challenges, such as the permutation-invariant self-attention mechanism leading to temporal information loss \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/1704.04110)\]. To address this, researchers have proposed models like LTSF-Linear, which outperforms complex Transformer-based models in some cases \[[5](http://arxiv.org/abs/2205.13504)\]. Additionally, models like LogSparse Transformer and iTransformer have been developed to overcome memory bottlenecks and improve the handling of long-term dependencies \[[4](http://arxiv.org/abs/1703.07015)\]\[[6](http://arxiv.org/abs/2004.13408)\]. The Autoformer introduces an Auto-Correlation mechanism to enhance dependency discovery and achieve state-of-the-art results in long-term forecasting \[[7](http://arxiv.org/abs/1907.00235)\]. Furthermore, the Frozen Pretrained Transformer (FPT) leverages pre-trained language or image models for time series analysis, demonstrating comparable or superior performance across various tasks \[[8](http://arxiv.org/abs/2210.02186)\]. These advancements highlight the ongoing efforts to refine Transformer-based models for more accurate and efficient time series forecasting.

### <a id='category-time-series-forecasting-models'></a>Time Series Forecasting Models: 9 papers found.
Time series forecasting models have seen significant advancements, particularly in handling complex temporal patterns and uncertainty. Traditional methods such as Autoregressive models and Gaussian Processes often struggle with long-term and short-term dependencies, as well as scalability issues \[[4](http://arxiv.org/abs/1703.07015)\]. In contrast, deep learning approaches like DeepAR \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/1704.04110)\], LSTNet \[[4](http://arxiv.org/abs/1703.07015)\], and TimesNet \[[8](http://arxiv.org/abs/2210.02186)\] have demonstrated superior performance by leveraging recurrent and convolutional networks to capture intricate temporal dynamics. These models are particularly effective in scenarios involving multivariate time series and large datasets, where classical methods fall short \[[4](http://arxiv.org/abs/1703.07015)\]. Moreover, recent frameworks such as SCINet \[[19](http://arxiv.org/abs/2106.09305)\] introduce novel architectures that enhance forecasting accuracy by incorporating downsampling and convolution techniques to model complex temporal variations. Additionally, hybrid models that combine the strengths of classical statistical methods with deep learning, such as those proposed in \[[18](http://arxiv.org/abs/1905.12417)\], offer a balanced approach by integrating uncertainty estimation and scalability. These developments underscore the evolving landscape of time series forecasting, emphasizing the need for models that can adapt to diverse applications and provide reliable predictions under varying conditions \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/1704.04110)\]\[[4](http://arxiv.org/abs/1703.07015)\]\[[8](http://arxiv.org/abs/2210.02186)\]\[[18](http://arxiv.org/abs/1905.12417)\]\[[19](http://arxiv.org/abs/2106.09305)\].

### <a id='category-deep-learning-architectures-for-forecasting'></a>Deep Learning Architectures for Forecasting: 3 papers found.
In the context of deep learning architectures for forecasting, recent research has focused on developing models that effectively capture temporal dependencies and improve prediction accuracy across diverse domains. For instance, the N-BEATS architecture leverages backward and forward residual links along with a deep stack of fully-connected layers, demonstrating state-of-the-art performance on benchmark datasets such as M3, M4, and TOURISM \[[13](http://arxiv.org/abs/1905.10437)\]. This architecture is notable for its interpretability, adaptability to various domains, and fast training speed, suggesting that deep learning primitives like residual blocks can be sufficient for forecasting tasks without domain-specific components \[[13](http://arxiv.org/abs/1905.10437)\]. Similarly, the Spatio-Temporal Graph Convolutional Networks (STGCN) framework addresses the challenges of modeling complex spatio-temporal dependencies in traffic forecasting by using graph-based convolutional structures, enabling faster training with fewer parameters and outperforming existing baselines \[[15](http://arxiv.org/abs/1709.04875)\]. These developments highlight the growing trend of integrating deep learning with domain-specific requirements to enhance forecasting accuracy and robustness. Additionally, there is increasing interest in hybrid models that combine statistical methods with neural networks to improve forecast reliability, as seen in the integration of domain-adjusted hybrid models with neural networks \[[6](http://arxiv.org/abs/2004.13408)\]. These approaches reflect the broader effort to bridge the gap between traditional forecasting techniques and modern deep learning methods, aiming to provide more accurate and reliable predictions in real-world applications \[[6](http://arxiv.org/abs/2004.13408)\].
