## Related Works

Vision Transformers (ViTs) have emerged as a powerful alternative to convolutional neural networks (CNNs) in computer vision tasks, leveraging the transformer architecture to effectively capture global context \[[5](http://arxiv.org/abs/2010.11929)\]. However, the computational complexity and memory requirements of ViTs pose significant challenges for hardware acceleration, particularly due to the sensitivity of operations like Softmax and LayerNorm to model accuracy \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2208.05163)\]. Existing approaches often rely on CPU for these complex operations, resulting in suboptimal performance due to communication overhead between the CPU and accelerator \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2208.05163)\]. This section reviews related works that address these challenges through hardware acceleration, quantization, and architectural improvements.

Several works have explored hardware acceleration for ViTs. Auto-ViT-Acc introduces an FPGA-aware framework for ViT acceleration using mixed-scheme quantization, achieving a 5.6x improvement in frame rate with minimal accuracy loss \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2208.05163)\]. ViTCoD proposes a co-design framework that integrates algorithmic and hardware-level optimizations, achieving speedups of up to 235.3× over general computing platforms \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/2210.09573)\]. Similarly, DynamicViT employs dynamic token sparsification to reduce computational load while maintaining accuracy \[[12](http://arxiv.org/abs/2106.02034)\]. HeatViT further enhances hardware efficiency through adaptive token pruning and quantization, achieving significant computation reduction and accuracy improvements \[[13](http://arxiv.org/abs/2211.08110)\]. These works highlight the potential of hardware-specific optimizations for ViT acceleration.

Quantization has also been explored to reduce computational costs and improve inference efficiency. VAQF introduces a fully automatic framework for low-bit ViT acceleration on FPGAs, achieving real-time execution with 8-bit activation quantization \[[7](http://arxiv.org/abs/2201.06618)\]. FQ-ViT addresses the challenges of post-training quantization for ViTs, proposing techniques like Power-of-Two Factor (PTF) and Log-Int-Softmax (LIS) to mitigate performance degradation \[[14](http://arxiv.org/abs/2111.13824)\]. These methods demonstrate that quantization can be effectively applied to ViTs with minimal accuracy loss.

Architectural improvements have also been proposed to enhance ViT performance and efficiency. T2T-ViT introduces a Tokens-to-Token transformation to better capture local structures and reduce parameter count \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/2101.11986)\]. CvT integrates convolutions into ViT to improve efficiency and performance while retaining the benefits of transformers \[[9](http://arxiv.org/abs/2103.15808)\]. LeViT further optimizes the speed/accuracy trade-off by incorporating principles from CNNs into the transformer architecture \[[10](http://arxiv.org/abs/2104.01136)\]. These works illustrate the ongoing efforts to refine ViT architectures for practical deployment.

### <a id='category-fpga-based-vit-acceleration-approaches'></a>FPGA-Based ViT Acceleration Approaches: 3 papers found.
Recent advancements in Vision Transformers (ViTs) have demonstrated significant improvements in computer vision tasks, but their complex architecture and high computational demands pose challenges for hardware acceleration \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2208.05163)\]. To address these issues, several FPGA-based acceleration frameworks have been proposed, focusing on optimizing both model accuracy and hardware efficiency. For instance, one approach introduces a mixed-scheme quantization method that enhances Top-1 accuracy by 0.47% to 1.36% compared to state-of-the-art quantization techniques without hardware acceleration \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2208.05163)\]. Another framework, VAQF, automatically determines quantization precision and accelerator settings to meet frame rate requirements, achieving 24 FPS with 8-bit activation quantization and 30 FPS with 6-bit activation quantization \[[7](http://arxiv.org/abs/2201.06618)\]. Additionally, HeatViT introduces an image-adaptive token pruning technique, improving accuracy by 0.7% to 8.9% and reducing computation by 28.4% to 65.3% while maintaining hardware efficiency \[[13](http://arxiv.org/abs/2211.08110)\]. These works highlight the growing interest in FPGA-based ViT acceleration, emphasizing the need for efficient quantization, hardware-aware optimization, and automated design methodologies to enable real-time deployment on resource-constrained devices.

	Document 1: [Snippet]: «Vision Transformers (ViTs) have achieved state-of-the-art performance on various vision tasks. However, ViTs’ self-attention module is still arguably a major bottleneck, limiting their achievable hardware efficiency and more extensive applications to resource constrained platforms. Meanwhile, existing accelerators dedicated to NLP Transformers are not optimal for ViTs. This is because there is a large difference between ViTs and Transformers for natural language processing (NLP) tasks: ViTs have a relatively fixed number of input tokens, whose attention maps can be pruned by up to 90% even with fixed sparse patterns, without severely hurting the model accuracy (e.g., <=1.5% under 90% pruning ratio); while NLP Transformers need to handle input sequences of varying numbers of tokens and rely on on-the-fly predictions of dynamic sparse attention patterns for each input to achieve a decent sparsity (e.g., >=50%). To this end, we propose a dedicated algorithm and accelerator co-design framework dubbed ViTCoD for accelerating ViTs. Specifically, on the algorithm level, ViTCoD prunes and polarizes the attention maps to have either denser or sparser fixed patterns for regularizing two levels of workloads without hurting the accuracy, largely reducing the attention computations while leaving room for alleviating the remaining dominant data movements; on top of that, we further integrate a lightweight and learnable auto-encoder module to enable trading the dominant high-cost data movements for lower-cost computations. On the hardware level, we develop a dedicated accelerator to simultaneously coordinate the aforementioned enforced denser and sparser workloads for boosted hardware utilization, while integrating on-chip encoder and decoder engines to leverage ViTCoD’s algorithm pipeline for much reduced data movements. Extensive experiments and ablation studies validate that ViTCoD largely reduces the dominant data movement costs, achieving speedups of up to 235.3×, 142.9×, 86.0×, 10.1×, and 6.8× over general computing platforms CPUs, EdgeGPUs, GPUs, and prior-art Transformer accelerators SpAtten and Sanger under an attention sparsity of 90%, respectively. Our code implementation is available at https://github.com/GATECH-EIC/ViTCoD.»

### <a id='category-vision-transformer-(vit)-architecture-and-performance'></a>Vision Transformer (ViT) Architecture and Performance: 10 papers found.
Vision Transformer (ViT) architectures have been extensively studied for their ability to capture global context in computer vision tasks, often outperforming traditional convolutional neural networks (CNNs) when pre-trained on large datasets \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/2210.09573)\]. However, ViTs face challenges in training efficiency and performance on midsize datasets like ImageNet due to issues such as poor tokenization that fails to model local structures \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2208.05163)\], and inefficient attention mechanisms that limit feature richness \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2208.05163)\]. To address these limitations, several variants have been proposed, such as T2T-ViT, which introduces a Tokens-to-Token transformation to better model local structures and reduce token count \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2208.05163)\], and CvT, which integrates convolutional components into ViT to improve efficiency and performance while retaining transformer benefits \[[9](http://arxiv.org/abs/2103.15808)\]. Additionally, approaches like CrossViT use dual-branch transformers with cross-attention mechanisms to combine features from different patch sizes effectively \[[6](http://arxiv.org/abs/2103.14899)\], while PiT incorporates spatial dimension reduction principles from CNNs to enhance ViT performance \[[16](http://arxiv.org/abs/2103.16302)\]. Furthermore, methods such as token labeling have been introduced to improve ViT performance by utilizing all image patch tokens for training rather than relying on a single class token \[[18](http://arxiv.org/abs/2104.10858)\]. These advancements highlight ongoing efforts to enhance ViT efficiency, accuracy, and applicability across various vision tasks.

### <a id='category-dynamic-optimization-strategies-for-vits'></a>Dynamic Optimization Strategies for ViTs: 3 papers found.
Dynamic Optimization Strategies for ViTs focus on reducing computational costs while maintaining model accuracy. These strategies include methods such as dynamic token sparsification, where redundant tokens are progressively pruned based on their importance \[[12](http://arxiv.org/abs/2106.02034)\], and patch slimming, which discards useless patches in a top-down manner to guide efficient patch selection \[[8](http://arxiv.org/abs/2106.02852)\]. Additionally, there are approaches that integrate sparsity from end to end, dynamically extracting and training sparse subnetworks while adhering to a fixed parameter budget \[[4](http://arxiv.org/abs/2106.04533)\]. These techniques aim to reduce FLOPs and improve inference speed without significant accuracy drops, with some methods even reporting improved accuracy due to sparsity \[[4](http://arxiv.org/abs/2106.04533)\]. The use of learnable token selectors to adaptively determine vital patches further enhances efficiency gains by combining data and architecture sparsity \[[4](http://arxiv.org/abs/2106.04533)\]. Overall, these strategies highlight the potential of dynamic optimization in making ViTs more efficient for practical deployment.

	Document 1: [Snippet]: «Network quantization significantly reduces model inference complexity and has been widely used in real-world deployments. However, most existing quantization methods have been developed mainly on Convolutional Neural Networks (CNNs), and suffer severe degradation when applied to fully quantized vision transformers. In this work, we demonstrate that many of these difficulties arise because of serious inter-channel variation in LayerNorm inputs, and present, Power-of-Two Factor (PTF), a systematic method to reduce the performance degradation and inference complexity of fully quantized vision transformers. In addition, observing an extreme non-uniform distribution in attention maps, we propose Log-Int-Softmax (LIS) to sustain that and simplify inference by using 4-bit quantization and the BitShift operator. Comprehensive experiments on various transformer-based architectures and benchmarks show that our Fully Quantized Vision Transformer (FQ-ViT) outperforms previous works while even using lower bit-width on attention maps. For instance, we reach 84.89% top-1 accuracy with ViT-L on ImageNet and 50.8 mAP with Cascade Mask R-CNN (Swin-S) on COCO. To our knowledge, we are the first to achieve lossless accuracy degradation (~1%) on fully quantized vision transformers. The code is available at https://github.com/megvii-research/FQ-ViT.»
