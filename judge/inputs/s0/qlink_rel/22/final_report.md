## Related Works

Unsupervised domain adaptation (UDA) for semantic segmentation has attracted significant attention due to the high cost of pixel-level annotations in real-world scenarios \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/1909.13589)\]. Traditional UDA methods assume that the category settings between source and target domains are known, which is often not the case in practical applications \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/2004.07703)\]. This assumption leads to performance degradation when private classes exist in the target domain \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/1910.13049)\]. To address this limitation, several approaches have been proposed to improve domain adaptation without prior knowledge of category settings.

One line of research focuses on improving the alignment between source and target domains by leveraging adversarial learning. For example, \[[4](http://arxiv.org/abs/1904.01886)\] introduces a method that aligns the marginal distribution in the feature space through adversarial learning. However, this global alignment strategy does not consider the local category-level feature distribution, which can lead to misalignment of well-aligned categories \[[5](http://arxiv.org/abs/1904.10620)\]. To address this, \[[6](http://arxiv.org/abs/2004.05498)\] introduces a category-level adversarial network that enforces local semantic consistency during global alignment. This approach improves the alignment of categories by adjusting the adversarial loss based on category-level alignment \[[7](http://arxiv.org/abs/1809.09478)\].

Another approach, \[[8](http://arxiv.org/abs/1707.09465)\], proposes a curriculum-style learning method to minimize the domain gap by first solving easy tasks and then inferring properties about the target domain. This method is particularly effective for urban scenes, where strong idiosyncrasies exist in the spatial layout of objects \[[9](http://arxiv.org/abs/2007.08702)\]. Similarly, \[[10](http://arxiv.org/abs/1802.10349)\] introduces a two-step self-supervised domain adaptation approach that minimizes both inter-domain and intra-domain gaps. This method separates the target domain into easy and hard splits and then uses self-supervised adaptation from the easy to the hard subdomain \[[11](http://arxiv.org/abs/2101.10979)\].

In addition to adversarial learning, some works focus on improving pseudo-label quality. \[[12](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/3f9f801aec1f87ae81524f1928407673805927cb)\] proposes a method that estimates prediction uncertainty during training to rectify pseudo label learning, which helps improve the robustness of the adaptation process. This approach dynamically sets confidence thresholds based on prediction variance and rectifies learning from noisy pseudo labels \[[13](http://arxiv.org/abs/1612.02649)\]. Similarly, \[[14](http://arxiv.org/abs/2003.03773)\] introduces a framework that uses self-ensembling with GAN-based data augmentation to enhance the performance of the segmentation network on the target domain \[[15](http://arxiv.org/abs/2103.16372)\].

This method achieves state-of-the-art results on synthetic-to-real benchmarks \[[17](http://arxiv.org/abs/1711.03213)\]. ### <a id='category-pseudo-label-quality-improvement'></a>Pseudo-Label Quality Improvement: 6 papers found.
In the context of pseudo-label quality improvement for unsupervised domain adaptation (UDA) in semantic segmentation, several approaches have been proposed to address the challenges posed by noisy or unreliable pseudo-labels. These pseudo-labels, generated from models trained on source domains, often suffer from domain shift and class imbalance, leading to poor generalization on target domains \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/1909.13589)\]. To mitigate these issues, methods such as entropy minimization \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/1909.13589)\], image-wise weighting ratios \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/1909.13589)\], and uncertainty estimation \[[14](http://arxiv.org/abs/2003.03773)\] have been introduced to refine the training process and improve the reliability of pseudo-labels. Additionally, techniques like DACS \[[9](http://arxiv.org/abs/2007.08702)\] and ProDA \[[11](http://arxiv.org/abs/2101.10979)\] have been developed to enhance pseudo-label quality by leveraging mixed domain samples and prototype-based feature alignment, respectively. Furthermore, instance adaptive self-training frameworks \[[21](http://arxiv.org/abs/2008.12197)\] and region-guided regularization \[[5](http://arxiv.org/abs/1904.10620)\] have been proposed to improve pseudo-label consistency and reduce noise. Recent studies also highlight the importance of network architecture in UDA, with Transformers showing promising potential for domain adaptation tasks \[[28](http://arxiv.org/abs/2111.14887)\]. These approaches collectively aim to enhance the robustness and accuracy of semantic segmentation models when adapting from synthetic to real-world data.

### <a id='category-domain-alignment-techniques'></a>Domain Alignment Techniques: 13 papers found.
Unsupervised domain adaptation for semantic segmentation (UDA-SS) has been extensively studied, with a focus on aligning features between source and target domains to improve generalization \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/1910.13049)\]. Early approaches relied on adversarial learning to make models invariant to domain shifts, but they often struggled with category-level misalignment and failed to account for private classes in the target domain \[[11](http://arxiv.org/abs/2101.10979)\]. More recent methods, such as the category anchor-guided (CAG) model, have introduced category-aware feature alignment to explicitly learn shared discriminative features and classifiers \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/1910.13049)\]. Similarly, the Separated Semantic Feature based domain adaptation network (SSF-DAN) addresses inconsistent adaptation by independently adapting semantic features across domains \[[12](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/3f9f801aec1f87ae81524f1928407673805927cb)\]. Another approach, the fine-grained adversarial learning strategy, preserves internal semantic structures by differentiating domains at the class level \[[22](http://arxiv.org/abs/2007.09222)\]. Additionally, methods like the Dual Channel-wise Alignment Networks (DCAN) perform channel-wise feature alignment to preserve spatial structures and semantic information \[[23](http://arxiv.org/abs/1804.05827)\]. These techniques highlight the growing emphasis on domain-specific and class-level alignment to mitigate domain shift and improve performance on real-world scenarios. The proposed UniMAP framework builds upon these advancements by introducing domain-specific prototypes and target-based image matching to enhance common class identification and reduce confusion with private classes \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/1909.13589)\].

In this paper, we introduce a self-ensembling technique, one of the successful methods for domain adaptation in classification. However, applying self-ensembling to semantic segmentation is very difficult because heavily-tuned manual data augmentation used in self-ensembling is not useful to reduce the large domain gap in the semantic segmentation. To overcome this limitation, we propose a novel framework consisting of two components, which are complementary to each other. First, we present a data augmentation method based on Generative Adversarial Networks (GANs), which is computationally efficient and effective to facilitate domain alignment. Given those augmented images, we apply self-ensembling to enhance the performance of the segmentation network on the target domain. The proposed method outperforms state-of-the-art semantic segmentation methods on unsupervised domain adaptation benchmarks.»
