## Related Works

Modern databases often trade strong transactional isolation for weak isolation guarantees to achieve low latency and high throughput, especially in distributed systems that must tolerate network partitions \[[11](http://arxiv.org/abs/1302.0309)\]. This trade-off has led to the proliferation of weak isolation levels such as Read Committed (RC), Read Atomic (RA), and Causal Consistency (CC), which are designed to balance availability with correctness \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/1908.04509)\]. However, this approach has introduced new challenges in ensuring data consistency, as several production databases have been found to suffer from isolation bugs that violate their promised guarantees \[[16](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/04178561c9d8cd42904ed0a836e08e55b84703cf)\].

Black-box testing has emerged as a prominent technique for detecting these isolation bugs by verifying whether transaction histories adhere to a prescribed isolation level \[[16](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/04178561c9d8cd42904ed0a836e08e55b84703cf)\]. Early work on black-box checking focused on strong isolation levels, such as serializability, which was shown to be NP-complete \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/1908.04509)\]. More recently, weak isolation levels like RC, RA, and CC have been shown to be polynomial-time checkable, enabling the development of scalable testing tools \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/1908.04509)\]. However, existing testers often have large polynomial complexity, limiting their applicability to moderate-sized workloads \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/1908.04509)\].

Several tools have been proposed to address these limitations. Elle \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/2003.10554)\] is a checker that infers isolation anomalies by generating client workloads and injecting faults, but it lacks completeness and efficiency for real-world databases. Similarly, PolySI \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/2301.07313)\] and Viper \[[8](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/9f96a48d2bc0e77004b32960495685be6c301c63)\] have been developed to efficiently check Snapshot Isolation (SI), but they are limited to specific isolation levels. Plume \[[5](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/cbe191bda20907994d45bc835d20446968370add)\] introduces a more general approach, supporting multiple weak isolation levels with sound and complete characterizations. However, these tools still face challenges in scalability and efficiency for large workloads.

The complexity of checking weak isolation levels has also been studied in the context of formal verification and automated analysis. For instance, the work by \[[12](http://arxiv.org/abs/1806.08416)\] combines dependency graphs with abstract executions to detect serializability violations under weak consistency models, leveraging SMT solvers for automated reasoning. Similarly, \[[15](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/322ca3c1911230a84aab81b0f5ff8c2b543c56f7)\] proposes a static analysis technique for detecting non-serializable behaviors in causally consistent databases, using SMT solvers to check first-order logic formulas. The category "Efficient Testing Tools for Weak Isolation" focuses on the development of tools that enable the detection and verification of isolation bugs in databases that use weak isolation levels, such as Snapshot Isolation (SI) and Read Committed (RC). These tools are essential because weak isolation levels, while offering better performance, can introduce subtle correctness issues that are difficult to detect and debug \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/2301.07313)\]. For instance, PolySI \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/2301.07313)\] is a black-box checker that efficiently identifies SI violations by leveraging generalized polygraphs and SMT solvers, while also providing counterexamples to aid in debugging. Similarly, Plume \[[5](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/cbe191bda20907994d45bc835d20446968370add)\] introduces a complete and efficient black-box checker for weak isolation levels, utilizing modular transactional anomalous patterns and novel techniques like vectors and tree clocks to enhance performance. MonkeyDB \[[4](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/615ff2896b52bee2cbfd64ced324106a29b73397)\] serves as a mock storage system that simulates weak isolation behaviors, allowing developers to test their applications under various isolation levels and identify potential issues. IsoDiff \[[13](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/405ae0ebf74cfbf5f2edec62e2966827dcdc9bef)\] further supports developers by identifying representative anomalies and reducing false positives through correlation detection and timing relationship checks. Lastly, Emme \[[16](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/04178561c9d8cd42904ed0a836e08e55b84703cf)\] addresses the challenge of predicate operations in isolation checking by introducing version certificate recovery and expected serialization order, significantly improving the efficiency of checking serializable histories. These tools collectively highlight the ongoing efforts to improve the reliability and correctness of weak isolation implementations in modern databases.
