## Related Works

Narrative summarization, which involves condensing long-form texts such as books, movies, and TV scripts into coherent and accurate summaries, remains a challenging task for large language models (LLMs). Existing approaches often struggle with maintaining thematic coherence, capturing intricate plotlines, and preserving character interactions \[[16](http://arxiv.org/abs/1908.08960)\]. This challenge is exacerbated by the lack of fine-tuning, which is typically required to adapt LLMs to specific tasks \[[5](http://arxiv.org/abs/2005.14165)\]. In contrast, our proposed NexusSum framework introduces a multi-agent LLM approach that enables structured, sequential processing of long-form narratives without the need for fine-tuning \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2308.00352)\].

Several studies have explored the use of multi-agent systems to enhance the performance of LLMs in complex tasks. For instance, MetaGPT introduces a framework that incorporates standardized operating procedures (SOPs) into prompts to improve structured coordination among agents \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2308.00352)\]. This approach mandates modular outputs, allowing agents to validate their work and minimize compounded errors. Similarly, AutoGen enables next-generation LLM applications through a multi-agent conversation framework, facilitating collaborative problem-solving \[[6](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/1a4c6856292b8c64d19a812a77f0aa6fd47cb96c)\]. These frameworks highlight the potential of multi-agent systems to improve task performance by leveraging structured collaboration and role assignment.

In the realm of multi-agent deep reinforcement learning, communication has been identified as a key mechanism for coordinating behaviors and improving learning performance \[[7](http://arxiv.org/abs/2203.08975)\]. This aligns with the principles of our NexusSum framework, which uses a structured pipeline to optimize chunk processing and control output length. The hierarchical multi-LLM summarization approach in NexusSum is inspired by the modular and task-specific design of such systems, ensuring that each agent contributes effectively to the overall summarization process \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2308.00352)\].

The use of external tools to enhance LLM capabilities has also been explored. For example, Toolformer demonstrates that LLMs can be trained to use external APIs, such as calculators and search engines, to improve their performance on tasks requiring factual lookup or arithmetic \[[15](http://arxiv.org/abs/2302.04761)\]. This is particularly relevant to our work, as our framework leverages structured processing to ensure accurate and coherent outputs without relying on external tools.

In addition, the importance of structured data formats in improving the coherence of generated text has been highlighted in several studies. For example, the Dialogue-to-Description Transformation in NexusSum is inspired by the need to standardize input formats, similar to the preprocessing techniques used in neural machine translation \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/1409.0473)\]. This ensures that the input is consistent and facilitates more effective summarization.

### <a id='category-fine-tuning-in-llms'></a>Fine-Tuning in LLMs: 4 papers found.
The category "Fine-Tuning in LLMs" encompasses a range of approaches aimed at improving the performance of large language models (LLMs) on specific tasks through transfer learning and few-shot learning techniques. Transfer learning, where models are pre-trained on large corpora and then fine-tuned on downstream tasks, has become a cornerstone of modern NLP, enabling state-of-the-art results across various benchmarks \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/1910.10683)\]. This approach often involves converting tasks into text-to-text formats and leveraging pre-trained models with extensive unlabeled data \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2308.00352)\]. Recent work has shown that scaling up LLMs significantly enhances few-shot performance, sometimes rivaling traditional fine-tuning methods, as demonstrated by GPT-3, which achieves strong results without any gradient updates or fine-tuning \[[5](http://arxiv.org/abs/2005.14165)\]. Additionally, models like BART, which use denoising autoencoders for pre-training, have shown effectiveness in both text generation and comprehension tasks, achieving new state-of-the-art results on abstractive dialogue and summarization \[[13](http://arxiv.org/abs/1910.13461)\]. Furthermore, retrieval-augmented generation (RAG) models combine pre-trained parametric and non-parametric memory to enhance knowledge-intensive tasks, outperforming traditional seq2seq models in open-domain question answering \[[14](http://arxiv.org/abs/2005.11401)\]. These developments highlight the ongoing evolution of fine-tuning strategies in LLMs, emphasizing the importance of scalable, task-agnostic methods for handling complex and diverse NLP challenges.
