## Related Works

Recent advancements in large language models (LLMs) have emphasized the importance of understanding how feature representations evolve across layers, as this knowledge is critical for improving interpretability and robustness \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2307.09288)\]. Several studies have explored the dynamics of representation learning in LLMs, focusing on how fine-tuning affects model behavior. For example, the work of \[[17](http://arxiv.org/abs/2202.10054)\] highlights that fine-tuning can distort pretrained features and underperform out-of-distribution, suggesting that alternative strategies like linear probing may yield better generalization. This aligns with the findings of \[[18](http://arxiv.org/abs/2109.05687)\], which introduces Child-Tuning—a method that updates only a subset of parameters in large models, thereby preserving pretrained features while adapting to new tasks.

The role of fine-tuning in aligning models with human preferences has also been extensively studied. \[[20](http://arxiv.org/abs/2203.02155)\] demonstrates that fine-tuning with human feedback can significantly improve the truthfulness and reduce toxic outputs of language models, as seen in the InstructGPT framework. This approach has been further refined with methods like Direct Preference Optimization (DPO), which simplifies the alignment process by eliminating the need for complex reinforcement learning setups \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/2305.18290)\]. DPO has shown promising results in improving response quality and sentiment control, making it a valuable technique for practical deployment.

Another critical aspect of fine-tuning is its impact on model performance across different tasks and datasets. \[[15](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/1e077413b25c4d34945cc2707e17e46ed4fe784a)\] introduces Universal Language Model Fine-tuning (ULMFiT), which achieves state-of-the-art results on text classification tasks with minimal training data. This is further supported by \[[12](http://arxiv.org/abs/1902.00751)\], which proposes adapter modules as a parameter-efficient alternative to full fine-tuning, allowing for compact and extensible models that maintain high performance while reducing computational overhead.

The effectiveness of fine-tuning can also be influenced by architectural choices and training strategies. \[[14](http://arxiv.org/abs/2012.13255)\] investigates the intrinsic dimensionality of language models and finds that pre-trained models have a low intrinsic dimension, enabling efficient fine-tuning even with limited data. This insight is crucial for understanding why fine-tuning remains a powerful technique despite the large number of parameters in modern models.

Moreover, the use of prompt-based methods like prefix-tuning has emerged as a lightweight alternative to full fine-tuning. \[[19](http://arxiv.org/abs/2101.00190)\] shows that prefix-tuning, which optimizes a sequence of continuous task-specific vectors, can achieve comparable performance to full fine-tuning while significantly reducing parameter updates. This approach is particularly beneficial for tasks where storage and computational resources are constrained.

Existing methods for gaining such steerability collect human labels of the relative quality of model generations and fine-tune the unsupervised LM to align with these preferences, often with reinforcement learning from human feedback (RLHF). However, RLHF is a complex and often unstable procedure, first fitting a reward model that reflects the human preferences, and then fine-tuning the large unsupervised LM using reinforcement learning to maximize this estimated reward without drifting too far from the original model. Our experiments show that DPO can fine-tune LMs to align with human preferences as well as or better than existing methods. Notably, fine-tuning with DPO exceeds PPO-based RLHF in ability to control sentiment of generations, and matches or improves response quality in summarization and single-turn dialogue while being substantially simpler to implement and train.»

The category "Feature Representation Evolution in LLMs" explores how feature representations change across layers in large language models, with a focus on improving interpretability and robustness. Recent studies have identified critical layers linked to specific functions or behaviors, but these approaches often rely on data-dependent analyses of fine-tuned models, limiting their applicability to post-hoc settings \[[10](http://arxiv.org/abs/1810.04805)\]. In contrast, a data-oblivious approach has been introduced, which identifies intrinsic critical layers in pre-fine-tuned LLMs by analyzing representation dynamics via Centered Kernel Alignment (CKA) \[[10](http://arxiv.org/abs/1810.04805)\]. This method reveals that layers with significant shifts in representation space are also most affected during fine-tuning, a pattern consistent across tasks for a given model \[[10](http://arxiv.org/abs/1810.04805)\]. Spectral analysis further shows that these shifts are driven by changes in the top principal components, which encode semantic transitions from rationales to conclusions \[[10](http://arxiv.org/abs/1810.04805)\]. This understanding has practical implications, such as in efficient domain adaptation, where fine-tuning critical layers leads to greater loss reduction compared to non-critical layers, and in backdoor defense, where freezing critical layers reduces attack success rates by up to 40% \[[10](http://arxiv.org/abs/1810.04805)\]. The evolution of feature representations is also reflected in the development of models like BERT, which uses bidirectional encoder representations from Transformers to achieve state-of-the-art results on various NLP tasks \[[10](http://arxiv.org/abs/1810.04805)\]. Similarly, the introduction of Llama 3 highlights advancements in multilingual support, coding, reasoning, and tool usage, with models capable of handling up to 128K tokens and achieving competitive performance on diverse tasks \[[6](http://arxiv.org/abs/2407.21783)\]. These developments underscore the ongoing evolution of feature representation techniques in LLMs, aiming to enhance both performance and interpretability.

### <a id='category-fine-tuning-impact-on-pretrained-features'></a>Fine-Tuning Impact on Pretrained Features: 1 papers found.
Context: 
	Document 1: [Snippet]: «When transferring a pretrained model to a downstream task, two popular methods are full fine-tuning (updating all the model parameters) and linear probing (updating only the last linear layer—the “head”). It is well known that fine-tuning leads to better accuracy in-distribution (ID). However, in this paper, we find that fine-tuning can achieve worse accuracy than linear probing out-of-distribution (OOD) when the pretrained features are good and the distribution shift is large. On 10 distribution shift datasets, fine-tuning obtains on average 2% higher accuracy ID but 7% lower accuracy OOD than linear probing. We show theoretically that this tradeoff between ID and OOD accuracy arises even in a simple setting: fine-tuning overparameterized two-layer linear networks. We prove that the OOD error of fine-tuning is high when we initialize with a fixed or random head—this is because while fine-tuning learns the head, the lower layers of the neural network change simultaneously and distort the pretrained features. Our analysis suggests that the easy two-step strategy of linear probing then full fine-tuning (LP-FT), sometimes used as a fine-tuning heuristic, combines the benefits of both fine-tuning and linear probing. Empirically, LP-FT outperforms both fine-tuning and linear probing on the above datasets (1% better ID, 10% better OOD than full fine-tuning).»
