## Related Works

The rapid growth of open-source machine learning (ML) resources has significantly impacted information retrieval (IR) research, as noted in recent studies \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2305.11164)\]. However, existing platforms such as Hugging Face have limitations in their ability to support advanced queries and analyses, such as tracing model evolution and recommending relevant datasets \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2305.11164)\]. This section reviews related works that address these challenges and contribute to the broader field of ML resource management and IR.

Several studies have examined the characteristics and usage patterns of ML models on Hugging Face. For instance, a repository mining study found that while Hugging Face hosts a vast number of ML models, the carbon footprint of these models is often poorly reported, with only a small proportion of models providing detailed emissions data \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/2311.13380)\]. This highlights a gap in the transparency and documentation of ML models, which is critical for effective resource management \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/2311.13380)\]. Similarly, another study analyzed the evolution and maintenance of ML models on Hugging Face, revealing that model maintenance is often inconsistent, with a lack of structured documentation and versioning practices \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/2401.13822)\].

The importance of structured representations for advanced querying and analysis has been emphasized in multiple studies. For example, a paper on dataset documentation practices on Hugging Face found that while dataset cards are widely used, they often lack completeness and consistency, particularly in sections related to usage and considerations \[[4](http://arxiv.org/abs/2303.02552)\]. This lack of structured data limits the ability to perform comprehensive analyses, such as tracing model evolution or recommending datasets based on specific criteria \[[4](http://arxiv.org/abs/2303.02552)\]. Another study on the Hugging Face Hub highlighted the need for improved documentation and versioning practices to support better resource management and reuse \[[5](http://arxiv.org/abs/2307.14841)\].

The development of knowledge graphs for ML resources has been explored in prior work. A study on the Hugging Face ecosystem proposed the use of semantic versioning to improve the clarity and consistency of model releases, which is essential for effective resource management \[[6](http://arxiv.org/abs/2406.08205)\]. Additionally, a survey on transfer learning and domain adaptation highlighted the importance of structured data for knowledge transfer, which aligns with the goals of HuggingKG \[[7](http://arxiv.org/abs/2405.13058)\]. These studies underscore the need for structured representations to enable advanced queries and analyses in ML resource management.

In the context of IR, the use of knowledge graphs for resource recommendation and classification has been explored. A paper on Hugging Face and Pyserini interoperability proposed the integration of IR tools with the Hugging Face ecosystem to facilitate qualitative data analysis \[[8](http://arxiv.org/abs/2401.13177)\]. This approach aligns with the development of HuggingBench, which leverages the structured data in HuggingKG to support multi-task benchmarking for IR tasks

### <a id='category-carbon-footprint-of-ml-models'></a>Carbon Footprint of ML Models: 2 papers found.
The category "Carbon Footprint of ML Models" is central to understanding the environmental impact of machine learning systems. Existing research highlights that the rapid growth of ML models, particularly on platforms like Hugging Face, has significantly increased their carbon footprint due to larger model sizes and more complex training processes \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2305.11164)\]. Studies have shown that while there is growing awareness of this issue, the measurement and reporting of carbon emissions remain inconsistent, with only a small proportion of models providing detailed emissions data \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2305.11164)\]. This lack of standardization hinders efforts to compare and optimize the energy efficiency of different models. Furthermore, research indicates that factors such as model size, dataset size, and application domain (e.g., NLP) are strongly correlated with carbon emissions \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2305.11164)\]. To address these challenges, scholars have proposed frameworks for categorizing models based on their carbon emission reporting practices and carbon efficiency \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2305.11164)\]. These initiatives aim to promote transparency and sustainability within the ML community by encouraging more rigorous energy reporting and the development of carbon-efficient models \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2305.11164)\]. Additionally, there is a growing emphasis on the need for improved software measurement tools to support more accurate and consistent energy reporting practices \[[9](http://arxiv.org/abs/2402.07323)\].

### <a id='category-model-evolution-and-maintenance'></a>Model Evolution and Maintenance: 4 papers found.
The category "Model Evolution and Maintenance" encompasses a range of studies focused on understanding how machine learning models are developed, maintained, and evolve over time within platforms like Hugging Face. Research in this area highlights the rapid turnover of pre-trained models (PTMs) compared to traditional software, indicating a more dynamic and fast-paced evolution process \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/2311.13380)\]. This is further supported by findings that show a strong correlation between documentation quality and model popularity, suggesting that well-documented models are more likely to be reused and adopted by the community \[[6](http://arxiv.org/abs/2406.08205)\]. Additionally, studies have identified challenges faced by users when reusing PTMs, such as limited guidance for beginners, difficulties in interpreting model outputs, and a lack of comprehensive understanding of model behavior \[[8](http://arxiv.org/abs/2401.13177)\]. These challenges are compounded by the observation that despite the introduction of documentation tools, the quantity and quality of model documentation have not significantly improved over time, leading to continued difficulties in model comprehension and selection \[[8](http://arxiv.org/abs/2401.13177)\]. Furthermore, research has explored the maintenance aspects of models, including the classification of commit messages into corrective, perfective, and adaptive categories, and the development of new systems to estimate maintenance status based on multiple attributes \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2305.11164)\]. These studies collectively underscore the importance of structured representations and advanced querying capabilities to better manage and analyze the evolution of ML models, as highlighted in the paper's abstract \[[14](http://arxiv.org/abs/2105.01984)\].

By analyzing all 7,433 dataset documentation on Hugging Face, our investigation provides an overview of the Hugging Face dataset ecosystem and insights into dataset documentation practices, yielding 5 main findings: (1) The dataset card completion rate shows marked heterogeneity correlated with dataset popularity. (4) Our findings also highlight the need for improved accessibility and reproducibility of datasets in the Usage sections. (5) In addition, our human annotation evaluation emphasizes the pivotal role of comprehensive dataset content in shaping individuals' perceptions of a dataset card's overall quality. Overall, our study offers a unique perspective on analyzing dataset documentation through large-scale data science analysis and underlines the need for more thorough dataset documentation in machine learning research.»

### <a id='category-ml-resource-management-platforms'></a>ML Resource Management Platforms: 2 papers found.
The category "ML Resource Management Platforms" encompasses efforts to organize and enhance the use of open-source machine learning resources, such as models and datasets, through structured representations and advanced querying capabilities. Existing platforms like Hugging Face have become central to the open AI ecosystem, but they often lack the structured data necessary for complex analyses such as tracing model evolution or recommending relevant datasets \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2305.11164)\]. This limitation has prompted the development of knowledge graphs, such as HuggingKG, which integrates large-scale, structured representations of ML resources to enable more sophisticated queries and analyses \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/2311.13380)\]. HuggingKG, constructed from the Hugging Face community, includes 2.6 million nodes and 6.2 million edges, capturing domain-specific relations and textual attributes to support tasks like resource recommendation, classification, and tracing \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/2401.13822)\]. Such platforms are part of a broader trend toward improving the management and accessibility of open-source ML resources, which is critical for advancing research and development in artificial intelligence \[[4](http://arxiv.org/abs/2303.02552)\]. Additionally, there is growing interest in leveraging large language models (LLMs) to act as controllers for managing diverse AI models, enabling the execution of complex tasks across multiple modalities and domains \[[5](http://arxiv.org/abs/2307.14841)\]. These developments highlight the increasing importance of structured data and intelligent systems in the management of open-source ML resources.
