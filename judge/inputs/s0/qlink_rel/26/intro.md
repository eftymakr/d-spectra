## Related Works

Differential privacy (DP) has become the de facto standard for achieving privacy in data analysis \[[20](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/e4ce10063cd25447dcde75c2d9ce327446ced952)\]. However, its implementation is vulnerable to malicious behaviors by servers, especially in distributed settings \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/1811.12469)\]. For instance, servers may sample noise from incorrect distributions or generate correlated noise while appearing to follow established protocols \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/1811.12469)\]. This issue has been explored in several works that examine the robustness of DP mechanisms against adversarial manipulation \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/1909.09630)\]. 

In the local differential privacy (LDP) model, each user perturbs their data locally before sending it to an untrusted data collector \[[14](http://arxiv.org/abs/1606.05053)\]. While LDP provides strong privacy guarantees, it is vulnerable to manipulation attacks where a small fraction of users can obscure the distribution of honest users' inputs \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/1909.09630)\]. This vulnerability has led to the development of countermeasures such as verifiable randomization mechanisms that allow the data collector to verify the completeness of the randomization process \[[4](http://arxiv.org/abs/2104.06569)\]. These mechanisms prevent malicious data providers from manipulating their output before sending it to the collector \[[4](http://arxiv.org/abs/2104.06569)\].

Several studies have focused on improving the utility of LDP protocols while ensuring robustness against adversarial attacks. For example, the paper \[[6](http://arxiv.org/abs/1905.08320)\] discusses the use of frequency oracle (FO) protocols to estimate frequencies of values, and shows that adding post-processing steps can significantly improve accuracy. Similarly, \[[7](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/25b7fe2ac81189952c72c5b869a6be1c5fcf410c)\] introduces PrivKV, a system for key-value data collection under LDP, which addresses the poor estimation accuracy due to clueless perturbation by proposing iterative solutions. 

The shuffled model of differential privacy has also been proposed as a middle ground between the local and central models, offering the accuracy of the central model without requiring a trusted server \[[17](http://arxiv.org/abs/1808.01394)\]. This model uses an anonymous channel to randomly permute user-supplied messages, allowing for more accurate statistical computations while preserving privacy. 

In addition, the work \[[18](http://arxiv.org/abs/2005.11753)\] addresses the challenge of publishing streaming data under both centralized and local differential privacy, proposing a framework that includes a threshold optimizer, a perturber, and a smoother to improve the utility of the results. This approach is particularly useful for scenarios where the maximal value in the stream can be large, leading to high noise and poor utility.

The paper \[[15](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/770ad70607ce60d8c411916129a423b5aad45e93)\] provides a comprehensive survey of LDP, covering its fundamental concepts, privatization mechanisms, and applications in statistical estimation and machine learning. It also highlights the importance of privacy amplification techniques and the challenges of deploying LDP in practice.
