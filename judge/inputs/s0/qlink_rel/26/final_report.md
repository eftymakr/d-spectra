## Related Works

Differential privacy (DP) has become the de facto standard for achieving privacy in data analysis \[[20](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/e4ce10063cd25447dcde75c2d9ce327446ced952)\]. However, its implementation is vulnerable to malicious behaviors by servers, especially in distributed settings \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/1811.12469)\]. For instance, servers may sample noise from incorrect distributions or generate correlated noise while appearing to follow established protocols \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/1811.12469)\]. This issue has been explored in several works that examine the robustness of DP mechanisms against adversarial manipulation \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/1909.09630)\]. 

In the local differential privacy (LDP) model, each user perturbs their data locally before sending it to an untrusted data collector \[[14](http://arxiv.org/abs/1606.05053)\]. While LDP provides strong privacy guarantees, it is vulnerable to manipulation attacks where a small fraction of users can obscure the distribution of honest users' inputs \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/1909.09630)\]. This vulnerability has led to the development of countermeasures such as verifiable randomization mechanisms that allow the data collector to verify the completeness of the randomization process \[[4](http://arxiv.org/abs/2104.06569)\]. These mechanisms prevent malicious data providers from manipulating their output before sending it to the collector \[[4](http://arxiv.org/abs/2104.06569)\].

Several studies have focused on improving the utility of LDP protocols while ensuring robustness against adversarial attacks. For example, the paper \[[6](http://arxiv.org/abs/1905.08320)\] discusses the use of frequency oracle (FO) protocols to estimate frequencies of values, and shows that adding post-processing steps can significantly improve accuracy. Similarly, \[[7](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/25b7fe2ac81189952c72c5b869a6be1c5fcf410c)\] introduces PrivKV, a system for key-value data collection under LDP, which addresses the poor estimation accuracy due to clueless perturbation by proposing iterative solutions. 

The shuffled model of differential privacy has also been proposed as a middle ground between the local and central models, offering the accuracy of the central model without requiring a trusted server \[[17](http://arxiv.org/abs/1808.01394)\]. This model uses an anonymous channel to randomly permute user-supplied messages, allowing for more accurate statistical computations while preserving privacy.

### <a id='category-local-differential-privacy-(ldp)-models'></a>Local Differential Privacy (LDP) Models: 9 papers found.
Local Differential Privacy (LDP) has been extensively studied for enabling privacy-preserving data analytics in distributed settings, where users perturb their data locally before sending it to an untrusted data collector \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/1909.09630)\]. However, existing LDP protocols are vulnerable to adversarial manipulation, where a small fraction of users can significantly distort the results by injecting fake data, leading to inaccurate frequency estimates or heavy hitter identification \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/1811.12469)\]. This issue is exacerbated when the privacy level is high or the domain size is large, as the adversary can obscure the true distribution of honest users' inputs \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/1911.02046)\]. To address these limitations, researchers have proposed various countermeasures, such as post-processing steps that leverage the non-negativity and normalization of frequency estimates to improve accuracy \[[6](http://arxiv.org/abs/1905.08320)\], or iterative methods like PrivKVM and PrivKVM+ that refine estimation results through multiple rounds of perturbation and calibration \[[7](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/25b7fe2ac81189952c72c5b869a6be1c5fcf410c)\]. Additionally, new mechanisms have been developed for repeated data collection, ensuring robustness over time while maintaining formal privacy guarantees \[[8](http://arxiv.org/abs/1712.01524)\]. Furthermore, recent work has focused on improving the accuracy of LDP for multidimensional data and complex tasks like stochastic gradient descent, demonstrating that novel mechanisms can outperform existing solutions in terms of worst-case noise variance \[[11](http://arxiv.org/abs/1907.00782)\]. These advancements highlight the ongoing efforts to enhance the utility and security of LDP protocols while addressing their fundamental limitations.

### <a id='category-shuffled-model-of-differential-privacy'></a>Shuffled Model of Differential Privacy: 1 papers found.
The shuffled model of differential privacy represents a middle ground between the local and central models, offering a balance between accuracy and trust assumptions \[[17](http://arxiv.org/abs/1808.01394)\]. Unlike the local model, where each user individually randomizes their data before sending it to a server, the shuffled model introduces an anonymous channel that randomly permutes messages, allowing for more efficient aggregation without requiring a fully trusted server \[[17](http://arxiv.org/abs/1808.01394)\]. This model is particularly relevant in distributed settings where users wish to avoid trusting a central authority but still achieve higher accuracy than the local model \[[17](http://arxiv.org/abs/1808.01394)\]. The shuffled model is a special case of the ESA framework \[[17](http://arxiv.org/abs/1808.01394)\], and it has been shown to provide the benefits of the central model, such as higher accuracy, while avoiding its trust assumptions \[[17](http://arxiv.org/abs/1808.01394)\]. However, it is also demonstrated that the shuffled model's power lies strictly between the local and central models, as certain tasks, like the selection problem, require exponentially higher sample complexity in the shuffled model compared to the central model \[[17](http://arxiv.org/abs/1808.01394)\]. This distinction highlights the unique trade-offs and limitations of the shuffled model, making it an important area of study for developing scalable and secure privacy-preserving protocols.
