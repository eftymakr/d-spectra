## Related Works

Anomaly detection in industrial settings has garnered significant attention due to its critical role in quality control and defect identification. A wide range of methods have been proposed to address this challenge, particularly in the context of unsupervised learning, where labeled anomaly data is scarce or unavailable \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/2011.08785)\]. Early approaches relied on handcrafted features and classical machine learning models, but these often struggled with the complexity and variability of real-world industrial data \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2106.08265)\]. Recent advances have focused on deep learning-based methods, leveraging convolutional neural networks (CNNs) for feature extraction and anomaly localization \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/2011.08785)\].

Several methods have been developed for unsupervised anomaly detection and localization. PatchCore \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2106.08265)\] introduces a memory bank of representative patch features to improve detection performance, achieving an AUROC score of 99.6% on the MVTec AD benchmark. Similarly, CFLOW-AD \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/2107.12571)\] employs conditional normalizing flows for real-time anomaly detection and localization, achieving competitive results with lower computational overhead. PaDiM \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/2011.08785)\] proposes a patch distribution modeling framework that leverages multivariate Gaussian distributions for probabilistic representation of normal classes, outperforming existing methods on multiple datasets. These approaches highlight the importance of efficient feature representation and distribution modeling in anomaly detection.

The MVTec AD dataset \[[4](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/3aa681914a7da79f7d7293f51a058eefe61c8bb7)\] has become a standard benchmark for evaluating unsupervised anomaly detection methods, providing high-resolution images with pixel-precise ground truth annotations. This dataset includes over 5,000 images with more than 70 types of defects, making it a challenging yet realistic testbed for evaluating detection and localization performance. The dataset has been used extensively to benchmark various methods, including those based on autoencoders, GANs, and pre-trained CNNs \[[4](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/3aa681914a7da79f7d7293f51a058eefe61c8bb7)\].

Recent work has also explored discriminative approaches for anomaly detection. DRÆM \[[5](http://arxiv.org/abs/2108.07610)\] introduces a discriminatively trained reconstruction embedding model that learns a joint representation of an anomalous image and its anomaly-free reconstruction, enabling direct anomaly localization without post-processing. FastFlow \[[6](http://arxiv.org/abs/2111.07677)\] utilizes 2D normalizing flows to map image features to a tractable distribution, achieving high accuracy and inference efficiency on the MVTec AD dataset. These methods demonstrate the potential of normalizing flows in capturing complex distributions of normal data.

	Document 1: [Snippet]: «Unsupervised anomaly detection with localization has many practical applications when labeling is infeasible and, moreover, when anomaly examples are completely missing in the train data. While recently proposed models for such data setup achieve high accuracy metrics, their complexity is a limiting factor for real-time processing. In this paper, we propose a real-time model and analytically derive its relationship to prior methods. Our CFLOW-AD model is based on a conditional normalizing flow framework adopted for anomaly detection with localization. In particular, CFLOW-AD consists of a discriminatively pretrained encoder followed by a multi-scale generative decoder where the latter explicitly estimate likelihood of the encoded features. Our approach results in a computationally and memory-efficient model: CFLOW-AD is faster and smaller by a factor of 10× than prior state-of-the-art with the same input setting. Our experiments on the MVTec dataset show that CFLOW-AD outperforms previous methods by 0.36% AUROC in detection task, by 1.12% AUROC and 2.5% AUPRO in localization task, respectively.»

### <a id='category-deep-learning-based-anomaly-detection'></a>Deep Learning-Based Anomaly Detection: 3 papers found.
In the realm of deep learning-based anomaly detection, recent approaches have focused on improving both detection accuracy and computational efficiency, particularly for deployment on resource-constrained devices. These advancements highlight the ongoing efforts to balance performance with practicality, especially in industrial settings where deployment on embedded systems is crucial. The proposed KairosAD model builds on these trends by utilizing the Mobile Segment Anything Model (MobileSAM) to achieve a significant reduction in parameters and inference time while maintaining competitive performance on standard benchmarks \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2106.08265)\].

### <a id='category-self-supervised-learning-approaches'></a>Self-Supervised Learning Approaches: 3 papers found.
In the realm of self-supervised learning approaches for anomaly detection, recent studies have focused on improving the generalization and performance of models trained solely on normal data. One approach involves learning self-supervised deep representations through data augmentation techniques such as CutPaste, where image patches are cut and pasted to create synthetic variations, enabling the model to learn robust features without requiring anomalous data \[[10](http://arxiv.org/abs/2104.04015)\]. Another method extends traditional algorithms like Support Vector Data Description (SVDD) by integrating self-supervised learning and patch-based strategies, allowing for both anomaly detection and segmentation with improved performance on benchmark datasets \[[11](http://arxiv.org/abs/2006.16067)\]. Additionally, researchers have addressed the limitations of auto-encoder-based methods by introducing self-supervised reconstruction-by-inpainting, where partial image regions are removed and reconstructed to better identify anomalies \[[14](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/2c89b183df320c3ef698989bdc5d1d4731c4d65d)\]. These approaches collectively highlight the potential of self-supervised learning in overcoming the challenges of anomaly detection in real-world industrial settings.
