## Related Works

Instruction-following text embedding has emerged as a critical area in natural language processing (NLP), aiming to generate dynamic text embeddings that adapt to user instructions \[[20](http://arxiv.org/abs/2109.01652)\]. This task is particularly important for applications requiring real-time adaptation, such as information retrieval, semantic similarity, and task-specific modeling \[[19](http://arxiv.org/abs/2204.07705)\]. However, existing methods often suffer from significant computational overhead due to the necessity of re-encoding the entire corpus for each new instruction \[[20](http://arxiv.org/abs/2109.01652)\]. This limitation motivates the development of more efficient frameworks that can adapt pre-computed embeddings in real time \[[20](http://arxiv.org/abs/2109.01652)\].

Several approaches have been proposed to improve the efficiency of instruction-following text embeddings. One line of work focuses on instruction tuning, where language models are fine-tuned on a collection of tasks described via instructions \[[20](http://arxiv.org/abs/2109.01652)\]. This method has shown substantial improvements in zero-shot performance on unseen tasks \[[20](http://arxiv.org/abs/2109.01652)\]. For instance, FLAN, a model instruction-tuned on over 60 NLP tasks, outperforms zero-shot GPT-3 on 20 of 25 evaluated tasks \[[20](http://arxiv.org/abs/2109.01652)\]. Similarly, InstructGPT demonstrates significant improvements in instruction-following capabilities by leveraging human feedback during fine-tuning \[[9](http://arxiv.org/abs/2203.02155)\]. These methods, however, still require re-encoding the entire dataset for each instruction, which is computationally expensive \[[20](http://arxiv.org/abs/2109.01652)\].

Another approach involves the use of contrastive learning for sentence embeddings, such as SimCSE, which achieves state-of-the-art results on semantic textual similarity tasks \[[12](http://arxiv.org/abs/2104.08821)\]. SimCSE uses a contrastive learning framework to generate sentence embeddings that are robust and perform well across various tasks \[[12](http://arxiv.org/abs/2104.08821)\]. Similarly, E5, a family of text embeddings trained with weakly-supervised contrastive pre-training, achieves strong performance in both zero-shot and fine-tuned settings \[[18](http://arxiv.org/abs/2212.03533)\]. These models, while effective, still require re-encoding the entire dataset for each instruction, which limits their scalability \[[20](http://arxiv.org/abs/2109.01652)\].

Recent work on instruction tuning with GPT-4 has shown that machine-generated instruction-following data can lead to superior zero-shot performance on new tasks \[[8](http://arxiv.org/abs/2304.03277)\]. This approach reduces the dependency on human-written instruction data, which is often limited in quantity and diversity \[[8](http://arxiv.org/abs/2304.03277)\]. The concept of instruction-following text embedding, which generates dynamic embeddings that adapt to user instructions, has been explored in various recent works. These approaches aim to enhance the flexibility of text embeddings by allowing them to highlight specific attributes of text based on given instructions. However, existing methods often suffer from significant computational overhead, as they require re-encoding the entire corpus for each new instruction \[[16](http://arxiv.org/abs/2005.14165)\]. To address this challenge, researchers have proposed models that leverage pre-trained embeddings and fine-tune them with minimal additional data, enabling efficient adaptation to new tasks \[[19](http://arxiv.org/abs/2204.07705)\]. For instance, models like INSTRUCTOR demonstrate that it is possible to generate task-specific embeddings without re-encoding the entire corpus, by incorporating instruction-focused labels during training \[[21](http://arxiv.org/abs/2212.09741)\]. Similarly, the work on Super-NaturalInstructions highlights the importance of benchmarking models across diverse tasks and instructions, showing that models trained on a variety of tasks can generalize better to unseen ones \[[19](http://arxiv.org/abs/2204.07705)\]. Additionally, the Transformer architecture, which relies solely on attention mechanisms, has been shown to improve the efficiency and quality of sequence transduction tasks, such as machine translation \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/1706.03762)\]. These advancements suggest that the field is moving towards more efficient and adaptable text embedding methods that can respond to user instructions without requiring extensive retraining \[[16](http://arxiv.org/abs/2005.14165)\].

### <a id='category-instruction-tuning-approaches'></a>Instruction tuning approaches: 2 papers found.
Instruction tuning has emerged as a promising approach to improve the alignment of language models with user intent and enhance their zero-shot learning capabilities. Several studies have demonstrated that fine-tuning models on instruction-based tasks can significantly improve performance on unseen tasks. For instance, one study showed that instruction tuning, which involves finetuning language models on a collection of tasks described via instructions, substantially improves zero-shot performance on unseen tasks, with a 137B parameter model outperforming zero-shot 175B GPT-3 on 20 of 25 tasks \[[20](http://arxiv.org/abs/2109.01652)\]. Another study highlighted that aligning language models with user intent can be achieved through fine-tuning with human feedback, leading to improvements in truthfulness and reductions in toxic outputs, even with significantly fewer parameters \[[9](http://arxiv.org/abs/2203.02155)\]. These findings suggest that instruction tuning not only enhances model performance but also contributes to better alignment with user needs. Additionally, the effectiveness of instruction tuning is influenced by factors such as the number of finetuning datasets, model scale, and the use of natural language instructions \[[20](http://arxiv.org/abs/2109.01652)\]. Overall, these works underscore the importance of instruction tuning in improving both the functional capabilities and ethical alignment of language models.
