## Related Works

Maximum Inner Product Search (MIPS) has been a focal point in machine learning and information retrieval, particularly in high-dimensional data applications \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/1405.5869)\]. Traditional approaches to MIPS often rely solely on inner product (IP) similarity, which can suffer from local optima and redundant computations \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/1405.5869)\]. Alternatively, some methods reduce MIPS to Euclidean metric-based nearest neighbor search through space projection, which may lead to topology destruction and information loss \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/1405.5869)\]. However, recent studies have shown that there is no inherent binary opposition between IP and Euclidean metrics, and that integrating both can lead to significant performance improvements \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/1405.5869)\].

Asymmetric LSH (ALSH) was introduced as a provably sublinear time algorithm for approximate MIPS, leveraging asymmetric transformations to convert the problem into approximate near neighbor search \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/1610.03317)\]. This approach was later improved with a different transformation that converted MIPS into cosine similarity search using signed random projections, showing theoretical and empirical superiority \[[6](http://arxiv.org/abs/1410.5410)\]. Similarly, the Norm-Ranging LSH method addressed the issue of long-tailed 2-norm distributions by partitioning datasets and building hash indexes independently, achieving lower query time complexity \[[5](http://arxiv.org/abs/1809.08782)\].

Graph-based methods have also gained traction in MIPS. The inner-product navigable small world graph (ip-NSW) represents a state-of-the-art method, achieving an order of magnitude speedup over baselines by exploiting norm bias in the data \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/1909.13459)\]. This norm bias, where large norm items are more likely to be the result of MIPS, is effectively leveraged by ip-NSW through high in-degrees for large norm items \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/1909.13459)\]. Further improvements were made with ip-NSW+, which introduced an angular proximity graph to refine search results \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/1909.13459)\].

Quantization-based approaches have also been proposed, such as the method that quantizes database vectors into multiple subspaces and approximates inner products as sums of inner products with subspace quantizers \[[8](http://arxiv.org/abs/1509.01469)\]. This avoids the need for higher-dimensional feature space augmentation, offering a more efficient solution \[[8](http://arxiv.org/abs/1509.01469)\].

In addition, the Norm Adjusted Proximity Graph (NAPG) was introduced as a graph-based indexing method that adjusts factors based on sampled data to improve the selection of meaningful data points during graph construction \[[12](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/25454f456c514219c9d6eeb42200ebbf378db9ca)\]. This method has shown strong empirical performance in real-world MIPS tasks \[[12](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/25454f456c514219c9d6eeb42200ebbf378db9ca)\].

### <a id='category-approximate-mips-methods'></a>Approximate MIPS Methods: 11 papers found.
The category "Approximate MIPS Methods" encompasses a range of techniques aimed at efficiently solving the Maximum Inner Product Search (MIPS) problem, which is crucial in high-dimensional machine learning applications. These methods often rely on approximate approaches to balance computational efficiency with search quality, addressing the limitations of exact methods that are often too slow for large-scale datasets. For instance, the Greedy-MIPS algorithm \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/1610.03317)\] introduces a novel approach that allows for budgeted search, offering significant speed improvements while maintaining high search quality. Similarly, the branch-and-bound algorithm \[[4](http://arxiv.org/abs/1202.6101)\] and dual-tree algorithm \[[4](http://arxiv.org/abs/1202.6101)\] utilize tree-based data structures and novel inner-product bounds to enhance search efficiency, achieving up to five orders of magnitude improvement in query time over naive search techniques. Additionally, hashing-based methods such as SIMPLE-LSH \[[5](http://arxiv.org/abs/1809.08782)\] and NORM-RANGING LSH \[[5](http://arxiv.org/abs/1809.08782)\] have been developed to address normalization issues and improve query efficiency, with the latter showing better performance by partitioning datasets and building independent hash indexes. Furthermore, asymmetric transformations have been explored to convert MIPS into more tractable problems, such as approximate near neighbor search \[[6](http://arxiv.org/abs/1410.5410)\] or cosine similarity search \[[6](http://arxiv.org/abs/1410.5410)\], leveraging techniques like signed random projections. These methods collectively highlight the ongoing efforts to improve the efficiency and accuracy of approximate MIPS, adapting to the specific characteristics of real-world datasets.

### <a id='category-graph-based-mips-techniques'></a>Graph-Based MIPS Techniques: 3 papers found.
The category "Graph-Based MIPS Techniques" encompasses a range of methods aimed at improving the efficiency of Maximum Inner Product Search (MIPS) through graph-based indexing strategies. These techniques leverage the structural properties of graphs to navigate high-dimensional data more effectively than traditional approaches. For instance, the inner-product navigable small world graph (ip-NSW) has been shown to achieve significant speedups over existing baselines by exploiting the norm bias in MIPS, where items with larger norms are more likely to be relevant \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/1909.13459)\]. This bias is reflected in the graph's structure, as large norm items tend to have higher in-degrees, allowing the search process to focus computational resources on these critical points \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/1909.13459)\]. Furthermore, the introduction of the ip-NSW+ algorithm, which combines angular and inner-product proximity graphs, demonstrates how hybrid approaches can enhance performance by first narrowing down candidates through angular similarity before refining results with inner-product metrics \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/1909.13459)\]. Similarly, the norm adjusted proximity graph (NAPG) improves upon existing graph-based methods by incorporating adjustment factors derived from sampled data, enabling more accurate selection of meaningful connections during graph construction \[[12](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/25454f456c514219c9d6eeb42200ebbf378db9ca)\]. These advancements highlight the ongoing evolution of graph-based MIPS techniques, emphasizing their empirical effectiveness and adaptability to diverse data distributions \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/1909.13459)\]\[[12](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/25454f456c514219c9d6eeb42200ebbf378db9ca)\].

The brute-force approach to solving exact MIPS is computationally expensive, thus spurring recent development of novel indexes and pruning techniques for this task. In this paper we show that a hardware-efficient brute-force approach, blocked matrix multiply (BMM), can outperform the state-of-the-art MIPS solvers by over an order of magnitude, for some—but not all—inputs. Together, OPTIMUS and MAXIMUS outperform state-of-the-art MIPS solvers by 3.2× on average, and up to 10.9×, on widely studied MIPS datasets.»

	Document 1: [Snippet]: «Given two sets of vectors, A = (a1→, . . . , am→) and B = (b1→, . . . , bn→), our problem is to find the top-t dot products, i.e., the largest |ai→ · bj→| among all possible pairs. This is a fundamental mathematical problem that appears in numerous data applications involving similarity search, link prediction, and collaborative filtering.
