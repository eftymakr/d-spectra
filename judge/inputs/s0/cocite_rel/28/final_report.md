## Related Works

Federated Learning (FL) has emerged as a promising paradigm for collaborative model training while preserving data privacy. FL enables multiple clients to collaboratively train a model under the orchestration of a central server, without sharing raw data \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/1912.04977)\]. This decentralized approach mitigates privacy risks and aligns with data minimization principles \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/1912.04977)\]. However, FL faces significant challenges due to the heterogeneity of client data distributions and the associated fluctuations in model parameters \[[14](http://arxiv.org/abs/1806.00582)\]. These issues are exacerbated by the non-IID (non-independent and identically distributed) nature of data across clients, which can lead to significant accuracy degradation \[[14](http://arxiv.org/abs/1806.00582)\].

Several works have addressed the challenges of non-IID data in FL. For instance, FedProx introduces a framework that generalizes FedAvg and provides convergence guarantees under statistical and system heterogeneity \[[12](http://arxiv.org/abs/1812.06127)\]. Similarly, FedCS improves communication efficiency by actively managing clients based on their resource conditions, allowing for faster convergence \[[6](http://arxiv.org/abs/1804.08333)\]. The problem of non-IID data has also been tackled through clustered FL approaches, such as CFL, which groups clients into clusters with similar data distributions to improve model performance \[[4](http://arxiv.org/abs/1910.01991)\]. Another approach, FedRS, focuses on label distribution skew by proposing a "Restricted Softmax" mechanism to limit the update of missing classes during local training \[[19](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/e2232e7d10cabb0a767edb46ce3c8f1d9f7e5fc0)\].

Active learning (AL) has also been explored in the federated setting. While AL reduces annotation costs by selecting the most informative samples, it often suffers from limited data diversity and annotation budget \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/1812.01097)\]. Federated Active Learning (FAL) extends AL to a decentralized setting, enabling collaborative data selection and model training while preserving data confidentiality \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/1812.01097)\]. However, existing FAL methods fail to account for the heterogeneity of data distribution across clients and the associated fluctuations in global and local model parameters, which can negatively impact model accuracy \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/1812.01097)\]. This gap motivates the development of CHASe, a novel FAL method that addresses these challenges by focusing on identifying samples with high epistemic variations (EVs), which oscillate around decision boundaries during training \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/1812.01097)\].

To achieve both effectiveness and efficiency, CHASe incorporates three key techniques: 1) tracking EVs by analyzing inference inconsistencies across training epochs, 2) calibrating decision boundaries of inaccurate models with a new alignment loss, and 3) enhancing data selection efficiency via a data freeze and awaken mechanism with subset sampling \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/1812.01097)\]. These techniques are inspired by prior work on communication-efficient FL, such as structured and sketched updates, which reduce uplink communication costs

### <a id='category-federated-learning-(fl)-foundations'></a>Federated Learning (FL) Foundations: 5 papers found.
Federated learning (FL) has emerged as a critical paradigm for training machine learning models while preserving data privacy, enabling collaborative learning across decentralized clients such as mobile devices or organizations \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/1912.04977)\]. This approach addresses the challenges of data heterogeneity and scalability, which are inherent in real-world federated networks \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/1812.01097)\]. Recent advancements in FL have focused on improving model accuracy and efficiency, particularly in scenarios where clients may have limited or unlabeled data \[[4](http://arxiv.org/abs/1910.01991)\]. For instance, Federated Semi-Supervised Learning (FSSL) has been introduced to handle partially labeled data, leveraging techniques like FedMatch to improve performance by incorporating inter-client consistency losses and parameter decomposition \[[4](http://arxiv.org/abs/1910.01991)\]. Additionally, clustered federated learning has been proposed to address the issue of heterogeneous user groups, where users are partitioned into clusters with similar learning tasks, and the Iterative Federated Clustering Algorithm (IFCA) has been developed to optimize model parameters while estimating cluster identities \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/1610.05492)\]. Furthermore, multi-task learning has been integrated into FL to mitigate communication costs and improve robustness against system failures, as demonstrated by the MOCHA framework \[[5](http://arxiv.org/abs/2003.00295)\]. These developments highlight the growing interest in addressing the statistical and systems challenges of FL, with a focus on enhancing model performance and efficiency in diverse and realistic settings.

### <a id='category-communication-efficient-fl-techniques'></a>Communication-Efficient FL Techniques: 2 papers found.

Communication-efficient Federated Learning (FL) techniques aim to reduce the communication overhead in distributed training scenarios where data remains on local clients. These methods are critical for mobile and edge devices, where network bandwidth is limited and latency is high \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/1610.05492)\]. Two primary strategies have been proposed to achieve this: structured updates and sketched updates. Structured updates involve learning model parameters within a constrained space, such as low-rank matrices or masked representations, which inherently reduce the amount of data transmitted \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/1610.05492)\]. Sketched updates, on the other hand, involve compressing full model updates through quantization, random rotations, and subsampling before sending them to the server \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/1610.05492)\]. These approaches have demonstrated significant reductions in communication costs, often by two orders of magnitude, across various network conditions and model architectures \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/1610.05492)\]. Additionally, federated learning frameworks have been shown to handle non-IID (non-independent and identically distributed) data distributions, a common challenge in real-world applications \[[9](http://arxiv.org/abs/1602.05629)\]. By aggregating locally computed model updates, these systems enable the training of high-quality global models without compromising data privacy \[[9](http://arxiv.org/abs/1602.05629)\]. The effectiveness of these techniques has been validated through extensive empirical studies, highlighting their robustness and scalability in practical deployment scenarios \[[9](http://arxiv.org/abs/1602.05629)\].

### <a id='category-client-heterogeneity-in-federated-systems'></a>Client Heterogeneity in Federated Systems: 8 papers found.
Client heterogeneity in federated systems has been a focal point of recent research, with multiple approaches aimed at improving model performance in the presence of non-identically distributed data and varying client capabilities. Federated learning (FL) typically assumes uniform data distributions, but real-world scenarios often involve significant statistical heterogeneity, leading to suboptimal convergence and accuracy \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/1812.01097)\]. To address this, several works have proposed adaptive optimization methods, such as federated versions of Adagrad, Adam, and Yogi, which improve convergence by better handling client-specific data distributions \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/1912.04977)\]. Additionally, frameworks like FedProx have been introduced to generalize FedAvg, allowing for more robust convergence by accommodating both statistical and system heterogeneity \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/1610.05492)\]. Another approach, clustered FL (CFL), leverages geometric properties of the loss surface to group clients into clusters with similar data distributions, enabling more specialized model training without requiring prior knowledge of the number of clusters \[[4](http://arxiv.org/abs/1910.01991)\]. Furthermore, personalized federated learning has emerged as a promising direction, where models are adapted to individual clients by incorporating meta-learning techniques like MAML, allowing for efficient personalization while maintaining global model performance \[[5](http://arxiv.org/abs/2003.00295)\]. In parallel, efforts have been made to optimize communication efficiency and convergence speed through adaptive client sampling strategies, which dynamically adjust sampling probabilities to minimize wall-clock time \[[6](http://arxiv.org/abs/1804.08333)\]. These advancements collectively highlight the growing recognition of client heterogeneity as a critical challenge in federated learning and the diverse strategies being developed to address it.

### <a id='category-non-iid-data-challenges-in-fl'></a>Non-IID Data Challenges in FL: 4 papers found.
Non-IID data challenges in federated learning (FL) have been extensively studied, with a growing body of research addressing the impact of heterogeneous data distributions on model performance. The non-IID nature of data, where local datasets across clients are not independently and identically distributed, leads to significant reductions in learning accuracy, with some studies reporting accuracy drops of up to 55% for neural networks trained on highly skewed non-IID data \[[14](http://arxiv.org/abs/1806.00582)\]. This degradation is attributed to weight divergence, which can be quantified using the earth mover's distance (EMD) between local and global data distributions \[[14](http://arxiv.org/abs/1806.00582)\]. Additionally, the heterogeneity of data affects different layers of deep models differently, with classifiers being more biased than other layers, and post-calibration of the classifier after training has been shown to significantly improve performance \[[18](http://arxiv.org/abs/2106.05001)\]. To mitigate these issues, researchers have proposed various strategies, including enforcing regularization in local optimization, improving model aggregation schemes, and introducing methods like "Restricted Softmax" to address label distribution skew by limiting the update of missing classes' weights during local training \[[19](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/e2232e7d10cabb0a767edb46ce3c8f1d9f7e5fc0)\]. These approaches highlight the complexity of non-IID data in FL and the need for adaptive solutions that account for both data and model-level heterogeneity.
