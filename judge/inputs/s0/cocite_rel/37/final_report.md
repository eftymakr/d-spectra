## Related Works

Vision Transformers (ViTs) have gained significant attention in computer vision due to their ability to capture global context through self-attention mechanisms, outperforming traditional convolutional neural networks (CNNs) in various tasks \[[5](http://arxiv.org/abs/2010.11929)\]. However, the deployment of ViTs on hardware platforms, especially FPGAs, remains challenging due to their complex arithmetic operations, such as Softmax and LayerNorm, which are sensitive to precision and difficult to map onto efficient hardware \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2208.05163)\]. Prior work has primarily focused on optimizing matrix multiplication operations while keeping complex operations on the CPU, resulting in suboptimal performance due to communication overhead between the CPU and accelerator \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2208.05163)\]. This section reviews related works that address ViT acceleration, quantization, and hardware design, highlighting their contributions and limitations.

Several studies have explored quantization techniques to reduce the computational and memory costs of ViTs. Auto-ViT-Acc introduces a mixed-scheme quantization approach for FPGA-based ViT acceleration, achieving higher accuracy than state-of-the-art quantization methods under the same bit-width \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2208.05163)\]. VAQF proposes a fully automatic software-hardware co-design framework for low-bit ViTs, enabling real-time execution on FPGAs with minimal compilation time \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/2201.06618)\]. Similarly, Q-ViT addresses the performance drop in low-bit ViTs by introducing an information rectification module and distribution-guided distillation to maintain accuracy \[[13](http://arxiv.org/abs/2210.06707)\]. These works demonstrate that quantization can significantly reduce inference latency without compromising accuracy, but they often focus on specific aspects of the problem.

Efforts to improve hardware efficiency for ViTs have also been explored. ME-ViT introduces a memory-efficient FPGA accelerator that minimizes off-chip memory access by implementing a single-load policy, storing intermediate results on-chip, and using a custom processing element to handle complex operations like Softmax and LayerNorm \[[18](http://arxiv.org/abs/2402.09709)\]. This approach improves memory bandwidth and throughput compared to existing accelerators. Similarly, Evo-ViT proposes a slow-fast token evolution mechanism to reduce computational cost while maintaining performance, achieving over 60% throughput improvement on DeiT-S with minimal accuracy loss \[[16](http://arxiv.org/abs/2108.01390)\]. These works highlight the importance of optimizing both the model and the hardware to achieve real-time inference.

Recent advancements in efficient ViT architectures have also contributed to the field. ### <a id='category-fpga-based-vit-acceleration'></a>FPGA-Based ViT Acceleration: 3 papers found.
Vision Transformers (ViTs) have gained significant attention for their ability to capture global context in computer vision tasks, yet their deployment on hardware remains challenging due to their complex arithmetic operations, such as Softmax and LayerNorm, which are sensitive to model accuracy \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2208.05163)\]. Existing approaches often rely on CPU execution for these operations, leading to suboptimal performance due to communication overhead between the CPU and accelerator \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2208.05163)\]. To address this, recent work has focused on FPGA-based ViT acceleration, aiming to map all operations directly onto hardware for improved efficiency \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/2201.06618)\]. For instance, VAQF introduces a fully automatic framework for quantized ViTs with binary weights and low-precision activations, enabling real-time execution on FPGAs while maintaining high accuracy \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/2201.06618)\]. Similarly, ME-ViT proposes a memory-efficient design that minimizes off-chip memory access by implementing a single-load policy and integrating Softmax and LayerNorm into the processing element, significantly improving memory bandwidth and throughput \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/2104.01136)\]. These efforts highlight the growing interest in optimizing ViT inference on FPGAs through advanced quantization techniques and custom hardware designs. The proposed MXInt format further advances this field by enabling high area efficiency and accuracy through tailored arithmetic operations \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2208.05163)\]. Overall, these studies underscore the importance of hardware-aware design methodologies in overcoming the limitations of ViT deployment on edge devices.

### <a id='category-vit-architecture-and-performance'></a>ViT Architecture and Performance: 13 papers found.
Vision Transformer (ViT) architectures have gained significant traction in computer vision due to their ability to capture global context through self-attention mechanisms, outperforming traditional convolutional neural networks (CNNs) in many scenarios \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/2104.01136)\]. However, the computational and memory costs of ViTs remain a challenge, particularly due to the quadratic complexity of self-attention with respect to image resolution \[[6](http://arxiv.org/abs/2103.15808)\]. To address these limitations, researchers have explored various strategies, such as introducing convolutional components into ViTs to improve efficiency and performance \[[4](http://arxiv.org/abs/2106.04533)\], designing hybrid models like LeViT that balance speed and accuracy \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2208.05163)\], and proposing novel architectures such as Convolutional vision Transformer (CvT) that combine the strengths of CNNs and Transformers \[[4](http://arxiv.org/abs/2106.04533)\]. Additionally, methods like dual-branch transformers \[[5](http://arxiv.org/abs/2010.11929)\] and Tokens-To-Token Vision Transformers (T2T-ViT) \[[6](http://arxiv.org/abs/2103.15808)\] have been introduced to enhance feature representation and reduce computational overhead. Furthermore, efforts have been made to compress ViTs through techniques like pruning, knowledge distillation, and structured sparsity \[[8](http://arxiv.org/abs/2101.11986)\], with some approaches even improving accuracy while reducing computational costs \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/2201.06618)\]. These advancements highlight the ongoing efforts to make ViTs more efficient and suitable for real-world applications.
