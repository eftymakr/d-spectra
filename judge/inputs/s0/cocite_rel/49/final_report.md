## Related Works

Neural retrieval models have seen significant advancements in recent years, particularly in the domain of Web search. These models often rely on large-scale labeled query-document pairs for training, which are typically expensive to obtain \[[8](http://arxiv.org/abs/1611.09268)\]. However, the availability of Web document collections like ClueWeb22 has enabled the use of synthetic queries generated by large language models as a scalable alternative \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2305.18290)\]. Despite this, synthetic training queries can vary in quality, leading to suboptimal performance in downstream retrieval tasks \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2305.18290)\]. 

Existing methods typically filter out noisy query-document pairs using signals from an external re-ranker \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2305.18290)\]. In contrast, we propose a framework that integrates ranking signals directly into the query generation process, leveraging Direct Preference Optimization (DPO) to optimize the model towards generating high-quality queries that maximize downstream retrieval effectiveness \[[6](http://arxiv.org/abs/2402.13228)\]. This approach differs from prior work that relies on post-hoc filtering \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2305.18290)\].

DPO has been shown to improve the alignment of large language models with human preferences, achieving better performance than traditional reinforcement learning from human feedback (RLHF) methods \[[6](http://arxiv.org/abs/2402.13228)\]. It enables the extraction of optimal policies in closed form, eliminating the need for complex sampling during fine-tuning \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2305.18290)\]. This makes DPO a more stable and computationally lightweight alternative to methods like PPO \[[6](http://arxiv.org/abs/2402.13228)\]. 

In the context of dense retrieval, several studies have explored the use of hard negative sampling to improve ranking performance \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/2104.08051)\]. The STAR and ADORE training strategies have been proposed to enhance the stability and effectiveness of dense retrieval models by introducing dynamic negative sampling \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/2104.08051)\]. Similarly, RocketQA introduces cross-batch negatives and denoised hard negatives to improve the training of dual-encoder models \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/2010.08191)\]. These approaches highlight the importance of carefully curating training data to improve retrieval performance.

The MS MARCO dataset has been widely used to evaluate retrieval models, with recent work showing that dense retrievers can outperform traditional sparse retrieval methods \[[12](http://arxiv.org/abs/2004.04906)\]. This dataset is particularly valuable for benchmarking the effectiveness of different retrieval strategies \[[8](http://arxiv.org/abs/1611.09268)\].

### <a id='category-direct-preference-optimization-(dpo)-framework'></a>Direct Preference Optimization (DPO) Framework: 6 papers found.
Direct Preference Optimization (DPO) is a framework designed to improve the alignment of large language models (LLMs) with human preferences by directly optimizing policies from preference data without the need for complex reward modeling or reinforcement learning \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2305.18290)\]. This approach simplifies the fine-tuning process, making it more stable and computationally efficient compared to traditional methods like RLHF, which require training a separate reward model and then using reinforcement learning to maximize that reward \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2305.18290)\]. DPO has been shown to outperform existing methods in controlling the sentiment of generated text and matching or improving response quality in tasks such as summarization and single-turn dialogue \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2305.18290)\]. Furthermore, DPO-Positive (DPOP), an enhanced version of DPO, addresses potential failure modes by avoiding the reduction of model likelihood for preferred examples, leading to better performance across various datasets and downstream tasks \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/2010.08191)\]. The framework also enables the creation of high-performing open-source models, such as Smaug-72B, which achieves strong results on benchmark tasks \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/2010.08191)\]. Additionally, DPO has been applied in automatic alignment methods like Direct Large Model Alignment (DLMA), which generates preference data without relying on human-annotated labels, further demonstrating its versatility and effectiveness in aligning models with human expectations \[[5](http://arxiv.org/abs/2009.01325)\]. The theoretical underpinnings of DPO have also been explored, with researchers deriving new objectives that bypass key approximations in reinforcement learning from human feedback \[[4](http://arxiv.org/abs/2305.15115)\]. These advancements highlight the growing importance of DPO in improving the performance and alignment of LLMs with human preferences.

### <a id='category-differentiable-search-index-integration'></a>Differentiable Search Index Integration: 3 papers found.
The Differentiable Search Index (DSI) represents a paradigm shift in document retrieval by integrating indexing and retrieval into a unified neural model, eliminating the need for separate components \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/2104.08051)\]. This approach allows the model to directly map queries to document identifiers, leveraging a sequence-to-sequence architecture \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/2104.08051)\]. However, existing DSI models face challenges related to data distribution mismatches, particularly when handling short queries versus long documents, and in cross-lingual settings \[[9](http://arxiv.org/abs/2206.10128)\]. To address these issues, researchers have proposed enhancements such as DSI-QG, which generates relevant queries during indexing to better align with retrieval-phase query characteristics \[[9](http://arxiv.org/abs/2206.10128)\]. Additionally, the Semantic-Enhanced DSI (SE-DSI) model improves document identifier assignment by incorporating elaborative descriptions inspired by cognitive psychology, and enhances memorization through semantic feature-based rehearsal strategies \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2305.18290)\]. These advancements aim to improve retrieval performance by better aligning indexing and retrieval processes, as demonstrated through empirical evaluations on various benchmarks \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2305.18290)\]\[[9](http://arxiv.org/abs/2206.10128)\].
