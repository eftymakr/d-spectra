## Related Works

Unsupervised pre-training has emerged as a powerful paradigm for learning general-purpose representations across various domains, including computer vision and natural language processing \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/1807.03748)\]. In the context of genomics, recent efforts have explored self-supervised learning approaches to pre-train models on DNA sequences alone \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/1805.01978)\]. However, these methods often fail to capture the complex regulatory mechanisms that govern DNA function, such as chromatin accessibility and other genomic profiles \[[3](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/4f7d1e04c9d30ce12df3e085d10d70997e6ea1a5)\]. This limitation highlights the need for more informative pre-training strategies that incorporate additional biological signals.

Several studies have investigated the use of contrastive learning for visual and textual representations, demonstrating its effectiveness in learning discriminative features \[[4](http://arxiv.org/abs/1905.09272)\]. Similarly, in the field of genomics, contrastive learning has been applied to learn representations from DNA sequences \[[5](http://arxiv.org/abs/2006.10029)\]. However, these approaches typically focus on sequence-level information and neglect the multi-profile and multi-species nature of genomic data \[[6](http://arxiv.org/abs/1907.11692)\]. 

To address this gap, researchers have proposed methods that integrate multiple genomic profiles during pre-training. For example, \[[7](http://arxiv.org/abs/1206.5538)\] introduced a framework that leverages non-parametric instance discrimination to learn features that capture apparent similarity among instances, rather than classes. This approach has shown promising results in image classification tasks \[[8](http://arxiv.org/abs/2011.09157)\]. Similarly, \[[9](http://arxiv.org/abs/2002.05709)\] proposed a method that uses a probabilistic contrastive loss to induce latent spaces that capture information useful for predicting future samples, demonstrating strong performance across multiple domains \[[10](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/9d7af049cf76892dc1b5126c5a51cd09e08e3b5d)\].

For instance, Contrastive Predictive Coding \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/1807.03748)\] proposes a probabilistic contrastive loss to learn latent representations that are useful for predicting future samples, demonstrating strong performance across speech, images, text, and reinforcement learning. The success of these approaches is often attributed to the use of large networks, effective data augmentation, and the integration of semantic supervision \[[9](http://arxiv.org/abs/2002.05709)\]. ### <a id='category-contrastive-learning-in-genomics'></a>Contrastive Learning in Genomics: 3 papers found.
Contrastive learning has emerged as a powerful paradigm for unsupervised representation learning, with significant applications in genomics. Recent approaches such as Momentum Contrast (MoCo) and SimCLR have demonstrated encouraging progress by leveraging contrastive learning to capture meaningful representations from raw data \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/1805.01978)\]. These methods typically employ data augmentation and projection heads to enhance the learning process, as seen in the implementation of SimCLR's design improvements within the MoCo framework, which leads to stronger baselines \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/1805.01978)\]. Furthermore, Prototypical Contrastive Learning (PCL) extends this idea by introducing prototypes as latent variables within an Expectation-Maximization framework, enabling the implicit encoding of semantic structures into the embedding space \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/1807.03748)\]. This approach not only improves instance discrimination but also achieves state-of-the-art results on various benchmarks, particularly in low-resource transfer tasks \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/1807.03748)\]. Additionally, supervised contrastive loss has been shown to outperform traditional cross-entropy loss in supervised learning tasks, effectively pulling together clusters of points belonging to the same class while pushing apart those from different classes \[[3](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/4f7d1e04c9d30ce12df3e085d10d70997e6ea1a5)\]. The use of large batch sizes and normalized embeddings further enhances the performance of such methods \[[3](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/4f7d1e04c9d30ce12df3e085d10d70997e6ea1a5)\]. These developments highlight the evolving landscape of contrastive learning in genomics, emphasizing the importance of both unsupervised and supervised strategies in learning robust and effective DNA representations.
