## Related Works

Generative retrieval (GR) has recently emerged as a promising paradigm in information retrieval (IR), shifting the focus from traditional index-retrieve pipelines to end-to-end document retrieval via sequence-to-sequence models \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/2202.06991)\]. However, most existing GR models are primarily developed and evaluated using static document collections, with limited exploration of their performance in dynamic corpora where document collections evolve over time \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2308.14968)\]. This gap motivates our work, which systematically evaluates various GR approaches on dynamic corpora and identifies key design factors that influence their effectiveness \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2308.14968)\].

Several recent works have explored the use of differentiable search indexes (DSIs) to encode document corpora into model parameters, enabling direct query-to-docid generation \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/2202.06991)\]. For example, the Differentiable Search Index (DSI) maps queries to relevant document identifiers using a single transformer model, eliminating the need for separate indexing and retrieval stages \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/2202.06991)\]. However, these models often struggle with continual learning, as retraining is required when new documents are added to the corpus \[[7](http://arxiv.org/abs/2212.09744)\]. To address this, DSI++ was proposed, introducing a continual learning framework that allows incremental indexing of new documents while maintaining retrieval performance over previously indexed documents \[[7](http://arxiv.org/abs/2212.09744)\]. Despite these advances, existing GR models with numeric-based docids still exhibit performance degradation in dynamic settings, likely due to overfitting on the initial document set \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2308.14968)\].

In contrast, GR models with text-based docids have shown superior generalization to unseen documents, particularly in dynamic corpora \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2308.14968)\]. This is attributed to their fine-grained design, which aligns better with the semantic understanding of language models \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2308.14968)\]. For instance, the Neural Corpus Indexer (NCI) generates relevant document identifiers directly for a query using a sequence-to-sequence network, achieving significant improvements in recall performance compared to traditional methods \[[4](http://arxiv.org/abs/2206.02743)\]. Similarly, the CorpusBrain model, pre-trained for knowledge-intensive language tasks, demonstrates strong generalization capabilities by encoding all corpus information into its parameters \[[6](http://arxiv.org/abs/2208.07652)\]. These findings align with our observations that text-based docids offer better performance in dynamic settings \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2308.14968)\].

To further enhance performance, recent works have explored hybrid approaches that combine the efficiency of numeric-based docids with the effectiveness of text-based ones. For example, the Auto Search Indexer (ASI) proposes a fully end-to-end retrieval paradigm that automatically learns optimal docids for both existing and new documents \[[16](http://arxiv.org/abs/2310.12455)\]. This approach eliminates the need for preprocessed docids, allowing models to adapt to evolving corpora without additional retraining \[[16](http://arxiv.org/abs/2310.12455)\]. ### <a id='category-docid-design-approaches'></a>DocID Design Approaches: 8 papers found.
Generative retrieval (GR) has gained significant attention in information retrieval (IR) for its ability to directly generate document identifiers (docids) based on a parametric model, enabling end-to-end retrieval \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2308.14968)\]. However, most existing GR models are evaluated on static document collections, limiting their applicability to dynamic corpora where new documents are continuously added \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2308.14968)\]. Recent works have addressed this limitation by proposing continual learning approaches for GR, such as CLEVER, which introduces Incremental Product Quantization for efficient incremental indexing and a memory-augmented mechanism to retain knowledge from previously indexed documents \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2308.14968)\]. Similarly, GenRet has been developed to learn semantic docids through a discrete auto-encoding framework, improving retrieval performance on unseen documents \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/2206.10128)\]. These methods highlight the importance of dynamic docid design in GR, as static or numeric-based docids often suffer from poor generalization and overfitting to initial document sets \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2308.14968)\]\[[7](http://arxiv.org/abs/2212.09744)\]. Additionally, the use of text-based docids, which align better with language models' pretrained knowledge and offer fine-grained representation, has shown superior performance in dynamic settings \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2308.14968)\]\[[7](http://arxiv.org/abs/2212.09744)\]. This trend is further supported by models like Ultron, which aim to unify indexing and retrieval through a model-based approach, and SE-DSI, which enhances semantic representation of docids through cognitive-inspired strategies \[[4](http://arxiv.org/abs/2206.02743)\]\[[5](http://arxiv.org/abs/2311.09134)\]. Overall, these studies emphasize the need for flexible and adaptive docid designs in GR to handle evolving document collections effectively.

### <a id='category-semantic-alignment-with-pretrained-models'></a>Semantic Alignment with Pretrained Models: 5 papers found.
The category "Semantic Alignment with Pretrained Models" explores how pretrained models can be leveraged for information retrieval tasks by aligning semantic representations between queries and documents. This is exemplified by the Differentiable Search Index (DSI), which uses a single transformer model to encode both queries and documents, enabling direct retrieval without separate indexing and retrieval stages \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/2206.10128)\]. The DSI approach has been shown to outperform traditional dual encoder models and even BM25 baselines in zero-shot settings \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/2206.10128)\]. However, DSI models face challenges when dealing with dynamic corpora, as retraining is required whenever the corpus changes, leading to computational inefficiencies \[[7](http://arxiv.org/abs/2212.09744)\]. To address this, DSI++ was introduced, enabling continual learning by incrementally indexing new documents while retaining performance on previously indexed ones \[[7](http://arxiv.org/abs/2212.09744)\]. Additionally, the DSI-QG framework improves retrieval by generating and incorporating relevant queries during indexing, which helps mitigate data distribution mismatches between indexing and retrieval \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2308.14968)\]. These efforts highlight the importance of semantic alignment in improving retrieval accuracy and adaptability, especially in cross-lingual and dynamic environments. The alignment of document identifiers with semantic representations also plays a crucial role in enhancing generalization and performance over evolving document collections \[[15](http://arxiv.org/abs/2105.02274)\].

### <a id='category-generative-retrieval-(gr)-overview'></a>Generative Retrieval (GR) Overview: 4 papers found.
Generative retrieval (GR) has gained significant attention as a novel paradigm in information retrieval, shifting from traditional index-based methods to sequence-to-sequence modeling that encodes entire document corpora within a single Transformer \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2308.14968)\]. This approach forgoes external indices, allowing for more flexible and dynamic retrieval \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2308.14968)\]. However, existing GR models have primarily been evaluated on static document collections, with limited exploration of their performance in dynamic corpora where document sets evolve continuously \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2308.14968)\]. Recent studies have begun to address this gap, revealing that GR models with text-based document IDs (docids) exhibit superior generalization to unseen documents compared to numeric-based docids \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2308.14968)\]. The finer-grained design of text-based docids contributes to better performance in dynamic settings, surpassing BM25 and even matching dense retrieval methods \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2308.14968)\]. In contrast, numeric-based docids, while efficient, suffer from performance degradation in dynamic environments, likely due to overfitting on the initial training set \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2308.14968)\]. These findings highlight the importance of docid design in GR models, with text-based approaches offering advantages in semantic alignment with pretrained language models, fine-grained representation, and lexical diversity \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2308.14968)\]. Additionally, research has shown that synthetic queries can play a crucial role in improving the effectiveness of generative retrieval, particularly when scaling to large corpora \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/2202.06991)\]. Efforts to unify generative retrievers for diverse tasks, such as document, passage, sentence, and entity retrieval, have also emerged, leveraging prompt learning strategies to enhance generalization across tasks \[[4](http://arxiv.org/abs/2206.02743)\]. These advancements underscore the evolving landscape of GR, emphasizing the need for models that balance efficiency with adaptability in real-world, dynamic search environments.
