## Related Works

Recent advancements in Text-to-Speech (TTS) systems have significantly enhanced speech naturalness, leading to increased demand for precise prosody control and mispronunciation correction \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/1909.10302)\]. Traditional approaches for prosody manipulation often rely on specialized modules or additional training, which limits their capacity for post-hoc adjustments \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/1909.10302)\]. Similarly, conventional mispronunciation correction methods depend on grapheme-to-phoneme dictionaries, making them less practical in low-resource settings \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/1909.10302)\]. This section reviews related works that address these challenges.

Several studies have focused on improving prosody control in TTS systems. For example, \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/2006.04558)\] introduces FastSpeech 2, which addresses the one-to-many mapping problem in TTS by incorporating more variation information such as pitch, energy, and accurate duration as conditional inputs. This allows for better control over the prosody of synthesized speech. Similarly, \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/2110.02952)\] proposes a hierarchical prosody modeling approach for non-autoregressive TTS, enabling fine-grained control over speaking styles and word-wise emphasis. These models demonstrate the potential for more flexible and controllable TTS systems.

Another line of research explores the use of prosody embeddings for emotional and expressive speech synthesis. \[[4](http://arxiv.org/abs/2009.06775)\] introduces prosody embeddings that enable fine-grained control of the speaking style of the synthesized speech by introducing temporal structures in the embedding networks. This allows for adjustments at both the frame and phoneme levels, improving the expressiveness of the generated speech. Additionally, \[[5](http://arxiv.org/abs/2011.06465)\] presents a method for generating diverse and natural TTS samples using a quantized fine-grained VAE and an autoregressive prosody prior, which helps in producing more natural and varied speech outputs.

In the realm of multi-speaker TTS, \[[6](http://arxiv.org/abs/2006.06873)\] introduces CopyCat2, a model capable of synthesizing speech with different speaker identities and transferring prosody at a fine-grained level between any pair of seen speakers. This model uses a two-stage training approach to achieve both multi-speaker TTS and fine-grained prosody transfer. Furthermore, \[[7](http://arxiv.org/abs/1811.02122)\] discusses the use of style tokens for unsupervised style modeling, control, and transfer in end-to-end speech synthesis. These tokens are trained without explicit labels and can be used to control synthesis in novel ways, such as varying speed and speaking style.

The importance of prosody in TTS has also been highlighted in studies like \[[8](http://arxiv.org/abs/2002.03788)\], which explores the use of prosody embeddings for emotional and expressive speech synthesis. These embeddings are plugged into end-to-end speech synthesis networks and trained without any other supervision

These methods collectively highlight the growing interest in improving the flexibility and controllability of TTS systems, particularly in terms of prosody and pronunciation.

### <a id='category-end-to-end-speech-synthesis-networks'></a>End-to-End Speech Synthesis Networks: 4 papers found.
In the realm of end-to-end speech synthesis networks, several models have been developed to improve the efficiency and quality of text-to-speech (TTS) systems. Tacotron 2, for instance, employs a recurrent sequence-to-sequence architecture to generate mel-scale spectrograms from text, followed by a modified WaveNet vocoder to produce time-domain waveforms, achieving a mean opinion score (MOS) of 4.53 \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/2006.04558)\]. This approach leverages mel spectrograms as a compact acoustic intermediate representation, allowing for a reduced WaveNet architecture \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/2006.04558)\]. FastSpeech, another notable model, introduces non-autoregressive training to speed up synthesis, though it faces challenges with duration prediction and output quality \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/1909.10302)\]. To address these issues, FastSpeech 2 was proposed, which directly uses ground-truth targets and incorporates more speech variation information, such as pitch and energy, during training \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/1909.10302)\]. Additionally, FastSpeech 2s further enhances the system by enabling parallel speech waveform generation from text, offering faster inference speeds \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/1909.10302)\]. These advancements highlight the ongoing efforts to improve both the efficiency and quality of TTS systems through end-to-end training and better handling of prosody and variation \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/1909.10302)\]\[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/2006.04558)\].

### <a id='category-non-autoregressive-tts-architectures'></a>Non-Autoregressive TTS Architectures: 3 papers found.
Non-autoregressive text-to-speech (NAR-TTS) architectures have gained significant attention for their ability to generate speech in parallel, offering speed advantages over autoregressive models \[[23](http://arxiv.org/abs/2109.15166)\]. These models, such as FastSpeech 2 and Glow-TTS, address the limitations of traditional sequential TTS systems by leveraging parallel processing and advanced generative techniques \[[14](http://arxiv.org/abs/2005.11129)\]. For instance, Glow-TTS introduces a flow-based generative model that eliminates the need for external aligners, enabling robust and efficient speech synthesis with high quality \[[14](http://arxiv.org/abs/2005.11129)\]. Similarly, PortaSpeech combines a lightweight VAE with a flow-based post-net to achieve high-quality speech generation while maintaining a compact model size \[[23](http://arxiv.org/abs/2109.15166)\]. These models also emphasize the importance of prosody modeling, with some approaches conditioning phoneme-level prosody predictions on word-level features to enhance naturalness and control \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/1909.10302)\]. Overall, these works highlight the potential of NAR-TTS in achieving both efficiency and quality, particularly in scenarios requiring real-time or resource-constrained speech synthesis.

### <a id='category-multi-speaker-tts-and-prosody-transfer'></a>Multi-Speaker TTS and Prosody Transfer: 6 papers found.
In the realm of multi-speaker TTS and prosody transfer, recent advancements have focused on improving the naturalness and expressiveness of synthesized speech while enabling fine-grained control over speaker identity and prosody. Models like CopyCat2 \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/1909.10302)\] introduce a two-stage training approach to achieve multi-speaker TTS with contextually appropriate prosody and fine-grained prosody transfer between speakers. Similarly, systems such as the one described in \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/2006.04558)\] utilize a speaker encoder, a sequence-to-sequence synthesis network, and a WaveNet vocoder to generate speech in the voice of unseen speakers, leveraging a large and diverse speaker dataset for better generalization. The ability to transfer speaker characteristics to novel, unseen speakers is further enhanced by models that learn high-quality speaker representations, allowing for the synthesis of speech in voices not present in the training data \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/2006.04558)\]. Additionally, research has explored controllable architectures that enable users to adjust expressiveness levels, balancing naturalness with stylistic variation \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/2110.02952)\]. These approaches highlight the growing emphasis on flexibility and adaptability in TTS systems, particularly in scenarios where training data is limited or where post-hoc adjustments are necessary. The integration of attention mechanisms and hierarchical structures has also played a crucial role in improving alignment between text and speech, as seen in models like Deep Voice 3 \[[4](http://arxiv.org/abs/2009.06775)\] and MultiSpeech \[[5](http://arxiv.org/abs/2011.06465)\], which address common error modes in attention-based synthesis and enable efficient, high-quality multi-speaker TTS. Overall, these works collectively advance the field by addressing key challenges in speaker variability, prosody control, and generalization across diverse speaking conditions.
