## Related Works

Time series forecasting has been a cornerstone in financial applications, with recent advancements focusing on improving accuracy and robustness in the presence of uncertainty. Traditional approaches such as ARIMA and exponential smoothing have been widely used, but they often struggle with capturing complex temporal dependencies and non-stationarity \[[16](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/7b74990bff941aece41437ab9aa9c1f48a3fafca)\]. More recently, deep learning models have emerged as a powerful alternative, offering greater flexibility and performance in forecasting tasks \[[17](http://arxiv.org/abs/2407.13278)\].

Several studies have explored the use of Transformers for time series forecasting, leveraging their ability to model long-range dependencies. However, Transformers face challenges in handling non-stationary data and large lookback windows, leading to performance degradation and computational inefficiency \[[9](http://arxiv.org/abs/2205.13504)\]. To address these issues, researchers have proposed various modifications, such as the PatchTST model, which introduces a patching design to retain local semantic information and reduce computational overhead \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2211.14730)\]. Similarly, the Autoformer model incorporates an Auto-Correlation mechanism to improve efficiency and accuracy in long-term forecasting \[[18](http://arxiv.org/abs/2106.13008)\]. These models demonstrate the potential of Transformers when properly adapted for time series tasks.

In addition to Transformers, recurrent neural networks (RNNs) and their variants, such as LSTMs and GRUs, have been extensively studied for time series analysis \[[19](http://arxiv.org/abs/1703.07015)\]. The LSTNet framework, for example, combines CNNs and RNNs to capture both short-term and long-term patterns, achieving significant improvements over traditional methods \[[19](http://arxiv.org/abs/1703.07015)\]. However, these models often require careful tuning and may not generalize well to diverse forecasting tasks without additional adaptations.

Probabilistic forecasting has also gained traction, with models like DeepAR providing accurate forecasts by estimating the probability distribution of future values \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/1704.04110)\]. This approach is particularly useful in financial applications where uncertainty quantification is essential. Similarly, the Multi-Horizon Quantile Recurrent Forecaster (MQRF) extends probabilistic forecasting to multi-step predictions, accommodating both static and dynamic covariates \[[10](http://arxiv.org/abs/1711.11053)\]. These models highlight the importance of incorporating uncertainty into forecasting frameworks, especially in high-stakes domains like finance.

Recent work has also explored the use of large language models (LLMs) for time series forecasting, aiming to leverage their strong pattern recognition and reasoning capabilities \[[20](http://arxiv.org/abs/2310.01728)\]. However, studies have shown that LLMs may not always provide significant advantages over traditional models, particularly when pre-trained without specific time series knowledge \[[11](http://arxiv.org/abs/2406.16964)\]. The Chronos framework, which pretrains language models on time series data, has shown promising results in improving zero-shot forecasting performance \[[12](http://arxiv.org/abs/2403.07815)\].
