## Related Works

Sensor calibration is a fundamental step in multi-sensor fusion for autonomous driving systems, ensuring accurate alignment between heterogeneous sensors such as radar and camera \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2103.07505)\]. Early approaches for radar-camera calibration often relied on dedicated calibration targets, which limited their applicability in dynamic environments \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/1904.08743)\]. More recent methods have sought to automate this process without requiring external targets, leveraging data-driven techniques and deep learning \[[3](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/4d4dbbcf27d991aeab43dafb82a105bb5512b799)\]. For example, \[[4](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/fa7f4199640500d48c0671181ea6a8dce3141123)\] proposed a targetless method for rotational radar-camera calibration using a coarse and fine convolutional neural network, demonstrating robustness in real-world conditions. Similarly, \[[5](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/0955326c67a947b5fac5fbae7238d04fc9adc251)\] introduced a novel calibration tool that supports joint calibration of radar, camera, and lidar, enabling simultaneous optimization across multiple sensor pairs.

The challenge of sparse radar data and height uncertainty has motivated the development of specialized calibration techniques. \[[6](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/c80265fa719eb85c8a56a6b36c74b99b793f39b2)\] addressed this by introducing a dual-perspective representation that combines frontal and bird's-eye views, leveraging the rich height information from the frontal view while using the robust features of the bird's-eye view to mitigate height uncertainty. This approach aligns with the proposed Dual-Perspective representation in this work, which similarly aims to reduce the impact of height uncertainty during automatic calibration \[[7](http://arxiv.org/abs/2011.04841)\]. Additionally, \[[8](http://arxiv.org/abs/2210.09267)\] proposed a method that uses radar cross section estimates to improve the accuracy of radar-camera calibration, particularly in addressing elevation resolution limitations. These insights are relevant to the design of the Selective Fusion Mechanism in this paper, which identifies and fuses reliable features from both perspectives \[[9](http://arxiv.org/abs/2008.05711)\].

To enhance the robustness of the calibration process, several works have explored cross-modal matching and attention mechanisms. \[[10](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/0e88fc24ae7694d0b1a2fe78322936fc568bc7d2)\] introduced a radar-camera fusion network that uses a ray-constrained cross-attention mechanism to resolve geometric ambiguities between radar and camera features. This is conceptually similar to the Multi-Modal Cross-Attention Mechanism proposed in this work, which explicitly finds location correspondences through cross-modal matching \[[11](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/2726fb04936e6c668dff18b2117e92e34c826698)\]. Furthermore, \[[12](http://arxiv.org/abs/2209.06535)\] proposed a method for radar-camera association using deep representation learning, which overcomes the limitations of traditional rule-based approaches by leveraging feature-level interactions and global reasoning. This aligns with the use of deep learning in this paper to improve the accuracy and robustness of the calibration process \[[13](http://arxiv.org/abs/2103.07825)\].

The issue of noise and sparsity in radar data has also been addressed in prior work. \[[14](http://arxiv.org/abs/2204.13483)\] introduced a noise-resistant matcher to improve the supervision of the matching mechanism during training, which is similar to the Noise-Resistant Matcher designed in this paper to enhance robustness against sparsity
