## Related Works

Multi-Domain Recommendation (MDR) has emerged as a promising approach to improve recommendation performance by leveraging knowledge transfer across different domains \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2103.01696)\]. Traditional MDR methods often assume a shared structure for transferring domain knowledge, but this approach can lead to the Negative Transfer Problem (NTP) when there is knowledge conflict or domain quality issues \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/1804.06769)\]. The NTP occurs when transferring information from one domain negatively affects the performance of another domain \[[3](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/2b031c7170c9dccbe506f3d3a1d6104f65e835ae)\]. To address this challenge, several approaches have been proposed to model domain relationships and selectively transfer knowledge.

One line of work focuses on modeling domain relationships using graph-based methods. For instance, BiTGCF introduces a bi-directional transfer learning method that uses Graph Collaborative Filtering networks to capture high-order interactions and enable two-way knowledge transfer between domains \[[4](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/d5630291a4ba3f6266c77e6d00920502f986afa6)\]. Similarly, MAGRec employs graph neural networks to learn sequential user interactions across domains, improving representation by capturing both intra- and inter-domain relationships \[[5](http://arxiv.org/abs/1910.05189)\]. These methods highlight the importance of modeling domain interactions to avoid NTP.

Another approach is to use multi-task learning (MTL) frameworks that allow for joint optimization across domains. For example, MAMDR proposes a model-agnostic learning framework that enables scalable MDR by leveraging domain negotiation and domain regularization strategies \[[6](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/24a473700894dbfab0bfd9dc3d542acfeef66bca)\]. PLE, a progressive layered extraction model, further improves MTL by explicitly separating shared and task-specific components, enabling more efficient joint representation learning \[[7](http://arxiv.org/abs/1203.3535)\]. These methods demonstrate how MTL can be adapted to handle complex domain interactions and reduce NTP.

In addition to MTL, some works focus on domain selection mechanisms to dynamically choose relevant domains for knowledge transfer. For example, DDTCDR introduces a dual transfer mechanism that iteratively transfers information between domains while preserving latent relations \[[8](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/7807d924fa46caec7b706b98156962caf8458d59)\]. CAT-ART extends this idea by proposing a multi-target CDR method that transfers knowledge across multiple domains using global and domain-specific user embeddings \[[9](http://arxiv.org/abs/2211.11964)\]. These approaches emphasize the need for dynamic and selective domain selection to avoid NTP.

Moreover, some studies focus on embedding and mapping strategies to transfer user representations across domains. The Embedding and Mapping (EMCDR) approach is widely used, where user embeddings are pre-trained in the source domain and then mapped to the target domain \[[10](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/fa990aee9a8f157b5d393f5f3eaa014e1e5c67aa)\]. However, this approach often ignores domain interactions, leading to biased representations \[[11](http://arxiv.org/abs/2203.16863)\]. To address this, CDRIB introduces variational information bottleneck principles to learn unbiased representations by modeling cross-domain and in-domain interactions \[[11](http://arxiv.org/abs/2203.16863)\].
