## Related Works

Fine-grained emotion classification in app reviews has received increasing attention in recent years, driven by the need to better understand user sentiment and emotional expression in digital platforms. While sentiment analysis has been extensively studied, focusing primarily on polarity classification (positive, negative, neutral) \[[1](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/7d0531aaaa4dbf4033da964289843e7812262702)\], the detection of discrete emotions remains a challenging and underexplored area \[[2](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/6a6f1b8ef3eefaf8027a76134859b44886a5f795)\]. This is particularly true in the context of app reviews, where emotional nuances are often expressed in complex and varied ways \[[3](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/ff921a209cc9e1c2120669d3d81e627ca5e53572)\].

Several studies have attempted to address the lack of large-scale annotated datasets for emotion detection in natural language. For example, EmoNet \[[4](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/030ea3f854d688659500a949eac095cdba087682)\] introduced a large dataset for fine-grained emotion detection and achieved state-of-the-art results on 24 emotion types using deep learning models. Similarly, GoEmotions \[[5](http://arxiv.org/abs/1505.04657)\] presented a manually annotated dataset of 58k Reddit comments, labeled for 27 emotion categories, demonstrating the potential of large-scale datasets for emotion analysis. However, these datasets are not specifically tailored to app reviews, which often contain domain-specific language and emotional expressions unique to user feedback \[[6](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/872352b0a53ab6cbb4420f81df64d215d86c7d9b)\].

The challenge of annotating emotions in app reviews has also been explored in the context of crowdsourcing and annotation frameworks. CROWDSOURCING A WORD–EMOTION ASSOCIATION LEXICON \[[7](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/c32ed8d14de92c4a8d8cfbeac7d04e99431887cf)\] demonstrated how crowdsourcing can be used to generate high-quality emotion lexicons, while the SemEval-2018 Task 1 \[[8](http://arxiv.org/abs/2005.00547)\] focused on affect in tweets, highlighting the need for robust annotation guidelines and inter-annotator agreement. These efforts underscore the importance of structured annotation frameworks for consistent and reliable emotion labeling \[[9](http://arxiv.org/abs/2205.01996)\].

In the context of app reviews, several studies have focused on sentiment analysis and opinion mining. For instance, the work on AR-miner \[[10](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/723f744455914bf1ead6ad267976a1500b7dee4a)\] introduced a computational framework for mining informative reviews, emphasizing the need for effective review analytics tools. Similarly, the study on feature-oriented sentiment rating \[[11](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/695886bf2b7bf90e98e98f630b26dabcdbe78247)\] proposed a framework for analyzing user reviews by extracting relevant features and associating them with sentiment. These approaches highlight the potential of combining sentiment analysis with feature extraction to gain deeper insights into user feedback \[[12](http://arxiv.org/abs/2305.15005)\].

Recent advancements in large language models (LLMs) have also been explored for sentiment analysis tasks. The paper on Sentiment Analysis in the Era of Large Language Models \[[13](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/66c8ab0b3b7698cf5333c3f7350a6eaa5302294b)\] evaluated the performance of LLMs on various sentiment analysis tasks, revealing their potential in few-shot learning settings but also highlighting limitations in complex tasks requiring deeper understanding.
