## Related Works

The growing adoption of cloud-native applications has led to increased interest in optimizing both resource usage and financial costs. Several studies have explored system-level optimizations for containerized environments, particularly focusing on resource allocation and cost efficiency \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2501.14753)\]. However, the analysis of network costs in Kubernetes clusters remains underexplored, despite its critical role in overall cloud expenditures \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2501.14753)\]. This paper contributes to the emerging field of FinOps by addressing the financial and operational aspects of managing network costs in cloud-native environments \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2501.14753)\].

FinOps, as a discipline, combines financial management and cloud operations to optimize costs in cloud-based applications \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2501.14753)\]. Prior research has focused on system-level optimizations for cost and resource efficiency in containerized systems, such as the use of Infrastructure-as-Code to alert engineering teams of expected deployment costs \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2501.14753)\]. Additionally, automated budget analysis and cloud usage surveillance have been proposed to enforce budgets and alert teams when spending exceeds thresholds \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2501.14753)\]. These approaches are essential for achieving holistic cloud spend visibility and cost optimization \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2501.14753)\].

In the context of Kubernetes, several studies have examined resource scheduling and management schemes. Research on Kubernetes' resource scheduling schemes highlights the importance of efficient scheduling algorithms to improve resource utilization and reduce costs \[[9](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/d11e5c5c08af88ee8abb9ebaa166b1d6fd198501)\]. The scheduling process in Kubernetes is critical for assigning physical resources to containers, and various approaches have been proposed based on Quality of Service (QoS) parameters to optimize performance and energy consumption \[[9](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/d11e5c5c08af88ee8abb9ebaa166b1d6fd198501)\]. These findings are relevant to the broader discussion on cost optimization in cloud-native environments.

Furthermore, the integration of Kubernetes with cloud platforms such as Google Cloud Platform has enabled rapid application development and management \[[4](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/b5634b15badc73f3cda72b960fd51ef03ac1265d)\]. The use of Docker and Kubernetes has revolutionized the era of software development and operations, allowing for faster and more reliable deployments \[[4](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/b5634b15badc73f3cda72b960fd51ef03ac1265d)\]. This is particularly important for organizations seeking to reduce costs and improve scalability in their cloud infrastructure \[[4](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/b5634b15badc73f3cda72b960fd51ef03ac1265d)\].

The paper also highlights the importance of monitoring and managing network costs in Kubernetes clusters. While prior studies have focused on system-level optimizations, the analysis of network expenditures is crucial for understanding the full financial impact of containerized applications \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2501.14753)\]. The methodology presented in this paper combines measurement analysis, experimentation, and cost modelling to provide actionable insights into network cost optimization \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2501.14753)\]. This approach aligns with the goals of FinOps, which aims to bring accountability to cloud spend and optimize costs \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2501.14753)\].
