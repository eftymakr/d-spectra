## Related Works

Fairness in re-ranking has gained significant attention in recent years, driven by the increasing awareness of potential biases in recommendation systems \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2204.08085)\]. Early work on fairness in recommendation systems primarily focused on user-side fairness, often neglecting the impact on item-side fairness \[[10](http://arxiv.org/abs/2104.10671)\]. This approach, however, fails to account for the two-sided nature of recommender systems, where both users and items (or producers) are stakeholders \[[11](http://arxiv.org/abs/2201.01180)\]. 

Several studies have proposed fairness-aware ranking mechanisms to address these concerns. For instance, \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/1805.01788)\] introduced the concept of "Equity of Attention," emphasizing the importance of mitigating position bias in rankings, which can lead to an unfair distribution of attention and opportunities. Similarly, \[[6](http://arxiv.org/abs/1802.07281)\] proposed a framework for fairness in rankings by formulating fairness constraints in terms of exposure allocation, ensuring that rankings are both useful and equitable. These works laid the foundation for understanding the interplay between ranking quality and fairness.

More recently, \[[11](http://arxiv.org/abs/2201.01180)\] introduced FairRec, a method that ensures two-sided fairness by guaranteeing exposure fairness for producers while maintaining customer satisfaction. This approach was further extended in \[[13](http://arxiv.org/abs/2110.15781)\], where the authors proposed a fairness criterion based on Lorenz dominance, aiming to maximize the utility of worse-off individuals. These works highlight the growing recognition of the need for fairness considerations that span both sides of the recommendation system.

In addition to fairness-aware ranking, \[[14](http://arxiv.org/abs/1712.09752)\] developed a system for designing fair ranking schemes by allowing users to choose criterion weights that lead to greater fairness. This approach enables a balance between different fairness criteria, such as proportionality, while maintaining ranking performance. The work in \[[15](http://arxiv.org/abs/2204.03046)\] further advanced the understanding of fairness in ranking by proposing a novel algorithm, VerFair, that balances exposure fairness with ranking performance more effectively than existing methods.

The concept of fairness through awareness, introduced in \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/1104.3913)\], has also influenced the development of fairness-aware recommendation systems. This framework ensures that similar individuals are treated similarly, promoting fairness in classification tasks. While primarily focused on classification, the principles of fairness through awareness have been adapted to recommendation systems \[[10](http://arxiv.org/abs/2104.10671)\].

The challenge of balancing fairness and accuracy in re-ranking has also been addressed in \[[18](http://arxiv.org/abs/1706.06368)\], where the authors proposed a fair top-k ranking algorithm that ensures group fairness while maintaining utility. This work demonstrates that fairness considerations can be integrated into ranking algorithms without significant loss of performance.
