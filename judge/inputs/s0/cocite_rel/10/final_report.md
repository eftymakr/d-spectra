## Related Works

Multi-token prediction (MTP) has emerged as a promising pre-training objective for language models, offering potential improvements in downstream performance, inference speed, and training efficiency \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2404.19737)\]. Unlike traditional next-token prediction (NTP), which predicts one token at a time, MTP predicts multiple future tokens simultaneously using multiple prediction heads \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2404.19737)\]. This approach has been shown to enhance model capabilities, particularly in generative tasks such as coding, where models trained with MTP outperform comparable NTP models by several percentage points \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2404.19737)\]. For example, 13B parameter models trained with 4-token prediction solve 12% more problems on HumanEval and 17% more on MBPP than next-token models \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2404.19737)\].

Several studies have explored alternative training paradigms that aim to improve the efficiency and effectiveness of language models. For instance, process supervision, which provides feedback at each intermediate reasoning step, has been found to significantly outperform outcome supervision for solving complex problems, such as those in the MATH dataset \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/2305.20050)\]. Similarly, speculative decoding has been proposed as a method to accelerate inference by predicting multiple tokens in parallel and validating them in a single step \[[8](http://arxiv.org/abs/2401.07851)\]. This approach has demonstrated gains on tasks such as SQuAD, CommonSenseQA, and GSM8k \[[4](http://arxiv.org/abs/2310.02226)\]. Additionally, blockwise parallel decoding has been introduced to enable parallel prediction across multiple time steps, reducing generation time while maintaining quality \[[12](http://arxiv.org/abs/1811.03115)\].

Curriculum learning has also been explored as a strategy to improve training outcomes. While some studies have suggested that curriculum learning can enhance model performance and training efficiency, others have found no compelling evidence of improvement in language model pretraining \[[13](http://arxiv.org/abs/2108.02170)\]. However, curriculum learning has been successfully applied in other domains, such as computer vision and machine translation \[[13](http://arxiv.org/abs/2108.02170)\]. In the context of MTP, curriculum learning has been proposed as a way to gradually increase the complexity of the pre-training objective, starting from NTP and moving toward MTP \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2404.19737)\]. This approach has shown promise in enabling smaller language models to better leverage the MTP objective during pre-training, improving downstream performance and generative output quality \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2404.19737)\].

Transfer learning involves pre-training on a large corpus of text and fine-tuning on specific tasks, which has led to significant gains in NLP tasks \[[5](http://arxiv.org/abs/1910.10683)\]. ### <a id='category-multi-token-prediction-(mtp)-frameworks'></a>Multi-token Prediction (MTP) Frameworks: 3 papers found.
Multi-token prediction (MTP) frameworks have emerged as a promising alternative to traditional next-token prediction (NTP) in language model training, offering potential improvements in downstream performance, inference speed, and training efficiency \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2404.19737)\]. These frameworks train models to predict multiple future tokens simultaneously, often using independent output heads atop a shared model trunk, which allows for more efficient learning of complex patterns \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2404.19737)\]. This approach has been shown to particularly benefit large models, with gains observed in tasks like coding and generative benchmarks, where models trained with MTP outperform next-token models by several percentage points \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2404.19737)\]. Additionally, MTP can lead to faster inference times, with models trained using 4-token prediction demonstrating up to 3 times speed improvements even with large batch sizes \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2404.19737)\]. However, smaller language models (SLMs) face challenges in adapting to the MTP objective, prompting the development of curriculum learning strategies to gradually introduce complexity, either by increasing from NTP to MTP (forward curriculum) or decreasing (reverse curriculum) \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/2305.20050)\]. These strategies aim to balance the benefits of MTP with the limitations of SLMs, with the forward curriculum showing improved downstream NTP performance and generative quality while retaining self-speculative decoding advantages, whereas the reverse curriculum achieves stronger NTP performance but lacks these decoding benefits \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/2305.20050)\]. Other approaches, such as Medusa, further enhance MTP by introducing parallel decoding heads that predict multiple tokens simultaneously, reducing decoding steps and improving inference efficiency without compromising generation quality \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/2005.14165)\].

### <a id='category-self-speculative-decoding-techniques'></a>Self-speculative Decoding Techniques: 2 papers found.
The category "Self-speculative Decoding Techniques" focuses on methods designed to enhance the efficiency of Large Language Model (LLM) inference by addressing the limitations of autoregressive decoding. These techniques, such as speculative decoding, aim to reduce inference latency by allowing the simultaneous decoding of multiple tokens per step, rather than sequentially generating each token \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2404.19737)\]. This approach leverages the observation that certain subtasks within language modeling can be approximated effectively by more efficient models, enabling parallel computation \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/2305.20050)\]. By integrating speculative execution with novel sampling methods, these techniques enable faster decoding without altering the output distribution or requiring retraining or architectural changes \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/2005.14165)\]. For instance, speculative decoding has been demonstrated to achieve a 2X-3X speedup in inference for models like T5-XXL, maintaining identical output quality as standard implementations \[[4](http://arxiv.org/abs/2310.02226)\]. Additionally, the method supports the integration of self-speculative decoding benefits during pre-training, as seen in curriculum learning strategies for multi-token prediction (MTP) training, where it helps improve downstream performance and generative output quality \[[5](http://arxiv.org/abs/1910.10683)\]. These techniques represent a significant advancement in making LLMs more efficient for real-time applications.

[Snippet]: «Deep autoregressive sequence-to-sequence models have demonstrated impressive performance across a wide variety of tasks in recent years. While common architecture classes such as recurrent, convolutional, and self-attention networks make different trade-offs between the amount of computation needed per layer and the length of the critical path at training time, generation still remains an inherently sequential process. To overcome this limitation, we propose a novel blockwise parallel decoding scheme in which we make predictions for multiple time steps in parallel then back off to the longest prefix validated by a scoring model. This allows for substantial theoretical improvements in generation speed when applied to architectures that can process output sequences in parallel.
