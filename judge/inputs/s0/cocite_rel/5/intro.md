## Related Works

Instruction-following text embedding has emerged as a critical area in natural language processing, aiming to generate dynamic text embeddings that adapt to user instructions. This task requires models to produce embeddings that reflect specific attributes of text, guided by task-specific instructions \[[17](http://arxiv.org/abs/2204.07705)\]. Prior approaches often rely on re-encoding the entire corpus for each new instruction, resulting in significant computational overhead \[[16](http://arxiv.org/abs/2402.09906)\]. This limitation has motivated the development of more efficient methods that can adapt pre-computed embeddings in real time \[[18](http://arxiv.org/abs/2401.00368)\].

Several recent works have explored the use of instruction-tuned models to improve the generalization and adaptability of text embeddings. For instance, INSTRUCTOR introduces a method where text is embedded together with instructions explaining the use case, enabling a single embedder to generate embeddings tailored to different downstream tasks without further training \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2212.09741)\]. Similarly, E5 is a family of state-of-the-art text embeddings that transfer well to a wide range of tasks, trained in a contrastive manner with weak supervision signals from a large-scale text pair dataset \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/2212.03533)\]. These models demonstrate strong performance in both zero-shot and fine-tuned settings, highlighting the importance of instruction alignment in embedding quality \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/2212.03533)\].

The concept of instruction finetuning has also been explored in the context of language models. For example, InstructGPT demonstrates that fine-tuning large language models with human feedback improves their ability to follow instructions, leading to better alignment with user intent \[[9](http://arxiv.org/abs/2203.02155)\]. Similarly, Self-Instruct introduces a framework for improving instruction-following capabilities by bootstrapping off the model's own generations, reducing reliance on human-annotated datasets \[[5](http://arxiv.org/abs/2212.10560)\]. These methods emphasize the value of instruction data in enhancing the adaptability of models to new tasks \[[5](http://arxiv.org/abs/2212.10560)\].

In the realm of vision-and-language navigation, several approaches have been proposed to enable agents to interpret natural language instructions in real environments. For example, Vision-and-Language Navigation (VLN) tasks require models to navigate an embodied agent to carry out instructions inside 3D environments. Reinforced Cross-Modal Matching (RCM) is a method that enforces cross-modal grounding both locally and globally via reinforcement learning, significantly improving performance on VLN benchmarks \[[10](http://arxiv.org/abs/1811.10092)\]. Similarly, Speaker-Follower Models use embedded speaker models to synthesize instructions for data augmentation and implement pragmatic reasoning, leading to improved performance on navigation tasks \[[12](http://arxiv.org/abs/1806.02724)\].
