## Related Works

Zero-Knowledge Proofs (ZKPs) have garnered significant attention for their ability to enable secure and private computation, allowing a prover to demonstrate the truth of a statement without revealing additional information \[[8](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/b179cf70666c9f67fc8c330be3a55a5dd2266c2e)\]. This capability has led to their adoption in various domains, including blockchain technologies, verifiable machine learning, and electronic voting \[[1](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/e149d93531d8c07cf43f6cae327a4795144d1304)\]. However, the computational complexity of the proving process has hindered their widespread adoption \[[1](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/e149d93531d8c07cf43f6cae327a4795144d1304)\]. Recent research has focused on accelerating the key primitives of ZKP protocols using hardware accelerators such as GPUs and ASICs \[[7](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/e4a4608e8e35379e63b9df5bda1b7599ee8ab9ac)\]. 

Several works have explored methods to improve the performance of ZKP systems. For instance, GZKP introduces a GPU-accelerated approach that significantly reduces proof generation time by optimizing memory access patterns and eliminating costly external shuffles \[[1](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/e149d93531d8c07cf43f6cae327a4795144d1304)\]. Similarly, PipeZK proposes a pipelined architecture with two subsystems dedicated to handling polynomial computations and multi-scalar multiplications on elliptic curves, achieving up to 10x speedup on cryptographic benchmarks \[[2](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/6ee84674b7c426849c3da56cb278c08eb7a903cf)\]. cuZK further enhances performance by introducing a faster parallel multi-scalar multiplication algorithm on GPUs, achieving speedups of up to 4.86x on a cryptocurrency application \[[7](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/e4a4608e8e35379e63b9df5bda1b7599ee8ab9ac)\]. 

Despite these advancements, many existing ZKP protocols face limitations. For example, some require a trusted setup for each application, which can be a barrier to deployment in decentralized environments \[[1](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/e149d93531d8c07cf43f6cae327a4795144d1304)\]. Others generate larger proof sizes or have higher verification costs, which can be problematic in scenarios with numerous verifiers or strict time constraints \[[1](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/e149d93531d8c07cf43f6cae327a4795144d1304)\]. These challenges highlight the need for more efficient and scalable solutions. 

The proposed accelerator, zkSpeed, addresses these limitations by supporting both one-time, universal setup and small proof sizes, making it suitable for publicly verifiable, consensus-based systems \[[1](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/e149d93531d8c07cf43f6cae327a4795144d1304)\]. It accelerates the entire protocol, including the SumCheck and Multi-scalar Multiplication (MSM) primitives, which are critical for proof generation \[[1](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/e149d93531d8c07cf43f6cae327a4795144d1304)\]. The design of zkSpeed leverages a full-chip architecture with 366.46 mm² and 2 TB/s of bandwidth, achieving geometric mean speedups of 801× over CPU baselines \[[1](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/e149d93531d8c07cf43f6cae327a4795144d1304)\]. This represents a significant improvement over previous approaches, which often focused on individual components rather than the entire protocol \[[7](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/e4a4608e8e35379e63b9df5bda1b7599ee8ab9ac)\].

### <a id='category-gpu-and-asic-acceleration-techniques'></a>GPU and ASIC Acceleration Techniques: 2 papers found.
GPU and ASIC acceleration techniques for zero-knowledge proofs (ZKPs) have been actively researched to address the performance bottlenecks in proof generation, which is critical for enabling widespread adoption of ZKP-based applications \[[1](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/e149d93531d8c07cf43f6cae327a4795144d1304)\]. Recent works such as GZKP and cuZK have introduced GPU-accelerated solutions that significantly improve the efficiency of ZKP systems by optimizing key computational primitives like multi-scalar multiplication (MSM) and large-scale matrix-vector multiplication \[[1](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/e149d93531d8c07cf43f6cae327a4795144d1304)\]\[[7](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/e4a4608e8e35379e63b9df5bda1b7599ee8ab9ac)\]. For instance, GZKP enhances polynomial computation over large finite fields by promoting cache-friendly memory access patterns and eliminating costly external shuffling, while cuZK introduces a new parallel MSM algorithm that achieves nearly perfect linear speedup over existing serial implementations \[[1](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/e149d93531d8c07cf43f6cae327a4795144d1304)\]\[[7](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/e4a4608e8e35379e63b9df5bda1b7599ee8ab9ac)\]. Additionally, these approaches reduce CPU-GPU data transfer latency by overlapping computation with data movement, further enhancing performance \[[7](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/e4a4608e8e35379e63b9df5bda1b7599ee8ab9ac)\]. These advancements demonstrate the potential of hardware acceleration in making ZKP systems more practical for real-world applications such as cryptocurrencies and verifiable machine learning.

### <a id='category-theoretical-foundations-of-zkps'></a>Theoretical Foundations of ZKPs: 5 papers found.
The theoretical foundations of zero-knowledge proofs (ZKPs) focus on defining and understanding the knowledge conveyed in proofs, ensuring that they reveal only the correctness of a proposition without additional information \[[3](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/9ccf7b6cb32cf89752a35bd910555adac54773e0)\]. This concept is crucial for developing secure and privacy-preserving systems, as it allows for the verification of statements without exposing underlying data \[[3](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/9ccf7b6cb32cf89752a35bd910555adac54773e0)\]. The idea of perfect zero-knowledge interactive proof systems further emphasizes that a verifier should gain no extra knowledge beyond the truth of the statement, even in an information-theoretic sense \[[9](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/d5ccb8b351118b35b1a565ad27de4cd2ebdd0287)\]. However, achieving this in practice is challenging, as it implies that for certain languages, such as NP-complete ones, perfect zero-knowledge protocols may not exist unless the polynomial time hierarchy collapses \[[9](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/d5ccb8b351118b35b1a565ad27de4cd2ebdd0287)\]. Additionally, the design of efficient argument systems for NP, which allow for non-interactive and publicly-verifiable proofs, plays a key role in enabling scalable and practical ZKP implementations \[[8](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/b179cf70666c9f67fc8c330be3a55a5dd2266c2e)\]. These foundational concepts underpin the development of modern ZKP protocols, such as HyperPlonk, which aim to balance efficiency with security and privacy \[[1](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/e149d93531d8c07cf43f6cae327a4795144d1304)\].
