## Related Works

Anomaly detection has gained significant attention in recent years due to its critical role in quality control and fault diagnosis in industrial settings. Several approaches have been proposed to address the challenges of anomaly detection, particularly in the context of unsupervised and one-class learning scenarios, where only normal data is available for training \[[11](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/3aa681914a7da79f7d7293f51a058eefe61c8bb7)\]. Early methods relied on handcrafted features and classical machine learning models, but recent advances have shifted toward deep learning-based techniques that leverage the power of convolutional neural networks (CNNs) for feature extraction and anomaly localization \[[11](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/3aa681914a7da79f7d7293f51a058eefe61c8bb7)\].

One of the most influential works in this area is the MVTec AD dataset, which provides a comprehensive benchmark for evaluating unsupervised anomaly detection methods. This dataset contains 5354 high-resolution images with over 70 different types of defects, including scratches, dents, and contaminations, and includes pixel-accurate ground truth annotations \[[11](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/3aa681914a7da79f7d7293f51a058eefe61c8bb7)\]. The dataset has been widely used to evaluate the performance of various deep learning-based approaches, such as convolutional autoencoders, generative adversarial networks (GANs), and feature descriptors using pre-trained CNNs \[[11](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/3aa681914a7da79f7d7293f51a058eefe61c8bb7)\].

PatchCore \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2106.08265)\] and PaDiM \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/2011.08785)\] are two notable methods that have achieved state-of-the-art results on the MVTec AD benchmark. PatchCore utilizes a maximally representative memory bank of nominal patch-features to improve detection and localization accuracy, achieving an AUROC score of up to 99.6% \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2106.08265)\]. PaDiM, on the other hand, employs a pretrained CNN for patch embedding and multivariate Gaussian distributions to model the normal class, while also exploiting correlations between different semantic levels of the CNN for better localization \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/2011.08785)\]. Both methods have demonstrated competitive performance in industrial settings, particularly for real-time applications \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2106.08265)\]\[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/2011.08785)\].

Another approach that has shown promise is the use of hybrid fusion schemes for multimodal anomaly detection. The M3DM method \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/2303.00601)\] combines 3D point cloud and RGB image data using a hybrid fusion strategy that includes unsupervised feature fusion with patch-wise contrastive learning and decision layer fusion with multiple memory banks. This approach has been evaluated on the MVTec-3D AD dataset, where it outperforms existing methods in both detection and segmentation precision \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/2303.00601)\].

In addition to industrial applications, anomaly detection has also been explored in the medical domain. ### <a id='category-medical-domain-applications-of-anomaly-detection'></a>Medical Domain Applications of Anomaly Detection: 3 papers found.
In the medical domain, anomaly detection (AD) has gained significant attention for its ability to identify rare diseases and anomalies in imaging data, such as brain MRI, liver CT, retinal OCT, chest X-ray, and digital histopathology \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2106.08265)\]. AD methods often rely on generative models, such as denoising diffusion probabilistic models (DDPMs), which offer superior mode coverage and sample quality compared to GANs and VAEs, although they face challenges in scalability and sampling efficiency \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/2011.08785)\]. To address these challenges, researchers have proposed partial diffusion strategies, such as AnoDDPM, which significantly improves performance in anomaly detection tasks, particularly in medical imaging \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/2011.08785)\]. Furthermore, the lack of standardized benchmarks has hindered the development of robust AD methods, prompting efforts to create comprehensive evaluation frameworks that include multiple medical datasets and diverse AD algorithms \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2106.08265)\]. These benchmarks aim to provide a fair and unified comparison, enabling researchers to evaluate and refine their methods more effectively \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2106.08265)\]. Additionally, recent works have explored the use of self-supervised learning and reconstruction-based approaches to enhance the accuracy and generalizability of AD models in medical imaging \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/2303.00601)\]. These advancements highlight the growing importance of AD in healthcare, driven by the need for reliable and efficient tools for early disease detection and diagnostic support.

### <a id='category-deep-learning-approaches-for-anomaly-localization'></a>Deep Learning Approaches for Anomaly Localization: 4 papers found.
In the realm of deep learning for anomaly localization, recent approaches have focused on improving both detection accuracy and computational efficiency, particularly for deployment on resource-constrained devices. Methods such as DRÆM \[[6](http://arxiv.org/abs/2108.07610)\] and UniAD \[[7](http://arxiv.org/abs/2206.03687)\] have introduced discriminative and unified frameworks to enhance anomaly detection and localization without requiring extensive post-processing. DRÆM leverages a joint representation of anomalous images and their reconstructions, enabling direct anomaly localization, while UniAD addresses the challenge of multi-class anomaly detection by introducing layer-wise query decoders and neighbor masked attention modules to avoid shortcut learning. Additionally, memory-augmented autoencoders (MemAE) \[[10](http://arxiv.org/abs/1904.02639)\] have been proposed to improve generalization by incorporating memory modules that store prototypical normal patterns, thereby enhancing anomaly detection performance. These methods collectively highlight the ongoing efforts to balance accuracy with efficiency, especially in industrial settings where deployment on embedded systems is critical.
