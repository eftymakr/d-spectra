## Related Works

Nonverbal communication plays a crucial role in human interaction, encompassing gestures, facial expressions, and body language that convey intent and emotion \[[7](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/4814337c5145dbea034b9c646163dc2e71e8ebff)\]. Existing large language models (LLMs) have primarily focused on text-based understanding and generation, often neglecting the integration of nonverbal cues \[[7](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/4814337c5145dbea034b9c646163dc2e71e8ebff)\]. This limitation restricts their ability to create fully immersive conversational experiences \[[7](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/4814337c5145dbea034b9c646163dc2e71e8ebff)\]. Recent efforts have sought to address this gap by exploring multimodal approaches that incorporate visual and acoustic modalities \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/1802.00923)\]. For instance, the Multi-attention Recurrent Network (MARN) was introduced to understand human communication by integrating language, vision, and acoustic modalities \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/1802.00923)\]. MARN utilizes a Multi-attention Block to discover interactions between modalities over time and stores these interactions in a hybrid memory using the Long-short Term Hybrid Memory (LSTHM) \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/1802.00923)\]. This model demonstrates state-of-the-art performance on multimodal sentiment analysis and speaker trait recognition \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/1802.00923)\].

Similarly, the CMU-MOSEI dataset and the Dynamic Fusion Graph (DFG) technique were developed to analyze human multimodal language, emphasizing the need for large-scale datasets and interpretable fusion methods \[[4](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/006fdeff6e1a81c404317ee4056d6cc72f9c0e50)\]. The DFG approach allows for the exploration of how modalities interact in human multimodal language, offering competitive performance compared to previous state-of-the-art methods \[[4](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/006fdeff6e1a81c404317ee4056d6cc72f9c0e50)\]. Another notable work is the introduction of the Qwen-VL series, which combines vision and language understanding, enabling tasks such as image description, question-answering, and visual grounding \[[8](http://arxiv.org/abs/2308.12966)\]. This model demonstrates superior performance on various visual-centric benchmarks and real-world dialog benchmarks \[[8](http://arxiv.org/abs/2308.12966)\].

The Kosmos-1 model also highlights the importance of multimodal perception and language understanding, demonstrating capabilities in vision tasks, perception-language tasks, and OCR-free NLP \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2302.14045)\]. Kosmos-1 is trained on web-scale multimodal corpora and evaluates performance across zero-shot, few-shot, and multimodal chain-of-thought prompting settings \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2302.14045)\]. Additionally, the Kosmos-2 model extends this work by integrating grounding capabilities, allowing for the alignment of text with visual descriptions and bounding boxes \[[12](http://arxiv.org/abs/2306.14824)\]. These advancements underscore the growing interest in multimodal models that can effectively understand and generate nonverbal cues alongside text.

In addition to these models, the Macaw-LLM model integrates visual, audio, and textual information, demonstrating the potential for multi-modal language modeling \[[9](http://arxiv.org/abs/2306.09093)\].
