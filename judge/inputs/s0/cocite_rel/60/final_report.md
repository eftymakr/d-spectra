## Related Works

Calibration of deep learning models has been a central topic in recent years, particularly due to the overconfidence issue exhibited by modern neural networks \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/1706.04599)\]. Early work focused on understanding the calibration properties of neural networks, revealing that they are often poorly calibrated, especially when trained with hard labels \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/1706.04599)\]. This has motivated the development of post-hoc calibration techniques, which aim to adjust the predicted probabilities to better reflect the true likelihood of correct predictions \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/1706.04599)\].

Several methods have been proposed to address this issue. Temperature scaling, a simple variant of Platt scaling, has been shown to be surprisingly effective in calibrating predictions across various architectures and datasets \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/1706.04599)\]. However, its effectiveness is limited to single-parameter adjustments, which may not be sufficient for complex calibration tasks \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/1706.04599)\]. More recently, deep ensembles have been introduced as an alternative to Bayesian neural networks, offering a simple and scalable approach to uncertainty estimation \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/1612.01474)\]. These ensembles produce well-calibrated uncertainty estimates that are as good or better than approximate Bayesian methods \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/1612.01474)\].

The importance of calibration in real-world applications has led to the development of methods that explicitly account for dataset shift. For example, mixup training has been shown to improve calibration by generating augmented data during training, leading to better-calibrated models and reduced overconfidence on out-of-distribution data \[[4](http://arxiv.org/abs/1905.11001)\]. Additionally, the use of label smoothing in mixup training plays a significant role in improving calibration \[[4](http://arxiv.org/abs/1905.11001)\].

Despite these advances, many existing calibration methods struggle under dataset shift, where the test distribution differs from the training distribution \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/1906.02530)\]. This has led to the development of more robust calibration techniques, such as Bayesian binning, which provides a non-parametric approach to calibration \[[6](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/8d115c3b2ee80e0754360a154a9369bc1658b607)\]. Furthermore, methods like histogram binning have been explored, although they are sample inefficient compared to scaling-based methods \[[5](http://arxiv.org/abs/1909.10155)\].

To address these limitations, the paper proposes the use of an Unconstrained Monotonic Neural Network (UMNN) as a flexible calibrator, capable of learning arbitrary monotonic functions \[[12](http://arxiv.org/abs/1908.05164)\]. This approach significantly improves the flexibility and expressiveness of calibration methods while maintaining the order-preserving property necessary for accurate calibration \[[12](http://arxiv.org/abs/1908.05164)\]. To further enhance the calibration performance, the paper introduces a novel loss function called Smooth Calibration Loss (SCLoss), which ensures the necessary conditions for ideal calibration \[[12](http://arxiv.org/abs/1908.05164)\].

Temperature scaling, a simple post-processing method, has been shown to effectively calibrate predictions across various architectures and datasets \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/1706.04599)\]. In contrast, Bayesian Binning into Quantiles (BBQ) introduces a non-parametric approach that post-processes the output of a binary classifier, allowing for more accurate and flexible calibration \[[6](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/8d115c3b2ee80e0754360a154a9369bc1658b607)\]. This method is computationally tractable and has been validated through experiments on both real and simulated datasets, demonstrating its empirical accuracy \[[6](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/8d115c3b2ee80e0754360a154a9369bc1658b607)\]. Additionally, the modeling of epistemic and aleatoric uncertainties in Bayesian deep learning frameworks further enhances the ability to capture model uncertainty, leading to more robust predictions and new loss functions that improve performance in tasks such as semantic segmentation and depth regression \[[13](http://arxiv.org/abs/1703.04977)\]. These approaches highlight the importance of non-parametric and Bayesian methods in achieving well-calibrated models, which is crucial for reliable decision-making in AI systems.

[Snippet]: «Monotonic neural networks have recently been proposed as a way to define invertible transformations. These transformations can be combined into powerful autoregressive flows that have been shown to be universal approximators of continuous probability distributions. Architectures that ensure monotonicity typically enforce constraints on weights and activation functions, which enables invertability but leads to a cap on the expressiveness of the resulting transformations. In this work, we propose the Unconstrained Monotonic Neural Network (UMNN) architecture based on the insight that a function is monotonic as long as its derivative is strictly positive. In particular, this latter condition can be enforced with a free-form neural network whose only constraint is the positiveness of its output. We evaluate our new invertible building block within a new autoregressive flow (UMNN-MAF) and demonstrate its effectiveness on density estimation experiments. We also illustrate the ability of UMNNs to improve variational inference.»
