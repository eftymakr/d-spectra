## Related Works

Maximum Inner Product Search (MIPS) has been a focal point in machine learning and information retrieval, particularly in high-dimensional data applications \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/1405.5869)\]. Traditional approaches to MIPS often rely solely on inner product (IP) similarity, which can lead to issues such as local optima and redundant computations \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/1405.5869)\]. Alternatively, some methods reduce the MIPS problem to Nearest Neighbor Search (NNS) under the Euclidean metric through space projection, which may result in topology destruction and information loss \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/1405.5869)\]. However, recent research suggests that there is no inherent binary opposition between IP and Euclidean metrics, and integrating both can significantly enhance MIPS performance \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/1405.5869)\].

Asymmetric LSH (ALSH) was introduced to address the limitations of traditional LSH for MIPS, by converting inner products into approximate near neighbor search problems through asymmetric transformations \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/1610.03317)\]. This approach demonstrated that inner products could be efficiently handled with hashing schemes, leading to sublinear time algorithms for MIPS \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/1610.03317)\]. However, existing asymmetric transformations, such as L2-ALSH and XBOX, suffer from large distortion errors, leading to poor search accuracy \[[18](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/865488b467250ceb85bb59d35b8d4fb4f10ae421)\].

Graph-based methods have also emerged as a promising alternative to hashing-based approaches. The inner-product navigable small world graph (ip-NSW) represents a state-of-the-art method for approximate MIPS, achieving significant speedups over existing baselines \[[4](http://arxiv.org/abs/1909.13459)\]. However, the performance of ip-NSW is attributed to its ability to match the norm bias of the MIPS problem, where large norm items dominate the search process \[[4](http://arxiv.org/abs/1909.13459)\]. To further improve performance, ip-NSW+ was introduced, which combines angular and inner-product proximity graphs to enhance search accuracy and robustness \[[4](http://arxiv.org/abs/1909.13459)\].

Norm-Ranging LSH (NORM-RANGING LSH) was proposed to address the limitations of SIMPLE-LSH by partitioning datasets into sub-datasets and building hash indexes independently, thereby reducing query time complexity \[[6](http://arxiv.org/abs/1809.08782)\]. The category "Hashing-Based MIPS Methods" explores hashing techniques for Maximum Inner Product Search (MIPS), a critical task in high-dimensional data applications. Early work introduced the first provably sublinear time algorithm for approximate MIPS, leveraging asymmetric transformations to convert the problem into approximate near neighbor search, enabling efficient hashing schemes \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/1405.5869)\]. This approach extended the Locality Sensitive Hashing (LSH) framework to accommodate asymmetric hashing, offering a theoretically grounded solution \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/1405.5869)\]. Subsequent research, such as SIMPLE-LSH, improved practical performance but faced challenges with long-tailed data distributions, prompting the development of NORM-RANGING LSH, which partitions datasets to reduce normalization errors and improve query efficiency \[[6](http://arxiv.org/abs/1809.08782)\]. Another line of work proposed alternative transformations, converting MIPS into cosine similarity search using signed random projections, offering a different theoretical and empirical approach with improved accuracy \[[7](http://arxiv.org/abs/1410.5410)\]. More recently, H2-ALSH was introduced, addressing distortion errors through novel transformations and homocentric hypersphere partitioning, achieving better search accuracy and efficiency \[[18](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/865488b467250ceb85bb59d35b8d4fb4f10ae421)\]. These methods collectively highlight the evolving strategies for hashing-based MIPS, emphasizing the importance of asymmetric transformations, dataset partitioning, and theoretical guarantees to enhance both performance and practical applicability.

### <a id='category-mips-problem-definition-and-challenges'></a>MIPS Problem Definition and Challenges: 6 papers found.
The MIPS problem, which involves efficiently finding the maximum inner product between a query and a set of vectors, has been a focal point in machine learning and information retrieval, particularly for high-dimensional data applications \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/1405.5869)\]. Existing approaches often rely on inner product similarity, which can suffer from local optima and redundant computations, or transform the problem into nearest neighbor search under the Euclidean metric, which may distort data topology and lead to information loss \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/1405.5869)\]. However, recent studies have highlighted the potential of combining inner product and Euclidean metrics through hybrid indexing and search strategies to enhance performance \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/1405.5869)\]. For instance, some works have proposed branch-and-bound algorithms based on tree data structures to address the inner-product search problem, offering significant improvements over naive methods \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/1610.03317)\]. Others have introduced novel data structures like the cone tree to further optimize dual-tree algorithms for multiple queries \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/1610.03317)\]. Additionally, there has been a focus on developing hardware-efficient methods, such as blocked matrix multiply (BMM), which can outperform state-of-the-art solvers in certain scenarios \[[3](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/25454f456c514219c9d6eeb42200ebbf378db9ca)\]. These efforts underscore the ongoing challenges and innovations in the MIPS domain, aiming to balance search efficiency with accuracy while adapting to varying data characteristics \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/1405.5869)\].

A notable method, the inner-product navigable small world graph (ip-NSW), has emerged as a state-of-the-art solution, achieving significant speedups by constructing a graph that prioritizes high-norm items, which are more likely to be relevant in MIPS \[[4](http://arxiv.org/abs/1909.13459)\]. Additionally, graph-based methods have been integrated with reinforcement learning and imitation learning to adaptively navigate the graph, especially when ground truths are unavailable \[[16](http://arxiv.org/abs/2201.09290)\]. These strategies highlight the flexibility and effectiveness of graph-based indexing in MIPS, offering robust solutions across diverse data distributions \[[3](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/25454f456c514219c9d6eeb42200ebbf378db9ca)\].
