## Related Works

Cross-domain Sequential Recommendation (CDSR) has gained significant attention due to its ability to alleviate data sparsity and cold-start problems by leveraging information across multiple domains \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2304.03891)\]. Early approaches primarily focused on transferring knowledge from a source domain to a target domain using latent space mappings \[[7](http://arxiv.org/abs/1910.05189)\]. However, these methods often relied on overlapping users, limiting their applicability in real-world scenarios where user interactions are sparse \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2304.03891)\]. To address this, several works have proposed graph-based models to capture both intra- and inter-sequence relationships \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/2106.02768)\]. For instance, C2DSR \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2304.03891)\] introduces a graph neural network to mine inter-sequence item collaborative relationships and a sequential attentive encoder to capture intra-sequence item relationships, enabling joint learning of single- and cross-domain user preferences. Similarly, DA-GCN \[[6](http://arxiv.org/abs/2105.03300)\] utilizes domain-aware graph convolution networks to learn user-specific node representations, enhancing cross-domain recommendations by considering explicit cross-domain graph structures.

Another line of research focuses on dual learning mechanisms to iteratively transfer information between domains \[[7](http://arxiv.org/abs/1910.05189)\]. The DASL model \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/2106.02768)\] introduces dual embedding and dual attention components to simultaneously learn user preferences in both domains. This approach improves cross-domain recommendations by matching latent embeddings with candidate items through a dual-attention learning mechanism. Similarly, DDTCDR \[[7](http://arxiv.org/abs/1910.05189)\] employs a latent orthogonal mapping to preserve relations between users across different latent spaces, enhancing cross-domain knowledge transfer. These methods highlight the importance of bidirectional latent relations and domain-specific preferences in improving cross-domain recommendation performance.

Recent studies have also explored the use of attention mechanisms to model complex sequential patterns \[[10](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/fa36b8d483b06e0bce366970fde747761151f411)\]. For example, the Mixed Information Flow Network \[[5](http://arxiv.org/abs/2012.00485)\] incorporates behavior transfer and knowledge transfer units to capture both behavioral and knowledge flows across domains. This allows the model to decide when and which cross-domain information to use based on users' current preferences. Similarly, the DREAM model \[[12](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/96bfe33a2503e960153a934e10be91da4d8b13a6)\] introduces a decoupled representation framework that simultaneously learns single- and cross-domain user preferences. It leverages supervised contrastive learning and focal loss to address data sparsity and class imbalance issues, demonstrating significant improvements over existing methods.

### <a id='category-cross-domain-sequential-recommendation-frameworks'></a>Cross-domain Sequential Recommendation Frameworks: 12 papers found.
Cross-domain Sequential Recommendation (CDSR) has emerged as a critical research area to address data sparsity and cold-start problems by leveraging user interactions across multiple domains \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2304.03891)\]. Existing methods often rely on overlapping users to transfer knowledge between domains, but this limits their applicability in real-world scenarios where user overlap is minimal \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/2311.04590)\]. To overcome this, recent works have explored adaptive frameworks that can handle open-world assumptions, such as the Adaptive Multi-Interest Debiasing (AMID) framework, which introduces a multi-interest information module and a doubly robust estimator to improve performance in non-overlapping user scenarios \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/2311.04590)\]. Additionally, some studies have focused on modeling inter-sequence and intra-sequence item interactions to better capture user preferences, such as the C2DSR framework, which uses graph neural networks and sequential attentive encoders to jointly learn single- and cross-domain representations \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2304.03891)\]. Other approaches, like the Dual Attentive Sequential Learning (DASL) model, employ a dual learning mechanism to iteratively transfer information between domains, enhancing cross-domain click-through rate predictions \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/2106.02768)\]. Furthermore, the Shared-account Cross-domain Sequential Recommendation (SCSR) task has gained attention, particularly in scenarios where multiple users share a single account across domains, requiring methods like π-Net and PSJNet to handle shared account and cross-domain challenges through parallel modeling and information-sharing mechanisms \[[7](http://arxiv.org/abs/1910.05189)\]\[[8](http://arxiv.org/abs/2103.01696)\]. These approaches highlight the ongoing efforts to improve cross-domain sequential recommendation by addressing both domain-specific and cross-domain challenges through advanced modeling techniques.

### <a id='category-knowledge-transfer-and-behavior-flow-models'></a>Knowledge Transfer and Behavior Flow Models: 3 papers found.
The category "Knowledge Transfer and Behavior Flow Models" focuses on enhancing recommendation systems through advanced techniques like cross-domain learning and leveraging large language models (LLMs). Cross-domain sequential recommendation aims to predict user preferences by analyzing interactions across multiple domains, but it faces challenges such as the overlap dilemma, where methods rely heavily on users with interactions in all domains, and transition complexity, which involves learning intricate patterns from mixed behavior sequences \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/2106.02768)\]. To address these issues, recent studies have explored mixed information flow networks that integrate both behavioral and knowledge transfer mechanisms, enabling more effective cross-domain information utilization \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/2106.02768)\]. Additionally, transfer learning approaches, such as collaborative cross networks (CoNet), have been proposed to facilitate knowledge sharing between domains by introducing cross connections and joint loss functions, leading to improved performance in recommendation tasks \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/2311.04590)\]. LLMs, with their strong representation and reasoning capabilities, offer a promising avenue for overcoming these challenges by capturing semantic relationships between items and users, thus enhancing the accuracy and adaptability of recommendation systems \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2304.03891)\]. These advancements highlight the growing importance of integrating knowledge transfer and behavior flow models in improving the robustness and effectiveness of cross-domain recommendation systems.

[Snippet]: «Providing accurate recommendations to newly joined users (or potential users, so-called cold-start users) has remained a challenging yet important problem in recommender systems. To infer the preferences of such cold-start users based on their preferences observed in other domains, several cross-domain recommendation (CDR) methods have been studied. The state-of-the-art Embedding and Mapping approach for CDR (EMCDR) aims to infer the latent vectors of cold-start users by supervised mapping from the latent space of another domain. In this paper, we propose a novel CDR framework based on semi-supervised mapping, called SSCDR, which effectively learns the cross-domain relationship even in the case that only a few number of labeled data is available. To this end, it first learns the latent vectors of users and items for each domain so that their interactions are represented by the distances, then trains a cross-domain mapping function to encode such distance information by exploiting both overlapping users as labeled data and all the items as unlabeled data. In addition, SSCDR adopts an effective inference technique that predicts the latent vectors of cold-start users by aggregating their neighborhood information. Our extensive experiments on different CDR scenarios show that SSCDR outperforms the state-of-the-art methods in terms of CDR accuracy, particularly in the realistic settings that a small portion of users overlap between two domains.»
