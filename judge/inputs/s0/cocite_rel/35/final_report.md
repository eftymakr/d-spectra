The category "Model Uncertainty and Flexibility in Protein Conformations" addresses the challenges of predicting and representing the ensemble nature of protein structures, which is essential for understanding their functional dynamics. Recent advancements in deep learning, such as AlphaFold2, have significantly improved the accuracy of protein structure prediction, but they face limitations in capturing conformational diversity and flexibility \[[1](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/21a4afa74caf78e28880b7fe4c30a09a5f9c14e3)\]. Studies have shown that AlphaFold2 often fails to reproduce the full range of conformations observed experimentally, particularly when the conformational diversity of a protein increases \[[1](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/21a4afa74caf78e28880b7fe4c30a09a5f9c14e3)\]. This limitation is exacerbated by the heterogeneity in conformational flexibility among homologous protein families, which complicates the prediction of multiple conformers from a single sequence \[[1](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/21a4afa74caf78e28880b7fe4c30a09a5f9c14e3)\]. Furthermore, the main-chain flexibility associated with apo-holo conformers has been linked to the local model quality score (plDDT), suggesting that plDDT values can provide insights into conformational changes during ligand binding \[[1](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/21a4afa74caf78e28880b7fe4c30a09a5f9c14e3)\]. These findings highlight the need for more sophisticated models that can better account for the dynamic and ensemble nature of protein conformations, which remains a critical challenge in structural biology \[[1](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/21a4afa74caf78e28880b7fe4c30a09a5f9c14e3)\].
