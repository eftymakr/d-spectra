## Related Works

Video super-resolution (VSR) has been a focal point in computer vision, with numerous methods proposed to enhance the resolution of low-quality videos while preserving temporal consistency and visual quality. Early approaches relied on optical flow and motion compensation to align frames, but these methods often introduced artifacts or were computationally expensive \[[4](http://arxiv.org/abs/1812.02898)\]. More recently, deep learning-based methods have emerged as a promising alternative, leveraging convolutional neural networks (CNNs) to learn complex mappings between low-resolution (LR) and high-resolution (HR) frames \[[17](http://arxiv.org/abs/2002.11616)\]. These methods typically process multiple frames in a sliding window fashion, but they often suffer from high computational costs and limited temporal consistency \[[14](http://arxiv.org/abs/2008.00455)\].

Several works have attempted to address these limitations by introducing recurrent architectures that allow for better exploitation of temporal dependencies. For instance, the Frame-Recurrent Video Super-Resolution framework \[[14](http://arxiv.org/abs/2008.00455)\] uses a recurrent structure to iteratively refine HR estimates, reducing computational overhead and improving temporal consistency. Similarly, the Recurrent Back-Projection Network (RBPN) \[[18](http://arxiv.org/abs/1903.10128)\] integrates spatial and temporal contexts from continuous video frames using a recurrent encoder-decoder module, allowing for more accurate and efficient super-resolution. These methods demonstrate the potential of recurrent networks in VSR, but they still face challenges in handling large motions and maintaining high-quality outputs \[[18](http://arxiv.org/abs/1903.10128)\].

Another line of research has focused on transformers for VSR, leveraging their ability to model long-range dependencies. The Video Super-Resolution Transformer \[[28](http://arxiv.org/abs/2106.06847)\] introduces a spatial-temporal convolutional self-attention layer to exploit locality information and a bidirectional optical flow-based feed-forward layer to align features across frames. While this approach shows promise, it still requires careful design to balance computational efficiency and performance \[[28](http://arxiv.org/abs/2106.06847)\].

In addition to recurrent and transformer-based methods, some works have explored the use of attention mechanisms to improve alignment and feature fusion. For example, the Progressive Fusion Video Super-Resolution Network \[[2](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/755227d7ea41b1d90df31f411f9ecbc998c20d26)\] introduces an improved non-local operation to avoid the need for explicit motion estimation and compensation, thereby improving efficiency and performance. Similarly, the Temporally-Deformable Alignment Network (TDAN) \[[4](http://arxiv.org/abs/1812.02898)\] dynamically aligns frames at the feature level without computing optical flow, leading to better artifact reduction and motion handling.

### <a id='category-transformer-based-vsr-with-spatial-temporal-attention'></a>Transformer-Based VSR with Spatial-Temporal Attention: 3 papers found.
Video restoration methods have evolved to address the challenges of temporal information utilization and parallel processing in video super-resolution (VSR). Traditional approaches often rely on sliding window strategies or recurrent architectures, which are limited by frame-by-frame processing \[[12](http://arxiv.org/abs/2206.02146)\]. More recently, transformer-based models have been introduced to leverage parallel computation for sequence-to-sequence modeling, offering better scalability and efficiency \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/2201.12288)\]. However, standard transformers face challenges in exploiting spatial-temporal locality and feature alignment, which are crucial for VSR \[[28](http://arxiv.org/abs/2106.06847)\]. To address these issues, researchers have proposed specialized transformer variants, such as the Video Restoration Transformer (VRT), which incorporates temporal reciprocal self attention (TRSA) and parallel warping to enable joint motion estimation and feature fusion across video clips \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/2201.12288)\]. Similarly, the Recurrent Video Restoration Transformer (RVRT) integrates parallel processing with a globally recurrent framework, allowing for a balance between model size, effectiveness, and efficiency \[[12](http://arxiv.org/abs/2206.02146)\]. These advancements highlight the growing trend of using transformer architectures with spatial-temporal attention mechanisms to improve the quality and efficiency of video restoration tasks.

### <a id='category-global-spatio-temporal-attention-mechanisms'></a>Global Spatio-Temporal Attention Mechanisms: 3 papers found.
Global spatio-temporal attention mechanisms have been increasingly explored in video super-resolution (VSR) to effectively integrate spatial and temporal information. These mechanisms aim to address the challenges of recovering missing details and maintaining consistency across frames, especially in videos with large motion or low frame rates. For instance, some approaches divide the input sequence into groups based on frame rates, allowing complementary information to be used for detail recovery, which is then integrated with attention modules and intra-group fusion techniques \[[1](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/c6a8ff4d9dcdf9e5d82310b847af999ed6655f65)\]. Others propose one-stage frameworks that directly synthesize high-resolution slow-motion videos from low-resolution, low-frame-rate inputs by temporally interpolating LR frame features and aligning temporal information through deformable ConvLSTM \[[2](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/755227d7ea41b1d90df31f411f9ecbc998c20d26)\]. Additionally, models like STARnet jointly handle spatial and temporal super-resolution, leveraging mutual relationships between resolution and frame rate to improve performance \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/2201.12288)\]. These methods highlight the importance of combining spatial and temporal processing to achieve better quality and efficiency in VSR tasks.
