## Related Works

Deep learning has emerged as a powerful paradigm for solving inverse problems in imaging, with two prominent approaches being deep-unrolling and plug-and-play (PnP) methods. PnP approaches, which integrate off-the-shelf deep denoisers into iterative algorithms, offer flexibility in handling varying compression ratios (CRs) \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/1704.03488)\]. However, they often suffer from limitations in reconstruction accuracy and speed \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/1902.05399)\]. On the other hand, unrolling methods, which transform truncated iterative optimization processes into end-to-end trainable neural networks, typically achieve better accuracy and faster inference \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/1712.02862)\]. Yet, they require fine-tuning or retraining when CR changes, limiting their adaptability \[[4](http://arxiv.org/abs/1611.03679)\].

The integration of both approaches has been explored in several works. For instance, the work by \[[5](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/b954a94af132294a4a4e2efce15027758a535742)\] proposed a neural network architecture based on algorithm unrolling, connecting iterative algorithms to deep learning models. This approach improved interpretability while maintaining performance gains \[[6](http://arxiv.org/abs/1508.02848)\]. Similarly, \[[7](http://arxiv.org/abs/1704.03264)\] introduced a model-based deep learning architecture for inverse problems, leveraging the forward model to reduce the need for training data and improve performance \[[8](http://arxiv.org/abs/2003.10428)\]. These studies highlight the potential of combining model-based methods with deep learning to achieve both flexibility and accuracy.

The concept of algorithm unrolling has also been applied to image deblurring tasks. For example, \[[9](http://arxiv.org/abs/1704.06625)\] proposed a neural network architecture that unrolled the half-quadratic splitting algorithm, achieving significant performance gains while maintaining interpretability \[[10](http://arxiv.org/abs/1409.2574)\]. This approach demonstrated the effectiveness of unrolling in capturing the iterative structure of traditional algorithms while benefiting from the power of deep learning \[[11](http://arxiv.org/abs/1802.01770)\]. Additionally, \[[12](http://arxiv.org/abs/1608.03981)\] introduced a tuning-free PnP algorithm that automatically determines internal parameters, improving the practicality of PnP methods \[[13](http://arxiv.org/abs/0912.3522)\].

The use of proximal operators in deep learning for inverse problems has also been explored. \[[14](http://arxiv.org/abs/1612.02177)\] discussed the role of proximal operators in variational methods and proposed replacing them with denoising neural networks to improve generalizability and reduce the need for problem-specific training \[[15](http://arxiv.org/abs/1809.04693)\]. ### <a id='category-model-based-deep-learning-architectures'></a>Model-Based Deep Learning Architectures: 11 papers found.
Model-based deep learning architectures have gained significant attention for their ability to integrate domain-specific knowledge into neural network designs, improving performance on inverse problems such as image restoration and deblurring \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/1712.02862)\]. These approaches often rely on unrolling iterative optimization algorithms, such as conjugate gradients or proximal gradient descent, into neural network layers, allowing for end-to-end training while preserving the physical fidelity of the forward model \[[7](http://arxiv.org/abs/1704.03264)\]. This strategy enables the network to adapt to varying conditions, such as different noise levels or blur kernels, without requiring retraining from scratch \[[14](http://arxiv.org/abs/1612.02177)\]. For instance, the TNRD framework incorporates dynamic nonlinear reaction diffusion models, which are trained to learn time-dependent parameters, leading to efficient and high-quality image restoration \[[6](http://arxiv.org/abs/1508.02848)\]. Similarly, the DnCNN model leverages residual learning and batch normalization to improve training efficiency and denoising performance, particularly for blind Gaussian denoising tasks \[[12](http://arxiv.org/abs/1608.03981)\]. The SRN-DeblurNet further simplifies network structures while maintaining high performance in single image deblurring, demonstrating that simpler models can still achieve state-of-the-art results \[[11](http://arxiv.org/abs/1802.01770)\]. Additionally, the PGD-Net framework explicitly integrates forward models into the learning process, ensuring prediction consistency with physical principles, which is crucial for tasks like Magnetic Resonance Fingerprinting \[[7](http://arxiv.org/abs/1704.03264)\]. These model-based approaches often outperform traditional plug-and-play methods by offering better accuracy and flexibility, especially when dealing with complex or dynamic scenes \[[14](http://arxiv.org/abs/1612.02177)\]. The integration of domain knowledge through model unrolling not only enhances performance but also reduces the need for extensive training data and computational resources \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/1712.02862)\].

### <a id='category-deep-unfolding-for-inverse-problems'></a>Deep Unfolding for Inverse Problems: 11 papers found.
The category "Deep Unfolding for Inverse Problems" encompasses a range of approaches that integrate deep learning with traditional iterative optimization methods to solve inverse problems in imaging and signal processing. These methods typically involve unrolling iterative algorithms into deep neural networks, allowing for end-to-end training while retaining interpretability \[[10](http://arxiv.org/abs/1409.2574)\]. For example, the Learned Primal-Dual algorithm unrolls a proximal primal-dual optimization method, replacing proximal operators with convolutional neural networks to directly work from raw measured data \[[10](http://arxiv.org/abs/1409.2574)\]. Similarly, the LDAMP network mimics the behavior of the denoising-based approximate message passing (D-AMP) algorithm, offering improved accuracy and speed over existing methods like BM3D-AMP and NLR-CS \[[9](http://arxiv.org/abs/1704.06625)\]. Plug-and-play (PnP) approaches, which use off-the-shelf deep denoisers within iterative algorithms, have also gained traction for their flexibility in handling varying compression ratios, though they often face limitations in reconstruction accuracy and speed \[[27](http://arxiv.org/abs/2005.06001)\]. Recent works have sought to bridge these gaps by proposing hybrid models that combine the strengths of both PnP and unrolling approaches, such as the end-to-end trainable unfolding network that leverages both learning-based and model-based methods to achieve flexibility and performance \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/1712.02862)\]. These techniques highlight the growing trend of integrating domain knowledge with deep learning to enhance the efficiency and effectiveness of solving inverse problems in imaging and beyond \[[10](http://arxiv.org/abs/1409.2574)\].
