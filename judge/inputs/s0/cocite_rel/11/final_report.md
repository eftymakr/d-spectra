## Related Works

3D human pose estimation has attracted significant research interest due to its applications in action recognition, human-computer interaction, and augmented reality. Early methods primarily relied on 2D pose estimation followed by 3D reconstruction using geometric constraints or prior knowledge \[[11](http://arxiv.org/abs/1705.03098)\]. However, these approaches often suffer from depth ambiguities and self-occlusions \[[1](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/50167a9e6bdc1f64a58fe8a3c990105fec0bd2bc)\]. Recent advances have focused on leveraging deep learning to directly estimate 3D poses from 2D inputs, with an emphasis on capturing spatial and temporal dependencies \[[16](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/0646857f867d9a624f2b53d1ffb4de99e225b2d3)\]. 

Several works have explored the use of graph-based models to exploit the structural relationships between body joints. For instance, \[[1](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/50167a9e6bdc1f64a58fe8a3c990105fec0bd2bc)\] proposed a graph convolutional network to model spatial-temporal relationships, while \[[12](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/2170b81eaac4095f5948b7922f29200a25db9b2b)\] introduced a high-order GCN to capture long-range dependencies. These approaches have shown promise in improving the accuracy of 3D pose estimation by incorporating anatomical knowledge and modeling complex interactions between joints \[[1](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/50167a9e6bdc1f64a58fe8a3c990105fec0bd2bc)\]. However, they often struggle with the computational complexity of modeling long-range dependencies \[[12](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/2170b81eaac4095f5948b7922f29200a25db9b2b)\].

To address the limitations of traditional GCNs, \[[16](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/0646857f867d9a624f2b53d1ffb4de99e225b2d3)\] introduced a modulated GCN that allows for dynamic modulation of graph structures, enabling the model to adapt to different motion patterns. This approach significantly improves the accuracy of 3D pose estimation while maintaining a compact model size \[[16](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/0646857f867d9a624f2b53d1ffb4de99e225b2d3)\]. Similarly, \[[24](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/92d61a05d76cc859c073e57eedbd82186aecdaff)\] proposed a Spatio-Temporal Criss-Cross Attention (STC) block to decompose correlation learning into spatial and temporal components, reducing computational costs while preserving long-range dependencies \[[24](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/92d61a05d76cc859c073e57eedbd82186aecdaff)\].

Another line of research focuses on improving generalization to rare or unseen poses by leveraging local pose distributions \[[4](http://arxiv.org/abs/2007.09389)\]. This approach splits the body into local regions and processes them separately, allowing the model to better generalize to unseen configurations. Additionally, \[[26](http://arxiv.org/abs/2310.16288)\] introduced a hybrid transformer-GCN architecture that combines the strengths of both models to capture both local and global dependencies, achieving state-of-the-art results on benchmark datasets \[[26](http://arxiv.org/abs/2310.16288)\].

In addition to graph-based models, attention mechanisms have been widely adopted to model long-range dependencies and improve temporal consistency \[[8](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/30de7fecb146356d7ae6cafa072997674601e0f5)\]. For example, \[[8](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/30de7fecb146356d7ae6cafa072997674601e0f5)\] proposed an attention-based framework that adaptively identifies significant frames and tensor outputs, leading to more accurate 3D pose estimation. Similarly, \[[19](http://arxiv.org/abs/2103.14304)\] introduced a Strided Transformer that reduces redundancy in 2D pose sequences while maintaining long-range dependencies, achieving competitive results on benchmark datasets \[[19](http://arxiv.org/abs/2103.14304)\].

### <a id='category-graph-convolutional-networks-for-pose-estimation'></a>Graph Convolutional Networks for Pose Estimation: 10 papers found.
Graph Convolutional Networks (GCNs) have been extensively explored for 3D human pose estimation, with various approaches addressing challenges such as long-range dependencies and occlusions \[[1](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/50167a9e6bdc1f64a58fe8a3c990105fec0bd2bc)\]. Early works focused on incorporating spatial and temporal consistency through graph-based methods, leveraging domain knowledge about human body configurations to improve estimation accuracy \[[1](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/50167a9e6bdc1f64a58fe8a3c990105fec0bd2bc)\]. More recent studies have introduced multi-scale architectures, such as the Graph Stacked Hourglass Networks, which process features across different skeletal representations to capture both local and global patterns \[[5](http://arxiv.org/abs/2103.16385)\]. High-order GCNs have also been proposed to aggregate features from nodes at various distances, enabling the modeling of long-range interactions between body joints \[[4](http://arxiv.org/abs/2007.09389)\]. Additionally, residual connections have been integrated to preserve important information from initial features as the network depth increases \[[9](http://arxiv.org/abs/2111.00950)\]. The Modulated GCN further advances this field by introducing weight and affinity modulation, allowing for more flexible and accurate modeling of body joint relationships \[[6](http://arxiv.org/abs/1711.08585)\]. Furthermore, the Global-local Adaptive Graph Convolutional Network (GLA-GCN) has been developed to improve 3D human pose lifting by leveraging ground truth data and spatiotemporal graph structures \[[15](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/22e305b7b66bdedf379fa678802cb46e8bb546b1)\]. These approaches collectively highlight the importance of graph-based modeling in capturing complex human motion patterns and improving the robustness of 3D pose estimation.

### <a id='category-2d-to-3d-reconstruction-methods'></a>2D to 3D Reconstruction Methods: 5 papers found.
Recent approaches to 2D-to-3D human pose reconstruction have primarily focused on leveraging deep learning to either directly predict 3D poses from images or to refine 2D poses into 3D space \[[11](http://arxiv.org/abs/1705.03098)\]. While end-to-end methods have shown promise, they often struggle with the visual analysis component, which contributes significantly to the overall error \[[11](http://arxiv.org/abs/1705.03098)\]. In contrast, two-step methods first estimate 2D poses using convolutional networks and then map these 2D joint locations into 3D space \[[1](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/50167a9e6bdc1f64a58fe8a3c990105fec0bd2bc)\]. This approach has been further refined by incorporating temporal consistency, where sequence-to-sequence models with LSTM units and temporal smoothness constraints are used to improve the coherence of 3D pose estimates across frames \[[1](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/50167a9e6bdc1f64a58fe8a3c990105fec0bd2bc)\]. Additionally, methods like Pose2Mesh have been proposed to directly estimate 3D mesh coordinates from 2D poses, addressing challenges related to appearance domain gaps and 3D rotation representation issues \[[17](http://arxiv.org/abs/2008.09047)\]. Another line of research has focused on volumetric representations, where 3D space is discretized into voxels, and convolutional networks are trained to predict per-voxel likelihoods for joints, leading to more accurate 3D pose estimation \[[29](http://arxiv.org/abs/1611.07828)\]. Furthermore, pre-trained models such as the P-STMO have been developed to capture both spatial and temporal dependencies through self-supervised pre-training and fine-tuning, achieving competitive performance with reduced computational overhead \[[30](http://arxiv.org/abs/2203.07628)\]. These methods collectively highlight the ongoing efforts to improve the accuracy and efficiency of 2D-to-3D human pose reconstruction, with a growing emphasis on temporal consistency, volumetric representations, and pre-trained models.

Recent advancements in transformer architectures for 3D pose estimation have focused on addressing the challenges of modeling long-range spatial and temporal dependencies, as well as capturing both local and global joint relationships \[[13](http://arxiv.org/abs/2111.12707)\]. While traditional convolutional approaches remain dominant in human pose estimation, transformer-based models have shown promising results by leveraging self-attention mechanisms to better capture complex interactions between joints \[[1](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/50167a9e6bdc1f64a58fe8a3c990105fec0bd2bc)\]. For instance, PoseFormer introduces a purely transformer-based approach that models spatial-temporal correlations across frames to achieve state-of-the-art performance on benchmark datasets like Human3.6M and MPI-INF-3DHP \[[1](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/50167a9e6bdc1f64a58fe8a3c990105fec0bd2bc)\]. Similarly, MHFormer addresses the ambiguity in 3D pose estimation by proposing a multi-hypothesis transformer that generates and refines multiple plausible pose hypotheses, improving accuracy through self- and cross-hypothesis communication \[[13](http://arxiv.org/abs/2111.12707)\]. GraFormer enhances joint relationship modeling by integrating graph convolution layers with transformer blocks, allowing for more effective representation of both local and non-local connections \[[15](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/22e305b7b66bdedf379fa678802cb46e8bb546b1)\]. The Strided Transformer further improves efficiency by reducing redundancy in 2D pose sequences through strided convolutions, enabling the generation of accurate 3D poses with fewer parameters \[[19](http://arxiv.org/abs/2103.14304)\]. Additionally, STCFormer introduces a spatio-temporal criss-cross attention mechanism to decompose correlation learning into spatial and temporal components, achieving superior performance on challenging datasets \[[24](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/92d61a05d76cc859c073e57eedbd82186aecdaff)\]. Finally, AGFormer combines transformer and graph convolutional networks to better capture local joint relationships while maintaining global context, leading to improved accuracy and efficiency \[[26](http://arxiv.org/abs/2310.16288)\]. These works collectively highlight the growing trend of using transformer architectures to overcome the limitations of traditional methods in 3D human pose estimation.
