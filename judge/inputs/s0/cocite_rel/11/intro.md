## Related Works

3D human pose estimation has attracted significant research interest due to its applications in action recognition, human-computer interaction, and augmented reality. Early methods primarily relied on 2D pose estimation followed by 3D reconstruction using geometric constraints or prior knowledge \[[11](http://arxiv.org/abs/1705.03098)\]. However, these approaches often suffer from depth ambiguities and self-occlusions \[[1](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/50167a9e6bdc1f64a58fe8a3c990105fec0bd2bc)\]. Recent advances have focused on leveraging deep learning to directly estimate 3D poses from 2D inputs, with an emphasis on capturing spatial and temporal dependencies \[[16](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/0646857f867d9a624f2b53d1ffb4de99e225b2d3)\]. 

Several works have explored the use of graph-based models to exploit the structural relationships between body joints. For instance, \[[1](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/50167a9e6bdc1f64a58fe8a3c990105fec0bd2bc)\] proposed a graph convolutional network to model spatial-temporal relationships, while \[[12](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/2170b81eaac4095f5948b7922f29200a25db9b2b)\] introduced a high-order GCN to capture long-range dependencies. These approaches have shown promise in improving the accuracy of 3D pose estimation by incorporating anatomical knowledge and modeling complex interactions between joints \[[1](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/50167a9e6bdc1f64a58fe8a3c990105fec0bd2bc)\]. However, they often struggle with the computational complexity of modeling long-range dependencies \[[12](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/2170b81eaac4095f5948b7922f29200a25db9b2b)\].

To address the limitations of traditional GCNs, \[[16](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/0646857f867d9a624f2b53d1ffb4de99e225b2d3)\] introduced a modulated GCN that allows for dynamic modulation of graph structures, enabling the model to adapt to different motion patterns. This approach significantly improves the accuracy of 3D pose estimation while maintaining a compact model size \[[16](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/0646857f867d9a624f2b53d1ffb4de99e225b2d3)\]. Similarly, \[[24](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/92d61a05d76cc859c073e57eedbd82186aecdaff)\] proposed a Spatio-Temporal Criss-Cross Attention (STC) block to decompose correlation learning into spatial and temporal components, reducing computational costs while preserving long-range dependencies \[[24](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/92d61a05d76cc859c073e57eedbd82186aecdaff)\].

Another line of research focuses on improving generalization to rare or unseen poses by leveraging local pose distributions \[[4](http://arxiv.org/abs/2007.09389)\]. This approach splits the body into local regions and processes them separately, allowing the model to better generalize to unseen configurations. Additionally, \[[26](http://arxiv.org/abs/2310.16288)\] introduced a hybrid transformer-GCN architecture that combines the strengths of both models to capture both local and global dependencies, achieving state-of-the-art results on benchmark datasets \[[26](http://arxiv.org/abs/2310.16288)\].

In addition to graph-based models, attention mechanisms have been widely adopted to model long-range dependencies and improve temporal consistency \[[8](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/30de7fecb146356d7ae6cafa072997674601e0f5)\]. For example, \[[8](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/30de7fecb146356d7ae6cafa072997674601e0f5)\] proposed an attention-based framework that adaptively identifies significant frames and tensor outputs, leading to more accurate 3D pose estimation. Similarly, \[[19](http://arxiv.org/abs/2103.14304)\] introduced a Strided Transformer that reduces redundancy in 2D pose sequences while maintaining long-range dependencies, achieving competitive results on benchmark datasets \[[19](http://arxiv.org/abs/2103.14304)\].
