## Related Works

Brain tumor segmentation remains a critical challenge in medical image analysis due to the high variability in tumor morphology, intensity, and spatial distribution across multimodal MRI scans \[[1](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/420f3f1078a6d8e0696572c032877079286051c6)\]. Early approaches relied on traditional image processing techniques such as thresholding and region growing, which often struggled with irregular tumor shapes and indistinct boundaries \[[24](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/8689d964b3d1acacfa698c9d81314a9571819757)\]. With the advent of deep learning, convolutional neural networks (CNNs) have emerged as a powerful tool for this task, offering improved accuracy and robustness \[[15](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/f89fe7d194c63a62b2cb76fac08ef525b483b964)\]. 

The U-Net architecture, introduced by Ronneberger et al., has become a foundational model for biomedical image segmentation due to its encoder-decoder structure and skip connections that preserve spatial information \[[27](http://arxiv.org/abs/1606.06650)\]. Variants of U-Net, such as 3D U-Net, have been proposed to handle volumetric data, enabling dense 3D segmentation from sparse annotations \[[27](http://arxiv.org/abs/1606.06650)\]. However, training such models from scratch is computationally intensive, and pre-trained models are often underutilized \[[1](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/420f3f1078a6d8e0696572c032877079286051c6)\]. 

Several studies have explored the use of pre-trained models to improve segmentation performance. For example, \[[15](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/f89fe7d194c63a62b2cb76fac08ef525b483b964)\] proposed a method that combines Fuzzy C-Means clustering with CNNs to achieve high accuracy in brain tumor segmentation. Similarly, \[[26](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/237e15ea6558e45e544cf2efc68e675cf1c44547)\] evaluated U-Net architectures with transfer learning using pre-trained models like ResNet50, DenseNet169, and EfficientNet-B7, demonstrating significant improvements in segmentation metrics. The use of EfficientNet-B7 as an encoder in U-Net led to superior performance, achieving high Dice scores and IoU metrics \[[26](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/237e15ea6558e45e544cf2efc68e675cf1c44547)\].

The nnU-Net framework, introduced by isensee et al., is a self-adapting U-Net architecture that automatically adjusts its configuration based on the input data \[[10](http://arxiv.org/abs/1809.10486)\]. This framework has been widely adopted in the BraTS challenges due to its robustness and efficiency \[[18](http://arxiv.org/abs/2011.00848)\]. However, it still requires extensive training and may not fully leverage pre-trained weights from other domains \[[1](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/420f3f1078a6d8e0696572c032877079286051c6)\]. To address this, recent works have focused on integrating pre-trained weights from ImageNet into medical segmentation models. For instance, \[[12](http://arxiv.org/abs/2404.05911)\] proposed LATUP-Net, a lightweight 3D attention U-Net with parallel convolutions that significantly reduces computational demand while maintaining high segmentation accuracy. This model achieved impressive Dice scores on BraTS 2020 and 2021 datasets \[[12](http://arxiv.org/abs/2404.05911)\].
