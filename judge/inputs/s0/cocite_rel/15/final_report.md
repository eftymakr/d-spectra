## Related Works

Brain tumor segmentation remains a critical challenge in medical image analysis due to the high variability in tumor morphology, intensity, and spatial distribution across multimodal MRI scans \[[1](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/420f3f1078a6d8e0696572c032877079286051c6)\]. Early approaches relied on traditional image processing techniques such as thresholding and region growing, which often struggled with irregular tumor shapes and indistinct boundaries \[[24](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/8689d964b3d1acacfa698c9d81314a9571819757)\]. With the advent of deep learning, convolutional neural networks (CNNs) have emerged as a powerful tool for this task, offering improved accuracy and robustness \[[15](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/f89fe7d194c63a62b2cb76fac08ef525b483b964)\]. 

The U-Net architecture, introduced by Ronneberger et al., has become a foundational model for biomedical image segmentation due to its encoder-decoder structure and skip connections that preserve spatial information \[[27](http://arxiv.org/abs/1606.06650)\]. Variants of U-Net, such as 3D U-Net, have been proposed to handle volumetric data, enabling dense 3D segmentation from sparse annotations \[[27](http://arxiv.org/abs/1606.06650)\]. However, training such models from scratch is computationally intensive, and pre-trained models are often underutilized \[[1](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/420f3f1078a6d8e0696572c032877079286051c6)\]. 

Several studies have explored the use of pre-trained models to improve segmentation performance. For example, \[[15](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/f89fe7d194c63a62b2cb76fac08ef525b483b964)\] proposed a method that combines Fuzzy C-Means clustering with CNNs to achieve high accuracy in brain tumor segmentation. Similarly, \[[26](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/237e15ea6558e45e544cf2efc68e675cf1c44547)\] evaluated U-Net architectures with transfer learning using pre-trained models like ResNet50, DenseNet169, and EfficientNet-B7, demonstrating significant improvements in segmentation metrics. The use of EfficientNet-B7 as an encoder in U-Net led to superior performance, achieving high Dice scores and IoU metrics \[[26](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/237e15ea6558e45e544cf2efc68e675cf1c44547)\].

The nnU-Net framework, introduced by isensee et al., is a self-adapting U-Net architecture that automatically adjusts its configuration based on the input data \[[10](http://arxiv.org/abs/1809.10486)\]. This framework has been widely adopted in the BraTS challenges due to its robustness and efficiency \[[18](http://arxiv.org/abs/2011.00848)\]. However, it still requires extensive training and may not fully leverage pre-trained weights from other domains \[[1](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/420f3f1078a6d8e0696572c032877079286051c6)\]. To address this, recent works have focused on integrating pre-trained weights from ImageNet into medical segmentation models. For instance, \[[12](http://arxiv.org/abs/2404.05911)\] proposed LATUP-Net, a lightweight 3D attention U-Net with parallel convolutions that significantly reduces computational demand while maintaining high segmentation accuracy. This model achieved impressive Dice scores on BraTS 2020 and 2021 datasets \[[12](http://arxiv.org/abs/2404.05911)\].

Efficient neural network design for brain tumor segmentation has been a focal point in recent research, with several studies emphasizing the importance of architectural innovations and preprocessing techniques to enhance performance. For instance, the use of small 3×3 kernels in Convolutional Neural Networks (CNNs) has been shown to enable deeper architectures while reducing overfitting through fewer weights \[[1](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/420f3f1078a6d8e0696572c032877079286051c6)\]. Additionally, intensity normalization as a preprocessing step, combined with data augmentation, has proven effective in improving segmentation accuracy in MRI images \[[1](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/420f3f1078a6d8e0696572c032877079286051c6)\]. Another study highlights the benefits of leveraging both local and global features in CNNs, introducing a novel architecture that incorporates a final convolutional layer acting as a fully connected layer, resulting in a 40-fold speedup \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/2201.01266)\]. This approach not only improves efficiency but also enhances the model's ability to capture contextual information crucial for accurate segmentation. Furthermore, a cascaded architecture has been explored, where the output of an initial CNN serves as input for a subsequent CNN, allowing for more precise modeling of local label dependencies \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/2201.01266)\]. These advancements underscore the ongoing efforts to balance accuracy with computational efficiency in brain tumor segmentation tasks.

### <a id='category-u-net-architecture-and-variants'></a>U-Net Architecture and Variants: 10 papers found.
U-Net and its variants have been extensively studied and adapted for brain tumor segmentation in medical imaging, with numerous improvements proposed to enhance performance and efficiency \[[1](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/420f3f1078a6d8e0696572c032877079286051c6)\]. The original U-Net architecture, introduced in 2015, features a contracting path for capturing context and an expanding path for precise localization, enabling effective segmentation of neuronal structures in electron microscopic stacks \[[1](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/420f3f1078a6d8e0696572c032877079286051c6)\]. Subsequent works have extended the U-Net framework to 3D, such as the 3D U-Net, which has become a standard for multi-region brain tumor segmentation \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/2201.01266)\]. Variants like the E1D3 U-Net introduce multiple decoders to segment different tumor regions (whole tumor, tumor core, and enhancing core) simultaneously, improving performance without ensembling \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/2201.01266)\]. Additionally, the nnU-Net framework was developed to automate the adaptation of U-Net architectures, reducing manual adjustments and improving generalizability across diverse medical imaging tasks \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/1505.03540)\]. The integration of pre-trained models, such as EfficientNet-B7, into U-Net encoders has further enhanced performance, achieving high Dice scores and IoU metrics on datasets like BraTS2020 \[[4](http://arxiv.org/abs/1810.11654)\]. These advancements highlight the ongoing evolution of U-Net-based architectures, focusing on reducing training requirements, improving efficiency, and leveraging pre-trained weights for better segmentation outcomes \[[5](http://arxiv.org/abs/1505.04597)\].
