## Related Works

The integration of knowledge graphs (KGs) into large language models (LLMs) to enhance reasoning capabilities has gained significant attention in recent years. While LLMs have demonstrated strong reasoning abilities, they often struggle with multi-step reasoning and incorporating structured knowledge from KGs without adaptation \[[9](http://arxiv.org/abs/2306.08302)\]. This limitation has motivated researchers to explore methods that combine the strengths of LLMs with the structured knowledge represented in KGs \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2308.09729)\]. 

Several approaches have been proposed to improve the reasoning capabilities of LLMs using KGs. One notable work is the MindMap method, which leverages KGs to enhance LLMs' inference and transparency by enabling them to comprehend KG inputs and infer with a combination of implicit and external knowledge \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2308.09729)\]. This approach also elicits the mind map of LLMs, revealing their reasoning pathways based on the ontology of knowledge \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2308.09729)\]. Similarly, the Knowledge Graph Prompting (KGP) method for multi-document question answering constructs a knowledge graph over multiple documents and uses an LLM-based graph traversal agent to gather supporting passages, improving the accuracy of LLMs in complex reasoning tasks \[[5](http://arxiv.org/abs/2308.11730)\].

Another line of research focuses on retrieval-augmented generation (RAG) models, which combine pre-trained parametric and non-parametric memory to enhance the ability of LLMs to access and manipulate knowledge \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/2005.11401)\]. These models use a dense vector index of Wikipedia, accessed with a pre-trained neural retriever, to improve the factual accuracy of generated text \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/2005.11401)\]. Additionally, the Program-Guided Fact-Checking (ProgramFC) model decomposes complex claims into sub-tasks that can be solved using a shared library of specialized functions, enabling more accurate and interpretable fact-checking \[[4](http://arxiv.org/abs/2305.12744)\].

The KEPLER model proposes a unified approach for integrating knowledge embedding and pre-trained language representation, allowing for better integration of factual knowledge into LLMs \[[7](http://arxiv.org/abs/1911.06136)\]. This model encodes textual entity descriptions with a pre-trained language model and jointly optimizes the knowledge embedding and language modeling objectives, leading to improved performance on various NLP tasks \[[7](http://arxiv.org/abs/1911.06136)\]. Similarly, the JAKET framework jointly pre-trains language models and KGs to model both the knowledge graph and language, enabling the pre-trained model to adapt to unseen knowledge graphs in new domains \[[12](http://arxiv.org/abs/2010.00796)\].

The CoLAKE model further advances the integration of knowledge and language by jointly learning contextualized representations for both, using a modified Transformer encoder to pre-train on large-scale word-knowledge graphs \[[14](http://arxiv.org/abs/2010.00309)\].
