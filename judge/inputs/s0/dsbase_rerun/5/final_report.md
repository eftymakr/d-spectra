## Related Works

Instruction-following in large language models (LLMs) has attracted significant attention in recent years, as it enables models to adapt their outputs based on user-provided instructions. Prior work has explored various approaches to improve instruction-following capabilities, ranging from fine-tuning on instruction-response pairs to leveraging in-context learning (ICL) and non-instructional data \[[12' 2024-09-21](http://arxiv.org/abs/2409.14254v1)\]. For instance, \[[12' 2024-09-21](http://arxiv.org/abs/2409.14254v1)\] demonstrated that instruction-response pairs are not strictly necessary for instruction-following, as training solely on responses can still yield instruction-following behavior. This suggests that pre-trained models may already possess some level of instruction-response mapping that can be revealed through appropriate training strategies. Furthermore, \[[8' 2023-04-06](http://arxiv.org/abs/2304.03277v1)\] proposed using GPT-4 to generate instruction-following data for LLM fine-tuning, which led to superior zero-shot performance on new tasks compared to previous methods.

Another line of research focuses on improving instruction-following through structured reasoning frameworks. \[[6' 2025-03-05](http://arxiv.org/abs/2503.03669v1)\] introduced Attentive Reasoning Queries (ARQs), a novel approach that guides LLMs through systematic reasoning steps with targeted queries to ensure adherence to complex instructions. This method showed significant improvements in instruction-following performance, particularly in multi-turn conversations and scenarios requiring precise task execution. Similarly, \[[16' 2022-11-08](http://arxiv.org/abs/2211.04591v1)\] studied the ability of reinforcement learning (RL) agents to follow instructions in text-based games, finding that equipping agents with structured representations of natural language instructions in the form of Linear Temporal Logic (LTL) improved their performance in executing tasks to completion.

This corpus expands traditional <query, passage> pairs into expressive <instruction, query, passage> triplets, enabling more efficient representation learning for encoder-only models.
