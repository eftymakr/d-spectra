## Related Works

Dense retrieval systems typically rely on pre-trained language models (e.g., BERT, LLaMA) that are fine-tuned using contrastive learning to encode text into dense representations that can be compared via a shallow similarity operation, such as an inner product \[[1' 2024-08-13](http://arxiv.org/abs/2408.06663v5)\]. Recent research has questioned the relative roles of fine-tuning versus pre-training in dense retrievers, suggesting that retrieval knowledge is primarily acquired during pre-training, and that fine-tuning may not be able to acquire knowledge not learned during pre-training \[[1' 2024-08-13](http://arxiv.org/abs/2408.06663v5)\]. This idea was initially studied in the context of a BERT-based encoder using DPR as a representative dense retriever \[[1' 2024-08-13](http://arxiv.org/abs/2408.06663v5)\]. However, this analysis has been extended in this work by testing other representation approaches, such as comparing the use of CLS tokens with mean pooling, and by evaluating different backbone architectures, including encoder-only BERT and decoder-only LLaMA \[[1' 2024-08-13](http://arxiv.org/abs/2408.06663v5)\]. Additionally, the study includes additional datasets, such as MSMARCO and Natural Questions, to further validate the findings \[[1' 2024-08-13](http://arxiv.org/abs/2408.06663v5)\].

The claim that pre-trained knowledge underpins retrieval performance, with fine-tuning primarily adjusting neuron activation rather than reorganizing knowledge, has been supported by several studies. For example, Amuro and Char \[[2' 2024-05-13](http://arxiv.org/abs/2405.13008v1)\] found that continual pre-training improves the model in a latent way that becomes evident after fine-tuning, and that extra fine-tuning can significantly enhance performance on datasets where the model does not demonstrate capability during pre-training. Furthermore, the study by Amuro and Char \[[2' 2024-05-13](http://arxiv.org/abs/2405.13008v1)\] also noted that supervised fine-tuning can lead to forgetting of previously known domain knowledge, highlighting the limitations of fine-tuning in acquiring new knowledge. Similarly, the work by Zhang et al. \[[3' 2021-09-22](http://arxiv.org/abs/2109.10739v1)\] found that fine-tuning may not be effective in acquiring knowledge that was not learned during pre-training, reinforcing the idea that retrieval knowledge is primarily gained during pre-training.

However, this pattern does not hold universally. For example, in mean-pooled models such as Contriever, the fine-tuning process appears to have a more significant impact on retrieval performance \[[1' 2024-08-13](http://arxiv.org/abs/2408.06663v5)\]. Similarly, in decoder-based models such as LLaMA, the role of fine-tuning in acquiring retrieval knowledge is more pronounced \[[1' 2024-08-13](http://arxiv.org/abs/2408.06663v5)\]. These findings suggest that the effectiveness of fine-tuning may vary depending on the representation approach and the architecture of the model.
