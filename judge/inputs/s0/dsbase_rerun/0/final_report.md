## Related Works

Economic inequality remains a critical challenge, influencing disparities in education, healthcare, and social stability \[[1' 2025-01-15](http://arxiv.org/abs/2501.08573v1)\]. Traditional tax systems, such as the U.S. federal income tax, aim to reduce inequality but often lack adaptability to complex economic dynamics \[[1' 2025-01-15](http://arxiv.org/abs/2501.08573v1)\]. Models like the Saez Optimal Taxation framework attempt to dynamically adjust tax policies but struggle with addressing taxpayer heterogeneity and irrational behavior \[[1' 2025-01-15](http://arxiv.org/abs/2501.08573v1)\]. This paper introduces TaxAgent, an innovative integration of large language models (LLMs) with agent-based modeling (ABM) to design adaptive tax policies \[[1' 2025-01-15](http://arxiv.org/abs/2501.08573v1)\].

Agent-based models (ABMs) have been widely used to simulate economic systems and evaluate policy interventions \[[2' 2020-07-08](http://arxiv.org/abs/2007.04192v1)\]. These models allow for the exploration of emergent behaviors in multi-agent decision-making settings \[[2' 2020-07-08](http://arxiv.org/abs/2007.04192v1)\]. However, the validity of inferences from ABMs depends on the quality of the agents' behavioral models \[[3' 2020-06-09](http://arxiv.org/abs/2006.05048v3)\]. Standard specifications of agent behavioral models rely on heuristic decision-making rules or regressions trained on past data, which can be limiting \[[3' 2020-06-09](http://arxiv.org/abs/2006.05048v3)\]. Reinforcement learning (RL) models offer an alternative, providing adaptive, high-performing, and behaviorally-valid models for agent decision-making in ABMs \[[3' 2020-06-09](http://arxiv.org/abs/2006.05048v3)\]. This is particularly relevant for TaxAgent, where heterogeneous H-Agents simulate real-world taxpayer behaviors \[[1' 2025-01-15](http://arxiv.org/abs/2501.08573v1)\].

The use of LLMs in policy analysis is also gaining traction \[[4' 2024-02-22](http://arxiv.org/abs/2402.14800v2)\]. LLMs can be fine-tuned to act as agents, interacting with environments through tools such as search engines \[[8' 2024-02-18](http://arxiv.org/abs/2402.11651v2)\]. However, LLMs are optimized for language generation rather than tool use, limiting their effectiveness as agents \[[8' 2024-02-18](http://arxiv.org/abs/2402.11651v2)\]. Recent work has explored the integration of negative examples during fine-tuning, allowing LLMs to learn from failed trajectories and improve performance on tasks like mathematical reasoning and strategic question answering \[[8' 2024-02-18](http://arxiv.org/abs/2402.11651v2)\]. This aligns with the approach taken in TaxAgent, where LLMs iteratively optimize tax rates to balance equity and productivity \[[1' 2025-01-15](http://arxiv.org/abs/2501.08573v1)\].

In the context of economic inequality, several studies have examined the effects of taxation and redistribution policies on wealth distribution \[[6' 2019-01-13](http://arxiv.org/abs/1901.03951v1)\]. For example, Castro de Oliveira’s work highlights the role of non-uniform income redistribution and tax evasion in shaping wealth inequality \[[6' 2019-01-13](http://arxiv.org/abs/1901.03951v1)\]. Similarly, the financial accumulation process, particularly compound returns, has been identified as a significant source of inequality, while institutional arrangements such as taxation play a crucial role in shaping economic outcomes \[[6' 2019-01-13](http://arxiv.org/abs/1901.03951v1)\]. These findings support the need for adaptive tax policies that account for taxpayer heterogeneity and irrational behavior, as proposed by TaxAgent \[[1' 2025-01-15](http://arxiv.org/abs/2501.08573v1)\].

In the context of economic inequality and taxation frameworks, existing research highlights the critical role of income redistribution policies and tax control in mitigating wealth disparities. Studies have shown that in capitalist systems, the unrestricted accumulation of wealth leads to increasing inequality, emphasizing the need for well-designed fiscal interventions \[[1' 2025-01-15](http://arxiv.org/abs/2501.08573v1)\]. Computational economic models, such as those extending the work of Castro de Oliveira \[[1' 2025-01-15](http://arxiv.org/abs/2501.08573v1)\], demonstrate that non-uniform income redistribution and strict tax enforcement can foster more equitable wealth distributions \[[1' 2025-01-15](http://arxiv.org/abs/2501.08573v1)\]. Similarly, other analyses underscore the significance of institutional arrangements, including taxation, in shaping long-term economic outcomes, particularly when considering the compounding effects of financial returns on inequality \[[6' 2019-01-13](http://arxiv.org/abs/1901.03951v1)\]. These findings align with broader discussions on how taxation and economic mobility interact, suggesting that heuristic models of taxation can influence the trajectory of wealth accumulation and social mobility \[[6' 2019-01-13](http://arxiv.org/abs/1901.03951v1)\]. The integration of such insights into modern policy design is essential for addressing the complexities of contemporary economic inequality.

### <a id='category-reinforcement-learning-in-agent-modeling'></a>Reinforcement Learning in Agent Modeling: 3 papers found.
Agent-based models (ABMs) are increasingly used in policy analysis to simulate complex systems and evaluate the impact of interventions, such as tax policies \[[3' 2020-06-09](http://arxiv.org/abs/2006.05048v3)\]. These models rely on accurate behavioral representations of agents, which traditionally have been specified through heuristic rules or regression-based methods, both of which have limitations in capturing dynamic and adaptive decision-making \[[3' 2020-06-09](http://arxiv.org/abs/2006.05048v3)\]. Recent research has explored the use of reinforcement learning (RL) to improve the behavioral models of agents within ABMs, as RL agents can learn to maximize rewards and exhibit more realistic, utility-driven behaviors \[[3' 2020-06-09](http://arxiv.org/abs/2006.05048v3)\]. This approach has been tested in policy-relevant scenarios, including minority games and influenza transmission models, where RL-based agents outperformed traditional models in terms of adaptability and effectiveness \[[3' 2020-06-09](http://arxiv.org/abs/2006.05048v3)\]. Additionally, advancements in post-training techniques for large language models (LLMs) have enabled better alignment with specific tasks and human preferences, though challenges such as training instability persist \[[7' 2025-04-28](http://arxiv.org/abs/2504.19599v4)\]. Furthermore, studies have highlighted the value of incorporating both successful and unsuccessful interaction trajectories in fine-tuning LLMs as agents, as these trajectories provide valuable insights for improving performance in tasks like mathematical reasoning and strategic question answering \[[8' 2024-02-18](http://arxiv.org/abs/2402.11651v2)\]. These developments underscore the growing integration of RL and LLMs in agent modeling, offering more robust and flexible frameworks for simulating and optimizing real-world policy systems.
