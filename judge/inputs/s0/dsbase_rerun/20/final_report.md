## Related Works

Single-pixel imaging (SPI) has gained significant attention due to its ability to capture images at non-visible wavelengths and under low-light conditions \[[4' 2017-06-26](http://arxiv.org/abs/1706.08383v1)\]. However, traditional iterative reconstruction methods suffer from limited image quality and long computational times \[[4' 2017-06-26](http://arxiv.org/abs/1706.08383v1)\]. To address these challenges, deep learning approaches have been introduced, offering superior reconstruction quality and faster speeds \[[4' 2017-06-26](http://arxiv.org/abs/1706.08383v1)\]. These deep learning methods often rely on deep denoisers for regularization, forming the basis of plug-and-play (PnP) approaches \[[4' 2017-06-26](http://arxiv.org/abs/1706.08383v1)\].

PnP methods are flexible for varying compression ratios (CRs) but are limited in reconstruction accuracy and speed \[[4' 2017-06-26](http://arxiv.org/abs/1706.08383v1)\]. In contrast, unrolling approaches transform iterative optimization processes into end-to-end trainable networks, achieving better accuracy and faster inference \[[4' 2017-06-26](http://arxiv.org/abs/1706.08383v1)\]. However, unrolling networks typically require fine-tuning or retraining when CR changes \[[4' 2017-06-26](http://arxiv.org/abs/1706.08383v1)\]. This limitation has prompted research into hybrid methods that combine the strengths of both PnP and unrolling approaches \[[4' 2017-06-26](http://arxiv.org/abs/1706.08383v1)\].

Recent work has explored the use of algorithm unrolling to design interpretable neural networks based on iterative algorithms \[[1' 2019-12-22](http://arxiv.org/abs/1912.10557v3)\]. This technique has been applied to various imaging tasks, including image deconvolution and biomedical imaging \[[1' 2019-12-22](http://arxiv.org/abs/1912.10557v3)\]. For instance, the unrolling of the Half-Quadratic Splitting (HQS) algorithm has been shown to improve performance while maintaining convergence properties \[[3' 2024-02-20](http://arxiv.org/abs/2402.12872v2)\]. Similarly, the unrolling of the Alternating Direction Method of Multipliers (ADMM) has been used to develop efficient and interpretable networks for image restoration \[[3' 2024-02-20](http://arxiv.org/abs/2402.12872v2)\].

A key challenge in unrolling is the trade-off between retaining algorithmic properties and enhancing performance \[[3' 2024-02-20](http://arxiv.org/abs/2402.12872v2)\]. To address this, a general proximal trajectory (PT) loss function has been proposed to train HQS/ADMM-unrolling networks such that the learned deep image restorer (DIR) approximates the proximal operator of an ideal explicit restoration regularizer \[[4' 2017-06-26](http://arxiv.org/abs/1706.08383v1)\]. This approach enables the network to flexibly handle varying CRs with a single model, similar to PnP methods, while achieving higher accuracy and speed than previous CR-specific unrolling networks \[[4' 2017-06-26](http://arxiv.org/abs/1706.08383v1)\].

The effectiveness of unrolling techniques has also been demonstrated in other domains, such as medical imaging and computational imaging \[[14' 2021-08-15](http://arxiv.org/abs/2108.06637v1)\]. For example, deep unrolling has been used to improve source localization in biological imaging by combining the interpretability of iterative algorithms with the performance gains of supervised deep learning \[[5' 2021-09-28](http://arxiv.org/abs/2109.14025v1)\]. Additionally, unrolling has been applied to lensless imaging and diffractive lens systems to achieve high-resolution multi-spectral imaging \[[8' 2020-08-26](http://arxiv.org/abs/2008.11625v3)\].
