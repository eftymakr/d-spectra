## Related Works

In-context learning (ICL) has emerged as a critical capability for large language models (LLMs), enabling them to leverage examples from a demonstration corpus to generate accurate outputs without explicit training \[[15' 2023-06-23](http://arxiv.org/abs/2306.13549v4)\]. However, the effectiveness of ICL is heavily dependent on the quality of these demonstrations, which can be compromised by noisy or biased annotations \[[1' 2022-07-06](http://arxiv.org/abs/2207.02463v1)\]. Existing approaches to detecting noisy annotations typically rely on ranking local perplexities, assuming that noisy samples yield higher perplexities than their clean counterparts \[[16' 2023-12-19](http://arxiv.org/abs/2312.12006v1)\]. This assumption, however, is not robust in scenarios with high noise ratios, where many demonstrations may be flawed, leading to unreliable performance \[[16' 2023-12-19](http://arxiv.org/abs/2312.12006v1)\].

The issue of bias in perplexity-based methods has been explored in prior work. For instance, \[[1' 2022-07-06](http://arxiv.org/abs/2207.02463v1)\] highlights that the annotation itself can introduce bias, while \[[14' 2016-12-11](http://arxiv.org/abs/1612.03430v1)\] discusses how domain-specific knowledge within LLMs can also affect the reliability of perplexity estimates. These findings underscore the need for more robust methods that account for both annotation quality and model-specific biases \[[1' 2022-07-06](http://arxiv.org/abs/2207.02463v1)\]\[[14' 2016-12-11](http://arxiv.org/abs/1612.03430v1)\].

To address these challenges, recent studies have proposed alternative approaches to improve the reliability of ICL. For example, \[[1' 2022-07-06](http://arxiv.org/abs/2207.02463v1)\] introduces a dual debiasing framework that uses synthesized neighbors to correct perplexity estimates, thereby improving the accuracy of noise detection. Similarly, \[[1' 2022-07-06](http://arxiv.org/abs/2207.02463v1)\] demonstrates that the performance of ICL can be significantly affected by the presence of noisy samples, emphasizing the importance of developing methods that can effectively identify and mitigate such issues. These studies align with our approach, which introduces a robust Sample Cleanliness Score that is independent of the overall corpus noise level \[[1' 2022-07-06](http://arxiv.org/abs/2207.02463v1)\].

In addition, \[[1' 2022-07-06](http://arxiv.org/abs/2207.02463v1)\] explores the use of LLMs to measure gender representation bias in training data, highlighting the importance of addressing biases at the corpus level. This work is relevant to our discussion, as it underscores the broader implications of bias in training data and the need for methods that can detect and correct such biases \[[1' 2022-07-06](http://arxiv.org/abs/2207.02463v1)\].

Furthermore, \[[1' 2022-07-06](http://arxiv.org/abs/2207.02463v1)\] investigates the use of LLMs to automatically instantiate ontologies with domain-specific knowledge, demonstrating the potential of LLMs in tasks that require structured knowledge. While this work focuses on ontology generation, it highlights the broader capabilities of LLMs in handling complex, knowledge-intensive tasks, which is relevant to the development of robust ICL methods \[[1' 2022-07-06](http://arxiv.org/abs/2207.02463v1)\].
