## Related Works

In-context learning (ICL) has emerged as a critical capability for large language models (LLMs), enabling them to leverage examples from a demonstration corpus to generate accurate outputs without explicit training \[[15' 2023-06-23](http://arxiv.org/abs/2306.13549v4)\]. However, the effectiveness of ICL is heavily dependent on the quality of these demonstrations, which can be compromised by noisy or biased annotations \[[1' 2022-07-06](http://arxiv.org/abs/2207.02463v1)\]. Existing approaches to detecting noisy annotations typically rely on ranking local perplexities, assuming that noisy samples yield higher perplexities than their clean counterparts \[[16' 2023-12-19](http://arxiv.org/abs/2312.12006v1)\]. This assumption, however, is not robust in scenarios with high noise ratios, where many demonstrations may be flawed, leading to unreliable performance \[[16' 2023-12-19](http://arxiv.org/abs/2312.12006v1)\].

The issue of bias in perplexity-based methods has been explored in prior work. For instance, \[[1' 2022-07-06](http://arxiv.org/abs/2207.02463v1)\] highlights that the annotation itself can introduce bias, while \[[14' 2016-12-11](http://arxiv.org/abs/1612.03430v1)\] discusses how domain-specific knowledge within LLMs can also affect the reliability of perplexity estimates. These findings underscore the need for more robust methods that account for both annotation quality and model-specific biases \[[1' 2022-07-06](http://arxiv.org/abs/2207.02463v1)\]\[[14' 2016-12-11](http://arxiv.org/abs/1612.03430v1)\].

To address these challenges, recent studies have proposed alternative approaches to improve the reliability of ICL. The category "Broader Implications of Bias in Training Data" highlights the pervasive and multifaceted impact of bias in large language models (LLMs) and their training data. Bias in training data can manifest as gender representation bias, where certain genders are underrepresented or overrepresented, leading to imbalances that propagate through model outputs \[[3' 2024-04-05](http://arxiv.org/abs/2404.04108v2)\]. This issue is particularly pronounced in gendered languages, where grammatical gender complicates the application of methods developed for English \[[4' 2024-06-19](http://arxiv.org/abs/2406.13677v3)\]. Additionally, the quality and integrity of data science-driven statistics are at risk due to changes in data sources, which can introduce biases, inaccuracies, and ethical concerns \[[5' 2023-06-07](http://arxiv.org/abs/2306.04338v1)\]. The presence of bias in training data also affects the performance and fairness of models, as demonstrated by the bias-performance trade-off, where better performance often correlates with increased bias \[[1' 2022-07-06](http://arxiv.org/abs/2207.02463v1)\]. Furthermore, the use of large language models as oracles for ontology instantiation can introduce biases based on the personal background of the model developers or the data they are trained on \[[3' 2024-04-05](http://arxiv.org/abs/2404.04108v2)\]. These findings underscore the need for rigorous bias analysis at the corpus level, especially in multilingual NLP, and emphasize the importance of developing robust frameworks to detect, quantify, and mitigate bias in training data \[[3' 2024-04-05](http://arxiv.org/abs/2404.04108v2)\]\[[4' 2024-06-19](http://arxiv.org/abs/2406.13677v3)\].

### <a id='category-noise-in-demonstrations-and-its-impact'></a>Noise in Demonstrations and Its Impact: 5 papers found.
The category "Noise in Demonstrations and Its Impact" explores how noise in data, particularly in annotated corpora and demonstrations, affects the performance and reliability of machine learning models. Existing approaches often rely on perplexity metrics to detect noisy annotations, assuming that noisy samples yield higher perplexities than clean ones \[[1' 2022-07-06](http://arxiv.org/abs/2207.02463v1)\]. However, this assumption is challenged when noise ratios are high, as the presence of many flawed demonstrations can distort the perplexity-based paradigm \[[1' 2022-07-06](http://arxiv.org/abs/2207.02463v1)\]. This issue is further compounded by biases stemming from the annotation process itself and the domain-specific knowledge embedded in large language models (LLMs) \[[1' 2022-07-06](http://arxiv.org/abs/2207.02463v1)\]. To address these challenges, researchers have proposed alternative frameworks, such as dual debiasing methods that use synthesized neighbors to correct perplexity estimates and yield a robust Sample Cleanliness Score \[[1' 2022-07-06](http://arxiv.org/abs/2207.02463v1)\]. Similarly, other works have examined the impact of label noise, particularly instance-dependent noise, which is more prevalent in human-annotated datasets and can lead to imbalances in training \[[2' 2024-08-13](http://arxiv.org/abs/2408.06717v3)\]. These studies highlight the need for more sophisticated methods to handle noise in demonstrations, especially in settings where the noise level is high or the data is inherently unreliable \[[2' 2024-08-13](http://arxiv.org/abs/2408.06717v3)\]. Additionally, the design of data-aware models, such as Graph Neural Networks (GNNs), is also affected by noise, as it introduces knowledge gaps and challenges the systematic accumulation of design knowledge \[[3' 2024-04-05](http://arxiv.org/abs/2404.04108v2)\]. Overall, the category underscores the critical role of noise in shaping the effectiveness of learning systems and the importance of developing robust methods to mitigate its impact \[[3' 2024-04-05](http://arxiv.org/abs/2404.04108v2)\].

### <a id='category-in-context-learning-(icl)-overview'></a>In-context Learning (ICL) Overview: 2 papers found.
In-context learning (ICL) has been increasingly recognized as a critical mechanism for enhancing the performance of machine learning models, particularly in settings where labeled data is scarce or noisy \[[7' 2024-06-18](http://arxiv.org/abs/2406.12785v1)\]. Recent work has explored the limitations of traditional ICL approaches, which often rely on assumptions about the distribution of data and the quality of annotations \[[11' 2024-10-10](http://arxiv.org/abs/2410.08355v3)\]. For instance, existing methods assume that noisy annotations lead to higher perplexity, but this breaks down under high noise ratios, where many demonstrations become unreliable \[[7' 2024-06-18](http://arxiv.org/abs/2406.12785v1)\]. To address these challenges, researchers have proposed alternative frameworks, such as energy-based modeling, which allows for more flexible and general forms of ICL by learning unconstrained energy functions \[[7' 2024-06-18](http://arxiv.org/abs/2406.12785v1)\]. Additionally, in the domain of protein engineering, meta-learning approaches have been introduced to improve generalization under data scarcity, leveraging in-context learning and fine-tuning to adapt to new tasks with limited data \[[11' 2024-10-10](http://arxiv.org/abs/2410.08355v3)\]. These developments highlight the growing interest in refining ICL methods to better handle real-world complexities, such as noisy annotations and domain-specific knowledge biases \[[7' 2024-06-18](http://arxiv.org/abs/2406.12785v1)\]\[[11' 2024-10-10](http://arxiv.org/abs/2410.08355v3)\].
