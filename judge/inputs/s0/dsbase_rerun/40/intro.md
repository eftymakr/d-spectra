## Related Works

Zero-Knowledge Proofs (ZKPs) have gained significant attention for their ability to enable secure and private computation, allowing a prover to demonstrate the truth of a statement without revealing additional information \[[1' 2024-12-17](http://arxiv.org/abs/2412.12481v1)\]. This has led to their adoption in various domains, including blockchain technologies, verifiable machine learning, and electronic voting \[[1' 2024-12-17](http://arxiv.org/abs/2412.12481v1)\]. However, the computational complexity of ZKP generation has hindered their widespread adoption \[[1' 2024-12-17](http://arxiv.org/abs/2412.12481v1)\]. Recent efforts have focused on accelerating the key primitives of ZKP protocols, particularly on GPUs and ASICs \[[1' 2024-12-17](http://arxiv.org/abs/2412.12481v1)\]. For instance, \[[2' 2024-08-12](http://arxiv.org/abs/2408.05890v1)\] presents a scalable accelerator framework, SZKP, that achieves full-proof speedups of over 400×, 3×, and 12× over CPU, ASIC, and GPU implementations by leveraging structured dataflows for both Number Theoretic Transform (NTT) and Multi-scalar Multiplication (MSM) operations \[[2' 2024-08-12](http://arxiv.org/abs/2408.05890v1)\]. Similarly, \[[3' 2018-01-08](http://arxiv.org/abs/1801.06027v2)\] introduces a novel architecture for accelerating zk-SNARK prover compute on FPGAs, focusing on the multi-scalar multiplication (MSM) which accounts for the majority of computation time in zk-SNARK systems \[[3' 2018-01-08](http://arxiv.org/abs/1801.06027v2)\]. The proposed architecture exploits parallelism and uses Intel OneAPI for FPGA implementation, achieving 110×–150× speedups compared to reference software libraries \[[3' 2018-01-08](http://arxiv.org/abs/1801.06027v2)\]. 

While these works address performance improvements, they often face challenges such as requiring a trusted setup for each application or generating larger proof sizes with higher verification costs, which limits their applicability in scenarios with numerous verifiers or strict verification time constraints \[[1' 2024-12-17](http://arxiv.org/abs/2412.12481v1)\]. In contrast, this work introduces an accelerator, zkSpeed, for HyperPlonk, a ZKP protocol that supports one-time, universal setup and small proof sizes for typical ZKP applications in publicly verifiable, consensus-based systems \[[1' 2024-12-17](http://arxiv.org/abs/2412.12481v1)\]. The accelerator focuses on two major primitives: SumCheck and Multi-scalar Multiplications (MSMs), which are critical for proof generation \[[1' 2024-12-17](http://arxiv.org/abs/2412.12481v1)\]. The full-chip architecture developed for zkSpeed uses 366.46 mm² and 2 TB/s of bandwidth, achieving geometric mean speedups of 801× over CPU baselines \[[1' 2024-12-17](http://arxiv.org/abs/2412.12481v1)\]. 

Other works have also explored hardware acceleration for ZKPs, such as \[[4' 2024-11-10](http://arxiv.org/abs/2411.06350v1)\], which introduces AMAZE, a highly optimized framework for computing the MiMC block cipher and hash function, which is used in ZKP applications.
