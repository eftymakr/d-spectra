## Related Works

Anomaly detection has been a focal point in machine learning, especially with the rise of intelligent manufacturing and the need for robust quality control in production lines \[[1' 2023-07-12](http://arxiv.org/abs/2307.06052v1)\]. Traditional approaches often rely on statistical models or supervised learning techniques, but these methods struggle with the complexity and variability of real-world industrial data \[[1' 2023-07-12](http://arxiv.org/abs/2307.06052v1)\]. Recent advances have focused on improving both the efficiency and effectiveness of anomaly detection models, particularly in resource-constrained environments \[[1' 2023-07-12](http://arxiv.org/abs/2307.06052v1)\].

Several studies have explored the use of deep learning for anomaly detection, particularly in image-based settings. The MVTec-AD dataset has been widely used to benchmark image anomaly detection models, with many approaches leveraging convolutional neural networks (CNNs) to extract discriminative features \[[1' 2023-07-12](http://arxiv.org/abs/2307.06052v1)\]. One notable method is the PaDiM framework, which fits a multivariate Gaussian distribution to feature vectors extracted from a CNN and uses Mahalanobis distance as the anomaly score \[[1' 2023-07-12](http://arxiv.org/abs/2307.06052v1)\]. This method has been further enhanced with a whitening transformation to enable visualization of anomaly regions \[[1' 2023-07-12](http://arxiv.org/abs/2307.06052v1)\]. Similarly, the SplatPose framework has been proposed for 3D anomaly detection, leveraging Gaussian splatting to estimate object poses and detect anomalies in multi-view settings \[[4' 2024-04-10](http://arxiv.org/abs/2404.06832v1)\].

In the realm of time series anomaly detection, recurrent neural networks (RNNs) have been extensively studied for their ability to model sequential data \[[7' 2024-07-26](http://arxiv.org/abs/2407.18439v1)\]. However, the choice of RNN variant and deep learning framework significantly impacts performance, with limited comparative studies on different implementations \[[7' 2024-07-26](http://arxiv.org/abs/2407.18439v1)\]. Additionally, multiple instance learning (MIL) has been applied to sequential datasets where labels are incomplete or unreliable, offering a promising alternative to single-instance learning \[[8' 2022-10-04](http://arxiv.org/abs/2210.01707v1)\]. This approach is particularly useful in real-world scenarios where annotation is labor-intensive \[[8' 2022-10-04](http://arxiv.org/abs/2210.01707v1)\].

For tabular data, recent work has explored the use of large language models (LLMs) for anomaly detection, demonstrating their potential as zero-shot batch-level detectors \[[5' 2024-06-24](http://arxiv.org/abs/2406.16308v1)\]. These models can identify low-density regions without explicit distribution fitting, making them suitable for a wide range of applications \[[5' 2024-06-24](http://arxiv.org/abs/2406.16308v1)\]. Furthermore, the use of graph neural networks (GNNs) has been investigated for graph-based anomaly detection, with spectral analysis playing a key role in identifying anomalies through the `right-shift' phenomenon in the graph spectrum \[[11' 2022-05-31](http://arxiv.org/abs/2205.15508v1)\].

The deployment of deep learning models in embedded systems remains a challenge due to computational constraints. To address this, lightweight models such as MobileSAM have been proposed, enabling efficient inference on resource-constrained devices \[[1' 2023-07-12](http://arxiv.org/abs/2307.06052v1)\].
