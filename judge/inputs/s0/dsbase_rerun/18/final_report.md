## Related Works

Anomaly detection has been a focal point in machine learning, especially with the rise of intelligent manufacturing and the need for robust quality control in production lines \[[1' 2023-07-12](http://arxiv.org/abs/2307.06052v1)\]. Traditional approaches often rely on statistical models or supervised learning techniques, but these methods struggle with the complexity and variability of real-world industrial data \[[1' 2023-07-12](http://arxiv.org/abs/2307.06052v1)\]. Recent advances have focused on improving both the efficiency and effectiveness of anomaly detection models, particularly in resource-constrained environments \[[1' 2023-07-12](http://arxiv.org/abs/2307.06052v1)\].

Several studies have explored the use of deep learning for anomaly detection, particularly in image-based settings. The MVTec-AD dataset has been widely used to benchmark image anomaly detection models, with many approaches leveraging convolutional neural networks (CNNs) to extract discriminative features \[[1' 2023-07-12](http://arxiv.org/abs/2307.06052v1)\]. One notable method is the PaDiM framework, which fits a multivariate Gaussian distribution to feature vectors extracted from a CNN and uses Mahalanobis distance as the anomaly score \[[1' 2023-07-12](http://arxiv.org/abs/2307.06052v1)\]. This method has been further enhanced with a whitening transformation to enable visualization of anomaly regions \[[1' 2023-07-12](http://arxiv.org/abs/2307.06052v1)\]. Similarly, the SplatPose framework has been proposed for 3D anomaly detection, leveraging Gaussian splatting to estimate object poses and detect anomalies in multi-view settings \[[4' 2024-04-10](http://arxiv.org/abs/2404.06832v1)\].

In the realm of time series anomaly detection, recurrent neural networks (RNNs) have been extensively studied for their ability to model sequential data \[[7' 2024-07-26](http://arxiv.org/abs/2407.18439v1)\]. However, the choice of RNN variant and deep learning framework significantly impacts performance, with limited comparative studies on different implementations \[[7' 2024-07-26](http://arxiv.org/abs/2407.18439v1)\]. Additionally, multiple instance learning (MIL) has been applied to sequential datasets where labels are incomplete or unreliable, offering a promising alternative to single-instance learning \[[8' 2022-10-04](http://arxiv.org/abs/2210.01707v1)\]. This approach is particularly useful in real-world scenarios where annotation is labor-intensive \[[8' 2022-10-04](http://arxiv.org/abs/2210.01707v1)\].

For tabular data, recent work has explored the use of large language models (LLMs) for anomaly detection, demonstrating their potential as zero-shot batch-level detectors \[[5' 2024-06-24](http://arxiv.org/abs/2406.16308v1)\]. These models can identify low-density regions without explicit distribution fitting, making them suitable for a wide range of applications \[[5' 2024-06-24](http://arxiv.org/abs/2406.16308v1)\]. Furthermore, the use of graph neural networks (GNNs) has been investigated for graph-based anomaly detection, with spectral analysis playing a key role in identifying anomalies through the `right-shift' phenomenon in the graph spectrum \[[11' 2022-05-31](http://arxiv.org/abs/2205.15508v1)\].

The deployment of deep learning models in embedded systems remains a challenge due to computational constraints. To address this, lightweight models such as MobileSAM have been proposed, enabling efficient inference on resource-constrained devices \[[1' 2023-07-12](http://arxiv.org/abs/2307.06052v1)\]. ### <a id='category-deep-learning-for-image-based-anomaly-detection'></a>Deep Learning for Image-Based Anomaly Detection: 4 papers found.
In the realm of image-based anomaly detection, recent research has focused on improving efficiency and interpretability while maintaining high detection performance. For instance, a simplified variation of the PaDiM method has been proposed, which uses a multivariate Gaussian distribution and Mahalanobis distance to compute anomaly scores, with additional whitening transformations enabling visual explanations through heatmaps \[[1' 2023-07-12](http://arxiv.org/abs/2307.06052v1)\]. Similarly, the 3D Gaussian splatting-based framework SplatPose addresses the challenge of detecting anomalies in 3D objects from varying poses, offering state-of-the-art results in speed and accuracy \[[4' 2024-04-10](http://arxiv.org/abs/2404.06832v1)\]. Meanwhile, alternative approaches such as Locally Linear Image Structural Embedding (LLISE) have been introduced to better capture image structure and discriminative features, moving beyond traditional MSE-based methods \[[6' 2019-08-25](http://arxiv.org/abs/1908.09288v1)\]. Additionally, the detection of deepfakes has spurred the development of advanced architectures like CAE-Net, which combines spatial and frequency-domain features using EfficientNet, DeiT, and ConvNeXt, achieving high accuracy on imbalanced datasets \[[14' 2025-02-15](http://arxiv.org/abs/2502.10682v3)\]. These advancements highlight the ongoing efforts to balance model complexity, computational demands, and practical deployment, especially in resource-constrained environments such as those found in SMEs.

### <a id='category-comparison-of-anomaly-detection-techniques'></a>Comparison of Anomaly Detection Techniques: 3 papers found.
In the realm of anomaly detection, recent research has focused on improving efficiency and performance, particularly for deployment in resource-constrained environments. One line of work explores batch anomaly detection in multivariate time series, leveraging the Kullback-Leibler divergence to maximize the separation between normal and anomalous data distributions, offering advantages over methods that treat time steps independently \[[2' 2016-10-21](http://arxiv.org/abs/1610.06761v1)\]. Another area of interest is group anomaly detection, which identifies anomalous patterns in collective data, such as overdensities, and has found applications in fields like fundamental physics, where it aids in the discovery of new particles and forces \[[3' 2021-07-06](http://arxiv.org/abs/2107.02821v1)\]. Additionally, there is growing attention on the use of time series foundational models (TSFMs) for anomaly detection, though their black-box nature and computational demands have raised concerns, with traditional statistical and deep learning models often matching or outperforming them in these tasks \[[10' 2024-12-26](http://arxiv.org/abs/2412.19286v1)\]. These developments highlight the ongoing efforts to balance accuracy with practicality, especially for real-world applications such as industrial quality control.

A comprehensive evaluation is then conducted to analyze the performance of each implementation across real-world, open-source time series datasets. The evaluation results provide valuable guidance for selecting the appropriate RNN variant and deep learning framework for real-time, lightweight time series anomaly detection.»

[Snippet]: «Detecting anomalies over real-world datasets remains a challenging task. Data annotation is an intensive human labor problem, particularly in sequential datasets, where the start and end time of anomalies are not known. As a result, data collected from sequential real-world processes can be largely unlabeled or contain inaccurate labels. These characteristics challenge the application of anomaly detection techniques based on supervised learning. In contrast, Multiple Instance Learning (MIL) has been shown effective on problems with incomplete knowledge of labels in the training dataset, mainly due to the notion of bags. While largely under-leveraged for anomaly detection, MIL provides an appealing formulation for anomaly detection over real-world datasets, and it is the primary contribution of this paper. In this paper, we propose an MIL-based formulation and various algorithmic instantiations of this framework based on different design decisions for key components of the framework. We evaluate the resulting algorithms over four datasets that capture different physical processes along different modalities. The experimental evaluation draws out several observations. The MIL-based formulation performs no worse than single instance learning on easy to moderate datasets and outperforms single-instance learning on more challenging datasets. Altogether, the results show that the framework generalizes well over diverse datasets resulting from different real-world application domains.»

[Snippet]: «Anomaly detection presents a unique challenge in machine learning, due to the scarcity of labeled anomaly data. Recent work attempts to mitigate such problems by augmenting training of deep anomaly detection models with additional labeled anomaly samples. However, the labeled data often does not align with the target distribution and introduces harmful bias to the trained model. In this paper, we aim to understand the effect of a biased anomaly set on anomaly detection. Concretely, we view anomaly detection as a supervised learning task where the objective is to optimize the recall at a given false positive rate. We formally study the relative scoring bias of an anomaly detector, defined as the difference in performance with respect to a baseline anomaly detector. We establish the first finite sample rates for estimating the relative scoring bias for deep anomaly detection, and empirically validate our theoretical results on both synthetic and real-world datasets. We also provide an extensive empirical study on how a biased training anomaly set affects the anomaly score function and therefore the detection performance on different anomaly classes. Our study demonstrates scenarios in which the biased anomaly set can be useful or problematic, and provides a solid benchmark for future research.»
