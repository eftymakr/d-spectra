## Related Works

Multi-Domain Recommendation (MDR) has emerged as a promising approach to improve recommendation performance by leveraging knowledge transfer across different domains \[[1' 2023-02-12](http://arxiv.org/abs/2302.05990v2)\]. However, existing methods often adopt a single structure to transfer complex domain-shared knowledge, which can lead to the Negative Transfer Problem (NTP) when there is knowledge conflict or poor-quality domains \[[2' 2023-09-12](http://arxiv.org/abs/2309.06217v2)\]. This issue has been recognized in various contexts, including cross-domain sequential recommendation \[[3' 2023-11-22](http://arxiv.org/abs/2311.13188v1)\], where heterogeneous knowledge between dissimilar domains can lead to performance degradation \[[4' 2019-05-26](http://arxiv.org/abs/1905.10756v4)\]. 

Several approaches have been proposed to address the NTP. For instance, the work by \[[5' 2024-05-21](http://arxiv.org/abs/2405.12892v2)\] introduces a cooperative game-theoretic approach to assess the extent of negative transfer and adaptively assign low weights to prediction losses. Similarly, \[[6' 2023-06-09](http://arxiv.org/abs/2306.06302v2)\] proposes a hierarchical contrastive learning approach that mitigates negative transfer by incorporating information from coarse-level categories into fine-level ones. These methods highlight the need for more nuanced strategies to handle domain interactions \[[7' 2022-01-04](http://arxiv.org/abs/2201.01002v1)\].

In the context of multi-domain recommendation, \[[8' 2022-01-15](http://arxiv.org/abs/2201.05867v1)\] introduces a domain-to-domain translation model (D2D-TM) based on GANs and VAEs to capture both homogeneous and divergent features across domains. This approach is particularly useful when the source domain is not accessible \[[9' 2018-12-15](http://arxiv.org/abs/1812.06229v1)\]. Additionally, \[[10' 2025-04-29](http://arxiv.org/abs/2504.20371v3)\] explores the use of domain-specific adapters and hyper-networks to dynamically generate parameters for multi-domain models, improving flexibility and performance \[[11' 2023-11-03](http://arxiv.org/abs/2311.01831v1)\].

Another line of research focuses on the selection of relevant source domains to avoid negative transfer. For example, \[[12' 2023-06-30](http://arxiv.org/abs/2306.17555v2)\] proposes a data-selective transfer learning approach that efficiently selects speech data based on relevance to the target domain, thereby reducing negative transfer \[[13' 2015-09-08](http://arxiv.org/abs/1509.02409v1)\]. This idea is closely related to the proposed Similar Domain Selection Principle (SDSP) in this work, which dynamically identifies similar domains based on domain metrics and unsupervised distance measures \[[14' 2024-02-09](http://arxiv.org/abs/2402.06213v2)\].

Moreover, \[[15' 2023-03-02](http://arxiv.org/abs/2303.01003v1)\] introduces a method for multi-source-free domain adaptation that leverages uncertainty-aware adaptive distillation to improve model performance without access to source data \[[16' 2021-02-24](http://arxiv.org/abs/2102.12225v3)\]. This aligns with the lightweight and flexible nature of SDSP, which can be integrated into existing MDR methods without introducing significant overhead \[[17' 2025-02-12](http://arxiv.org/abs/2502.08139v1)\].

In summary, while many approaches have been proposed to address the challenges of multi-domain recommendation, the problem of negative transfer remains a critical issue. The proposed SDSP offers a novel solution by explicitly measuring domain-level gaps and dynamically selecting appropriate domains, thereby improving the effectiveness of knowledge transfer \[[18' 2024-04-26](http://arxiv.org/abs/2404.17450v2)\].

### <a id='category-negative-transfer-problem-(ntp)-in-multi-domain-learning'></a>Negative Transfer Problem (NTP) in Multi-Domain Learning: 6 papers found.
The Negative Transfer Problem (NTP) in multi-domain learning has been extensively studied, with various approaches proposed to mitigate its adverse effects. Research has shown that NTP arises when knowledge transfer between domains leads to performance degradation, often due to differences in user preferences or domain characteristics \[[1' 2023-02-12](http://arxiv.org/abs/2302.05990v2)\]. For instance, in cross-domain sequential recommendation, the interplay among multiple domains can lead to negative transfer, where heterogeneous knowledge between dissimilar domains hinders model performance \[[3' 2023-11-22](http://arxiv.org/abs/2311.13188v1)\]. To address this, some studies have introduced frameworks that assess the extent of negative transfer and adaptively assign lower weights to prediction losses from problematic domains \[[3' 2023-11-22](http://arxiv.org/abs/2311.13188v1)\]. Additionally, hierarchical contrastive learning has been employed to incorporate information from coarse-level categories into fine-level interactions, thereby reducing negative transfer \[[3' 2023-11-22](http://arxiv.org/abs/2311.13188v1)\]. In other contexts, such as acoustic model training, negative transfer is mitigated through efficient data selection based on relevance to the target domain, using submodular functions to prioritize acoustically similar training samples \[[13' 2015-09-08](http://arxiv.org/abs/1509.02409v1)\]. Furthermore, in scenarios where target domains have disjoint classes from surrogate domains, including the target domain in training can lead to significant performance degradation due to intermediate shortcuts, highlighting the need for careful domain integration strategies \[[15' 2023-03-02](http://arxiv.org/abs/2303.01003v1)\]. These findings underscore the importance of developing robust methods to handle NTP in multi-domain learning, ensuring that knowledge transfer remains beneficial rather than detrimental.

[Snippet]: «One crucial aspect of partial domain adaptation (PDA) is how to select the relevant source samples in the shared classes for knowledge transfer. Previous PDA methods tackle this problem by re-weighting the source samples based on their high-level information (deep features). However, since the domain shift between source and target domains, only using the deep features for sample selection is defective. We argue that it is more reasonable to additionally exploit the pixel-level information for PDA problem, as the appearance difference between outlier source classes and target classes is significantly large. In this paper, we propose a reinforced transfer network (RTNet), which utilizes both high-level and pixel-level information for PDA problem. Our RTNet is composed of a reinforced data selector (RDS) based on reinforcement learning (RL), which filters out the outlier source samples, and a domain adaptation model which minimizes the domain discrepancy in the shared label space. Specifically, in the RDS, we design a novel reward based on the reconstruct errors of selected source samples on the target generator, which introduces the pixel-level information to guide the learning of RDS. Besides, we develope a state containing high-level information, which used by the RDS for sample selection. The proposed RDS is a general module, which can be easily integrated into existing DA models to make them fit the PDA situation. Extensive experiments indicate that RTNet can achieve state-of-the-art performance for PDA tasks on several benchmark datasets.»

[Snippet]: «With the increase in the business scale and number of domains in online advertising, multi-domain ad recommendation has become a mainstream solution in the industry. The core of multi-domain recommendation is effectively modeling the commonalities and distinctions among domains. Existing works are dedicated to designing model architectures for implicit multi-domain modeling while overlooking an in-depth investigation from a more fundamental perspective of feature distributions. This paper focuses on features with significant differences across various domains in both distributions and effects on model predictions. Extensive offline and online experiments demonstrate the superiority of our method in capturing domain distinctions and improving multi-domain recommendation performance.»
