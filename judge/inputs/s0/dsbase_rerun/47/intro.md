## Related Works

Multi-Domain Recommendation (MDR) has emerged as a promising approach to improve recommendation performance by leveraging knowledge transfer across different domains \[[1' 2023-02-12](http://arxiv.org/abs/2302.05990v2)\]. However, existing methods often adopt a single structure to transfer complex domain-shared knowledge, which can lead to the Negative Transfer Problem (NTP) when there is knowledge conflict or poor-quality domains \[[2' 2023-09-12](http://arxiv.org/abs/2309.06217v2)\]. This issue has been recognized in various contexts, including cross-domain sequential recommendation \[[3' 2023-11-22](http://arxiv.org/abs/2311.13188v1)\], where heterogeneous knowledge between dissimilar domains can lead to performance degradation \[[4' 2019-05-26](http://arxiv.org/abs/1905.10756v4)\]. 

Several approaches have been proposed to address the NTP. For instance, the work by \[[5' 2024-05-21](http://arxiv.org/abs/2405.12892v2)\] introduces a cooperative game-theoretic approach to assess the extent of negative transfer and adaptively assign low weights to prediction losses. Similarly, \[[6' 2023-06-09](http://arxiv.org/abs/2306.06302v2)\] proposes a hierarchical contrastive learning approach that mitigates negative transfer by incorporating information from coarse-level categories into fine-level ones. These methods highlight the need for more nuanced strategies to handle domain interactions \[[7' 2022-01-04](http://arxiv.org/abs/2201.01002v1)\].

In the context of multi-domain recommendation, \[[8' 2022-01-15](http://arxiv.org/abs/2201.05867v1)\] introduces a domain-to-domain translation model (D2D-TM) based on GANs and VAEs to capture both homogeneous and divergent features across domains. This approach is particularly useful when the source domain is not accessible \[[9' 2018-12-15](http://arxiv.org/abs/1812.06229v1)\]. Additionally, \[[10' 2025-04-29](http://arxiv.org/abs/2504.20371v3)\] explores the use of domain-specific adapters and hyper-networks to dynamically generate parameters for multi-domain models, improving flexibility and performance \[[11' 2023-11-03](http://arxiv.org/abs/2311.01831v1)\].

Another line of research focuses on the selection of relevant source domains to avoid negative transfer. For example, \[[12' 2023-06-30](http://arxiv.org/abs/2306.17555v2)\] proposes a data-selective transfer learning approach that efficiently selects speech data based on relevance to the target domain, thereby reducing negative transfer \[[13' 2015-09-08](http://arxiv.org/abs/1509.02409v1)\]. This idea is closely related to the proposed Similar Domain Selection Principle (SDSP) in this work, which dynamically identifies similar domains based on domain metrics and unsupervised distance measures \[[14' 2024-02-09](http://arxiv.org/abs/2402.06213v2)\].

Moreover, \[[15' 2023-03-02](http://arxiv.org/abs/2303.01003v1)\] introduces a method for multi-source-free domain adaptation that leverages uncertainty-aware adaptive distillation to improve model performance without access to source data \[[16' 2021-02-24](http://arxiv.org/abs/2102.12225v3)\]. This aligns with the lightweight and flexible nature of SDSP, which can be integrated into existing MDR methods without introducing significant overhead \[[17' 2025-02-12](http://arxiv.org/abs/2502.08139v1)\].

In summary, while many approaches have been proposed to address the challenges of multi-domain recommendation, the problem of negative transfer remains a critical issue. The proposed SDSP offers a novel solution by explicitly measuring domain-level gaps and dynamically selecting appropriate domains, thereby improving the effectiveness of knowledge transfer \[[18' 2024-04-26](http://arxiv.org/abs/2404.17450v2)\].
