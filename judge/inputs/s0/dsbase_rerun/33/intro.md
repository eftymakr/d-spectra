## Related Works

Rehearsal-based methods have been widely studied in continual learning (CL) to mitigate catastrophic forgetting. These methods store a subset of past data and train the model on this data alongside new data during the current task \[[1' 2021-04-15](http://arxiv.org/abs/2104.07446v1)\]. While concurrent rehearsal, where past and new data are trained together, is commonly used, its optimality remains unclear \[[2' 2024-05-27](http://arxiv.org/abs/2405.17583v1)\]. Recent studies have explored alternative strategies, such as sequential rehearsal, where new data is trained first, followed by revisiting past data sequentially \[[3' 2019-04-15](http://arxiv.org/abs/1904.07734v1)\]. This approach is inspired by human learning, where revisiting tasks helps mitigate forgetting \[[4' 2025-02-11](http://arxiv.org/abs/2502.10442v1)\]. 

Theoretical analyses of rehearsal-based CL have primarily focused on overparameterized linear models \[[5' 2019-04-24](http://arxiv.org/abs/1904.10644v1)\]. In these models, concurrent rehearsal involves training past and new data together, while sequential rehearsal involves training new data first and then revisiting past data \[[6' 2025-03-24](http://arxiv.org/abs/2503.18511v1)\]. A key finding from these analyses is that sequential rehearsal performs better when tasks are less similar \[[7' 2023-04-03](http://arxiv.org/abs/2304.01239v1)\]. This suggests that the effectiveness of rehearsal strategies may depend on the similarity between tasks \[[8' 2021-10-21](http://arxiv.org/abs/2110.11526v3)\]. 

Several studies have investigated the impact of rehearsal on generalization and forgetting in neural networks. For example, \[[9' 2022-02-20](http://arxiv.org/abs/2202.09826v3)\] showed that models trained sequentially with rehearsal tend to stay in the same low-loss region after a task has finished, but are at risk of overfitting on its sample memory, hence harming generalization. This aligns with the idea that sequential rehearsal may be more effective in reducing overfitting to past data \[[10' 2020-06-19](http://arxiv.org/abs/2006.10974v3)\]. 

In addition to concurrent and sequential rehearsal, hybrid approaches have been proposed to combine the strengths of both strategies. For instance, \[[11' 2021-02-15](http://arxiv.org/abs/2102.07686v4)\] introduced a hybrid rehearsal method that trains similar tasks concurrently and revisits dissimilar tasks sequentially. This approach aims to balance the benefits of concurrent and sequential rehearsal \[[12' 2017-08-07](http://arxiv.org/abs/1708.02072v4)\]. 

The effectiveness of rehearsal strategies has also been studied in the context of deep neural networks. For example, \[[13' 2020-11-24](http://arxiv.org/abs/2011.12216v3)\] demonstrated that rehearsal can be combined with other continual learning methods to improve performance. Similarly, \[[14' 2023-12-01](http://arxiv.org/abs/2312.00276v3)\] showed that rehearsal can be effective in reducing catastrophic forgetting in online learning scenarios. 

Moreover, the role of overparameterization in mitigating forgetting has been explored. \[[15' 2023-12-28](http://arxiv.org/abs/2312.17210v1)\] showed that overparameterization alone can help reduce forgetting in linear regression models. This finding suggests that the capacity of the model may play a crucial role in the effectiveness of rehearsal strategies \[[16' 2019-06-16](http://arxiv.org/abs/1906.06635v1)\].
