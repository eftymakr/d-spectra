## Related Works

Rehearsal-based methods have been widely studied in continual learning (CL) to mitigate catastrophic forgetting. These methods store a subset of past data and train the model on this data alongside new data during the current task \[[1' 2021-04-15](http://arxiv.org/abs/2104.07446v1)\]. While concurrent rehearsal, where past and new data are trained together, is commonly used, its optimality remains unclear \[[2' 2024-05-27](http://arxiv.org/abs/2405.17583v1)\]. Recent studies have explored alternative strategies, such as sequential rehearsal, where new data is trained first, followed by revisiting past data sequentially \[[3' 2019-04-15](http://arxiv.org/abs/1904.07734v1)\]. This approach is inspired by human learning, where revisiting tasks helps mitigate forgetting \[[4' 2025-02-11](http://arxiv.org/abs/2502.10442v1)\]. 

Theoretical analyses of rehearsal-based CL have primarily focused on overparameterized linear models \[[5' 2019-04-24](http://arxiv.org/abs/1904.10644v1)\]. In these models, concurrent rehearsal involves training past and new data together, while sequential rehearsal involves training new data first and then revisiting past data \[[6' 2025-03-24](http://arxiv.org/abs/2503.18511v1)\]. A key finding from these analyses is that sequential rehearsal performs better when tasks are less similar \[[7' 2023-04-03](http://arxiv.org/abs/2304.01239v1)\]. This suggests that the effectiveness of rehearsal strategies may depend on the similarity between tasks \[[8' 2021-10-21](http://arxiv.org/abs/2110.11526v3)\]. 

Several studies have investigated the impact of rehearsal on generalization and forgetting in neural networks. For example, \[[9' 2022-02-20](http://arxiv.org/abs/2202.09826v3)\] showed that models trained sequentially with rehearsal tend to stay in the same low-loss region after a task has finished, but are at risk of overfitting on its sample memory, hence harming generalization. This aligns with the idea that sequential rehearsal may be more effective in reducing overfitting to past data \[[10' 2020-06-19](http://arxiv.org/abs/2006.10974v3)\]. 

In addition to concurrent and sequential rehearsal, hybrid approaches have been proposed to combine the strengths of both strategies. For instance, \[[11' 2021-02-15](http://arxiv.org/abs/2102.07686v4)\] introduced a hybrid rehearsal method that trains similar tasks concurrently and revisits dissimilar tasks sequentially. This approach aims to balance the benefits of concurrent and sequential rehearsal \[[12' 2017-08-07](http://arxiv.org/abs/1708.02072v4)\]. 

The effectiveness of rehearsal strategies has also been studied in the context of deep neural networks. For example, \[[13' 2020-11-24](http://arxiv.org/abs/2011.12216v3)\] demonstrated that rehearsal can be combined with other continual learning methods to improve performance. Similarly, \[[14' 2023-12-01](http://arxiv.org/abs/2312.00276v3)\] showed that rehearsal can be effective in reducing catastrophic forgetting in online learning scenarios. 

Moreover, the role of overparameterization in mitigating forgetting has been explored. \[[15' 2023-12-28](http://arxiv.org/abs/2312.17210v1)\] showed that overparameterization alone can help reduce forgetting in linear regression models. This finding suggests that the capacity of the model may play a crucial role in the effectiveness of rehearsal strategies \[[16' 2019-06-16](http://arxiv.org/abs/1906.06635v1)\].

The category "Generalization and Forgetting in Rehearsal" encompasses a range of research focused on mitigating catastrophic forgetting in continual learning (CL) through rehearsal strategies. Rehearsal-based methods, which store and train on a subset of past data alongside new data, have shown promise in improving CL performance \[[1' 2021-04-15](http://arxiv.org/abs/2104.07446v1)\]. However, the optimal approach remains unclear, with debates over whether concurrent rehearsal—training past and new data together—is always superior to sequential rehearsal, where new data is trained first and past data is revisited later \[[1' 2021-04-15](http://arxiv.org/abs/2104.07446v1)\]. Theoretical analyses suggest that sequential rehearsal can offer greater benefits when tasks are less similar, as it helps reduce forgetting and improve generalization \[[1' 2021-04-15](http://arxiv.org/abs/2104.07446v1)\]. This insight has led to the development of hybrid rehearsal methods, which combine concurrent and sequential strategies based on task similarity \[[1' 2021-04-15](http://arxiv.org/abs/2104.07446v1)\]. Additionally, research has explored alternative approaches to rehearsal, such as using Bayesian models to retain knowledge through posterior distributions and coresets \[[3' 2019-04-15](http://arxiv.org/abs/1904.07734v1)\], or leveraging energy-based models (EBMs) to minimize interference with previously learned information \[[7' 2023-04-03](http://arxiv.org/abs/2304.01239v1)\]. Furthermore, the impact of task sequence and algorithmic parameters on forgetting has been analyzed in linear regression models and deep neural networks, revealing that task order and step size significantly influence performance \[[2' 2024-05-27](http://arxiv.org/abs/2405.17583v1)\]. These findings highlight the complexity of generalization and forgetting in rehearsal-based CL, and the need for more nuanced strategies that balance memory efficiency with model performance \[[10' 2020-06-19](http://arxiv.org/abs/2006.10974v3)\].

### <a id='category-experiments-on-deep-neural-networks'></a>Experiments on Deep Neural Networks: 8 papers found.
The category "Experiments on Deep Neural Networks" explores various challenges and solutions related to continual learning (CL), particularly focusing on catastrophic forgetting and methods to mitigate it. Research indicates that standard neural networks suffer from catastrophic forgetting, making it difficult to learn new tasks without losing previously acquired knowledge \[[3' 2019-04-15](http://arxiv.org/abs/1904.07734v1)\]. This issue has led to the development of multiple approaches, such as regularization-based methods, replaying past experiences, and ensembling techniques \[[3' 2019-04-15](http://arxiv.org/abs/1904.07734v1)\]. However, these methods have not been consistently compared due to varying evaluation protocols \[[12' 2017-08-07](http://arxiv.org/abs/1708.02072v4)\]. Recent studies have emphasized the importance of structured comparisons across different CL scenarios, such as class incremental learning, where task identity must be inferred \[[3' 2019-04-15](http://arxiv.org/abs/1904.07734v1)\]. Additionally, the choice of optimization algorithms significantly impacts the degree of catastrophic forgetting, with classical methods like vanilla SGD showing less forgetting than modern algorithms like Adam \[[11' 2021-02-15](http://arxiv.org/abs/2102.07686v4)\]. To better understand and quantify catastrophic forgetting, new metrics and benchmarks have been introduced, highlighting the need for more rigorous experimental methodologies \[[12' 2017-08-07](http://arxiv.org/abs/1708.02072v4)\]. Furthermore, sequential Bayesian inference has been proposed as a framework for continual learning, allowing for more flexible parameter adaptation and reducing reliance on maintaining past data \[[15' 2023-12-28](http://arxiv.org/abs/2312.17210v1)\]. In the context of rehearsal-based methods, recent work has explored sequential rehearsal strategies, showing that they can outperform standard concurrent rehearsal, especially when tasks are less similar \[[17' 2023-08-25](http://arxiv.org/abs/2308.13646v2)\]. These findings have led to the development of hybrid rehearsal approaches that combine concurrent and sequential strategies, demonstrating improved performance in deep neural networks \[[17' 2023-08-25](http://arxiv.org/abs/2308.13646v2)\]. Overall, the field continues to evolve with a focus on both theoretical analysis and practical implementation of continual learning techniques \[[1' 2021-04-15](http://arxiv.org/abs/2104.07446v1)\].

The category "Overparameterization and Model Capacity" explores how overparameterization influences catastrophic forgetting in continual learning. Research indicates that overparameterized models can mitigate forgetting by maintaining a low-risk estimator for previously learned tasks, even when trained on new tasks sequentially \[[4' 2025-02-11](http://arxiv.org/abs/2502.10442v1)\]. This effect is further supported by studies showing that increasing the width of neural networks significantly reduces catastrophic forgetting, suggesting that model architecture plays a crucial role in learning dynamics \[[8' 2021-10-21](http://arxiv.org/abs/2110.11526v3)\]. Additionally, the impact of overparameterization extends to the design of continual learning algorithms, such as the Self-Controlled Dynamic Expansion Model (SCDEM), which leverages multiple pre-trained ViT backbones to enhance adaptability across diverse tasks while minimizing parameter inefficiency \[[29' 2025-04-14](http://arxiv.org/abs/2504.10561v2)\]. These findings highlight the importance of model capacity in balancing the trade-off between learning new tasks and preserving knowledge from past tasks.
