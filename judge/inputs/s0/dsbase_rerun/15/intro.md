## Related Works

Brain tumor segmentation in medical imaging has been extensively studied, with a growing body of research focusing on improving segmentation accuracy, reducing computational costs, and enhancing model efficiency. Early approaches relied on manual delineation, which was time-consuming and prone to human error \[[1' 2024-01-02](http://arxiv.org/abs/2401.01303v1)\]. With the advent of deep learning, U-Net and its variants have become the de facto standard for semantic segmentation of medical images, achieving significant improvements in accuracy \[[3' 2023-05-10](http://arxiv.org/abs/2305.06203v1)\]. However, these models often suffer from over-segmentation and struggle with precise edge detection, which is critical for accurate diagnosis and treatment planning \[[1' 2024-01-02](http://arxiv.org/abs/2401.01303v1)\]. 

To address these limitations, several studies have explored the integration of edge information into segmentation models. For instance, one work proposed extracting edges from ground truth using derivative-like filters and edge reconstruction, then incorporating these edge maps as additional targets in the training process \[[1' 2024-01-02](http://arxiv.org/abs/2401.01303v1)\]. This approach led to improved performance in core tumor regions, with models trained on edge information achieving results comparable to state-of-the-art models like Swin U-Net and hybrid MR-U-Net \[[1' 2024-01-02](http://arxiv.org/abs/2401.01303v1)\]. Additionally, the use of attention mechanisms has been shown to enhance segmentation accuracy by emphasizing malignant tissues and suppressing healthy ones \[[3' 2023-05-10](http://arxiv.org/abs/2305.06203v1)\]. 

Recent work has also focused on improving the efficiency of U-Net-based models. For example, the MBDRes-U-Net model integrates multi-branch residual blocks and fused attention mechanisms to reduce computational overhead while maintaining high segmentation accuracy \[[7' 2024-11-04](http://arxiv.org/abs/2411.01896v1)\]. Similarly, the UKAN-EP model introduces efficient channel attention and pyramid feature aggregation modules, achieving superior performance with significantly fewer computational resources \[[5' 2024-08-01](http://arxiv.org/abs/2408.00273v3)\]. These efforts highlight the importance of lightweight architectures and efficient feature extraction in medical image segmentation. 

Transfer learning has also been explored as a means to improve segmentation performance, particularly in low-resource settings. For instance, a study demonstrated that fine-tuning pre-trained models on specific medical tasks can improve average DICE performance by up to 6.68% \[[2' 2023-04-28](http://arxiv.org/abs/2304.14660v7)\]. Moreover, the use of complementary 2D diffusion models has been proposed to reduce the reliance on extensive labeled data, enabling label-efficient 3D brain segmentation \[[20' 2024-07-17](http://arxiv.org/abs/2407.12329v1)\]. These approaches demonstrate the potential of transfer learning in overcoming the challenges of data scarcity in medical imaging. 

In addition to model architecture improvements, the integration of contextual feedback loops has been shown to enhance segmentation robustness. For example, a method that employs a context feedback system to refine predictions iteratively has been demonstrated to produce more anatomically plausible and robust segmentation results \[[28' 2021-03-04](http://arxiv.org/abs/2103.02844v1)\].
