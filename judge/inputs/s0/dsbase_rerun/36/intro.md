## Related Works

Deploying Large Language Models (LLMs) on edge devices presents significant challenges due to the resource constraints of these platforms, including limited storage, memory, and power \[[1' 2024-09-05](http://arxiv.org/abs/2409.03384v1)\]. Prior work has explored various strategies to optimize LLM deployment, such as hardware acceleration, model compression, and algorithmic improvements. For instance, hardware accelerators like FPGAs have been proposed to enhance the performance and energy efficiency of LLM inference \[[3' 2021-10-01](http://arxiv.org/abs/2110.00478v1)\]. These accelerators leverage the reconfigurability of FPGAs to tailor computations to specific model architectures, offering advantages over general-purpose processors \[[7' 2024-08-01](http://arxiv.org/abs/2408.00462v1)\]. Additionally, frameworks like SECDA have been introduced to streamline the design and integration of FPGA-based accelerators for edge inference, reducing development overhead and improving performance \[[7' 2024-08-01](http://arxiv.org/abs/2408.00462v1)\].

Model compression techniques, such as quantization and sparsity, have also been widely studied to reduce the computational and memory demands of LLMs \[[9' 2024-12-13](http://arxiv.org/abs/2412.12178v2)\]. Mixed-precision quantization, for example, allows for the efficient use of hardware that supports different bitwidths, improving both performance and energy efficiency \[[4' 2022-05-30](http://arxiv.org/abs/2205.15437v2)\]. Activation sparsity has further been explored as a method to compress LLMs by reducing the number of non-zero activations, leading to significant memory and computational savings with minimal accuracy loss \[[9' 2024-12-13](http://arxiv.org/abs/2412.12178v2)\]. These approaches are crucial for enabling LLMs to run on resource-constrained edge devices \[[1' 2024-09-05](http://arxiv.org/abs/2409.03384v1)\].

Moreover, algorithmic optimizations such as pruning and attention mechanisms have been proposed to enhance the efficiency of LLMs \[[6' 2025-04-07](http://arxiv.org/abs/2505.03745v1)\]. For instance, Λ-shaped attention has been introduced to reduce memory and bandwidth requirements while enabling long-sequence generation \[[6' 2025-04-07](http://arxiv.org/abs/2505.03745v1)\]. These algorithmic improvements are often complemented by hardware co-design strategies that align with the specific characteristics of the target hardware \[[6' 2025-04-07](http://arxiv.org/abs/2505.03745v1)\]. The integration of these algorithmic and hardware-level optimizations is essential for achieving the desired performance and efficiency on edge devices \[[6' 2025-04-07](http://arxiv.org/abs/2505.03745v1)\].

Recent work has also focused on the deployment of LLMs on edge devices using specialized hardware accelerators, such as those based on resistive memory and in-memory computing \[[18' 2021-09-08](http://arxiv.org/abs/2109.03934v1)\]. These accelerators leverage non-volatile memory devices to perform computations directly within the memory, reducing data movement and improving energy efficiency \[[18' 2021-09-08](http://arxiv.org/abs/2109.03934v1)\]. Additionally, hybrid architectures that combine different hardware components, such as GPUs and FPGAs, have been explored to balance performance and power consumption \[[16' 2022-06-10](http://arxiv.org/abs/2206.05128v1)\].
