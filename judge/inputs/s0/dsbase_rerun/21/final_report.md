## Related Works

Video frame interpolation (VFI) has been extensively studied for its ability to synthesize intermediate frames in video sequences, which is crucial for applications such as video compression, slow-motion generation, and 3D reconstruction. Early methods relied on optical flow estimation and warping \[[8' 2019-04-01](http://arxiv.org/abs/1904.00830v1)\], but recent advances have introduced deep learning-based approaches that leverage neural networks to model motion and appearance \[[13' 2017-08-05](http://arxiv.org/abs/1708.01692v1)\]. For example, the Adaptive Separable Convolution method \[[13' 2017-08-05](http://arxiv.org/abs/1708.01692v1)\] merges motion estimation and pixel synthesis into a single convolutional process, improving efficiency and quality by using spatially adaptive kernels. Similarly, the Flow Transformer \[[11' 2023-07-30](http://arxiv.org/abs/2307.16144v1)\] incorporates motion dynamics from optical flows into the self-attention mechanism, enabling better handling of large motion scenarios. These methods have demonstrated significant improvements in visual quality and robustness to motion blur and occlusion.

Neural rendering methods have also gained attention for their ability to reconstruct 3D scenes from 2D images, often using implicit neural representations (INRs) to model geometry and appearance \[[1' 2022-06-22](http://arxiv.org/abs/2206.10886v1)\]. INRs have been used in video frame interpolation by explicitly constraining the derivatives of the INR to satisfy the optical flow constraint equation, leading to state-of-the-art results on limited motion ranges \[[1' 2022-06-22](http://arxiv.org/abs/2206.10886v1)\]. Additionally, the use of neural implicit representations for 3D scene reconstruction has been explored, with methods like the feature grid interpolation mechanism \[[3' 2025-03-24](http://arxiv.org/abs/2503.18897v1)\] allowing for continuous updates of object-centric neural implicit representations as new object parts are revealed. These approaches have shown promise in improving reconstruction accuracy and completeness, especially in dynamic and complex environments.

The challenge of handling dynamic scenes with limited input views has also been addressed. For instance, the work on 3D scene reconstruction using neural object priors \[[3' 2025-03-24](http://arxiv.org/abs/2503.18897v1)\] introduces a feature grid interpolation mechanism and leverages shape priors to initialize geometric models, enabling efficient and accurate reconstruction. Similarly, the Depth-Aware Video Frame Interpolation \[[8' 2019-04-01](http://arxiv.org/abs/1904.00830v1)\] explicitly detects occlusion by exploring depth information, leading to improved interpolation quality. These methods highlight the importance of incorporating spatial and temporal information to enhance the robustness of VFI in dynamic scenarios.

Video frame interpolation techniques have seen significant advancements, with recent works focusing on improving interpolation quality, handling complex motion, and reducing dependency on training data. Several methods leverage neural representations and optical flow constraints to achieve high-quality frame synthesis. Recent approaches has started tackling this issue, however, they typically either only discard dynamic information, represent it using bounding boxes or per-frame depth or rely on approaches that are inherently slow and not suitable to online settings.
  We propose an end-to-end system for live reconstruction of large-scale outdoor dynamic environments. We leverage recent advances in computationally efficient data-driven approaches for 6-DoF object pose estimation to segment the scene into objects and stationary `background'. For each time step, our dynamic map maintains a relative pose of each volume with respect to the stationary background.
