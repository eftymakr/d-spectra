## Related Works

Fine-grained emotion classification in app reviews has received increasing attention in recent years due to its critical role in understanding user sentiment and improving app development practices. While traditional sentiment analysis typically focuses on coarse-grained labels such as positive, negative, and neutral \[[16' 2020-06-17](http://arxiv.org/abs/2006.09739v1)\], fine-grained emotion classification aims to distinguish between more nuanced emotional states, such as joy, anger, fear, and surprise \[[6' 2021-08-17](http://arxiv.org/abs/2108.07638v2)\]. This distinction is particularly important in app reviews, where users often express complex emotional experiences that can inform product improvement and user experience design \[[15' 2024-01-19](http://arxiv.org/abs/2401.11063v2)\].

Several studies have explored the use of large language models (LLMs) for sentiment analysis and emotion detection in app reviews. For instance, \[[16' 2020-06-17](http://arxiv.org/abs/2006.09739v1)\] applied machine learning algorithms using TF-IDF text representation and ensemble learning to classify app reviews, achieving high accuracy with SVM on tri-gram + TF-IDF schemes. Similarly, \[[12' 2022-10-12](http://arxiv.org/abs/2210.06235v1)\] proposed a framework called SOLAR to automatically summarize helpful app reviews by predicting review helpfulness, performing topic-sentiment modeling, and ranking reviews based on multiple factors. These approaches demonstrate the potential of automated methods in handling the large volume of app reviews \[[12' 2022-10-12](http://arxiv.org/abs/2210.06235v1)\].

However, the accuracy of LLMs in fine-grained emotion classification remains a challenge. \[[1' 2024-06-04](http://arxiv.org/abs/2406.02642v4)\] highlights that in-context learning (ICL) struggles with fine-grained emotion recognition due to its reliance on semantically similar but emotionally inaccurate prototypes. ### <a id='category-fine-grained-emotion-classification-frameworks'></a>Fine-Grained Emotion Classification Frameworks: 7 papers found.
Fine-grained emotion classification has been a focal point in recent NLP research, with multiple approaches addressing the challenges of accurate and robust emotion detection. Several studies have explored the limitations of in-context learning (ICL) in emotion recognition, identifying issues such as reliance on semantically similar but emotionally inaccurate prototypes and interference from irrelevant categories \[[1' 2024-06-04](http://arxiv.org/abs/2406.02642v4)\]. To mitigate these, E-ICL was proposed, incorporating emotionally accurate prototypes and an exclusionary prediction strategy, achieving superior performance on datasets like EDOS and GoEmotions \[[1' 2024-06-04](http://arxiv.org/abs/2406.02642v4)\]. In Japanese sentiment analysis, fine-tuned DeBERTa-v3-large models have demonstrated high accuracy and F1-scores, outperforming other pre-trained models and large language models like ChatGPT-4o \[[3' 2025-04-22](http://arxiv.org/abs/2505.00013v1)\]. Additionally, frameworks such as SentiXRL integrate emotion retrieval enhancement and self-circulating analysis mechanisms to improve classification accuracy across multiple datasets, including CPED and MELD \[[4' 2024-11-27](http://arxiv.org/abs/2411.18162v1)\]. Lexical-based weakly supervised approaches have also been explored, particularly in low-resource settings, where BERT-based models achieved F1-scores of 0.64 for fine-grained emotion classification in Portuguese \[[5' 2013-09-11](http://arxiv.org/abs/1309.2853v1)\]. SensoryT5, which integrates sensory cues into the T5 model, has shown improved performance in fine-grained emotion classification by enhancing contextual and sensory understanding \[[8' 2024-03-22](http://arxiv.org/abs/2403.15574v1)\]. These studies collectively highlight the ongoing efforts to refine emotion classification through advanced models, improved training strategies, and domain-specific adaptations.
