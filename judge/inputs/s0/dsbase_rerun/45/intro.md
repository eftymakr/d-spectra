## Related Works

Fine-grained emotion classification in app reviews has received increasing attention in recent years due to its critical role in understanding user sentiment and improving app development practices. While traditional sentiment analysis typically focuses on coarse-grained labels such as positive, negative, and neutral \[[16' 2020-06-17](http://arxiv.org/abs/2006.09739v1)\], fine-grained emotion classification aims to distinguish between more nuanced emotional states, such as joy, anger, fear, and surprise \[[6' 2021-08-17](http://arxiv.org/abs/2108.07638v2)\]. This distinction is particularly important in app reviews, where users often express complex emotional experiences that can inform product improvement and user experience design \[[15' 2024-01-19](http://arxiv.org/abs/2401.11063v2)\].

Several studies have explored the use of large language models (LLMs) for sentiment analysis and emotion detection in app reviews. For instance, \[[16' 2020-06-17](http://arxiv.org/abs/2006.09739v1)\] applied machine learning algorithms using TF-IDF text representation and ensemble learning to classify app reviews, achieving high accuracy with SVM on tri-gram + TF-IDF schemes. Similarly, \[[12' 2022-10-12](http://arxiv.org/abs/2210.06235v1)\] proposed a framework called SOLAR to automatically summarize helpful app reviews by predicting review helpfulness, performing topic-sentiment modeling, and ranking reviews based on multiple factors. These approaches demonstrate the potential of automated methods in handling the large volume of app reviews \[[12' 2022-10-12](http://arxiv.org/abs/2210.06235v1)\].

However, the accuracy of LLMs in fine-grained emotion classification remains a challenge. \[[1' 2024-06-04](http://arxiv.org/abs/2406.02642v4)\] highlights that in-context learning (ICL) struggles with fine-grained emotion recognition due to its reliance on semantically similar but emotionally inaccurate prototypes. To address this, the authors proposed E-ICL, which incorporates emotionally accurate prototypes and an exclusionary prediction strategy to improve accuracy and robustness. This work aligns with the findings of \[[12' 2022-10-12](http://arxiv.org/abs/2210.06235v1)\], which evaluated the effectiveness of automated summarization in capturing user sentiment \[[12' 2022-10-12](http://arxiv.org/abs/2210.06235v1)\].

Moreover, \[[15' 2024-01-19](http://arxiv.org/abs/2401.11063v2)\] analyzed ethical concerns in app reviews, finding that users frequently report issues such as censorship, identity theft, and safety. These findings underscore the importance of accurate emotion detection in identifying and addressing ethical concerns in app development \[[15' 2024-01-19](http://arxiv.org/abs/2401.11063v2)\].

In addition, \[[17' 2019-04-11](http://arxiv.org/abs/1904.12607v1)\] studied fake reviews in app stores, developing a classifier to detect fake reviews with high recall and AUC/ROC scores. This work is relevant to the broader challenge of ensuring the reliability of user feedback, which is essential for accurate emotion classification \[[17' 2019-04-11](http://arxiv.org/abs/1904.12607v1)\].
