Mixture of Experts (MoE) architectures have gained significant attention for their ability to improve the efficiency and scalability of machine learning models by distributing complex tasks among specialized sub-models termed experts \[[1' 2024-08-20](http://arxiv.org/abs/2408.10681v1)\]. A key component of MoE is the adaptive softmax gating mechanism, which dynamically assigns weights to experts based on input relevance \[[4' 2025-03-05](http://arxiv.org/abs/2503.03213v1)\]. Recent studies have explored variations of this gating mechanism, including dense-to-sparse and hierarchical softmax approaches, aiming to enhance parameter estimation and expert identification \[[4' 2025-03-05](http://arxiv.org/abs/2503.03213v1)\]. Additionally, heterogeneous MoE models have been proposed, where experts differ in size and capacity, allowing for more specialized handling of varying input complexities \[[1' 2024-08-20](http://arxiv.org/abs/2408.10681v1)\]. This heterogeneity improves computational efficiency and parameter utilization by encouraging the frequent activation of smaller, more efficient experts \[[1' 2024-08-20](http://arxiv.org/abs/2408.10681v1)\]. Furthermore, MoE has been applied to graph data, where it helps model natural distributional shifts by employing a gating model and multiple expert models tailored to specific shifts \[[4' 2025-03-05](http://arxiv.org/abs/2503.03213v1)\]. These developments highlight the versatility and effectiveness of MoE in capturing complex relationships and improving model performance across diverse applications.
