## Related Works

Vision Transformers (ViTs) have emerged as a powerful alternative to convolutional neural networks (CNNs) in computer vision tasks due to their ability to capture global context through self-attention mechanisms \[[9' 2021-06-04](http://arxiv.org/abs/2106.02277v1)\]. However, the computational and memory demands of ViTs, particularly due to complex operations like Softmax and LayerNorm, have posed significant challenges for hardware acceleration \[[1' 2024-05-07](http://arxiv.org/abs/2405.04134v1)\]. These operations are sensitive to precision and are often executed on the CPU, leading to communication overhead and suboptimal performance \[[1' 2024-05-07](http://arxiv.org/abs/2405.04134v1)\]. 

Several works have focused on improving the efficiency of ViTs through algorithm-hardware co-design. ViTALiTy \[[2' 2022-11-09](http://arxiv.org/abs/2211.05109v1)\] introduces a framework that unifies low-rank and sparse approximations for attention mechanisms, aiming to reduce the quadratic cost of attention matrices. This approach leverages a linear Taylor attention with row-mean centering to approximate the dot-product softmax operation, improving both efficiency and accuracy. Similarly, HeatViT \[[12' 2022-11-15](http://arxiv.org/abs/2211.08110v2)\] proposes an adaptive token pruning framework that dynamically identifies and removes non-informative tokens, reducing computational load while maintaining accuracy. This method is implemented on FPGAs, demonstrating significant speedups and accuracy improvements over existing pruning techniques.

SoftmAP \[[3' 2024-11-26](http://arxiv.org/abs/2411.17847v1)\] presents a software-hardware co-design approach for implementing integer-only Softmax using In-Memory Compute (IMC) hardware, achieving substantial improvements in energy-delay product compared to high-end GPUs. This aligns with the goal of reducing the computational overhead of non-linear operations in ViTs. Softermax \[[7' 2021-03-16](http://arxiv.org/abs/2103.09301v1)\] further enhances the efficiency of Softmax by introducing a hardware-friendly design that includes base replacement, low-precision computation, and online normalization, achieving 2.35x energy efficiency with minimal accuracy loss.

Hardware accelerators for ViTs have also been explored. ViTA \[[10' 2023-02-17](http://arxiv.org/abs/2302.09108v1)\] is a configurable hardware accelerator targeting edge devices, employing a head-level pipeline and inter-layer MLP optimizations to reduce off-chip memory accesses. This design achieves high hardware utilization and low power consumption, making it suitable for resource-constrained environments. FLASH-D \[[6' 2025-05-20](http://arxiv.org/abs/2505.14201v1)\] re-evaluates the FlashAttention kernel, presenting a simplified formulation that reduces area and power consumption while maintaining numerical stability and computational efficiency. This work is particularly relevant for hardware implementations of attention mechanisms.

### <a id='category-hardware-optimization-for-attention-mechanisms'></a>Hardware Optimization for Attention Mechanisms: 4 papers found.
Vision Transformers (ViTs) have gained significant traction in computer vision due to their ability to capture global context effectively \[[1' 2024-05-07](http://arxiv.org/abs/2405.04134v1)\]. However, their hardware acceleration remains challenging due to the computational complexity of operations like Softmax and LayerNorm, which are sensitive to model accuracy and difficult to map onto low-precision hardware \[[1' 2024-05-07](http://arxiv.org/abs/2405.04134v1)\]. Existing approaches often rely on CPU for these complex operations, leading to communication overhead and suboptimal performance \[[1' 2024-05-07](http://arxiv.org/abs/2405.04134v1)\]. To address this, recent works have explored hardware optimization strategies for attention mechanisms. For instance, ViTALiTy proposes an algorithm-hardware codesigned framework that unifies low-rank and sparse components of attention to improve inference efficiency, achieving up to 3× speedup and 3× energy efficiency \[[2' 2022-11-09](http://arxiv.org/abs/2211.05109v1)\]. Similarly, FlashAttention introduces a method that integrates softmax computation with matrix arithmetic, enabling tiled computation independent of sequence length, and its simplified formulation, FLASH-D, achieves a 22.8% reduction in area and 20.3% reduction in power \[[6' 2025-05-20](http://arxiv.org/abs/2505.14201v1)\]. Additionally, the GG-Transformer addresses the quadratic complexity of self-attention in vision tasks by introducing a dual-branch architecture that efficiently models both long-range dependencies and local context \[[9' 2021-06-04](http://arxiv.org/abs/2106.02277v1)\]. These advancements highlight the growing focus on optimizing attention mechanisms for hardware efficiency, particularly in resource-constrained environments.

To overcome these limitations, methodologies like SoftmAP and Softermax have been proposed, which leverage low-precision integer-based computations and hardware-friendly designs to achieve significant improvements in energy efficiency and performance \[[3' 2024-11-26](http://arxiv.org/abs/2411.17847v1)\]\[[7' 2021-03-16](http://arxiv.org/abs/2103.09301v1)\]. For ViTs, which are known for their high computational demands, quantization techniques and hardware acceleration have been explored to enable efficient deployment on specialized hardware \[[8' 2024-05-01](http://arxiv.org/abs/2405.00314v1)\]. These approaches emphasize the importance of algorithm-hardware co-design, where both the model structure and the underlying hardware are tailored to each other’s strengths \[[8' 2024-05-01](http://arxiv.org/abs/2405.00314v1)\]. Additionally, the use of novel data formats, such as Microscaling Integer (MXInt), has been introduced to enable efficient and accurate execution of complex arithmetic operations on FPGAs \[[3' 2024-11-26](http://arxiv.org/abs/2411.17847v1)\]. Overall, these studies highlight the growing trend of integrating software and hardware innovations to enhance the performance and scalability of deep learning models.

### <a id='category-fpga-based-acceleration-for-vits'></a>FPGA-Based Acceleration for ViTs: 4 papers found.
Vision Transformer (ViT) hardware acceleration has been a focal point of recent research, with several studies addressing the challenges of deploying these models on resource-constrained edge devices. Existing accelerators primarily focus on optimizing matrix multiplication operations while leaving complex arithmetic operations like Softmax and LayerNorm on the CPU, leading to communication overhead and suboptimal performance \[[10' 2023-02-17](http://arxiv.org/abs/2302.09108v1)\]. To overcome these limitations, researchers have proposed hardware accelerators that map all operations of ViT models onto FPGAs, enabling more efficient execution \[[12' 2022-11-15](http://arxiv.org/abs/2211.08110v2)\]. For instance, the ViTA accelerator introduces a head-level pipeline and inter-layer MLP optimizations to reduce off-chip memory accesses, achieving nearly 90% hardware utilization efficiency and a power consumption of 0.88W \[[10' 2023-02-17](http://arxiv.org/abs/2302.09108v1)\]. Similarly, the HeatViT framework introduces an image-adaptive token pruning approach, dynamically identifying and consolidating non-informative tokens to reduce computation and memory costs while maintaining model accuracy \[[12' 2022-11-15](http://arxiv.org/abs/2211.08110v2)\]. Additionally, the use of novel data formats, such as 8-bit fixed-point quantization and polynomial approximations, has been explored to improve hardware efficiency and accuracy \[[12' 2022-11-15](http://arxiv.org/abs/2211.08110v2)\]. Furthermore, the L-Mul algorithm has been proposed as an approximate multiplication method to reduce energy consumption and computational complexity in emerging neural network models, with hardware implementations targeting FPGAs \[[11' 2024-12-25](http://arxiv.org/abs/2412.18948v1)\]. These approaches collectively highlight the growing interest in developing efficient and accurate ViT accelerators that can operate within the constraints of edge computing environments.
