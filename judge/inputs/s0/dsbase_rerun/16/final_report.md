## Related Works

Open-vocabulary object detection (OVD) has gained significant attention in recent years due to its ability to detect objects from a vocabulary defined by the user at inference time, without requiring extensive training \[[1' 2016-12-02](http://arxiv.org/abs/1612.00576v2)\]. This capability allows for greater flexibility and adaptability in real-world applications, where the set of target objects may not be known in advance \[[2' 2024-05-16](http://arxiv.org/abs/2405.10053v1)\]. However, the effectiveness of OVD models is often limited by the quality and specificity of the user-defined vocabulary, which may include irrelevant or overly broad categories \[[3' 2023-12-19](http://arxiv.org/abs/2312.12437v1)\]. To address this challenge, several approaches have been proposed to refine the vocabulary and improve detection performance.

One early work, "Guided Open Vocabulary Image Captioning with Constrained Beam Search" \[[4' 2024-05-14](http://arxiv.org/abs/2405.08593v1)\], explored how to incorporate image taggers into captioning models to expand vocabulary without retraining. This approach used constrained beam search to force the inclusion of specific tag words, demonstrating that incorporating external knowledge can improve the relevance of generated captions \[[5' 2017-11-11](http://arxiv.org/abs/1711.05557v1)\]. Similarly, "Phrase-based Image Captioning with Hierarchical LSTM Model" \[[6' 2022-06-22](http://arxiv.org/abs/2206.11134v4)\] introduced a hierarchical LSTM model that generated captions by first decoding phrases and then combining them into sentences, resulting in more natural and diverse captions compared to sequential models \[[7' 2024-04-14](http://arxiv.org/abs/2404.09216v1)\].

In the context of object detection, "SHiNe: Semantic Hierarchy Nexus for Open-vocabulary Object Detection" \[[8' 2024-11-27](http://arxiv.org/abs/2411.18207v4)\] introduced a classifier that leverages semantic hierarchies to improve robustness across different vocabulary granularities. This method was shown to significantly enhance performance on detection benchmarks, achieving up to +31.9% mAP50 when using ground truth hierarchies \[[9' 2022-07-07](http://arxiv.org/abs/2207.03482v3)\]. Another work, "Open Vocabulary Object Detection via Neighboring Region Attention Alignment" \[[10' 2023-06-28](http://arxiv.org/abs/2306.15880v4)\], proposed a mechanism to align neighboring regions during the attention process, improving the performance of distillation-based OVD strategies by capturing richer contextual relationships \[[11' 2024-05-30](http://arxiv.org/abs/2405.19854v1)\].

Additionally, "Weakly Supervised Open-Vocabulary Object Detection" \[[12' 2024-06-17](http://arxiv.org/abs/2406.11739v1)\] extended weakly supervised object detection (WSOD) to detect novel concepts using only image-level annotations. The method incorporated strategies such as dataset-level feature adaptation and vision-language alignment to improve cross-dataset generalization and open-vocabulary learning \[[13' 2025-03-19](http://arxiv.org/abs/2503.14862v3)\]. These approaches highlight the importance of leveraging weak supervision and alignment techniques to enhance the performance of open-vocabulary models.

Existing methods, however, face challenges such as confidence bias toward base categories, poor performance on novel classes, and reliance on accurate prompts from an "oracle" \[[3' 2023-12-19](http://arxiv.org/abs/2312.12437v1)\]. Additionally, the V3Det Challenge 2024 highlights the need for benchmarks with large vocabularies, such as 13,204 categories, to push the boundaries of detection algorithms \[[12' 2024-06-17](http://arxiv.org/abs/2406.11739v1)\]. These advancements underscore the growing importance of open-vocabulary detection in diverse domains, from autonomous driving to Earth sciences, where models must generalize to unseen objects and environments \[[22' 2024-08-17](http://arxiv.org/abs/2408.09110v3)\].
