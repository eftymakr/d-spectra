## Related Works

Nonverbal communication plays a crucial role in human interaction, encompassing gestures, facial expressions, and body language that convey intent and emotion \[[1' 2024-07-24](http://arxiv.org/abs/2407.17209v1)\]. Existing large language models (LLMs) often lack the ability to effectively integrate these nonverbal elements, thereby limiting their capacity to create fully immersive conversational experiences \[[1' 2024-07-24](http://arxiv.org/abs/2407.17209v1)\]. This limitation has been acknowledged in prior research, which highlights the importance of nonverbal cues in social and educational contexts \[[3' 2022-07-20](http://arxiv.org/abs/2207.10574v2)\]. For instance, studies have shown that nonverbal immediacy (NVI) in educational settings, such as gesture intensity and perceived distance, can significantly influence teaching effectiveness \[[3' 2022-07-20](http://arxiv.org/abs/2207.10574v2)\]. These findings underscore the need for models that can understand and generate nonverbal cues alongside text.

Several recent works have explored the integration of nonverbal cues into AI systems. One such effort is the development of multimodal large language models (MLLMs), which leverage powerful LLMs to perform tasks such as image-based storytelling and OCR-free math reasoning \[[2' 2023-06-23](http://arxiv.org/abs/2306.13549v4)\]. These models have demonstrated emergent capabilities that traditional multimodal methods lack, suggesting a promising path toward artificial general intelligence \[[2' 2023-06-23](http://arxiv.org/abs/2306.13549v4)\]. However, current MLLMs still struggle with nonverbal social understanding, as they rely primarily on language-dominant approaches for benchmarking and training \[[4' 2025-02-23](http://arxiv.org/abs/2502.16671v3)\]. This limitation is evident in models like VideoLLMs, which achieve low accuracy (20–30%) in tasks involving nonverbal social reasoning, compared to human performance (86%) \[[4' 2025-02-23](http://arxiv.org/abs/2502.16671v3)\].

In the realm of educational technology, nonverbal communication has also been a focus of research. A study on nonverbal immediacy in classrooms demonstrated that models trained on multimodal data, such as facial expressions and gestures, could approximate human ratings with moderate to strong correlations \[[3' 2022-07-20](http://arxiv.org/abs/2207.10574v2)\]. This work highlights the potential of integrating nonverbal cues into educational AI systems. Similarly, the EmotionCLIP model was introduced to extract visual emotion representations from verbal and nonverbal communication using uncurated data, achieving performance comparable to supervised methods \[[6' 2023-05-22](http://arxiv.org/abs/2305.13500v1)\]. These approaches demonstrate the value of nonverbal cues in emotion understanding and their potential for broader applications.

### <a id='category-multimodal-large-language-models-(mllms)'></a>Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs): 3 papers found.
Recent advancements in Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs) have focused on enhancing the integration of diverse modalities, such as text, vision, and audio, to better capture the nuances of human communication \[[1' 2024-07-24](http://arxiv.org/abs/2407.17209v1)\]. These models leverage powerful pre-trained language models as a central component, enabling them to perform complex tasks like generating stories from images, conducting OCR-free mathematical reasoning, and understanding contextual relationships in videos \[[1' 2024-07-24](http://arxiv.org/abs/2407.17209v1)\]. This capability has sparked significant interest in both academia and industry, with efforts aimed at developing MLLMs that can rival or surpass existing models like GPT-4V \[[1' 2024-07-24](http://arxiv.org/abs/2407.17209v1)\]. Additionally, researchers have explored techniques such as Multimodal ICL (M-ICL), Multimodal CoT (M-CoT), and LLM-Aided Visual Reasoning (LAVR) to improve the models' ability to handle multimodal inputs and generate more accurate and context-aware outputs \[[1' 2024-07-24](http://arxiv.org/abs/2407.17209v1)\]. Furthermore, the integration of Retrieval-Augmented Generation (RAG) with MLLMs has shown promise in enhancing information retrieval and expanding the practical applications of AI, although challenges remain in accurately interpreting user intent and filtering responses \[[2' 2023-06-23](http://arxiv.org/abs/2306.13549v4)\]. Another approach involves using large language models to reason about textual descriptions of visual and auditory modalities, enabling zero-shot multimodal classification of videos without additional fine-tuning \[[3' 2022-07-20](http://arxiv.org/abs/2207.10574v2)\]. These developments highlight the growing importance of MLLMs in achieving more holistic and immersive conversational experiences, aligning with the goals of models like MARS, which aim to incorporate nonverbal cues such as gestures and facial expressions into conversational AI \[[4' 2025-02-23](http://arxiv.org/abs/2502.16671v3)\].
