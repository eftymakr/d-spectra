## Related Works

Cross-domain sequential recommendation (CDSR) has gained significant attention in recent years due to its ability to leverage user behavior across multiple domains to improve recommendation performance. Several approaches have been proposed to address the challenges of CDSR, primarily focusing on improving the modeling of cross-domain interactions and mitigating the cold-start problem. Early works such as \[[1' 2024-05-21](http://arxiv.org/abs/2405.12473v3)\] introduced the concept of cross-domain sequential recommendation, emphasizing the importance of capturing both intra- and inter-sequence item interactions. More recently, \[[2' 2018-10-10](http://arxiv.org/abs/1810.04650v2)\] proposed a multi-task learning framework that explicitly casts multi-task learning as a multi-objective optimization problem, which has inspired subsequent works in the field.

A key challenge in CDSR is the alignment of item representations across domains, which can lead to sub-optimal sequential modeling and user representation alignment \[[3' 2020-12-01](http://arxiv.org/abs/2012.00485v3)\]. To address this, \[[4' 2023-05-31](http://arxiv.org/abs/2305.19798v2)\] introduced a model-agnostic framework called CA-CDSR, which achieves sequence-aware generation and adaptively partial alignment for item representations. This work emphasized the importance of aligning item representations to enhance performance in cross-domain settings. Similarly, \[[5' 2024-12-31](http://arxiv.org/abs/2502.15694v2)\] proposed a method that incorporates item image information to better capture visual preferences, demonstrating the value of multimodal data in cross-domain sequential recommendation.

Another important direction in CDSR is the integration of attention mechanisms to model complex dependencies in sequential data. \[[6' 2023-02-07](http://arxiv.org/abs/2302.03221v1)\] introduced a novel attention mechanism called Primal-Attention, which improves the efficiency of self-attention by leveraging asymmetric Kernel Singular Value Decomposition (KSVD). This work highlights the potential of attention mechanisms in capturing high-order dependencies in sequential recommendation. Additionally, \[[7' 2024-08-15](http://arxiv.org/abs/2408.08209v2)\] proposed a lightweight external attention-enhanced graph convolution network (LEA-GCN) to address the computational complexity of existing methods, demonstrating the feasibility of lightweight models in cross-domain settings.

The role of user feedback and transition patterns has also been explored in CDSR. \[[8' 2024-10-28](http://arxiv.org/abs/2410.21048v1)\] introduced a method that models domain and feedback transitions to capture changes in user preferences, showing that incorporating feedback transitions can significantly improve recommendation performance. Furthermore, \[[9' 2022-09-21](http://arxiv.org/abs/2209.10163v1)\] proposed a method that uses a dual dynamic graph with hybrid metric training to enhance the representation of users and items in cross-domain settings, demonstrating the effectiveness of graph-based approaches in capturing complex interactions.
