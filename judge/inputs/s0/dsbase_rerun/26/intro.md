## Related Works

Differential privacy (DP) has emerged as a de facto standard for ensuring data privacy in statistical databases and machine learning systems \[[19' 2020-06-10](http://arxiv.org/abs/2006.05609v2)\]. However, its implementation in distributed settings is vulnerable to malicious behaviors by servers, such as sampling noise from incorrect distributions or generating correlated noise while appearing to follow established protocols \[[1' 2021-04-14](http://arxiv.org/abs/2104.06569v2)\]. This vulnerability has been extensively studied, with several works highlighting the risks of unfaithful execution of DP mechanisms \[[1' 2021-04-14](http://arxiv.org/abs/2104.06569v2)\]. For instance, \[[1' 2021-04-14](http://arxiv.org/abs/2104.06569v2)\] demonstrates that malicious data providers can manipulate their output before sending, leveraging the plausible deniability provided by randomization in LDP protocols. This issue has led to the development of verifiable randomization mechanisms to ensure that the data collector can verify the completeness of the randomization process \[[1' 2021-04-14](http://arxiv.org/abs/2104.06569v2)\].

Verifiable differential privacy (VDP) has been proposed as a solution to close the loophole introduced by the randomness in DP, where a malicious entity could manipulate results and use the randomness as a smokescreen \[[3' 2022-08-18](http://arxiv.org/abs/2208.09011v2)\]. The concept of VDP requires the publishing entity to output a zero-knowledge proof that convinces an efficient verifier that the output is both DP and reliable \[[3' 2022-08-18](http://arxiv.org/abs/2208.09011v2)\]. This idea has been further extended to distributed settings, where the introduction of verifiable distributed differential privacy (VDDP) aims to address the challenges of unfaithful execution by incorporating additional verification mechanisms \[[1' 2021-04-14](http://arxiv.org/abs/2104.06569v2)\]. The relationship between zero-knowledge proofs (ZKP) and DP has also been explored, showing that while ZKPs are sufficient for achieving DP under verifiability requirements, they are not necessary \[[1' 2021-04-14](http://arxiv.org/abs/2104.06569v2)\].

Several works have proposed mechanisms to achieve verifiable DP. For example, \[[3' 2022-08-18](http://arxiv.org/abs/2208.09011v2)\] introduces a method that carefully mixes private and public randomness to compute verifiable DP counting queries with theoretical guarantees. Similarly, \[[1' 2021-04-14](http://arxiv.org/abs/2104.06569v2)\] proposes a verifiable randomization mechanism that allows the data collector to verify the completeness of the randomization process for every data provider. These approaches have been further refined to improve efficiency and reduce overhead \[[1' 2021-04-14](http://arxiv.org/abs/2104.06569v2)\]. In addition, \[[20' 2025-04-24](http://arxiv.org/abs/2504.17523v1)\] introduces a novel approach to answering subset counting queries under LDP by applying randomization to indexes rather than values, achieving more accurate results with less distortion.

The integration of ZKP with DP has also been explored in the context of verifiable machine learning \[[2' 2025-02-25](http://arxiv.org/abs/2502.18535v2)\]. Zero-knowledge proofs provide a compelling foundation for verifiable machine learning because they allow one party to certify that a training, testing, or inference result was produced by the claimed computation without revealing sensitive data or proprietary model parameters \[[2' 2025-02-25](http://arxiv.org/abs/2502.18535v2)\].
