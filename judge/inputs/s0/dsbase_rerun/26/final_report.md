## Related Works

Differential privacy (DP) has emerged as a de facto standard for ensuring data privacy in statistical databases and machine learning systems \[[19' 2020-06-10](http://arxiv.org/abs/2006.05609v2)\]. However, its implementation in distributed settings is vulnerable to malicious behaviors by servers, such as sampling noise from incorrect distributions or generating correlated noise while appearing to follow established protocols \[[1' 2021-04-14](http://arxiv.org/abs/2104.06569v2)\]. This vulnerability has been extensively studied, with several works highlighting the risks of unfaithful execution of DP mechanisms \[[1' 2021-04-14](http://arxiv.org/abs/2104.06569v2)\]. For instance, \[[1' 2021-04-14](http://arxiv.org/abs/2104.06569v2)\] demonstrates that malicious data providers can manipulate their output before sending, leveraging the plausible deniability provided by randomization in LDP protocols. This issue has led to the development of verifiable randomization mechanisms to ensure that the data collector can verify the completeness of the randomization process \[[1' 2021-04-14](http://arxiv.org/abs/2104.06569v2)\].

Verifiable differential privacy (VDP) has been proposed as a solution to close the loophole introduced by the randomness in DP, where a malicious entity could manipulate results and use the randomness as a smokescreen \[[3' 2022-08-18](http://arxiv.org/abs/2208.09011v2)\]. The concept of VDP requires the publishing entity to output a zero-knowledge proof that convinces an efficient verifier that the output is both DP and reliable \[[3' 2022-08-18](http://arxiv.org/abs/2208.09011v2)\]. This idea has been further extended to distributed settings, where the introduction of verifiable distributed differential privacy (VDDP) aims to address the challenges of unfaithful execution by incorporating additional verification mechanisms \[[1' 2021-04-14](http://arxiv.org/abs/2104.06569v2)\]. The relationship between zero-knowledge proofs (ZKP) and DP has also been explored, showing that while ZKPs are sufficient for achieving DP under verifiability requirements, they are not necessary \[[1' 2021-04-14](http://arxiv.org/abs/2104.06569v2)\].

Several works have proposed mechanisms to achieve verifiable DP. For example, \[[3' 2022-08-18](http://arxiv.org/abs/2208.09011v2)\] introduces a method that carefully mixes private and public randomness to compute verifiable DP counting queries with theoretical guarantees. Similarly, \[[1' 2021-04-14](http://arxiv.org/abs/2104.06569v2)\] proposes a verifiable randomization mechanism that allows the data collector to verify the completeness of the randomization process for every data provider. These approaches have been further refined to improve efficiency and reduce overhead \[[1' 2021-04-14](http://arxiv.org/abs/2104.06569v2)\]. In addition, \[[20' 2025-04-24](http://arxiv.org/abs/2504.17523v1)\] introduces a novel approach to answering subset counting queries under LDP by applying randomization to indexes rather than values, achieving more accurate results with less distortion.

The integration of ZKP with DP has also been explored in the context of verifiable machine learning \[[2' 2025-02-25](http://arxiv.org/abs/2502.18535v2)\]. Zero-knowledge proofs provide a compelling foundation for verifiable machine learning because they allow one party to certify that a training, testing, or inference result was produced by the claimed computation without revealing sensitive data or proprietary model parameters \[[2' 2025-02-25](http://arxiv.org/abs/2502.18535v2)\]. ### <a id='category-local-differential-privacy-(ldp)-solutions'></a>Local Differential Privacy (LDP) Solutions: 7 papers found.
Local Differential Privacy (LDP) has been extensively studied for its strong privacy guarantees, particularly in scenarios where data is collected from multiple sources without a trusted third party \[[21' 2024-08-16](http://arxiv.org/abs/2408.08475v2)\]. However, LDP is vulnerable to malicious data providers who can manipulate their outputs to skew estimations, as the data collector relies on randomized data to infer background distributions \[[1' 2021-04-14](http://arxiv.org/abs/2104.06569v2)\]. To address this, researchers have proposed verifiable randomization mechanisms that allow the data collector to verify the correctness of the randomization process, thereby preventing output manipulation and mitigating potential attacks \[[1' 2021-04-14](http://arxiv.org/abs/2104.06569v2)\]. Additionally, integrating LDP with federated learning has been explored to enhance privacy-preserving machine learning, leveraging momentum gradient descent to improve model performance while maintaining privacy \[[5' 2022-09-28](http://arxiv.org/abs/2209.14086v2)\]. Theoretical advancements in LDP include the development of privacy mechanisms like the edge-flip mechanism, which enables differentially private community detection with convergence rates comparable to non-private methods \[[10' 2021-05-26](http://arxiv.org/abs/2105.12615v3)\]. Furthermore, novel approaches such as the sign mechanism have been introduced to optimize the estimation of parameters under LDP, particularly in high-privacy regimes, by maximizing Fisher information while ensuring rigorous privacy guarantees \[[11' 2024-02-07](http://arxiv.org/abs/2402.04840v3)\]. These developments highlight ongoing efforts to enhance the utility and robustness of LDP mechanisms against adversarial behaviors and to align them with practical deployment scenarios \[[6' 2008-03-27](http://arxiv.org/abs/0803.3946v4)\]\[[7' 2016-01-24](http://arxiv.org/abs/1601.06426v3)\]\[[8' 2018-03-06](http://arxiv.org/abs/1803.02139v1)\].

### <a id='category-zero-knowledge-proofs-(zkp)-and-dp-integration'></a>Zero-Knowledge Proofs (ZKP) and DP Integration: 2 papers found.
Zero-knowledge proofs (ZKPs) have emerged as a critical tool for enhancing privacy in machine learning systems, particularly in scenarios where data and model confidentiality are paramount \[[15' 2019-11-06](http://arxiv.org/abs/1911.02459v2)\]. This is especially relevant in distributed settings where servers may deviate from established protocols, such as by sampling noise from incorrect distributions or generating correlated noise, thereby undermining differential privacy (DP) guarantees \[[2' 2025-02-25](http://arxiv.org/abs/2502.18535v2)\]. To address these challenges, researchers have explored the integration of ZKPs with DP, demonstrating that while ZKPs can provide a sufficient framework for achieving verifiable DP, they are not strictly necessary \[[2' 2025-02-25](http://arxiv.org/abs/2502.18535v2)\]. This has led to the development of novel mechanisms like the Verifiable Distributed Discrete Laplacian Mechanism (VDDLM), which significantly improves proof generation efficiency while maintaining acceptable error rates \[[2' 2025-02-25](http://arxiv.org/abs/2502.18535v2)\]. Additionally, advancements in ZK-based systems, such as the zksk library, have facilitated the implementation of sigma protocols, enabling more robust and flexible privacy-preserving solutions \[[15' 2019-11-06](http://arxiv.org/abs/1911.02459v2)\]. These developments highlight the growing intersection between ZKPs and DP, with a focus on improving both the efficiency and reliability of privacy-preserving machine learning systems.

However, in many embodiments, DP introduces a new attack surface: a malicious entity entrusted with releasing statistics could manipulate the results and use the randomness of DP as a convenient smokescreen to mask its nefariousness. To close this loophole, we introduce the idea of \textit(Verifiable Differential Privacy), which requires the publishing entity to output a zero-knowledge proof that convinces an efficient verifier that the output is both DP and reliable. We resolve this paradox by carefully mixing private and public randomness to compute verifiable DP counting queries with theoretical guarantees and show that it is also practical for real-world deployment. We also demonstrate that computational assumptions are necessary by showing a separation between information-theoretic DP and computational DP under our definition of verifiability.»

### <a id='category-differential-privacy-foundations'></a>Differential Privacy Foundations: 8 papers found.
The category "Differential Privacy Foundations" encompasses foundational research on differential privacy (DP), including its theoretical underpinnings, practical implementations, and extensions to address specific challenges. Early work by Dwork and McSherry \[[6' 2008-03-27](http://arxiv.org/abs/0803.3946v4)\] laid the groundwork for DP as a formal framework for privacy, ensuring that the output of an algorithm does not reveal information about any individual's data. Subsequent studies have explored the privacy-utility tradeoff, demonstrating that symmetric DP mechanisms are optimal for certain source distributions, while asymmetric mechanisms are better suited for others \[[7' 2016-01-24](http://arxiv.org/abs/1601.06426v3)\]. The calibration of noise in DP mechanisms remains a critical challenge, with recent work emphasizing the role of both explicit and implicit randomness in achieving stronger privacy guarantees \[[9' 2020-03-02](http://arxiv.org/abs/2003.00973v2)\]. Additionally, the integration of DP into real-world systems, such as Google's Privacy Sandbox, highlights the practical application of DP in privacy-preserving advertising \[[14' 2024-12-22](http://arxiv.org/abs/2412.16916v3)\]. The shuffle model of privacy has also gained attention, as it offers improved privacy guarantees through random shuffling of data, achieving asymptotically optimal results \[[16' 2020-12-23](http://arxiv.org/abs/2012.12803v3)\]. Furthermore, the concept of heterogeneous differential privacy has emerged to address varying privacy expectations among users, introducing mechanisms that adapt to individual privacy preferences \[[18' 2015-04-27](http://arxiv.org/abs/1504.06998v1)\]. These contributions collectively underscore the evolving landscape of differential privacy, balancing theoretical rigor with practical deployment.

### <a id='category-state-of-the-art-dp-mechanism-comparisons'></a>State-of-the-Art DP Mechanism Comparisons: 2 papers found.
The category "State-of-the-Art DP Mechanism Comparisons" highlights ongoing research into improving differential privacy (DP) mechanisms, particularly in distributed settings where trust in servers is limited. Existing DP models, such as the central and local models, face trade-offs between utility and privacy guarantees \[[12' 2024-09-16](http://arxiv.org/abs/2409.10667v2)\]. The central model relies on a trusted server for data aggregation, while the local model requires individuals to add noise, which reduces the utility of aggregated results \[[12' 2024-09-16](http://arxiv.org/abs/2409.10667v2)\]. Recent studies have explored the distributed model, which combines the utility of the central model with the privacy guarantees of the local model by using Secure Multi-party Computation (MPC) \[[12' 2024-09-16](http://arxiv.org/abs/2409.10667v2)\]. However, challenges remain in efficiently sampling noise with MPC, as existing protocols vary in security assumptions and lack comprehensive experimental evaluations \[[12' 2024-09-16](http://arxiv.org/abs/2409.10667v2)\]. Additionally, the optimal $ε$-differentially private mechanism for single real-valued queries has been derived, revealing that the staircase mechanism, with its geometrically decaying probability density functions, outperforms the Laplacian mechanism in certain regimes, particularly in the low privacy regime \[[13' 2012-12-05](http://arxiv.org/abs/1212.1186v3)\]. These findings underscore the importance of developing more efficient and robust DP mechanisms that balance privacy, utility, and computational feasibility.
