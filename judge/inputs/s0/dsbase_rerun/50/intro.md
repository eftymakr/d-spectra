## Related Works

Recent advancements in machine learning (ML) have led to the proliferation of open-source resources, including models and datasets, which have significantly accelerated research in information retrieval (IR) \[[1' 2021-12-18](http://arxiv.org/abs/2112.10006v6)\]. However, existing platforms such as Hugging Face, while rich in resources, lack structured representations that enable advanced queries and analyses such as tracing model evolution and recommending relevant datasets \[[1' 2021-12-18](http://arxiv.org/abs/2112.10006v6)\]. This limitation has prompted the development of knowledge graph-based approaches to better organize and leverage these resources.

Knowledge graphs (KGs) have been extensively studied for their ability to capture structured relationships and semantic information, which is crucial for tasks such as recommendation and classification \[[2' 2024-09-25](http://arxiv.org/abs/2409.16674v3)\]. For instance, zero-shot and few-shot learning with KGs have been explored to reduce reliance on labeled data by leveraging auxiliary information \[[2' 2024-09-25](http://arxiv.org/abs/2409.16674v3)\]. These methods often employ paradigms such as mapping-based, data augmentation, and propagation-based approaches to enhance model performance \[[2' 2024-09-25](http://arxiv.org/abs/2409.16674v3)\]. Similarly, the integration of KGs into recommendation systems has been investigated to improve the semantic representation of users and items \[[3' 2020-10-28](http://arxiv.org/abs/2011.01103v1)\]. A recent work introduced a prompting-based representation learning method for recommendation using large language models (LLMs), which generates personalized item profiles and aligns them with collaborative filtering representations \[[3' 2020-10-28](http://arxiv.org/abs/2011.01103v1)\].

The construction of knowledge graphs from textual data has also been a focus of research, particularly in the scholarly domain \[[4' 2020-06-29](http://arxiv.org/abs/2006.15772v2)\]. Techniques such as natural language processing (NLP) and machine learning have been employed to extract entities and relationships from research publications, leading to the creation of large-scale scientific knowledge graphs \[[4' 2020-06-29](http://arxiv.org/abs/2006.15772v2)\]. These graphs capture metadata such as authors, venues, and citations, and have been used to support tasks like knowledge extraction and semantic analysis \[[4' 2020-06-29](http://arxiv.org/abs/2006.15772v2)\].

In the context of recommendation systems, multi-stakeholder recommendation has emerged as a critical area, addressing the needs of both users and other stakeholders \[[5' 2022-10-04](http://arxiv.org/abs/2210.01942v1)\]. This includes addressing issues such as popularity bias, which can disproportionately favor popular items over less common ones \[[5' 2022-10-04](http://arxiv.org/abs/2210.01942v1)\]. Metrics have been proposed to evaluate exposure bias from the perspective of different stakeholders, ensuring a more balanced recommendation process \[[5' 2022-10-04](http://arxiv.org/abs/2210.01942v1)\].

Furthermore, the interpretability of machine learning models remains a key challenge, especially in high-stakes domains such as healthcare \[[6' 2017-07-27](http://arxiv.org/abs/1707.08913v1)\]. Approaches inspired by physics, such as energy landscape analysis, have been proposed to identify relevant features that drive model decisions \[[6' 2017-07-27](http://arxiv.org/abs/1707.08913v1)\]. These methods aim to enhance the transparency and trustworthiness of AI systems by providing human-interpretable explanations \[[6' 2017-07-27](http://arxiv.org/abs/1707.08913v1)\].
