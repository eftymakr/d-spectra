## Related Works

Efficient inference in large language models (LLMs) has been a focal point of recent research, particularly with the increasing context lengths that enable more complex reasoning tasks. KV cache reuse has emerged as a critical technique for improving the efficiency of long-context inference by reducing redundant computations \[[1' 2024-12-13](http://arxiv.org/abs/2412.10319v2)\]. However, prior studies have primarily focused on synthetic workloads, overlooking the nuanced patterns observed in real-world scenarios \[[1' 2024-12-13](http://arxiv.org/abs/2412.10319v2)\]. This section reviews key related works that address KV cache management, eviction strategies, and their impact on LLM performance.

Several approaches have been proposed to optimize KV cache usage. For instance, SCBench introduces a comprehensive benchmark to evaluate long-context methods from a KV cache-centric perspective, covering four categories of long-context capabilities \[[1' 2024-12-13](http://arxiv.org/abs/2412.10319v2)\]. This work highlights the importance of analyzing KV cache generation, compression, retrieval, and loading, which are essential for understanding the performance of various inference strategies \[[1' 2024-12-13](http://arxiv.org/abs/2412.10319v2)\]. Additionally, the study identifies challenges such as attention distribution shift in long-generation scenarios, emphasizing the need for adaptive and efficient cache management \[[1' 2024-12-13](http://arxiv.org/abs/2412.10319v2)\].

KeyDiff presents a training-free KV cache eviction method based on key similarity, which allows efficient processing of arbitrarily long prompts under strict resource constraints \[[2' 2025-04-21](http://arxiv.org/abs/2504.15364v4)\]. This method leverages geometrically distinctive keys to identify tokens with high attention scores, enabling effective cache eviction without relying on attention scores \[[2' 2025-04-21](http://arxiv.org/abs/2504.15364v4)\]. KeyDiff has been shown to achieve near baseline performance on benchmarks like LongBench, with a notable reduction in inference latency \[[2' 2025-04-21](http://arxiv.org/abs/2504.15364v4)\].

Lookahead Q-Cache (LAQ) improves KV cache eviction by generating pseudo lookahead queries to better approximate real inference queries \[[3' 2025-05-24](http://arxiv.org/abs/2505.20334v2)\]. This approach enhances the consistency and accuracy of cache eviction, leading to improved performance on benchmarks such as LongBench \[[3' 2025-05-24](http://arxiv.org/abs/2505.20334v2)\]. LAQ is also complementary to existing methods, allowing for flexible integration and further improvements \[[3' 2025-05-24](http://arxiv.org/abs/2505.20334v2)\].

KVShare introduces a multi-tenant KV cache management system that enables efficient and accurate LLM serving by sharing cache across requests \[[4' 2025-03-17](http://arxiv.org/abs/2503.16525v2)\]. This system uses a Dual-Stage High Deviation algorithm and a cache-aware scheduler to optimize performance and reduce Time to First Token (TTFT) \[[4' 2025-03-17](http://arxiv.org/abs/2503.16525v2)\]. KVShare has demonstrated significant improvements in throughput and accuracy compared to full KV recompute \[[4' 2025-03-17](http://arxiv.org/abs/2503.16525v2)\].

Ada-KV proposes an adaptive budget allocation strategy for KV cache eviction, optimizing the compression budget across attention heads \[[5' 2024-10-15](http://arxiv.org/abs/2410.12876v3)\]. This method improves the quality of cache eviction by considering the unique attention patterns of each head, leading to better performance across various scenarios \[[5' 2024-10-15](http://arxiv.org/abs/2410.12876v3)\].

### <a id='category-kv-cache-reuse-patterns-in-real-world-workloads'></a>KV Cache Reuse Patterns in Real-World Workloads: 3 papers found.
The category "KV Cache Reuse Patterns in Real-World Workloads" focuses on the challenges and opportunities of optimizing KV cache reuse in large language model (LLM) inference. Existing benchmarks often evaluate single-request scenarios, neglecting the full lifecycle of the KV cache in real-world use \[[1' 2024-12-13](http://arxiv.org/abs/2412.10319v2)\]. This oversight is significant, as KV cache reuse is widely adopted in frameworks like vLLM and SGLang, and by major LLM providers such as OpenAI, Microsoft, Google, and Anthropic \[[1' 2024-12-13](http://arxiv.org/abs/2412.10319v2)\]. To address this gap, SCBench was introduced as a comprehensive benchmark evaluating long-context methods from a KV cache-centric perspective, covering generation, compression, retrieval, and loading \[[1' 2024-12-13](http://arxiv.org/abs/2412.10319v2)\]. Additionally, research has explored specific use cases, such as infilling tasks, where KV cache reuse is hindered by prompt structure, leading to the development of EFIM, a transformed prompt format that enhances KV cache efficiency while addressing subtoken generation issues \[[7' 2025-05-28](http://arxiv.org/abs/2505.21889v2)\]. Furthermore, studies have examined the economic benefits of KV cache reuse, proposing analytical models to assess cloud costs associated with storing and reusing precomputed KV caches, demonstrating potential cost savings across various workloads \[[9' 2025-03-18](http://arxiv.org/abs/2503.14647v1)\]. These works collectively highlight the importance of understanding and optimizing KV cache reuse patterns for improving LLM serving efficiency and performance.

### <a id='category-multi-tenant-kv-cache-systems'></a>Multi-Tenant KV Cache Systems: 1 papers found.

	Document 1: [Category]: «Multi-Tenant KV Cache Systems»
[Snippet]: «Recent advances in long-text understanding have pushed the context length of large language models (LLMs) up to one million tokens. It boosts LLMs's accuracy and reasoning capacity but causes exorbitant computational costs and unsatisfactory Time to First Token (TTFT). KV cache reuse, which reuses the exact same KV cache of prefixes and templates or shares similar ones but with extra selective recomputation, offers a promising way to tackle this issue. However, prior studies overlook the cross-request KV reuse and the attention deviations introduced by new tokens during the decoding stage. In this paper, we present a KV cache management module that shares the KV cache across requests under multi-tenant scenarios without sacrificing model accuracy. Our system, KVShare, enables accurate and efficient LLM serving by 1) a Dual-Stage High Deviation algorithm (DHD) that conditionally selects a small portion of KV cache to be recomputed during both prefill and decode phases, and 2) a cache-aware scheduler that prioritizes requests based on their KV cache hit rates and orchestrates continuous batching to achieve enhanced system efficiency and faster TTFT. Multi-task experiments conducted on models such as Qwen2.5-7B,Llama3.1-8B and Yi1.5-9B demonstrate that KVShare reduces TTFT by up to 9.39x and increases 1.2x of the throughput compared to the full KV recompute. Moreover, KVShare achieves 20.38% boost in terms of accuracy compared to SOTA methods.»

### <a id='category-attention-based-cache-optimization-methods'></a>Attention-Based Cache Optimization Methods: 6 papers found.
Attention-based cache optimization methods have emerged as critical techniques for improving the efficiency of large language model (LLM) inference, particularly in managing the growing Key-Value (KV) cache required for long-sequence generation \[[13' 2025-03-11](http://arxiv.org/abs/2503.08879v1)\]. These methods aim to reduce the memory and computational overhead by selectively evicting less critical tokens from the KV cache while preserving generation quality \[[6' 2024-07-16](http://arxiv.org/abs/2407.11550v5)\]. For instance, the Attention-Gate module enables dynamic eviction by analyzing global context and generating eviction flags for each token, allowing the model to cache only essential KV states \[[5' 2024-10-15](http://arxiv.org/abs/2410.12876v3)\]. Similarly, Ada-KV introduces an adaptive budget allocation strategy that considers the unique attention patterns of each head, leading to significant quality improvements over uniform compression methods \[[6' 2024-07-16](http://arxiv.org/abs/2407.11550v5)\]. Another approach, DefensiveKV, proposes a perturbation-constrained selection algorithm that identifies critical KV entries by analyzing the impact of value states and pretrained parameters, achieving substantial compression loss reduction \[[10' 2025-02-06](http://arxiv.org/abs/2502.03805v2)\]. KVLink enhances cache reuse by precomputing document-specific KV caches and concatenating them during inference, reducing redundant computation and improving question answering accuracy \[[11' 2025-02-21](http://arxiv.org/abs/2502.16002v4)\]. AttentionPredictor, a learning-based method, dynamically predicts attention patterns to identify critical tokens, achieving high compression ratios and speedups while maintaining performance \[[12' 2025-02-06](http://arxiv.org/abs/2502.04077v3)\]. These methods collectively highlight the importance of context-aware and workload-specific strategies in optimizing KV cache management for efficient LLM inference.

[Snippet]: «Large language models (LLMs) excel at processing long sequences, boosting demand for key-value (KV) caching. While recent efforts to evict KV cache have alleviated the inference burden, they often fail to allocate resources rationally across layers with different attention patterns. In this paper, we introduce Cascading and Adaptive KV cache Eviction (CAKE), a novel approach that frames KV cache eviction as a "cake-slicing problem." CAKE assesses layer-specific preferences by considering attention dynamics in both spatial and temporal dimensions, allocates rational cache size for layers accordingly, and manages memory constraints in a cascading manner. This approach enables a global view of cache allocation, adaptively distributing resources across diverse attention mechanisms while maintaining memory budgets. CAKE also employs a new eviction indicator that considers the shifting importance of tokens over time, addressing limitations in existing methods that overlook temporal dynamics. Comprehensive experiments on LongBench and NeedleBench show that CAKE maintains model performance with only 3.2% of the KV cache and consistently outperforms current baselines across various models and memory constraints, particularly in low-memory settings. Additionally, CAKE achieves over 10x speedup in decoding latency compared to full cache when processing contexts of 128K tokens with FlashAttention-2. Our code is available at https://github.com/antgroup/cakekv.»
