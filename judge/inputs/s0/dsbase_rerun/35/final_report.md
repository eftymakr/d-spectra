Protein dynamics play a crucial role in determining biological functions and properties, and traditional methods for studying these dynamics rely on computationally intensive molecular dynamics (MD) simulations \[[1' 2024-10-24](http://arxiv.org/abs/2410.18403v2)\]. These simulations, while accurate, are often too slow for large-scale or high-resolution studies \[[2' 2024-03-21](http://arxiv.org/abs/2403.14088v2)\]. Recent advances in generative modeling, particularly denoising diffusion models, have enabled more efficient and accurate protein structure prediction and conformation sampling by learning distributions over crystallographic structures \[[3' 2023-10-03](http://arxiv.org/abs/2310.02391v4)\]. However, integrating physical supervision into these data-driven models remains a challenge, as standard energy-based objectives often lead to intractable optimization \[[4' 2012-08-02](http://arxiv.org/abs/1208.2680v2)\].

Several approaches have attempted to address this issue by incorporating physical knowledge into generative models. Similarly, FoldFlow and its variants, such as FoldFlow-OT and FoldFlow-SFM, utilize SE(3) diffusion models to generate protein backbones with high accuracy and stability \[[6' 2020-04-27](http://arxiv.org/abs/2004.13167v1)\]. These models operate on the group SE(3), which represents rigid motions in three-dimensional space, and are designed to be more stable and faster to train than traditional diffusion-based approaches \[[7' 2010-08-24](http://arxiv.org/abs/1008.4006v3)\].

The category "SE(3) Diffusion Models for Protein Generation" encompasses a range of research efforts aimed at leveraging diffusion models to generate and predict protein conformations with high fidelity and physical plausibility. This approach addresses the limitations of traditional physics-based methods, such as molecular dynamics, which suffer from slow equilibration and poor sampling of rare events \[[1' 2024-10-24](http://arxiv.org/abs/2410.18403v2)\]. Recent works have focused on integrating physical priors into diffusion models, either through force-guided networks \[[2' 2024-03-21](http://arxiv.org/abs/2403.14088v2)\] or by learning SE(3) equivariant score functions \[[5' 2020-01-05](http://arxiv.org/abs/2001.01161v1)\]. These methods enable the generation of diverse and accurate protein conformations, as demonstrated in tasks involving fast-folding proteins and complex structures like BPTI \[[2' 2024-03-21](http://arxiv.org/abs/2403.14088v2)\]. Additionally, some studies have extended these models to handle multi-objective optimization problems in robotics, where SE(3) diffusion models are used to learn cost functions for tasks such as grasping and motion planning \[[10' 2022-09-08](http://arxiv.org/abs/2209.03855v4)\]. Furthermore, there is growing interest in applying these models to joint design of protein and nucleic acid complexes, with models like MMDiff demonstrating the ability to generate micro-RNA and single-stranded DNA molecules \[[17' 2023-12-21](http://arxiv.org/abs/2401.06151v1)\]. Overall, the field is advancing through the development of more sophisticated and physically grounded diffusion models that can capture the complex behavior of proteins in a computationally efficient manner.

### <a id='category-efficiency-and-stability-in-diffusion-models'></a>Efficiency and Stability in Diffusion Models: 2 papers found.
Efficiency and stability in diffusion models have been a focal point in recent research, particularly in the context of medical imaging and materials science. In medical imaging, diffusion models have been leveraged to improve outcome prediction for head and neck cancer patients by integrating radiomics with deep learning frameworks, allowing for more accurate survival risk assessment without relying on manual segmentation \[[21' 2022-11-10](http://arxiv.org/abs/2211.05409v1)\]. Similarly, in materials science, diffusion models have been used to study magnetic interactions in iron-based superconductors, where inelastic neutron scattering measurements revealed the two-dimensional nature of magnetic interactions in LaFeAsO, highlighting the importance of interlayer and intralayer exchange ratios in determining superconducting properties \[[25' 2013-03-17](http://arxiv.org/abs/1303.4033v1)\]. These studies underscore the potential of diffusion models to enhance both medical diagnostics and materials research by improving efficiency and stability in complex data-driven tasks.
