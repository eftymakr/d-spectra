## Related Works

Recent advancements in Text-to-Speech (TTS) systems have significantly enhanced speech naturalness, driving the need for precise prosody control and mispronunciation correction \[[1' 2024-08-13](http://arxiv.org/abs/2408.06827v1)\]. Traditional methods for prosody manipulation often rely on specialized modules or additional training, limiting their flexibility for post-hoc adjustments \[[1' 2024-08-13](http://arxiv.org/abs/2408.06827v1)\]. Similarly, conventional mispronunciation correction methods depend on grapheme-to-phoneme dictionaries, which are impractical in low-resource settings \[[1' 2024-08-13](http://arxiv.org/abs/2408.06827v1)\]. This section reviews related works that address these challenges, focusing on methods for prosody control, mispronunciation correction, and post-hoc model editing.

Prosody modeling in TTS has been a central research topic, with several approaches aiming to improve the expressiveness and naturalness of synthesized speech \[[2' 2022-02-16](http://arxiv.org/abs/2202.07816v1)\]. For instance, ProsoSpeech enhances prosody using quantized latent vectors pre-trained on large-scale unpaired and low-quality text and speech data \[[2' 2022-02-16](http://arxiv.org/abs/2202.07816v1)\]. This method introduces a word-level prosody encoder that quantizes the low-frequency band of speech and compresses prosody attributes in the latent prosody vector (LPV). The LPV predictor then generates LPV based on the word sequence, enabling richer prosody generation \[[2' 2022-02-16](http://arxiv.org/abs/2202.07816v1)\]. Similarly, Hierarchical prosody modeling in non-autoregressive parallel neural TTS enables intuitive control over prosody dimensions such as pitch, duration, and energy \[[4' 2021-10-06](http://arxiv.org/abs/2110.02952v2)\]. These models condition on both coarse and fine-grained acoustic features to learn a latent prosody space that supports diverse speaking styles \[[4' 2021-10-06](http://arxiv.org/abs/2110.02952v2)\].

Mispronunciation correction in TTS has also been explored, particularly in low-resource settings. One approach involves using prosody embeddings to model emotional and expressive speech synthesis, allowing for fine-grained control over the speaking style \[[7' 2018-11-06](http://arxiv.org/abs/1811.02122v2)\]. These embeddings introduce temporal structures that enable adjustments at the frame and phoneme levels, improving pitch and amplitude control \[[7' 2018-11-06](http://arxiv.org/abs/1811.02122v2)\]. Additionally, the use of prosody embeddings has been shown to enhance the robustness of TTS systems against speaker perturbations during prosody transfer tasks \[[7' 2018-11-06](http://arxiv.org/abs/1811.02122v2)\]. Another method, Visual-Aware Text-to-Speech (VA-TTS), integrates visual feedback from the listener to adjust speech prosody dynamically, enhancing naturalness in face-to-face interactions \[[29' 2023-06-21](http://arxiv.org/abs/2306.12020v1)\].

Post-hoc model editing has gained attention as a way to refine TTS outputs without retraining. For example, the work on Prosody-TTS introduces an end-to-end model that explicitly models the duration of phonemes and the fundamental frequency (f0), enabling fine-grained control over prosody during synthesis \[[8' 2021-10-06](http://arxiv.org/abs/2110.02854v1)\].
