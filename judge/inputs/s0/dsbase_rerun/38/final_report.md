### <a id='category-knapsack-problem-variants-and-solutions'></a>Knapsack Problem Variants and Solutions: 3 papers found.
The category "Knapsack Problem Variants and Solutions" encompasses a range of research addressing different formulations and solution methods for knapsack-type problems. The 0/1 knapsack problem, for instance, is known to be weakly NP-hard, with pseudo-polynomial time dynamic programming solutions and core branch and bound algorithms capable of solving large instances efficiently \[[3' 2019-02-15](http://arxiv.org/abs/1903.03494v1)\]. However, as variable correlations increase, solving these problems becomes more challenging, as seen in spanner knapsack instances introduced by Pisinger, which resist traditional dynamic programming and branch and bound approaches, necessitating alternative methods like genetic algorithms \[[3' 2019-02-15](http://arxiv.org/abs/1903.03494v1)\]. Additionally, the inverse fractional knapsack problem, which involves modifying problem parameters to achieve a desired optimal solution, has been studied under different norms, with NP-hardness under the $l_1$-norm and quadratic-time algorithms available for specific cases \[[10' 2017-04-01](http://arxiv.org/abs/1704.00145v1)\]. Furthermore, knapsack-type problems have been generalized to non-commutative groups, where the complexity can be influenced by operations such as free and direct products, offering insights into broader computational challenges \[[14' 2014-08-27](http://arxiv.org/abs/1408.6509v2)\]. These contributions highlight the evolving landscape of knapsack problem research, from classical formulations to more complex variants and novel solution strategies.
