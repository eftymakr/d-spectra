## Related Works

The rapid adoption of large language models (LLMs) has underscored the critical need for robust fairness benchmarks \[[1' 2024-09-22](http://arxiv.org/abs/2409.14583v4)\]. Traditional fairness metrics, such as Equalized Odds, focus on discrete accuracy-based evaluations, which fail to capture the implicit impact of model uncertainty \[[1' 2024-09-22](http://arxiv.org/abs/2409.14583v4)\]. This limitation has prompted researchers to explore alternative fairness evaluation frameworks that incorporate uncertainty-aware metrics \[[1' 2024-09-22](http://arxiv.org/abs/2409.14583v4)\]. For instance, \[[2' 2025-04-22](http://arxiv.org/abs/2504.15941v2)\] highlights the persistent challenges in achieving equitable outcomes in machine translation, emphasizing the need for more nuanced fairness measures. Similarly, \[[3' 2024-07-15](http://arxiv.org/abs/2407.10853v6)\] discusses the importance of evaluating fairness across different deployment contexts, suggesting that fairness risks cannot be reliably assessed from benchmark performance alone. This aligns with the need for a more comprehensive fairness metric that reflects model uncertainty.

Several studies have examined gender and occupational biases in LLMs. \[[4' 2025-05-05](http://arxiv.org/abs/2505.02456v2)\] reports significant gender and country biases in occupation recommendations, showing that even when models show parity for gender or country individually, intersectional occupational biases persist. This highlights the complexity of addressing fairness in multilingual and intersectional settings. \[[5' 2023-11-30](http://arxiv.org/abs/2311.18580v2)\] further explores the impact of gender and occupation stereotypes in sentiment analysis, demonstrating how these biases can influence model outputs. The findings from \[[5' 2023-11-30](http://arxiv.org/abs/2311.18580v2)\] support the need for datasets that capture diverse occupational and gender representations to evaluate LLM fairness effectively.

The development of new datasets for fairness evaluation has also been a focus of recent research. \[[6' 2024-04-30](http://arxiv.org/abs/2404.19371v1)\] introduces FairTranslate, a dataset designed to evaluate non-binary gender biases in machine translation, emphasizing the importance of diverse and annotated data. \[[7' 2017-11-14](http://arxiv.org/abs/1711.05144v5)\] discusses the challenges of fairness evaluation in multilingual settings, noting that existing benchmarks often focus on single languages, such as English, while neglecting non-English languages. This is particularly relevant given the global deployment of LLMs and the need for safety alignment across multiple languages \[[7' 2017-11-14](http://arxiv.org/abs/1711.05144v5)\].

In addition to fairness, the safety and reliability of LLMs have been extensively studied. \[[8' 2020-01-21](http://arxiv.org/abs/2001.09784v1)\] introduces XSafety, a multilingual safety benchmark that covers 14 safety issues across 10 languages, highlighting the need for safety alignment in non-English languages. \[[9' 2023-10-02](http://arxiv.org/abs/2310.00905v2)\] further explores the safety risks of LLMs, showing that non-English queries often result in more unsafe responses than English ones, underscoring the necessity of multilingual safety benchmarks.

The integration of fairness and safety considerations into LLM evaluation is increasingly recognized as essential. \[[10' 2025-03-09](http://arxiv.org/abs/2503.06792v1)\] discusses the importance of evaluating fairness across different use cases, emphasizing that fairness risks vary substantially across deployment contexts.
