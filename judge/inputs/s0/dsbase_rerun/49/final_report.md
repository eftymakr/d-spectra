## Related Works

Neural retrieval models have shown significant promise in Web search tasks, particularly due to their ability to learn complex patterns from large-scale data \[[5' 2021-02-23](http://arxiv.org/abs/2102.11903v2)\]. However, training these models typically requires substantial amounts of labeled query-document pairs, which are both expensive and time-consuming to obtain \[[1' 2024-04-15](http://arxiv.org/abs/2404.09473v1)\]. This challenge has led researchers to explore alternative methods for generating synthetic queries, especially leveraging large language models (LLMs) to create scalable and cost-effective training data \[[11' 2024-06-10](http://arxiv.org/abs/2406.06729v1)\]. While synthetic queries offer a promising solution, they often suffer from variability in quality, which can negatively impact downstream retrieval performance \[[1' 2024-04-15](http://arxiv.org/abs/2404.09473v1)\].

Existing approaches typically rely on external re-rankers to filter out noisy or irrelevant query-document pairs during training \[[1' 2024-04-15](http://arxiv.org/abs/2404.09473v1)\]. These methods often use signal-based filtering to remove low-quality pairs, but they may not fully capture the nuances of user intent or the contextual relevance of queries \[[1' 2024-04-15](http://arxiv.org/abs/2404.09473v1)\]. In contrast, our proposed framework integrates ranking signals directly into the query generation process using Direct Preference Optimization (DPO) \[[12' 2024-05-30](http://arxiv.org/abs/2405.19749v2)\]. This approach aims to optimize the generation of high-quality synthetic queries that maximize downstream retrieval effectiveness by aligning with human preferences \[[12' 2024-05-30](http://arxiv.org/abs/2405.19749v2)\].

DPO has been successfully applied in various domains, including generative retrieval for E-commerce search, where it helps align autoregressive models with human preferences through click data \[[2' 2024-07-29](http://arxiv.org/abs/2407.19829v2)\]. Additionally, DPO has been used in the context of Retrieval-Augmented Generation (RAG) to improve the accuracy of generated responses by incorporating semantic search and hybrid query-based retrievers \[[6' 2024-03-22](http://arxiv.org/abs/2404.07220v2)\]. These methods demonstrate that integrating preference signals during query generation can lead to more effective and interpretable results \[[6' 2024-03-22](http://arxiv.org/abs/2404.07220v2)\].

Furthermore, the use of synthetic data for training retrieval models has been explored in the context of legal document retrieval, where it helps overcome the limitations of scarce annotated datasets \[[4' 2024-12-01](http://arxiv.org/abs/2412.00657v1)\]. By generating high-quality synthetic queries, researchers have been able to improve retrieval accuracy and achieve competitive performance on real-world datasets \[[4' 2024-12-01](http://arxiv.org/abs/2412.00657v1)\]. Similarly, in the domain of virtual assistants, synthetic query generation using LLMs has been shown to produce queries that are both specific and representative of real-world usage \[[11' 2024-06-10](http://arxiv.org/abs/2406.06729v1)\].

In addition to synthetic query generation, there has been growing interest in improving the robustness of retrieval pipelines with respect to query variations \[[3' 2021-11-25](http://arxiv.org/abs/2111.13057v3)\]. This includes the development of query variation generators that create semantically equivalent but syntactically different queries to test the robustness of retrieval systems \[[3' 2021-11-25](http://arxiv.org/abs/2111.13057v3)\]. These methods highlight the importance of query diversity and the need for retrieval systems to handle variations in user input while maintaining effectiveness \[[3' 2021-11-25](http://arxiv.org/abs/2111.13057v3)\].
