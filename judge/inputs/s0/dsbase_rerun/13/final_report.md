## Related Works

Long-tailed recognition has been a focal point in recent visual recognition research due to the inherent class imbalance in real-world data \[[1' 2022-07-19](http://arxiv.org/abs/2207.09052v3)\]. This imbalance leads to poor generalization for minority classes, as models tend to prioritize majority classes during training \[[2' 2021-11-27](http://arxiv.org/abs/2111.13998v2)\]. Several approaches have been proposed to address this issue, including data augmentation, loss function modification, and contrastive learning techniques \[[3' 2024-08-01](http://arxiv.org/abs/2408.00483v1)\].

Contrastive learning has emerged as a powerful method for representation learning, particularly in the context of long-tailed recognition \[[4' 2024-03-10](http://arxiv.org/abs/2403.06151v1)\]. Supervised Contrastive Learning (SCL) has shown promising results, but it suffers from issues such as gradient conflicts and imbalanced attraction and repulsion gradients between positive and negative pairs \[[5' 2024-06-04](http://arxiv.org/abs/2406.02223v1)\]. These issues are exacerbated in long-tailed distributions, where the number of samples per class is highly imbalanced \[[6' 2022-12-21](http://arxiv.org/abs/2212.11187v1)\]. To mitigate these problems, researchers have proposed various improvements, such as class-averaging and class-complement strategies to balance the gradient contributions of negative classes \[[7' 2022-11-22](http://arxiv.org/abs/2211.11938v1)\].

Decoupled Contrastive Learning (DSCL) addresses the imbalanced intra-category distance by decoupling the training objective and optimizing the relations of positives toward different objectives \[[8' 2023-09-28](http://arxiv.org/abs/2309.16135v1)\]. This approach also introduces a patch-based self-distillation mechanism to transfer knowledge from head to tail classes, improving the under-representation of tail classes \[[9' 2023-12-05](http://arxiv.org/abs/2312.02517v1)\]. Similarly, Targeted Supervised Contrastive Learning (TSC) improves the uniformity of the feature distribution on the hypersphere by generating targets uniformly distributed on a hypersphere and forcing features of different classes to converge to these targets \[[10' 2021-01-16](http://arxiv.org/abs/2101.06480v1)\].

Another approach, Balanced Contrastive Learning (BCL), introduces class-averaging and class-complement strategies to form a regular simplex, which is ideal for representation learning \[[11' 2023-09-13](http://arxiv.org/abs/2309.07186v1)\]. This method enhances the performance of long-tailed visual recognition by improving the feature representation and class boundaries \[[12' 2024-03-11](http://arxiv.org/abs/2403.06726v2)\]. Additionally, Probabilistic Contrastive Learning (ProCo) estimates the data distribution of samples from each class in the feature space and samples contrastive pairs accordingly, addressing the challenge of constructing contrastive pairs in imbalanced data \[[13' 2020-09-28](http://arxiv.org/abs/2009.12991v5)\].

Theoretical analysis of learning dynamics under class imbalance has also contributed to understanding the challenges in long-tailed recognition \[[14' 2022-07-01](http://arxiv.org/abs/2207.00391v4)\]. This analysis reveals that class imbalance affects the convergence of learning dynamics, particularly with gradient-based optimizers \[[15' 2020-07-23](http://arxiv.org/abs/2007.12229v2)\]. To mitigate these issues, methods such as data augmentation and class uncertainty measures have been proposed to improve the performance of models on imbalanced datasets \[[16' 2023-11-23](http://arxiv.org/abs/2311.14090v2)\].

### <a id='category-contrastive-learning-approaches'></a>Contrastive Learning Approaches: 10 papers found.
The category "Contrastive Learning Approaches" encompasses a range of methods aimed at improving representation learning through contrastive strategies, particularly in the context of long-tailed data distributions. Several studies have explored the limitations of supervised contrastive learning (SCL) in such scenarios, noting that SCL struggles with forming a regular simplex due to imbalanced data \[[1' 2022-07-19](http://arxiv.org/abs/2207.09052v3)\]. This issue leads to poor separability of minority classes in the feature space \[[2' 2021-11-27](http://arxiv.org/abs/2111.13998v2)\]. To address these challenges, researchers have proposed variants such as Balanced Contrastive Learning (BCL), which introduces class-averaging and class-complement techniques to balance gradient contributions and ensure uniform distribution of features \[[1' 2022-07-19](http://arxiv.org/abs/2207.09052v3)\]. Similarly, Targeted Supervised Contrastive Learning (TSC) improves feature uniformity by generating uniformly distributed targets on a hypersphere and aligning class features with these targets \[[2' 2021-11-27](http://arxiv.org/abs/2111.13998v2)\]. Another approach, Decoupled Supervised Contrastive Learning (DSCL), addresses intra-category distance bias by decoupling positive samples and introduces patch-based self-distillation to transfer knowledge from head to tail classes \[[4' 2024-03-10](http://arxiv.org/abs/2403.06151v1)\]. Additionally, Probabilistic Contrastive (ProCo) learning estimates class distributions in the feature space to sample contrastive pairs efficiently, especially for imbalanced data \[[12' 2024-03-11](http://arxiv.org/abs/2403.06726v2)\]. These methods collectively highlight the need for tailored contrastive strategies to enhance generalization and performance on long-tailed recognition tasks \[[1' 2022-07-19](http://arxiv.org/abs/2207.09052v3)\]\[[2' 2021-11-27](http://arxiv.org/abs/2111.13998v2)\]\[[4' 2024-03-10](http://arxiv.org/abs/2403.06151v1)\]\[[12' 2024-03-11](http://arxiv.org/abs/2403.06726v2)\].

### <a id='category-class-imbalance-mitigation-strategies'></a>Class Imbalance Mitigation Strategies: 11 papers found.
The long-tailed recognition problem, where data is highly imbalanced across classes, has been a focal point in recent research, with multiple strategies proposed to mitigate its impact on model performance \[[3' 2024-08-01](http://arxiv.org/abs/2408.00483v1)\]. Existing methods often rely on data augmentation, re-balancing techniques, or specialized loss functions to address the imbalance \[[5' 2024-06-04](http://arxiv.org/abs/2406.02223v1)\]. For instance, some approaches use saliency masking and contrastive learning to reduce bias toward majority classes by focusing on minority classes \[[5' 2024-06-04](http://arxiv.org/abs/2406.02223v1)\]. Others introduce novel techniques like MixUp-based data augmentation and supervised contrastive learning to enhance feature representation and generalization \[[7' 2022-11-22](http://arxiv.org/abs/2211.11938v1)\]. Additionally, there are efforts to improve the training pipeline by tuning standard components such as batch size, optimizer, and label smoothing, achieving state-of-the-art results without requiring specialized imbalance methods \[[9' 2023-12-05](http://arxiv.org/abs/2312.02517v1)\]. Furthermore, recent work has explored causal inference frameworks to better understand and resolve the biases introduced by long-tailed distributions, leveraging SGD momentum to disentangle its conflicting effects on model learning \[[13' 2020-09-28](http://arxiv.org/abs/2009.12991v5)\]. These approaches highlight the diverse strategies used to tackle the long-tailed recognition problem, ranging from data-level modifications to model architecture and training procedures \[[3' 2024-08-01](http://arxiv.org/abs/2408.00483v1)\]\[[5' 2024-06-04](http://arxiv.org/abs/2406.02223v1)\]\[[7' 2022-11-22](http://arxiv.org/abs/2211.11938v1)\]\[[9' 2023-12-05](http://arxiv.org/abs/2312.02517v1)\]\[[13' 2020-09-28](http://arxiv.org/abs/2009.12991v5)\].

[Snippet]: «Data imbalance is a common problem in machine learning that can have a critical effect on the performance of a model. Various solutions exist but their impact on the convergence of the learning dynamics is not understood. Here, we elucidate the significant negative impact of data imbalance on learning, showing that the learning curves for minority and majority classes follow sub-optimal trajectories when training with a gradient-based optimizer. This slowdown is related to the imbalance ratio and can be traced back to a competition between the optimization of different classes. Our main contribution is the analysis of the convergence of full-batch (GD) and stochastic gradient descent (SGD), and of variants that renormalize the contribution of each per-class gradient. We find that GD is not guaranteed to decrease the loss for each class but that this problem can be addressed by performing a per-class normalization of the gradient. With SGD, class imbalance has an additional effect on the direction of the gradients: the minority class suffers from a higher directional noise, which reduces the effectiveness of the per-class gradient normalization. Our findings not only allow us to understand the potential and limitations of strategies involving the per-class gradients, but also the reason for the effectiveness of previously used solutions for class imbalance such as oversampling.»
