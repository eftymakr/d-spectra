## Related Works

Recent research has explored various methods for identifying and controlling behaviors in large language models (LLMs), particularly focusing on refusal mechanisms and steering techniques. Refusal tokens have been proposed as a way to calibrate refusal behavior in LLMs, allowing models to generate refusal messages for specific categories of queries \[[1' 2024-12-09](http://arxiv.org/abs/2412.06748v2)\]. This approach involves training models with varying proportions of refusal messages to achieve desired refusal rates, though it is computationally expensive \[[1' 2024-12-09](http://arxiv.org/abs/2412.06748v2)\]. In contrast, the proposed COSMIC framework uses cosine similarity to identify steering directions without relying on predefined refusal templates or model outputs \[[1' 2024-12-09](http://arxiv.org/abs/2412.06748v2)\].

The use of cosine similarity in LLMs has also been explored in the context of safety-aligned languages. Research has shown that refusal behavior can be mediated by a single direction in activation space, which can be transferred across languages with near-perfect effectiveness \[[2' 2025-05-22](http://arxiv.org/abs/2505.17306v2)\]. This cross-lingual universality suggests that a single refusal direction may be sufficient for multiple languages, reducing the need for language-specific fine-tuning \[[2' 2025-05-22](http://arxiv.org/abs/2505.17306v2)\]. These findings align with the COSMIC approach, which leverages cosine similarity for direction selection without requiring language-specific adjustments.

In addition to refusal mechanisms, steering techniques have been investigated to control LLM behavior. Prompt-based steering methods have been found to outperform other methods in terms of steering accuracy and model-to-human alignment \[[4' 2025-05-25](http://arxiv.org/abs/2505.19333v2)\]. However, LLMs often exhibit a bias towards `kind' similarity and struggle with `size' alignment, highlighting the importance of carefully designed steering strategies \[[4' 2025-05-25](http://arxiv.org/abs/2505.19333v2)\]. The COSMIC framework addresses these challenges by using cosine similarity to identify steering directions independently of model outputs, ensuring robustness across different alignment conditions \[[1' 2024-12-09](http://arxiv.org/abs/2412.06748v2)\].

The effectiveness of steering techniques has also been evaluated using human similarity judgments, revealing that prompt-based methods achieve higher alignment with human cognition \[[4' 2025-05-25](http://arxiv.org/abs/2505.19333v2)\]. This underscores the importance of aligning model behavior with human expectations, a key consideration in the design of the COSMIC framework \[[1' 2024-12-09](http://arxiv.org/abs/2412.06748v2)\]. Furthermore, one-shot optimized steering vectors have been shown to mediate safety-relevant behaviors in LLMs, demonstrating the potential for fine-grained control over model outputs \[[6' 2025-02-26](http://arxiv.org/abs/2502.18862v2)\].

In the realm of information retrieval, cosine similarity and distance fusion have been proposed to improve retrieval performance, particularly for sparse data \[[5' 2024-06-02](http://arxiv.org/abs/2406.00638v1)\]. This hybrid approach combines cosine similarity and cosine distance measures to provide a more comprehensive understanding of semantic relationships between documents or items \[[5' 2024-06-02](http://arxiv.org/abs/2406.00638v1)\]. While not directly related to steering or refusal mechanisms, this work highlights the broader utility of cosine similarity in modeling semantic relationships, which is also leveraged in the COSMIC framework \[[1' 2024-12-09](http://arxiv.org/abs/2412.06748v2)\].
