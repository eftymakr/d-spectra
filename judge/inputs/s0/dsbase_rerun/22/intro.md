## Related Works

Unsupervised domain adaptation (UDA) for semantic segmentation has attracted significant research interest due to the high cost of obtaining pixel-level annotations in real-world scenarios \[[10' 2020-05-21](http://arxiv.org/abs/2005.10876v1)\]. Traditional UDA methods assume a shared label space between the source and target domains, which limits their applicability when novel classes appear in the target domain \[[1' 2024-05-30](http://arxiv.org/abs/2405.19899v1)\]. This assumption is unrealistic in many practical settings, where the presence of private classes can lead to performance degradation \[[1' 2024-05-30](http://arxiv.org/abs/2405.19899v1)\]. To address this limitation, several works have explored open-set domain adaptation (OSDA), where the target domain may contain unknown classes \[[1' 2024-05-30](http://arxiv.org/abs/2405.19899v1)\].

In \[[1' 2024-05-30](http://arxiv.org/abs/2405.19899v1)\], the authors introduce Open-Set Domain Adaptation for Semantic Segmentation (OSDA-SS), where the target domain includes unknown classes. They identify two major challenges: accurately predicting the boundaries and shapes of unknown classes. To tackle these issues, they propose the Boundary and Unknown Shape-Aware open-set domain adaptation (BUS) method, which uses a novel dilation-erosion-based contrastive loss to improve boundary detection. Additionally, they introduce OpenReMix, a domain mixing augmentation method to enhance shape detection \[[1' 2024-05-30](http://arxiv.org/abs/2405.19899v1)\].

Another approach to handling unknown classes is proposed in \[[6' 2020-04-02](http://arxiv.org/abs/2004.01130v2)\], where the authors define and address a novel domain adaptation problem in semantic scene segmentation, where the target domain includes novel classes not present in the source. They propose a framework that combines domain adaptation and zero-shot learning techniques to enable "boundless" adaptation. This framework leverages a novel architecture and learning scheme to map new classes' labels to relevant visual representations, improving performance through self-training on pseudo-labels \[[6' 2020-04-02](http://arxiv.org/abs/2004.01130v2)\].

The work in \[[12' 2020-12-23](http://arxiv.org/abs/2012.12545v1)\] also tackles the issue of domain shift between synthetic and real data, proposing a zero-style loss to reduce the class imbalance problem in UDA. They focus on separating content and style in images, where only content is relevant for semantic segmentation, and style causes domain shift. This method achieves state-of-the-art performance in semantic segmentation on major UDA settings \[[12' 2020-12-23](http://arxiv.org/abs/2012.12545v1)\].

In \[[17' 2022-06-07](http://arxiv.org/abs/2206.03600v2)\], the authors investigate Source-free Open-partial Domain Adaptation (SF-OPDA), where the model cannot access source data during target adaptation. They propose a novel training scheme to learn a (n+1)-way classifier to predict the n source classes and an unknown class. Their method outperforms current OPDA approaches that require source data during adaptation, demonstrating the feasibility of source-free adaptation \[[17' 2022-06-07](http://arxiv.org/abs/2206.03600v2)\].
