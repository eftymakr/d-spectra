## Related Works

Unsupervised domain adaptation (UDA) for semantic segmentation has attracted significant research interest due to the high cost of obtaining pixel-level annotations in real-world scenarios \[[10' 2020-05-21](http://arxiv.org/abs/2005.10876v1)\]. Traditional UDA methods assume a shared label space between the source and target domains, which limits their applicability when novel classes appear in the target domain \[[1' 2024-05-30](http://arxiv.org/abs/2405.19899v1)\]. This assumption is unrealistic in many practical settings, where the presence of private classes can lead to performance degradation \[[1' 2024-05-30](http://arxiv.org/abs/2405.19899v1)\]. To address this limitation, several works have explored open-set domain adaptation (OSDA), where the target domain may contain unknown classes \[[1' 2024-05-30](http://arxiv.org/abs/2405.19899v1)\].

In \[[1' 2024-05-30](http://arxiv.org/abs/2405.19899v1)\], the authors introduce Open-Set Domain Adaptation for Semantic Segmentation (OSDA-SS), where the target domain includes unknown classes. They identify two major challenges: accurately predicting the boundaries and shapes of unknown classes. To tackle these issues, they propose the Boundary and Unknown Shape-Aware open-set domain adaptation (BUS) method, which uses a novel dilation-erosion-based contrastive loss to improve boundary detection. Additionally, they introduce OpenReMix, a domain mixing augmentation method to enhance shape detection \[[1' 2024-05-30](http://arxiv.org/abs/2405.19899v1)\].

Another approach to handling unknown classes is proposed in \[[6' 2020-04-02](http://arxiv.org/abs/2004.01130v2)\], where the authors define and address a novel domain adaptation problem in semantic scene segmentation, where the target domain includes novel classes not present in the source. They propose a framework that combines domain adaptation and zero-shot learning techniques to enable "boundless" adaptation. This framework leverages a novel architecture and learning scheme to map new classes' labels to relevant visual representations, improving performance through self-training on pseudo-labels \[[6' 2020-04-02](http://arxiv.org/abs/2004.01130v2)\].

The work in \[[12' 2020-12-23](http://arxiv.org/abs/2012.12545v1)\] also tackles the issue of domain shift between synthetic and real data, proposing a zero-style loss to reduce the class imbalance problem in UDA. They focus on separating content and style in images, where only content is relevant for semantic segmentation, and style causes domain shift. This method achieves state-of-the-art performance in semantic segmentation on major UDA settings \[[12' 2020-12-23](http://arxiv.org/abs/2012.12545v1)\].

### <a id='category-domain-mismatch-challenges'></a>Domain Mismatch Challenges: 14 papers found.
The category "Domain Mismatch Challenges" highlights the critical issues faced in unsupervised domain adaptation (UDA) for semantic segmentation, particularly the domain shift between source and target data. This challenge is exacerbated by differences in visual appearance, geometric properties, and distribution between domains, leading to poor generalization of models trained on labeled source data to unlabeled target data \[[3' 2018-04-23](http://arxiv.org/abs/1804.08286v1)\]. To address this, various approaches have been proposed, such as appearance-level and representation-level domain adaptation, where the former aims to make source-domain images resemble the target domain's style, while the latter focuses on learning domain-invariant representations \[[1' 2024-05-30](http://arxiv.org/abs/2405.19899v1)\]. Adversarial learning has been widely adopted to bridge domain gaps by aligning feature distributions between domains \[[7' 2021-03-29](http://arxiv.org/abs/2103.15467v2)\]. Additionally, pseudo-label generation mechanisms have been refined to improve self-supervised learning, with category-adaptive thresholding strategies ensuring balanced pseudo-labels across classes \[[2' 2019-11-27](http://arxiv.org/abs/1911.11981v2)\]. The problem of domain mismatch is also evident in medical imaging, where models trained on one acquisition protocol may fail on another, necessitating domain adaptation techniques that leverage consistency loss and adversarial learning \[[5' 2021-10-21](http://arxiv.org/abs/2110.10916v1)\]. Furthermore, the use of synthetic data, such as GTA5, for training semantic segmentation models introduces domain discrepancies with real-world data, prompting research into methods that enhance generalization through domain adaptation frameworks like Fully Convolutional Adaptation Networks (FCAN) \[[1' 2024-05-30](http://arxiv.org/abs/2405.19899v1)\]. In the context of panoramic and LiDAR semantic segmentation, domain adaptation is crucial due to the mismatch between training data from pinhole cameras and deployment in panoramic or 3D sensor setups \[[4' 2021-01-03](http://arxiv.org/abs/2101.00588v3)\]. Recent advances have also explored brain-inspired architectures, such as the core-periphery constrained transformer (RCCT), to improve UDA performance by mimicking human cognitive structures \[[6' 2020-04-02](http://arxiv.org/abs/2004.01130v2)\]. Overall, the domain mismatch challenge remains a central focus in UDA research, driving the development of novel strategies to ensure robust cross-domain generalization.

### <a id='category-source-free-adaptation-methods'></a>Source-Free Adaptation Methods: 5 papers found.
The category "Source-Free Adaptation Methods" encompasses approaches that address domain adaptation in semantic segmentation without access to source data during adaptation, which is critical in privacy-sensitive or data-restricted scenarios \[[3' 2018-04-23](http://arxiv.org/abs/1804.08286v1)\]. These methods aim to transfer knowledge from a labeled source domain to an unlabeled target domain without requiring retraining on the source data, thereby enhancing scalability and practicality \[[11' 2023-02-13](http://arxiv.org/abs/2302.06378v1)\]. For instance, one method introduces a "multi-target UDA without external data" strategy, where a segmentation model is first trained on external data and then adapted to new unseen target domains without accessing the external data during adaptation \[[1' 2024-05-30](http://arxiv.org/abs/2405.19899v1)\]. This approach leverages self-distillation and adversarial learning to preserve knowledge from the external data during adaptation through "one-way" adversarial learning \[[1' 2024-05-30](http://arxiv.org/abs/2405.19899v1)\]. Another method, Source-Free Open-partial Domain Adaptation (SF-OPDA), tackles both domain and category shifts by learning a (n+1)-way classifier to predict the n source classes and an unknown class, using only samples of known source categories for training \[[17' 2022-06-07](http://arxiv.org/abs/2206.03600v2)\]. Additionally, a source-free domain adaptation framework (SFDA) enables knowledge transfer from a well-trained source model to an unlabeled target domain, preserving source domain knowledge while distilling information from the target domain for self-supervised learning \[[25' 2021-12-06](http://arxiv.org/abs/2112.03241v1)\]. These methods collectively highlight the growing importance of source-free adaptation in semantic segmentation, especially in scenarios where source data is inaccessible or private \[[11' 2023-02-13](http://arxiv.org/abs/2302.06378v1)\].
