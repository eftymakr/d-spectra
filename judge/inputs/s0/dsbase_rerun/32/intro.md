## Related Works

Understanding the evolution of feature representations across layers in large language models (LLMs) is crucial for improving interpretability and robustness \[[1' 2012-03-02](http://arxiv.org/abs/1203.0550v3)\]. Recent studies have identified critical layers linked to specific functions or behaviors, but these efforts typically rely on data-dependent analyses of fine-tuned models, limiting their use to post-hoc settings \[[1' 2012-03-02](http://arxiv.org/abs/1203.0550v3)\]. In contrast, our approach introduces a data-oblivious method to identify intrinsic critical layers in pre-fine-tuned LLMs by analyzing representation dynamics via Centered Kernel Alignment (CKA) \[[1' 2012-03-02](http://arxiv.org/abs/1203.0550v3)\]. This method allows for a more general understanding of how representations change during fine-tuning without requiring task-specific data \[[1' 2012-03-02](http://arxiv.org/abs/1203.0550v3)\].

The use of CKA to analyze representation alignment has been explored in several works. For instance, \[[6' 2021-12-17](http://arxiv.org/abs/2112.09305v1)\] demonstrates that CKA based on a Gaussian RBF kernel converges to linear CKA in the large-bandwidth limit, highlighting the sensitivity of convergence to the geometry of feature representations. This aligns with our findings that significant shifts in representation space are driven by changes in the top principal components, which encode semantic transitions from rationales to conclusions \[[1' 2012-03-02](http://arxiv.org/abs/1203.0550v3)\]. Furthermore, \[[7' 2025-02-23](http://arxiv.org/abs/2502.16722v1)\] investigates the layer-wise evolution of representations in fine-tuned transformers, observing that early layers retain general representations while later layers specialize in task adaptation. This supports our observation that layers with significant representation shifts are most affected during fine-tuning \[[1' 2012-03-02](http://arxiv.org/abs/1203.0550v3)\].

Efforts to improve the efficiency of fine-tuning have also been explored. For example, \[[2' 2024-08-16](http://arxiv.org/abs/2408.08670v1)\] introduces an adaptive layer selection method for Vision Transformers (ViTs) that reduces computational cost and memory usage by freezing less important layers. Similarly, \[[13' 2025-01-31](http://arxiv.org/abs/2501.19389v4)\] proposes a federated sketching LoRA (FSLoRA) framework that enables clients to selectively update submatrices of global LoRA modules, adapting to varying client capabilities. These methods share a common goal of reducing the computational burden of fine-tuning while maintaining performance \[[1' 2012-03-02](http://arxiv.org/abs/1203.0550v3)\].

In addition to efficiency, the impact of fine-tuning on model robustness has been studied. \[[14' 2020-04-21](http://arxiv.org/abs/2004.10190v2)\] demonstrates that fine-tuning pre-trained policies can lead to substantial performance gains, especially when adapting to new environments with minimal data. This aligns with our findings that fine-tuning critical layers leads to greater loss reduction compared to non-critical layers \[[1' 2012-03-02](http://arxiv.org/abs/1203.0550v3)\]. Moreover, \[[16' 2021-02-08](http://arxiv.org/abs/2102.03983v1)\] proposes a method to transfer partial knowledge by freezing or fine-tuning specific layers, which can improve performance in few-shot learning scenarios \[[1' 2012-03-02](http://arxiv.org/abs/1203.0550v3)\].
