## Related Works

Diffusion Transformers (DiT) have emerged as a powerful architecture for image and video generation, offering superior performance compared to traditional diffusion models based on U-Net architectures \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2406.02540)\]. However, their large parameter counts and high computational demands pose significant challenges for deployment on resource-constrained devices \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2406.02540)\]. To address these limitations, model quantization has been explored as a means to reduce storage requirements and accelerate inference by lowering the bit-width of model parameters \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2406.02540)\]. While quantization has been extensively studied for image generation models, its application to video generation tasks remains challenging due to the unique temporal dynamics and multi-frame processing involved \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2406.02540)\].

Several recent works have investigated quantization techniques tailored for diffusion models. ViDiT-Q was introduced as an efficient quantization method for DiT models, achieving W8A8 quantization with minimal degradation in visual quality \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2406.02540)\]. Q-DiT further advanced this field by addressing the spatial and temporal variance in DiT weights and activations, achieving a notable reduction in FID when quantizing DiT-XL/2 to W6A8 \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/2406.17343)\]. Similarly, DVD-Quant proposed a data-free quantization framework for video DiTs, enabling W4A4 PTQ without compromising video quality \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/2505.18663)\]. These methods highlight the growing interest in quantizing DiT models for efficient deployment.

The challenges of quantization in diffusion models are further compounded by the temporal variation in activations and the asymmetric distribution of network values \[[4](http://arxiv.org/abs/2502.04056)\]. TQ-DiT introduced time-aware quantization techniques to mitigate these issues, achieving performance comparable to full-precision models at W8A8 \[[5](http://arxiv.org/abs/2411.14172)\]. PTQ4DiT was specifically designed for DiTs, addressing salient channel magnitudes and temporal variability in activation distributions, enabling W4A8 quantization for the first time \[[7](http://arxiv.org/abs/2405.16005)\]. These approaches demonstrate the importance of tailoring quantization strategies to the unique characteristics of DiT models.

In addition to post-training quantization (PTQ), quantization-aware training (QAT) has also been explored for diffusion models. EfficientDM introduced a data-free and parameter-efficient fine-tuning framework, achieving QAT-level performance with PTQ-like efficiency \[[10](http://arxiv.org/abs/2310.03270)\]. QVGen further advanced this area by proposing a quantization-aware training framework tailored for video diffusion models under extremely low-bit quantization \[[12](http://arxiv.org/abs/2505.11497)\]. These methods underscore the potential of QAT in achieving high-quality quantization for diffusion models.
