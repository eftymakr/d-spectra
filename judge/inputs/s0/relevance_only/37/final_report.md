## Related Works

Vision Transformers (ViTs) have gained significant attention for their ability to capture global context through self-attention mechanisms, leading to strong performance in computer vision tasks \[[15](http://arxiv.org/abs/2205.11397)\]. However, the high computational complexity and memory demands of ViTs pose challenges for efficient hardware acceleration, particularly on resource-constrained devices \[[18](http://arxiv.org/abs/2503.02891)\]. Existing approaches often rely on CPU-based execution for complex operations like Softmax and LayerNorm, which are sensitive to model accuracy and difficult to map onto low-precision hardware \[[1](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/0ade4d722e48379761d17cf1efc05200315c27d9)\]. This results in suboptimal performance due to communication overhead between the CPU and accelerator \[[1](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/0ade4d722e48379761d17cf1efc05200315c27d9)\].

Several works have explored hardware acceleration for ViTs. HDViT introduces a heterogeneous FPGA accelerator that addresses the mismatch between operators and hardware architectures, achieving significant performance improvements \[[1](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/0ade4d722e48379761d17cf1efc05200315c27d9)\]. Auto-ViT-Acc proposes an FPGA-aware automatic acceleration framework with mixed-scheme quantization, achieving up to 5.6x speedup on frame rate with minimal accuracy drop \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/2208.05163)\]. HeatViT introduces hardware-efficient adaptive token pruning, reducing computation by up to 65.3% while maintaining high accuracy \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/2211.08110)\]. ME-ViT focuses on minimizing memory traffic through a single-load policy, achieving up to 17.89x improvement in memory bandwidth \[[5](http://arxiv.org/abs/2402.09709)\]. Recent advancements in FPGA-based acceleration techniques for Vision Transformers (ViTs) have focused on addressing the computational and memory challenges inherent in their complex architectures. Several studies have proposed heterogeneous and reconfigurable hardware accelerators to efficiently support the diverse operations in ViTs, such as convolution, attention, and normalization. For instance, HDViT introduces a heterogeneous architecture composed of multiple processing engines (PEs) to accelerate various operators, along with a hybrid-oriented dataflow mapping strategy to reduce data transmission granularity and alleviate storage pressure \[[1](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/0ade4d722e48379761d17cf1efc05200315c27d9)\]. Similarly, HeatViT employs an image-adaptive token pruning framework that dynamically identifies and consolidates non-informative tokens, improving hardware efficiency through 8-bit fixed-point quantization and polynomial approximations \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/2211.08110)\]. EfficientViT, on the other hand, features a Convolution-Transformer hybrid architecture, and an FPGA-based accelerator has been designed to fully exploit its hardware benefits by supporting lightweight convolutions and attention operations \[[7](http://arxiv.org/abs/2403.20230)\]. Additionally, a comprehensive algorithm-hardware codesign approach has been proposed to simultaneously integrate static weight pruning and dynamic token pruning, enabling significant computation complexity reduction while maintaining model accuracy \[[13](http://arxiv.org/abs/2403.14047)\]. These works collectively highlight the importance of tailored hardware designs and efficient dataflow strategies to enhance ViT performance on FPGAs.

These approaches highlight the importance of optimizing memory access and computational efficiency to enable real-time inference on edge devices. Additionally, the use of specialized hardware for complex operations, such as integrating Softmax and LayerNorm into the processing element, further reduces stalls and improves throughput \[[5](http://arxiv.org/abs/2402.09709)\]. These strategies collectively aim to enhance the performance and efficiency of ViT inference on hardware platforms with limited resources.
