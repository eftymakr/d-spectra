## Related Works

Generative retrieval (GR) has gained significant attention in information retrieval (IR) as an alternative to traditional retrieval paradigms \[[1](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/5beaeee1e27007bd12967480bb9c379cebd15e6e)\]. Unlike conventional methods that rely on index-retrieve-then-rank pipelines, GR models directly generate document identifiers (docids) based on a query, enabling end-to-end optimization \[[1](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/5beaeee1e27007bd12967480bb9c379cebd15e6e)\]. However, most existing GR models are primarily evaluated on static document collections, with limited exploration of their performance in dynamic environments \[[1](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/5beaeee1e27007bd12967480bb9c379cebd15e6e)\]. This section reviews related works that address dynamic corpora, docid design, and continual learning in GR.

Several studies have explored the adaptability of GR models to dynamic corpora. For instance, \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/2305.18952)\] evaluated GR on the StreamingQA benchmark and found that GR models are more adaptable to evolving knowledge compared to Dual Encoders (DE), with improvements in inference efficiency and robustness to temporal information. Similarly, \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/2308.14968)\] introduced the CLEVER model, which enables continual learning for GR by incrementally indexing new documents while preserving retrieval performance on previously indexed documents. This work emphasizes the importance of efficient indexing and memory-augmented learning mechanisms for dynamic scenarios \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/2308.14968)\].

Docid design plays a critical role in GR performance. While numeric-based docids are often favored for their efficiency \[[1](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/5beaeee1e27007bd12967480bb9c379cebd15e6e)\], they tend to underperform in dynamic settings due to their tendency to overfit to the initial document set \[[1](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/5beaeee1e27007bd12967480bb9c379cebd15e6e)\]. This issue is attributed to the lack of semantic alignment with language models' pre-trained knowledge \[[1](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/5beaeee1e27007bd12967480bb9c379cebd15e6e)\]. In contrast, text-based docids, such as those generated through n-grams or titles, offer better generalization to unseen documents \[[1](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/5beaeee1e27007bd12967480bb9c379cebd15e6e)\]. \[[9](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/6682a3679a17d090830635f1c420126d20eae54a)\] proposed NOVO docids, which are learnable and interpretable, allowing for semantic optimization during training. ### <a id='category-end-to-end-modeling-and-discrete-auto-encoding-approaches'></a>End-to-End Modeling and Discrete Auto-Encoding Approaches: 6 papers found.
End-to-end modeling and discrete auto-encoding approaches have been pivotal in advancing generative retrieval (GR) by integrating sequence-to-sequence models to directly map queries to relevant document identifiers (docids) \[[1](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/5beaeee1e27007bd12967480bb9c379cebd15e6e)\]. These approaches aim to consolidate corpus information into a single model, enhancing retrieval efficiency and effectiveness \[[1](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/5beaeee1e27007bd12967480bb9c379cebd15e6e)\]. However, existing GR models often face limitations, such as reliance on binary relevance data and challenges in handling multi-graded relevance \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/2305.18952)\]. To address these issues, frameworks like GR$^2$ have been introduced, focusing on generating semantically relevant and distinct docids through joint optimization of relevance and distinctness, along with multi-graded constrained contrastive training \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/2305.18952)\]. Additionally, the pointwise approach, which uses maximum likelihood estimation (MLE) to optimize single docid likelihoods, has been shown to be sub-optimal compared to listwise approaches that consider relevance at the docid list level \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/2308.14968)\]. Recent work has also explored the integration of GR with other paradigms, such as retrieval-augmented generation (RAG), to enhance factual accuracy in knowledge-intensive tasks \[[4](http://arxiv.org/abs/2406.01197)\]. Furthermore, specific applications like book search have been addressed with frameworks like GBS, which incorporate data augmentation and outline-oriented encoding to handle the complexity of book information \[[5](http://arxiv.org/abs/2409.18409)\]. Challenges such as token-level misalignment in GenIR models have also been tackled through direct document relevance optimization (DDRO), which aligns token-level generation with document-level relevance without requiring reinforcement learning \[[6](http://arxiv.org/abs/2408.02152)\]. These developments highlight the ongoing efforts to refine GR models for both static and dynamic corpora, emphasizing the importance of docid design, training strategies, and application-specific adaptations \[[1](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/5beaeee1e27007bd12967480bb9c379cebd15e6e)\]\[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/2305.18952)\]\[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/2308.14968)\]\[[4](http://arxiv.org/abs/2406.01197)\]\[[5](http://arxiv.org/abs/2409.18409)\]\[[6](http://arxiv.org/abs/2408.02152)\].
