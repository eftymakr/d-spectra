## Related Works

Video super-resolution (VSR) has been a focal point in computer vision, aiming to enhance low-resolution videos into high-resolution ones while preserving temporal consistency and visual quality. Early approaches relied on traditional methods such as interpolation and motion compensation, but recent advances have shifted towards deep learning-based frameworks \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2502.07381)\]. These methods typically process each frame independently or use recurrent architectures to model temporal dependencies \[[2](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/c6a8ff4d9dcdf9e5d82310b847af999ed6655f65)\]. However, they often struggle with maintaining consistency across frames due to the inherent limitations of recurrent structures \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/2410.18137)\].

Diffusion models have emerged as a promising alternative for VSR, offering high-quality generation capabilities by iteratively denoising low-resolution inputs \[[4](http://arxiv.org/abs/2407.13987)\]. However, existing diffusion-based VSR methods suffer from introducing artifacts and degradations due to the stochastic nature of the diffusion process \[[5](http://arxiv.org/abs/2501.02269)\]. This issue is exacerbated in real-world scenarios where videos are often compressed and contain artifacts \[[6](http://arxiv.org/abs/2103.10081)\]. To address this, several works have proposed diffusion models tailored for compressed videos, incorporating modules to mitigate spatial distortions and refine temporal consistency \[[7](http://arxiv.org/abs/2002.11616)\]. For instance, the Spatial Degradation-Aware and Temporal Consistent (SDATC) diffusion model introduces a distortion control module and a spatio-temporal attention module to enhance the generation process \[[8](http://arxiv.org/abs/2407.01960)\].

Another line of research focuses on improving the efficiency and performance of VSR by leveraging advanced architectures. For example, the Deep Video Super-Resolution Network using Dynamic Upsampling Filters avoids explicit motion compensation by generating dynamic upsampling filters and a residual image directly from the input \[[9](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/a2c50f93f6d0892918e0ff653750d198ccab48d0)\]. Similarly, the RealViformer framework investigates the impact of artifacts on attention mechanisms and proposes channel-attention-based techniques to reduce feature redundancy and improve temporal consistency \[[10](http://arxiv.org/abs/2207.06345)\]. These approaches highlight the importance of modeling both spatial and temporal information effectively.

In addition to attention mechanisms, recurrent architectures have been explored to model long-term dependencies in video sequences \[[11](http://arxiv.org/abs/2201.12288)\]. The Frame-Recurrent Video Super-Resolution framework, for instance, uses previously inferred high-resolution estimates to super-resolve subsequent frames, naturally encouraging temporal consistency \[[12](http://arxiv.org/abs/1801.04590)\]. Similarly, the Video Super-Resolution Transformer (VSR-Transformer) adapts the Transformer architecture for VSR by introducing spatial-temporal convolutional self-attention and bidirectional optical flow-based feed-forward layers to capture correlations across frames \[[13](http://arxiv.org/abs/2007.10595)\].

### <a id='category-diffusion-models-in-vsr'></a>Diffusion Models in VSR: 8 papers found.
The category "Diffusion Models in VSR" encompasses a range of approaches that leverage diffusion models to enhance video super-resolution (VSR) by addressing issues such as compression artifacts, temporal consistency, and detail restoration. Existing methods often struggle with the inherent randomness of diffusion processes, leading to artifacts and inconsistencies in generated videos \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2502.07381)\]. To mitigate these challenges, researchers have introduced specialized modules and training strategies. For instance, the SDATC diffusion model incorporates a distortion control module (DCM) to minimize the impact of noise from low-quality frames, while a spatio-temporal attention module (STAM) captures temporal correlations \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2502.07381)\]. Similarly, the SeeClear framework introduces semantic distillation and pixel condensation to enhance detail consistency across frames, supported by instance-centric alignment and channel-wise texture aggregation \[[6](http://arxiv.org/abs/2103.10081)\]. The Upscale-A-Video method ensures temporal coherence through local temporal layers in U-Net and VAE-Decoder, along with a flow-guided recurrent latent propagation module that maintains stability across entire sequences \[[7](http://arxiv.org/abs/2002.11616)\]. Additionally, the Video Motion Customization (VMC) framework adapts temporal attention layers to generate videos with tailored motions, using residual vectors as motion references during diffusion \[[8](http://arxiv.org/abs/2407.01960)\]. These approaches collectively highlight the growing trend of integrating diffusion models with advanced attention mechanisms and self-supervised learning to improve the quality and consistency of VSR results.

### <a id='category-spatio-temporal-attention-mechanisms'></a>Spatio-Temporal Attention Mechanisms: 4 papers found.
In the realm of spatio-temporal attention mechanisms for video super-resolution (VSR), recent works have focused on addressing the challenges posed by real-world degradations and artifacts. For instance, one study highlights that recurrent VSR methods are particularly vulnerable to artifact propagation over time, and that channel attention mechanisms, while less sensitive to artifacts, can lead to feature redundancy due to high covariance among output channels \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2502.07381)\]. To counteract this, the paper proposes techniques such as squeeze-excite mechanisms and covariance-based rescaling, leading to the development of RealViformer, which outperforms existing methods with fewer parameters and faster runtimes \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2502.07381)\]. Another work introduces a hierarchical approach to incorporate temporal information, dividing the input sequence into groups based on frame rates, and integrating these with attention modules and deep intra-group fusion to recover missing details \[[13](http://arxiv.org/abs/2007.10595)\]. Additionally, a method adapts the Transformer architecture for VSR by addressing its limitations in exploiting data locality and feature alignment, introducing a spatial-temporal convolutional self-attention layer and a bidirectional optical flow-based feed-forward layer \[[21](http://arxiv.org/abs/2106.06847)\]. Furthermore, STARnet is designed to jointly super-resolve videos in space and time, leveraging mutual relationships between spatial and temporal dimensions to enhance performance across multiple super-resolution tasks \[[27](http://arxiv.org/abs/2003.13170)\]. These approaches collectively demonstrate the ongoing efforts to improve the robustness and effectiveness of spatio-temporal attention mechanisms in VSR.

### <a id='category-efficient-vsr-architectures'></a>Efficient VSR Architectures: 11 papers found.
Efficient video super-resolution (VSR) architectures have been extensively explored to address the limitations of traditional two-stage methods, which often suffer from high computational costs and suboptimal temporal-spatial integration. Recent works propose one-stage frameworks that directly synthesize high-resolution (HR) slow-motion videos from low-resolution (LR) and low-frame-rate (LFR) inputs, leveraging advanced recurrent and attention-based modules \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2502.07381)\]. For instance, a deformable ConvLSTM is introduced to align and aggregate temporal information, enabling better exploitation of global temporal contexts \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2502.07381)\]. Similarly, a bidirectional recurrent convolutional network is designed for efficient multi-frame SR, reducing parameter counts and computational complexity by using weight-sharing convolutional connections and 3D feedforward convolutions \[[2](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/c6a8ff4d9dcdf9e5d82310b847af999ed6655f65)\]. These methods emphasize patch-based temporal modeling, which allows for faster processing of complex motions \[[2](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/c6a8ff4d9dcdf9e5d82310b847af999ed6655f65)\]. Another approach introduces a recurrent network with bidirectional interaction, requiring only one alignment and fusion step, thereby reducing redundant operations and improving efficiency \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/2410.18137)\]. Additionally, a frame-recurrent framework is proposed, where the previously inferred HR estimate is used to super-resolve subsequent frames, ensuring temporal consistency while minimizing computational overhead \[[4](http://arxiv.org/abs/2407.13987)\]. Transformers have also been integrated into VSR pipelines, such as the real-time video super-resolution transformer (RVSRT), which uses an end-to-end Transformer network and two-stage interpolation sampling to achieve faster inference speeds and smaller model sizes \[[5](http://arxiv.org/abs/2501.02269)\]. These architectures collectively highlight the shift towards more efficient and effective methods that integrate temporal and spatial information seamlessly, reducing computational costs while maintaining or improving performance \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2502.07381)\]\[[2](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/c6a8ff4d9dcdf9e5d82310b847af999ed6655f65)\]\[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/2410.18137)\]\[[4](http://arxiv.org/abs/2407.13987)\]\[[5](http://arxiv.org/abs/2501.02269)\].
