## Related Works

Generative retrieval has emerged as a promising alternative to traditional retrieval methods, which rely on static index structures and separate encoding and decoding stages \[[10](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/5beaeee1e27007bd12967480bb9c379cebd15e6e)\]. Unlike conventional approaches, generative retrieval integrates the entire retrieval process into a single neural network, enabling direct generation of document identifiers from queries \[[10](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/5beaeee1e27007bd12967480bb9c379cebd15e6e)\]. This paradigm has attracted significant attention, particularly due to its potential for improved efficiency and seamless integration with large language models \[[10](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/5beaeee1e27007bd12967480bb9c379cebd15e6e)\]. However, several challenges remain, including the generalization of generative retrieval to out-of-distribution corpora and the limitations of constrained auto-regressive generation \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2305.11841)\].

Early work on generative retrieval focused on the use of sequence-to-sequence models to directly map queries to document identifiers \[[10](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/5beaeee1e27007bd12967480bb9c379cebd15e6e)\]. These models were often trained on static corpora and evaluated on tasks such as passage ranking \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2305.11841)\]. However, recent studies have highlighted the limitations of these approaches when scaling to large-scale document collections \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2305.11841)\]. For instance, \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2305.11841)\] found that generative retrieval techniques, while competitive with state-of-the-art dual encoders on small corpora, face significant challenges when scaling to millions of passages. This includes issues such as the need for synthetic queries during indexing and the inefficacy of certain architectural modifications when considering computational costs \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2305.11841)\].

The use of constrained decoding has also been explored to improve the accuracy of generative retrieval \[[10](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/5beaeee1e27007bd12967480bb9c379cebd15e6e)\]. Constrained decoding ensures that generated document identifiers are valid and relevant to the query \[[10](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/5beaeee1e27007bd12967480bb9c379cebd15e6e)\]. However, the effectiveness of this approach depends on the design of the constraints and the underlying model architecture \[[10](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/5beaeee1e27007bd12967480bb9c379cebd15e6e)\]. For example, \[[9](http://arxiv.org/abs/2408.02152)\] proposed a few-shot indexing-based generative retrieval framework that eliminates the need for extensive training by prompting a large language model to generate document identifiers for the entire corpus. This approach significantly reduces training costs and improves adaptability to dynamic document corpora \[[9](http://arxiv.org/abs/2408.02152)\].

In addition to training and indexing strategies, the choice of decoding algorithm has a significant impact on the performance of generative retrieval \[[12](http://arxiv.org/abs/2502.03678)\]. Beam search, a commonly used decoding method, has been shown to be suboptimal in certain scenarios due to its inability to account for the joint probability of the entire generated response \[[12](http://arxiv.org/abs/2502.03678)\]. To address this, \[[12](http://arxiv.org/abs/2502.03678)\] proposed a selective refinement framework that incorporates a sliding reflection window and a pausing criterion, allowing for more efficient and accurate text generation \[[12](http://arxiv.org/abs/2502.03678)\].
