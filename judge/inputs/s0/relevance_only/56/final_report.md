## Related Works

Cross-Domain Sequential Recommendation (CDSR) has attracted significant attention due to its ability to alleviate data sparsity and cold-start issues by leveraging sequential interactions across multiple domains \[[1](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/e33f51c13728bedeeb54a76b398949a381d6152f)\]. Traditional approaches often rely on overlapping users to transfer knowledge between domains, but this limits their applicability to real-world scenarios where such users are rare \[[5](http://arxiv.org/abs/2410.13588)\]. Moreover, these methods struggle to capture complex transition patterns from mixed behavior sequences \[[5](http://arxiv.org/abs/2410.13588)\]. Recent studies have explored the integration of Large Language Models (LLMs) into CDSR to address these limitations by leveraging their semantic reasoning capabilities \[[15](http://arxiv.org/abs/2209.10163)\].

Several works have attempted to enhance CDSR through the use of LLMs. For instance, \[[1](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/e33f51c13728bedeeb54a76b398949a381d6152f)\] proposes LLMCDSR, which employs LLMs to predict pseudo items within single-domain interactions and uses a Collaborative-Textual Contrastive Pre-Training strategy to infuse collaborative information into textual features. This approach aims to improve the reliability of LLM-generated recommendations by reducing hallucination and enhancing semantic alignment. Similarly, \[[19](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/7768e63d08be5df8ad7295b271d6bb11bc801cc4)\] introduces MIBR, which addresses the issue of negative transfer by capturing diverse user interests through a Multi-Interest Extraction (MIE) module and using a cross-domain bridging (CDB) module to align interests across domains. These methods highlight the potential of LLMs to improve the semantic understanding of user preferences in cross-domain settings.

Another line of research focuses on the integration of LLMs with traditional sequential recommendation models. For example, \[[20](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/6dd0605f7de7581c69b4acf0ab78000748a09f3e)\] proposes CD-ASR, which combines general and current user preferences to improve recommendation accuracy. The model uses a general attention mechanism to aggregate user information from multiple domains and a self-attention mechanism to capture current preferences in the target domain. This dual approach enables more accurate knowledge transfer from source to target domains. Additionally, \[[22](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/c13603992b90529918bd170e2f7a10858a340d43)\] introduces MARL4CDSR, a multi-agent reinforcement learning framework that selects relevant items from source domains to enhance recommendations in sparse target domains. This method optimizes the transfer process by coordinating agents' strategies and aligning embeddings between source and target domains.

### <a id='category-cross-domain-sequential-recommendation-(cdsr)-overview'></a>Cross-Domain Sequential Recommendation (CDSR) Overview: 4 papers found.
Cross-Domain Sequential Recommendation (CDSR) focuses on uncovering and transferring users' sequential preferences across multiple recommendation domains, addressing challenges such as aligning user and item representations and modeling complex transition patterns \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/2401.04971)\]. Existing approaches have primarily concentrated on transfer modules and self-supervised learning for user representation alignment, but item representation alignment has received limited attention, potentially leading to sub-optimal sequential modeling \[[10](http://arxiv.org/abs/2405.12473)\]. To address this, CA-CDSR introduces a model-agnostic framework that enables sequence-aware generation and adaptive partial alignment of item representations, improving the accuracy of sequential recommendations \[[10](http://arxiv.org/abs/2405.12473)\]. Additionally, MDSR-DSL tackles the challenge of capturing accurate domain-specific sequential preferences by utilizing cross-domain items to supplement missing sequential context and introducing a domain space learning mechanism to adapt item representations across domains \[[14](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/756e3e6c0956d1daa19367eb13115151b20eeb7f)\]. Furthermore, DDGHM proposes a dual dynamic graph modeling approach combined with hybrid metric training to alleviate data sparsity and enhance prediction accuracy by capturing both intra-domain and inter-domain sequential transitions \[[15](http://arxiv.org/abs/2209.10163)\]. These methods collectively highlight the evolving strategies in CDSR, emphasizing the integration of cross-domain information, alignment of representations, and modeling of complex sequential patterns.

### <a id='category-traditional-cdsr-approaches-and-limitations'></a>Traditional CDSR Approaches and Limitations: 7 papers found.
Cross-domain sequential recommendation (CDSR) aims to predict future user interactions by leveraging historical behavior across multiple domains, but existing approaches face significant challenges. One major limitation is the reliance on overlapping users to transfer knowledge between domains, which restricts the generalizability of models in real-world scenarios where such users are scarce \[[5](http://arxiv.org/abs/2410.13588)\]. Additionally, most methods fail to capture the complex inter-sequence item relationships and time-interval-aware patterns that are essential for accurate sequential recommendations \[[7](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/27670ed18e96550c674e022e284fc5356a7458c2)\]. These issues are further compounded by data sparsity and class imbalance, where popular items dominate the dataset while rare items receive minimal attention \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/2401.04971)\]. To address these limitations, recent works have introduced innovative solutions such as shared proxy item encoders to generate universal item representations \[[7](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/27670ed18e96550c674e022e284fc5356a7458c2)\], time-interval-aware attention mechanisms to model sequential dynamics \[[7](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/27670ed18e96550c674e022e284fc5356a7458c2)\], and contrastive learning frameworks to enhance cross-domain alignment \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/2401.04971)\]. Moreover, some studies have highlighted the need for open-world assumptions, moving away from the closed-world assumption that assumes full user overlap and stable data distribution \[[7](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/27670ed18e96550c674e022e284fc5356a7458c2)\]. These efforts have led to the development of more robust models, such as the Decoupled Representation via Extraction Attention Module (DREAM) and the Adaptive Multi-Interest Debiasing framework (AMID), which aim to improve performance in real-world, dynamic environments \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/2401.04971)\]\[[7](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/27670ed18e96550c674e022e284fc5356a7458c2)\].
