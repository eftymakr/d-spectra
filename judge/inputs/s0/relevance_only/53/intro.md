## Related Works

Sequential recommendation systems aim to predict the next item a user may interact with, based on their historical behavior sequences. These systems have evolved significantly, incorporating advanced neural architectures to better capture user preferences and interaction patterns. Early approaches, such as Markov Chains (MCs), assumed that the next action depends only on the last few actions, while Recurrent Neural Networks (RNNs) were introduced to model longer-term dependencies \[[7](http://arxiv.org/abs/1808.09781)\]. However, RNNs often struggle with long-term memory and computational efficiency \[[15](http://arxiv.org/abs/2008.08273)\]. More recently, self-attention mechanisms, as used in SASRec, have been proposed to capture both long-term and short-term dependencies by selectively focusing on relevant items in the sequence \[[7](http://arxiv.org/abs/1808.09781)\]. While SASRec improves performance, it assumes uniform time intervals and does not explicitly model the temporal dynamics of interactions \[[6](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/9c7a455b1e48d01a99e58e884a6c1acb75074ad0)\].

To address the limitations of uniform time assumptions, TiSASRec was introduced, which explicitly models the time intervals between interactions by incorporating time-aware positional encodings into the self-attention mechanism \[[6](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/9c7a455b1e48d01a99e58e884a6c1acb75074ad0)\]. This allows for more accurate modeling of user behavior sequences with irregular time intervals. Similarly, TLSRec integrates time-aware attention mechanisms to capture both the duration and spectral characteristics of time intervals, enabling more nuanced modeling of user preferences over time \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/2208.04760)\]. These models highlight the importance of temporal information in sequential recommendation, but they often lack mechanisms to capture both global and local fluctuations in user preferences \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/2208.04760)\].

To better model the dynamic evolution of user preferences, SS4Rec was proposed, which integrates a time-aware state space model (SSM) to handle irregular time intervals and a relation-aware SSM to model contextual dependencies \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2502.08132)\]. This dual SSM approach enables the model to infer user interest from both temporal and sequential perspectives, making it particularly effective in capturing complex user-item transitions. Additionally, KATRec leverages knowledge graphs to enhance item representations by incorporating higher-order relationships and user dynamic sequences \[[9](http://arxiv.org/abs/2012.03323)\]. This approach improves the modeling of both short-term and long-term user interests by integrating side information.

In contrast, models such as IRUP integrate item relationships and user preferences by combining graph convolution networks (GCN) with attention mechanisms to capture both long-term and short-term preferences \[[12](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/eb2ad6471c9e665a6393383a2deb2224f1ef07d7)\]. The co-attention mechanism in IRUP further enhances user preference representation by learning cross-correlation information between long-term and short-term preferences. This approach improves recommendation performance by effectively fusing these complementary aspects of user behavior.
