## Related Works

Sequential recommendation systems aim to predict the next item a user may interact with, based on their historical behavior sequences. These systems have evolved significantly, incorporating advanced neural architectures to better capture user preferences and interaction patterns. Early approaches, such as Markov Chains (MCs), assumed that the next action depends only on the last few actions, while Recurrent Neural Networks (RNNs) were introduced to model longer-term dependencies \[[7](http://arxiv.org/abs/1808.09781)\]. However, RNNs often struggle with long-term memory and computational efficiency \[[15](http://arxiv.org/abs/2008.08273)\]. More recently, self-attention mechanisms, as used in SASRec, have been proposed to capture both long-term and short-term dependencies by selectively focusing on relevant items in the sequence \[[7](http://arxiv.org/abs/1808.09781)\]. While SASRec improves performance, it assumes uniform time intervals and does not explicitly model the temporal dynamics of interactions \[[6](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/9c7a455b1e48d01a99e58e884a6c1acb75074ad0)\].

To address the limitations of uniform time assumptions, TiSASRec was introduced, which explicitly models the time intervals between interactions by incorporating time-aware positional encodings into the self-attention mechanism \[[6](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/9c7a455b1e48d01a99e58e884a6c1acb75074ad0)\]. This allows for more accurate modeling of user behavior sequences with irregular time intervals. Similarly, TLSRec integrates time-aware attention mechanisms to capture both the duration and spectral characteristics of time intervals, enabling more nuanced modeling of user preferences over time \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/2208.04760)\]. These models highlight the importance of temporal information in sequential recommendation, but they often lack mechanisms to capture both global and local fluctuations in user preferences \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/2208.04760)\].

To better model the dynamic evolution of user preferences, SS4Rec was proposed, which integrates a time-aware state space model (SSM) to handle irregular time intervals and a relation-aware SSM to model contextual dependencies \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2502.08132)\]. This dual SSM approach enables the model to infer user interest from both temporal and sequential perspectives, making it particularly effective in capturing complex user-item transitions. Additionally, KATRec leverages knowledge graphs to enhance item representations by incorporating higher-order relationships and user dynamic sequences \[[9](http://arxiv.org/abs/2012.03323)\]. This approach improves the modeling of both short-term and long-term user interests by integrating side information.

In contrast, models such as IRUP integrate item relationships and user preferences by combining graph convolution networks (GCN) with attention mechanisms to capture both long-term and short-term preferences \[[12](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/eb2ad6471c9e665a6393383a2deb2224f1ef07d7)\]. The co-attention mechanism in IRUP further enhances user preference representation by learning cross-correlation information between long-term and short-term preferences. This approach improves recommendation performance by effectively fusing these complementary aspects of user behavior.

### <a id='category-time-aware-attention-mechanisms'></a>Time-Aware Attention Mechanisms: 5 papers found.
Time-aware attention mechanisms have emerged as a critical approach in sequential recommendation systems, aiming to better capture the temporal dynamics of user interactions. These mechanisms address the limitations of traditional models that often neglect the time intervals between interactions, focusing solely on the order of items \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/2208.04760)\]. By explicitly modeling timestamps, researchers have introduced models like TiSASRec, which integrates both the absolute positions of items and the time intervals between them, leading to more accurate next-item predictions \[[6](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/9c7a455b1e48d01a99e58e884a6c1acb75074ad0)\]. Similarly, TLSRec introduces a hierarchical self-attention network to learn user preferences at both global and local time scales, while also employing a neural time gate to adaptively regulate the contributions of long-term and short-term preferences based on the time lag between interactions \[[2](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/417f89b6992377e3e5d706672bfa4da837f011df)\]. The TLSTSRec model further enhances this by leveraging a trainable timestamp matrix that captures time duration and time spectrum, enabling more precise modeling of user behavior over time \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2502.08132)\]. These approaches collectively highlight the importance of incorporating temporal information to better reflect the evolving nature of user preferences, which is essential for improving the effectiveness of sequential recommendation systems.

### <a id='category-self-attention-mechanisms-in-sequential-recommendation'></a>Self-Attention Mechanisms in Sequential Recommendation: 4 papers found.
Sequential recommendation models have seen significant advancements, particularly through the integration of self-attention mechanisms to better capture user behavior dynamics \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/2208.04760)\]. Traditional approaches, such as Markov Chains and RNNs, have limitations in handling long-term dependencies and varying sequence lengths \[[7](http://arxiv.org/abs/1808.09781)\]. Recent work has focused on leveraging self-attention to identify relevant items in a user's history while considering both similarity and diversity in preferences \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/2208.04760)\]. For instance, models like SASRec combine self-attention with the efficiency of Markov Chains, enabling the capture of long-term semantics while maintaining computational efficiency \[[7](http://arxiv.org/abs/1808.09781)\]. Similarly, LimaRec introduces a lifelong incremental multi-interest self-attention framework to adaptively model diverse user interests over time \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/2208.04760)\]. Additionally, S3-Rec employs self-supervised learning to enhance data representations by capturing correlations between items, attributes, and subsequences \[[5](http://arxiv.org/abs/2008.07873)\]. These methods highlight the growing emphasis on self-attention as a tool to improve both the accuracy and adaptability of sequential recommendation systems. Furthermore, the Probabilistic Attention mechanism (PAtt) extends self-attention by incorporating global probabilistic models to balance relevance and diversity in recommendations \[[4](http://arxiv.org/abs/2105.14060)\]. Overall, these studies underscore the importance of self-attention in addressing the challenges of sequence modeling in recommendation systems.
