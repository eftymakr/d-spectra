## Related Works

Sensor fusion is a cornerstone of autonomous driving, enabling robust perception in diverse environmental conditions \[[20](http://arxiv.org/abs/2304.10410)\]. Among the most commonly used sensor pairs, radar and camera systems offer complementary advantages: radar provides reliable measurements in adverse weather, while cameras capture rich visual information \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2307.15264)\]. However, achieving accurate calibration between these sensors remains a critical challenge, particularly in dynamic and unstructured environments \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2307.15264)\]. This section reviews existing approaches to radar-camera calibration and fusion, highlighting their limitations and the innovations presented in this work.

Early methods for radar-camera calibration often relied on target-based approaches, which require carefully designed calibration environments and specialized hardware \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2307.15264)\]. For instance, one method places a single corner reflector on the ground and uses iterative data collection to estimate the extrinsic calibration matrix \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2307.15264)\]. While these methods achieve reasonable accuracy, they are not suitable for real-world deployment due to their dependency on controlled environments \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2307.15264)\]. Another approach leverages the 6-DoF pose estimation of LiDAR sensors to build a 3D map and then calibrates radar sensors against this map \[[2](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/0e88fc24ae7694d0b1a2fe78322936fc568bc7d2)\]. However, this method assumes overlapping fields of view between LiDAR and radar, which is not always feasible \[[2](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/0e88fc24ae7694d0b1a2fe78322936fc568bc7d2)\].

More recent methods have focused on targetless calibration, enabling real-time and automatic calibration without the need for dedicated targets \[[5](http://arxiv.org/abs/2309.00787)\]. For example, one method uses the radar's ability to measure its own ego-velocity to perform spatiotemporal calibration \[[6](http://arxiv.org/abs/2211.01871)\]. Another approach employs deep learning to extract common features from radar and camera data, allowing for feature-based matching and extrinsic transformation estimation \[[5](http://arxiv.org/abs/2309.00787)\]. These methods improve the robustness of calibration but often require extensive training data or specialized hardware \[[5](http://arxiv.org/abs/2309.00787)\].

In addition to calibration, several studies have explored sensor fusion techniques that integrate radar and camera data for object detection and scene understanding \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/2009.08428)\]. For example, one method uses a middle-fusion approach to combine radar point clouds with RGB images, leveraging both modalities for improved detection accuracy \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/2009.08428)\]. Another method, CRAB, addresses depth ambiguity in backward projection by incorporating radar data to refine depth estimates \[[10](http://arxiv.org/abs/2509.05785)\]. These approaches demonstrate the potential of radar-camera fusion but often struggle with feature misalignment and spatial inconsistencies \[[13](http://arxiv.org/abs/2504.16368)\].

To address these challenges, recent works have proposed more sophisticated fusion frameworks, such as CramNet, which introduces a ray-constrained cross-attention mechanism to resolve geometric correspondences between camera and radar features \[[11](http://arxiv.org/abs/2210.09267)\]. ### <a id='category-radar-camera-calibration-methods'></a>Radar-Camera Calibration Methods: 6 papers found.
Recent advancements in radar-camera calibration methods have focused on addressing the challenges of accurate extrinsic calibration without requiring complex setups or dedicated calibration targets. Several approaches leverage innovative techniques such as deep learning, geometric constraints, and optimization algorithms to improve calibration accuracy and robustness. For instance, one method utilizes a single corner reflector and ROS to iteratively collect radar and camera data, then solves the perspective-n-point problem to derive the extrinsic calibration matrix, with RANSAC and Levenberg-Marquardt (LM) optimization enhancing robustness and accuracy \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2307.15264)\]. Another study proposes a deep learning-based approach that implicitly uses common features from radar and camera data to enable an online, targetless calibration method, again relying on RANSAC and LM for refinement \[[2](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/0e88fc24ae7694d0b1a2fe78322936fc568bc7d2)\]. Additionally, a method that employs a dual-perspective representation and selective fusion mechanism has been introduced to mitigate height uncertainty in radar data, demonstrating improved performance on the nuScenes dataset \[[12](http://arxiv.org/abs/1904.08743)\]. Furthermore, research has explored the use of pattern analysis and two-step optimization to address radar's sparse informativeness and elevation resolution issues, ensuring reliable estimation of all six extrinsic parameters \[[5](http://arxiv.org/abs/2309.00787)\]. These methods collectively highlight the growing trend toward automated, efficient, and robust calibration techniques that can operate in real-world conditions without the need for specialized equipment or controlled environments.

### <a id='category-sensor-fusion-techniques'></a>Sensor Fusion Techniques: 11 papers found.
The Related Works section of the paper focuses on radar-camera fusion techniques, which are critical for enhancing perception in autonomous driving systems. Existing methods have primarily addressed the challenges of feature misalignment and domain gaps between radar and camera data \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/2009.08428)\]. For instance, early approaches relied on forward or backward projection to generate bird's-eye view (BEV) features, but these methods often struggled with sparse BEV generation or depth ambiguity \[[10](http://arxiv.org/abs/2509.05785)\]. To overcome these limitations, recent works have proposed more sophisticated fusion strategies, such as the CRAB model, which uses backward projection and integrates radar data to reduce depth ambiguity \[[10](http://arxiv.org/abs/2509.05785)\]. Similarly, the CramNet framework introduces a ray-constrained cross-attention mechanism to resolve geometric correspondence issues between camera and radar features, improving robustness in case of sensor failures \[[11](http://arxiv.org/abs/2210.09267)\]. Another notable approach is RCAlign, which employs a Dual-Route Alignment (DRA) module based on contrastive learning to align and fuse radar and camera features more effectively \[[13](http://arxiv.org/abs/2504.16368)\]. Additionally, the RC-BEVFusion framework presents a modular network that operates on the BEV plane, enabling better integration of sparse radar data with dense image features \[[17](http://arxiv.org/abs/2305.15883)\]. These methods collectively highlight the ongoing efforts to improve the accuracy and reliability of radar-camera fusion, particularly in challenging environmental conditions \[[19](http://arxiv.org/abs/2304.00670)\]. The proposed method in this paper builds upon these advancements by introducing an online automatic geometric calibration approach, addressing the long-standing issue of data sparsity and measurement uncertainty in radar height data \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2307.15264)\].
