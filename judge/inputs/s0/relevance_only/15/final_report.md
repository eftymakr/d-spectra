## Related Works

Brain tumor segmentation remains a critical challenge in medical image analysis due to the complexity of tumor morphology, limited data availability, and the need for accurate and efficient segmentation methods \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2011.11052)\]. Recent advances in deep learning have significantly improved the performance of segmentation models, with U-Net-based architectures dominating the field \[[2](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/1bcdc3823fea644a507075b933912fa82167c887)\]. The U-Net architecture, originally designed for biomedical image segmentation, has been widely adapted for brain tumor segmentation tasks due to its symmetric encoder-decoder structure and skip connections \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/1812.01049)\]. However, training 3D U-Net models often requires extensive computational resources and large datasets, limiting their practical application in clinical settings \[[4](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/8689d964b3d1acacfa698c9d81314a9571819757)\].

Several studies have explored ways to improve the efficiency of 3D U-Net models by integrating pre-trained weights from other domains, such as ImageNet, to reduce training time and improve performance \[[5](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/237e15ea6558e45e544cf2efc68e675cf1c44547)\]. For instance, the EfficientNet model has been combined with U-Net to create hybrid architectures that achieve promising results on the BraTS datasets \[[6](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/281ede296764b95c329a7093e3b40b0b0c3ddee8)\]. Similarly, the use of transfer learning with pre-trained models like ResNet50, DenseNet169, and EfficientNet-B7 has been shown to enhance segmentation accuracy, particularly for challenging tumor sub-regions \[[7](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/e7485173b124c2b5a86f6cd00a871b9c1fa8a028)\]. These models often outperform traditional U-Net variants, achieving high Dice scores and Intersection Over Union (IoU) values \[[8](http://arxiv.org/abs/2504.13200)\].

To address the computational burden of 3D U-Net models, researchers have proposed lightweight architectures that reduce the number of parameters while maintaining segmentation accuracy. For example, the MBDRes-U-Net model integrates multibranch residual blocks and fused attention mechanisms to achieve high precision with reduced computational overhead \[[9](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/c4ccda0585b7f8040923ab86270d9b7ff2f4e03e)\]. Similarly, the use of depthwise separable convolutions has been explored to decrease the number of parameters in 3D U-Net models without compromising performance \[[10](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/6c9c65db8099038733cc0644265764c0570d4fbe)\]. These approaches align with the goal of developing more efficient models that can be deployed in resource-constrained environments \[[11](http://arxiv.org/abs/1907.00281)\].

Another line of research focuses on improving the generalization of segmentation models by incorporating additional information, such as lesion priors or multi-scale features, to enhance the model's ability to capture complex tumor structures \[[12](http://arxiv.org/abs/2402.07008)\]. For instance, the introduction of lesion priors through heatmaps has been shown to improve segmentation accuracy by providing additional context to the model \[[13](http://arxiv.org/abs/2210.13336)\]. Additionally, the use of attention mechanisms in the decoder of U-Net models has been explored to emphasize malignant tissues and reduce the impact of healthy tissues on segmentation accuracy \[[14](http://arxiv.org/abs/1709.02967)\].

We present a novel method for applying deep neural networks to the problem of glioma tissue segmentation that takes into account the structured nature of gliomas - edematous tissue surrounding mutually-exclusive regions of enhancing and non-enhancing tumor.
