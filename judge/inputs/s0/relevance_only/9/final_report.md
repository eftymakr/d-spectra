## Related Works

Quantitative susceptibility mapping (QSM) has emerged as a powerful tool for identifying and characterizing multiple sclerosis (MS) lesions, particularly those with paramagnetic rims (PRLs) \[[1](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/09b2a6040c15e8672169531fc76bba0b5a04296b)\]. These PRLs, characterized by a hyperintense rim due to iron deposition, are associated with more severe disease progression and greater disability \[[2](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/422983aa9f20d9e5eb7d17f618f8c4cf785971f8)\]. Traditional manual segmentation of PRLs is time-consuming and prone to inter- and intra-observer variability \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/2412.10492)\]. Automated methods have been proposed to address these challenges, including the use of machine learning and deep learning techniques \[[4](http://arxiv.org/abs/2208.02135)\].

Several studies have focused on the detection and segmentation of PRLs using various imaging modalities and algorithms. For instance, a fully automated method was developed to detect PRLs using T1-weighted, T2-FLAIR, and T2*-phase MRI sequences, achieving a high correlation with gold standard counts \[[5](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/45bf918158d3f779cb41b982fa9237682c11dd96)\]. Another study introduced QSM-RimDS, a U-Net-based method that improves PRL detection and provides rim segmentation, outperforming previous methods like QSM-RimNet \[[6](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/bb5b80dc4165d0ac3e28d1882b898633e84779c2)\]. These methods rely on the availability of lesion masks and often require manual annotation for training \[[7](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/b1d6aa6e4f34601f21fdc459d2aed99a464726fa)\].

The challenge of class imbalance between rim and non-rim lesions has been a significant hurdle in developing accurate classifiers. To address this, synthetic QSM images of PRLs have been generated to augment training data, improving classifier performance \[[8](http://arxiv.org/abs/2201.07463)\]. This approach is supported by studies that demonstrate the effectiveness of synthetic data in enhancing model generalization, especially in rare class scenarios \[[9](http://arxiv.org/abs/2104.10029)\]. Additionally, a multi-channel extension was proposed to generate accompanying contrasts and probabilistic segmentation maps, enabling more comprehensive lesion analysis \[[10](http://arxiv.org/abs/2312.16835)\].

Recent advancements in deep learning have also contributed to the field. For example, QSMRim-Net, a deep neural network that fuses lesion-level radiomic and convolutional features, has shown improved performance in detecting rim+ lesions on QSM \[[11](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/b4d08f780bb2ec7f8686ebc4890761ab8b1b4f5c)\]. Similarly, the use of attention mechanisms in U-Net architectures has been explored to enhance lesion segmentation accuracy \[[12](http://arxiv.org/abs/2008.02001)\]. [Snippet]: «Magnetic resonance (MR) imaging is often used to characterize and quantify multiple sclerosis (MS) lesions in the brain and spinal cord. The number and volume of lesions have been used to evaluate MS disease burden, to track the progression of the disease and to evaluate the effect of new pharmaceuticals in clinical trials. Accurate identification of MS lesions in MR images is extremely difficult due to variability in lesion location, size and shape in addition to anatomical variability between subjects. Since manual segmentation requires expert knowledge, is time consuming and is subject to intra- and inter-expert variability, many methods have been proposed to automatically segment lesions. The objective of this study was to carry out a systematic review of the literature to evaluate the state of the art in automated multiple sclerosis lesion segmentation. From 1240 hits found initially with PubMed and Google scholar, our selection criteria identified 80 papers that described an automatic lesion segmentation procedure applied to MS. Only 47 of these included quantitative validation with at least one realistic image. In this paper, we describe the complexity of lesion segmentation, classify the automatic MS lesion segmentation methods found, and review the validation methods applied in each of the papers reviewed. Although many segmentation solutions have been proposed, including some with promising results using MRI data obtained on small groups of patients, no single method is widely employed due to performance issues related to the high variability of MS lesion appearance and differences in image acquisition. The challenge remains to provide segmentation techniques that work in all cases regardless of the type of MS, duration of the disease, or MRI protocol, and this within a comprehensive, standardized validation framework. MS lesion segmentation remains an open problem.»

Recent advancements in the application of artificial intelligence to the segmentation of multiple sclerosis lesions have focused on improving the accuracy and robustness of automated methods. While traditional architectures like U-Net have been widely used, recent studies have explored the integration of temporal-aware features and attention mechanisms to enhance performance \[[1](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/09b2a6040c15e8672169531fc76bba0b5a04296b)\]. For instance, one study introduced an augmented U-Net architecture incorporating a convolutional long short-term memory layer and attention mechanisms, which significantly improved segmentation accuracy and generalization ability \[[1](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/09b2a6040c15e8672169531fc76bba0b5a04296b)\]. Such innovations highlight the potential of combining temporal features with attention mechanisms to better capture the spatial and temporal dynamics of lesion evolution, which is critical for accurate diagnosis and monitoring of multiple sclerosis progression.
