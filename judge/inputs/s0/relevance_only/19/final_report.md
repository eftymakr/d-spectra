## Related Works

Speech-driven 3D facial animation has been a focal point in recent research, with numerous approaches aiming to generate realistic and synchronized facial expressions from audio inputs. Early methods primarily relied on phoneme-based models, where phoneme sequences were used to drive facial animations \[[1](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/435b15745fb279db4ff8daf9d5facb9b21d58f3b)\]. However, these approaches often suffered from limited expressiveness and poor synchronization due to the lack of contextual and semantic information \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/2312.02781)\]. 

Recent advances have introduced deep learning techniques that leverage audio-visual data to improve the realism and accuracy of facial animations. For instance, Voice2Face employs a conditional Variational Autoencoder (cVAE) to generate face and tongue animations directly from speech, achieving significant improvements in perceived animation quality \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/2301.06059)\]. Similarly, PMMTalk introduces a framework that integrates visual and textual cues to enhance the accuracy of facial animation, demonstrating superior performance compared to state-of-the-art methods \[[4](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/e80391ba7a62bd8db2f7e7c529182fa916b7d918)\]. 

The importance of semantic information in speech-driven animation has also been emphasized. For example, the work in \[[5](http://arxiv.org/abs/2408.07005)\] proposes a method that disentangles content and style representations to provide more flexible control over facial animations. This aligns with the idea of using semantic features to improve the realism and diversity of generated animations. 

In addition, the integration of multi-modal data has shown promise in improving the quality of facial animations. For example, the work in \[[6](http://arxiv.org/abs/2404.12888)\] presents a joint audio-text model that leverages contextual text embeddings to synthesize realistic facial expressions while maintaining audio-lip synchronization. This approach addresses the limitations of purely audio-based models by incorporating semantic information from text \[[7](http://arxiv.org/abs/2503.09852)\]. 

Furthermore, the challenge of lip synchronization and the generalization across speakers has been a key focus in recent research. For example, the work in \[[8](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/ebf164c1d4f942e50d3515689b9c095350f207e2)\] introduces a two-stage transfer-learning framework that pretrains on large-scale pseudo-3D facial data and fine-tunes on high-fidelity 3D datasets, achieving state-of-the-art performance in lip synchronization and expression fidelity \[[9](http://arxiv.org/abs/2112.02214)\]. 

The issue of style modeling in speech-driven facial animation has also been explored. StyleSpeaker explicitly extracts speaking styles based on speaker characteristics, enabling more accurate and adaptable facial animations \[[10](http://arxiv.org/abs/2312.10877)\]. This approach addresses the limitations of prior methods that lacked fine-grained learning in style modeling \[[11](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/012c8325a4dbf2479462d685b48564a96717f0c1)\]. 

In summary, while existing methods have made significant strides in generating realistic 3D facial animations from speech, there remains a need for more robust and expressive models that can handle the complexities of speech and facial motion. In the realm of audio-visual synchronization techniques for speech-driven 3D facial animation, recent research has focused on improving the realism and accuracy of lip movements by integrating audio-visual perceptual losses and multimodal learning strategies \[[1](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/435b15745fb279db4ff8daf9d5facb9b21d58f3b)\]. Another approach leverages diffusion models, such as SAiD, which employs a lightweight Transformer-based U-Net with cross-modality alignment bias to enhance lip synchronization and generate more diverse lip movements \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/2312.02781)\]. Additionally, LinguaLinker utilizes a holistic diffusion-based framework to synchronize facial dynamics with multilingual audio inputs, offering nuanced control over mouth, eye, and head movements while maintaining compatibility between output video and input audio \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/2301.06059)\]. Furthermore, a procedural audio-driven method has been proposed to generate lip-synchronized speech animation for interactive virtual characters, incorporating emotional features of audio signals and a coarticulation model based on linguistic rules to improve realism \[[4](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/e80391ba7a62bd8db2f7e7c529182fa916b7d918)\]. These methods collectively highlight the growing emphasis on multimodal learning, perceptual losses, and diffusion-based techniques to achieve more natural and expressive facial animations.
