## Related Works

Fund allocation in financial domains has attracted significant research interest, with a growing emphasis on integrating time series forecasting with optimization strategies to improve decision-making. Traditional approaches such as prediction-only or predict-then-optimize (PnO) methods suffer from goal mismatch, where the forecasting and allocation objectives are not aligned \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2503.06928)\]. This issue is further compounded by the uncertainty introduced by state-of-the-art time series forecasting models, which can lead to unreliable allocation decisions \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/1809.04206)\]. To address these challenges, recent works have explored frameworks that combine forecasting and allocation in a unified manner, ensuring that the model is trained with objective alignment and uncertainty calibration \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/2308.11200)\].

One line of research focuses on improving the accuracy of time series forecasting for financial applications. For instance, FinTSBridge \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2503.06928)\] introduces a new evaluation suite that bridges advanced time series forecasting models with financial asset pricing, emphasizing the need for metrics like msIC and msIR to capture time series correlation. Similarly, the work on Temporal pattern attention for multivariate time series forecasting \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/1809.04206)\] proposes a novel attention mechanism to model long-term dependencies, achieving state-of-the-art performance on real-world tasks. These advancements highlight the importance of capturing both temporal and multivariate patterns in financial forecasting.

Another key area is the application of deep learning models for financial time series. The use of Convolutional Neural Networks (CNNs) and Transformers has gained traction, as they can effectively model both short-term and long-term dependencies \[[6](http://arxiv.org/abs/2304.04912)\]. For example, the work on Financial Time Series Forecasting using CNN and Transformer \[[6](http://arxiv.org/abs/2304.04912)\] demonstrates that combining these architectures can improve forecasting accuracy for stock price changes. Additionally, the introduction of models like PatchTST \[[15](http://arxiv.org/abs/2211.14730)\] and GridTST \[[16](http://arxiv.org/abs/2405.13810)\] has enabled more efficient and accurate long-term forecasting by leveraging patch-based and grid-based representations, respectively.

Transfer learning and reinforcement learning have also been explored to address the challenge of limited training data in financial forecasting. The paper on Realized Volatility Forecasting for New Issues and Spin-Offs using Multi-Source Transfer Learning \[[5](http://arxiv.org/abs/2503.12648)\] demonstrates that transfer learning can significantly improve forecasting performance for assets with limited historical data. Similarly, the work on On the Benefits of Transfer Learning and Reinforcement Learning for Electric Short-term Load Forecasting \[[7](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/e11d20dbd5103c75c58a5f5f241f7df663aabb2d)\] proposes a transfer learning approach that leverages historical data from other buildings to improve forecasting accuracy for new targets.
