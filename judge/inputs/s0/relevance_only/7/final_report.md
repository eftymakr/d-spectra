## Related Works

Large Language Models (LLMs) have been extensively studied for their ability to encode and exhibit behaviors such as refusal within their activation space \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2505.17306)\]. However, identifying and manipulating these behaviors remains a significant challenge. Existing approaches often rely on predefined refusal templates detectable in output tokens \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2505.17306)\], or require manual analysis of model responses \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2505.17306)\]. These methods are limited in their ability to generalize across different alignment conditions and may not account for the nuanced mechanisms underlying refusal behavior \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2505.17306)\].

Recent research has explored the idea that refusal behavior in LLMs is mediated by a single direction in activation space \[[4](http://arxiv.org/abs/2406.11717)\]. This finding has been further validated across multiple languages, showing that a single refusal direction extracted from one language can effectively bypass refusals in other languages \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2505.17306)\]. This cross-lingual transferability suggests that refusal behavior is governed by a universal mechanism that can be leveraged for multilingual safety defenses \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2505.17306)\].

To address the limitations of manual analysis and predefined templates, several works have proposed automated methods for identifying and steering refusal behavior. Refusal tokens have been introduced as a simple way to calibrate refusal rates in LLMs \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/2412.06748)\]. These tokens are prepended to model responses during training, allowing for fine-grained control over refusal behavior without the need for additional fine-tuning \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/2412.06748)\]. However, this approach still requires explicit tokenization and may not capture the full complexity of refusal mechanisms \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2505.17306)\].

### <a id='category-cosine-similarity-for-steering'></a>Cosine Similarity for Steering: 2 papers found.
The category "Cosine Similarity for Steering" explores the application of cosine similarity in steering large language models (LLMs) by identifying viable steering directions and target layers without relying on model outputs or predefined refusal templates. This approach is particularly relevant in scenarios where models exhibit refusal behaviors that are not easily detectable through conventional methods \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2505.17306)\]. The use of cosine similarity allows for an automated framework, such as COSMIC, which leverages the model's activation space to determine steering directions independently of output tokens \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2505.17306)\]. This method demonstrates robustness across various alignment conditions and can steer models toward safer behaviors with minimal increase in false refusals \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2505.17306)\]. The limitations of traditional cosine similarity in high-dimensional spaces, such as its dependency on vector dimensionality leading to biased outcomes, have been addressed by introducing the Dimension Insensitive Euclidean Metric (DIEM), which offers improved generalizability and interpretability \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/2412.06748)\]. Additionally, the development of Textual Spatial Cosine Similarity has enabled semantic-level document similarity detection using word placement information, offering a more nuanced approach to text analysis \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/2505.19333)\]. These advancements highlight the evolving role of cosine similarity in steering LLMs and its potential to enhance model alignment and safety.
