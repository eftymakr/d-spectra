## Related Works

Collaborative filtering (CF) has been a cornerstone in recommendation systems, leveraging user-item interactions to generate personalized recommendations \[[1](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/de2562febbdf3181834648d1fc679e2cb75aa973)\]. Recent advances in graph neural networks (GNNs) have significantly enhanced CF methods by enabling the modeling of high-order interactions through graph convolutional networks (GCNs) \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/2401.12590)\]. However, existing GCN-based CF models face several limitations. For instance, many models rely on linear transformations and nonlinear activation functions, which may not be optimal for recommendation tasks \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/2406.08827)\]. Moreover, the use of neighborhood aggregation often leads to oversmoothing and fails to capture fine-grained user preferences \[[4](http://arxiv.org/abs/1808.10523)\]. 

Several works have explored the use of graph signal processing (GSP) to improve CF. For example, PolyCF reframes CF as a graph signal processing problem, leveraging polynomial graph filters to capture spectral features across multiple eigenspaces \[[5](http://arxiv.org/abs/2007.03383)\]. Similarly, SpectralCF introduces a new spectral convolution operation to directly process the spectral domain of user-item bipartite graphs, thereby capturing deeper connections between users and items \[[6](http://arxiv.org/abs/2001.00267)\]. These methods highlight the potential of spectral graph filters in CF but often rely on complex training procedures or fail to address the issue of over-smoothing \[[7](http://arxiv.org/abs/1905.08108)\].

The importance of graph filters in recommendation has also been studied in the context of low-pass filtering. LCF proposes a low-pass collaborative filter to remove noise caused by exposure and quantization in observed data, improving the efficiency of graph convolution \[[8](http://arxiv.org/abs/2311.03316)\]. However, most existing methods are limited to low-pass filtering, ignoring the potential of middle and high-frequency components in graph signals \[[9](http://arxiv.org/abs/2307.04384)\]. AutoGCN addresses this by automatically adjusting the bandwidth of graph convolutional filters to capture the full spectrum of graph signals \[[10](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/219fd0434ce709f39d37df5452c811e7e9c4c9f9)\].

In addition to spectral graph filters, other approaches have focused on improving the efficiency and effectiveness of graph-based CF. LightGCN simplifies the design of GCN by removing unnecessary nonlinearities and feature transformations, resulting in a more efficient and effective model \[[11](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/a194dae103ce4593a8e15ecb1c1385cef3c60004)\]. Similarly, MDGCF enhances graph-based CF by mining neighborhood- and homogeneous-level dependencies to capture richer user-item interactions \[[12](http://arxiv.org/abs/2406.13996)\]. These methods demonstrate the importance of carefully designing graph convolutional layers to avoid over-smoothing and improve the expressiveness of embeddings \[[13](http://arxiv.org/abs/2002.02126)\].

Moreover, the issue of feature leakage in GNN-based recommendation systems has been addressed by EFLEC, which proposes an accurate removal algorithm to prevent label information from leaking into node embeddings \[[14](http://arxiv.org/abs/2006.15516)\]. Furthermore, BernNet introduces a novel framework for learning arbitrary graph spectral filters using Bernstein polynomial approximations, enabling the design of complex filters such as band-rejection and comb filters \[[5](http://arxiv.org/abs/2007.03383)\]. The work on edge-variant graph filters also advances distributed implementations by allowing nodes to weight their neighbors differently while maintaining linear aggregation, reducing communication rounds without sacrificing accuracy \[[7](http://arxiv.org/abs/1905.08108)\]. These studies collectively emphasize the potential of spectral graph filtering to overcome limitations in traditional collaborative filtering methods, such as oversimplified filters and restricted linear forms, by enabling more expressive and adaptive modeling of graph signals \[[6](http://arxiv.org/abs/2001.00267)\].

As for the second limitation, we propose an individualized graph filter (IGF) adapting to the different confidence levels of the user preference that interactions can reflect, which is proved to be able to generate arbitrary embeddings. ### <a id='category-automatic-graph-filter-adjustment'></a>Automatic Graph Filter Adjustment: 1 papers found.

[Snippet]: «Graph convolutional networks are becoming indispensable for deep learning from graph-structured data. Most of the existing graph convolutional networks share two big shortcomings. First, they are essentially low-pass filters, thus the potentially useful middle and high frequency band of graph signals are ignored. Second, the bandwidth of existing graph convolutional filters is fixed. Parameters of a graph convolutional filter only transform the graph inputs without changing the curvature of a graph convolutional filter function. In reality, we are uncertain about whether we should retain or cut off the frequency at a certain point unless we have expert domain knowledge. In this paper, we propose Automatic Graph Convolutional Networks (AutoGCN) to capture the full spectrum of graph signals and automatically update the bandwidth of graph convolutional filters. While it is based on graph spectral theory, our AutoGCN is also localized in space and has a spatial form. Experimental results show that AutoGCN achieves significant improvement over baseline methods which only work as low-pass filters.»

### <a id='category-over-smoothing-and-its-mitigation'></a>Over-smoothing and Its Mitigation: 1 papers found.
Context: 
	Document 1: [_citation_number]: «27»
[Snippet]: «Graph Convolutional Network (GCN) has achieved great success and has been applied in various fields including recommender systems. However, GCN still suffers from many issues such as training difficulties, over-smoothing, vulnerable to adversarial attacks, etc. Distinct from current GCN-based methods which simply employ GCN for recommendation, in this paper we are committed to build a robust GCN model for collaborative filtering. Firstly, we argue that recursively incorporating messages from different order neighborhood mixes distinct node messages indistinguishably, which increases the training difficulty; instead we choose to separately aggregate different order neighbor messages with a simple GCN model which has been shown effective; then we accumulate them together in a hierarchical way without introducing additional model parameters. Secondly, we propose a solution to alleviate over-smoothing by randomly dropping out neighbor messages at each layer, which also well prevents over-fitting and enhances the robustness. Extensive experiments on three real-world datasets demonstrate the effectiveness and robustness of our model.»
