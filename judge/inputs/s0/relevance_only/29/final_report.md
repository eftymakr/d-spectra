## Related Works

Maximum Inner Product Search (MIPS) has been extensively studied in recent years due to its critical role in recommendation systems, information retrieval, and high-dimensional data applications \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2503.06882)\]. Traditional approaches to MIPS either rely solely on inner product similarity, which can lead to local optima and redundant computations \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2503.06882)\], or reduce the problem to nearest neighbor search (NNS) under the Euclidean metric through space projection, which often results in topology destruction and information loss \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2503.06882)\]. These two paradigms, while distinct, are not inherently opposed. In fact, recent work has demonstrated that integrating inner product and Euclidean metrics in indexing and search algorithms can significantly enhance MIPS performance \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2503.06882)\].

Several methods have attempted to address the limitations of existing approaches. For instance, \[[2](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/25454f456c514219c9d6eeb42200ebbf378db9ca)\] introduces the Norm Adjusted Proximity Graph (NAPG), which adjusts the graph construction process to select more meaningful data points, thereby improving the efficiency of inner product search. Similarly, \[[3](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/386854741f828d30e25a5e01d75e3363d49cad1e)\] proposes Indexable Probabilistic Matrix Factorization (IPMF), which integrates the indexing process into the training phase, achieving faster retrieval times while maintaining accuracy. These methods highlight the potential of combining post-processing steps with model training to improve performance.

Graph-based methods have also gained traction for their empirical advantages in real-world MIPS tasks \[[2](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/25454f456c514219c9d6eeb42200ebbf378db9ca)\]. For example, \[[4](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/92fb60fde7aa7c94a22571dec416d4a795548f67)\] presents a reinforcement learning approach for navigating a proximity graph to solve MIPS, while \[[5](http://arxiv.org/abs/2201.09290)\] introduces the inner-product navigable small world graph (ip-NSW), which achieves an order of magnitude speedup over existing methods. However, ip-NSW suffers from a strong norm bias, where large-norm items dominate the search process \[[7](http://arxiv.org/abs/1909.13459)\]. Efforts to improve search efficiency and accuracy have also focused on adaptive and budgeted approaches. ### <a id='category-graph-based-mips-techniques'></a>Graph-Based MIPS Techniques: 5 papers found.
Graph-based methods have emerged as a powerful approach for solving Maximum Inner Product Search (MIPS) problems, offering significant improvements in efficiency and scalability compared to traditional methods \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2503.06882)\]\[[2](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/25454f456c514219c9d6eeb42200ebbf378db9ca)\]. These methods leverage proximity graphs to transform the MIPS problem into a graph traversal task, where the goal is to find the node with the maximum inner product with the query vector \[[3](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/386854741f828d30e25a5e01d75e3363d49cad1e)\]. This approach avoids the limitations of reducing MIPS to Nearest Neighbor Search (NNS) under the Euclidean metric, which often leads to topology distortion and information loss \[[2](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/25454f456c514219c9d6eeb42200ebbf378db9ca)\]. Several studies have proposed graph-based indexing strategies, such as the norm adjusted proximity graph (NAPG) \[[2](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/25454f456c514219c9d6eeb42200ebbf378db9ca)\], which incorporates adjusting factors to enhance the selection of meaningful data points during graph construction. Additionally, the inner-product navigable small world graph (ip-NSW) has been introduced to achieve a substantial speedup by focusing on large-norm items, which are more likely to yield high inner products \[[4](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/92fb60fde7aa7c94a22571dec416d4a795548f67)\]. To further refine these approaches, techniques like the Proximity Graph with Spherical Pathway (PSP) and Adaptive Early Termination (AET) have been proposed to address clustering issues and reduce redundant computations \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2503.06882)\]. Moreover, the integration of reinforcement learning and imitation learning has enabled the development of models that can automatically search the proximity graph for MIPS without requiring ground truth data \[[3](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/386854741f828d30e25a5e01d75e3363d49cad1e)\]. These advancements highlight the growing importance of graph-based methods in efficiently addressing the challenges of MIPS in high-dimensional spaces.

[Snippet]: «The Maximum Inner Product Search (MIPS) problem, prevalent in matrix factorization-based recommender systems, scales linearly with the number of objects to score. Recent work has shown that clever post-processing steps can turn the MIPS problem into a nearest neighbour one, allowing sublinear retrieval time either through Locality Sensitive Hashing or various tree structures that partition the Euclidian space. This work shows that instead of employing post-processing steps, substantially faster retrieval times can be achieved for the same accuracy when inference is not decoupled from the indexing process. By framing matrix factorization to be natively indexable, so that any solution is immediately sublinearly searchable, we use the machinery of Machine Learning to best learn such a solution. We introduce Indexable Probabilistic Matrix Factorization (IPMF) to shift the traditional post-processing complexity into the training phase of the model. Its inference procedure is based on Geodesic Monte Carlo, and adds minimal additional computational cost to standard Monte Carlo methods for matrix factorization. By coupling inference and indexing in this way, we achieve more than a 50% improvement in retrieval time against two state of the art methods, for a given level of accuracy in the recommendations of two large-scale recommender systems.»

### <a id='category-diversity-aware-mips-approaches'></a>Diversity-Aware MIPS Approaches: 4 papers found.
The category of Diversity-Aware MIPS Approaches addresses the limitations of traditional Maximum Inner Product Search (MIPS) by incorporating diversity into recommendation and search results. While standard MIPS focuses solely on maximizing inner product similarity, it often leads to skewed and non-diverse outputs, which can negatively impact user satisfaction \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2503.06882)\]. To overcome this, several studies propose variants that introduce diversity constraints. For instance, one approach introduces a categorical diversity-aware IPS problem, allowing users to select preferred categories and ensuring that the results are more varied \[[2](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/25454f456c514219c9d6eeb42200ebbf378db9ca)\]. Another study formulates a fair and independent IPS problem, where results are sampled randomly to ensure fairness and diversity, with an algorithm that runs in sub-linear time \[[3](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/386854741f828d30e25a5e01d75e3363d49cad1e)\]. Additionally, the diversity-aware k-MIPS problem enables users to control the degree of diversity in their recommendation lists through a parameter, though solving it exactly is NP-hard \[[4](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/92fb60fde7aa7c94a22571dec416d4a795548f67)\]. To address this, IP-Greedy is proposed, which incorporates early termination and skipping techniques to improve efficiency \[[5](http://arxiv.org/abs/2201.09290)\]. Furthermore, the D$k$MIPS problem integrates diversity objectives such as minimizing pairwise item similarities, offering a controllable trade-off between relevance and diversity \[[6](http://arxiv.org/abs/1610.03317)\]. These approaches collectively highlight the growing interest in diversifying search results while maintaining high relevance, addressing a critical gap in existing MIPS methods.

### <a id='category-adaptive-and-budgeted-search-methods'></a>Adaptive and Budgeted Search Methods: 3 papers found.
Adaptive and budgeted search methods for Maximum Inner Product Search (MIPS) have been actively explored to address the computational challenges in high-dimensional spaces \[[3](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/386854741f828d30e25a5e01d75e3363d49cad1e)\]. Recent works such as Greedy-MIPS \[[6](http://arxiv.org/abs/1610.03317)\] and BanditMIPS \[[9](http://arxiv.org/abs/2212.07551)\] introduce novel approaches that aim to balance search efficiency and quality by incorporating budget constraints. Greedy-MIPS achieves significant speedup by designing a greedy algorithm that inherently supports budgeted search, outperforming naive approaches on large-scale datasets \[[6](http://arxiv.org/abs/1610.03317)\]. BanditMIPS, on the other hand, leverages a randomized approach inspired by multi-armed bandits, reducing the dependency on dimensionality and enabling constant-time complexity \[[9](http://arxiv.org/abs/2212.07551)\]. Additionally, the paper \[[11](http://arxiv.org/abs/1908.08656)\] extends budgeted top-k MIPS by integrating advanced sampling techniques like wedge and diamond sampling, highlighting the trade-offs between efficiency and accuracy. These methods collectively demonstrate the importance of adaptive strategies in MIPS, offering flexibility in handling varying computational budgets while maintaining search performance \[[3](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/386854741f828d30e25a5e01d75e3363d49cad1e)\]\[[6](http://arxiv.org/abs/1610.03317)\]\[[9](http://arxiv.org/abs/2212.07551)\]\[[11](http://arxiv.org/abs/1908.08656)\].

[Snippet]: «Maximum inner product search (MIPS) over dense and sparse vectors have progressed independently in a bifurcated literature for decades; the latter is better known as top- $k$ retrieval in Information Retrieval. This duality exists because sparse and dense vectors serve different end goals. That is despite the fact that they are manifestations of the same mathematical problem. In this work, we ask if algorithms for dense vectors could be applied effectively to sparse vectors, particularly those that violate the assumptions underlying top- $k$ retrieval methods. We study clustering-based approximate MIPS where vectors are partitioned into clusters and only a fraction of clusters are searched during retrieval. We conduct a comprehensive analysis of dimensionality reduction for sparse vectors, and examine standard and spherical k-means for partitioning. Our experiments demonstrate that clustering-based retrieval serves as an efficient solution for sparse MIPS. As byproducts, we identify two research opportunities and explore their potential. First, we cast the clustering-based paradigm as dynamic pruning and turn that insight into a novel organization of the inverted index for approximate MIPS over general sparse vectors. Second, we offer a unified regime for MIPS over vectors that have dense and sparse subspaces, that is robust to query distributions.»

The brute-force approach to solving exact MIPS is computationally expensive, thus spurring recent development of novel indexes and pruning techniques for this task. In this paper we show that a hardware-efficient brute-force approach, blocked matrix multiply (BMM), can outperform the state-of-the-art MIPS solvers by over an order of magnitude, for some—but not all—inputs. Since no single solution offers the best runtime performance for all inputs, we introduce a new data-dependent optimizer, OPTIMUS, that selects online with minimal overhead the best MIPS solver for a given input.
