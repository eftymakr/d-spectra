## Related Works

Rehearsal-based methods have been widely studied in continual learning (CL) to mitigate catastrophic forgetting. These methods store a subset of past data and train the model on this data alongside new data during the current task \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2104.07446)\]. While concurrent rehearsal, where past and new data are trained together, is commonly used, its optimality remains unclear \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2104.07446)\]. Recent studies have explored alternative strategies, such as sequential rehearsal, where new data is trained first, followed by revisiting past data sequentially \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2104.07446)\]. This approach is inspired by human learning, where sequential revisiting of tasks helps mitigate forgetting \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2104.07446)\].

Theoretical analysis of rehearsal-based CL in overparameterized linear models has shown that sequential rehearsal performs better when tasks are less similar \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2104.07446)\]. This insight motivates the development of hybrid rehearsal strategies that combine concurrent and sequential approaches \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2104.07446)\]. For instance, the Hybrid Rehearsal method trains similar tasks concurrently and revisits dissimilar tasks sequentially \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2104.07446)\]. This approach aims to balance the benefits of both strategies while minimizing the risk of catastrophic forgetting \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2104.07446)\].

Several studies have investigated the effectiveness of rehearsal in mitigating catastrophic forgetting. For example, \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/2306.17091)\] highlights that models trained sequentially with rehearsal tend to stay in the same low-loss region after a task has finished, but are at risk of overfitting on its sample memory, hence harming generalization. This suggests that rehearsal can lead to both benefits and drawbacks depending on the task similarity and the model's generalization ability \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/2306.17091)\]. Similarly, \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/2302.05836)\] discusses how the effectiveness of rehearsal can be influenced by task similarity and task ordering, and how these factors affect forgetting and generalization error \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/2302.05836)\].

### <a id='category-rehearsal-based-frameworks'></a>Rehearsal-Based Frameworks: 8 papers found.
Rehearsal-based frameworks in continual learning (CL) focus on mitigating catastrophic forgetting by storing and reusing a subset of past data during training. These methods typically rely on a memory buffer to retain informative samples from previous tasks, which are then replayed alongside new data to maintain model performance \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2104.07446)\]. The effectiveness of such approaches depends on how well the rehearsal memory captures the essential features of past tasks, often achieved through techniques like gradient matching or coreset selection \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/2302.05836)\]. Some studies highlight the importance of strategic memory management, such as reservoir sampling or adaptive scheduling, to ensure that the rehearsal data remains relevant and efficient \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/2306.17091)\]. Additionally, recent work explores the role of task similarity in determining the optimal rehearsal strategy, suggesting that sequential rehearsal may offer advantages when tasks are dissimilar \[[4](http://arxiv.org/abs/2203.14544)\]. Furthermore, frameworks like Robust Rehearsal incorporate concepts from neuroscience, such as memory re-consolidation, to enhance the stability of learned representations \[[5](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/8eaed8eafb25d5c41cf0fce53d276765ddded613)\]. The integration of Bayesian methods and posterior-replay techniques further advances the field by enabling models to revisit and update task-specific knowledge without requiring access to past data \[[6](http://arxiv.org/abs/1812.02464)\]. These approaches collectively demonstrate the potential of rehearsal-based strategies to improve CL performance while addressing the limitations of traditional methods \[[7](http://arxiv.org/abs/2103.07492)\].

### <a id='category-catastrophic-forgetting-mitigation'></a>Catastrophic Forgetting Mitigation: 16 papers found.
The category "Catastrophic Forgetting Mitigation" encompasses a wide range of approaches aimed at preventing models from forgetting previously learned tasks when learning new ones in a continual learning setting. Many studies focus on the challenges of catastrophic forgetting, particularly in deep learning models, where learning new tasks can overwrite previously acquired knowledge \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2104.07446)\]. To address this, researchers have proposed various strategies, including memory-based methods that store past data for replay \[[6](http://arxiv.org/abs/1812.02464)\], regularization techniques that constrain parameter updates to preserve prior knowledge \[[16](http://arxiv.org/abs/2012.12631)\], and architectural modifications that enable the model to retain and reuse previous knowledge \[[14](http://arxiv.org/abs/2208.14307)\]. Some approaches leverage pre-trained models and adapt them for continual learning by using random projectors or class-prototype accumulation to avoid forgetting \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/2306.17091)\]. Others introduce modular architectures that allow for the composition of atomic skills, enabling efficient learning on long streams of tasks \[[8](http://arxiv.org/abs/2307.02251)\]. Additionally, methods such as knowledge distillation and parameter regularization have been explored to achieve continual learning without requiring explicit rehearsal of past data \[[29](http://arxiv.org/abs/2203.17269)\]. These approaches highlight the diversity of strategies used to mitigate catastrophic forgetting, ranging from explicit memory storage to implicit regularization and architectural innovations. The effectiveness of these methods varies depending on the specific learning scenario, task similarity, and model design, as demonstrated in empirical evaluations across multiple benchmarks \[[15](http://arxiv.org/abs/2205.00329)\].
