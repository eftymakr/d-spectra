## Related Works

Zero-Knowledge Proofs (ZKPs) have emerged as a critical cryptographic primitive for privacy-preserving computation, enabling a prover to demonstrate the validity of a statement without revealing additional information \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2408.05890)\]. Despite their potential, the computational complexity of ZKP generation has hindered widespread adoption, particularly in applications requiring high throughput or low-latency verification \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2408.05890)\]. Recent efforts have focused on accelerating the key primitives of ZKP protocols, such as Number Theoretic Transform (NTT) and Multi-scalar Multiplication (MSM), using hardware accelerators like GPUs and ASICs \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2408.05890)\]. However, these approaches often face limitations, such as requiring trusted setup for each application or generating larger proofs with higher verification costs \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2408.05890)\].

Several works have explored hardware acceleration for ZKP generation. For instance, GZKP introduces a GPU-accelerated ZKP system that improves performance by optimizing memory access patterns and eliminating costly external shuffles \[[2](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/e149d93531d8c07cf43f6cae327a4795144d1304)\]. Similarly, cuZK presents a faster parallel MSM algorithm on GPUs, achieving nearly linear speedup over serial implementations \[[6](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/e4a4608e8e35379e63b9df5bda1b7599ee8ab9ac)\]. The work in \[[6](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/e4a4608e8e35379e63b9df5bda1b7599ee8ab9ac)\] also reduces CPU-GPU data transfer latency, further enhancing performance. Another GPU-based solution, PipeZK, employs a pipelined architecture with two subsystems to handle large polynomial computations and MSMs, achieving up to 10x speedup on cryptographic benchmarks \[[5](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/6ee84674b7c426849c3da56cb278c08eb7a903cf)\]. 

FPGA-based accelerators have also been proposed for ZKP. The if-ZKP architecture leverages Intel FPGAs to accelerate the MSM operation in zk-SNARKs, achieving 110x–150x speedup over software implementations for specific elliptic curves \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/2412.12481)\]. ReZK, a highly reconfigurable accelerator, focuses on NTT/INTT and MSM, supporting variable bit-widths and sizes, and enabling efficient computation for different elliptic curves \[[10](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/8b12309fd3c5a1851ff8b6d9cfff018811d08d93)\]. These accelerators demonstrate the potential of hardware solutions to reduce the computational overhead of ZKP generation.

Despite these advancements, existing accelerators often face challenges in scalability and flexibility. For example, some systems require a trusted setup, limiting their use in dynamic or decentralized environments \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2408.05890)\]. Others generate larger proofs, increasing verification costs and making them unsuitable for applications with strict constraints \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2408.05890)\]. These limitations highlight the need for more efficient and flexible ZKP accelerators that can support both one-time and universal setups while maintaining small proof sizes \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2408.05890)\].

Additionally, research has explored the theoretical foundations of ZKPs, emphasizing the importance of intractable problems in group theory for constructing secure and efficient protocols \[[2](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/e149d93531d8c07cf43f6cae327a4795144d1304)\]. The integration of ZKPs into real-world applications remains an active area of study, with ongoing efforts to enhance usability, reduce computational overhead, and expand their applicability across domains such as verifiable machine learning and electronic voting \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/2412.12481)\].
