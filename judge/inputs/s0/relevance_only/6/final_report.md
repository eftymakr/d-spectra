## Related Works

Narrative summarization, a critical task in natural language processing, involves condensing long-form texts such as books, movies, and TV scripts while preserving intricate plotlines, character interactions, and thematic coherence. Existing approaches, particularly those based on large language models (LLMs), often struggle with maintaining faithfulness and coherence, especially in long, complex narratives \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2504.09071)\]. Early efforts focused on improving summarization through plan-guided approaches, which attempted to reduce hallucinations by grounding summaries to the source text \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2504.09071)\]. However, these methods often failed to significantly improve summary quality or faithfulness, as plans themselves were prone to hallucinations \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2504.09071)\]. 

Recent work has explored the use of multi-agent frameworks to enhance the capabilities of LLMs in complex tasks, such as tool use and financial decision-making \[[4](http://arxiv.org/abs/2401.07324)\]. These frameworks decompose tasks into modular components—planner, caller, and summarizer—each implemented by a single LLM, enabling more effective task execution and reducing hallucination risks \[[4](http://arxiv.org/abs/2401.07324)\]. This modular approach has been further extended to collaborative systems, such as MetaGPT, which introduces structured coordination and modular outputs to enhance reliability in multi-agent settings \[[5](http://arxiv.org/abs/2308.00352)\]. 

In the medical domain, MedAgents has been proposed as a zero-shot framework that leverages multi-agent collaboration to improve reasoning and decision-making by simulating expert discussions and reaching consensus \[[7](http://arxiv.org/abs/2311.10537)\]. This approach has shown significant improvements in mining medical expertise and enhancing reasoning capabilities \[[7](http://arxiv.org/abs/2311.10537)\]. Similarly, FinCon, a multi-agent system tailored for financial tasks, incorporates conceptual verbal reinforcement and a manager-analyst communication hierarchy to improve decision-making accuracy and reduce communication costs \[[6](http://arxiv.org/abs/2407.06567)\]. 

The use of knowledge graphs has also been explored for narrative understanding, particularly in visual storytelling. Hierarchical knowledge graphs provide structured semantic understanding by organizing narrative content across panel, event, and macro-event levels, enabling interpretable symbolic reasoning \[[8](http://arxiv.org/abs/2506.10008)\]. This approach emphasizes transparency and structured inference, aligning with cognitive theories of event segmentation and visual storytelling \[[8](http://arxiv.org/abs/2506.10008)\]. 

For dialogue-based tasks, Dial2Desc introduces a new task where the goal is to generate concise descriptions of the object or action discussed in a conversation, rather than maintaining the natural flow of dialogue \[[12](http://arxiv.org/abs/1811.00185)\]. This method leverages neural attentive models to improve the accuracy of generated descriptions by capturing interactions between utterances \[[12](http://arxiv.org/abs/1811.00185)\]. 

In addition, the importance of structured communication in multi-agent reinforcement learning has been highlighted, with frameworks like Learning Structured Communication (LSC) enabling flexible and efficient communication topologies through hierarchical graph neural networks

### <a id='category-evaluation-metrics-for-summarization'></a>Evaluation Metrics for Summarization: 3 papers found.
The evaluation of summarization systems remains a critical area of research, with existing approaches facing several limitations. One major issue is the reliance on automatically collected datasets, which can introduce noise and underconstrained task conditions, thereby hindering model training and evaluation \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/1908.08960)\]. Additionally, current evaluation metrics often fail to align closely with human judgment, lacking considerations such as factual correctness and coherence \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/1908.08960)\]. These shortcomings are exacerbated by the tendency of models to overfit to layout biases present in training data, resulting in limited output diversity \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/1908.08960)\]. Transfer learning has been proposed as a solution to improve model performance by leveraging pre-trained models on large-scale tasks, which can then be fine-tuned for specific summarization tasks \[[10](http://arxiv.org/abs/1910.10683)\]. This approach has led to significant improvements in various NLP tasks, including summarization, by utilizing unified frameworks that convert problems into text-to-text formats \[[10](http://arxiv.org/abs/1910.10683)\]. Furthermore, neural machine translation techniques, which employ encoder-decoder architectures, have demonstrated the potential to enhance summarization by allowing models to dynamically attend to relevant parts of the input \[[18](http://arxiv.org/abs/1409.0473)\]. These advancements highlight the ongoing efforts to refine evaluation protocols and improve model generalization in summarization tasks.

### <a id='category-multi-agent-frameworks-for-llms'></a>Multi-Agent Frameworks for LLMs: 4 papers found.
Recent advancements in multi-agent frameworks for large language models (LLMs) have focused on enhancing task execution through modular decomposition and collaborative reasoning. These frameworks aim to overcome the limitations of single LLMs by breaking down complex tasks into specialized components such as planning, tool invocation, and summarization \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2504.09071)\]. For instance, one approach introduces a two-stage training paradigm where a backbone LLM is first fine-tuned on a broad dataset, followed by the instantiation of separate agents for specific sub-tasks, which are then fine-tuned individually \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/2504.07459)\]. Another framework, MetaGPT, incorporates structured workflows and modular outputs to reduce hallucination and improve coordination among agents, particularly in complex tasks like software engineering \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/1908.08960)\]. Additionally, multi-agent prompting strategies have been proposed to enhance reasoning and factual accuracy by allowing multiple LLMs to debate and refine responses iteratively \[[4](http://arxiv.org/abs/2401.07324)\]. These methods highlight the potential of multi-agent systems to improve performance in diverse applications, including narrative summarization, by leveraging specialized agents and structured collaboration.
