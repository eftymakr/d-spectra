However, these models often fail to capture the complexity of taxpayer heterogeneity and behavioral irrationality \[[15](http://arxiv.org/abs/2303.13994)\].

Traditional economic models often rely on mechanistic approaches and assume unbounded rationality, which can fail to capture the adaptive and heterogeneous behaviors of economic agents \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/1804.09284)\]. In contrast, ABM allows for the simulation of diverse agent types, including households, firms, and governments, with behaviors defined by simple rules that can lead to complex macro-level outcomes \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/2406.04151)\]. Furthermore, ABM has been used to explore the impact of government policies, such as tax credit distribution, on household behavior and social welfare \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2311.17252)\]. Additionally, the development of large multimodal agents (LMAs) demonstrates how LLMs can be extended beyond text-based interactions to handle complex, multimodal user queries, enhancing their applicability in diverse economic and social contexts \[[13](http://arxiv.org/abs/2402.15116)\]. These contributions collectively underscore the growing interest in leveraging LLMs to improve the adaptability, realism, and effectiveness of economic simulations and policy modeling.
