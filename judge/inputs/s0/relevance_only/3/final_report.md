## Related Works

Recent advancements in Text-to-Speech (TTS) systems have significantly improved speech naturalness, leading to increased demand for precise prosody control and mispronunciation correction \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/1909.10302)\]. Traditional approaches for prosody manipulation often rely on specialized modules or additional training, which limits their capacity for post-hoc adjustments \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/1909.10302)\]. Similarly, conventional mispronunciation correction methods depend on grapheme-to-phoneme dictionaries, making them less practical in low-resource settings \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/1909.10302)\]. This section reviews related works that address these challenges.

Several studies have explored methods for prosody control in TTS systems. For example, \[[2](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/ffe8bc7ea78838e8f35b2bc518f33037374ae0ef)\] introduces a fully automatic method that makes the system aware of the prosody and enables sentence-wise speaking pace and expressiveness control on a continuous scale. This approach improves the overall quality and expressiveness of the synthesized speech. Additionally, \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/2102.08328)\] proposes a novel augmented attention mechanism that facilitates better pace control sensitivity and faster attention convergence. These methods demonstrate the importance of explicit prosody modeling for generating natural-sounding speech.

In the realm of prosody modeling, \[[4](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/fb2be38253698f5267bc0125a86f4f36e0a8c3a5)\] explores methods for evaluating the effectiveness of prosodic encodings in language models by cross-analyzing state-of-the-art TTS models with corpus data of natural speech. The study highlights the significance of correct assignments of major intonational events and phrasal pausing for TTS naturalness. \[[5](http://arxiv.org/abs/2309.05423)\] further advances this by proposing a two-stage automatic annotation pipeline that enhances prosodic information in latent representations and builds a multi-modal prosody annotator. These works underscore the challenges of accurately modeling prosody in TTS.

These methods highlight the limitations of traditional approaches and the need for more flexible and effective solutions.

Furthermore, methods like the one introduced in \[[10](http://arxiv.org/abs/2008.01490)\] utilize perceptual losses to incorporate utterance-level style information during training, enhancing expressiveness without requiring explicit prosody annotations. ### <a id='category-prosody-annotation-and-evaluation-methods'></a>Prosody Annotation and Evaluation Methods: 3 papers found.
The Related Works section for the paper's category on "Prosody Annotation and Evaluation Methods" highlights recent advancements in automatically annotating and evaluating prosodic features in Text-to-Speech (TTS) systems. Existing research has shown that prosodic accuracy is crucial for achieving natural-sounding speech, as errors at the prosodic level can significantly impact perceived quality \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/1909.10302)\]. Manual annotation of prosodic boundaries remains labor-intensive and inconsistent, prompting the development of automated methods \[[2](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/ffe8bc7ea78838e8f35b2bc518f33037374ae0ef)\]. A two-stage pipeline has been proposed to enhance prosodic information in latent representations through contrastive pretraining of speech-silence and word-punctuation pairs, followed by a multi-modal annotator that integrates text and speech data \[[2](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/ffe8bc7ea78838e8f35b2bc518f33037374ae0ef)\]. Additionally, neural text-speech models with pre-trained audio encoders have been used to automatically extract prosodic boundary labels from text-audio data, achieving results comparable to human annotations \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/2102.08328)\]. These methods not only improve the efficiency of prosody annotation but also enable more robust TTS systems by incorporating automatically derived prosodic information \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/2102.08328)\]. Furthermore, the evaluation of prosodic accuracy has been addressed through quantitative measures that capture the plausibility of intonational events and phrasal pausing, offering insights into the limitations of current deep learning approaches \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/1909.10302)\]. These contributions collectively advance the field by providing more reliable and scalable solutions for prosody annotation and evaluation in TTS.
