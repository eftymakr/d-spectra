## Related Works

Black-box testing has emerged as a critical technique for detecting isolation bugs in databases, ensuring that transaction histories adhere to prescribed isolation levels \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2504.02344)\]. Early approaches to black-box isolation checking, such as those based on randomized workloads, often suffered from high overhead due to the computational complexity of verifying strong isolation levels like serializability and snapshot isolation \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2504.02344)\]. This led to the development of more efficient methods, such as Mini-Transactions (MTs), which reduce overhead by executing shorter, faster transactions that still capture common isolation anomalies \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2504.02344)\]. These methods improved the scalability of isolation checking, but they often still faced limitations in handling large-scale workloads \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2504.02344)\].

Recent work has focused on improving the efficiency of black-box isolation checkers, particularly for weak isolation levels such as Read Committed (RC), Read Atomic (RA), and Causal Consistency (CC). Plume was introduced as the first efficient, complete black-box checker for weak isolation levels, leveraging vectors and tree clocks to accelerate checking \[[2](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/cbe191bda20907994d45bc835d20446968370add)\]. Similarly, PolySI was developed to efficiently check snapshot isolation (SI) and reproduce known anomalies while detecting novel violations in production databases \[[5](http://arxiv.org/abs/2301.07313)\]. These tools demonstrated significant improvements over earlier methods, but they often still had high polynomial complexity, limiting their applicability to large-scale workloads \[[5](http://arxiv.org/abs/2301.07313)\].

The complexity of isolation testing has also been a key area of study. It was shown that testing strong isolation levels is NP-complete, while weak isolation levels can be tested in polynomial time \[[16](http://arxiv.org/abs/2003.10554)\]. This theoretical result paved the way for the development of more scalable testing tools. However, existing testers still had large polynomial complexity, restricting their use to moderate-sized workloads \[[16](http://arxiv.org/abs/2003.10554)\]. This gap was addressed by the introduction of tools like Viper, which provides a sound and complete SI checker with fast performance \[[14](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/9f96a48d2bc0e77004b32960495685be6c301c63)\]. Viper's use of BC-polygraphs and heuristic pruning further improved its efficiency, making it suitable for larger workloads \[[14](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/9f96a48d2bc0e77004b32960495685be6c301c63)\].

In addition to improving performance, recent tools have also focused on providing better diagnostics and counterexamples to help developers understand and fix isolation bugs. For instance, Elle was designed to infer isolation anomalies from experimental observations and provide concise explanations of detected violations \[[16](http://arxiv.org/abs/2003.10554)\]. Similarly, IsoVista was introduced as the first black-box isolation checking system that ensures the absence of false positives and missed bugs while also visualizing violation scenarios \[[3](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/4f3081dbb68287f0d9d7bec3e7173e6c8ff035b9)\]. These advancements have made black-box testing more practical and effective for real-world database systems.

### <a id='category-efficient-isolation-checkers'></a>Efficient Isolation Checkers: 6 papers found.
Efficient isolation checkers have been a focal point in recent research, addressing the challenges of verifying database correctness under various isolation levels. Tools like MTC \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2504.02344)\] and Plume \[[2](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/cbe191bda20907994d45bc835d20446968370add)\] have introduced novel approaches to improve the efficiency of black-box isolation checking, particularly for strong and weak isolation levels. MTC leverages Mini-Transactions (MTs) to reduce overhead during history generation and verification, achieving linear or quadratic time complexity for strong isolation levels \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2504.02344)\]. Plume, on the other hand, focuses on weak isolation levels, utilizing vectors and tree clocks to accelerate checking and detect bugs in production databases \[[2](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/cbe191bda20907994d45bc835d20446968370add)\]. Similarly, PolySI \[[3](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/4f3081dbb68287f0d9d7bec3e7173e6c8ff035b9)\] presents a black-box checker for snapshot isolation (SI), using generalized polygraphs and SMT solvers to detect anomalies and provide counterexamples. The work on Emme \[[4](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/405ae0ebf74cfbf5f2edec62e2966827dcdc9bef)\] introduces a white-box approach that efficiently checks isolation levels by recovering version certificates and using expected serialization orders, significantly improving performance over existing checkers. Chronos \[[5](http://arxiv.org/abs/2301.07313)\] and Aion \[[5](http://arxiv.org/abs/2301.07313)\] extend timestamp-based methods to online isolation checking, enabling real-time validation of SI and SER. Viper \[[6](http://arxiv.org/abs/2303.12606)\] further enhances SI checking with BC-polygraphs and heuristic pruning, offering sound and complete verification with improved efficiency. These advancements collectively highlight the shift towards more scalable and efficient isolation checkers, addressing the limitations of traditional methods and enabling practical testing of large-scale databases.

[Snippet]: «Consistency guarantees among concurrently executing transactions in local- and distributed systems, commonly referred to as isolation levels, have been formalized in a number of models. Thus far, no model can reason about executable implementations of databases or local transaction libraries providing weak isolation levels. Weak isolation levels are characterized by being highly concurrent and, unlike their stronger counterpart serializability, they are not equivalent to the consistency guarantees provided by a transaction library implemented using a global lock. Industrial-strength databases almost exclusively implement weak isolation levels as their default level. This calls for formalism as numerous bugs violating isolation have been detected in these databases. In this paper, we formalize three weak isolation levels in separation logic, namely read uncommitted, read committed, and snapshot isolation. We define modular separation logic specifications that are independent of the underlying transaction library implementation. Historically, isolation levels have been specified using examples of executions between concurrent transactions that are not allowed to occur, and we demonstrate that our specifications correctly prohibit such examples. To show that our specifications are realizable, we formally verify that an executable implementation of a key-value database running the multi-version concurrency control algorithm from the original snapshot isolation paper satisfies our specification of snapshot isolation. Moreover, we prove implications between the specifications—snapshot isolation implies read committed and read committed implies read uncommitted—and thus the verification effort of the database serves as proof that all of our specifications are realizable. All results are mechanized in the Rocq proof assistant on top of the Iris separation logic framework.»
