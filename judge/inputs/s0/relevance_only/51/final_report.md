## Related Works

Federated unlearning has emerged as a critical research area in response to the "right to be forgotten" legislation, which mandates that users can request the removal of their data from machine learning models \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2412.19069)\]. This has spurred the development of unlearning techniques that aim to remove the influence of specific data contributions from federated learning (FL) models without requiring full retraining \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/2401.05146)\]. Several approaches have been proposed to address this challenge, each with varying degrees of efficiency, accuracy, and privacy guarantees.

Early work in federated unlearning, such as FedEraser, introduced a method that leverages historical parameter updates to reconstruct an unlearned model without retraining from scratch \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/2310.20448)\]. This approach significantly reduces the time required for unlearning while maintaining model efficacy \[[4](http://arxiv.org/abs/2310.19218)\]. However, FedEraser requires the server to retain historical updates, which can be storage-intensive \[[5](http://arxiv.org/abs/2406.03078)\]. Subsequent studies, such as those proposing the $^(u)$S2U method, have focused on improving efficiency and reducing storage overhead by using non-invasive verification techniques \[[6](http://arxiv.org/abs/2403.02437)\].

The importance of verifiable unlearning has also been emphasized, as unlearning effects can be canceled by contributions from other clients \[[7](http://arxiv.org/abs/2210.10958)\]. To address this, VeriFi was introduced as a framework that enables systematic analysis of federated unlearning and quantifies its effect through marking and checking mechanisms \[[8](http://arxiv.org/abs/2308.12502)\]. This approach allows clients to verify the unlearning effect independently, ensuring transparency and accountability \[[9](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/a52c3ec7325e81bd87fdf8d74bf9acfd1e1ffe5f)\].

In the context of federated online learning to rank (FOLTR), privacy is preserved by training ranking models across decentralized data sources without sharing raw data \[[10](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/39da2d48f2132b919405e3bb6df6ece44ac6e15a)\]. However, FOLTR faces challenges in unlearning due to the dynamic nature of user interactions and the need for continuous model updates \[[11](http://arxiv.org/abs/2306.02216)\]. Recent studies have explored how unlearning can be integrated into FOLTR to ensure compliance with privacy regulations \[[12](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/c78de63d34189a0d6b7a6407781415cbe024e9af)\].

Efforts to improve the efficiency of unlearning have led to the development of streamlined federated unlearning (SFU), which uses a multi-teacher system to guide the unlearned model while preserving performance on retained data \[[13](http://arxiv.org/abs/2012.13891)\]. SFU is computationally and storage-efficient, making it suitable for large-scale FL systems \[[14](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/a08f1c5c9f0bdb24f0dc75c0d969eb353899156b)\]. ### <a id='category-federated-learning-and-unlearning-overview'></a>Federated Learning and Unlearning Overview: 8 papers found.
Federated unlearning has emerged as a critical area of research to address the "right to be forgotten" in federated learning (FL) settings, ensuring that users can remove their data contributions from the global model without compromising privacy or model performance \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/2310.20448)\]. This concept extends from machine unlearning, which enables models to selectively eliminate identifiable information, and has been increasingly explored in FL due to its distributed nature and privacy-preserving characteristics \[[6](http://arxiv.org/abs/2403.02437)\]. However, the unique challenges of FL—such as interactivity, stochasticity, and heterogeneity—make direct application of centralized unlearning methods difficult \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/2310.20448)\]. Existing studies have proposed various federated unlearning schemes, including methods that aim to remove specific clients or data samples efficiently without full retraining \[[6](http://arxiv.org/abs/2403.02437)\]. These approaches often rely on techniques like reverse stochastic gradient ascent (SGA) and elastic weight consolidation (EWC) to maintain model utility while enabling unlearning \[[6](http://arxiv.org/abs/2403.02437)\]. Despite these advances, challenges remain, such as ensuring complete data erasure, preventing information leakage, and incentivizing users to remain engaged \[[4](http://arxiv.org/abs/2310.19218)\]\[[5](http://arxiv.org/abs/2406.03078)\]. Additionally, the effectiveness of unlearning strategies is often evaluated using single metrics, which may not fully capture the complexities of unlearning in FL \[[9](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/a52c3ec7325e81bd87fdf8d74bf9acfd1e1ffe5f)\]. Recent work has called for more comprehensive evaluation frameworks that consider multiple metrics to assess both under-unlearning and over-unlearning scenarios \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2412.19069)\]. Overall, federated unlearning remains an active and evolving field with significant implications for privacy, fairness, and model reliability in decentralized learning systems \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/2310.20448)\]\[[6](http://arxiv.org/abs/2403.02437)\].
