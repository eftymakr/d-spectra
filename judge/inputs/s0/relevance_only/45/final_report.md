## Related Works

Fine-grained emotion classification in app reviews has received increasing attention in recent years due to its potential to enhance user experience and improve software development practices. While sentiment analysis has been extensively studied, the ability to detect and classify more nuanced emotional states remains underexplored \[[1](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/7d0531aaaa4dbf4033da964289843e7812262702)\]. Early efforts in sentiment analysis focused on binary or ternary classifications (e.g., positive, negative, neutral), but recent work has shifted toward more detailed emotion categorization \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/2412.07906)\]. For instance, the GoEmotions dataset, which includes 58,000 Reddit comments labeled with 27 emotion categories, demonstrates the feasibility of fine-grained emotion analysis \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/2005.00547)\]. This dataset was created through manual annotation and validated using statistical methods to ensure reliability \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/2005.00547)\].

Several studies have explored the challenges of emotion annotation, particularly in the context of social media and user-generated content. One such study highlights the limitations of manual annotation, including high costs, subjectivity, and the difficulty of maintaining consistent labeling across annotators \[[4](http://arxiv.org/abs/1703.02256)\]. To address these issues, researchers have proposed using large language models (LLMs) to assist with emotion annotation tasks. For example, GPT-4 has been shown to achieve high agreement with human annotators in emotion perception tasks, suggesting its potential as a tool for semi-automated annotation \[[5](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/6fe8d604e8e19dc6ed738c8e58b7351aeeed515c)\]. However, differences between human and LLM annotations persist, underscoring the importance of human input in annotation studies \[[5](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/6fe8d604e8e19dc6ed738c8e58b7351aeeed515c)\].

This approach leverages topic modeling and sentiment analysis to provide a more granular understanding of user experiences \[[7](http://arxiv.org/abs/2107.12135)\].

The use of emotion definitions to improve fine-grained emotion prediction has also been explored. [Snippet]: «The most prominent tasks in emotion analysis are to assign emotions to texts and to understand how emotions manifest in language. An important observation for natural language processing is that emotions can be communicated implicitly by referring to events alone, appealing to an empathetic, intersubjective understanding of events, even without explicitly mentioning an emotion name. In psychology, the class of emotion theories known as appraisal theories aims at explaining the link between events and emotions. Appraisals can be formalized as variables that measure a cognitive evaluation by people living through an event that they consider relevant. They include the assessment if an event is novel, if the person considers themselves to be responsible, if it is in line with their own goals, and so forth. Such appraisals explain which emotions are developed based on an event, for example, that a novel situation can induce surprise or one with uncertain consequences could evoke fear. We analyze the suitability of appraisal theories for emotion analysis in text with the goal of understanding if appraisal concepts can reliably be reconstructed by annotators, if they can be predicted by text classifiers, and if appraisal concepts help to identify emotion categories. To achieve that, we compile a corpus by asking people to textually describe events that triggered particular emotions and to disclose their appraisals. Then, we ask readers to reconstruct emotions and appraisals from the text. This set-up allows us to measure if emotions and appraisals can be recovered purely from text and provides a human baseline to judge a model’s performance measures. Our comparison of text classification methods to human annotators shows that both can reliably detect emotions and appraisals with similar performance. Therefore, appraisals constitute an alternative computational emotion analysis paradigm and further improve the categorization of emotions in text with joint models.»
