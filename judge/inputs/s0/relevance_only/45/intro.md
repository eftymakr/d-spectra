## Related Works

Fine-grained emotion classification in app reviews has received increasing attention in recent years due to its potential to enhance user experience and improve software development practices. While sentiment analysis has been extensively studied, the ability to detect and classify more nuanced emotional states remains underexplored \[[1](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/7d0531aaaa4dbf4033da964289843e7812262702)\]. Early efforts in sentiment analysis focused on binary or ternary classifications (e.g., positive, negative, neutral), but recent work has shifted toward more detailed emotion categorization \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/2412.07906)\]. For instance, the GoEmotions dataset, which includes 58,000 Reddit comments labeled with 27 emotion categories, demonstrates the feasibility of fine-grained emotion analysis \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/2005.00547)\]. This dataset was created through manual annotation and validated using statistical methods to ensure reliability \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/2005.00547)\].

Several studies have explored the challenges of emotion annotation, particularly in the context of social media and user-generated content. One such study highlights the limitations of manual annotation, including high costs, subjectivity, and the difficulty of maintaining consistent labeling across annotators \[[4](http://arxiv.org/abs/1703.02256)\]. To address these issues, researchers have proposed using large language models (LLMs) to assist with emotion annotation tasks. For example, GPT-4 has been shown to achieve high agreement with human annotators in emotion perception tasks, suggesting its potential as a tool for semi-automated annotation \[[5](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/6fe8d604e8e19dc6ed738c8e58b7351aeeed515c)\]. However, differences between human and LLM annotations persist, underscoring the importance of human input in annotation studies \[[5](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/6fe8d604e8e19dc6ed738c8e58b7351aeeed515c)\].

In the context of app reviews, several studies have investigated the emotional sentiment expressed by users. For example, one study analyzed over seven million Apple AppStore reviews and found that emotional sentiment can provide valuable insights for software engineers \[[6](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/33c95f4d13958e65e676a9cd64bd4d8df0fdb44d)\]. Another study proposed a framework for extracting features from app reviews and analyzing the sentiment associated with each feature, enabling developers to better understand user feedback \[[7](http://arxiv.org/abs/2107.12135)\]. This approach leverages topic modeling and sentiment analysis to provide a more granular understanding of user experiences \[[7](http://arxiv.org/abs/2107.12135)\].

The use of emotion definitions to improve fine-grained emotion prediction has also been explored. A recent paper introduced a multi-task learning framework that models emotion definitions as an auxiliary task, leading to improved performance on the GoEmotions dataset \[[8](http://arxiv.org/abs/2003.07723)\]. This approach demonstrates the potential of incorporating semantic knowledge into emotion classification models \[[8](http://arxiv.org/abs/2003.07723)\].
