## Related Works

Recent research has focused on understanding how feature representations evolve across layers in large language models (LLMs), aiming to improve interpretability and robustness \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2411.09539)\]. While several studies have identified critical layers linked to specific functions or behaviors, these approaches often rely on data-dependent analyses of fine-tuned models, limiting their applicability to post-hoc settings \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2411.09539)\]. In contrast, our work introduces a data-oblivious approach to identify intrinsic critical layers in pre-fine-tuned LLMs by analyzing representation dynamics via Centered Kernel Alignment (CKA) \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2411.09539)\]. This method enables the identification of critical layers without requiring fine-tuning, offering a more generalizable framework \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2411.09539)\].

The importance of analyzing representation dynamics during fine-tuning has been explored in several studies. For instance, \[[6](http://arxiv.org/abs/2502.16722)\] investigates how fine-tuning affects the representation space of pre-trained models, showing that certain layers undergo significant shifts, which are closely correlated with performance changes. Similarly, \[[7](http://arxiv.org/abs/2402.16363)\] highlights the role of layer-specific adaptations in enhancing model performance on downstream tasks, emphasizing the need for targeted fine-tuning strategies. Our work builds on these findings by demonstrating that layers with significant representation shifts are also most affected during fine-tuning, a pattern that holds consistently across tasks for a given model \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2411.09539)\].

Further, our spectral analysis reveals that these shifts are driven by changes in the top principal components, which encode semantic transitions from rationales to conclusions \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2411.09539)\]. This insight aligns with prior work that has examined the role of principal components in capturing semantic information during model training \[[8](http://arxiv.org/abs/2210.05643)\]. Additionally, \[[9](http://arxiv.org/abs/2501.05032)\] explores how layer-specific adaptations influence the generalization of LLMs, reinforcing the importance of identifying critical layers for efficient fine-tuning.

Our findings have practical implications for two key applications: efficient domain adaptation and backdoor defense. In domain adaptation, we show that fine-tuning critical layers leads to greater loss reduction compared to non-critical layers \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2411.09539)\]. This is supported by \[[10](http://arxiv.org/abs/2402.11651)\], which demonstrates that targeted fine-tuning of specific layers can significantly improve performance on downstream tasks, particularly in data-limited settings. For backdoor defense, we find that freezing critical layers reduces attack success rates by up to 40% \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2411.09539)\]. This is consistent with \[[11](http://arxiv.org/abs/2408.15600)\], which shows that certain layers are more susceptible to adversarial attacks, suggesting that protecting these layers can enhance model security.

In summary, our work contributes to the growing body of research on representation dynamics in LLMs, offering a data-oblivious approach to identify critical layers

### <a id='category-performance-improvement-via-targeted-fine-tuning'></a>Performance Improvement via Targeted Fine-Tuning: 10 papers found.
The category "Performance Improvement via Targeted Fine-Tuning" encompasses a range of approaches aimed at enhancing the efficiency and effectiveness of fine-tuning large language models (LLMs) under constrained conditions. These methods include parameter-efficient fine-tuning techniques, such as prompt tuning and LoRA, which reduce training and deployment costs \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2411.09539)\]. Additionally, domain and cross-lingual adaptation strategies are explored to improve model performance in specialized or multilingual settings \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2411.09539)\]. Studies also highlight the importance of leveraging both successful and unsuccessful interaction trajectories during fine-tuning, as negative trajectories can provide valuable insights for improving model behavior in agent-like tasks \[[4](http://arxiv.org/abs/2308.07633)\]. Furthermore, model compression techniques like quantization, pruning, and knowledge distillation are discussed as means to reduce computational demands while maintaining performance \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/2503.04346)\]. Research also addresses the issue of catastrophic forgetting during fine-tuning, proposing methods such as weight merging to preserve model safety and alignment \[[5](http://arxiv.org/abs/2402.17193)\]. Knowledge-aware fine-tuning methods are introduced to enhance the accuracy and reliability of LLMs by improving their ability to distinguish between factual and non-factual information \[[6](http://arxiv.org/abs/2502.16722)\]. Human-inspired data ordering strategies are also evaluated for their impact on fine-tuning efficiency, showing that interleaved strategies can lead to significant accuracy gains \[[7](http://arxiv.org/abs/2402.16363)\]. Finally, the use of synthetic data generation pipelines is proposed to overcome the challenge of limited domain-specific annotated data, further improving embedding performance in semantic caching applications \[[9](http://arxiv.org/abs/2501.05032)\]. These contributions collectively highlight the diverse and evolving landscape of fine-tuning techniques aimed at optimizing LLM performance under resource constraints.

### <a id='category-layer-specific-adaptation-and-generalization'></a>Layer-Specific Adaptation and Generalization: 3 papers found.
Layer-specific adaptation and generalization have been extensively explored in recent research to improve the efficiency and effectiveness of fine-tuning large language models (LLMs). Several approaches have focused on identifying and modifying specific layers to achieve better performance while reducing computational overhead. For instance, post-training alignment techniques aim to enhance model usability by incorporating alignment control through an identity layer, allowing for interpolation between aligned and unaligned spaces during inference \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2411.09539)\]. Similarly, federated learning (FL) has been leveraged to enable distributed fine-tuning, where clients can selectively tune subsets of layers based on their local data and resources, with strategies guided by local gradients to optimize convergence \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/2503.04346)\]. Additionally, methods like HyPe have been proposed to address issues such as overfitting and representation collapse by perturbing hidden representations in Transformers layers, thereby improving the robustness and generalization of fine-tuned models \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/2303.18223)\]. These approaches highlight the importance of layer-specific adaptation in achieving efficient and effective model fine-tuning, while also emphasizing the need for strategies that account for client heterogeneity and training dynamics in distributed settings.

### <a id='category-fine-tuning-impact-on-representation-space'></a>Fine-Tuning Impact on Representation Space: 3 papers found.
The category "Fine-Tuning Impact on Representation Space" explores how fine-tuning affects the internal representation dynamics of large language models. Research indicates that fine-tuning pre-trained models, even with limited data, can lead to significant changes in the representation space, often aligning with kernel-based dynamics \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2411.09539)\]. This phenomenon is supported by studies showing that formulating downstream tasks as masked word prediction problems through prompting can induce kernel-based dynamics during fine-tuning \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2411.09539)\]. Additionally, the impact of fine-tuning on representation space is closely tied to the effectiveness of parameter-efficient subspace-based methods, which leverage the observed kernel-like behavior to achieve successful fine-tuning with minimal parameter updates \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2411.09539)\]. Furthermore, the relationship between continuous pre-training and instruction fine-tuning is critical for maintaining up-to-date knowledge and instruction-following capabilities in large language models, highlighting the need for efficient strategies that minimize computational overhead while preserving model performance \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/2303.18223)\]. These findings underscore the importance of understanding how fine-tuning modifies representation spaces, offering insights into both theoretical and practical aspects of model adaptation \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2411.09539)\]\[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/2303.18223)\].
