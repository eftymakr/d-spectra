## Related Works

Differential privacy (DP) has been widely recognized as the de facto standard for privacy-preserving data analysis \[[16](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/770ad70607ce60d8c411916129a423b5aad45e93)\]. However, its implementation in distributed settings is vulnerable to malicious behaviors by servers, such as sampling noise from incorrect distributions or generating correlated noise while appearing to follow established protocols \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2406.18940)\]. This issue has been explored in several works that focus on enhancing the robustness of DP mechanisms against adversarial attacks. For instance, \[[4](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/375e25dfc061ec12c2c870a2177d7c2abaa5f36e)\] analyzes output poisoning attacks on LDP protocols, where malicious users can manipulate their output before sending it to the aggregator. To counteract such attacks, \[[6](http://arxiv.org/abs/2104.06569)\] proposes verifiable LDP protocols that allow the aggregator to verify the perturbation process and prevent output poisoning. These protocols typically rely on zero-knowledge proofs (ZKPs), which, while effective, introduce significant computational and communication overheads \[[4](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/375e25dfc061ec12c2c870a2177d7c2abaa5f36e)\]. 

In response to these challenges, \[[15](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/25b7fe2ac81189952c72c5b869a6be1c5fcf410c)\] introduces a framework for verifiable LDP that allows the data collector to verify the completeness of the randomization process performed by each user. This approach provides a stronger guarantee against output manipulation attacks without requiring additional assumptions about the users' behavior. Similarly, \[[17](http://arxiv.org/abs/1909.01917)\] presents a system called Cryptε that leverages cryptographic techniques to ensure DP guarantees even in the presence of untrusted servers. This system allows for the execution of DP programs on encrypted data, thereby mitigating the risk of malicious server behavior. However, such approaches often come with performance limitations due to the complexity of cryptographic operations \[[18](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/ac8be9c934d6ff9ae095ebde81c8d3a57f7b918d)\].

The shuffle model with LDP provides an additional layer of privacy, by disconnecting the link between clients and the aggregator. The category "Privacy-Utility Trade-offs in LDP" explores the challenges and solutions related to balancing privacy and data utility in Local Differential Privacy (LDP) mechanisms. Existing LDP protocols, while effective in protecting user privacy by perturbing data locally, often suffer from poor utility due to uniform privacy guarantees that do not account for varying user privacy requirements \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/2208.09011)\]. This issue is further exacerbated by the fact that LDP mechanisms typically apply the same level of noise to all data, which can lead to over-protection of some users and under-protection of others \[[10](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/e40f49994b0d5122e2c45ee04e38defb973a9d52)\]. To address these limitations, researchers have proposed personalized differential privacy (PDP) frameworks that allow users to specify their own privacy preferences, leading to more accurate and utility-preserving results \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/2208.09011)\]. Additionally, there is ongoing work on input-discriminative LDP (ID-LDP) that tailors privacy protection based on the sensitivity of individual data inputs, improving both privacy and utility \[[8](http://arxiv.org/abs/1911.01402)\]. Furthermore, the problem of collecting multidimensional data under LDP remains underexplored, with recent studies focusing on improving the accuracy of mean and frequency estimation for numeric and categorical attributes \[[11](http://arxiv.org/abs/1907.00782)\]. These efforts highlight the growing interest in developing more flexible and efficient LDP mechanisms that can better accommodate the diverse needs of users while maintaining strong privacy guarantees \[[15](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/25b7fe2ac81189952c72c5b869a6be1c5fcf410c)\].

### <a id='category-verifiable-differential-privacy-frameworks'></a>Verifiable Differential Privacy Frameworks: 4 papers found.
Verifiable Differential Privacy Frameworks have emerged as a critical area of research to address the vulnerabilities in traditional differential privacy (DP) mechanisms, particularly in distributed settings where unfaithful execution of privacy-preserving protocols can compromise data integrity \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/2208.09011)\]. These frameworks aim to ensure that the randomness introduced by DP is faithfully generated and that the output is both private and reliable \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2406.18940)\]. This is achieved through interactive proofs, where a verifier can confirm that the output adheres to DP principles without learning the underlying randomness \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/2208.09011)\]. For instance, in local differential privacy (LDP), users perturb their data before submission, but this can be exploited by malicious actors to manipulate results \[[2](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/f1ba5a27cf487335fc54c0dd38d54ccdbdade063)\]. To counteract such attacks, verifiable LDP protocols have been proposed, which involve interactive frameworks where users and aggregators collaborate to ensure the perturbation process is verified \[[2](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/f1ba5a27cf487335fc54c0dd38d54ccdbdade063)\]. These protocols often rely on zero-knowledge proofs to provide additional guarantees, although they can introduce significant computational overhead \[[2](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/f1ba5a27cf487335fc54c0dd38d54ccdbdade063)\]. Recent advancements have focused on improving efficiency while maintaining strong security guarantees, such as through the use of commitment vectors and optimized perturbation mechanisms \[[4](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/375e25dfc061ec12c2c870a2177d7c2abaa5f36e)\]. These developments highlight the ongoing efforts to enhance the robustness of DP against adversarial behaviors while preserving utility and practicality \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/2208.09011)\].

### <a id='category-differential-privacy-foundations'></a>Differential Privacy Foundations: 4 papers found.
The category "Differential Privacy Foundations" encompasses foundational research on differential privacy (DP), focusing on its theoretical underpinnings, practical challenges, and alternative privacy definitions. Existing work highlights that DP quantifies privacy loss by analyzing noise injected into output statistics, ensuring that the inclusion of any individual in the dataset does not significantly alter query results \[[14](http://arxiv.org/abs/2204.01102)\]. However, traditional DP mechanisms often add excessive noise, especially for small datasets, leading to a loss of data utility \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2406.18940)\]. To address this, researchers have proposed per-instance DP (pDP), which measures privacy loss for each data instance and optimizes noise tailored to individual cases, demonstrating significant improvements in performance metrics like KL divergence \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2406.18940)\]. Additionally, alternative privacy definitions such as random differential privacy (RDP) have been introduced, offering more accurate results than standard DP when releasing histograms \[[20](http://arxiv.org/abs/1112.2680)\]. Furthermore, challenges in implementing DP in real-world scenarios, such as the assumption that each individual is associated with only one database record, have been addressed through scalable methods for differentially private aggregations \[[17](http://arxiv.org/abs/1909.01917)\]. These contributions collectively advance the understanding and practical application of DP, emphasizing both theoretical rigor and empirical validation.
