## Related Works

Deep-unrolling and plug-and-play (PnP) approaches have become the de-facto standard solvers for single-pixel imaging (SPI) inverse problems \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/1912.10557)\]. PnP approaches, a class of iterative algorithms where regularization is implicitly performed by an off-the-shelf deep denoiser, are flexible for varying compression ratios (CRs) but are limited in reconstruction accuracy and speed \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/1502.03167)\]. This limitation is due to the reliance on denoisers that may not be well-suited for the specific inverse problem at hand \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/1902.05399)\]. Conversely, unrolling approaches, a class of multi-stage neural networks where a truncated iterative optimization process is transformed into an end-to-end trainable network, typically achieve better accuracy with faster inference but require fine-tuning or even retraining when CR changes \[[4](http://arxiv.org/abs/1712.02862)\]. This makes them less flexible for applications where CR varies significantly \[[5](http://arxiv.org/abs/2402.12971)\].

Several works have explored the integration of PnP and unrolling methods to combine their strengths. For instance, \[[6](http://arxiv.org/abs/2109.14025)\] introduced a framework that integrates PnP with deep learning-based denoisers, demonstrating improved performance on image restoration tasks. Similarly, \[[7](http://arxiv.org/abs/1611.03679)\] proposed a method that unrolls iterative algorithms into neural networks, allowing for the incorporation of physical models into the learning process. This approach has been successfully applied to various imaging problems, including image deblurring \[[8](http://arxiv.org/abs/1809.04693)\] and magnetic resonance imaging \[[9](http://arxiv.org/abs/1508.02848)\]. ### <a id='category-unrolling-approaches-and-their-trade-offs'></a>Unrolling Approaches and Their Trade-offs: 13 papers found.
Unrolling approaches have gained significant traction in bridging the gap between traditional iterative algorithms and deep learning models, offering interpretable and efficient solutions for inverse problems in imaging and signal processing \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/1912.10557)\]. These methods involve unrolling the iterations of an optimization algorithm into a deep neural network, allowing the network to learn the parameters of the iterative process while maintaining the structure of the original algorithm \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/1902.05399)\]. This approach has been particularly effective in tasks such as image deblurring, where it has enabled the development of interpretable networks that outperform state-of-the-art methods \[[6](http://arxiv.org/abs/2109.14025)\]. Additionally, unrolling has been applied to problems like magnetic resonance imaging (MRI) and computed tomography, where incorporating physical models into the network enhances performance and reliability \[[15](http://arxiv.org/abs/2207.00698)\]. The flexibility of unrolling allows networks to handle varying compression ratios without requiring retraining, similar to plug-and-play (PnP) methods, while maintaining the accuracy and speed of unrolled networks \[[13](http://arxiv.org/abs/2003.10428)\]. Furthermore, the integration of numerical solvers with neural networks in unrolling setups has shown that non-differentiable training can yield substantial improvements over fully differentiable prediction setups \[[5](http://arxiv.org/abs/2402.12971)\]. These advancements highlight the potential of unrolling to combine the strengths of both model-based and data-driven approaches, leading to more robust and versatile solutions for a wide range of imaging and signal processing tasks.
