## Related Works

The integration of knowledge graphs (KGs) with large language models (LLMs) to enhance reasoning capabilities in claim verification has gained significant attention in recent research. While LLMs have demonstrated impressive reasoning abilities, they often struggle with hallucination and lack up-to-date knowledge \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2306.08302)\]. KGs, on the other hand, provide structured, semantically rich representations that are well-suited for reasoning tasks \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2306.08302)\]. However, most existing claim verification methods rely on unstructured text corpora, limiting their ability to effectively leverage KGs \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2306.08302)\]. 

Several studies have explored the use of LLMs for KG construction and reasoning. For instance, \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/2305.13168)\] presents an exhaustive evaluation of LLMs for KG construction and reasoning, showing that LLMs like GPT-4 excel in reasoning tasks but are less effective as few-shot information extractors. The authors propose AutoKG, a multi-agent-based approach that combines LLMs with external sources for KG construction and reasoning \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/2305.13168)\]. Similarly, \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/2104.06378)\] introduces QA-GNN, a model that addresses the challenges of question answering by combining LLMs and KGs through relevance scoring and joint reasoning \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/2104.06378)\].

The use of KGs to improve LLM reasoning has also been explored in the context of explainable claim verification. \[[4](http://arxiv.org/abs/2310.01061)\] proposes a novel method called reasoning on graphs (RoG), which synergizes LLMs with KGs to enable faithful and interpretable reasoning. RoG employs a planning-retrieval-reasoning framework, where LLMs generate relation paths grounded in KGs, and these paths are used to retrieve valid reasoning paths for faithful reasoning \[[4](http://arxiv.org/abs/2310.01061)\]. Additionally, \[[5](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/08cc15080d093fa776e9b3377bc96a67ea2693e4)\] introduces KGLens, a framework that efficiently measures the alignment between KGs and LLMs, helping to identify knowledge blind spots in LLMs \[[5](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/08cc15080d093fa776e9b3377bc96a67ea2693e4)\].

In the domain of claim verification, \[[6](http://arxiv.org/abs/1905.00537)\] presents FOLK, a method that leverages LLMs to verify complex claims and generate explanations without relying on human-annotated data. FOLK uses first-order logic (FOL) to translate claims into logical clauses and performs FOL-guided reasoning over knowledge-grounded question-and-answer pairs \[[6](http://arxiv.org/abs/1905.00537)\]. Similarly, \[[7](http://arxiv.org/abs/2310.05253)\] introduces Program-Guided Fact-Checking (ProgramFC), a model that decomposes complex claims into sub-tasks and executes them using a shared library of specialized functions, making the model both explanatory and data-efficient \[[7](http://arxiv.org/abs/2310.05253)\].

The integration of knowledge graphs (KGs) with large language models (LLMs) to enhance reasoning and factual accuracy has been a growing area of research, with multiple approaches focusing on synergizing the strengths of both systems \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2306.08302)\]. One key direction involves enhancing LLMs with KGs during pre-training or inference, allowing them to leverage structured knowledge for more accurate and interpretable reasoning \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2306.08302)\]. For instance, methods like QA-GNN use LMs to score the relevance of KG nodes and perform joint reasoning over both the question-answer context and the KG, improving performance on tasks like CommonsenseQA \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/2104.06378)\]. Similarly, RoG introduces a planning-retrieval-reasoning framework that generates reasoning paths from KGs and uses LLMs to conduct faithful reasoning, achieving state-of-the-art results on KG reasoning tasks \[[4](http://arxiv.org/abs/2310.01061)\]. Another approach, KEPLER, unifies knowledge embedding and pre-trained language representation by encoding textual entity descriptions with LLMs and jointly optimizing knowledge and language modeling objectives, leading to better performance on NLP tasks \[[8](http://arxiv.org/abs/1911.06136)\]. Additionally, methods like LinkQ simplify KG query construction by using LLMs to interpret natural language questions and convert them into structured queries, ensuring answers are grounded in the KG \[[14](http://arxiv.org/abs/2406.06621)\]. These efforts highlight the potential of combining LLMs with KGs to improve reasoning, reduce hallucinations, and enable more transparent and accurate inference \[[10](http://arxiv.org/abs/2308.09729)\].

Additionally, despite possessing strong reasoning abilities, modern LLMs struggle with multi-step modular pipelines and reasoning over KGs without adaptation. To address these challenges, we propose ClaimPKG, an end-to-end framework that seamlessly integrates LLM reasoning with structured knowledge from KGs. Specifically
