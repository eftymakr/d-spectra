## Related Works

Vision Transformers (ViTs) have gained significant traction in computer vision due to their ability to capture global context effectively \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2208.05163)\]. However, their deployment on hardware, particularly FPGAs, remains challenging due to the complexity of their arithmetic operations, such as Softmax and LayerNorm, which are sensitive to model accuracy and difficult to map onto efficient hardware with low precision \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2208.05163)\]. Existing methods often exploit parallelism in matrix multiplication operations while keeping these complex operations on the CPU, resulting in suboptimal performance due to communication overhead between the CPU and accelerator \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2208.05163)\]. This section reviews related works that address these challenges through hardware acceleration, quantization, and efficient design strategies.

Several studies have explored hardware acceleration for ViTs. Auto-ViT-Acc proposes an FPGA-aware automatic acceleration framework based on mixed-scheme quantization, achieving a 5.6x improvement in frame rate with minimal accuracy loss \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/2211.08110)\]. HeatViT introduces a hardware-efficient token pruning framework that reduces computation by up to 65.3% while maintaining high accuracy \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/2210.09573)\]. ViTCoD focuses on algorithm and accelerator co-design, achieving up to 235.3× speedup by reducing dominant data movement costs \[[4](http://arxiv.org/abs/2302.09108)\]. ViTA presents a configurable hardware accelerator for edge deployment, achieving nearly 90% hardware utilization efficiency and a power consumption of 0.88W \[[5](http://arxiv.org/abs/2205.11397)\]. SuperViT reduces computational costs while improving accuracy, achieving a 2× reduction in FLOPs with a 0.2% accuracy gain \[[6](http://arxiv.org/abs/2211.05109)\]. VITALITY unifies low-rank and sparse approximation for ViT acceleration, achieving 3× faster inference with comparable accuracy \[[7](http://arxiv.org/abs/2103.15358)\]. 

Efficient ViT architectures have also been explored. Multi-Scale Vision Longformer enhances ViT for high-resolution image encoding using linear complexity attention mechanisms \[[8](http://arxiv.org/abs/2101.11986)\]. Tokens-to-Token ViT improves training efficiency by structuring image tokens progressively, reducing parameter count and MACs by half \[[9](http://arxiv.org/abs/2103.14899)\]. ### <a id='category-vision-transformer-(vit)-hardware-acceleration-approaches'></a>Vision Transformer (ViT) Hardware Acceleration Approaches: 3 papers found.
Vision Transformer (ViT) hardware acceleration has seen significant advancements, particularly in addressing the computational and memory challenges inherent in their complex architecture. Existing approaches often rely on algorithmic quantization without hardware-specific optimizations, resulting in limited performance gains \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2208.05163)\]. More recently, researchers have explored algorithm-hardware co-design frameworks to enhance efficiency. For instance, VITALITY introduces a novel approach that combines low-rank and sparse components of attention mechanisms, enabling both algorithmic accuracy improvements and hardware-level efficiency through dedicated accelerators \[[6](http://arxiv.org/abs/2211.05109)\]. Similarly, ME-ViT focuses on minimizing memory traffic by implementing a single-load policy and optimizing on-chip storage, leading to substantial improvements in memory bandwidth and throughput \[[15](http://arxiv.org/abs/2402.09709)\]. These works highlight the growing emphasis on tailored hardware solutions that address the unique challenges of ViT inference, such as the quadratic cost of attention matrices and the need for efficient execution of complex operations like Softmax and LayerNorm. The proposed methods in this paper build upon these advancements by introducing a new arithmetic format, MXInt, to map all ViT operations onto FPGAs, achieving high accuracy and performance gains \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2208.05163)\].

### <a id='category-token-pruning-and-computation-reduction-strategies'></a>Token Pruning and Computation Reduction Strategies: 8 papers found.
The related works in the area of token pruning and computation reduction strategies for Vision Transformers (ViTs) highlight several approaches aimed at improving hardware efficiency and model performance. Researchers have focused on reducing the computational costs of ViTs by pruning non-informative tokens, leveraging sparse attention patterns, and optimizing hardware implementations. For instance, HeatViT introduces a hardware-efficient token pruning framework that dynamically identifies and consolidates non-informative tokens, achieving significant computation reduction while maintaining high accuracy \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2208.05163)\]. Similarly, ViTCoD proposes an algorithm and accelerator co-design framework that prunes and polarizes attention maps, reducing attention computations and data movements, with speedups up to 235.3× over general computing platforms \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/2211.08110)\]. SuperViT presents a novel training paradigm that enables models to handle varying computational costs and input sizes, achieving performance improvements with reduced FLOPs \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/2210.09573)\]. CrossViT introduces a dual-branch transformer that combines small and large patch tokens to enhance feature representation, with linear complexity for computation and memory \[[4](http://arxiv.org/abs/2302.09108)\]. SViTE explores end-to-end sparsity integration, dynamically extracting and training sparse subnetworks, leading to reduced computational costs and improved accuracy \[[5](http://arxiv.org/abs/2205.11397)\]. CF-ViT employs a coarse-to-fine inference strategy, reducing FLOPs by 53% while maintaining performance \[[6](http://arxiv.org/abs/2211.05109)\]. EViT reorganizes image tokens during inference, preserving attentive tokens and fusing inattentive ones, achieving 50% faster inference with minimal accuracy loss \[[7](http://arxiv.org/abs/2103.15358)\]. Dynamic-ViT automatically configures the number of tokens based on input characteristics, reducing redundant computations and improving efficiency \[[8](http://arxiv.org/abs/2101.11986)\]. These works collectively emphasize the importance of token pruning, sparse attention, and hardware-aware design in enhancing ViT performance and efficiency.

While achieving high performance, ViTs suffer from large model size and high computation complexity that hinders the deployment of them on edge devices. To achieve high throughput on hardware and preserve the model accuracy simultaneously, we propose VAQF, a framework that builds inference accelerators on FPGA platforms for quantized ViTs with binary weights and low-precision activations. Given the model structure and the desired frame rate, VAQF will automatically output the required quantization precision for activations as well as the optimized parameter settings of the accelerator that fulfill the hardware requirements. The implementations are developed with Vivado High-Level Synthesis (HLS) on the Xilinx ZCU102 FPGA board, and the evaluation results with the DeiT-base model indicate that a frame rate requirement of 24 frames per second (FPS) is satisfied with 8-bit activation quantization, and a target of 30 FPS is met with 6-bit activation quantization. To the best of our knowledge, this is the first time quantization has been incorporated into ViT acceleration on FPGAs with the help of a fully automatic framework to guide the quantization strategy on the software side and the accelerator implementations on the hardware side given the target frame rate. Very small compilation time cost is incurred compared with quantization training, and the generated accelerators show the capability of achieving real-time execution for state-of-the-art ViT models on FPGAs.»
