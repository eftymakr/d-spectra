## Related Works

Recent research has explored the internal mechanisms of large language models (LLMs), particularly focusing on how feature representations evolve across layers. A significant body of work has investigated the role of specific layers in performing particular functions or behaviors, often through data-dependent analyses of fine-tuned models \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2309.02144)\]. However, these approaches are typically limited to post-hoc settings, making them less useful for understanding the intrinsic properties of pre-fine-tuned models \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2309.02144)\]. In contrast, our work introduces a data-oblivious approach that identifies critical layers in pre-fine-tuned LLMs by analyzing representation dynamics using Centered Kernel Alignment (CKA) \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2309.02144)\]. This method allows for a more systematic and generalizable understanding of how layers contribute to model behavior.

Several studies have examined the impact of fine-tuning on LLMs, particularly in terms of how it affects model performance and robustness \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/1802.05365)\]. For example, \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/1409.1556)\] highlights the importance of fine-tuning strategies in improving zero-shot learning abilities, demonstrating that instruction tuning can significantly enhance model performance on unseen tasks. Similarly, \[[4](http://arxiv.org/abs/2109.01652)\] explores the scaling relationship between LLM capacity and mathematical reasoning performance, showing that pre-training loss is a better indicator of model performance than parameter count. These findings underscore the importance of understanding how fine-tuning impacts model behavior and performance.

The use of CKA for analyzing representation dynamics has also been explored in other contexts, such as in vision models \[[5](http://arxiv.org/abs/2308.01825)\]. In these studies, CKA has been used to compare the similarity of representations across layers, providing insights into how models process information. Our work extends this idea to LLMs, showing that layers with significant shifts in representation space are also those most affected during fine-tuning \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2309.02144)\]. This finding aligns with previous work that has demonstrated the importance of layer-specific analysis in understanding model behavior \[[6](http://arxiv.org/abs/2305.18290)\].

In addition to fine-tuning, the role of parameter-efficient tuning methods has been extensively studied \[[7](http://arxiv.org/abs/2402.11651)\]. These methods aim to improve model performance with minimal parameter updates, which is particularly useful in resource-constrained settings \[[7](http://arxiv.org/abs/2402.11651)\]. Our approach to identifying critical layers complements these efforts by providing a framework for determining which layers should be prioritized during fine-tuning or other adaptation tasks \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2309.02144)\].

Finally, the challenges of evaluating LLMs and ensuring fairness have also been addressed in the literature \[[8](http://arxiv.org/abs/2305.17926)\]. These studies highlight the need for robust evaluation methods that account for biases and ensure that models are evaluated fairly across different tasks and datasets \[[8](http://arxiv.org/abs/2305.17926)\].
