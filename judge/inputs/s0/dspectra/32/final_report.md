## Related Works

Recent research has explored the internal mechanisms of large language models (LLMs), particularly focusing on how feature representations evolve across layers. A significant body of work has investigated the role of specific layers in performing particular functions or behaviors, often through data-dependent analyses of fine-tuned models \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2309.02144)\]. However, these approaches are typically limited to post-hoc settings, making them less useful for understanding the intrinsic properties of pre-fine-tuned models \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2309.02144)\]. In contrast, our work introduces a data-oblivious approach that identifies critical layers in pre-fine-tuned LLMs by analyzing representation dynamics using Centered Kernel Alignment (CKA) \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2309.02144)\]. This method allows for a more systematic and generalizable understanding of how layers contribute to model behavior.

Several studies have examined the impact of fine-tuning on LLMs, particularly in terms of how it affects model performance and robustness \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/1802.05365)\]. For example, \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/1409.1556)\] highlights the importance of fine-tuning strategies in improving zero-shot learning abilities, demonstrating that instruction tuning can significantly enhance model performance on unseen tasks. Similarly, \[[4](http://arxiv.org/abs/2109.01652)\] explores the scaling relationship between LLM capacity and mathematical reasoning performance, showing that pre-training loss is a better indicator of model performance than parameter count. These findings underscore the importance of understanding how fine-tuning impacts model behavior and performance.

The use of CKA for analyzing representation dynamics has also been explored in other contexts, such as in vision models \[[5](http://arxiv.org/abs/2308.01825)\]. In these studies, CKA has been used to compare the similarity of representations across layers, providing insights into how models process information. Our work extends this idea to LLMs, showing that layers with significant shifts in representation space are also those most affected during fine-tuning \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2309.02144)\]. This finding aligns with previous work that has demonstrated the importance of layer-specific analysis in understanding model behavior \[[6](http://arxiv.org/abs/2305.18290)\].

In addition to fine-tuning, the role of parameter-efficient tuning methods has been extensively studied \[[7](http://arxiv.org/abs/2402.11651)\]. These methods aim to improve model performance with minimal parameter updates, which is particularly useful in resource-constrained settings \[[7](http://arxiv.org/abs/2402.11651)\]. Our approach to identifying critical layers complements these efforts by providing a framework for determining which layers should be prioritized during fine-tuning or other adaptation tasks \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2309.02144)\].

However, we find that the fine-tuned LLMs suffer from an \textit(Assessment Misalignment) problem, i.e., they frequently assign higher scores to subpar COTs, leading to potential limitations in their reasoning abilities. To address this problem, we introduce an \textit(Alignment Fine-Tuning (AFT)) paradigm, which involves three steps: 1) fine-tuning LLMs with COT training data; 2) generating multiple COT responses for each question, and categorizing them into positive and negative ones based on whether they achieve the correct answer; 3) calibrating the scores of positive and negative responses given by LLMs with a novel constraint alignment loss. Beyond just the binary positive and negative feedback, the constraint alignment loss can be seamlessly adapted to the ranking situations when ranking feedback is accessible. Furthermore, we also delve deeply into recent ranking-based alignment methods, such as DPO, RRHF, and PRO, and discover that the constraint, which has been overlooked by these approaches, is also crucial for their performance. Extensive experiments on four reasoning benchmarks with both binary and ranking feedback demonstrate the effectiveness of AFT.»

### <a id='category-parameter-efficient-tuning-strategies'></a>Parameter-Efficient Tuning Strategies: 4 papers found.
Parameter-efficient tuning strategies have gained significant attention in the field of NLP, particularly for fine-tuning large pre-trained language models on downstream tasks with limited labeled data \[[10](http://arxiv.org/abs/2012.13255)\]. These strategies aim to achieve strong performance by modifying only a small subset of parameters, rather than fine-tuning the entire model \[[14](http://arxiv.org/abs/2110.04366)\]. This approach is motivated by the observation that pre-trained models often exhibit a low intrinsic dimension, meaning that a low-dimensional reparameterization can be as effective as the full parameter space \[[10](http://arxiv.org/abs/2012.13255)\]. For instance, optimizing just 200 randomly projected parameters can achieve 90% of the full parameter performance on tasks like MRPC \[[10](http://arxiv.org/abs/2012.13255)\]. Additionally, the Neural Tangent Kernel (NTK) has been explored to understand the gradient dynamics during fine-tuning, showing that certain conditions allow for kernel-based dynamics, which supports the effectiveness of parameter-efficient methods \[[11](http://arxiv.org/abs/2210.05643)\]. Furthermore, studies have demonstrated that larger models tend to have lower intrinsic dimension after pre-training, contributing to their effectiveness in low-data settings \[[10](http://arxiv.org/abs/2012.13255)\]. Systematic experiments have also revealed that parameter-efficient tuning methods, such as prompt tuning and LoRA, exhibit different scaling behaviors depending on the task and data size, highlighting the need for careful selection of tuning strategies \[[13](http://arxiv.org/abs/2402.17193)\]. Overall, these findings underscore the importance of understanding the inductive biases and design principles of parameter-efficient tuning methods to improve their effectiveness and generalization \[[14](http://arxiv.org/abs/2110.04366)\].
