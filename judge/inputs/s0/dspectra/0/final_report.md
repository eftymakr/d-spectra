## Related Works

Economic inequality remains a critical challenge with significant implications for education, healthcare, and social stability \[[10](http://arxiv.org/abs/2311.17252)\]. Traditional systems, such as the U.S. federal income tax, aim to reduce inequality but often lack adaptability to dynamic economic conditions \[[10](http://arxiv.org/abs/2311.17252)\]. Recent approaches, such as the Saez Optimal Taxation model, attempt to address this by dynamically adjusting tax policies \[[10](http://arxiv.org/abs/2311.17252)\]. However, these models often fail to account for taxpayer heterogeneity and irrational behavior, limiting their effectiveness in real-world applications \[[10](http://arxiv.org/abs/2311.17252)\]. 

Agent-based modeling (ABM) has been increasingly used to simulate complex economic systems and policy interventions \[[7](http://arxiv.org/abs/2006.05048)\]. ABMs allow for the exploration of emergent behaviors and the evaluation of policy impacts through the interaction of heterogeneous agents \[[7](http://arxiv.org/abs/2006.05048)\]. For instance, studies have shown that ABMs can effectively model economic inequality and the effects of various policy interventions \[[9](http://arxiv.org/abs/1804.09284)\]. However, many existing ABMs rely on heuristic decision-making rules or regressions trained on historical data, which may not fully capture the adaptive and rational behavior of economic agents \[[7](http://arxiv.org/abs/2006.05048)\]. 

Recent advancements in large language models (LLMs) have opened new possibilities for integrating sophisticated reasoning and adaptive decision-making into economic models \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2309.02144)\]. LLMs have demonstrated the ability to generate reasoning traces and task-specific actions, enhancing their utility in complex decision-making scenarios \[[4](http://arxiv.org/abs/2210.03629)\]. The ReAct framework, which synergizes reasoning and acting in LLMs, has shown promise in improving task-solving trajectories and human interpretability \[[4](http://arxiv.org/abs/2210.03629)\]. Similarly, the Toolformer model enables LLMs to self-teach the use of external tools, achieving improved performance on a range of tasks without sacrificing core language modeling abilities \[[6](http://arxiv.org/abs/2302.04761)\]. 

The integration of LLMs into ABMs, as proposed in the TaxAgent model, represents a novel approach to designing adaptive tax policies \[[10](http://arxiv.org/abs/2311.17252)\]. By leveraging LLMs to iteratively optimize tax rates, TaxAgent balances equity and productivity, achieving superior equity-efficiency trade-offs compared to traditional models \[[10](http://arxiv.org/abs/2311.17252)\]. This approach aligns with broader efforts to enhance the capabilities of LLMs in economic modeling, such as the use of LLMs to simulate and predict economic outcomes \[[12](http://arxiv.org/abs/2405.11357)\].

### <a id='category-llm-reasoning-and-decision-making-frameworks'></a>LLM Reasoning and Decision-Making Frameworks: 2 papers found.
The category "LLM Reasoning and Decision-Making Frameworks" encompasses recent advancements in leveraging large language models (LLMs) for reasoning and decision-making tasks. Research has focused on enhancing LLMs' ability to perform structured reasoning, such as through chain-of-thought (COT) prompting, which improves their capacity to generate logical step-by-step explanations \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2309.02144)\]. However, challenges persist, including the Assessment Misalignment problem, where fine-tuned models may overestimate the quality of subpar COTs \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2309.02144)\]. To address this, the Alignment Fine-Tuning (AFT) paradigm was introduced, incorporating a constraint alignment loss to ensure positive responses outperform negative ones while keeping negative scores within a reasonable range \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2309.02144)\]. This approach not only improves reasoning accuracy but also adapts to ranking-based feedback scenarios. Concurrently, the ReAct framework has been proposed to integrate reasoning and action generation in an interleaved manner, enabling LLMs to dynamically update action plans using external knowledge sources, thereby enhancing interpretability and performance in tasks like question answering and interactive decision making \[[4](http://arxiv.org/abs/2210.03629)\]. These frameworks highlight the growing emphasis on combining reasoning with real-world task execution, addressing limitations such as hallucination and error propagation in traditional reasoning methods \[[4](http://arxiv.org/abs/2210.03629)\]. The development of such frameworks underscores the importance of aligning model outputs with human-like reasoning and decision-making processes.

### <a id='category-llm-limitations-in-text-understanding'></a>LLM Limitations in Text Understanding: 6 papers found.
Language models, while powerful, exhibit significant limitations in text understanding, particularly in tasks requiring nuanced comprehension or complex reasoning. For instance, they often struggle with character-level understanding, failing to reliably perform tasks that humans handle effortlessly \[[12](http://arxiv.org/abs/2405.11357)\]. Similarly, in mathematical reasoning, despite the use of large models, accuracy remains low, and scaling alone may not be sufficient to achieve strong performance \[[16](http://arxiv.org/abs/2103.03874)\]. These limitations are further compounded by issues in evaluation paradigms, where the order of context can manipulate the perceived quality of responses, highlighting a systematic bias in how models are assessed \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/2305.17926)\]. Additionally, while models show improvements with scale, their performance is still subpar compared to human raters, especially in tasks involving common-sense reasoning, social bias, and multi-step problem-solving \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2309.02144)\]. These findings underscore the need for more robust evaluation frameworks and a deeper understanding of the underlying mechanisms that govern model behavior \[[8](http://arxiv.org/abs/2112.11446)\].

Furthermore, LLMs are being explored for their ability to automate and improve the accuracy of tax software maintenance by translating regulatory updates into executable code, reducing the reliance on manual and error-prone processes \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/2305.17926)\]. These advancements suggest that LLMs can contribute to more dynamic and responsive economic systems, offering new tools for policy design and evaluation. Additionally, the use of LLMs in conjunction with agent-based modeling, such as in the TaxAgent framework, demonstrates how these models can simulate heterogeneous taxpayer behaviors and iteratively optimize tax policies to achieve better equity-efficiency trade-offs \[[11](http://arxiv.org/abs/2303.18223)\]. This aligns with broader efforts to develop more sophisticated methods for understanding and improving economic systems through the integration of AI technologies.

In contrast, reinforcement learning (RL) has been proposed as a more robust method for modeling agent decision-making, offering adaptive and behaviorally valid representations that can outperform conventional models \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2309.02144)\]. Recent studies have also emphasized the importance of incorporating diverse agent types and adaptive policies, such as tax credit distribution strategies, to better reflect real-world economic dynamics \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/2305.17926)\]. The integration of large language models (LLMs) with ABMs, as seen in the TaxAgent framework, further demonstrates the potential of combining advanced AI techniques with agent-based modeling to create more responsive and equitable fiscal policies \[[4](http://arxiv.org/abs/2210.03629)\]. These developments highlight the growing interest in using ABMs to address pressing economic challenges through more sophisticated and data-driven approaches.
