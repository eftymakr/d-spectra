## Related Works

The rapid growth of open-source machine learning (ML) resources has significantly impacted information retrieval (IR) research, particularly in the areas of resource management and recommendation systems. Existing platforms such as Hugging Face have become central hubs for sharing and collaborating on ML models and datasets \[[10](http://arxiv.org/abs/2405.13058)\]. However, these platforms often lack structured representations of resources, which limits advanced querying and analysis capabilities such as tracing model evolution and recommending relevant datasets \[[10](http://arxiv.org/abs/2405.13058)\]. This limitation has been identified in multiple studies that examine the Hugging Face ecosystem \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2311.13380)\]\[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/2305.11164)\]\[[10](http://arxiv.org/abs/2405.13058)\].

Several studies have explored the characteristics and challenges of ML model and dataset documentation on Hugging Face. For example, \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2311.13380)\] highlights the importance of structured documentation for improving the reliability and reproducibility of ML models. Similarly, \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/2305.11164)\] discusses the need for better documentation practices to enhance the usability of datasets and models. These studies emphasize the importance of metadata and structured representations in enabling more sophisticated analyses of ML resources \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2311.13380)\]\[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/2305.11164)\].

In addition to documentation, the maintenance and evolution of ML models on Hugging Face have been a focus of recent research. \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2311.13380)\] examines the maintenance status of models, classifying commit messages into categories such as corrective, perfective, and adaptive. This classification system helps in understanding how models evolve over time. Furthermore, \[[10](http://arxiv.org/abs/2405.13058)\] discusses the challenges of model reuse, including issues related to provenance, reproducibility, and portability. These challenges underscore the need for better infrastructure and standardization in model registries \[[10](http://arxiv.org/abs/2405.13058)\].

The carbon footprint of ML models on Hugging Face has also been a topic of interest. \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/2305.11164)\] analyzes the carbon emissions of 1,417 ML models and highlights the importance of energy-efficient model development. The study proposes classifications for categorizing models based on their carbon emission reporting practices and efficiency, which could help in promoting sustainable development within the ML community \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/2305.11164)\].

Moreover, the use of Hugging Face for empirical studies has been explored in \[[9](http://arxiv.org/abs/2307.14841)\], which evaluates the platform's suitability for conducting research on the development and evolution of AI-related projects. The study finds that Hugging Face offers a rich set of features and diverse data that can support empirical studies in software engineering and AI \[[9](http://arxiv.org/abs/2307.14841)\].

In summary, while Hugging Face has become a vital platform for ML research and development, there are significant opportunities for improvement in terms of structured representation, documentation, maintenance, and sustainability of ML resources. ### <a id='category-sustainability-and-carbon-footprint'></a>Sustainability and Carbon Footprint: 2 papers found.
The category "Sustainability and Carbon Footprint" is central to understanding the environmental impact of machine learning (ML) systems, particularly within platforms like Hugging Face. Existing research highlights that the rapid growth of ML models and datasets has significantly increased their carbon footprint, driven by larger model sizes and more complex training processes \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2311.13380)\]. Studies have shown that while there is growing awareness of the need for energy-efficient practices, there remains a lack of standardized methods for measuring and reporting carbon emissions associated with ML models \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2311.13380)\]. This issue is compounded by the fact that only a small proportion of models on Hugging Face provide detailed carbon footprint data, and even fewer offer actionable insights for optimization \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2311.13380)\]. Furthermore, research indicates that factors such as model size, dataset size, and application domain (e.g., natural language processing) are strongly correlated with carbon emissions, underscoring the importance of transparency and structured reporting \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2311.13380)\]. To address these challenges, scholars have proposed frameworks for categorizing models based on their carbon efficiency and reporting practices, aiming to promote sustainable development within the ML community \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2311.13380)\]. These efforts align with broader initiatives to enhance energy reporting and encourage the adoption of environmentally responsible practices in ML research and deployment \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/2305.11164)\].

Empirical studies on Hugging Face have focused on understanding the practices and challenges in the development, sharing, and reuse of machine learning models and datasets. Researchers have analyzed the platform's ecosystem, revealing that dataset documentation practices show marked heterogeneity, with popular datasets often having more complete documentation \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/2401.13822)\]. Additionally, the structure and content of dataset cards indicate a tendency to prioritize technical details over considerations for using the data, highlighting a gap in addressing ethical and social implications \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/2401.13822)\]. Studies have also examined the usage patterns of models on Hugging Face, finding that activity is highly imbalanced, with a small number of models accounting for the majority of downloads \[[10](http://arxiv.org/abs/2405.13058)\]. Furthermore, the reuse of pre-trained models has been explored, identifying challenges such as missing attributes, performance discrepancies, and model risks, which hinder effective reuse \[[12](http://arxiv.org/abs/2303.02552)\]. These findings underscore the need for improved documentation, licensing transparency, and infrastructure to support the growing open-source machine learning community \[[15](http://arxiv.org/abs/2502.04484)\].
