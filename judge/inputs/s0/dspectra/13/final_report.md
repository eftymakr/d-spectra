## Related Works

Long-tailed recognition remains a challenging problem in visual recognition due to the imbalance in class distribution, where a few head classes dominate the training data while tail classes are underrepresented \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/1910.09217)\]. Several approaches have been proposed to address this issue, primarily focusing on class-balancing strategies, such as loss re-weighting, data resampling, or transfer learning from head to tail classes \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/1910.09217)\]. However, most of these methods jointly learn representations and classifiers, which may not be optimal for long-tailed scenarios \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/1910.09217)\]. 

Recent work has explored the potential of contrastive learning for long-tailed recognition. Supervised Contrastive Learning (SCL) has shown promising performance on balanced data, but it struggles with long-tailed distributions due to its inability to form a regular simplex in the feature space \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/2207.09052)\]. This leads to imbalanced attraction and repulsion gradients between positive and negative pairs, which hinder the generalization of the model \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/2207.09052)\]. To address this, several methods have been proposed. For example, Balanced Contrastive Learning (BCL) introduces class-averaging and class-complement strategies to balance the gradient contributions and improve the formation of a regular simplex \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/2207.09052)\]. Similarly, Parametric Contrastive Learning (PaCo) introduces class-wise learnable centers to rebalance the optimization process and improve performance on long-tailed datasets \[[4](http://arxiv.org/abs/2107.12028)\]. 

Another line of research focuses on decoupling representation learning from classifier learning. The Decoupling Representation and Classifier (DRC) approach suggests that data imbalance may not be a significant issue in learning high-quality representations, and that adjusting the classifier alone can lead to strong long-tailed recognition performance \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/1910.09217)\]. This idea has been further explored in methods like Equalization Loss (EQL), which aims to re-balance gradients during training to improve performance on rare categories \[[14](http://arxiv.org/abs/2003.05176)\]. EQL v2, an improved version of EQL, introduces a gradient-guided reweighing mechanism that independently balances the training process for each category, leading to significant improvements on benchmarks like LVIS \[[5](http://arxiv.org/abs/2012.08548)\]. 

Additionally, methods like Subclass-Balancing Contrastive Learning (SBCL) and Latent Categories based long-tail Recognition (LCReg) have been proposed to address the limitations of existing approaches. Equalization loss (EQL) has been introduced as a method to address the long-tailed object detection problem by mitigating imbalanced gradients between positive and negative samples \[[14](http://arxiv.org/abs/2003.05176)\]. The core idea is that rare categories receive disproportionately discouraging gradients, hindering their learning \[[14](http://arxiv.org/abs/2003.05176)\]. To counteract this, EQL introduces a simple yet effective loss function that ignores gradients from rare categories, allowing the model to learn better discriminative features for these classes \[[14](http://arxiv.org/abs/2003.05176)\]. This approach has shown significant improvements, achieving AP gains of 4.1% and 4.8% for rare and common categories, respectively, on the LVIS benchmark \[[14](http://arxiv.org/abs/2003.05176)\]. Further refinements, such as EQL v2, have been proposed to enhance gradient reweighing by independently balancing the training process for each category \[[5](http://arxiv.org/abs/2012.08548)\]. EQL v2 outperforms the original EQL by about 4 points overall AP, with notable improvements on rare categories, and also surpasses decoupled training methods without additional tuning \[[5](http://arxiv.org/abs/2012.08548)\]. These developments highlight the importance of gradient reweighing in long-tailed recognition tasks \[[5](http://arxiv.org/abs/2012.08548)\]\[[14](http://arxiv.org/abs/2003.05176)\].
