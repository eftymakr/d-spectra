## Related Works

Time series forecasting has been extensively studied due to its critical role in various domains, including finance, energy, and traffic management. Early approaches relied on autoregressive models and Gaussian processes, which often struggled with capturing complex temporal dependencies and long-term patterns \[[5](http://arxiv.org/abs/1703.07015)\]. More recently, deep learning models such as Long-Short-Term Memory (LSTM) networks and Gated Recurrent Units (GRUs) have been proposed to better model sequential data \[[15](http://arxiv.org/abs/2403.07815)\]. However, these models still face challenges in capturing global dependencies and handling long-term forecasting tasks effectively \[[17](http://arxiv.org/abs/2310.06625)\].

Transformers have emerged as a powerful alternative due to their ability to model long-range dependencies through self-attention mechanisms \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/1907.00235)\]. However, the canonical Transformer architecture suffers from two major limitations: (1) locality-agnostic behavior, which makes the model insensitive to local context and prone to anomalies in time series, and (2) a quadratic memory complexity with respect to sequence length, which limits their applicability to long time series \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/1907.00235)\]. To address these issues, several works have proposed modifications to the Transformer architecture. For instance, LogSparse Transformer reduces the memory cost to $O(L(\log L)^2)$, enabling efficient modeling of long time series \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/1907.00235)\]. Similarly, Convolutional Self-Attention incorporates causal convolutions to better capture local context within the attention mechanism \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/1907.00235)\].

In addition to architectural improvements, recent works have focused on improving the interpretability and performance of time series forecasting models. The Temporal Fusion Transformer (TFT) combines recurrent layers for local processing with self-attention layers for learning long-term dependencies, enabling interpretable insights into temporal dynamics \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/1912.09363)\]. Similarly, the Temporal Pattern Attention (TPA) model introduces a novel attention mechanism that captures temporal patterns across multiple time steps, leading to state-of-the-art performance in multivariate forecasting tasks \[[4](http://arxiv.org/abs/1809.04206)\].

For financial time series forecasting, the DeepAR model leverages autoregressive recurrent networks to produce probabilistic forecasts, demonstrating significant accuracy improvements over classical methods \[[14](http://arxiv.org/abs/1704.04110)\]. More recently, the Chronos framework has been introduced as a pretrained probabilistic time series model, achieving competitive performance on both training and zero-shot tasks \[[15](http://arxiv.org/abs/2403.07815)\]. Additionally, the iTransformer model proposes an inverted Transformer architecture that improves forecasting accuracy for series with larger lookback windows by applying attention and feed-forward networks on inverted dimensions \[[17](http://arxiv.org/abs/2310.06625)\].

The proposed RTS-PnO framework builds upon these advancements by introducing a risk-aware approach that addresses the limitations of prediction-only and Predict-then-Optimize methods.

The Related Works section for the paper's category on Transformer Architectures for Time Series focuses on recent advancements in applying Transformer models to time series forecasting, particularly addressing challenges such as local context sensitivity, memory constraints, and non-stationarity. Early work highlighted the limitations of canonical Transformers, including their inability to effectively incorporate local context due to point-wise self-attention mechanisms \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/1907.00235)\], and their quadratic memory complexity, which hinders the modeling of long sequences \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/1907.00235)\]. To mitigate these issues, researchers proposed convolutional self-attention mechanisms to better capture local dependencies \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/1907.00235)\], and LogSparse Transformers that reduce memory usage to $O(L(\log L)^2)$ \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/1907.00235)\]. The Temporal Fusion Transformer (TFT) further advanced the field by integrating recurrent layers for local processing and interpretable self-attention for long-term dependencies, offering both high performance and interpretability \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/1912.09363)\]. Non-stationary Transformers addressed the issue of over-stationarization, where stationarization techniques can reduce the model's ability to capture real-world bursty events by removing inherent non-stationarity \[[6](http://arxiv.org/abs/2205.14415)\]. Meanwhile, iTransformer re-evaluated the role of Transformer components, proposing an inverted dimension approach to better handle multivariate correlations and arbitrary lookback windows \[[17](http://arxiv.org/abs/2310.06625)\]. These contributions collectively highlight the evolving strategies to enhance Transformer-based time series forecasting, emphasizing adaptability, efficiency, and robustness to real-world complexities.

### <a id='category-time-series-forecasting-models'></a>Time Series Forecasting Models: 8 papers found.
Time Series Forecasting Models have seen significant advancements with the integration of deep learning techniques, particularly in addressing the challenges of long-term and multivariate forecasting. Traditional RNN-based methods have faced limitations in long-term time-series forecasting (LTSF) due to the high number of recurrent iterations required, which affects both accuracy and computational efficiency \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/2211.14730)\]. To mitigate these issues, researchers have proposed innovative strategies such as segment-wise iterations and parallel multistep forecasting (PMF), leading to the development of SegRNN, which significantly reduces the number of iterations while improving forecast accuracy and inference speed \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/2211.14730)\]. Additionally, the use of attention mechanisms has been enhanced by incorporating frequency domain information to better capture temporal patterns, as seen in the TPA-LSTM model, which achieves state-of-the-art performance in multivariate forecasting tasks \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/1907.00235)\]. Furthermore, the introduction of diffusion models has opened new avenues for generative modeling in time series, with TSDiff demonstrating effectiveness in forecasting, refinement, and synthetic data generation without requiring task-specific training \[[21](http://arxiv.org/abs/2307.11494)\]. These models often leverage both time and frequency domain analyses, as exemplified by CoST, which uses contrastive learning to disentangle seasonal and trend components, leading to substantial improvements in forecasting accuracy \[[10](http://arxiv.org/abs/2202.01575)\]. The importance of capturing both intra-series and inter-series correlations has also been emphasized, with models like StemGNN utilizing spectral domain techniques to jointly model temporal dependencies and inter-series relationships \[[12](http://arxiv.org/abs/2103.07719)\]. Overall, these developments highlight a shift towards more efficient, accurate, and adaptable forecasting frameworks that can handle the complexities of real-world time series data.
