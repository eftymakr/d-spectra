## Related Works

Opinion mining has gained significant traction in recent years due to the increasing availability of user-generated content, particularly in app reviews \[[19](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/695886bf2b7bf90e98e98f630b26dabcdbe78247)\]. While most studies focus on sentiment polarity classification (positive, negative, neutral), the analysis of fine-grained emotions in app reviews remains underexplored \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2005.00547)\]. This gap is addressed in this work by adapting Plutchik’s emotion taxonomy to the context of app reviews and developing a structured annotation framework \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2005.00547)\]. 

Several studies have explored sentiment analysis in app reviews, highlighting the importance of user feedback for improving software quality and user satisfaction \[[7](http://arxiv.org/abs/1505.04657)\]. For instance, the MARK framework proposes a keyword-based approach for semi-automated review analysis, enabling developers to filter and summarize relevant feedback \[[7](http://arxiv.org/abs/1505.04657)\]. Similarly, AR-Miner presents a computational framework for mining informative reviews, grouping them using topic modeling and prioritizing them based on their relevance \[[12](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/ff921a209cc9e1c2120669d3d81e627ca5e53572)\]. These approaches, however, typically focus on sentiment polarity rather than fine-grained emotion classification \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2005.00547)\].

The GoEmotions dataset, which contains 58,000 manually annotated Reddit comments labeled for 27 emotion categories, represents one of the largest manually annotated datasets for fine-grained emotion analysis \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2005.00547)\]. This dataset has been used to evaluate the performance of BERT-based models, achieving an average F1-score of 0.46 across the proposed taxonomy \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2005.00547)\]. The dataset's high-quality annotations are validated through Principal Component Analysis, demonstrating its utility for transfer learning and generalization to other domains \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2005.00547)\].

In the context of app reviews, the work by \[[15](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/030ea3f854d688659500a949eac095cdba087682)\] explores fine-grained sentiment analysis, focusing on how users perceive specific features. This aligns with the broader goal of capturing nuanced emotional responses in user feedback \[[15](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/030ea3f854d688659500a949eac095cdba087682)\]. Additionally, the study by \[[18](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/6a6f1b8ef3eefaf8027a76134859b44886a5f795)\] introduces a large dataset for fine-grained emotion detection, achieving a new state-of-the-art on 24 emotion types with an average accuracy of 87.58% \[[18](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/6a6f1b8ef3eefaf8027a76134859b44886a5f795)\]. These datasets provide valuable resources for training and evaluating emotion classification models in app reviews.

The use of large language models (LLMs) for sentiment analysis has also been explored. A recent study evaluates the performance of LLMs on various sentiment analysis tasks, revealing that while they perform well on simpler tasks, they lag behind in more complex tasks requiring deeper understanding \[[20](http://arxiv.org/abs/2305.15005)\].
