## Related Works

De novo genome assembly has been a focal point of computational biology research, driven by the need to reconstruct genomes from short, error-prone reads without a reference. Early approaches such as SOAPdenovo \[[1](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/f8d1052e2c9d1cdd63093427ccc9169e848d777d)\] laid the groundwork for de novo assembly, but they faced limitations in handling large genomes and repeat regions. Subsequent improvements like SOAPdenovo2 \[[1](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/f8d1052e2c9d1cdd63093427ccc9169e848d777d)\] introduced memory-efficient algorithms and enhanced scaffold construction, significantly improving coverage and accuracy. However, these methods still struggled with memory-bound behavior and irregular data patterns \[[1](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/f8d1052e2c9d1cdd63093427ccc9169e848d777d)\]. 

The development of de Bruijn graph-based assemblers, such as those used in the SHARCGS algorithm \[[14](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/fbd59e17f72c3aedb3cd06af2e50a91526ccd81a)\], enabled faster and more accurate assembly of short reads, particularly for bacterial and eukaryotic genomes. These tools demonstrated high accuracy and scalability, with SHARCGS achieving N50 sizes greater than 20 kbp for Drosophila and Arabidopsis \[[14](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/fbd59e17f72c3aedb3cd06af2e50a91526ccd81a)\]. Despite these advancements, the computational demands of de novo assembly remain high, especially for large genomes. 

Recent work has focused on optimizing assembly pipelines for extreme-scale data. For instance, the HipMER assembler \[[19](http://arxiv.org/abs/1705.11147)\] leverages efficient parallelization to handle large genomes, such as the human genome and hexaploid wheat, on supercomputers. Similarly, the SWAP-Assembler \[[20](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/fe8d86bc5520b5049104ff58ee7e980982816ebf)\] introduced a scalable framework for processing massive datasets using thousands of cores, achieving high-quality contigs with good N50 sizes and low error rates. These tools highlight the importance of parallel processing and memory management in handling the computational complexity of de novo assembly.

The challenge of memory usage and computational efficiency has also been addressed in distributed-memory systems. The diBELLA 2D pipeline \[[5](http://arxiv.org/abs/2010.10055)\] introduced new algorithms for overlap detection and transitive reduction, achieving near-linear scaling and significant performance improvements. These methods are critical for handling the large datasets generated by next-generation sequencing technologies. 

Moreover, the use of probabilistic and heuristic methods has been explored to improve assembly accuracy. For example, the MinHash Alignment Process (MHAP) \[[15](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/618d82a202bb0408e388eba250c66c8a90700c81)\] enables the assembly of long reads with high accuracy by using locality-sensitive hashing. This approach has been successfully applied to assemble large eukaryotic genomes, including Saccharomyces cerevisiae and Drosophila melanogaster, with high continuity and accuracy. 

In addition to algorithmic improvements, the integration of linkage maps with de novo assemblies has been proposed to correct misassemblies and scaffold sequences \[[24](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/d200128ff098fc7da12747f7ce732d6f414ebe53)\]. This

### <a id='category-software-optimization-techniques'></a>Software Optimization Techniques: 2 papers found.
Software optimization techniques have been pivotal in improving the efficiency and accuracy of de novo genome assembly. Early assemblers like SOAPdenovo faced challenges in handling repeat regions, memory consumption, and coverage, which limited their performance on large and complex genomes \[[1](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/f8d1052e2c9d1cdd63093427ccc9169e848d777d)\]. To address these issues, subsequent tools such as SOAPdenovo2 introduced algorithmic improvements that reduced memory usage, enhanced scaffold construction, and improved accuracy, particularly in repeat resolution \[[1](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/f8d1052e2c9d1cdd63093427ccc9169e848d777d)\]. More recently, tools like minimap and miniasm have focused on optimizing the processing of long-read sequencing data, such as SMRT and ONT reads, by eliminating error correction stages and leveraging efficient mapping and assembly pipelines \[[21](http://arxiv.org/abs/1512.01801)\]. These optimizations enable faster assembly of large datasets while maintaining reasonable accuracy, even though consensus sequence error rates remain comparable to raw reads \[[21](http://arxiv.org/abs/1512.01801)\]. Such advancements highlight the importance of software-level innovations in overcoming computational bottlenecks and improving scalability in genome assembly workflows.

### <a id='category-probabilistic-and-heuristic-methods'></a>Probabilistic and Heuristic Methods: 7 papers found.
The category "Probabilistic and Heuristic Methods" encompasses a range of approaches aimed at improving the accuracy and efficiency of genome assembly, particularly in the context of de novo sequencing. These methods leverage probabilistic models to evaluate assembly quality and address the inherent challenges of assembling complex genomes from short reads \[[8](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/d7827222e6d751e13b1fe74b97ce413a037abfaf)\]. For instance, probabilistic measures of assembly quality, such as likelihood-based scores, have been introduced to enable objective comparisons between different assemblies without relying on reference genomes \[[8](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/d7827222e6d751e13b1fe74b97ce413a037abfaf)\]. This is particularly important given the limitations of traditional metrics like N50, which often fail to capture the full complexity of genomic variation \[[8](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/d7827222e6d751e13b1fe74b97ce413a037abfaf)\]. Additionally, heuristic approaches are widely used to handle the computational challenges of genome assembly, especially when dealing with large datasets and highly repetitive regions \[[4](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/f5cfb387a6bd8a1cb7bdf07562fadb9a351e37cd)\]. These methods often involve optimizing parameters within assemblers to improve contiguity and accuracy, even in the absence of a reference genome \[[8](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/d7827222e6d751e13b1fe74b97ce413a037abfaf)\]. Furthermore, probabilistic models are also applied to estimate genomic characteristics such as genome size, repeat content, and heterozygosity from sequencing data, which is crucial for designing effective sequencing strategies and improving the understanding of genome evolution \[[13](http://arxiv.org/abs/1308.2012)\]. The integration of probabilistic and heuristic methods has led to significant advancements in the field, enabling more efficient and accurate genome assembly, especially with the advent of long-read sequencing technologies \[[6](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/9cbccbe327e51dffd5c9b8b3199b6958d000b902)\]. These approaches continue to play a vital role in overcoming the computational and algorithmic challenges associated with de novo genome assembly \[[4](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/f5cfb387a6bd8a1cb7bdf07562fadb9a351e37cd)\].

### <a id='category-performance-and-scalability-metrics'></a>Performance and Scalability Metrics: 3 papers found.
Performance and scalability in genome assembly have been extensively studied, with a focus on improving efficiency and accuracy for large-scale datasets. Existing assemblers, such as Celera Assembler, have been adapted to handle long-read technologies like PacBio and Oxford Nanopore, which offer higher continuity but come with increased error rates \[[7](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/2b59fd18ada769ef76fb7fcc7382f063791abfab)\]. These challenges are addressed through advanced algorithms, such as adaptive overlapping strategies based on tf-idf weighted MinHash and sparse assembly graph construction, which help in managing complex repeats and haplotypes \[[7](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/2b59fd18ada769ef76fb7fcc7382f063791abfab)\]. Additionally, the choice of assembler depends on the type of data and the size of the genome, with string-based assemblers being suitable for shorter reads and graph-based assemblers for large datasets \[[16](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/7279acd9c61a8de019ea2d55d23124dfbb6b5269)\]. The Assemblathon competitions have further highlighted the variability in performance across different assemblers, emphasizing the need for tailored approaches for specific genome types \[[17](http://arxiv.org/abs/1301.5406)\]. These studies collectively underscore the ongoing efforts to enhance scalability and reduce computational demands in de novo assembly.
