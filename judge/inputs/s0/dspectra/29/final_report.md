## Related Works

Maximum Inner Product Search (MIPS) has been a central challenge in machine learning and information retrieval, particularly in high-dimensional data applications \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/1405.5869)\]. Existing approaches to MIPS typically fall into two categories: those that rely solely on inner product (IP) similarity, which can suffer from local optima and redundant computations, and those that reduce MIPS to nearest neighbor search under the Euclidean metric via space projection, which can lead to topology destruction and information loss \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/1405.5869)\]. However, recent studies have shown that these two paradigms are not inherently mutually exclusive \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/1405.5869)\]. Instead, integrating IP and Euclidean metrics in the design of indexing and search algorithms can significantly enhance MIPS performance \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/1405.5869)\].

Asymmetric LSH (ALSH) was introduced as the first provably sublinear time algorithm for approximate MIPS, leveraging asymmetric transformations to convert the problem into approximate near neighbor search \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/1509.01469)\]. This approach was later improved by proposing a different transformation that converted MIPS into approximate cosine similarity search using signed random projections \[[4](http://arxiv.org/abs/1410.5410)\]. These developments laid the foundation for efficient hashing-based MIPS solutions \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/1509.01469)\]\[[4](http://arxiv.org/abs/1410.5410)\].

Quantization-based methods have also emerged as a promising alternative to LSH for fast approximate MIPS. These methods quantize database vectors into multiple subspaces using learned codebooks, approximating inner products as the sum of inner products with subspace quantizers \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/1610.03317)\]. This approach avoids the need for augmenting vectors in a higher-dimensional space, and further improves accuracy when small sample queries are available during training \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/1610.03317)\].

Graph-based approaches have shown strong empirical advantages in real-world MIPS tasks. The inner-product navigable small world graph (ip-NSW) represents a state-of-the-art method for approximate MIPS, achieving an order of magnitude speedup over baselines \[[5](http://arxiv.org/abs/1909.13459)\]. However, its performance is closely tied to the norm bias in the MIPS problem, where large norm items are more likely to be the result of MIPS \[[5](http://arxiv.org/abs/1909.13459)\]. To address this, ip-NSW+ was proposed, which introduces an additional angular proximity graph to improve search efficiency and robustness \[[5](http://arxiv.org/abs/1909.13459)\].

Clustering-based methods have also been explored for approximate MIPS, with spherical $k$-means adapted to solve the problem \[[6](http://arxiv.org/abs/1507.05910)\]. These methods compare favorably with LSH-based approaches in terms of accuracy, particularly for large search spaces \[[6](http://arxiv.org/abs/1507.05910)\]. Similarly, norm-adjusted proximity graphs (NAPG) have been proposed to improve graph-based indexing for inner product search by adjusting factors estimated on sampled data \[[7](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/25454f456c514219c9d6eeb42200ebbf378db9ca)\].

### <a id='category-graph-based-indexing-techniques'></a>Graph-Based Indexing Techniques: 4 papers found.
The category "Graph-Based Indexing Techniques" encompasses several recent advancements in efficient Maximum Inner Product Search (MIPS) algorithms. These techniques leverage graph structures to approximate inner product searches, offering significant speedups over traditional methods. For instance, the inner-product navigable small world graph (ip-NSW) has been shown to achieve an order of magnitude speedup by exploiting the norm bias in MIPS, where large norm items dominate the search process \[[5](http://arxiv.org/abs/1909.13459)\]. This bias is further utilized in the ip-NSW+ algorithm, which introduces an angular proximity graph to enhance search efficiency by first narrowing down candidates through angular similarity before performing inner product search \[[5](http://arxiv.org/abs/1909.13459)\]. Similarly, the norm adjusted proximity graph (NAPG) improves graph-based indexing by incorporating adjusting factors estimated from sampled data, allowing for more meaningful connections during graph construction \[[7](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/25454f456c514219c9d6eeb42200ebbf378db9ca)\]. Additionally, the Inner Product Delaunay Graph (IPDG) transforms MIPS into a graph search problem, offering efficiency gains in retrieving relevant vectors for tasks such as recommendation systems and natural language processing \[[17](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/4d0dce74babcf393b5a4cbcb11c89ccca6a3b92b)\]. These methods collectively highlight the growing importance of graph-based indexing in addressing the challenges of MIPS, particularly in high-dimensional data applications. The theoretical and empirical connections between inner product and Euclidean metrics, as explored in the paper, further underscore the potential of hybrid approaches that combine different metrics for improved performance \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/1405.5869)\].

### <a id='category-mips-problem-definition-and-challenges'></a>MIPS Problem Definition and Challenges: 4 papers found.
The MIPS problem, which involves efficiently finding the best match for a query with respect to the inner product, has been a focal point in various applications such as recommender systems and high-dimensional similarity search \[[11](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/0b2790c2c1e23e5535421696e1ec763bbc651bbd)\]. While previous work has primarily focused on Euclidean distance or cosine similarity, the inner-product-based approach has received less attention, particularly in the general setting \[[9](http://arxiv.org/abs/1202.6101)\]. Recent studies have highlighted the computational challenges of exact Maximum Inner Product Search (MIPS), with brute-force methods being too slow for large-scale applications \[[11](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/0b2790c2c1e23e5535421696e1ec763bbc651bbd)\]. To address this, researchers have proposed novel indexing structures and pruning techniques, such as the blocked matrix multiply (BMM) approach, which leverages hardware efficiency to achieve significant speedups \[[11](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/0b2790c2c1e23e5535421696e1ec763bbc651bbd)\]. Additionally, the development of the MAX-IMUS algorithm has further improved performance by integrating efficient search space pruning \[[11](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/0b2790c2c1e23e5535421696e1ec763bbc651bbd)\]. Furthermore, the introduction of the OPTIMUS optimizer has enabled data-dependent selection of the most suitable MIPS solver, enhancing overall efficiency \[[11](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/0b2790c2c1e23e5535421696e1ec763bbc651bbd)\]. The diversity-aware k-MIPS problem has also emerged as a critical extension, aiming to balance recommendation diversity with inner product similarity \[[18](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/87bfc511e4ef4a8aeddad903b06036dbe7177625)\]. These advancements underscore the ongoing efforts to improve the efficiency and effectiveness of MIPS algorithms, addressing both theoretical and practical challenges in high-dimensional data settings \[[9](http://arxiv.org/abs/1202.6101)\]\[[11](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/0b2790c2c1e23e5535421696e1ec763bbc651bbd)\]\[[18](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/87bfc511e4ef4a8aeddad903b06036dbe7177625)\].
