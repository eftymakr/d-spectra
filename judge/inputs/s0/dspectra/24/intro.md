## Related Works

Diffusion models have become a cornerstone in generative AI, particularly for image and video synthesis due to their ability to iteratively refine outputs based on learned patterns \[[21](http://arxiv.org/abs/2105.05233)\]. However, the computational intensity of these models, especially diffusion transformers (DiT), poses challenges for deployment on resource-constrained devices \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2406.02540)\]. Prior work has focused on reducing the computational footprint of diffusion models through quantization, which involves reducing the bit-width of model parameters to lower memory usage and increase inference speed \[[25](http://arxiv.org/abs/2305.10657)\]. 

Several approaches have been proposed for quantizing diffusion models. For instance, Q-Diffusion introduces a novel PTQ method tailored to the unique multi-timestep pipeline of diffusion models, addressing the changing output distributions of noise estimation networks over time steps \[[5](http://arxiv.org/abs/2302.04304)\]. Similarly, PTQ4DiT identifies challenges in DiT quantization, such as salient channels with extreme magnitudes and temporal variability in activation distributions, and proposes solutions like Channel-wise Salience Balancing (CSB) and Spearmen's ρ-guided Salience Calibration (SSC) \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/2405.16005)\]. These methods aim to maintain high fidelity in image and video generation while enabling 4-bit quantization \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/2405.16005)\].

In the context of video generation, QVD introduces the first PTQ strategy specifically for video diffusion models, addressing the skewness of temporal features and inter-channel disparities in activation \[[20](http://arxiv.org/abs/2407.11585)\]. This work emphasizes the importance of preserving temporal consistency and spatial coherence in video generation, which is critical for maintaining visual quality \[[20](http://arxiv.org/abs/2407.11585)\]. Similarly, ViDiT-Q is designed for DiT models, achieving W8A8 and W4A8 quantization with minimal degradation in visual quality and metrics \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2406.02540)\]. The method also implements efficient GPU kernels to achieve significant memory savings and latency improvements \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2406.02540)\].

The challenge of maintaining spatiotemporal coherence during quantization has also been addressed in other works. For example, Q-DiT introduces automatic quantization granularity allocation and sample-wise dynamic activation quantization to handle the spatial and temporal variance in DiTs \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/2406.17343)\]. This approach improves the fidelity of image and video generation, particularly under challenging quantization settings like W4A8 \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/2406.17343)\]. Additionally, Temporal Dynamic Quantization for Diffusion Models dynamically adjusts the quantization interval based on time step information, improving output quality without increasing computational overhead during inference \[[9](http://arxiv.org/abs/2306.02316)\].
