## Related Works

Diffusion models have become a cornerstone in generative AI, particularly for image and video synthesis due to their ability to iteratively refine outputs based on learned patterns \[[21](http://arxiv.org/abs/2105.05233)\]. However, the computational intensity of these models, especially diffusion transformers (DiT), poses challenges for deployment on resource-constrained devices \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2406.02540)\]. Prior work has focused on reducing the computational footprint of diffusion models through quantization, which involves reducing the bit-width of model parameters to lower memory usage and increase inference speed \[[25](http://arxiv.org/abs/2305.10657)\]. 

Several approaches have been proposed for quantizing diffusion models. For instance, Q-Diffusion introduces a novel PTQ method tailored to the unique multi-timestep pipeline of diffusion models, addressing the changing output distributions of noise estimation networks over time steps \[[5](http://arxiv.org/abs/2302.04304)\]. Similarly, PTQ4DiT identifies challenges in DiT quantization, such as salient channels with extreme magnitudes and temporal variability in activation distributions, and proposes solutions like Channel-wise Salience Balancing (CSB) and Spearmen's ρ-guided Salience Calibration (SSC) \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/2405.16005)\]. These methods aim to maintain high fidelity in image and video generation while enabling 4-bit quantization \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/2405.16005)\].

In the context of video generation, QVD introduces the first PTQ strategy specifically for video diffusion models, addressing the skewness of temporal features and inter-channel disparities in activation \[[20](http://arxiv.org/abs/2407.11585)\]. This work emphasizes the importance of preserving temporal consistency and spatial coherence in video generation, which is critical for maintaining visual quality \[[20](http://arxiv.org/abs/2407.11585)\]. Similarly, ViDiT-Q is designed for DiT models, achieving W8A8 and W4A8 quantization with minimal degradation in visual quality and metrics \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2406.02540)\]. The method also implements efficient GPU kernels to achieve significant memory savings and latency improvements \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2406.02540)\].

The challenge of maintaining spatiotemporal coherence during quantization has also been addressed in other works. For example, Q-DiT introduces automatic quantization granularity allocation and sample-wise dynamic activation quantization to handle the spatial and temporal variance in DiTs \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/2406.17343)\]. ### <a id='category-quantization-techniques-for-diffusion-models'></a>Quantization Techniques for Diffusion Models: 9 papers found.
Quantization techniques for diffusion models have been actively researched to address the high computational and memory costs associated with large-scale diffusion transformers (DiTs) and video diffusion models (VDMs). Existing methods, such as post-training quantization (PTQ), face significant challenges when applied to DiTs and VDMs due to their unique architectural properties, including spatial and temporal variances in weights and activations \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2406.02540)\]\[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/2406.17343)\]. For instance, DiTs exhibit significant spatial variance in both weights and activations, along with temporal variance in activations, which complicates the quantization process \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/2406.17343)\]. Similarly, video diffusion models encounter challenges due to the integration of temporal features across all frame features, leading to pronounced skewness and inter-channel disparities \[[20](http://arxiv.org/abs/2407.11585)\]. To overcome these issues, researchers have proposed specialized quantization strategies, such as ViDiT-Q, which is tailored for DiT models and achieves W8A8 and W4A8 quantization with minimal degradation in visual quality \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2406.02540)\]. Other approaches, like Q-DiT, introduce techniques such as automatic quantization granularity allocation and sample-wise dynamic activation quantization to handle the variance in DiT models \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/2406.17343)\]. For video diffusion models, QVD introduces HTDQ and SCRI methods to address temporal and channel-specific quantization challenges, achieving near-lossless performance on W8A8 \[[20](http://arxiv.org/abs/2407.11585)\]. Additionally, PTQ4DiT addresses the challenges of salient channels and temporal activation variability in DiTs through techniques like Channel-wise Salience Balancing and Spearmen’s ρ-guided Salience Calibration \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/2405.16005)\]. These efforts highlight the ongoing focus on developing efficient and effective quantization methods that preserve the performance of diffusion models while reducing computational and memory demands \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2406.02540)\]\[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/2406.17343)\]\[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/2405.16005)\]\[[20](http://arxiv.org/abs/2407.11585)\].

### <a id='category-efficiency-improvements-in-quantized-models'></a>Efficiency Improvements in Quantized Models: 2 papers found.
Efficiency improvements in quantized models have been a focal point in recent research, particularly for vision transformers (ViTs) where quantization presents unique challenges due to their complex attention mechanisms. Existing methods often struggle with maintaining model performance when reducing bit-width, as the inter-channel variation in LayerNorm inputs and the non-uniform distribution of attention maps can lead to significant accuracy degradation \[[22](http://arxiv.org/abs/2111.13824)\]. To address these issues, researchers have proposed systematic approaches such as the Power-of-Two Factor (PTF), which mitigates performance loss by adjusting quantization strategies to better align with the characteristics of ViTs \[[22](http://arxiv.org/abs/2111.13824)\]. Additionally, methods like Log-Int-Softmax (LIS) have been introduced to preserve the structure of attention maps while enabling lower-bit quantization, such as 4-bit, without sacrificing accuracy \[[22](http://arxiv.org/abs/2111.13824)\]. These advancements are complemented by mixed-precision quantization schemes that leverage the nuclear norm of attention maps and output features to balance computational efficiency with model accuracy \[[10](http://arxiv.org/abs/2106.14156)\]. Such techniques have demonstrated promising results, with some approaches achieving top-1 accuracy of 84.89% on ImageNet using ViT-L with full quantization \[[22](http://arxiv.org/abs/2111.13824)\]. These developments highlight the ongoing efforts to enhance the efficiency of vision transformers through quantization, ensuring they can be deployed on resource-constrained devices without compromising performance.

Researchers have focused on leveraging diffusion models, which have shown promise in image synthesis, to video generation while addressing the challenge of maintaining consistency across frames. One approach involves using diffusion feature spaces to enforce consistency during video editing, allowing for high-quality results without requiring additional training \[[18](http://arxiv.org/abs/2307.10373)\].
