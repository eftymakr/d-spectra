## Related Works

Anomaly detection has become a critical component in modern industrial settings, particularly in the context of intelligent manufacturing, where maintaining quality control is essential \[[11](http://arxiv.org/abs/2106.08265)\]. Traditional approaches often rely on threshold-based methods or supervised learning, which are limited by the lack of labeled data and the complexity of real-world scenarios \[[9](http://arxiv.org/abs/1901.04997)\]. Recent advances in deep learning have enabled more robust and flexible anomaly detection methods, particularly for image-based and time-series data \[[5](http://arxiv.org/abs/2301.11514)\]\[[10](http://arxiv.org/abs/2007.10088)\].

Several studies have focused on improving the efficiency and performance of anomaly detection models for deployment in resource-constrained environments. For instance, \[[11](http://arxiv.org/abs/2106.08265)\] proposed PatchCore, a method that uses a maximally representative memory bank of nominal patch-features to achieve state-of-the-art performance for both detection and localization, with competitive inference times. This is particularly relevant for embedded systems where computational resources are limited. Similarly, \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/1607.00148)\] introduced an LSTM-based Encoder-Decoder scheme for anomaly detection, which is effective for both predictable and unpredictable time-series data. This approach leverages reconstruction errors to identify anomalies, demonstrating robustness across different types of time-series data.

In the realm of image-based anomaly detection, \[[8](http://arxiv.org/abs/2005.14140)\] proposed a method that leverages deep feature representations from pre-trained models, such as those trained on ImageNet, to detect anomalies in a transfer learning setting. This approach fits a multivariate Gaussian to deep features and uses the Mahalanobis distance as an anomaly score, achieving high performance on the MVTec dataset. Another study, \[[13](http://arxiv.org/abs/2310.07716)\], introduced the PAD dataset and benchmark for pose-agnostic anomaly detection, addressing the limitations of existing datasets that assume fixed object poses during training and testing. This work also proposed a novel method, OmniposeAD, specifically designed for pose-agnostic anomaly detection.

For multivariate time series, \[[6](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/440d248d148f7e36dad232e48f1c5c1cbc556d86)\] proposed OmniAnomaly, a stochastic recurrent neural network that captures normal patterns by learning robust representations and uses reconstruction probabilities to determine anomalies. This model is particularly effective for industrial devices such as servers and spacecrafts, where multivariate time series are common. Additionally, \[[9](http://arxiv.org/abs/1901.04997)\] introduced MAD-GAN, an unsupervised multivariate anomaly detection method based on Generative Adversarial Networks (GANs), which captures latent interactions among variables and uses a novel anomaly score for detection.

### <a id='category-deep-learning-approaches-for-anomaly-detection'></a>Deep Learning Approaches for Anomaly Detection: 7 papers found.
In the realm of deep learning approaches for anomaly detection, recent research has focused on leveraging neural networks to address the challenges posed by complex, high-dimensional, and often unlabeled data. Traditional methods, such as threshold-based or model-based approaches, struggle with dynamic and unpredictable time-series data \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/1607.00148)\]. To overcome these limitations, deep learning models like Long Short Term Memory (LSTM) networks have been employed to learn normal behavior and detect anomalies through reconstruction errors \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/1607.00148)\]. Similarly, semi-supervised approaches have been developed to utilize small sets of labeled data, with methods like Deep SAD introducing an information-theoretic framework based on entropy differences between normal and anomalous distributions \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/1906.02694)\]. In attributed networks, where both structural and attribute information is critical, dual autoencoder frameworks such as AnomalyDAE have been proposed to capture cross-modal interactions between network structure and node attributes \[[4](http://arxiv.org/abs/2002.03665)\]. For image-based anomaly detection, transfer learning has emerged as a powerful technique, where pre-trained models on large datasets are fine-tuned to detect anomalies in new domains \[[8](http://arxiv.org/abs/2005.14140)\]. Furthermore, in multivariate time-series data from real-world systems, Generative Adversarial Networks (GANs) have been adapted to exploit spatial-temporal correlations and detect anomalies through novel scoring mechanisms \[[9](http://arxiv.org/abs/1901.04997)\]. These advancements highlight the growing trend of using deep learning to address the diverse and complex challenges in anomaly detection across various domains.

### <a id='category-anomaly-detection-in-industrial-settings'></a>Anomaly Detection in Industrial Settings: 2 papers found.
In the realm of anomaly detection in industrial settings, recent research has focused on improving the reliability and robustness of detection systems, particularly in the context of Industrial Control Systems (ICS) and manufacturing environments. A systematic analysis of process-based anomaly detection schemes has revealed vulnerabilities to adversarial spoofing attacks, such as Synthetic Sensor Spoofing, which can significantly reduce the True Positive Rate of anomaly detectors \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/2012.03586)\]. These findings highlight the need for more comprehensive attack datasets and a critical reassessment of the physical properties that these systems aim to verify \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/2012.03586)\]. In parallel, efforts have been made to develop efficient and accurate anomaly detection models suitable for deployment on resource-constrained embedded devices, a critical requirement for Small and Medium Enterprises (SMEs) in intelligent manufacturing \[[11](http://arxiv.org/abs/2106.08265)\]. For instance, the PatchCore approach combines embeddings from pre-trained models with an outlier detection mechanism, achieving state-of-the-art performance on benchmark datasets like MVTec-AD with an AUROC score of up to 99.6% \[[11](http://arxiv.org/abs/2106.08265)\]. Similarly, the KairosAD framework leverages the Mobile Segment Anything Model (MobileSAM) to provide a lightweight and fast solution for image-based anomaly detection, demonstrating significant improvements in parameter efficiency and inference speed compared to existing models \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/1607.00148)\]. These advancements underscore the ongoing evolution in industrial anomaly detection, balancing the need for high accuracy with the practical constraints of real-world deployment.

However, due to the complex temporal dependence and stochasticity of multivariate time series, their anomaly detection remains a big challenge. This paper proposes OmniAnomaly, a stochastic recurrent neural network for multivariate time series anomaly detection that works well robustly for various devices. Its core idea is to capture the normal patterns of multivariate time series by learning their robust representations with key techniques such as stochastic variable connection and planar normalizing flow, reconstruct input data by the representations, and use the reconstruction probabilities to determine anomalies. Moreover, for a detected entity anomaly, OmniAnomaly can provide interpretations based on the reconstruction probabilities of its constituent univariate time series. The evaluation experiments are conducted on two public datasets from aerospace and a new server machine dataset (collected and released by us) from an Internet company. OmniAnomaly achieves an overall F1-Score of 0.86 in three real-world datasets, significantly outperforming the best performing baseline method by 0.09. The interpretation accuracy for OmniAnomaly is up to 0.89.»

	Document 1: [Snippet]: «Unsupervised anomaly detection with localization has many practical applications when labeling is infeasible and, moreover, when anomaly examples are completely missing in the train data. While recently proposed models for such data setup achieve high accuracy metrics, their complexity is a limiting factor for real-time processing. In this paper, we propose a real-time model and analytically derive its relationship to prior methods. Our CFLOW-AD model is based on a conditional normalizing flow framework adopted for anomaly detection with localization. In particular, CFLOW-AD consists of a discriminatively pretrained encoder followed by a multi-scale generative decoder where the latter explicitly estimate likelihood of the encoded features. 	Document 1: [Snippet]: «Object anomaly detection is an important problem in the field of machine vision and has seen remarkable progress recently. However, two significant challenges hinder its research and application. First, existing datasets lack comprehensive visual information from various pose angles. They usually have an unrealistic assumption that the anomaly-free training dataset is pose-aligned, and the testing samples have the same pose as the training data. However, in practice, anomaly may exist in any regions on a object, the training and query samples may have different poses, calling for the study on pose-agnostic anomaly detection. Second, the absence of a consensus on experimental protocols for pose-agnostic anomaly detection leads to unfair comparisons of different methods, hindering the research on pose-agnostic anomaly detection. To address these issues, we develop Multi-pose Anomaly Detection (MAD) dataset and Pose-agnostic Anomaly Detection (PAD) benchmark, which takes the first step to address the pose-agnostic anomaly detection problem. Specifically, we build MAD using 20 complex-shaped LEGO toys including 4K views with various poses, and high-quality and diverse 3D anomalies in both simulated and real environments. Additionally, we propose a novel method OmniposeAD, trained using MAD, specifically designed for pose-agnostic anomaly detection. Through comprehensive evaluations, we demonstrate the relevance of our dataset and method. Furthermore, we provide an open-source benchmark library, including dataset and baseline methods that cover 8 anomaly detection paradigms, to facilitate future research and application in this domain. Code, data, and models are publicly available at https://github.com/EricLee0224/PAD.»
