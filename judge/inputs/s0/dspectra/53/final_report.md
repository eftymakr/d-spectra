## Related Works

Sequential recommendation systems aim to predict the next item a user will interact with, based on their historical behavior. These systems have evolved significantly, incorporating advanced neural network architectures to better capture sequential patterns and user preferences. Early approaches relied on Markov Chains (MCs) \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/1808.09781)\], which assume that a user's next action depends only on their last (or few) actions. While effective in sparse datasets, MCs lack the ability to model long-term dependencies. Recurrent Neural Networks (RNNs) \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/1808.09781)\] were introduced to address this limitation, allowing for the modeling of longer-term semantics. However, RNNs often suffer from vanishing gradient problems and are computationally expensive \[[12](http://arxiv.org/abs/1904.06690)\]. 

To overcome these limitations, self-attention based models such as SASRec \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/1808.09781)\] have emerged. SASRec leverages self-attention to capture long-term dependencies while maintaining efficiency by focusing on relevant items. This approach balances the strengths of MCs and RNNs, offering a more flexible and efficient framework for sequential modeling. However, most self-attention models rely on simple positional encodings and ignore the temporal dynamics of user interactions \[[9](http://arxiv.org/abs/2008.08273)\]. 

Recent works have attempted to incorporate time-aware mechanisms into self-attention models. For example, TiSASRec \[[1](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/9c7a455b1e48d01a99e58e884a6c1acb75074ad0)\] explicitly models time intervals between interactions, enhancing the model's ability to capture temporal patterns. Similarly, MEANTIME \[[9](http://arxiv.org/abs/2008.08273)\] introduces multi-temporal embeddings to better represent the time-aware nature of user behavior. These models recognize that sequential recommendation differs from language modeling in that timestamp information is available and should be leveraged to extract additional contextual information. 

In addition to temporal modeling, recent studies have explored the use of hierarchical attention networks \[[4](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/0b42168f22713c48d8b0ce3bc8f680d347184d88)\] and feature-level self-attention \[[16](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/63555b4f363ee72dd8d49d8deca68760542c0cd2)\] to better capture diverse interaction patterns. Hierarchical attention networks \[[4](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/0b42168f22713c48d8b0ce3bc8f680d347184d88)\] integrate both long-term and short-term preferences, while feature-level self-attention \[[16](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/63555b4f363ee72dd8d49d8deca68760542c0cd2)\] considers both item-level and feature-level sequences to extract richer sequential patterns. These approaches highlight the importance of modeling both item transitions and feature transitions to improve recommendation accuracy.

Another key challenge in sequential recommendation is the sparsity of item-to-item transitions \[[7](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/455e11bde14967fc76d87d4649d821d2f27c3055)\]. To address this, Category-aware Collaborative Sequential Recommendation \[[7](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/455e11bde14967fc76d87d4649d821d2f27c3055)\] leverages item categories to enhance dependency modeling and collaborative learning across users. This approach improves the robustness of recommendation systems in sparse scenarios by incorporating category information into the modeling process.

### <a id='category-sequential-recommendation-models'></a>Sequential Recommendation Models: 10 papers found.
Sequential recommendation models have evolved significantly, incorporating advanced techniques to better capture user behavior dynamics. Early approaches relied on Markov Chains (MCs) and Recurrent Neural Networks (RNNs) to model sequential patterns, with MCs focusing on immediate past actions and RNNs capturing longer-term dependencies \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/1808.09781)\]. However, these methods often struggle with sparse data or dense datasets, prompting the development of self-attention based models like SASRec, which balance long-term semantics with efficient prediction by focusing on relevant items \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/1808.09781)\]. More recent models, such as TiSASRec, explicitly incorporate time intervals between interactions, enhancing the modeling of temporal dynamics \[[1](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/9c7a455b1e48d01a99e58e884a6c1acb75074ad0)\]. Similarly, MEANTIME introduces diverse temporal embeddings to better leverage timestamp information, addressing limitations in prior models that overlooked temporal nuances \[[7](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/455e11bde14967fc76d87d4649d821d2f27c3055)\]. The challenge of modeling dynamic user preferences has also been tackled by models like TLSRec, which integrate hierarchical self-attention to capture both global and local preference fluctuations \[[11](http://arxiv.org/abs/2208.04760)\]. Additionally, BERT4Rec employs bidirectional self-attention to model user behavior sequences from both left and right contexts, improving representation quality \[[12](http://arxiv.org/abs/1904.06690)\]. These advancements highlight a growing trend toward more sophisticated and flexible sequential recommendation frameworks that address key limitations in traditional approaches.

### <a id='category-category-aware-collaborative-methods'></a>Category-Aware Collaborative Methods: 1 papers found.
Context: 
	Document 1: [Snippet]: «Sequential recommendation is the task of predicting the next items for users based on their interaction history. Modeling the dependence of the next action on the past actions accurately is crucial to this problem. Moreover, sequential recommendation often faces serious sparsity of item-to-item transitions in a user's action sequence, which limits the practical utility of such solutions. To tackle these challenges, we propose a Category-aware Collaborative Sequential Recommender. Our preliminary statistical tests demonstrate that the in-category item-to-item transitions are often much stronger indicators of the next items than the general item-to-item transitions observed in the original sequence. Our method makes use of item category in two ways. First, the recommender utilizes item category to organize a user's own actions to enhance dependency modeling based on her own past actions. It utilizes self-attention to capture in-category transition patterns, and determines which of the in-category transition patterns to consider based on the categories of recent actions. Second, the recommender utilizes the item category to retrieve users with similar in-category preferences to enhance collaborative learning across users, and thus conquer sparsity. It utilizes attention to incorporate in-category transition patterns from the retrieved users for the target user. Extensive experiments on two large datasets prove the effectiveness of our solution against an extensive list of state-of-the-art sequential recommendation models.»

### <a id='category-state-space-modeling-for-sequences'></a>State-Space Modeling for Sequences: 1 papers found.
State-space models (SSMs) have emerged as a promising approach for continuous-time sequential recommendation, addressing limitations in capturing system continuity with traditional recurrent and attention-based models \[[14](http://arxiv.org/abs/2502.08132)\]. These models are particularly effective in modeling the dynamic evolution of user interest over time, especially when dealing with irregular time intervals in user interactions \[[14](http://arxiv.org/abs/2502.08132)\]. However, existing SSM-based approaches often overlook the impact of such irregular intervals, which complicates the modeling of complex user-item transitions \[[14](http://arxiv.org/abs/2502.08132)\]. To overcome this, the SS4Rec model was introduced, integrating a time-aware SSM to handle irregular time intervals and a relation-aware SSM to model contextual dependencies \[[14](http://arxiv.org/abs/2502.08132)\]. This dual approach allows SS4Rec to infer user interest from both temporal and sequential perspectives, enhancing the accuracy of time-specific personalized recommendations \[[14](http://arxiv.org/abs/2502.08132)\]. The model's training process involves discretizing the time-aware and relation-aware SSMs using variable stepsizes, aligning with user interaction time intervals and input data \[[14](http://arxiv.org/abs/2502.08132)\]. Experimental results on five benchmark datasets have demonstrated the superiority of SS4Rec in continuous-time sequential recommendation tasks \[[14](http://arxiv.org/abs/2502.08132)\].

### <a id='category-feature-level-self-attention'></a>Feature-Level Self-Attention: 1 papers found.
Context: 
	Document 1: [Snippet]: «Sequential recommendation, which aims to recommend next item that the user will likely interact in a near future, has become essential in various Internet applications. Existing methods usually consider the transition patterns between items, but ignore the transition patterns between features of items. We argue that only the item-level sequences cannot reveal the full sequential patterns, while explicit and implicit feature-level sequences can help extract the full sequential patterns.
