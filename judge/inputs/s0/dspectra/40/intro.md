## Related Works

Zero-Knowledge Proofs (ZKPs) have emerged as a critical cryptographic primitive for privacy-preserving computation, enabling a prover to demonstrate the truth of a statement without revealing additional information \[[10](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/9ccf7b6cb32cf89752a35bd910555adac54773e0)\]. This has led to their adoption in blockchain technologies, verifiable machine learning, and secure voting systems \[[6](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/65f1e83a53c5ce9dee04042e0dd4191a451df2b8)\]. However, the computational complexity of the proving process has hindered widespread adoption \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2408.05890)\]. Recent efforts have focused on accelerating the key primitives of ZKP protocols using hardware accelerators, particularly GPUs and ASICs \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2408.05890)\]. For instance, GZKP presents a GPU-accelerated zero-knowledge proof system that significantly improves performance by optimizing memory access patterns and parallelizing operations such as multi-scalar multiplication \[[3](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/e149d93531d8c07cf43f6cae327a4795144d1304)\]. Similarly, cuZK introduces a faster parallel multi-scalar multiplication algorithm on GPUs, achieving nearly linear speedup over existing methods \[[5](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/e4a4608e8e35379e63b9df5bda1b7599ee8ab9ac)\]. 

Despite these advancements, existing accelerators often face limitations. Some require a trusted setup for each application, which restricts their use in dynamic environments \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2408.05890)\]. Others generate larger proof sizes or incur higher verification costs, limiting their applicability in scenarios with numerous verifiers or strict time constraints \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2408.05890)\]. For example, PipeZK achieves a 10x speedup on standard cryptographic benchmarks but still faces challenges in balancing resource utilization and load distribution \[[2](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/6ee84674b7c426849c3da56cb278c08eb7a903cf)\]. Similarly, SZKP, the first ASIC to accelerate an entire proof on-chip, achieves over 400× speedup over CPU implementations but may not be suitable for all application domains due to its specialized design \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2408.05890)\]. 

To address these limitations, this work introduces zkSpeed, an accelerator for HyperPlonk, a ZKP protocol that supports one-time, universal setup and small proof sizes, making it suitable for publicly verifiable systems \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2408.05890)\]. Unlike prior works, zkSpeed accelerates the entire protocol, including two major primitives: SumCheck and Multi-scalar Multiplications (MSMs). This full-chip architecture, with a footprint of 366.46 mm² and 2 TB/s of bandwidth, achieves geometric mean speedups of 801× over CPU baselines \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2408.05890)\]. The design leverages optimized dataflow patterns and parallelism, addressing the irregular memory accesses and data-dependent operations inherent in NTT and MSM computations \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2408.05890)\].
