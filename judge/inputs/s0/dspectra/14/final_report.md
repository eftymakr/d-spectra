## Related Works

Video super-resolution (VSR) has been a focal point in computer vision, with recent advances leveraging deep learning to address the challenges of temporal consistency and artifact reduction in high-resolution video generation. Early methods, such as those based on single-image super-resolution (SR), often failed to capture temporal dependencies, leading to inconsistent results across frames \[[7](http://arxiv.org/abs/1501.00092)\]. To address this, researchers introduced multi-frame SR approaches that incorporated motion estimation and compensation, such as optical flow-based methods \[[6](http://arxiv.org/abs/1611.05250)\]. However, these approaches were often computationally intensive and prone to errors due to inaccurate motion estimation \[[16](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/755227d7ea41b1d90df31f411f9ecbc998c20d26)\].

More recently, deep learning has enabled significant improvements in VSR. The introduction of deep convolutional neural networks (CNNs) for SR, such as the Enhanced Deep Residual Networks (EDSR) \[[9](http://arxiv.org/abs/1707.02921)\], demonstrated that residual learning could enhance performance by allowing deeper networks to train more effectively. These models were later extended to video restoration tasks, where temporal information from multiple frames was exploited using sliding window strategies or recurrent architectures \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/2201.12288)\]. However, these methods often suffered from high computational costs and limited temporal coherence \[[12](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/a2c50f93f6d0892918e0ff653750d198ccab48d0)\].

To further improve temporal consistency, researchers proposed recurrent architectures, such as the Bidirectional Recurrent Convolutional Network (BRCN) \[[12](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/a2c50f93f6d0892918e0ff653750d198ccab48d0)\], which modeled long-term dependencies using convolutional operations. This approach reduced parameter counts and improved efficiency while maintaining performance. Similarly, the Frame-Recurrent Video Super-Resolution (FR-VSR) framework \[[8](http://arxiv.org/abs/1801.04590)\] used previously inferred high-resolution (HR) estimates to super-resolve subsequent frames, leading to more temporally consistent results. This method also reduced computational costs by warping only one image per step \[[8](http://arxiv.org/abs/1801.04590)\].

Another line of research focused on incorporating attention mechanisms into VSR models. The Video Super-Resolution Transformer (VSR-Transformer) \[[19](http://arxiv.org/abs/2106.06847)\] introduced spatial-temporal convolutional self-attention layers to exploit locality and feature alignment, improving performance on benchmark datasets. Similarly, the Temporal Group Attention (TGA) method \[[20](http://arxiv.org/abs/2007.10595)\] divided input sequences into groups based on frame rates and used attention modules to integrate complementary information, achieving favorable performance against state-of-the-art methods \[[20](http://arxiv.org/abs/2007.10595)\].

In addition to attention-based models, diffusion-based approaches have gained traction for their ability to generate high-quality, temporally consistent videos. The Stable Video Diffusion (SVD) model \[[4](http://arxiv.org/abs/2311.15127)\] demonstrated that latent diffusion models could be adapted for video generation by introducing temporal layers and finetuning on high-quality datasets. ### <a id='category-attention-mechanisms-in-video-super-resolution'></a>Attention Mechanisms in Video Super-Resolution: 5 papers found.
Video restoration and super-resolution have seen significant advancements with the integration of attention mechanisms and transformer-based architectures. Recent works focus on leveraging temporal information across video frames to improve restoration quality, often addressing the limitations of frame-by-frame processing. For instance, the Video Restoration Transformer (VRT) introduces a parallel frame prediction approach using temporal reciprocal self attention (TRSA) and parallel warping, enabling efficient cross-clip interactions and feature fusion \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/2201.12288)\]. Similarly, efforts to adapt transformers for video super-resolution (VSR) have highlighted the need for spatial-temporal convolutional self-attention layers to exploit locality and bidirectional optical flow-based feed-forward layers to align features across frames \[[19](http://arxiv.org/abs/2106.06847)\]. These methods aim to overcome the shortcomings of traditional transformers, which rely on fully connected self-attention and token-wise feed-forward layers that neglect data locality and feature alignment \[[19](http://arxiv.org/abs/2106.06847)\]. Additionally, research has explored the impact of artifacts in real-world scenarios, proposing channel-attention-based frameworks like RealViformer that reduce feature redundancy and improve robustness against degradation \[[22](http://arxiv.org/abs/2407.13987)\]. Furthermore, approaches such as TimeSformer introduce convolution-free methods using self-attention over space and time, achieving state-of-the-art results in video classification while maintaining efficiency \[[27](http://arxiv.org/abs/2102.05095)\]. These developments underscore the growing importance of attention mechanisms in enhancing the performance of video restoration and super-resolution tasks.

	Document 1: [Snippet]: «Recent advances in video super-resolution have shown that convolutional neural networks combined with motion compensation are able to merge information from multiple low-resolution (LR) frames to generate high-quality images. Current state-of-the-art methods process a batch of LR frames to generate a single high-resolution (HR) frame and run this scheme in a sliding window fashion over the entire video, effectively treating the problem as a large number of separate multi-frame super-resolution tasks. This approach has two main weaknesses: 1) Each input frame is processed and warped multiple times, increasing the computational cost, and 2) each output frame is estimated independently conditioned on the input frames, limiting the system's ability to produce temporally consistent results. In this work, we propose an end-to-end trainable frame-recurrent video super-resolution framework that uses the previously inferred HR estimate to super-resolve the subsequent frame. This naturally encourages temporally consistent results and reduces the computational cost by warping only one image in each step. Furthermore, due to its recurrent nature, the proposed method has the ability to assimilate a large number of previous frames without increased computational demands. Extensive evaluations and comparisons with previous methods validate the strengths of our approach and demonstrate that the proposed framework is able to significantly outperform the current state of the art.»

	Document 1: [Snippet]: «Self-supervised learning (SSL) is rapidly closing the gap with supervised methods on large computer vision benchmarks. A successful approach to SSL is to learn embeddings which are invariant to distortions of the input sample. However, a recurring issue with this approach is the existence of trivial constant solutions. Most current methods avoid such solutions by careful implementation details. We propose an objective function that naturally avoids collapse by measuring the cross-correlation matrix between the outputs of two identical networks fed with distorted versions of a sample, and making it as close to the identity matrix as possible. This causes the embedding vectors of distorted versions of a sample to be similar, while minimizing the redundancy between the components of these vectors. The method is called Barlow Twins, owing to neuroscientist H. Barlow's redundancy-reduction principle applied to a pair of identical networks. Barlow Twins does not require large batches nor asymmetry between the network twins such as a predictor network, gradient stopping, or a moving average on the weight updates. Intriguingly it benefits from very high-dimensional output vectors. Barlow Twins outperforms previous methods on ImageNet for semi-supervised classification in the low-data regime, and is on par with current state of the art for ImageNet classification with a linear classifier head, and for transfer tasks of classification and object detection.»
