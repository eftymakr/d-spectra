## Related Works

Calibration of deep neural networks has been a central topic in recent years, particularly due to its importance in safety-critical applications where accurate uncertainty estimation is essential \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2002.09437)\]. Traditional approaches to calibration often rely on post-processing techniques such as temperature scaling, which adjusts predicted probabilities to better match true accuracy \[[9](http://arxiv.org/abs/1706.04599)\]. However, these methods are limited in their flexibility and expressiveness, often relying on fixed functional forms like piece-wise linear transformations \[[12](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/7f6bb146b9c1504ec77100e640d624bdc204f0ac)\]. This restricts their ability to model complex calibration behaviors, especially in scenarios where the relationship between predicted scores and true accuracy is non-linear \[[12](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/7f6bb146b9c1504ec77100e640d624bdc204f0ac)\].

Several works have explored alternative calibration methods that offer greater flexibility. For instance, the use of deep ensembles has been shown to provide well-calibrated predictive uncertainty estimates, often matching or outperforming Bayesian neural networks in terms of both accuracy and calibration \[[6](http://arxiv.org/abs/1612.01474)\]. Similarly, mixup training has been found to improve calibration by encouraging smoother decision boundaries and reducing overconfidence in predictions \[[8](http://arxiv.org/abs/1905.11001)\]. This is attributed to the label smoothing effect inherent in mixup, which helps the model better estimate uncertainty \[[8](http://arxiv.org/abs/1905.11001)\].

Another approach to calibration involves the use of monotonic neural networks, such as the Unconstrained Monotonic Neural Network (UMNN), which can learn arbitrary monotonic functions while preserving the order of predictions \[[7](http://arxiv.org/abs/1908.05164)\]. This is particularly useful in ranking tasks, where the absolute values of predicted scores are as important as their relative order \[[12](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/7f6bb146b9c1504ec77100e640d624bdc204f0ac)\]. Unlike traditional calibration methods, UMNN-based calibrators do not impose rigid constraints on the form of the transformation, allowing for greater modeling flexibility \[[7](http://arxiv.org/abs/1908.05164)\]. This aligns with the findings of \[[12](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/7f6bb146b9c1504ec77100e640d624bdc204f0ac)\], which highlight the limitations of fixed-form calibration functions and the need for more expressive methods.

To further enhance calibration performance, some works have introduced novel loss functions that directly optimize for calibration.
