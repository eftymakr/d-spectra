## Related Works

Protein conformation sampling and structure prediction have long relied on physics-based simulations such as molecular dynamics (MD) and Monte Carlo (MC) methods \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/2306.03117)\]. These approaches, while accurate, are computationally intensive and often struggle with rare event sampling and long equilibration times \[[5](http://arxiv.org/abs/2403.14088)\]. To address these limitations, recent advances in deep learning have introduced generative modeling techniques, particularly denoising diffusion models, which enable efficient and accurate protein structure prediction and conformation sampling by learning distributions over crystallographic structures \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/1812.01729)\]. However, integrating physical supervision into these data-driven models remains a challenge, as standard energy-based objectives often lead to intractable optimization \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/1812.01729)\].

Several studies have explored the use of deep learning for protein structure prediction. For instance, EigenFold employs diffusion models to generate a distribution of structures from a given protein sequence, modeling the structure as a system of harmonic oscillators and inducing a cascading-resolution generative process along the eigenmodes of the system \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/2304.02198)\]. Similarly, Str2Str proposes a structure-to-structure translation framework capable of zero-shot conformation sampling with roto-translation equivariant property, leveraging an amortized denoising score matching objective trained on general crystal structures \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/2306.03117)\]. These methods demonstrate the potential of deep learning in capturing conformational ensembles and flexibility, but they often rely on simulation data for training, which can be costly and limit generalizability \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/2306.03117)\].

To improve the physical plausibility of generated structures, researchers have proposed methods that incorporate force fields and energy-based objectives. For example, ConfDiff introduces a force-guided SE(3) diffusion model that integrates physical prior knowledge to guide the generation process, ensuring that sampled conformations remain close to the equilibrium distribution \[[5](http://arxiv.org/abs/2403.14088)\]. Similarly, the Distributional Graphormer (DiG) framework employs deep neural networks to transform a simple distribution towards the equilibrium distribution, conditioned on a descriptor of a molecular system such as a chemical graph or a protein sequence \[[4](http://arxiv.org/abs/2306.05445)\]. These approaches highlight the importance of incorporating physical guidance in generative models to enhance the accuracy and reliability of predicted structures.

For instance, denoising diffusion policy optimization (DDPO) enables the direct optimization of diffusion models for such objectives by framing denoising as a multi-step decision-making problem \[[10](http://arxiv.org/abs/2305.13301)\].
