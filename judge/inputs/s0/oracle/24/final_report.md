## Related Works

Diffusion models have gained significant traction in image and video generation due to their ability to produce high-quality outputs \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2006.11239)\]. However, their deployment on resource-constrained devices is hindered by their high computational and memory demands \[[5](http://arxiv.org/abs/2211.15736)\]. To address this, quantization techniques have been explored to reduce model size and accelerate inference \[[4](http://arxiv.org/abs/2302.04304)\]. 

Early approaches to quantizing diffusion models focused on post-training quantization (PTQ), which compresses models without requiring retraining \[[5](http://arxiv.org/abs/2211.15736)\]. However, PTQ often leads to performance degradation, particularly in low-bit settings, due to the accumulation of quantization noise over multiple denoising steps \[[6](http://arxiv.org/abs/2305.10657)\]. To mitigate this, researchers have proposed methods that account for the temporal variability in diffusion models, such as timestep-aware calibration and split shortcut quantization \[[4](http://arxiv.org/abs/2302.04304)\]. These approaches aim to maintain the distribution consistency of noise estimation networks across time steps, which is crucial for preserving generation quality \[[4](http://arxiv.org/abs/2302.04304)\].

In the context of diffusion transformers (DiT), which have shown superior scalability and performance in image generation \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/2405.14854)\], quantization remains a challenge due to the unique architecture and temporal dependencies in these models \[[10](http://arxiv.org/abs/2405.16005)\]. Existing PTQ methods for DiTs face difficulties in preserving the spatiotemporal correlations between frames, which are essential for video generation \[[10](http://arxiv.org/abs/2405.16005)\]. To address this, PTQ4DiT was introduced, which includes techniques like Channel-wise Salience Balancing (CSB) and Spearmen's ρ-guided Salience Calibration (SSC) to handle the extreme magnitudes and temporal variations in DiT activations \[[10](http://arxiv.org/abs/2405.16005)\]. This work demonstrated that DiTs can be effectively quantized to 8-bit precision (W8A8) while maintaining generation quality \[[10](http://arxiv.org/abs/2405.16005)\].

For video generation, the challenges of quantization are even more pronounced due to the need to preserve temporal consistency across frames \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2006.11239)\]. Recent work has focused on developing quantization-aware training (QAT) and efficient fine-tuning methods to maintain performance at low bit-widths \[[8](http://arxiv.org/abs/2310.03270)\]. For instance, EfficientDM introduced a data-free and parameter-efficient fine-tuning framework that achieves QAT-level performance with PTQ-like efficiency \[[8](http://arxiv.org/abs/2310.03270)\]. ### <a id='category-quantization-techniques-for-diffusion-models'></a>Quantization Techniques for Diffusion Models: 13 papers found.
Quantization techniques for diffusion models have been extensively explored to address the high computational and memory costs associated with these models, particularly in resource-constrained environments \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2006.11239)\]. Several approaches have been proposed to enable efficient inference while maintaining generation quality. For instance, post-training quantization (PTQ) has been adapted to diffusion models, allowing for compression without retraining, though challenges remain due to the changing output distributions of noise estimation networks across time steps \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/2412.05926)\]. To mitigate this, methods like timestep-aware calibration and split shortcut quantization have been introduced to handle the unique multi-timestep structure of diffusion models \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/2405.14854)\]. Additionally, 1-bit binarization has been explored as an extreme form of quantization, leading to the development of BiDM, which fully binarizes weights and activations while maintaining performance through temporal and spatial strategies like Timestep-friendly Binary Structure (TBS) and Space Patched Distillation (SPD) \[[4](http://arxiv.org/abs/2302.04304)\]. Furthermore, mixed-precision quantization frameworks such as MixDQ have been proposed to address the challenges of preserving image quality and text alignment in few-step diffusion models, achieving W8A8 quantization without performance loss \[[5](http://arxiv.org/abs/2211.15736)\]. These methods highlight the ongoing efforts to balance model efficiency with generation quality, leveraging both architectural and algorithmic innovations \[[6](http://arxiv.org/abs/2305.10657)\].
