## Related Works

Multi-Domain Recommendation (MDR) has emerged as a critical area in recommendation systems, aiming to leverage knowledge transfer across different domains to enhance recommendation performance \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2302.01115)\]. Early approaches often relied on a single structure to transfer domain-shared knowledge, but this approach failed to account for the variability in beneficial transfer information across domains \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2302.01115)\]. This limitation led to the **Negative Transfer Problem (NTP)**, where unselective information transfer could degrade performance, especially when domains exhibit knowledge conflicts or poor quality \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2302.01115)\].

Several works have attempted to address the NTP by introducing more sophisticated transfer mechanisms. For instance, **PEPNet** \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/2410.05877)\] proposes a plug-and-play framework that dynamically scales embeddings and DNN hidden units through gate mechanisms, allowing for personalized adjustments based on user behavior across multiple domains. Similarly, **MDAP** \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/2308.05508)\] introduces a multi-view disentangled and adaptive preference learning framework, which captures diverse user preferences and adaptively combines embeddings from different views to generate a comprehensive user representation. This approach enhances adaptability and effectiveness in cross-domain scenarios.

Another notable work is **EDDA** \[[4](http://arxiv.org/abs/2101.11427)\], which tackles the challenges of disentangling domain-specific and generalizable knowledge and improving knowledge transfer across domains with small overlaps. EDDA introduces embedding disentangling and domain alignment techniques, leveraging random walks from graph processing to identify similar user-item pairs across domains. This enables more effective knowledge transfer by encouraging similar user-item pairs to have similar embeddings.

In the realm of multi-task learning, **STAR** \[[5](http://arxiv.org/abs/1905.07553)\] presents a star topology adaptive recommender that combines shared and domain-specific parameters to handle multiple domains simultaneously. This model adapts its parameters based on domain characteristics, improving performance across diverse domains. Additionally, **Pacer and Runner** \[[6](http://arxiv.org/abs/2407.11245)\] introduces a cooperative learning framework between single- and cross-domain sequential recommendation models, addressing the issue of negative transfer by estimating the degree of negative transfer for each domain and adaptively assigning it as a weight factor to the prediction loss.

These methods highlight the importance of dynamic and selective knowledge transfer in MDR. However, they often rely on complex architectures or require extensive fine-tuning. In contrast, the proposed **Similar Domain Selection Principle (SDSP)** \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2302.01115)\] offers a lightweight and dynamic approach to domain selection by measuring domain-level gaps and selecting appropriate domains based on both supervised and unsupervised signals. This method is designed to be easily integrated into existing MDR frameworks without introducing significant overhead.
