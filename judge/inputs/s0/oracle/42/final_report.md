Prior research has explored system-level optimization strategies for cost and resource efficiency in containerized systems \[[1]()\]. Several studies have investigated the use of Kubernetes for adaptive and cost-efficient resource management . For instance, some researchers have proposed hierarchical multi-agent optimization frameworks for resource allocation in cloud computing . These approaches leverage distributed decision-making to improve resource utilization and reduce waste. While these methods are promising, they often focus on computational resources rather than network costs, which are a critical component of cloud infrastructure expenses .

In the context of FinOps, recent efforts have aimed to integrate financial management with cloud operations to optimize costs in cloud-based applications . This includes the development of cost models that account for both compute and network expenditures. However, most existing models do not specifically address the unique challenges of network cost optimization in Kubernetes clusters . The paper under review fills this gap by providing a detailed analysis of network usage and cost implications in containerized applications running on Kubernetes .

A number of studies have also explored the use of measurement analysis and experimentation to evaluate the performance and cost of cloud-native systems . The methodology proposed in this paper builds on these insights by combining measurement analysis, experimentation, and cost modelling to provide actionable insights into network cost optimization .

In summary, while prior research has made significant contributions to the fields of containerization, resource management, and FinOps, there remains a need for more focused studies on network cost optimization in Kubernetes clusters. This paper addresses this gap by providing a comprehensive analysis of network usage and cost implications in containerized applications, contributing to the ongoing development of the FinOps discipline .
