## Related Works

Anomaly detection in industrial settings has garnered significant attention due to its critical role in quality control and fault prevention in modern manufacturing processes. Existing approaches often rely on deep learning techniques, which have seen remarkable progress in recent years. However, many state-of-the-art models are computationally intensive and unsuitable for deployment on resource-constrained embedded devices \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2301.11514)\]. This limitation is particularly relevant for Small and Medium Enterprises (SMEs), which often lack the infrastructure to support large-scale AI systems \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2301.11514)\].

Several studies have explored the use of autoencoders for unsupervised anomaly detection, where the reconstruction error is used to identify anomalies \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/1807.02011)\]. However, these methods often struggle with localization accuracy and fail to capture structural information, leading to suboptimal performance in real-world scenarios \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/1807.02011)\]. To address this, perceptual loss functions based on structural similarity have been proposed, which better capture local dependencies and improve segmentation accuracy \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/1807.02011)\].

Semi-supervised approaches, such as GANomaly, have also been introduced to tackle the class imbalance problem in anomaly detection \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/1805.06725)\]. These models leverage adversarial training to learn the distribution of normal data and detect anomalies based on their deviation from this distribution \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/1805.06725)\]. While effective, such models often require extensive training and are not always suitable for deployment on embedded systems \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/1805.06725)\].

Recent advances in foundation models, such as DINOv2, have demonstrated the potential of vision-only models for few-shot anomaly detection \[[4](http://arxiv.org/abs/2405.14529)\]. These models leverage high-quality visual features and can achieve competitive performance without the need for additional training or fine-tuning \[[4](http://arxiv.org/abs/2405.14529)\]. Similarly, AnomalyDINO, which builds upon DINOv2, has shown promising results in one-shot and few-shot settings, particularly in industrial applications \[[4](http://arxiv.org/abs/2405.14529)\].

Efforts to make anomaly detection models more lightweight and efficient have also been pursued. For example, PatchCore introduces a memory-based approach that uses a maximally representative memory bank of nominal patch-features to achieve state-of-the-art performance with competitive inference times \[[5](http://arxiv.org/abs/2106.08265)\]. This method is particularly effective in scenarios where only normal data is available during training \[[5](http://arxiv.org/abs/2106.08265)\].

In addition, the development of lightweight variants of the Segment Anything Model (SAM), such as MobileSAM, has enabled the deployment of high-performance anomaly detection systems on resource-constrained devices \[[13](http://arxiv.org/abs/2306.14289)\]. MobileSAM achieves comparable performance to the original SAM while being significantly smaller and faster, making it suitable for real-time applications \[[13](http://arxiv.org/abs/2306.14289)\].
