## Related Works

Nonverbal communication plays a crucial role in human interaction, encompassing gestures, facial expressions, and body language that convey intent and emotion \[[19]()\]. Existing large language models (LLMs) have primarily focused on textual understanding and generation, often neglecting the integration of nonverbal cues \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2305.06355)\]. This limitation restricts their ability to create fully immersive conversational experiences \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2305.06355)\]. Recent efforts have sought to bridge this gap by incorporating multimodal data into language models \[[14]()\]. For instance, VideoChat introduces a chat-centric video understanding system that integrates video foundation models with large language models, emphasizing spatiotemporal reasoning and causal relationship inference \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2305.06355)\]. Similarly, Unified-IO 2 is a multimodal model capable of understanding and generating image, text, audio, and action, using a shared semantic space for diverse modalities \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/2312.17172)\]. 

The importance of nonverbal cues in conversations has also been explored in datasets such as MELD, which contains multimodal data from conversations with emotion and sentiment annotations \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/1810.02508)\]. CHAMPAGNE, another model, incorporates visual contexts into conversations by training on a large-scale corpus of video-based dialogues \[[4](http://arxiv.org/abs/2303.09713)\]. These models highlight the potential of integrating nonverbal cues into dialogue systems. However, they often lack the comprehensive integration of both verbal and nonverbal elements in a unified framework \[[5](http://arxiv.org/abs/2406.07867)\].

Recent works have also focused on generating realistic nonverbal behaviors from speech. For example, EMOCA addresses the challenge of capturing high-fidelity facial expressions by introducing a deep perceptual emotion consistency loss \[[9](http://arxiv.org/abs/2204.11312)\]. Similarly, the work on generating holistic 3D human motion from speech uses a dataset with synchronized speech and body motions, and proposes a novel framework for generating diverse and realistic motions \[[10](http://arxiv.org/abs/2212.04420)\]. These approaches demonstrate the importance of aligning nonverbal cues with speech, a key aspect of the MARS model \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2305.06355)\].

The MARS model introduces VENUS, a large-scale dataset with time-aligned text, facial expressions, and body language, enabling the training of a multimodal language model that understands and generates nonverbal cues alongside text \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2305.06355)\]. This dataset is crucial for achieving the model's performance, as it provides the necessary alignment between modalities. The use of vector-quantized nonverbal representations in MARS is inspired by similar approaches in other models, such as those that generate facial gestures from speech \[[12](http://arxiv.org/abs/2308.10897)\].
