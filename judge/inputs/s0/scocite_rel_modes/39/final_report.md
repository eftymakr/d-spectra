## Related Works

De novo genome assembly has seen significant advancements with the advent of long-read sequencing technologies such as PacBio and Oxford Nanopore \[[1](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/2b59fd18ada769ef76fb7fcc7382f063791abfab)\]. These technologies produce reads that are significantly longer than traditional short-read sequencing, enabling the assembly of complex genomes with higher contiguity \[[1](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/2b59fd18ada769ef76fb7fcc7382f063791abfab)\]. However, the high error rates associated with long-read sequencing pose challenges for accurate assembly \[[1](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/2b59fd18ada769ef76fb7fcc7382f063791abfab)\]. To address these challenges, several assemblers have been developed, including Canu, which introduces adaptive overlapping strategies and sparse assembly graph construction to improve assembly continuity and reduce runtime \[[1](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/2b59fd18ada769ef76fb7fcc7382f063791abfab)\]. Similarly, Minimap and miniasm have been introduced to efficiently map and assemble long reads without an error correction stage, achieving faster assembly times compared to existing pipelines \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/1512.01801)\].

The computational complexity of de novo assembly has also driven the development of parallel and distributed assemblers. For example, PASHA employs a hybrid parallelism approach, combining multi-threading on multi-core CPUs with message passing on compute clusters, to achieve efficient and scalable assembly of large genomes \[[8](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/7bf2f1c55f90e11a4d1df4dd608ac621a08f8b33)\]. SWAP-Assembler, another parallel assembler, scales up to 2048 cores and demonstrates superior performance in terms of contig quality and scalability \[[4](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/fe8d86bc5520b5049104ff58ee7e980982816ebf)\]. Additionally, Ray is designed to handle reads from a mix of sequencing technologies, improving the accuracy and contiguity of assemblies by reducing the number of contigs and errors \[[12](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/38410204a2977a9484e6d1205697b4103b5482f7)\].

The use of k-mer frequency analysis has also been explored as a method to estimate genomic characteristics such as genome size, repeat structure, and heterozygous rate, providing an assembly-independent approach to these estimations \[[20](http://arxiv.org/abs/1308.2012)\]. Furthermore, the development of efficient data structures, such as succinct data structures, has enabled the creation of compact representations of de Bruijn graphs, reducing storage requirements and improving the scalability of assembly algorithms \[[18](http://arxiv.org/abs/1008.2555)\].

In addition, the integration of long-read sequencing with short-read data has been shown to improve the accuracy and contiguity of genome assemblies. For example, the use of proximity-ligation sequencing with nanopore reads has enabled near chromosome-level scaffolding for human genomes \[[23](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/f9d407ce133ec096ab4321e6d98e8f0081f5bdcc)\]. The development of tools such as BELLA, which uses probabilistic models and sparse matrix computations to detect overlaps, has also contributed to the advancement of long-read assembly \[[9](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/f27decfab0389e2003e7ca66260b34eb3c1b8827)\].

These advancements highlight the ongoing efforts to improve the accuracy, efficiency, and scalability of de novo genome assembly.
