## Related Works

Recent advancements in large language models (LLMs) have emphasized the importance of understanding how feature representations evolve across layers, as this knowledge is critical for improving interpretability and robustness \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2307.09288)\]. While prior work has identified critical layers linked to specific functions or behaviors, these approaches often rely on data-dependent analyses of fine-tuned models, limiting their applicability to post-hoc settings \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/2109.01652)\]. In contrast, this paper introduces a data-oblivious approach that identifies intrinsic critical layers in pre-fine-tuned LLMs by analyzing representation dynamics using Centered Kernel Alignment (CKA) \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/2309.02144)\]. This method enables the identification of layers that undergo significant shifts in representation space, which are also most affected during fine-tuning—a pattern that holds consistently across tasks for a given model \[[4](http://arxiv.org/abs/2305.18290)\].

The spectral analysis of these representation shifts reveals that they are driven by changes in the top principal components, which encode semantic transitions from rationales to conclusions \[[5](http://arxiv.org/abs/2106.09685)\]. This insight is particularly relevant to the broader field of transfer learning, where pre-trained models are adapted to downstream tasks through fine-tuning \[[6](http://arxiv.org/abs/2012.13255)\]. For instance, the effectiveness of transfer learning has been explored through a unified framework that converts every language problem into a text-to-text format, enabling state-of-the-art results across multiple benchmarks \[[7](http://arxiv.org/abs/2407.21783)\]. Similarly, the success of instruction tuning—fine-tuning models on tasks described via natural language instructions—has been shown to significantly improve zero-shot performance on unseen tasks \[[8](http://arxiv.org/abs/2302.13971)\].

This paper also applies its findings to two practical scenarios: efficient domain adaptation and backdoor defense. In domain adaptation, fine-tuning critical layers leads to greater loss reduction compared to non-critical layers, suggesting that targeted fine-tuning can improve performance with minimal effort \[[9](http://arxiv.org/abs/1910.10683)\]. In backdoor defense, freezing critical layers reduces attack success rates by up to 40%, highlighting the importance of understanding which layers are most vulnerable to adversarial attacks \[[10](http://arxiv.org/abs/1810.04805)\]. These results align with broader efforts to improve the robustness of LLMs, such as the development of parameter-efficient fine-tuning methods like LoRA, which injects trainable rank decomposition matrices into each layer of the Transformer architecture, greatly reducing the number of trainable parameters \[[11](http://arxiv.org/abs/2110.04366)\].

### <a id='category-parameter-efficient-fine-tuning-techniques'></a>Parameter Efficient Fine Tuning Techniques: 4 papers found.
Parameter efficient fine-tuning techniques have emerged as a critical area of research in natural language processing, particularly for adapting large-scale pre-trained models to downstream tasks without the computational and resource costs of full fine-tuning. Methods such as Low-Rank Adaptation (LoRA) \[[5](http://arxiv.org/abs/2106.09685)\] and Compacter \[[21](http://arxiv.org/abs/2106.04647)\] aim to reduce the number of trainable parameters while maintaining model performance. LoRA introduces trainable rank decomposition matrices into the Transformer architecture, significantly lowering the number of parameters and GPU memory requirements, with results comparable to full fine-tuning \[[5](http://arxiv.org/abs/2106.09685)\]. Similarly, Compacter leverages ideas from adapters and low-rank optimization to insert task-specific weight matrices into a pre-trained model, achieving performance on par with standard fine-tuning while training only a fraction of the original parameters \[[21](http://arxiv.org/abs/2106.04647)\]. Other approaches, such as BitFit \[[13](http://arxiv.org/abs/2106.10199)\], focus on modifying only bias terms, demonstrating that small-to-medium training data can yield results competitive with full fine-tuning. These methods are supported by empirical and theoretical insights into the intrinsic dimensionality of pre-trained models, which suggest that a low-dimensional reparameterization can be as effective as the full parameter space \[[6](http://arxiv.org/abs/2012.13255)\]. Such findings highlight the efficiency and effectiveness of parameter-efficient techniques in both resource-constrained and high-performance scenarios.

### <a id='category-language-model-reasoning-and-alignment'></a>Language Model Reasoning And Alignment: 2 papers found.
The category "Language Model Reasoning And Alignment" encompasses research aimed at improving the reasoning capabilities and alignment of large language models (LLMs). Recent studies have highlighted the challenges LLMs face in mathematical reasoning, emphasizing that pre-training loss is a more reliable indicator of model performance than parameter count \[[18](http://arxiv.org/abs/2308.01825)\]. Researchers have explored methods to enhance reasoning performance through supervised fine-tuning (SFT) and data augmentation, finding a log-linear relationship between data amount and model performance \[[18](http://arxiv.org/abs/2308.01825)\]. To improve performance without human effort, Rejection sampling Fine-Tuning (RFT) has been proposed, leveraging supervised models to generate correct reasoning paths, leading to significant improvements, especially for less performant models \[[18](http://arxiv.org/abs/2308.01825)\]. In alignment efforts, studies have investigated techniques like prompting and training objectives such as imitation learning, binary discrimination, and ranked preference modeling, with ranked preference modeling showing superior performance and scalability \[[20](http://arxiv.org/abs/2112.00861)\]. These findings underscore the importance of both reasoning enhancement and alignment strategies in advancing LLM capabilities.
