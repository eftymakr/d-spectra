## Related Works

Anomaly detection in industrial settings has garnered significant attention due to its critical role in quality control and operational efficiency. A wide range of methods have been proposed to address the challenges of detecting anomalies in images and time series data, particularly in scenarios where labeled anomaly data is scarce or unavailable \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/2011.08785)\]. Early approaches relied on handcrafted features and classical computer vision techniques, but recent advances have leveraged deep learning architectures to improve detection accuracy and localization capabilities \[[4](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/3aa681914a7da79f7d7293f51a058eefe61c8bb7)\].

Several works have focused on unsupervised anomaly detection, where models are trained solely on normal data. For instance, PatchCore \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2106.08265)\] introduces a method that uses a maximally representative memory bank of nominal patch features to achieve state-of-the-art performance on the MVTec AD benchmark, achieving an AUROC score of up to 99.6%. Similarly, CFLOW-AD \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/2107.12571)\] proposes a real-time model based on conditional normalizing flows, which is faster and smaller than prior state-of-the-art methods by a factor of 10×. PaDiM \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/2011.08785)\] further improves on this by using a probabilistic representation of the normal class through multivariate Gaussian distributions and exploiting correlations between different semantic levels of the CNN. These methods have demonstrated strong performance on benchmark datasets such as MVTec AD and STC \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/2011.08785)\].

In addition to image-based anomaly detection, there has been growing interest in time series anomaly detection for industrial control systems. For example, \[[5](http://arxiv.org/abs/1911.04831)\] proposes a sequence-to-sequence neural network approach that detects anomalies in operational data of industrial control systems (ICSs), achieving high detection rates on the SWaT dataset. Another study \[[10](http://arxiv.org/abs/1806.08110)\] explores the use of 1D convolutional networks for anomaly detection in ICSs, showing that they outperform recurrent networks in this setting due to their simplicity and efficiency. These methods are particularly relevant for real-time monitoring and fault detection in industrial environments.

Recent works have also explored the use of multimodal data for anomaly detection. For instance, \[[9](http://arxiv.org/abs/2303.00601)\] introduces a hybrid fusion approach that combines 3D point clouds and RGB images to improve detection performance. The proposed method, M3DM, uses unsupervised feature fusion and decision layer fusion to enhance the interaction between different modalities, leading to improved detection and segmentation accuracy on the MVTec-3D AD dataset.

### <a id='category-industrial-anomaly-detection-techniques'></a>Industrial Anomaly Detection Techniques: 3 papers found.
Recent advancements in deep learning have significantly impacted industrial image anomaly detection (IAD), with a growing focus on improving efficiency and performance for real-world deployment \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2106.08265)\]. Existing methods often rely on complex architectures that are computationally intensive, limiting their applicability in resource-constrained environments such as small and medium enterprises (SMEs) \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2106.08265)\]. To address this, researchers have explored lightweight models and novel architectures that maintain high detection accuracy while reducing computational demands \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2106.08265)\]. For instance, the Mobile Segment Anything Model (MobileSAM) has been adapted for anomaly detection, offering a 4x faster inference time and 78% fewer parameters compared to state-of-the-art models, without compromising AUROC performance \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2106.08265)\]. This aligns with broader efforts to develop more practical solutions for industrial settings, such as multimodal approaches that integrate 3D point clouds and RGB images \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/2107.12571)\]. These methods often employ hybrid fusion strategies to enhance feature interaction and reduce information loss \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/2107.12571)\]. Additionally, zero-shot and few-shot learning techniques have gained traction, leveraging pre-trained models like CLIP to address the challenge of limited annotated data \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/2011.08785)\]. These approaches use compositional ensembles and efficient feature alignment to improve classification and segmentation performance \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/2011.08785)\]. Overall, the field is moving toward more adaptable and efficient solutions that can be deployed on embedded devices, as demonstrated by successful implementations on NVIDIA Jetson platforms \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2106.08265)\].
