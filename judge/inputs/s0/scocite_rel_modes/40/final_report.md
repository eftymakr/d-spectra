## Related Works

Zero-Knowledge Proofs (ZKPs) have garnered significant attention for their ability to enable secure and private computation, allowing a prover to demonstrate the truth of a statement without revealing additional information \[[9](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/6bcd2606c8f0099a82bd03e520659058a5b12e51)\]. This capability has led to their adoption in various domains, including blockchain technologies, verifiable machine learning, and electronic voting \[[6](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/3becc2935d1f52c82ffd7216c28bc86f5e393208)\]. However, the computational complexity of the proving process has hindered their widespread adoption \[[1](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/e149d93531d8c07cf43f6cae327a4795144d1304)\]. Recent research has focused on accelerating the key primitives of ZKP protocols using hardware accelerators such as GPUs and ASICs \[[10](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/6336149196f02d0eb6c1a89bc6b662e4422bd794)\]. 

Several works have proposed GPU-accelerated ZKP systems to improve performance. GZKP, for instance, achieves significant speedups by optimizing memory access patterns and eliminating costly external shuffles \[[1](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/e149d93531d8c07cf43f6cae327a4795144d1304)\]. Similarly, PipeZK introduces a pipelined architecture with two subsystems to handle polynomial computations and multi-scalar multiplications on elliptic curves, achieving up to 10x speedup on cryptographic benchmarks \[[2](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/6ee84674b7c426849c3da56cb278c08eb7a903cf)\]. cuZK further improves performance by introducing a faster parallel multi-scalar multiplication algorithm and optimizing data transfer between CPU and GPU \[[4](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/e4a4608e8e35379e63b9df5bda1b7599ee8ab9ac)\]. These accelerators demonstrate the potential of hardware-based optimizations to reduce the overhead of ZKP generation.

Despite these advancements, existing accelerators often face limitations. Some require a trusted setup for each application, which limits their reusability \[[1](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/e149d93531d8c07cf43f6cae327a4795144d1304)\]. Others generate larger proof sizes or have higher verification costs, which can be problematic in scenarios with numerous verifiers or strict time constraints \[[1](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/e149d93531d8c07cf43f6cae327a4795144d1304)\]. For example, the Groth16 zkSNARK protocol, widely used in cryptocurrencies, suffers from high overhead due to its proof generation step, which involves large-scale matrix-vector multiplication, number-theoretic transforms, and multi-scalar multiplication \[[4](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/e4a4608e8e35379e63b9df5bda1b7599ee8ab9ac)\]. 

To address these challenges, recent works have focused on developing more efficient and scalable ZKP protocols. For instance, zkCNN introduces a zero-knowledge proof scheme for convolutional neural networks (CNNs), enabling the verification of model predictions without leaking information about the model itself \[[10](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/6336149196f02d0eb6c1a89bc6b662e4422bd794)\]. Similarly, protocols for zero-knowledge decision tree predictions and accuracy have been proposed, allowing models to prove their correctness on public datasets \[[6](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/3becc2935d1f52c82ffd7216c28bc86f5e393208)\]. These developments highlight the growing interest in applying ZKPs to machine learning and other data-intensive applications.

Zero-knowledge proof (ZKP) acceleration has seen significant advancements through hardware and algorithmic optimizations to address the performance bottlenecks in proof generation. Existing approaches, such as GZKP \[[1](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/e149d93531d8c07cf43f6cae327a4795144d1304)\], PipeZK \[[2](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/6ee84674b7c426849c3da56cb278c08eb7a903cf)\], and cuZK \[[4](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/e4a4608e8e35379e63b9df5bda1b7599ee8ab9ac)\], focus on improving the efficiency of critical operations like polynomial computations, multi-scalar multiplications (MSMs), and number-theoretic transforms (NTTs). GZKP leverages GPU acceleration to enhance speed by optimizing memory access patterns and eliminating costly external shuffles, achieving up to 48.1× speedup on cryptographic benchmarks. Similarly, PipeZK introduces a pipelined architecture with two subsystems to handle large polynomial computations and MSMs, delivering 10× speedup on standard benchmarks and 5× on Zcash. cuZK further improves performance by parallelizing MSMs and MUL operations, reducing CPU-GPU data transfer latency and achieving up to 4.86× speedup on benchmarks. These efforts highlight the growing need for efficient ZKP acceleration to support real-world applications such as blockchain and privacy-preserving machine learning \[[6](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/3becc2935d1f52c82ffd7216c28bc86f5e393208)\]\[[10](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/6336149196f02d0eb6c1a89bc6b662e4422bd794)\]. Additionally, recent works like zkSpeed  focus on accelerating entire ZKP protocols, including SumCheck and MSMs, using custom hardware to achieve geometric mean speedups of 801× over CPU baselines. These developments underscore the importance of optimizing ZKP performance to enable broader adoption in scenarios requiring fast and secure verification.

Zero-knowledge proofs (ZKPs) are cryptographic protocols that allow one party to prove the validity of a statement to another without revealing any additional information \[[3](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/9ccf7b6cb32cf89752a35bd910555adac54773e0)\]. This concept is foundational in privacy-preserving computing, as it ensures that only the truth of a proposition is conveyed, not the underlying data or methods used to derive it \[[3](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/9ccf7b6cb32cf89752a35bd910555adac54773e0)\]. Early research focused on defining zero-knowledge proof systems for languages such as quadratic residuosity and nonresiduosity, which were among the first examples of ZKPs for languages not known to be efficiently recognizable \[[3](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/9ccf7b6cb32cf89752a35bd910555adac54773e0)\]. These systems are critical for applications in blockchain, verifiable machine learning, and secure voting, where privacy and verification are paramount \[[3](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/9ccf7b6cb32cf89752a35bd910555adac54773e0)\]. However, the computational complexity of ZKP protocols has historically limited their adoption, particularly in scenarios requiring rapid verification or large-scale deployment \[[3](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/9ccf7b6cb32cf89752a35bd910555adac54773e0)\]. Recent advancements, such as the development of accelerators like zkSpeed for HyperPlonk, have addressed some of these challenges by improving both the speed and efficiency of ZKP generation, enabling smaller proof sizes and universal setup capabilities \[[3](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/9ccf7b6cb32cf89752a35bd910555adac54773e0)\]. These improvements are essential for expanding the practical applicability of ZKPs in real-world systems \[[3](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/9ccf7b6cb32cf89752a35bd910555adac54773e0)\].
