## Related Works

Protein dynamics are essential for understanding biological functions and properties, yet traditional methods such as molecular dynamics (MD) simulations are computationally expensive and time-consuming \[[1](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/21a4afa74caf78e28880b7fe4c30a09a5f9c14e3)\]. Recent advances in generative modeling have enabled more efficient and accurate protein structure prediction and conformation sampling by learning distributions over crystallographic structures \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/2304.02198)\]. However, integrating physical supervision into these data-driven approaches remains a challenge, as standard energy-based objectives often lead to intractable optimization \[[3](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/2eb1ed9d4baf548391d8220c0a8a3d0be79845ed)\]. This section reviews related works that address these challenges and contribute to the field of protein conformational sampling and structure prediction.

Several studies have explored the use of deep learning for protein structure prediction and conformational sampling. For instance, Boltzmann generators use neural networks to learn coordinate transformations that enable efficient sampling of equilibrium states of many-body systems \[[4](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/2cfc26b4af99f195b433fa7aa00f221b111c7cd4)\]. These models are trained to provide unbiased, one-shot samples from the equilibrium distribution, which is crucial for capturing the conformational diversity of proteins \[[5](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/dc32a984b651256a8ec282be52310e6bd33d9815)\]. Similarly, EigenFold employs a diffusion process to model protein structures as a system of harmonic oscillators, enabling the generation of conformational ensembles \[[6](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/98926d43356e87c22c82efc132dcaaac1ff40ebe)\]. This approach has been shown to provide a more comprehensive picture of model uncertainty compared to existing methods \[[6](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/98926d43356e87c22c82efc132dcaaac1ff40ebe)\].

Another notable work is Chroma, a generative model that can directly sample novel protein structures and sequences while being conditioned to steer the generative process towards desired properties and functions \[[7](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/c49a0912595a1cc70aab63524f64ed08c92194a8)\]. Chroma leverages a diffusion process that respects the conformational statistics of polymer ensembles and uses an efficient neural architecture for molecular systems \[[7](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/c49a0912595a1cc70aab63524f64ed08c92194a8)\]. This allows for long-range reasoning with sub-quadratic scaling, making it suitable for generating complex protein structures \[[7](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/c49a0912595a1cc70aab63524f64ed08c92194a8)\].

In addition, the development of AlphaFold has significantly advanced the field of protein structure prediction \[[8](http://arxiv.org/abs/2306.03117)\]. AlphaFold2 has demonstrated the ability to predict protein structures with atomic accuracy even in cases where no similar structure is known \[[8](http://arxiv.org/abs/2306.03117)\]. This has been achieved through a novel machine learning approach that incorporates physical and biological knowledge about protein structure \[[8](http://arxiv.org/abs/2306.03117)\]. Furthermore, AlphaFold3 has been extended to predict the joint structure of complexes including proteins, nucleic acids, small molecules, ions, and modified residues \[[9](http://arxiv.org/abs/2402.04845)\].

### <a id='category-protein-conformation-generation'></a>Protein Conformation Generation: 9 papers found.
Recent advances in protein conformation generation have focused on overcoming the computational limitations of traditional molecular dynamics (MD) simulations by leveraging machine learning techniques. Several studies have demonstrated the potential of generative models, such as generative adversarial networks (GANs) and diffusion models, to efficiently sample protein conformational ensembles with high fidelity \[[1](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/21a4afa74caf78e28880b7fe4c30a09a5f9c14e3)\]. For instance, idpGAN, a GAN-based model, was trained on coarse-grained simulations of intrinsically disordered peptides to generate sequence-dependent conformational ensembles without explicit sampling \[[1](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/21a4afa74caf78e28880b7fe4c30a09a5f9c14e3)\]. Similarly, Boltzmann generators combine deep learning with statistical mechanics to learn invertible transformations between complex system coordinates and simpler Gaussian representations, enabling one-shot sampling of equilibrium states \[[3](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/2eb1ed9d4baf548391d8220c0a8a3d0be79845ed)\]. These models can also compute free-energy differences and identify reaction coordinates, which are crucial for understanding protein dynamics \[[3](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/2eb1ed9d4baf548391d8220c0a8a3d0be79845ed)\]. Additionally, flow-based generative models like AlphaFlow and ESMFlow have been fine-tuned using single-state predictors such as AlphaFold to generate diverse and accurate protein structures, capturing conformational flexibility and ensemble properties \[[9](http://arxiv.org/abs/2402.04845)\]. Furthermore, diffusion models have been adapted for protein conformation generation by incorporating physical guidance, such as force fields, to ensure generated structures align with physical principles \[[24](http://arxiv.org/abs/2403.14088)\]. These approaches collectively highlight the growing trend of integrating physical knowledge into data-driven models to improve the accuracy and efficiency of protein conformation sampling.

### <a id='category-protein-structure-prediction-models'></a>Protein Structure Prediction Models: 8 papers found.
Protein structure prediction has seen significant advancements with the development of deep learning models that leverage evolutionary information and physical principles to predict high-resolution structures \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/2304.02198)\]. These models, such as AlphaFold \[[12](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/7572ba7f604ef95d7acdd657ebac458106bd35df)\], have demonstrated competitive accuracy with experimental methods, even in cases where no homologous structures are available \[[19](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/867d80c8779e1d301a5fc6e267e263f7e4c4c5c7)\]. However, traditional methods like molecular dynamics (MD) simulations remain computationally expensive and are often limited in their ability to sample conformational ensembles efficiently \[[8](http://arxiv.org/abs/2306.03117)\]. Recent approaches, such as EigenFold \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/2304.02198)\], utilize diffusion generative models to sample distributions of structures, providing a more comprehensive view of model uncertainty by generating ensembles of possible conformations. Similarly, Str2Str \[[8](http://arxiv.org/abs/2306.03117)\] introduces a structure-to-structure translation framework that enables zero-shot conformation sampling without relying on simulation data, offering a faster alternative to long MD simulations. Additionally, models like ProteinMPNN \[[6](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/98926d43356e87c22c82efc132dcaaac1ff40ebe)\] and OmegaFold \[[19](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/867d80c8779e1d301a5fc6e267e263f7e4c4c5c7)\] have shown promising results in protein design and prediction from single sequences, addressing challenges posed by orphan proteins and fast-evolving sequences. These developments highlight a growing trend toward integrating physical insights with data-driven approaches to improve the accuracy and efficiency of protein structure prediction and conformational sampling.

### <a id='category-molecular-dynamics-and-protein-modeling'></a>Molecular Dynamics And Protein Modeling: 3 papers found.
In the realm of molecular dynamics and protein modeling, recent advancements have focused on improving the efficiency and accuracy of protein structure prediction and conformation sampling. Traditional methods rely on time-consuming molecular dynamics (MD) simulations, while newer approaches leverage generative models, such as denoising diffusion models, to learn distributions over crystallographic structures, enabling faster and more accurate predictions \[[16](http://arxiv.org/abs/2302.00600)\]. However, integrating physical supervision into these models remains challenging, as standard energy-based objectives often lead to intractable optimization \[[1](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/21a4afa74caf78e28880b7fe4c30a09a5f9c14e3)\]. To address this, researchers have introduced methods like Energy-based Alignment (EBA), which aligns generative models with feedback from physical models, allowing for more physically plausible structure generation \[[1](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/21a4afa74caf78e28880b7fe4c30a09a5f9c14e3)\]. Additionally, novel generative models such as FoldFlow have been developed, utilizing flow-matching paradigms over $ \text(SE)(3) $ to enable accurate modeling of protein backbones, offering stability and faster training compared to diffusion-based approaches \[[15](http://arxiv.org/abs/2310.02391)\]. Furthermore, coarse-grained (CG) molecular dynamics has been enhanced by learning CG force fields using score-based generative models, which approximate force fields directly from simulated protein structures without requiring explicit force inputs during training \[[16](http://arxiv.org/abs/2302.00600)\]. These approaches highlight the growing integration of physics-based guidance with data-driven generative models to improve the accuracy and efficiency of protein modeling. Additionally, methods like AlphaFold2 have demonstrated the ability to predict multiple conformations of proteins, such as transporters and G-protein-coupled receptors, by modifying input multiple sequence alignments through stochastic subsampling \[[18](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/ff9b84297f16760472c791456befb31ff1d224ef)\]. These developments underscore the ongoing efforts to enhance the predictive power and versatility of protein modeling techniques through both simulation-free and physics-informed generative frameworks.
