## Related Works

Sequential recommendation systems aim to predict the next item a user will interact with, based on their historical behavior. These systems have evolved significantly, incorporating advanced neural network architectures to better capture sequential patterns and user preferences. Early approaches, such as Markov Chains (MCs), assumed that the next action depends only on the last few actions, while Recurrent Neural Networks (RNNs) introduced the ability to model longer-term dependencies \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/1808.09781)\]. However, RNNs often suffer from computational inefficiency and difficulty in capturing long-range dependencies \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/1808.09781)\]. More recently, self-attention mechanisms have gained popularity for their ability to dynamically weigh the importance of different items in a sequence \[[5](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/9c7a455b1e48d01a99e58e884a6c1acb75074ad0)\]. 

The use of self-attention in sequential recommendation was further refined with models like SASRec, which combines the flexibility of attention with the efficiency of MC-based methods by focusing on relevant items in the history \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/1808.09781)\]. Similarly, TiSASRec extends this idea by incorporating time intervals between interactions, allowing for more nuanced modeling of temporal dynamics \[[5](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/9c7a455b1e48d01a99e58e884a6c1acb75074ad0)\]. These models demonstrate the importance of temporal awareness in capturing user behavior accurately.

Convolutional approaches, such as Caser, have also been introduced to model sequential patterns by treating item sequences as images in the time and latent spaces \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/1809.07426)\]. This enables the extraction of local features, which can be useful for capturing both general preferences and sequential patterns. However, these models may not fully address the challenges of modeling long-range dependencies or capturing diverse interaction patterns \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/1809.07426)\].

The introduction of bidirectional models, such as BERT4Rec, has further advanced the field by allowing the model to consider both left and right context of an item, thereby improving the representation of user behavior \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/1904.06690)\]. This bidirectional approach helps in capturing more comprehensive user preferences, but it introduces challenges related to training efficiency and information leakage \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/1904.06690)\]. To mitigate these issues, the Cloze objective is used to train the model by predicting masked items based on their surrounding context \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/1904.06690)\].

In addition to attention-based and convolutional models, there has been growing interest in self-supervised learning for sequential recommendation. S3-Rec, for instance, leverages mutual information maximization to enhance the learning of correlations among item attributes and subsequences \[[4](http://arxiv.org/abs/2008.07873)\]. This approach is particularly beneficial in scenarios with limited training data, as it enables the model to learn meaningful representations without explicit supervision \[[4](http://arxiv.org/abs/2008.07873)\].

Another notable advancement is the use of graph neural networks (GNNs) in session-based recommendation, where session sequences are modeled as graph-structured data to capture complex item transitions
