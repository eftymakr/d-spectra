## Related Works

Automatic calibration of heterogeneous sensors such as radar and camera is a critical step in autonomous systems to ensure accurate perception and robust data fusion. Several approaches have been proposed in recent years to address the challenges of sensor calibration, particularly in the context of radar-camera systems. Early methods often relied on dedicated calibration targets, which limited their applicability in real-world scenarios \[[5](http://arxiv.org/abs/1904.08743)\]. More recent work has focused on targetless calibration techniques, which are essential for practical deployment in dynamic environments.

A notable approach is the automatic targetless extrinsic calibration of multiple 3D LiDAR and radar sensors, which requires only a short driving distance and does not depend on overlapping fields of view \[[2](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/0e88fc24ae7694d0b1a2fe78322936fc568bc7d2)\]. This method estimates the 6-DoF pose of each sensor by minimizing point-to-plane distances between scans and using radial velocity errors for radar sensors. Another method, proposed for radar-camera calibration without dedicated targets, employs a coarse and fine convolutional neural network to achieve precise sensor registration \[[5](http://arxiv.org/abs/1904.08743)\]. This approach is particularly useful in scenarios where manual calibration is impractical.

In addition to targetless calibration, some methods focus on improving the robustness of the calibration process against environmental uncertainties. A continuous-time approach for 3D radar-to-camera extrinsic calibration utilizes radar velocity measurements and does not require specialized radar retroreflectors \[[6](http://arxiv.org/abs/2103.07505)\]. This method is especially relevant for autonomous vehicles operating in adverse weather conditions where traditional calibration targets may be unavailable.

The problem of sparse radar data and height uncertainty is also addressed in several works. A method that combines radar and camera data using a dual-perspective representation, which includes both frontal and bird's-eye views, has been proposed to reduce the impact of height uncertainty \[[1](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/0955326c67a947b5fac5fbae7238d04fc9adc251)\]. This approach uses a selective fusion mechanism to integrate reliable features from both perspectives. Additionally, a multi-modal cross-attention mechanism is employed to find location correspondences through cross-modal matching \[[1](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/0955326c67a947b5fac5fbae7238d04fc9adc251)\].

For online calibration, a method based on moving object tracking has been proposed, which uses detection and tracking of moving objects to perform continuous calibration without requiring ego-motion estimation \[[7](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/c80265fa719eb85c8a56a6b36c74b99b793f39b2)\]. This approach is particularly useful for long-term reliability in autonomous systems. Another method leverages Gaussian processes for spatiotemporal calibration based on moving target tracking, achieving high accuracy in time delay estimation \[[9](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/90d807f4e4999443ce9910ba451675f3b9ce4ba0)\].

In the context of sensor fusion, several studies have explored the integration of radar and camera data for object detection and localization. A feature-level fusion framework, RVDet, has been proposed to align radar and camera features in the bird's-eye view domain, improving the accuracy of object detection

Additionally, we carry out identifiability analysis based on the Fisher Information Matrix to show minimal requirements on the dataset and to verify the method through simulations. We test the calibration method on a variety of sensor configurations and address the problem of radar vertical misalignment. In the end, we show via extensive experiment analysis that the proposed method is able to reliably estimate all the six parameters of the extrinsic calibration.»

### <a id='category-others'></a>Others: 3 papers found.
The related works in radar-camera fusion for autonomous driving systems have focused on improving data association and sensor complementarity. Traditional rule-based methods suffer from performance degradation in challenging scenarios due to noisy radar measurements and depth ambiguity from 2D bounding boxes \[[12](http://arxiv.org/abs/2103.07825)\]. To address these issues, recent studies have explored deep representation learning to enable feature-level interaction and global reasoning, leading to more robust fusion techniques \[[12](http://arxiv.org/abs/2103.07825)\]. For instance, one approach uses radar as the main sensor and camera as auxiliary, combining Mahalanobis distance with joint probability functions to match target sequences and improve environmental perception in severe weather \[[15](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/fd0f0607ad6ea49c4dc886a08ea19afa1a2be3eb)\]. Another method integrates sparse radar data with camera images using a deep learning-based fusion network, such as the CameraRadarFusion Net (CRF-Net), which automatically determines the optimal fusion level and enhances detection accuracy through a training strategy inspired by Dropout \[[19](http://arxiv.org/abs/2005.07431)\]. These approaches highlight the importance of combining radar and camera data to overcome the limitations of individual sensors, particularly in adverse conditions. The proposed method in this paper builds upon these advancements by introducing an online automatic geometric calibration technique that addresses the challenges of data sparsity and measurement uncertainty in radar height data \[[1](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/0955326c67a947b5fac5fbae7238d04fc9adc251)\].
