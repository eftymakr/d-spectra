## Related Works

Cross-domain Sequential Recommendation (CDSR) has gained significant attention due to its ability to address data sparsity and cold-start problems by leveraging user interactions across multiple domains \[[22](http://arxiv.org/abs/2108.03357)\]. Traditional approaches often rely on overlapping users to transfer knowledge between domains, but this limits their applicability in real-world scenarios where such overlap is rare \[[9](http://arxiv.org/abs/2311.04590)\]. To overcome this limitation, several studies have explored alternative strategies. For instance, \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2305.19860)\] proposes a dual learning mechanism that iteratively transfers information between domains, while \[[12](http://arxiv.org/abs/1910.05189)\] introduces a latent orthogonal mapping to preserve relations between users across different latent spaces. These methods aim to improve cross-domain knowledge transfer without relying on overlapping users.

Another key challenge in CDSR is modeling complex transition patterns from mixed behavior sequences \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2305.19860)\]. To address this, \[[7](http://arxiv.org/abs/2012.00485)\] proposes a mixed information flow network that considers both behavioral and knowledge transfer by incorporating behavior transfer units and knowledge transfer units. This approach enables the model to decide when and how to use cross-domain information based on user preferences. Similarly, \[[11](http://arxiv.org/abs/2304.05027)\] introduces a triple sequence learning framework that jointly models source, target, and mixed behavior sequences to highlight global and target preferences. This method captures triple correlations and improves performance by leveraging both coarse-grained similarities and fine-grained distinctions among sequences.

Recent advances in deep learning have also led to the development of more sophisticated models for CDSR. \[[18](http://arxiv.org/abs/2206.05941)\] introduces a universal sequence representation learning approach, named UniSRec, which utilizes item descriptions to learn transferable representations across different recommendation scenarios. This method is particularly useful for cross-platform settings, where item IDs may not be available. Additionally, \[[21](http://arxiv.org/abs/2405.12473)\] proposes a model-agnostic framework called CA-CDSR that focuses on aligning item representations to enhance sequential modeling and user representation alignment. This approach addresses the issue of misaligned item representations, which can lead to sub-optimal recommendations.

Furthermore, \[[19](http://arxiv.org/abs/2307.02046)\] discusses the potential of Large Language Models (LLMs) in enhancing recommender systems by leveraging their strong representation and reasoning capabilities. LLMs can bridge the gap between items and users, capturing preferences from a semantic perspective. This aligns with the motivation behind the proposed LLM4CDSR model, which aims to address the overlap dilemma and transition complexity in CDSR by integrating LLMs into the recommendation process.

### <a id='category-cross-domain-sequential-recommendation'></a>Cross Domain Sequential Recommendation: 9 papers found.
Cross-domain sequential recommendation (CDSR) has attracted significant research interest due to its ability to improve recommendation performance by leveraging user behavior across multiple domains \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2305.19860)\]. Existing approaches often face challenges such as the overlap dilemma, where models rely heavily on users with interactions across all domains, limiting practical applicability \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2305.19860)\], and transition complexity, which involves capturing complex patterns from mixed behavior sequences \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2305.19860)\]. To address these issues, recent works have explored various strategies, such as dual learning mechanisms that enable bidirectional information transfer between domains \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/2304.03891)\], graph-based models that explicitly model cross-domain relationships \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/2103.01696)\], and attention-based architectures that capture both intra- and inter-sequence interactions \[[4](http://arxiv.org/abs/2106.02768)\]. Additionally, some studies have focused on improving the quality of item representations by incorporating knowledge transfer units that model the flow of both behavioral and knowledge information \[[5](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/f3b7672aaab5b65e1eb66aee6f3a5b95fb0c304f)\]. These methods aim to enhance the ability of models to generalize across domains and improve recommendation accuracy. Furthermore, efforts have been made to address the limitations of unidirectional models by introducing bidirectional architectures that allow for more comprehensive modeling of user behavior \[[6](http://arxiv.org/abs/2105.03300)\]. Overall, these approaches highlight the ongoing efforts to overcome the challenges of cross-domain sequential recommendation and improve the effectiveness of recommendation systems in multi-domain settings.

### <a id='category-cross-domain-recommendation-research'></a>Cross Domain Recommendation Research: 7 papers found.

To overcome these limitations, recent studies have explored advanced techniques such as transfer learning and neural networks to improve cross-domain knowledge sharing \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/2103.01696)\]. 	Document 1: [Snippet]: «Cross-Domain Sequential Recommendation(CDSR) aims to generate accurate predictions for future interactions by leveraging users’ cross-domain historical interactions. One major challenge of CDSR is how to jointly learn the single- and cross-domain user preferences efficiently. To enhance the target domain’s performance, most existing solutions start by learning the single-domain user preferences within each domain and then transferring the acquired knowledge from the rich domain to the target domain. However, this approach ignores the inter-sequence item relationship and also limits the opportunities for target domain knowledge to enhance the rich domain performance. Moreover, it also ignores the information within the cross-domain sequence. Despite cross-domain sequences being generally noisy and hard to learn directly, they contain valuable user behavior patterns with great potential to enhance performance. Another key challenge of CDSR is data sparsity, which also exists in other recommendation system problems. In the real world, the data distribution of the recommendation system is highly skewed to the popular products, especially on the large-scale dataset with millions of users and items. One more challenge is the class imbalance problem, inherited by the sequential recommendation problem. Generally, each sample only has one positive and thousands of negative samples. To address the above problems together, an innovative Decoupled Representation via Extraction Attention Module (DREAM) is proposed for CDSR to simultaneously learn single- and cross-domain user preference via decoupled representations. A novel Supervised Contrastive Learning framework is introduced to model the inter-sequence relationship as well as address the data sparsity via data augmentations. DREAM also leverages Focal Loss to put more weight on misclassified samples to address the class-imbalance problem, with another uplift on the overall model performance. Extensive experiments had been conducted on two cross-domain recommendation datasets, demonstrating DREAM outperforms various SOTA cross-domain recommendation algorithms achieving up to a 75% uplift in Movie-Book Scenarios.»
