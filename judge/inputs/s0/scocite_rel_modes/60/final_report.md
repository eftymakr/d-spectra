## Related Works

Calibration of deep learning models has been a central topic in recent years, particularly due to the increasing reliance on neural networks in safety-critical applications. Early work on calibration focused on improving the reliability of confidence scores in classification tasks. For instance, it was observed that modern neural networks are often poorly calibrated, meaning that their predicted probabilities do not accurately reflect the true likelihood of correct predictions \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/1706.04599)\]. This issue was attributed to factors such as network depth, width, weight decay, and the use of Batch Normalization \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/1706.04599)\]. To address this, temperature scaling—a simple post-processing technique—was found to be surprisingly effective in calibrating predictions across various architectures and datasets \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/1706.04599)\].

Further research has explored alternative calibration methods, such as focal loss, which not only improves calibration but also maintains high accuracy \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/2002.09437)\]. Focal loss has been shown to be particularly effective when combined with temperature scaling, leading to state-of-the-art results in calibration without compromising accuracy \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/2002.09437)\]. Additionally, more sophisticated calibration techniques have been proposed, such as Dirichlet calibration, which extends the beta calibration method to multiclass settings and provides a natively multiclass calibration approach \[[11](http://arxiv.org/abs/1910.12656)\]. This method is particularly useful for models where accurate probability estimates are essential \[[11](http://arxiv.org/abs/1910.12656)\].

The limitations of traditional calibration methods have also been highlighted. For example, histogram binning, while effective in some cases, is sample inefficient, requiring a large number of samples to estimate calibration error accurately \[[4](http://arxiv.org/abs/1909.10155)\]. In contrast, scaling methods like temperature scaling are more efficient but may not always provide accurate calibration guarantees \[[4](http://arxiv.org/abs/1909.10155)\]. To address these issues, the scaling-binning calibrator was introduced, which combines the benefits of both approaches by first applying a parametric function to reduce variance and then binning the results to ensure true calibration \[[4](http://arxiv.org/abs/1909.10155)\].

Another line of work has focused on improving the expressiveness of calibration functions. ### <a id='category-uncertainty-calibration-in-deep-learning'></a>Uncertainty Calibration in Deep Learning: 12 papers found.
Modern neural networks often exhibit poor calibration, meaning their predicted probabilities do not accurately reflect the true likelihood of correct predictions \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/1706.04599)\]. This issue has been extensively studied, with research highlighting that factors such as depth, width, weight decay, and Batch Normalization significantly influence calibration \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/1706.04599)\]. To address this, various post-processing calibration methods have been proposed, including temperature scaling, which is a single-parameter variant of Platt Scaling and has been found to be surprisingly effective \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/1706.04599)\]. Additionally, focal loss has been shown to produce well-calibrated models while maintaining accuracy, and when combined with temperature scaling, it achieves state-of-the-art results \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/2002.09437)\]. However, traditional recalibration methods like Platt scaling and temperature scaling have limitations, such as being less calibrated than reported and not providing accurate estimates of miscalibration \[[4](http://arxiv.org/abs/1909.10155)\]. To overcome these challenges, new methods such as scaling-binning calibrator have been introduced, which combine parametric function fitting with binning to ensure true calibration \[[4](http://arxiv.org/abs/1909.10155)\]. Furthermore, Bayesian methods and approaches like Bayesian Binning into Quantiles (BBQ) have been developed to address key limitations of existing calibration methods, offering computationally tractable and empirically accurate solutions \[[6](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/8d115c3b2ee80e0754360a154a9369bc1658b607)\]. The calibration of deep learning models is crucial for applications requiring reliable uncertainty estimates, such as weather forecasting and personalized medicine, where miscalibration can lead to significant errors \[[5](http://arxiv.org/abs/1906.02530)\]. Recent studies also show that training methods like mixup can improve calibration by reducing overconfidence in predictions, particularly on out-of-distribution data \[[10](http://arxiv.org/abs/1905.11001)\]. Overall, the field of uncertainty calibration in deep learning continues to evolve, with a growing emphasis on developing flexible, accurate, and data-efficient calibration techniques \[[12](http://arxiv.org/abs/2106.07998)\].
