## Related Works

Vision Transformers (ViTs) have gained significant attention for their ability to capture global context in computer vision tasks, surpassing traditional convolutional neural networks (CNNs) in certain scenarios \[[4](http://arxiv.org/abs/2010.11929)\]. However, the deployment of ViTs on hardware, especially FPGAs, remains challenging due to the complexity of arithmetic operations such as Softmax and LayerNorm, which are sensitive to precision and difficult to map onto efficient hardware \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2208.05163)\]. Prior works have primarily focused on optimizing matrix multiplication operations on hardware, while keeping complex operations on the CPU, resulting in suboptimal performance due to communication overhead between the CPU and accelerator \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2208.05163)\]. 

Several studies have explored quantization techniques to reduce the computational and memory footprint of ViTs for efficient inference. For example, Auto-ViT-Acc proposes an FPGA-aware acceleration framework with mixed-scheme quantization, achieving higher accuracy than state-of-the-art quantization methods under the same bit-width \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2208.05163)\]. Similarly, VAQF introduces a fully automatic software-hardware co-design framework for low-bit ViTs, enabling real-time execution on FPGAs with minimal compilation time \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/2201.06618)\]. These approaches highlight the potential of quantization in reducing computational demands while preserving accuracy. However, they often rely on manual tuning or limited automation, which can hinder scalability and adaptability.

To address the limitations of existing methods, ViTCoD introduces a dedicated algorithm and accelerator co-design framework that prunes and polarizes attention maps to reduce dominant data movement costs, achieving significant speedups over general computing platforms \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/2210.09573)\]. Additionally, FQ-ViT presents a post-training quantization method for fully quantized ViTs, mitigating performance degradation through systematic techniques like Power-of-Two Factor (PTF) and Log-Int-Softmax (LIS) \[[9](http://arxiv.org/abs/2111.13824)\]. These methods demonstrate the feasibility of quantizing ViTs with minimal accuracy loss, but they often focus on specific aspects of compression without a unified approach.

Recent efforts have also explored structural modifications to ViTs for improved efficiency. For instance, T2T-ViT introduces a Tokens-to-Token transformation to progressively structurize images into tokens, reducing parameter count and MACs while improving performance \[[6](http://arxiv.org/abs/2101.11986)\]. Similarly, CvT integrates convolutions into ViTs to leverage the strengths of both architectures, achieving state-of-the-art performance with fewer parameters \[[13](http://arxiv.org/abs/2103.15808)\]. These approaches demonstrate the potential of hybrid architectures in balancing accuracy and efficiency.

In addition, MobileViT combines the strengths of CNNs and ViTs to create a lightweight, mobile-friendly vision transformer, achieving competitive performance

### <a id='category-vision-transformer-acceleration-techniques'></a>Vision Transformer Acceleration Techniques: 8 papers found.
Vision Transformer (ViT) acceleration has been a focal point in improving the efficiency and performance of transformer-based models in computer vision. Existing approaches have primarily focused on optimizing matrix multiplication operations while leaving complex operations like Softmax and LayerNorm on the CPU, leading to suboptimal performance due to CPU-accelerator communication overhead \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2208.05163)\]. To address this, recent works have explored dedicated hardware accelerators for ViTs, such as FPGA-based frameworks that incorporate model quantization to enhance performance without significant accuracy loss \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2208.05163)\]. Additionally, algorithmic and hardware co-design frameworks like ViTCoD have been proposed to prune and polarize attention maps, reducing computational costs and improving hardware utilization through optimized data movement \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/2210.09573)\]. Furthermore, novel architectures such as T2T-ViT and TNT have been introduced to enhance the local structure modeling and attention mechanisms, achieving better performance with reduced parameters and computational costs \[[6](http://arxiv.org/abs/2101.11986)\]\[[7](http://arxiv.org/abs/2103.00112)\]. These advancements highlight the ongoing efforts to improve ViT efficiency through both architectural innovations and hardware-specific optimizations.

### <a id='category-others'></a>Others: 5 papers found.
Vision Transformer (ViT) models have gained significant attention for their ability to capture global context in computer vision tasks, but their deployment on hardware, especially edge devices, remains challenging due to high computational complexity and model size \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2208.05163)\]. Several works have focused on improving the efficiency of ViT inference by leveraging quantization and hardware acceleration. For instance, VAQF introduces a fully automatic framework for quantizing ViTs with binary weights and low-precision activations, enabling real-time execution on FPGAs by optimizing both software and hardware components \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/2201.06618)\]. Similarly, FQ-ViT addresses the performance degradation in fully quantized ViTs by introducing the Power-of-Two Factor (PTF) to reduce inter-channel variation in LayerNorm inputs and Log-Int-Softmax (LIS) to sustain attention map distributions with 4-bit quantization \[[9](http://arxiv.org/abs/2111.13824)\]. Additionally, methods like patch slimming have been proposed to reduce computational costs by removing redundant patches while maintaining model performance \[[10](http://arxiv.org/abs/2106.02852)\]. These approaches highlight the ongoing efforts to balance accuracy and efficiency in ViT inference, particularly through quantization strategies and hardware-specific optimizations. Furthermore, LeViT introduces a hybrid architecture that combines the strengths of convolutional neural networks and transformers, optimizing for both speed and accuracy in image classification tasks \[[11](http://arxiv.org/abs/2104.01136)\]. Lastly, VITALITY presents a novel algorithm-hardware co-designed framework that unifies low-rank and sparse components in ViT attention mechanisms, significantly improving inference efficiency without compromising accuracy \[[16](http://arxiv.org/abs/2211.05109)\]. These contributions collectively emphasize the importance of addressing the computational challenges of ViTs through both algorithmic innovations and hardware-specific optimizations.

### <a id='category-vision-transformer-architectures'></a>Vision Transformer Architectures: 5 papers found.
Vision Transformer (ViT) architectures have emerged as a promising alternative to traditional convolutional neural networks (CNNs) in computer vision tasks, offering strong performance while reducing computational overhead \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2208.05163)\]. Unlike CNNs, which rely on spatial inductive biases, ViTs capture global dependencies through self-attention mechanisms, enabling them to achieve competitive results on tasks like image classification and dense prediction \[[4](http://arxiv.org/abs/2010.11929)\]. However, the computational complexity of ViTs, particularly due to multi-head self-attention modules, has posed challenges for efficient deployment, especially on resource-constrained devices \[[12](http://arxiv.org/abs/2110.02178)\]. To address this, researchers have explored various compression techniques, such as pruning, layer skipping, and knowledge distillation, to reduce model size and inference cost while maintaining accuracy \[[8](http://arxiv.org/abs/2203.08243)\]. Additionally, hybrid models like MobileViT aim to combine the strengths of CNNs and ViTs by integrating local convolutional operations with global attention mechanisms, resulting in lightweight and efficient architectures suitable for mobile vision tasks \[[12](http://arxiv.org/abs/2110.02178)\]. Furthermore, the Swin Transformer introduces a hierarchical design with shifted windows, enabling efficient processing of high-resolution images while maintaining cross-window connectivity, thereby improving performance on tasks such as object detection and semantic segmentation \[[17](http://arxiv.org/abs/2103.14030)\]. These advancements highlight the ongoing efforts to enhance the efficiency and applicability of ViT-based models in real-world scenarios.
