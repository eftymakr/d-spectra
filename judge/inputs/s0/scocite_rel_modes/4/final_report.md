## Related Works

The problem of learning from noisy labels has been extensively studied in recent years, with a focus on developing robust training strategies for deep neural networks. Early approaches often treated label noise as statistical outliers and employed techniques such as importance re-weighting and bootstrapping to mitigate its effects \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/1703.02391)\]. However, these methods typically assume that noisy labels are independent of the true labels, which is not always the case in real-world scenarios \[[10](http://arxiv.org/abs/1712.09482)\]. 

More recent work has explored the use of loss functions that are inherently robust to label noise. For example, the mean absolute error (MAE) loss has been proposed as a noise-robust alternative to the categorical cross entropy (CCE) loss \[[14](http://arxiv.org/abs/1805.07836)\]. While MAE shows promise, it can perform poorly with deep neural networks on challenging datasets \[[14](http://arxiv.org/abs/1805.07836)\]. To address this, generalized cross entropy loss functions have been introduced, offering a theoretically grounded approach that generalizes both MAE and CCE \[[14](http://arxiv.org/abs/1805.07836)\].

Another line of research has focused on leveraging auxiliary information, such as clean datasets or label relations in knowledge graphs, to improve learning from noisy labels \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/1703.02391)\]. This approach, known as distillation, uses side information to "hedge the risk" of learning from noisy labels \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/1703.02391)\]. Additionally, some methods have explored the use of symmetric cross entropy learning (SL), which combines cross entropy with a noise-robust counterpart, reverse cross entropy (RCE), to address both overfitting and under-learning issues \[[4](http://arxiv.org/abs/1908.06112)\].

The problem of label noise is also closely related to the broader issue of robustness in deep learning. Several studies have investigated the memorization effects of deep neural networks, showing that they tend to first memorize clean labels before those with noise \[[7](http://arxiv.org/abs/1804.06872)\]. This insight has led to the development of methods such as Co-teaching, where two neural networks are trained simultaneously and learn from each other \[[7](http://arxiv.org/abs/1804.06872)\]. Variants of this approach, such as Co-teaching+, have been proposed to address convergence issues and improve robustness \[[11](http://arxiv.org/abs/1901.04215)\].

In addition to these methods, there has been growing interest in semi-supervised learning as a way to reduce the reliance on clean labels. For example, DivideMix leverages semi-supervised learning techniques to dynamically divide training data into labeled and unlabeled sets, improving performance on noisy datasets \[[8](http://arxiv.org/abs/2002.07394)\]. Similarly, L_DMI introduces an information-theoretic loss function that is provably robust to instance-independent label noise, without requiring auxiliary information \[[12](http://arxiv.org/abs/1909.03388)\].

The category "Label Noise Robust Deep Learning" encompasses a range of approaches aimed at improving the performance of deep neural networks in the presence of noisy labels. These methods address the challenge of training accurate models when the training data contains incorrect or corrupted labels, which can severely degrade model performance. Several studies propose frameworks that leverage semi-supervised learning, auxiliary information, or modified loss functions to mitigate the impact of label noise \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/1706.00038)\]. For instance, some works introduce graphical models to represent the relationship between noisy and clean labels, enabling tractable inference and regularization during training \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/1706.00038)\]. Others, such as CleanNet, focus on reducing the need for human supervision by transferring knowledge of label noise across classes using a joint neural embedding network \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/1711.07131)\]. Additionally, approaches like Symmetric Cross Entropy Learning (SL) aim to address both overfitting to noisy labels and under-learning of hard classes by combining cross entropy with a noise-robust counterpart \[[4](http://arxiv.org/abs/1908.06112)\]. The use of dimensionality-driven learning strategies has also been explored, where the model adapts its loss function based on the dimensionality of the deep representation subspace to tolerate noisy labels \[[6](http://arxiv.org/abs/1806.02612)\]. Furthermore, methods such as Co-teaching and DivideMix leverage semi-supervised learning techniques to dynamically divide training data into clean and noisy subsets, improving robustness through collaborative learning between multiple models \[[7](http://arxiv.org/abs/1804.06872)\]\[[8](http://arxiv.org/abs/2002.07394)\]. Theoretical insights into noise-tolerant loss functions, such as mean absolute error (MAE), have also been proposed to ensure robustness without requiring additional hyperparameters or auxiliary information \[[10](http://arxiv.org/abs/1712.09482)\]. Overall, these approaches highlight the importance of designing models and training strategies that can effectively handle label noise, ensuring reliable performance in real-world scenarios where data annotation is imperfect \[[12](http://arxiv.org/abs/1909.03388)\].
