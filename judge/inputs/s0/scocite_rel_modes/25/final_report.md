## Related Works

The integration of knowledge graphs (KGs) with large language models (LLMs) to enhance reasoning capabilities has gained significant attention in recent years. While LLMs have demonstrated strong reasoning abilities, they often struggle with structured knowledge retrieval and multi-step reasoning tasks \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2306.08302)\]. KGs, on the other hand, provide structured, semantically rich representations that are well-suited for reasoning \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2306.08302)\]. However, most existing verification methods rely on unstructured text corpora, limiting their ability to effectively leverage KGs \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2306.08302)\]. 

Several studies have explored the use of KGs to improve the performance of LLMs. For instance, the QA-GNN model leverages LLMs to estimate the importance of KG nodes and performs joint reasoning over the QA context and KG \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/2104.06378)\]. This approach enhances the interpretability and accuracy of the reasoning process. Similarly, the RAG framework combines pre-trained parametric models with non-parametric memory, such as Wikipedia, to improve the factual accuracy of generated responses \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/2005.11401)\]. These approaches demonstrate the potential of integrating KGs with LLMs for knowledge-intensive tasks.

The concept of using KGs to enhance LLMs has also been explored in the context of multi-document question answering (MD-QA). The Knowledge Graph Prompting (KGP) method constructs a knowledge graph over multiple documents and uses an LLM-based graph traversal agent to gather supporting passages for MD-QA \[[6](http://arxiv.org/abs/2308.11730)\]. This approach improves the accuracy and efficiency of the reasoning process by leveraging the structured knowledge from KGs. 

Another line of research focuses on improving the zero-shot reasoning capabilities of LLMs. The Zero-shot-CoT method demonstrates that adding a simple prompt such as "Let's think step by step" significantly improves the performance of LLMs on diverse reasoning tasks \[[4](http://arxiv.org/abs/2205.11916)\]. This finding highlights the potential of LLMs to perform complex reasoning without the need for extensive training or fine-tuning. 

The challenge of hallucination in LLMs has also been addressed through retrieval-augmented generation (RAG) approaches. The LLM-Augmenter system augments LLMs with external knowledge and iteratively revises prompts to improve the factuality of responses \[[12](http://arxiv.org/abs/2302.12813)\]. This approach reduces hallucinations without sacrificing the fluency and informativeness of the responses. 

In addition, the use of KGs for commonsense reasoning has been explored. The KagNet model utilizes external commonsense knowledge graphs to perform explainable inferences \[[17](http://arxiv.org/abs/1909.02151)\]. This approach enhances the transparency and interpretability of the reasoning process

### <a id='category-knowledge-graphs-and-language-models'></a>Knowledge Graphs and Language Models: 3 papers found.
The integration of knowledge graphs (KGs) with large language models (LLMs) to enhance reasoning and factual accuracy has gained significant attention, as both models complement each other in different ways. KGs, such as Wikipedia and Huapu, provide structured, semantically rich representations that are ideal for reasoning tasks, while LLMs like GPT-4 offer strong generalization and inference capabilities \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2306.08302)\]. However, LLMs often struggle with hallucination, outdated knowledge, and lack transparency in reasoning processes, which limits their effectiveness in knowledge-intensive tasks \[[5](http://arxiv.org/abs/2312.10997)\]. To address these limitations, Retrieval-Augmented Generation (RAG) has emerged as a promising approach, combining LLMs with external databases to improve accuracy and enable continuous knowledge updates \[[5](http://arxiv.org/abs/2312.10997)\]. Additionally, recent studies have explored the use of LLMs for KG construction and reasoning, demonstrating that models like GPT-4 excel in reasoning tasks and can serve as inference assistants rather than just information extractors \[[14](http://arxiv.org/abs/2305.13168)\]. These findings highlight the potential of synergizing LLMs and KGs, either through KG-enhanced LLMs, LLM-augmented KGs, or fully integrated systems that leverage the strengths of both \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2306.08302)\]. The proposed ClaimPKG framework exemplifies this synergy by integrating LLM reasoning with structured knowledge from KGs, achieving state-of-the-art performance on benchmark datasets \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2306.08302)\].

### <a id='category-knowledge-graph-enhanced-question-answering'></a>Knowledge Graph Enhanced Question Answering: 3 papers found.
The category "Knowledge Graph Enhanced Question Answering" explores methods that integrate large language models (LLMs) with knowledge graphs (KGs) to improve reasoning and answer accuracy in question-answering tasks. Existing approaches address two main challenges: identifying relevant KG nodes relative to a question and performing joint reasoning over both the question-answer context and the KG \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2306.08302)\]. For instance, QA-GNN introduces relevance scoring using LMs to prioritize KG nodes and joint reasoning through graph-based message passing to align QA contexts with KG structures \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/2104.06378)\]. Similarly, a novel prompting pipeline, \method, enhances LLMs by incorporating KGs for transparent and interpretable inference, particularly in medical domains, while reducing hallucinations \[[8](http://arxiv.org/abs/2308.09729)\]. Another framework, KagNet, leverages graph convolutional networks and LSTMs with attention mechanisms to perform explainable inferences using commonsense knowledge graphs like ConceptNet, achieving state-of-the-art results on CommonsenseQA \[[17](http://arxiv.org/abs/1909.02151)\]. These methods highlight the growing interest in combining structured KG knowledge with LLMs to enable more robust, interpretable, and accurate question-answering systems.
