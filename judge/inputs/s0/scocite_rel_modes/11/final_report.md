## Related Works

3D human pose estimation has attracted significant research interest due to its applications in robotics, virtual reality, and human-computer interaction. Early approaches primarily relied on 2D pose estimation followed by 3D reconstruction, often using optimization techniques to infer depth \[[26](http://arxiv.org/abs/1611.07828)\]. However, these methods are limited by the need for accurate 3D ground truth and the inability to capture complex spatial relationships \[[23](http://arxiv.org/abs/1805.04095)\]. Recent advances have focused on deep learning models that directly estimate 3D poses from 2D inputs, leveraging convolutional neural networks (CNNs) and graph-based architectures to model joint dependencies \[[1](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/50167a9e6bdc1f64a58fe8a3c990105fec0bd2bc)\]\[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/2103.16385)\]\[[21](http://arxiv.org/abs/2111.12707)\].

A key challenge in 3D human pose estimation is modeling long-range dependencies between body joints, which are essential for accurate pose reconstruction \[[29](http://arxiv.org/abs/2307.05853)\]. Traditional CNNs struggle with this due to their limited receptive fields and inability to capture global context \[[26](http://arxiv.org/abs/1611.07828)\]. To address this, several works have introduced graph-based models that explicitly encode spatial relationships between joints. For instance, \[[1](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/50167a9e6bdc1f64a58fe8a3c990105fec0bd2bc)\] proposed a graph convolutional network (GCN) that incorporates domain knowledge about human body configurations to improve 3D pose estimation. Similarly, \[[11](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/0646857f867d9a624f2b53d1ffb4de99e225b2d3)\] introduced a modulated GCN that allows for more flexible modeling of joint relationships by learning different feature transformations for each node. These approaches demonstrate the effectiveness of graph-based models in capturing spatial dependencies.

Temporal consistency is another critical factor in 3D human pose estimation, particularly when dealing with video sequences \[[12](http://arxiv.org/abs/1711.08585)\]. Many methods have explored temporal modeling to improve the smoothness and accuracy of 3D pose estimates. For example, \[[12](http://arxiv.org/abs/1711.08585)\] introduced a sequence-to-sequence network with LSTM units to model temporal dependencies, while \[[20](http://arxiv.org/abs/2103.14304)\] proposed a strided transformer architecture to efficiently aggregate information across a sequence of 2D joint locations. These approaches highlight the importance of temporal modeling in achieving temporally coherent 3D pose estimates.

Recent works have also focused on improving the generalization of 3D human pose estimation models, especially for rare or unseen poses \[[5](http://arxiv.org/abs/2007.09389)\]. \[[5](http://arxiv.org/abs/2007.09389)\] proposed a split-and-recombine approach that processes local body regions separately and recombines global context to improve performance on rare poses. This approach is particularly useful in scenarios where training data is imbalanced. Additionally, \[[15](http://arxiv.org/abs/2108.07181)\] introduced a skeletal GNN learning solution that effectively captures long-range dependencies and improves performance on challenging poses.

In summary, the field of 3D human pose estimation has seen significant advancements, with a focus on modeling spatial and temporal dependencies
