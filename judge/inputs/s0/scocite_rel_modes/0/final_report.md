## Related Works

Taxation and economic inequality have been extensively studied in both theoretical and applied economics. Traditional models of income taxation, such as the U.S. federal income tax, aim to reduce inequality but often lack adaptability to heterogeneous taxpayer behavior and irrational decision-making \[[13](http://arxiv.org/abs/2103.16977)\]. The Saez Optimal Taxation model, for instance, provides a dynamic framework for tax policy but fails to account for the complexities of taxpayer heterogeneity and behavioral irrationality \[[13](http://arxiv.org/abs/2103.16977)\]. This limitation has motivated the development of more sophisticated models that integrate behavioral economics and agent-based modeling to better reflect real-world tax compliance and evasion dynamics.

Agent-based models (ABMs) have been widely used to simulate tax evasion and compliance behavior. These models often incorporate social interactions, heterogeneous agent behavior, and enforcement mechanisms to capture the complexity of tax systems \[[12](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/a065b1629ff85e2a0db928bd8ce115b2f5e2a80f)\]. For example, studies have shown that tax evasion can be influenced by social conformity, fairness, and trust in governmental institutions \[[12](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/a065b1629ff85e2a0db928bd8ce115b2f5e2a80f)\]. The Ising model has also been applied to study tax evasion dynamics, where agents' behaviors are influenced by their neighbors, and enforcement mechanisms such as audits can significantly reduce evasion \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/0805.0998)\]. Similarly, the majority-vote model has been used to analyze and control tax evasion fluctuations, showing that even minimal audit rates can have a substantial impact on compliance \[[11](http://arxiv.org/abs/0910.1964)\].

In addition to behavioral models, reinforcement learning techniques have been explored to improve the adaptability of tax policies. Deep reinforcement learning (DRL) has been applied to solve heterogeneous general equilibrium economic models, enabling the incorporation of diverse economic agents and their interactions \[[13](http://arxiv.org/abs/2103.16977)\]. This approach allows for more flexible and data-driven policy evaluation, as agents learn to optimize their behavior in response to changing economic conditions \[[13](http://arxiv.org/abs/2103.16977)\]. Furthermore, multi-agent reinforcement learning has been used to model complex interactions between taxpayers and tax authorities, with applications in both cooperative and competitive environments \[[16](http://arxiv.org/abs/1706.02275)\].

The integration of large language models (LLMs) with ABMs, as proposed in the TaxAgent framework, represents a novel approach to designing adaptive tax policies. This integration allows for the dynamic optimization of tax rates based on real-time data and behavioral insights, offering a more equitable and efficient alternative to traditional taxation systems \[[13](http://arxiv.org/abs/2103.16977)\]. By leveraging the strengths of both ABMs and LLMs, TaxAgent addresses the limitations of existing models by incorporating heterogeneous agent behavior and rational decision-making \[[13](http://arxiv.org/abs/2103.16977)\].

### <a id='category-tax-evasion-agent-based-models'></a>Tax Evasion Agent Based Models: 12 papers found.
The category "Tax Evasion Agent Based Models" explores the integration of agent-based modeling with economic theories to simulate and control tax evasion behaviors. Researchers have extended traditional models, such as the Ising model, to incorporate social interactions and enforcement mechanisms, demonstrating that tax evasion can be significantly reduced through strategic audits and policy interventions \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/0805.0998)\]. These models often simulate heterogeneous agents, allowing for the representation of diverse taxpayer behaviors and responses to external factors like trust in governmental institutions \[[9](http://arxiv.org/abs/0806.0344)\]. The inclusion of external magnetic fields in the Ising model helps capture the influence of societal trust on tax morale, showing that higher trust correlates with lower evasion rates \[[9](http://arxiv.org/abs/0806.0344)\]. Additionally, studies have shown that even minimal audit rates can have a substantial impact on reducing tax evasion, suggesting that enforcement mechanisms are crucial for policy effectiveness \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/0805.0998)\]. The use of Monte-Carlo simulations and network structures such as Barabasi–Albert and Voronoi–Delaunay networks further enhances the robustness of these models, allowing for the analysis of tax evasion across different social and economic contexts \[[10](http://arxiv.org/abs/1711.00832)\]. Moreover, models that incorporate social conformity and fairness effects have been tested using experimental data, revealing that certain behavioral patterns can be modeled and predicted with high accuracy \[[11](http://arxiv.org/abs/0910.1964)\]. These findings collectively highlight the potential of agent-based models to provide insights into tax evasion dynamics and support the development of more effective fiscal policies.

### <a id='category-others'></a>Others: 2 papers found.
The Related Works section for the paper abstract focuses on multi-agent reinforcement learning (MARL) and its applications in complex decision-making environments. Existing approaches, such as independent reinforcement learning (InRL), treat each agent's experience as part of a non-stationary environment, which can lead to overfitting during training and poor generalization during execution \[[10](http://arxiv.org/abs/1711.00832)\]. This limitation highlights the need for more robust methods that account for interactions among agents. Recent work has explored deep reinforcement learning methods that address these challenges by incorporating action policies of other agents, enabling complex multi-agent coordination \[[16](http://arxiv.org/abs/1706.02275)\]. These methods often utilize ensemble policies to improve robustness and adaptability in both cooperative and competitive scenarios. Additionally, algorithms based on approximate best responses and empirical game-theoretic analysis have been proposed to generalize previous approaches like InRL, iterated best response, and fictitious play \[[10](http://arxiv.org/abs/1711.00832)\]. These developments underscore the growing interest in scalable and adaptive MARL frameworks that can effectively model real-world interactions. The TaxAgent framework, which integrates large language models with agent-based modeling, builds upon these advancements by addressing taxpayer heterogeneity and irrational behavior in economic policy design.

### <a id='category-economic-models-with-reinforcement-learning'></a>Economic Models with Reinforcement Learning: 2 papers found.
The integration of reinforcement learning (RL) into economic modeling has gained traction as a method to address the limitations of traditional approaches in capturing heterogeneous agent behavior and bounded rationality \[[13](http://arxiv.org/abs/2103.16977)\]. Unlike conventional semi-analytical techniques, which struggle to incorporate diverse economic actors, RL-based methods enable models to simulate dynamic interactions between agents and their environment in a more flexible and computationally efficient manner \[[13](http://arxiv.org/abs/2103.16977)\]. This approach is particularly valuable in macroeconomic models where stochasticity and heterogeneity play a critical role, such as in analyzing the impact of pandemics or differential health risks across age groups \[[13](http://arxiv.org/abs/2103.16977)\]. Recent work has demonstrated that deep reinforcement learning can be used to solve dynamic stochastic general equilibrium (DSGE) models by representing agents as deep neural networks that learn optimal behavior through interaction with the model environment \[[14](http://arxiv.org/abs/2104.09368)\]. This method not only enhances the ability to model bounded rationality but also allows for greater adaptability across different policy regimes, as shown in applications involving monetary and fiscal policy interactions \[[14](http://arxiv.org/abs/2104.09368)\]. These advancements align with broader efforts to improve the realism and responsiveness of economic models, especially in the context of complex policy challenges such as taxation and inequality \[[13](http://arxiv.org/abs/2103.16977)\].
