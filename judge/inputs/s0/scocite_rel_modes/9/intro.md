## Related Works

Quantitative susceptibility mapping (QSM) has emerged as a powerful tool for characterizing multiple sclerosis (MS) lesions, particularly in distinguishing between rim and non-rim lesion types \[[12](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/b4d08f780bb2ec7f8686ebc4890761ab8b1b4f5c)\]. Rim lesions, characterized by a paramagnetic rim due to iron deposition, are associated with more aggressive disease progression and greater tissue damage \[[6](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/bb5b80dc4165d0ac3e28d1882b898633e84779c2)\]. The ability to accurately detect and segment these lesions is critical for monitoring disease progression and evaluating treatment efficacy \[[16](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/a6c3b459dcaed58347ae0d270c0cfd93ee8aed6f)\]. However, the rarity of rim lesions presents a significant challenge due to class imbalance in training datasets \[[1](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/45bf918158d3f779cb41b982fa9237682c11dd96)\].

Several studies have explored automated methods for detecting and segmenting MS lesions. QSMRim-Net, a deep neural network, was developed to address the class imbalance by incorporating synthetic minority oversampling techniques and fusing lesion-level radiomic and convolutional image features \[[1](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/45bf918158d3f779cb41b982fa9237682c11dd96)\]. This method demonstrated improved performance on both lesion-level and subject-level evaluations, achieving a high correlation with expert annotations \[[1](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/45bf918158d3f779cb41b982fa9237682c11dd96)\]. Similarly, a fully automated detection method for paramagnetic rim lesions (PRLs) was proposed using T2*-phase images and random forest classification, achieving a high correlation with gold-standard counts \[[7](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/09b2a6040c15e8672169531fc76bba0b5a04296b)\]. These approaches highlight the importance of integrating both radiomic and morphological features for accurate classification.

The use of deep learning for MS lesion segmentation has also gained traction. A cascaded 3D convolutional neural network (CNN) approach was introduced to improve segmentation accuracy by reducing misclassification in the first network \[[8](http://arxiv.org/abs/1702.04869)\]. Another study proposed a multi-branch CNN that leverages multi-modal data and multi-scale feature fusion to enhance segmentation performance \[[10](http://arxiv.org/abs/1811.02942)\]. Additionally, a 2.5D stacked slices approach using the Tiramisu architecture was shown to improve segmentation accuracy by combining information from different anatomical planes \[[27](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/3fe6e8e47663a38c037813bc35524fe0931b31b2)\]. These methods underscore the potential of deep learning in overcoming the challenges of lesion heterogeneity and variability in MRI data.

To address the class imbalance problem, synthetic data generation has been explored. In this work, synthetic QSM maps of PRLs were generated to augment training datasets and improve classifier performance \[[1](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/45bf918158d3f779cb41b982fa9237682c11dd96)\]. This approach is similar to previous efforts where synthetic data was used to enhance the minority class in lesion segmentation tasks \[[16](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/a6c3b459dcaed58347ae0d270c0cfd93ee8aed6f)\]. Furthermore, a geometric loss function was introduced to improve segmentation accuracy by incorporating lesion shape and spatial constraints \[[14](http://arxiv.org/abs/2009.13755)\]. These techniques demonstrate the importance of data augmentation and loss function design in improving segmentation performance.
