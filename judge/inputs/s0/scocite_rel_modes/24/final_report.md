## Related Works

Diffusion models have gained significant traction in image and video generation due to their ability to produce high-quality outputs \[[24](http://arxiv.org/abs/2310.00426)\]. However, their deployment on edge devices is hindered by high computational demands and memory usage \[[24](http://arxiv.org/abs/2310.00426)\]. To address these challenges, various quantization techniques have been proposed for diffusion models. Q-Diffusion \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2302.04304)\] introduces a post-training quantization (PTQ) method tailored for diffusion models, targeting the multi-timestep pipeline and model architecture. It identifies key challenges such as changing output distributions over time steps and bimodal activation distributions, and proposes timestep-aware calibration and split shortcut quantization to mitigate these issues. This method successfully quantizes full-precision models to 4-bit while maintaining comparable performance \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2302.04304)\].

Diffusion Transformers (DiTs), an alternative to traditional U-Net-based diffusion models, have shown superior scalability and performance in image and video generation \[[8](http://arxiv.org/abs/2212.09748)\]. Despite their advantages, DiTs face challenges in deployment due to their high parameter count and computational complexity \[[11](http://arxiv.org/abs/2406.17343)\]. PTQ4DiT \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/2405.16005)\] is a PTQ method specifically designed for DiTs, addressing challenges such as salient channels with extreme magnitudes and temporal variability in activation distributions. It introduces Channel-wise Salience Balancing (CSB) and Spearmen's ρ-guided Salience Calibration (SSC) to improve quantization accuracy. PTQ4DiT successfully quantizes DiTs to 8-bit precision (W8A8) and further enables 4-bit weight precision (W4A8) for the first time \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/2405.16005)\].

For video generation, diffusion models face additional challenges due to the need for temporal coherence and high-resolution outputs \[[22](http://arxiv.org/abs/2204.03458)\]. Latte \[[14](http://arxiv.org/abs/2401.03048)\] is a latent diffusion transformer designed for video generation, incorporating spatio-temporal tokens and efficient variants to model video distribution in the latent space. It achieves state-of-the-art performance on standard video generation datasets \[[14](http://arxiv.org/abs/2401.03048)\]. Similarly, Stable Video Diffusion \[[27](http://arxiv.org/abs/2311.15127)\] extends latent diffusion models to video generation, emphasizing the importance of well-curated pretraining datasets for generating high-quality videos. It demonstrates the necessity of a strong base model for downstream tasks like image-to-video generation \[[27](http://arxiv.org/abs/2311.15127)\].

Quantization methods for diffusion models often struggle with maintaining performance at low bit-widths due to the unique properties of diffusion models, such as temporal variation in activation \[[7](http://arxiv.org/abs/2306.02316)\]. PTQD \[[5](http://arxiv.org/abs/2305.10657)\] introduces a unified formulation for quantization noise and diffusion perturbed noise, addressing issues like mean deviation and variance schedule

### <a id='category-diffusion-model-quantization-techniques'></a>Diffusion Model Quantization Techniques: 18 papers found.
Diffusion model quantization techniques have emerged as critical for enabling efficient deployment on resource-constrained devices, addressing the high computational and memory demands of these models \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2302.04304)\]. Traditional post-training quantization (PTQ) methods, while effective for convolutional neural networks, face significant challenges when applied to diffusion models due to their unique multi-timestep structure and dynamic activation distributions \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/2211.15736)\]. Recent works have focused on developing PTQ methods tailored for diffusion models, such as PTQ4DM, which introduces calibration datasets and metrics specifically for multi-time-step scenarios \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/2211.15736)\]. These methods aim to reduce the bit-width of weights and activations without significant performance degradation, achieving 8-bit precision with minimal FID increases \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/2211.15736)\]. For diffusion transformers (DiTs), which have shown superior scalability and performance in image and video generation, quantization challenges include spatial and temporal variance in activations and weights \[[10](http://arxiv.org/abs/2111.12293)\]. Techniques like Q-DiT and PTQ4DiT have been proposed to address these issues, employing dynamic quantization strategies and calibration methods to maintain generation quality \[[10](http://arxiv.org/abs/2111.12293)\]. Additionally, methods such as HQ-DiT and ViDiT-Q have further advanced the field by enabling 4-bit quantization for DiTs with negligible performance loss \[[13](http://arxiv.org/abs/2106.08295)\]\[[12](http://arxiv.org/abs/2406.02540)\]. These advancements highlight the growing importance of efficient quantization techniques for diffusion models, ensuring their practicality in real-world applications \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2302.04304)\].

### <a id='category-video-generation-and-diffusion-models'></a>Video Generation and Diffusion Models: 7 papers found.
Diffusion models have shown significant promise in video generation, offering high-quality outputs through a sequential application of denoising processes \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2302.04304)\]. However, their computational demands pose challenges for deployment on resource-constrained devices \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/2405.16005)\]. To address this, researchers have explored quantization techniques to reduce memory usage and improve inference speed \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/2211.15736)\]. For instance, ZeroQuant enables efficient quantization of large Transformer-based models, achieving up to 5.19x speedup with minimal accuracy loss \[[4](http://arxiv.org/abs/2310.03270)\]. Similarly, SVDQuant introduces a 4-bit quantization paradigm that mitigates the sensitivity of weights and activations, achieving substantial memory reduction and speedup in diffusion models \[[5](http://arxiv.org/abs/2305.10657)\]. In the context of video generation, recent work has focused on extending diffusion models to handle temporal coherence, introducing conditional sampling techniques and temporal layers to generate long, high-resolution videos \[[6](http://arxiv.org/abs/2112.10752)\]. Additionally, latent diffusion models (LDMs) have been adapted for video by incorporating temporal layers and fine-tuning on high-quality video datasets, leading to state-of-the-art results in text-to-video and image-to-video generation \[[7](http://arxiv.org/abs/2306.02316)\]. These advancements highlight the ongoing efforts to balance quality and efficiency in video generation, leveraging both architectural innovations and quantization strategies \[[8](http://arxiv.org/abs/2212.09748)\].

The paper introduces a better architecture for unconditional image synthesis and improves conditional image synthesis through classifier guidance, which uses gradients from a classifier to trade off diversity for fidelity \[[18](http://arxiv.org/abs/2105.05233)\]. This method achieves low FID scores on various ImageNet resolutions, demonstrating high-quality image generation. Additionally, the paper explores the integration of classifier guidance with upsampling diffusion models, further enhancing performance \[[18](http://arxiv.org/abs/2105.05233)\].

Document 2 discusses the computational inefficiency of classifier-free guided diffusion models, which require evaluating both conditional and unconditional models multiple times during inference. To address this, the paper proposes a distillation approach that reduces the number of sampling steps needed while maintaining visual quality and performance metrics like FID and IS scores \[[30](http://arxiv.org/abs/2210.03142)\]. This method significantly accelerates inference, achieving up to 256 times speedup on certain datasets. The approach is applicable to both pixel-space and latent-space diffusion models, including popular frameworks like Stable Diffusion, and is also effective for tasks such as text-guided image editing and inpainting \[[30](http://arxiv.org/abs/2210.03142)\].

These two documents contribute to the broader field of diffusion models for image synthesis by addressing challenges related to quality, efficiency, and scalability. They provide foundational insights into improving sample quality and reducing computational costs, which are critical for deploying diffusion models in real-world applications.
