## Related Works

Maximum Inner Product Search (MIPS) has been a focal point in machine learning and information retrieval, particularly for high-dimensional data. Existing approaches to MIPS can be broadly categorized into two paradigms: those that rely solely on inner product (IP) similarity and those that reduce the problem to nearest neighbor search under the Euclidean metric via space projection. The former often faces challenges such as local optima and redundant computations \[[13](http://arxiv.org/abs/1610.03317)\]. The latter, while leveraging well-established Euclidean-based techniques, may suffer from topology destruction and information loss during the projection process \[[17](http://arxiv.org/abs/1509.01469)\]. 

Despite these divergent approaches, recent studies argue that there is no inherent binary opposition between IP and Euclidean metrics. Instead, the integration of both can lead to significant performance improvements \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/1405.5869)\]. This perspective aligns with the findings of several works that explore the theoretical and empirical connections between these two metrics from the MIPS perspective \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/1405.5869)\]. 

Graph-based methods have emerged as a promising direction for efficient MIPS. The inner-product navigable small world graph (ip-NSW) represents a state-of-the-art method for approximate MIPS, achieving an order of magnitude speedup over existing baselines \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/1909.13459)\]. However, the exceptional performance of ip-NSW is attributed to its ability to align with the norm bias of the MIPS problem, where large norm items dominate the search process \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/1909.13459)\]. This insight has led to the development of ip-NSW+, which introduces an additional angular proximity graph to improve search efficiency and robustness \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/1909.13459)\].

Efforts to improve the efficiency of MIPS have also included the use of hashing techniques. Asymmetric LSH (ALSH) was introduced as the first provably sublinear time algorithm for approximate MIPS, leveraging asymmetric transformations to convert the problem into approximate near neighbor search \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/1405.5869)\]. Later works, such as NORM-RANGING LSH, further refined these hashing approaches by addressing the normalization issues caused by long-tailed distributions \[[6](http://arxiv.org/abs/1809.08782)\]. 

Another approach to improving MIPS involves the use of quantization techniques. The Quantization based Fast Inner Product Search method proposes a novel quantization approach that minimizes the inner product quantization error, leading to improved performance on various datasets \[[17](http://arxiv.org/abs/1509.01469)\]. This work also highlights the importance of decomposing quantization error into norm and direction components, as norm errors have a more significant impact on inner product search \[[19](http://arxiv.org/abs/1911.04654)\].

The Maximum Inner Product Search (MIPS) problem has been extensively studied due to its critical role in recommendation systems, information retrieval, and high-dimensional similarity search \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/1405.5869)\]. Traditional approaches either rely solely on inner product similarity, which can suffer from local optima and redundant computations \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/1405.5869)\], or transform the problem into Euclidean metric-based nearest neighbor search, often leading to topology distortion and information loss \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/1405.5869)\]. However, recent works have shown that the inner product and Euclidean metrics are not inherently opposed, and integrating both can enhance MIPS performance \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/1405.5869)\]. For instance, graph-based methods have demonstrated strong empirical advantages by constructing proximity graphs that enable efficient traversal for inner product search \[[2](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/25454f456c514219c9d6eeb42200ebbf378db9ca)\]. The norm bias in MIPS, where large norm items are more likely to be top results, has also been identified, and algorithms like ip-NSW exploit this by prioritizing high-norm items during search \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/1909.13459)\]. Additionally, asymmetric hashing schemes and transformations have been proposed to convert MIPS into near neighbor search problems, enabling sublinear time algorithms \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/1405.5869)\]. Several hashing-based methods, such as SIMPLE-LSH and NORM-RANGING LSH, have been developed to address the challenges of normalization and long-tailed distributions in real datasets \[[6](http://arxiv.org/abs/1809.08782)\]. Furthermore, quantization-based approaches, like norm-explicit quantization (NEQ), have been introduced to reduce norm errors, which significantly impact inner product accuracy \[[15](http://arxiv.org/abs/1812.06360)\]. These advancements highlight the diverse strategies for tackling MIPS, ranging from hashing and graph-based indexing to quantization and budgeted search algorithms \[[12](http://arxiv.org/abs/2201.09290)\]. The integration of these techniques, as demonstrated by the Metric-Amphibious Graph (MAG) and Adaptive Navigation with Metric Switch (ANMS), showcases the potential of combining different metrics and strategies to achieve superior performance in MIPS \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/1405.5869)\].

Recent research has focused on developing sub-linear time approximate algorithms for Maximum Inner Product Search (MIPS), with a particular emphasis on reducing query time through preprocessing \[[15](http://arxiv.org/abs/1812.06360)\]. However, this approach can be inefficient when the number of queries is not sufficiently large to justify the preprocessing cost \[[15](http://arxiv.org/abs/1812.06360)\]. Additionally, existing methods lack the ability to directly control the suboptimality of their approximate results, often without theoretical guarantees \[[15](http://arxiv.org/abs/1812.06360)\]. In contrast, a novel approach has been proposed that eliminates the need for preprocessing while allowing users to control and bound the suboptimality of results \[[15](http://arxiv.org/abs/1812.06360)\]. This method frames MIPS as a Best Arm Identification problem, introducing a new bandit setting that leverages the unique structure of MIPS \[[15](http://arxiv.org/abs/1812.06360)\]. This advancement represents a significant improvement over previous techniques, as it achieves better performance on both synthetic and real-world datasets \[[15](http://arxiv.org/abs/1812.06360)\].
