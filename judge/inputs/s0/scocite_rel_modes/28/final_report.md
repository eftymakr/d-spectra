## Related Works

Federated Learning (FL) has emerged as a promising paradigm for training machine learning models on decentralized data while preserving privacy \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/1912.04977)\]. In FL, a central server coordinates with multiple clients to collaboratively train a global model without sharing raw data \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/1912.04977)\]. However, the heterogeneity of client data distributions and the associated fluctuations in model parameters pose significant challenges to model accuracy \[[10](http://arxiv.org/abs/1806.00582)\]. Existing FL approaches, such as Federated Averaging (FedAvg), often struggle with these issues due to their inability to adapt to non-IID data \[[10](http://arxiv.org/abs/1806.00582)\]. 

To address the issue of non-IID data, several works have proposed adaptive optimization methods, including variants of Adagrad, Adam, and Yogi, which have shown improved convergence behavior in heterogeneous settings \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/2003.00295)\]. Additionally, communication efficiency remains a critical concern in FL, particularly in mobile edge computing environments where clients have limited computational resources and unreliable network connections \[[5](http://arxiv.org/abs/1804.08333)\]. Techniques such as structured and sketched updates have been proposed to reduce communication costs by compressing model updates \[[4](http://arxiv.org/abs/1610.05492)\].

Client selection strategies have also been explored to improve the efficiency of FL, especially in scenarios where some clients have limited resources or poor network conditions \[[5](http://arxiv.org/abs/1804.08333)\]. The FedCS protocol, for instance, actively manages clients based on their resource conditions, allowing the server to aggregate as many updates as possible and accelerate performance improvement \[[5](http://arxiv.org/abs/1804.08333)\]. Similarly, the Power-of-Choice strategy has been introduced to bias client selection towards those with higher local loss, achieving faster convergence \[[15](http://arxiv.org/abs/2010.01243)\].

In the context of model personalization, FL has been extended to incorporate meta-learning techniques, such as Model Agnostic Meta Learning (MAML), which enables the global model to be adapted to individual clients' data distributions \[[19](http://arxiv.org/abs/1909.12488)\]. This aligns with the goal of Federated Active Learning (FAL), which aims to reduce annotation costs by strategically selecting the most informative unlabeled data for annotation while preserving data privacy \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/1812.01097)\]. ### <a id='category-federated-learning-research'></a>Federated Learning Research: 6 papers found.
Federated Learning Research explores the challenges and solutions in training machine learning models across distributed networks while preserving data privacy. Key challenges include communication efficiency, data heterogeneity, and fairness in model performance across clients. For instance, communication costs are critical in federated settings, where clients such as mobile phones have unreliable and slow network connections \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/2003.00295)\]. To address this, methods like structured and sketched updates have been proposed to reduce uplink communication costs by two orders of magnitude \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/2003.00295)\]. Additionally, data heterogeneity, where client data distributions are non-IID (independent and identically distributed), poses a significant challenge, as it affects model accuracy and convergence \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/1912.04977)\]. To mitigate this, approaches such as q-Fair Federated Learning (q-FFL) have been introduced to promote fairness by encouraging a more uniform accuracy distribution across devices \[[4](http://arxiv.org/abs/1610.05492)\]. Furthermore, federated learning is increasingly being combined with multi-task learning to handle the statistical challenges of distributed networks, with methods like MOCHA offering robustness to communication costs, stragglers, and fault tolerance \[[5](http://arxiv.org/abs/1804.08333)\]. The field also emphasizes the importance of benchmarking frameworks, such as LEAF, which provide open-source datasets, evaluation protocols, and reference implementations to capture the intricacies of practical federated environments \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/1812.01097)\]. These efforts collectively highlight the growing interest in developing efficient, fair, and scalable federated learning systems that can operate effectively in real-world scenarios.

### <a id='category-federated-learning-research-papers'></a>Federated Learning Research Papers: 7 papers found.
Federated learning (FL) has emerged as a critical paradigm for training machine learning models while preserving data privacy, allowing multiple clients to collaboratively train a model without sharing raw data \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/1812.01097)\]. However, the heterogeneity of client data distributions and system constraints poses significant challenges to convergence and performance \[[4](http://arxiv.org/abs/1610.05492)\]. Existing FL methods, such as FedAvg, often struggle with non-IID data and system heterogeneity, leading to suboptimal results \[[6](http://arxiv.org/abs/1910.01991)\]. To address these issues, researchers have proposed various improvements, including FedProx, which introduces a framework that generalizes FedAvg and provides convergence guarantees under statistical and system heterogeneity \[[9](http://arxiv.org/abs/1812.06127)\]. Additionally, adaptive optimization methods like Adam and Yogi have been adapted for FL to improve performance in non-convex settings \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/1812.01097)\]. Clustered FL (CFL) has also been introduced as a novel approach that groups clients based on data distribution without modifying the communication protocol, offering flexibility and strong mathematical guarantees \[[6](http://arxiv.org/abs/1910.01991)\]. Furthermore, ensemble distillation has been proposed as a method to enable flexible aggregation of heterogeneous client models, improving training efficiency and reducing communication rounds \[[7](http://arxiv.org/abs/2006.07242)\]. These advancements highlight the ongoing efforts to enhance FL's robustness and effectiveness in real-world scenarios with diverse and distributed data \[[5](http://arxiv.org/abs/1804.08333)\].

### <a id='category-others'></a>Others: 4 papers found.
Federated learning (FL) has gained significant attention as a decentralized approach to machine learning, enabling collaborative model training without exposing raw data \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/1912.04977)\]. This paradigm is particularly beneficial for resource-constrained edge devices, such as mobile phones and IoT devices, as it allows these devices to contribute to model training while keeping their data local \[[10](http://arxiv.org/abs/1806.00582)\]. However, FL faces challenges, especially when local data is non-IID (independent and identically distributed), leading to significant accuracy degradation, sometimes up to 55% for neural networks \[[10](http://arxiv.org/abs/1806.00582)\]. Researchers have explored strategies to mitigate these issues, such as creating a small subset of globally shared data to improve accuracy by up to 30% \[[10](http://arxiv.org/abs/1806.00582)\]. Additionally, the convergence of FL algorithms is influenced by client selection strategies, with biased selection towards clients with higher local loss leading to faster error convergence \[[15](http://arxiv.org/abs/2010.01243)\]. To address scalability and heterogeneity, frameworks like Flower have been introduced, enabling large-scale FL experiments on heterogeneous edge devices \[[16](http://arxiv.org/abs/2007.14390)\]. These advancements highlight the ongoing efforts to enhance the practicality and effectiveness of FL in real-world applications.

Toward this future goal, this work aims to extend Federated Learning (FL), a decentralized learning framework that enables privacy-preserving training of models, to work with heterogeneous clients in a practical cellular network. The FL protocol iteratively asks random clients to download a trainable model from a server, update it with own data, and upload the updated model to the server, while asking the server to aggregate multiple client updates to further improve the model. While clients in this protocol are free from disclosing own private data, the overall training process can become inefficient when some clients are with limited computational resources (i.e., requiring longer update time) or under poor wireless channel conditions (longer upload time). Our new FL protocol, which we refer to as FedCS, mitigates this problem and performs FL efficiently while actively managing clients based on their resource conditions. Specifically, FedCS solves a client selection problem with resource constraints, which allows the server to aggregate as many client updates as possible and to accelerate performance improvement in ML models. We conducted an experimental evaluation using publicly-available large-scale image datasets to train deep neural networks on MEC environment simulations. The experimental results show that FedCS is able to complete its training process in a significantly shorter time compared to the original FL protocol.»
