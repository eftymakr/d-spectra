## Related Works

Federated learning (FL) has emerged as a promising paradigm for collaborative machine learning while preserving data privacy by keeping training data decentralized \[[23](http://arxiv.org/abs/1912.04977)\]. This has led to increased interest in the "right to be forgotten," which requires users to have the ability to remove their data from trained models \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2203.07320)\]. This has spurred the development of federated unlearning (FU), a process that aims to eliminate the influence of specific data points or clients on the global model \[[2](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/39da2d48f2132b919405e3bb6df6ece44ac6e15a)\]. 

Several approaches have been proposed to achieve federated unlearning. One common method involves retraining the model from scratch after removing the data \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2203.07320)\]. However, this approach is computationally expensive and impractical for large-scale FL systems \[[3](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/eadeffdec9fac8fd7f9aea732ca410eb082b7dcf)\]. To address this, researchers have explored alternative methods such as local unlearning followed by a few rounds of federated learning \[[4](http://arxiv.org/abs/2207.05521)\]. This approach reduces the computational overhead by leveraging the locally unlearned model as an initialization for the global unlearning process \[[4](http://arxiv.org/abs/2207.05521)\]. 

Another line of research focuses on using historical parameter updates to reconstruct the unlearned model without requiring full retraining \[[3](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/eadeffdec9fac8fd7f9aea732ca410eb082b7dcf)\]. For example, FedEraser reconstructs the unlearned model by leveraging the historical parameter updates of federated clients, which are retained at the central server during the training process \[[3](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/eadeffdec9fac8fd7f9aea732ca410eb082b7dcf)\]. This method significantly reduces the time required to construct the unlearned model while maintaining its performance \[[3](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/eadeffdec9fac8fd7f9aea732ca410eb082b7dcf)\]. 

In addition to retraining-based methods, some studies have proposed using knowledge distillation to restore model performance without accessing the clients' data \[[5](http://arxiv.org/abs/2201.09441)\]. This method subtracts the accumulated historical updates from the model and leverages knowledge distillation to restore the model's performance \[[5](http://arxiv.org/abs/2201.09441)\]. This approach is particularly useful in scenarios where clients are not willing to share their data \[[5](http://arxiv.org/abs/2201.09441)\]. 

The challenge of verifying the effectiveness of federated unlearning has also been addressed in recent studies. For example, VeriFi introduces a framework that allows for systematic analysis of federated unlearning and quantification of its effect \[[8](http://arxiv.org/abs/2205.12709)\]. This framework enables the verification of the unlearning effect by marking and checking the global model's performance change on the markers \[[8](http://arxiv.org/abs/2205.12709)\]. 

The evaluation of unlearning effectiveness has also been a focus of recent research. ### <a id='category-federated-learning-unlearning-research'></a>Federated Learning Unlearning Research: 24 papers found.
The related works in federated unlearning focus on addressing the challenge of efficiently removing specific data contributions from a global model in federated learning (FL) while maintaining model performance and privacy. Early works, such as \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2203.07320)\], highlight the limitations of centralized unlearning techniques in FL, where full access to training data is not available, and propose a rapid retraining approach for federated unlearning. Similarly, \[[2](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/39da2d48f2132b919405e3bb6df6ece44ac6e15a)\] introduces the concept of federated unlearning, emphasizing the need for efficient methods to remove data from FL models without retraining from scratch. The work in \[[3](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/eadeffdec9fac8fd7f9aea732ca410eb082b7dcf)\] further explores the problem by proposing FedEraser, which leverages historical parameter updates to reconstruct unlearned models efficiently. \[[4](http://arxiv.org/abs/2207.05521)\] presents a method that removes a client's influence by performing local unlearning and retraining with remaining clients, avoiding the need for global data access. \[[5](http://arxiv.org/abs/2201.09441)\] introduces a novel method that eliminates a client's contribution by subtracting historical updates and using knowledge distillation, without requiring client participation. \[[6](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/06edb1f6eadb6c141d720fcca4c854578dc5d670)\] develops FedRecovery, which uses differential privacy to erase a client’s impact by modifying gradient residuals and ensuring statistical indistinguishability between unlearned and retrained models. \[[7](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/a52c3ec7325e81bd87fdf8d74bf9acfd1e1ffe5f)\] reviews the privacy leakage risks in federated unlearning and proposes a framework for verifiable unlearning, introducing methods like VeriFi to quantify unlearning effects. \[[8](http://arxiv.org/abs/2205.12709)\] introduces a method that decouples unlearning from training, enabling efficient client revocation and category removal without retraining. \[[9](http://arxiv.org/abs/2210.10958)\] proposes a Bayesian federated unlearning (BFU) approach that balances forgetting and retaining model knowledge, while \[[10](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/eff2be3b3ccfb1fc0650432b9b611ebd11ba0feb)\] introduces a quantized federated learning (Q-FL) algorithm for exact unlearning. These studies collectively highlight the growing interest in developing efficient, privacy-preserving, and verifiable federated unlearning methods that align with the "right to be forgotten" and address the unique challenges of FL.

This is particularly relevant in scenarios like Federated Online Learning to Rank (FOLTR), where user privacy is paramount, and models are trained across decentralized data sources \[[27](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/a08f1c5c9f0bdb24f0dc75c0d969eb353899156b)\]. Additionally, there is growing interest in revocable federated learning frameworks, such as RevFRF, which incorporate secure protocols for participant revocation and ensure that revoked users' data is effectively and securely removed from trained models \[[30](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/f07b5043bd6fbb41f85ed66cd73b8a7a1ba9a4f5)\]. These mechanisms are essential for maintaining data privacy and preventing conflicts of interest in collaborative learning environments. Moreover, incentive mechanisms have been proposed to retain valuable users by balancing the trade-offs between training losses and privacy costs, ensuring continued engagement in federated learning systems \[[27](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/a08f1c5c9f0bdb24f0dc75c0d969eb353899156b)\]. The integration of homomorphic encryption and multi-dimensional metrics for user contribution assessment further enhances the robustness and fairness of these mechanisms \[[27](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/a08f1c5c9f0bdb24f0dc75c0d969eb353899156b)\]\[[30](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/f07b5043bd6fbb41f85ed66cd73b8a7a1ba9a4f5)\]. Overall, these advancements highlight the evolving landscape of federated learning mechanisms, emphasizing the need for secure, efficient, and incentive-aligned approaches to address both technical and ethical challenges.
