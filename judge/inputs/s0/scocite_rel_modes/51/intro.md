## Related Works

Federated learning (FL) has emerged as a promising paradigm for collaborative machine learning while preserving data privacy by keeping training data decentralized \[[23](http://arxiv.org/abs/1912.04977)\]. This has led to increased interest in the "right to be forgotten," which requires users to have the ability to remove their data from trained models \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2203.07320)\]. This has spurred the development of federated unlearning (FU), a process that aims to eliminate the influence of specific data points or clients on the global model \[[2](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/39da2d48f2132b919405e3bb6df6ece44ac6e15a)\]. 

Several approaches have been proposed to achieve federated unlearning. One common method involves retraining the model from scratch after removing the data \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2203.07320)\]. However, this approach is computationally expensive and impractical for large-scale FL systems \[[3](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/eadeffdec9fac8fd7f9aea732ca410eb082b7dcf)\]. To address this, researchers have explored alternative methods such as local unlearning followed by a few rounds of federated learning \[[4](http://arxiv.org/abs/2207.05521)\]. This approach reduces the computational overhead by leveraging the locally unlearned model as an initialization for the global unlearning process \[[4](http://arxiv.org/abs/2207.05521)\]. 

Another line of research focuses on using historical parameter updates to reconstruct the unlearned model without requiring full retraining \[[3](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/eadeffdec9fac8fd7f9aea732ca410eb082b7dcf)\]. For example, FedEraser reconstructs the unlearned model by leveraging the historical parameter updates of federated clients, which are retained at the central server during the training process \[[3](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/eadeffdec9fac8fd7f9aea732ca410eb082b7dcf)\]. This method significantly reduces the time required to construct the unlearned model while maintaining its performance \[[3](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/eadeffdec9fac8fd7f9aea732ca410eb082b7dcf)\]. 

In addition to retraining-based methods, some studies have proposed using knowledge distillation to restore model performance without accessing the clients' data \[[5](http://arxiv.org/abs/2201.09441)\]. This method subtracts the accumulated historical updates from the model and leverages knowledge distillation to restore the model's performance \[[5](http://arxiv.org/abs/2201.09441)\]. This approach is particularly useful in scenarios where clients are not willing to share their data \[[5](http://arxiv.org/abs/2201.09441)\]. 

The challenge of verifying the effectiveness of federated unlearning has also been addressed in recent studies. For example, VeriFi introduces a framework that allows for systematic analysis of federated unlearning and quantification of its effect \[[8](http://arxiv.org/abs/2205.12709)\]. This framework enables the verification of the unlearning effect by marking and checking the global model's performance change on the markers \[[8](http://arxiv.org/abs/2205.12709)\]. 

The evaluation of unlearning effectiveness has also been a focus of recent research. For instance, the study by \[[18](http://arxiv.org/abs/2209.02299)\] highlights the importance of using multiple metrics to assess unlearning effectiveness, as previous proposals often relied on single evaluation metrics \[[18](http://arxiv.org/abs/2209.02299)\].
