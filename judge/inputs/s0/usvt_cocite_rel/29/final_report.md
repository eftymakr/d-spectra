## Related Works

Maximum Inner Product Search (MIPS) has been a focal point in machine learning and information retrieval, particularly for high-dimensional data. Existing approaches to MIPS can be broadly categorized into two paradigms: those that rely solely on inner product (IP) similarity and those that reduce the problem to nearest neighbor search under the Euclidean metric via space projection. The former often faces challenges such as local optima and redundant computations \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/1405.5869)\]. The latter, while effective, can lead to topology destruction and information loss due to the inherent differences between IP and Euclidean metrics \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/1909.13459)\]. However, recent studies argue that there is no inherent binary opposition between these two metrics \[[3](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/25454f456c514219c9d6eeb42200ebbf378db9ca)\]. Instead, integrating both in the design of indexing and search algorithms can significantly enhance MIPS performance \[[4](http://arxiv.org/abs/1610.03317)\].

Several works have explored hashing-based methods for approximate MIPS. Asymmetric LSH (ALSH) was introduced as the first provably sublinear time algorithm for approximate MIPS, leveraging asymmetric transformations to convert the problem into approximate near neighbor search \[[5](http://arxiv.org/abs/1202.6101)\]. This approach was later improved with the introduction of Norm-Ranging LSH, which addresses the issue of long-tailed 2-norm distributions by partitioning the dataset into sub-datasets and building hash indexes independently \[[9](http://arxiv.org/abs/1809.08782)\]. Similarly, the SIMPLE-LSH method was proposed, but it was found to suffer from performance degradation in the presence of long-tailed data distributions \[[9](http://arxiv.org/abs/1809.08782)\]. These findings highlight the importance of addressing data distribution characteristics in hashing-based approaches \[[9](http://arxiv.org/abs/1809.08782)\].

Graph-based methods have also gained traction for efficient MIPS. The ip-NSW graph, an inner-product navigable small world graph, has achieved an order of magnitude speedup over existing baselines by leveraging the norm bias of the MIPS problem \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/1909.13459)\]. This bias implies that large norm items are more likely to be the result of MIPS, and ip-NSW exploits this by assigning higher in-degrees to such items \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/1909.13459)\]. The ip-NSW+ algorithm further improves performance by introducing an angular proximity graph to refine the search process \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/1909.13459)\]. Another graph-based method, the norm adjusted proximity graph (NAPG), was proposed to improve the selection of meaningful data points during graph construction \[[3](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/25454f456c514219c9d6eeb42200ebbf378db9ca)\]. These methods demonstrate the potential of graph-based indexing for MIPS, especially when tailored to data-specific characteristics \[[3](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/25454f456c514219c9d6eeb42200ebbf378db9ca)\].

Efforts have also been made to develop lightweight and efficient indexing strategies for large-scale MIPS. The ProMIPS method, for instance, uses a lightweight iDistance index and 2-stable random projections to achieve c-approximate MIPS

### <a id='category-graph-based-indexing-for-mips'></a>Graph-Based Indexing for MIPS: 5 papers found.
The category "Graph-Based Indexing for MIPS" focuses on the development of graph-based indexing methods to efficiently perform Maximum Inner Product Search (MIPS), a critical task in machine learning and information retrieval. These methods aim to address the limitations of traditional approaches that either rely solely on inner product similarity, which can lead to local optima and redundant computations, or transform MIPS into Euclidean nearest neighbor search, which may distort data topology and lose information \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/1909.13459)\]. Graph-based indexing, such as the inner-product navigable small world graph (ip-NSW), has shown strong empirical advantages by leveraging the norm bias in MIPS, where large norm items are more likely to be relevant, thereby reducing unnecessary computations on smaller norm items \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/1405.5869)\]. Additionally, the norm adjusted proximity graph (NAPG) improves graph construction by incorporating adjusting factors estimated from sampled data, leading to more meaningful connections in the graph \[[3](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/25454f456c514219c9d6eeb42200ebbf378db9ca)\]. Another approach, the Inner Product Delaunay Graph (IPDG), transforms MIPS into a graph search problem, enhancing efficiency for retrieving relevant vectors in NLP tasks \[[14](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/4d0dce74babcf393b5a4cbcb11c89ccca6a3b92b)\]. The Hierarchical NSW (HNSW) further advances graph-based indexing by introducing a multi-layer structure with controllable hierarchy, enabling logarithmic complexity scaling and improved performance in high recall scenarios \[[19](http://arxiv.org/abs/1603.09320)\]. These methods collectively highlight the potential of graph-based indexing to adapt to different data topologies and improve MIPS performance through innovative graph construction and search strategies.

### <a id='category-performance-evaluation-and-benchmarks'></a>Performance Evaluation and Benchmarks: 4 papers found.
Performance Evaluation and Benchmarks: The performance of Maximum Inner Product Search (MIPS) has been extensively evaluated across various datasets and algorithms. Early approaches often relied on brute-force methods, which are computationally expensive, prompting the development of more efficient techniques \[[3](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/25454f456c514219c9d6eeb42200ebbf378db9ca)\]. For instance, the Greedy-MIPS algorithm was introduced to address budgeted MIPS, offering significant speed improvements while maintaining high search quality, achieving 200x faster performance on large-scale datasets \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/1405.5869)\]. Similarly, the branch-and-bound approach with tree-based data structures has been proposed to improve efficiency, achieving up to five orders of magnitude improvement in query time over naive search techniques \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/1909.13459)\]. More recently, hardware-efficient methods like Blocked Matrix Multiply (BMM) and novel algorithms such as MAX-IMUS have been developed, demonstrating up to an order of magnitude speedup over existing solvers \[[3](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/25454f456c514219c9d6eeb42200ebbf378db9ca)\]. Additionally, the reverse MIPS problem has been tackled with the Simpfer algorithm, which introduces an index-based approach to efficiently filter candidate vectors, achieving speedups of 500–8000 times compared to baselines \[[4](http://arxiv.org/abs/1610.03317)\]. These studies collectively highlight the ongoing efforts to enhance MIPS performance through algorithmic innovations and hardware optimization.
