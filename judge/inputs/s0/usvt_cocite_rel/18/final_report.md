## Related Works

Anomaly detection in industrial settings has garnered significant attention due to its critical role in quality control and system reliability. Early approaches relied on rule-based methods and statistical models, which were limited in their ability to generalize across complex, dynamic environments \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2012.03586)\]. With the advent of deep learning, numerous methods have been proposed to improve detection accuracy and adaptability. 

One line of research focuses on process-based anomaly detection for Industrial Control Systems (ICSs). A systematic analysis of such schemes revealed that many are vulnerable to adversarial spoofing attacks, which can reduce recall and compromise detection reliability \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2012.03586)\]. This highlights the need for more robust models that can learn and enforce physical properties of the system \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2012.03586)\]. 

Unsupervised anomaly detection has also been extensively explored, particularly for 3D data. The MVTec 3D-AD dataset was introduced to evaluate models for detecting geometric anomalies in manufactured products \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/2112.09045)\]. This dataset includes precise ground-truth annotations, enabling rigorous evaluation of detection and localization performance \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/2112.09045)\]. Similarly, the MVTec AD dataset has been widely used to benchmark methods for image-based anomaly detection \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/2106.08265)\]. 

To address the cold-start problem in industrial anomaly detection, where models are trained only on normal data, PatchCore was proposed. This method uses a memory bank of representative patch features to achieve state-of-the-art performance on the MVTec AD benchmark, with an AUROC score of 99.6% \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/2106.08265)\]. Another approach, CFLOW-AD, introduces a real-time model based on conditional normalizing flows, achieving faster inference times and better localization performance than prior methods \[[4](http://arxiv.org/abs/2107.12571)\]. 

For time-series data, LSTM-based encoder-decoder models have shown robustness in detecting anomalies from both predictable and unpredictable time-series \[[7](http://arxiv.org/abs/1607.00148)\]. Similarly, MAD-GAN leverages Generative Adversarial Networks (GANs) to capture spatial-temporal correlations in multivariate time series, improving detection accuracy on real-world datasets like SWaT and WADI \[[13](http://arxiv.org/abs/1901.04997)\]. 

Recent advances in anomaly detection have also focused on localization. PaDiM introduces a patch distribution modeling framework that outperforms existing methods on both detection and localization tasks \[[6](http://arxiv.org/abs/2011.08785)\]. DRÆM, another recent approach, treats anomaly detection as a discriminative problem, achieving superior performance on the MVTec dataset \[[8](http://arxiv.org/abs/2108.07610)\]. 

Additionally, methods such as FastFlow and RIAD have been proposed to improve the efficiency and accuracy of anomaly detection by leveraging normalizing flows and inpainting techniques \[[10](http://arxiv.org/abs/2111.07677)\]\[[12](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/2c89b183df320c3ef698989bdc5d1d4731c4d65d)\]. Context: 
	Document 1: [Snippet]: «In recent years, a number of process-based anomaly detection schemes for Industrial Control Systems were proposed. In this work, we provide the first systematic analysis of such schemes, and introduce a taxonomy of properties that are verified by those detection systems. We then present a novel general framework to generate adversarial spoofing signals that violate physical properties of the system, and use the framework to analyze four anomaly detectors published at top security conferences. We find that three of those detectors are susceptible to a number of adversarial manipulations (e.g., spoofing with precomputed patterns), which we call Synthetic Sensor Spoofing and one is resilient against our attacks. We investigate the root of its resilience and demonstrate that it comes from the properties that we introduced. Our attacks reduce the Recall (True Positive Rate) of the attacked schemes making them not able to correctly detect anomalies. Thus, the vulnerabilities we discovered in the anomaly detectors show that (despite an original good detection performance), those detectors are not able to reliably learn physical properties of the system. Even attacks that prior work was expected to be resilient against (based on verified properties) were found to be successful. We argue that our findings demonstrate the need for both more complete attacks in datasets, and more critical analysis of process-based anomaly detectors. We plan to release our implementation as open-source, together with an extension of two public datasets with a set of Synthetic Sensor Spoofing attacks as generated by our framework.»

### <a id='category-cold-start-problem-solutions-in-industrial-anomaly-detection'></a>Cold-Start Problem Solutions in Industrial Anomaly Detection: 1 papers found.
Context: 
	Document 1: [Snippet]: «Being able to spot defective parts is a critical component in large-scale industrial manufacturing. A particular challenge that we address in this work is the cold-start problem: fit a model using nominal (non-defective) example images only. While handcrafted solutions per class are possible, the goal is to build systems that work well simultaneously on many different tasks automatically. The best peforming approaches combine embeddings from ImageNet models with an outlier detection model. In this paper, we extend on this line of work and propose PatchCore, which uses a maximally representative memory bank of nominal patch-features. PatchCore offers competitive inference times while achieving state-of-the-art performance for both detection and localization. On the challenging, widely used MVTec AD benchmark PatchCore achieves an image-level anomaly detection AUROC score of up to 99.6%, more than halving the error compared to the next best competitor. We further report competitive results on two additional datasets and also find competitive results in the few samples regime. Code: github.com/amazon-research/patchcore-inspection.»

### <a id='category-real-time-anomaly-detection-models'></a>Real-Time Anomaly Detection Models: 1 papers found.
Context: 
	Document 1: [Snippet]: «Unsupervised anomaly detection with localization has many practical applications when labeling is infeasible and, moreover, when anomaly examples are completely missing in the train data. While recently proposed models for such data setup achieve high accuracy metrics, their complexity is a limiting factor for real-time processing. In this paper, we propose a real-time model and analytically derive its relationship to prior methods. Our CFLOW-AD model is based on a conditional normalizing flow framework adopted for anomaly detection with localization. In particular, CFLOW-AD consists of a discriminatively pretrained encoder followed by a multi-scale generative decoder where the latter explicitly estimate likelihood of the encoded features. Our experiments on the MVTec dataset show that CFLOW-AD outperforms previous methods by 0.36% AUROC in detection task, by 1.12% AUROC and 2.5% AUPRO in localization task, respectively. ### <a id='category-time-series-anomaly-detection-with-lstm-and-gans'></a>Time-Series Anomaly Detection with LSTM and GANs: 3 papers found.
In the realm of time-series anomaly detection, recent approaches have increasingly leveraged deep learning models to address the challenges posed by complex and dynamic industrial environments \[[5](http://arxiv.org/abs/1911.04831)\]. For instance, sequence-to-sequence neural networks have been applied to predict and interpret time-series characteristics of industrial control systems (ICSs), enabling the detection of anomalies using only a normal dataset \[[5](http://arxiv.org/abs/1911.04831)\]. Similarly, Long Short Term Memory (LSTM) networks have been employed in an Encoder-Decoder framework (EncDec-AD) to reconstruct normal time-series behavior, using reconstruction error as a metric for anomaly detection, which has shown robustness across various types of time-series data, including those with unpredictable components \[[7](http://arxiv.org/abs/1607.00148)\]. Furthermore, Generative Adversarial Networks (GANs) have been utilized in unsupervised multivariate anomaly detection methods, such as MAD-GAN, which exploit spatial-temporal correlations among multiple variables to detect anomalies in real-world cyber-physical systems \[[13](http://arxiv.org/abs/1901.04997)\]. These methods highlight a growing trend toward data-driven, model-based approaches that can adapt to the inherent complexities of industrial time-series data, offering improved performance over traditional threshold-based or supervised learning methods that struggle with unlabeled data and dynamic system behavior \[[5](http://arxiv.org/abs/1911.04831)\]\[[7](http://arxiv.org/abs/1607.00148)\]\[[13](http://arxiv.org/abs/1901.04997)\].

### <a id='category-recent-advances-in-anomaly-detection-frameworks'></a>Recent Advances in Anomaly Detection Frameworks: 6 papers found.
Recent advances in anomaly detection frameworks have focused on improving both detection accuracy and computational efficiency, particularly for industrial applications. Methods such as PaDiM leverage pretrained CNNs and multivariate Gaussian distributions to model normal patterns and localize anomalies effectively \[[6](http://arxiv.org/abs/2011.08785)\]. Similarly, DRÆM introduces a discriminatively trained reconstruction model that enables direct anomaly localization without complex post-processing, achieving competitive performance on challenging datasets like MVTec \[[8](http://arxiv.org/abs/2108.07610)\]. FastFlow, based on 2D normalizing flows, offers a flexible plug-in module for anomaly detection and localization, demonstrating high accuracy and inference efficiency with various backbones \[[10](http://arxiv.org/abs/2111.07677)\]. UniAD further advances the field by proposing a unified framework for multi-class anomaly detection, overcoming limitations of separate models through innovations like layer-wise query decoding and neighbor masked attention \[[11](http://arxiv.org/abs/2206.03687)\]. Additionally, RIAD addresses the limitations of auto-encoder-based methods by introducing a self-supervised reconstruction-by-inpainting approach, which improves detection by focusing on partial image reconstruction \[[12](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/2c89b183df320c3ef698989bdc5d1d4731c4d65d)\]. These approaches collectively highlight the trend toward more efficient, accurate, and adaptable anomaly detection systems suitable for real-world industrial settings.

The study was performed on a Secure Water Treatment testbed (SWaT) dataset, which represents a scaled-down version of a real-world industrial water treatment plant. We suggest a method for anomaly detection based on measuring the statistical deviation of the predicted value from the observed value. The findings also suggest that 1D convolutional neural networks are effective at time series prediction tasks which are traditionally considered to be best solved using recurrent neural networks. This observation is a promising one, as 1D convolutional neural networks are simpler, smaller, and faster than the recurrent neural networks.»
