## Related Works

Multimodal large language models (MLLMs) have emerged as a promising direction in artificial intelligence, enabling models to understand and generate content across multiple modalities, including text, images, and video. Early efforts in this field focused on integrating vision and language, often using pre-trained models for either vision or language and then combining them in a modular fashion \[[12](http://arxiv.org/abs/2204.14198)\]. However, these approaches typically lacked the ability to perform in-context learning and lacked robustness in handling complex, real-world tasks \[[12](http://arxiv.org/abs/2204.14198)\].

Recent advances have introduced models that can perform multimodal reasoning and generate responses grounded in both visual and textual information. For instance, Video-ChatGPT combines a video-adapted visual encoder with a large language model (LLM) to enable detailed conversations about videos, using a large-scale dataset of 100,000 video-instruction pairs \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/2306.05424)\]. Similarly, Video-LLaMA extends this idea by integrating both visual and auditory signals, addressing challenges such as temporal coherence and cross-modal alignment through specialized components like the Video Q-former and Audio Q-former \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/2306.02858)\]. These models demonstrate the potential of MLLMs to understand and generate content that is both visually and audibly grounded \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/2306.02858)\].

Another important development is the introduction of models that can perform grounding, i.e., aligning text with specific regions in images or videos. Kosmos-2, for example, introduces a novel way to represent referential expressions using Markdown links, enabling models to ground text to visual content through a large-scale dataset called GrIT \[[5](http://arxiv.org/abs/2306.14824)\]. This capability is crucial for tasks such as referring expression comprehension and phrase grounding, which require precise alignment between textual descriptions and visual elements \[[5](http://arxiv.org/abs/2306.14824)\].

The ability to perform instruction-following and in-context learning has also been a focus of recent research. Flamingo, for example, is a visual language model that enables few-shot learning by training on large-scale multimodal web corpora containing interleaved text and images \[[12](http://arxiv.org/abs/2204.14198)\]. This approach allows models to adapt to new tasks with minimal supervision, which is a significant advantage in real-world applications \[[12](http://arxiv.org/abs/2204.14198)\]. Similarly, LLaVA leverages instruction tuning using machine-generated instruction-following data to improve zero-shot performance on new tasks \[[9](http://arxiv.org/abs/2304.08485)\].

To evaluate the performance of MLLMs, researchers have introduced comprehensive benchmarks such as MME, which measures both perception and cognition abilities across 14 subtasks \[[10](http://arxiv.org/abs/2306.13394)\]. ### <a id='category-multimodal-integration-approaches'></a>Multimodal Integration Approaches: 5 papers found.
Recent advancements in multimodal large language models (MLLMs) have focused on integrating visual, auditory, and textual modalities to enhance understanding and generation capabilities in complex tasks. Researchers have developed frameworks such as Video-LLaMA, which enables video comprehension by addressing challenges in temporal visual changes and audio-visual integration through specialized modules like Video Q-former and Audio Q-former \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/2306.02858)\]. Similarly, the Qwen-VL series introduces large-scale vision-language models (LVLMs) with enhanced visual perception, grounding, and text-reading abilities, achieving state-of-the-art performance on visual-centric benchmarks \[[4](http://arxiv.org/abs/2308.12966)\]. Kosmos-1 represents another significant contribution, as it is trained from scratch on web-scale multimodal corpora and supports zero-shot and few-shot learning, demonstrating strong performance in language understanding, OCR-free NLP, and vision tasks \[[7](http://arxiv.org/abs/2302.14045)\]. Additionally, PaLM-E extends the capabilities of embodied language models by incorporating real-world sensor modalities, enabling sequential robotic manipulation planning and visual question answering while retaining generalist language capabilities \[[8](http://arxiv.org/abs/2303.03378)\]. These models collectively highlight the growing trend of developing MLLMs that can effectively handle nonverbal cues and multimodal interactions, aligning with the goals of MARS, which aims to bridge the gap in conversational AI by incorporating annotated videos with time-aligned nonverbal data \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2306.13549)\].

### <a id='category-model-robustness-and-generalization'></a>Model Robustness and Generalization: 2 papers found.
The category "Model Robustness and Generalization" encompasses research focused on enhancing the adaptability and performance of large language models (LLMs) across diverse tasks and environments. This includes advancements in pre-training methodologies that enable models to acquire robust foundational capabilities through extensive self-supervised learning and architectural innovations \[[11](http://arxiv.org/abs/2303.18223)\]. Additionally, post-training techniques such as supervised fine-tuning and reinforcement learning are explored to improve task-specific performance and model alignment \[[11](http://arxiv.org/abs/2303.18223)\]. The development of models like Flamingo highlights the importance of multimodal capabilities, where architectural innovations allow for seamless integration of visual and textual data, enabling effective few-shot learning from minimal annotated examples \[[12](http://arxiv.org/abs/2204.14198)\]. These models are trained on large-scale multimodal corpora, which significantly enhance their generalization abilities across a range of tasks, from open-ended visual question-answering to closed-ended multiple-choice tasks \[[12](http://arxiv.org/abs/2204.14198)\]. Such approaches are critical for achieving robustness in real-world applications, where models must interact with complex and varied input formats. The emphasis on evaluation methods that measure core language capabilities, reasoning, and safety further underscores the need for comprehensive frameworks to assess model performance \[[11](http://arxiv.org/abs/2303.18223)\]. These efforts collectively contribute to the broader goal of creating more adaptable and effective LLMs capable of handling the complexities of real-world interactions.
