## Related Works

Zero-Knowledge Proofs (ZKPs) have emerged as a critical cryptographic primitive for privacy-preserving computation, enabling a prover to demonstrate the validity of a statement without revealing additional information \[[5](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/9ccf7b6cb32cf89752a35bd910555adac54773e0)\]. Recent advancements have focused on accelerating the computational bottlenecks in ZKP generation, particularly for large-scale applications such as blockchain and machine learning \[[1](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/e149d93531d8c07cf43f6cae327a4795144d1304)\]. However, existing solutions face significant limitations, either requiring a trusted setup for each application \[[1](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/e149d93531d8c07cf43f6cae327a4795144d1304)\] or generating proofs with higher verification costs \[[1](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/e149d93531d8c07cf43f6cae327a4795144d1304)\]. This section reviews prior work on ZKP acceleration and related cryptographic protocols.

Several studies have explored hardware and software accelerations for ZKP protocols. GZKP leverages GPU acceleration to improve the performance of polynomial computations and multi-scalar multiplications (MSMs), achieving up to 48.1× speedup over state-of-the-art systems \[[1](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/e149d93531d8c07cf43f6cae327a4795144d1304)\]. Similarly, PipeZK introduces a pipelined architecture with two subsystems to handle large polynomial computations and elliptic curve operations, achieving 10× speedup on cryptographic benchmarks \[[2](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/6ee84674b7c426849c3da56cb278c08eb7a903cf)\]. cuZK further improves performance by optimizing the parallelization of MSM and matrix-vector multiplication (MUL) operations, achieving up to 4.86× speedup on Filecoin \[[3](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/e4a4608e8e35379e63b9df5bda1b7599ee8ab9ac)\]. These works highlight the importance of optimizing the key primitives in ZKP generation, particularly MSM and NTT, which are computationally intensive.

In the context of machine learning, zero-knowledge proofs have been applied to verify the correctness of decision tree and convolutional neural network (CNN) predictions \[[4](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/3becc2935d1f52c82ffd7216c28bc86f5e393208)\]\[[6](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/6336149196f02d0eb6c1a89bc6b662e4422bd794)\]. For example, zkCNN introduces a novel sumcheck protocol to efficiently prove CNN predictions, achieving 1264× speedup over existing schemes \[[6](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/6336149196f02d0eb6c1a89bc6b662e4422bd794)\]. These approaches demonstrate the potential of ZKPs in ensuring the integrity of machine learning models without exposing sensitive information about the model itself.

Recent hardware accelerators have also been proposed to address the computational overhead of ZKP protocols. CraterLake is the first FHE accelerator to support unbounded computation on encrypted data, achieving gmean 4,600× speedup over CPU implementations \[[8](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/b718a4b59ee6ee21c7c2ee1aa64085231853535e)\]. Similarly, F1 introduces a programmable accelerator for FHE, achieving up to 17,000× speedup over software implementations \[[10](http://arxiv.org/abs/2109.05371)\]. These works emphasize the need for specialized hardware to reduce the overhead of cryptographic computations, particularly for complex applications like deep learning.

### <a id='category-hardware-acceleration-techniques-for-zkp'></a>Hardware Acceleration Techniques for ZKP: 2 papers found.
Hardware acceleration techniques for zero-knowledge proofs (ZKP) have emerged as a critical area of research to address the performance bottlenecks in proof generation, which is a major obstacle in deploying ZKP in real-world applications \[[1](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/e149d93531d8c07cf43f6cae327a4795144d1304)\]. Existing ZKP systems often suffer from high computational overhead due to intensive polynomial computations and multi-scalar multiplications on elliptic curves \[[2](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/6ee84674b7c426849c3da56cb278c08eb7a903cf)\]. To overcome these challenges, several works have proposed hardware accelerators tailored for ZKP primitives. For instance, GZKP leverages GPU acceleration to achieve significant speedups by optimizing memory access patterns and eliminating costly external shuffling \[[1](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/e149d93531d8c07cf43f6cae327a4795144d1304)\]. Similarly, PipeZK introduces a pipelined architecture with two specialized subsystems to handle large polynomial computations and multi-scalar multiplications efficiently, achieving up to 10x speedup on cryptographic benchmarks \[[2](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/6ee84674b7c426849c3da56cb278c08eb7a903cf)\]. These accelerators aim to reduce the time and resources required for proof generation, making ZKP more practical for applications such as blockchain and verifiable computing. By focusing on hardware-level optimizations, these approaches demonstrate the potential to enhance the scalability and usability of ZKP systems \[[1](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/e149d93531d8c07cf43f6cae327a4795144d1304)\]\[[2](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/6ee84674b7c426849c3da56cb278c08eb7a903cf)\].

Optimization of key primitives such as Multi-scalar Multiplication (MSM) and Number Theoretic Transform (NTT) has been a focal point in improving the performance of Zero-Knowledge Proof (ZKP) systems, particularly for zkSNARKs \[[3](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/e4a4608e8e35379e63b9df5bda1b7599ee8ab9ac)\]. These primitives are critical in the proof generation step, which is often a bottleneck due to its computational intensity \[[3](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/e4a4608e8e35379e63b9df5bda1b7599ee8ab9ac)\]. Prior work has explored GPU and ASIC implementations to accelerate these operations, with notable efforts like cuZK and SZKP \[[3](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/e4a4608e8e35379e63b9df5bda1b7599ee8ab9ac)\]\[[9](http://arxiv.org/abs/2408.05890)\]. cuZK introduces a parallel MSM algorithm that achieves nearly linear speedup over the Pippenger algorithm, along with parallelized MUL and NTT operations, reducing CPU-GPU data transfer latency \[[3](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/e4a4608e8e35379e63b9df5bda1b7599ee8ab9ac)\]. Similarly, SZKP presents an ASIC-based accelerator that leverages structured dataflows for both NTT and MSM, achieving significant speedups over CPU, ASIC, and GPU implementations \[[9](http://arxiv.org/abs/2408.05890)\]. These approaches highlight the importance of optimizing these primitives to enhance the efficiency of ZKP protocols, enabling broader adoption in privacy-preserving applications \[[3](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/e4a4608e8e35379e63b9df5bda1b7599ee8ab9ac)\]\[[9](http://arxiv.org/abs/2408.05890)\].

### <a id='category-recent-advances-in-fhe-and-zkp-hardware'></a>Recent Advances in FHE and ZKP Hardware: 2 papers found.
Recent advances in FHE and ZKP hardware have focused on overcoming the computational overheads that hinder widespread adoption. Fully Homomorphic Encryption (FHE) enables secure computation on encrypted data, but its performance is severely limited by software execution, which is 10,000X slower than unencrypted computation \[[8](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/b718a4b59ee6ee21c7c2ee1aa64085231853535e)\]. To address this, accelerators like F1 and CraterLake have been developed, with F1 being the first programmable FHE accelerator that supports full FHE programs \[[10](http://arxiv.org/abs/2109.05371)\]. F1 leverages a wide-vector processor with specialized functional units for FHE primitives such as modular arithmetic and number-theoretic transforms, significantly reducing data movement bottlenecks \[[10](http://arxiv.org/abs/2109.05371)\]. CraterLake further advances this by introducing a hardware architecture that scales efficiently to large ciphertexts and supports unbounded multiplicative depth, essential for complex computations like deep neural networks \[[8](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/b718a4b59ee6ee21c7c2ee1aa64085231853535e)\]. These accelerators demonstrate substantial speedups, with CraterLake achieving a gmean of 4,600X over CPU baselines and outperforming prior accelerators by 11.2X under similar constraints \[[8](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/b718a4b59ee6ee21c7c2ee1aa64085231853535e)\]. Similarly, zkSpeed, an accelerator for the HyperPlonk ZKP protocol, addresses the computational complexity of ZKP proving by accelerating key primitives such as SumCheck and Multi-scalar Multiplications (MSMs), achieving geometric mean speedups of 801× over CPU baselines \[[10](http://arxiv.org/abs/2109.05371)\]. These developments highlight the growing focus on hardware solutions that reduce the overhead of cryptographic computations, enabling real-time applications in privacy-preserving computing \[[8](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/b718a4b59ee6ee21c7c2ee1aa64085231853535e)\]\[[10](http://arxiv.org/abs/2109.05371)\].
