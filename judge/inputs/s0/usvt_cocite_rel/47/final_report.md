## Related Works

Multi-Domain Recommendation (MDR) has emerged as a promising approach to address the data sparsity and cold start problems in recommender systems by leveraging knowledge transfer across multiple domains \[[15](http://arxiv.org/abs/2308.05508)\]. Traditional MDR methods often rely on a single structure to transfer domain-shared knowledge, which can be limiting when the transfer information varies across domains \[[17](http://arxiv.org/abs/2211.11964)\]. This limitation leads to the Negative Transfer Problem (NTP), where unselectively transferring information from all domains can degrade recommendation performance, especially when there is knowledge conflict or poor quality in certain domains \[[17](http://arxiv.org/abs/2211.11964)\]. 

To mitigate NTP, several studies have explored domain adaptation and knowledge transfer mechanisms. For instance, \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/1804.06769)\] proposes a deep transfer learning approach for cross-domain recommendation, which enables dual knowledge transfer across domains by introducing cross connections between networks. This method improves performance by learning complex user-item interaction relationships. Similarly, \[[3](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/d5630291a4ba3f6266c77e6d00920502f986afa6)\] introduces BiTGCF, a bi-directional transfer learning method that realizes two-way knowledge transfer across domains by using common users as bridges. It also fuses domain-specific and common features during transfer, enhancing recommendation accuracy.

Another line of research focuses on the challenges of multi-target cross-domain recommendation. \[[5](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/2b031c7170c9dccbe506f3d3a1d6104f65e835ae)\] presents DTCDR, a framework that simultaneously improves recommendation performance on both richer and sparser domains by leveraging rating and multi-source content information. It employs Multi-Task Learning (MTL) to combine embeddings of common users across domains, enabling dual-target CDR. \[[12](http://arxiv.org/abs/2108.07976)\] further extends this idea by proposing a unified framework for cross-domain and cross-system recommendations, aiming to improve performance across all datasets simultaneously. This approach uses graph embeddings and attention mechanisms to combine embeddings of common entities across domains.

The issue of domain-specific preferences and the need for adaptive knowledge transfer has also been addressed in recent works. \[[14](http://arxiv.org/abs/2211.11191)\] introduces a hierarchical hypergraph network for MDR, which captures correlative preferences across domains by representing user-item interactions in a unified graph. It incorporates dynamic item transfer and adaptive user aggregation modules to improve representation learning. \[[15](http://arxiv.org/abs/2308.05508)\] proposes EDDA, which disentangles embeddings to separate domain-invariant and domain-specific knowledge, and uses domain alignment to enhance knowledge transfer across domains with small overlaps.
