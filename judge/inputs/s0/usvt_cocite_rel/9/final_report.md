## Related Works

Quantitative susceptibility mapping (QSM) has emerged as a powerful tool for characterizing multiple sclerosis (MS) lesions, particularly in distinguishing between rim and non-rim lesion types \[[21](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/455e181fe505a36406af032700fea2ca87a2e9b9)\]. Rim lesions, characterized by a paramagnetic rim due to iron deposition, are associated with chronic active inflammation and are linked to greater disability and tissue damage \[[2](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/bb5b80dc4165d0ac3e28d1882b898633e84779c2)\]. These lesions are often difficult to detect and segment due to their rarity and variability in appearance \[[1](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/71b5e8d9970f3b725127eb7da3d5183b921ad017)\]. Several studies have explored automated methods for identifying rim lesions using QSM data \[[3](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/b4d08f780bb2ec7f8686ebc4890761ab8b1b4f5c)\]\[[4](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/09b2a6040c15e8672169531fc76bba0b5a04296b)\]\[[10](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/45bf918158d3f779cb41b982fa9237682c11dd96)\]. For instance, QSMRim-Net, a deep neural network, has been developed to identify rim+ lesions on QSM by fusing lesion-level radiomic and convolutional image features \[[10](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/45bf918158d3f779cb41b982fa9237682c11dd96)\]. This method outperforms other state-of-the-art techniques in terms of area under the receiver operating characteristic curve (AUC) and precision recall AUC \[[10](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/45bf918158d3f779cb41b982fa9237682c11dd96)\].

The challenge of class imbalance between rim and non-rim lesions has been a significant barrier to effective segmentation \[[1](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/71b5e8d9970f3b725127eb7da3d5183b921ad017)\]. To address this, researchers have proposed synthetic data generation techniques to augment training datasets \[[29](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/b1d6aa6e4f34601f21fdc459d2aed99a464726fa)\]. In particular, the use of generative adversarial networks (GANs) to create synthetic QSM images of paramagnetic rim lesions has been explored \[[29](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/b1d6aa6e4f34601f21fdc459d2aed99a464726fa)\]. These synthetic data improve classifier performance by increasing the representation of the minority class, which is crucial for accurate lesion detection \[[29](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/b1d6aa6e4f34601f21fdc459d2aed99a464726fa)\]. Additionally, methods such as CarveMix have been used to generate synthetic images for data augmentation, enhancing segmentation performance \[[29](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/b1d6aa6e4f34601f21fdc459d2aed99a464726fa)\].

Several studies have focused on improving automated lesion segmentation using deep learning techniques \[[11](http://arxiv.org/abs/1702.04869)\]\[[15](http://arxiv.org/abs/2002.12470)\]\[[25](http://arxiv.org/abs/1603.05959)\]. For example, a cascaded 3D convolutional neural network (CNN) approach has been proposed to improve the accuracy of white matter lesion segmentation in MS patients \[[11](http://arxiv.org/abs/1702.04869)\]. This method outperforms other state-of-the-art techniques on public datasets and clinical data \[[11](http://arxiv.org/abs/1702.04869)\]. Similarly, RSANet, a recurrent slice-wise attention network, has been developed to model 3D MRI images as sequences of slices, capturing long-range dependencies to improve segmentation accuracy \[[15](http://arxiv.org/abs/2002.12470)\]. The category "Evaluation Frameworks and Datasets for Lesion Segmentation" highlights the challenges and advancements in automated multiple sclerosis (MS) lesion segmentation. Automated segmentation methods are crucial due to the variability in lesion appearance and the limitations of manual segmentation, which is time-consuming and subject to inter- and intra-observer variability \[[1](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/71b5e8d9970f3b725127eb7da3d5183b921ad017)\]. Evaluation frameworks have also been developed to compare different algorithms, such as the ISBI 2015 longitudinal lesion segmentation challenge, which provided standardized training and test data sets with expert manual delineations \[[3](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/b4d08f780bb2ec7f8686ebc4890761ab8b1b4f5c)\]. Additionally, studies have explored the use of synthetic data and generative networks to address class imbalance issues, particularly in rare lesion types like paramagnetic rim lesions (PRLs), by improving classifier performance through synthetic data generation and denoising techniques \[[4](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/09b2a6040c15e8672169531fc76bba0b5a04296b)\]. The evaluation of these methods often involves quantitative metrics such as Dice coefficients, inter-rater correlation, and lesion volume agreement, which are essential for validating the reliability and reproducibility of automated segmentation techniques \[[5](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/2dfca37050afe466e81fbb8cc8ae9da3e368fb0c)\]. Despite these advancements, no single method has been universally accepted, and challenges remain in developing robust techniques that generalize across different MRI protocols and disease stages \[[8](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/8611112d0c105f752e239fe8729e652f8ee52ce3)\].

In this work, we present a novel foreground-based generative method for modelling the local lesion characteristics that can both generate synthetic lesions on healthy images and synthesize subject-specific pseudo-healthy images from pathological images. Furthermore, the proposed method can be used as a data augmentation module to generate synthetic images for training brain image segmentation networks. Experiments on multiple sclerosis (MS) brain images acquired on magnetic resonance imaging (MRI) demonstrate that the proposed method can generate highly realistic pseudo-healthy and pseudo-pathological brain images.
