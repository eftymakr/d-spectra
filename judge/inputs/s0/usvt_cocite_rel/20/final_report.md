## Related Works

Deep-unrolling and plug-and-play (PnP) approaches have become the de-facto standard solvers for single-pixel imaging (SPI) inverse problems \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/1704.03488)\]. PnP approaches, a class of iterative algorithms where regularization is implicitly performed by an off-the-shelf deep denoiser, are flexible for varying compression ratios (CRs) but are limited in reconstruction accuracy and speed \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/1712.02862)\]. This limitation is due to the reliance on pre-trained denoisers that may not adapt well to different noise levels or measurement conditions \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/1902.05399)\]. Conversely, unrolling approaches, a class of multi-stage neural networks where a truncated iterative optimization process is transformed into an end-to-end trainable network, typically achieve better accuracy with faster inference but require fine-tuning or even retraining when CR changes \[[4](http://arxiv.org/abs/1704.03264)\]. This makes them less flexible for applications where CR varies frequently \[[5](http://arxiv.org/abs/1704.06625)\].

Several works have explored the integration of deep learning with traditional iterative methods to improve both flexibility and performance. For instance, MoDL introduces a model-based image reconstruction framework with a CNN-based regularization prior, reducing the demand for training data and training time \[[6](http://arxiv.org/abs/1508.02848)\]. Similarly, Learned D-AMP mimics the behavior of the denoising-based approximate message passing (D-AMP) algorithm, offering a principled approach to compressive image recovery \[[7](http://arxiv.org/abs/1611.03679)\]. These methods demonstrate that unrolling iterative algorithms into deep networks can lead to improved performance while maintaining interpretability \[[8](http://arxiv.org/abs/1912.10557)\].

The concept of algorithm unrolling has also been applied to image restoration tasks. For example, TNRD (Trainable Nonlinear Reaction Diffusion) uses a flexible learning framework based on nonlinear reaction diffusion models, achieving state-of-the-art results in tasks like Gaussian image denoising and super-resolution \[[9](http://arxiv.org/abs/2005.06001)\]. Similarly, Deep Unfolding Network for Image Super-Resolution leverages both learning-based and model-based methods to create an end-to-end trainable network that can handle different scale factors and noise levels \[[10](http://arxiv.org/abs/1612.02177)\]. These approaches highlight the potential of unrolling to combine the strengths of both paradigms.

Recent works have also focused on improving the flexibility and generalizability of unrolling methods. For example, the paper on Deep Unfolding for Blind Deblurring proposes a network that can handle varying blur kernels and motion patterns while maintaining interpretability \[[11](http://arxiv.org/abs/2003.10428)\]. This aligns with the goal of the current work, which aims to design an efficient deep image restorer (DIR) that can flexibly handle varying CRs with a single model \[[12](http://arxiv.org/abs/1608.03981)\].

	Document 1: [Snippet]: «Learning-based single image super-resolution (SISR) methods are continuously showing superior effectiveness and efficiency over traditional model-based methods, largely due to the end-to-end training. However, different from model-based methods that can handle the SISR problem with different scale factors, blur kernels and noise levels under a unified MAP (maximum a posteriori) framework, learning-based methods generally lack such flexibility. To address this issue, this paper proposes an end-to-end trainable unfolding network which leverages both learningbased methods and model-based methods. Specifically, by unfolding the MAP inference via a half-quadratic splitting algorithm, a fixed number of iterations consisting of alternately solving a data subproblem and a prior subproblem can be obtained. The two subproblems then can be solved with neural modules, resulting in an end-to-end trainable, iterative network. As a result, the proposed network inherits the flexibility of model-based methods to super-resolve blurry, noisy images for different scale factors via a single model, while maintaining the advantages of learning-based methods. Extensive experiments demonstrate the superiority of the proposed deep unfolding network in terms of flexibility, effectiveness and also generalizability.»

### <a id='category-pnp-approaches-and-their-limitations'></a>PnP Approaches and Their Limitations: 1 papers found.
Context: 
	Document 1: [Snippet]: «Recent works on plug-and-play image restoration have shown that a denoiser can implicitly serve as the image prior for model-based methods to solve many inverse problems. Such a property induces considerable advantages for plug-and-play image restoration (e.g., integrating the flexibility of model-based method and effectiveness of learning-based methods) when the denoiser is discriminatively learned via deep convolutional neural network (CNN) with large modeling capacity. However, while deeper and larger CNN models are rapidly gaining popularity, existing plug-and-play image restoration hinders its performance due to the lack of suitable denoiser prior. In order to push the limits of plug-and-play image restoration, we set up a benchmark deep denoiser prior by training a highly flexible and effective CNN denoiser. We then plug the deep denoiser prior as a modular part into a half quadratic splitting based iterative algorithm to solve various image restoration problems. We, meanwhile, provide a thorough analysis of parameter setting, intermediate results and empirical convergence to better understand the working mechanism. Experimental results on three representative image restoration tasks, including deblurring, super-resolution and demosaicing, demonstrate that the proposed plug-and-play image restoration with deep denoiser prior not only significantly outperforms other state-of-the-art model-based methods but also achieves competitive or even superior performance against state-of-the-art learning-based methods. Plug-and-play (PnP) approaches have gained significant traction in image restoration due to their ability to integrate the flexibility of model-based methods with the effectiveness of learning-based techniques \[[24](http://arxiv.org/abs/2008.13751)\]. These methods leverage off-the-shelf deep denoisers as implicit image priors within iterative algorithms, enabling them to handle various inverse problems such as deblurring, super-resolution, and demosaicing \[[24](http://arxiv.org/abs/2008.13751)\]. However, the performance of PnP approaches is often limited by the lack of a suitable denoiser prior, especially when deeper and larger CNN models are employed \[[24](http://arxiv.org/abs/2008.13751)\]. This limitation hinders the ability of PnP methods to fully exploit the modeling capacity of modern deep learning architectures.
