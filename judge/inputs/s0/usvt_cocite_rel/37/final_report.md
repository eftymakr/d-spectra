## Related Works

Vision Transformers (ViTs) have emerged as a powerful alternative to convolutional neural networks (CNNs) in computer vision tasks, leveraging the transformer architecture to effectively capture global context \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/2010.11929)\]. However, the deployment of ViTs on hardware platforms, especially FPGAs, remains challenging due to the complex arithmetic operations such as Softmax and LayerNorm, which are sensitive to model accuracy and difficult to map onto efficient hardware with low precision \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2208.05163)\]. Prior work has primarily focused on optimizing matrix multiplication operations on hardware while keeping these complex operations on the CPU, resulting in suboptimal performance due to communication overhead between the CPU and accelerator \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2208.05163)\].

Several studies have explored hardware acceleration for ViTs. Auto-ViT-Acc proposes an FPGA-aware acceleration framework with mixed-scheme quantization, achieving higher accuracy than state-of-the-art quantization methods while improving frame rates \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2208.05163)\]. ViTCoD introduces a co-design framework that integrates algorithmic pruning and hardware acceleration, achieving significant speedups by reducing dominant data movement costs \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/2210.09573)\]. I-ViT enables integer-only inference for ViTs by approximating non-linear operations with integer arithmetic, achieving comparable accuracy to full-precision models \[[4](http://arxiv.org/abs/2207.01405)\]. FQ-ViT addresses the challenges of post-training quantization for ViTs, reducing inference complexity while maintaining high accuracy \[[7](http://arxiv.org/abs/2111.13824)\]. Patch Slimming reduces computational costs by discarding redundant patches in a top-down manner, achieving over 45% FLOPs reduction with minimal accuracy loss \[[8](http://arxiv.org/abs/2106.02852)\]. HeatViT introduces hardware-efficient token pruning, achieving higher accuracy and significant computation reduction on various ViT models \[[9](http://arxiv.org/abs/2211.08110)\]. VAQF proposes a fully automatic framework for low-bit ViT acceleration on FPGAs, achieving real-time execution with minimal compilation time \[[10](http://arxiv.org/abs/2201.06618)\].

Efforts to improve ViT efficiency have also focused on architectural innovations. T2T-ViT improves training efficiency by incorporating a Tokens-to-Token transformation, reducing parameter count and MACs while improving accuracy \[[5](http://arxiv.org/abs/2101.11986)\]. LeViT combines principles from CNNs and transformers to achieve faster inference, outperforming existing convnets and vision transformers in the speed/accuracy tradeoff \[[6](http://arxiv.org/abs/2104.01136)\]. CvT introduces convolutions into ViTs to improve performance and efficiency, achieving state-of-the-art results with fewer parameters and lower FLOPs \[[12](http://arxiv.org/abs/2103.15808)\]. DynamicViT proposes a dynamic token sparsification framework, reducing FLOPs and improving throughput with minimal accuracy loss \[[13](http://arxiv.org/abs/2106.02034)\]. Swin Transformer introduces a hierarchical architecture with shifted windows, enabling efficient and effective dense prediction tasks \[[15](http://arxiv.org/abs/2103.14030)\].

### <a id='category-fpga-based-acceleration-approaches'></a>FPGA-Based Acceleration Approaches: 3 papers found.
FPGA-based acceleration approaches for Vision Transformers (ViTs) have gained significant attention due to the computational intensity and memory demands of ViT models, which hinder their deployment on resource-constrained edge devices \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2208.05163)\]. Several studies have proposed hardware-aware methods to enhance the efficiency and accuracy of ViT inference on FPGAs. For instance, one work introduces a mixed-scheme quantization approach to achieve higher Top-1 accuracy compared to state-of-the-art ViT quantization methods, while maintaining a reasonable accuracy drop \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2208.05163)\]. Another study, HeatViT, presents an image-adaptive token pruning framework that dynamically identifies and removes non-informative tokens, leading to significant computation reduction and accuracy improvements \[[9](http://arxiv.org/abs/2211.08110)\]. Additionally, VAQF introduces an automatic framework for quantizing ViTs with binary weights and low-precision activations, enabling real-time execution on FPGAs by satisfying frame rate requirements with minimal compilation time \[[10](http://arxiv.org/abs/2201.06618)\]. These approaches highlight the importance of hardware-aware quantization, efficient token management, and custom hardware design to address the challenges of ViT acceleration on FPGAs.

However, ViTs’ self-attention module is still arguably a major bottleneck, limiting their achievable hardware efficiency and more extensive applications to resource constrained platforms. Meanwhile, existing accelerators dedicated to NLP Transformers are not optimal for ViTs. This is because there is a large difference between ViTs and Transformers for natural language processing (NLP) tasks: ViTs have a relatively fixed number of input tokens, whose attention maps can be pruned by up to 90% even with fixed sparse patterns, without severely hurting the model accuracy (e.g., <=1.5% under 90% pruning ratio); while NLP Transformers need to handle input sequences of varying numbers of tokens and rely on on-the-fly predictions of dynamic sparse attention patterns for each input to achieve a decent sparsity (e.g., >=50%). To this end, we propose a dedicated algorithm and accelerator co-design framework dubbed ViTCoD for accelerating ViTs. Specifically, on the algorithm level, ViTCoD prunes and polarizes the attention maps to have either denser or sparser fixed patterns for regularizing two levels of workloads without hurting the accuracy, largely reducing the attention computations while leaving room for alleviating the remaining dominant data movements; on top of that, we further integrate a lightweight and learnable auto-encoder module to enable trading the dominant high-cost data movements for lower-cost computations. On the hardware level, we develop a dedicated accelerator to simultaneously coordinate the aforementioned enforced denser and sparser workloads for boosted hardware utilization, while integrating on-chip encoder and decoder engines to leverage ViTCoD’s algorithm pipeline for much reduced data movements. Extensive experiments and ablation studies validate that ViTCoD largely reduces the dominant data movement costs, achieving speedups of up to 235.3×, 142.9×, 86.0×, 10.1×, and 6.8× over general computing platforms CPUs, EdgeGPUs, GPUs, and prior-art Transformer accelerators SpAtten and Sanger under an attention sparsity of 90%, respectively. 	Document 1: [Snippet]: «Vision Transformers (ViTs) have achieved state-of-the-art performance on various computer vision applications. However, these models have considerable storage and computational overheads, making their deployment and efficient inference on edge devices challenging. Quantization is a promising approach to reducing model complexity, and the dyadic arithmetic pipeline can allow the quantized models to perform efficient integer-only inference. Unfortunately, dyadic arithmetic is based on the homogeneity condition in convolutional neural networks, which is not applicable to the non-linear components in ViTs, making integer-only inference of ViTs an open issue. In this paper, we propose I-ViT, an integer-only quantization scheme for ViTs, to enable ViTs to perform the entire computational graph of inference with integer arithmetic and bit-shifting, and without any floating-point arithmetic. In I-ViT, linear operations (e.g., MatMul and Dense) follow the integer-only pipeline with dyadic arithmetic, and non-linear operations (e.g., Softmax, GELU, and LayerNorm) are approximated by the proposed light-weight integer-only arithmetic methods. More specifically, I-ViT applies the proposed Shiftmax and ShiftGELU, which are designed to use integer bit-shifting to approximate the corresponding floating-point operations. We evaluate I-ViT on various benchmark models and the results show that integer-only INT8 quantization achieves comparable (or even slightly higher) accuracy to the full-precision (FP) baseline. Furthermore, we utilize TVM for practical hardware deployment on the GPU’s integer arithmetic units, achieving 3.72 ~ 4.11 inference speedup compared to the FP model. Code of both Pytorch and TVM is released at https://github.com/zkkli/I-ViT.»

### <a id='category-algorithmic-improvements-for-vits'></a>Algorithmic Improvements for ViTs: 9 papers found.
The category "Algorithmic Improvements for ViTs" highlights various advancements aimed at enhancing the performance, efficiency, and practicality of Vision Transformers (ViTs) in computer vision tasks. Researchers have focused on addressing key limitations of ViTs, such as their reliance on high computational resources and poor handling of local structures. For instance, the Tokens-To-Token Vision Transformer (T2T-ViT) introduces a layer-wise Tokens-to-Token transformation to better model local structures and reduce token count, leading to improved efficiency and accuracy \[[5](http://arxiv.org/abs/2101.11986)\]. Similarly, the LeViT architecture optimizes the trade-off between accuracy and efficiency by incorporating principles from convolutional neural networks and introducing attention bias for positional information \[[6](http://arxiv.org/abs/2104.01136)\]. The CvT model integrates convolutions into ViT to leverage the strengths of both architectures, achieving state-of-the-art performance with fewer parameters and lower FLOPs \[[12](http://arxiv.org/abs/2103.15808)\]. The Transformer iN Transformer (TNT) further enhances ViT by introducing a hierarchical attention mechanism that divides local patches into smaller units, improving feature representation and achieving higher accuracy \[[14](http://arxiv.org/abs/2103.00112)\]. The Swin Transformer addresses the challenge of adapting Transformers to vision tasks by introducing a hierarchical structure with shifted windows, enabling efficient modeling of visual entities at different scales \[[15](http://arxiv.org/abs/2103.14030)\]. Additionally, efforts have been made to compress ViTs through unified frameworks that combine pruning, layer skipping, and knowledge distillation, achieving significant reductions in computational cost without sacrificing accuracy \[[16](http://arxiv.org/abs/2203.08243)\]. These improvements collectively demonstrate the ongoing evolution of ViTs, making them more viable for real-world applications.
