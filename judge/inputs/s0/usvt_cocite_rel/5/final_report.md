## Related Works

Instruction-following text embedding is a critical area of research that aims to generate dynamic text embeddings capable of adapting to user instructions, highlighting specific attributes of text \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/1506.06726)\]. This task is particularly challenging due to the computational overhead associated with re-encoding the entire corpus for each new instruction \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/1506.06726)\]. Several approaches have been proposed to address this challenge, but they often suffer from inefficiency or limited adaptability.

Early work in sentence representation includes Skip-Thought Vectors, which use an encoder-decoder model to reconstruct surrounding sentences and produce robust sentence embeddings \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/1706.03762)\]. These embeddings are evaluated on multiple tasks, including semantic relatedness and sentiment analysis, demonstrating their effectiveness \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/1706.03762)\]. Similarly, BERT, a bidirectional transformer model, pre-trains deep representations from unlabeled text and achieves state-of-the-art results on various natural language processing tasks \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/1810.04805)\]. BERT's ability to condition on both left and right context in all layers makes it particularly effective for tasks requiring fine-grained understanding \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/1810.04805)\].

More recently, Sentence-T5 explores the use of pre-trained text-to-text models to generate sentence embeddings, demonstrating that encoder-only models can outperform previous best models on semantic textual similarity tasks \[[4](http://arxiv.org/abs/2108.08877)\]. This work also introduces a new benchmark, SentGLUE, which extends the SentEval toolkit to nine tasks from the GLUE benchmark \[[4](http://arxiv.org/abs/2108.08877)\]. SimCSE, another contrastive learning framework, further advances the state-of-the-art in sentence embeddings by using dropout as a form of data augmentation and achieving strong performance on semantic textual similarity tasks \[[5](http://arxiv.org/abs/1711.07280)\].

The importance of instruction-aware models has also been recognized in the field of vision-and-language navigation, where models must interpret natural-language instructions based on visual input \[[6](http://arxiv.org/abs/2104.08821)\]. These models often rely on reinforcement learning and cross-modal matching to improve performance \[[6](http://arxiv.org/abs/2104.08821)\]. Similarly, the development of instruction-tuned models, such as InstructGPT, highlights the value of human feedback in aligning language models with user intent \[[7](http://arxiv.org/abs/1806.02724)\]. These models are fine-tuned using human demonstrations and rankings of model outputs, leading to improvements in truthfulness and reduction in toxic output generation \[[7](http://arxiv.org/abs/1806.02724)\].
