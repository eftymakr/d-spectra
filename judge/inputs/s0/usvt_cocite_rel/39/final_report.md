## Related Works

De novo genome assembly has been a focal point of computational biology research, driven by the advent of next-generation sequencing technologies that generate massive volumes of short reads \[[5](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/7279acd9c61a8de019ea2d55d23124dfbb6b5269)\]. Early assemblers, such as SOAPdenovo \[[18](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/f8d1052e2c9d1cdd63093427ccc9169e848d777d)\], were designed for short-read data and relied on de Bruijn graph representations to manage memory constraints. However, these methods struggled with repetitive regions and required significant computational resources, limiting their scalability \[[5](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/7279acd9c61a8de019ea2d55d23124dfbb6b5269)\]. The introduction of long-read technologies, such as PacBio and Oxford Nanopore, has shifted the focus toward algorithms capable of handling high-error, long sequences \[[2](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/2b59fd18ada769ef76fb7fcc7382f063791abfab)\]. Tools like Canu \[[2](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/2b59fd18ada769ef76fb7fcc7382f063791abfab)\] and Minimap2 \[[14](http://arxiv.org/abs/1708.01492)\] have emerged to address these challenges by introducing adaptive k-mer weighting, repeat separation, and efficient alignment strategies. Canu, for instance, leverages probabilistic models and sparse graph representations to improve assembly continuity and reduce runtime \[[2](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/2b59fd18ada769ef76fb7fcc7382f063791abfab)\].

The computational complexity of de novo assembly remains a critical challenge, especially when dealing with large genomes and high-throughput data \[[1](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/d04b3cc774151260a59a5e6768b6cfae7fc3e512)\]. Existing assemblers often suffer from memory-bound behavior and irregular data access patterns due to the interdependencies in overlapping reads \[[1](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/d04b3cc774151260a59a5e6768b6cfae7fc3e512)\]. To mitigate these issues, distributed-memory approaches have been proposed, such as the diBELLA 2D pipeline, which introduces parallel algorithms for overlap detection and layout simplification \[[4](http://arxiv.org/abs/2010.10055)\]. These methods aim to improve scalability by leveraging linear-algebra operations over semirings and distributed sparse matrices. However, they still face limitations in handling the high error rates and repetitive structures inherent in long-read data \[[1](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/d04b3cc774151260a59a5e6768b6cfae7fc3e512)\].

Recent advances in metagenome assembly have also highlighted the need for scalable and memory-efficient algorithms \[[23](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/c0471dc9500e266c2b3669f724dbf816e8b51f11)\]. MetaHipMer, for example, enables coassembly of terabase-scale datasets by optimizing for distributed memory and large clusters of compute nodes \[[23](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/c0471dc9500e266c2b3669f724dbf816e8b51f11)\]. This approach is particularly relevant for applications involving complex microbial communities, where the diversity of reads poses significant challenges for traditional assemblers \[[23](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/c0471dc9500e266c2b3669f724dbf816e8b51f11)\]. These methods demonstrate the importance of adapting assembly strategies to the specific characteristics of the sequencing data, such as error profiles and repeat content \[[21](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/788f8a32ed116f928f3cab554c6efd55385f2a80)\].

### <a id='category-computational-complexity-challenges'></a>Computational Complexity Challenges: 7 papers found.
The computational complexity challenges in genome assembly are well-documented, with multiple studies highlighting the algorithmic and resource-intensive nature of the problem. Existing assemblers often struggle with large datasets, memory constraints, and the inherent difficulties of handling repetitive or heterozygous genomes \[[1](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/d04b3cc774151260a59a5e6768b6cfae7fc3e512)\]. For instance, short-read sequencing technologies, while providing high throughput and cost efficiency, result in fragmented assemblies due to their limited read lengths, necessitating advanced methods to improve contiguity and accuracy \[[7](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/c6e319a023f932d6d2ff8897c2c91e56e872db8e)\]. Similarly, long-read technologies, though promising, require specialized alignment algorithms capable of handling errors and complex structural variations \[[14](http://arxiv.org/abs/1708.01492)\]. The performance of assemblers varies significantly, with string-based and overlap-layout-consensus (OLC) assemblers being more suitable for short reads and small genomes, whereas de Bruijn graph-based assemblers excel with large datasets \[[5](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/7279acd9c61a8de019ea2d55d23124dfbb6b5269)\]. Furthermore, the lack of standardized metrics for evaluating assembly quality complicates the comparison of different tools, emphasizing the need for more robust assessment frameworks \[[16](http://arxiv.org/abs/1301.5406)\]. These challenges underscore the necessity for innovative approaches, such as hardware-software co-designs like NMP-PaK, which aim to reduce memory footprints and improve computational efficiency through near-memory processing \[[1](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/d04b3cc774151260a59a5e6768b6cfae7fc3e512)\].

### <a id='category-long-read-assembly-tools'></a>Long-Read Assembly Tools: 5 papers found.
Long-read sequencing technologies, such as PacBio and Oxford Nanopore, have significantly advanced de novo genome assembly by enabling the reconstruction of complex genomes with high continuity \[[1](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/d04b3cc774151260a59a5e6768b6cfae7fc3e512)\]. However, these technologies are associated with high error rates, which pose challenges for assembling large repeats and closely related haplotypes \[[1](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/d04b3cc774151260a59a5e6768b6cfae7fc3e512)\]. To address these issues, tools like Canu have been developed, offering improved assembly continuity, reduced runtime, and support for nanopore data \[[1](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/d04b3cc774151260a59a5e6768b6cfae7fc3e512)\]. Similarly, other tools such as miniasm and minimap provide efficient mapping and assembly of long reads without requiring an error correction stage, achieving rapid assembly of bacterial and eukaryotic genomes \[[2](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/2b59fd18ada769ef76fb7fcc7382f063791abfab)\]. The MHAP algorithm, integrated with Celera Assembler, further enhances the accuracy of assemblies by leveraging probabilistic hashing for overlapping noisy long reads, enabling reference-grade assemblies of complex eukaryotic genomes \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/1512.01801)\]. Additionally, BELLA introduces a novel approach for computing overlaps using sparse matrix multiplication and reliable k-mer detection, improving the precision and recall of assembly processes \[[4](http://arxiv.org/abs/2010.10055)\]. These advancements highlight the ongoing efforts to improve the efficiency, accuracy, and scalability of long-read de novo assembly tools, addressing the computational challenges inherent in handling large and complex genomic datasets.

These approaches highlight the potential of distributed memory systems in handling the computational intensity and data volume inherent in modern bioinformatics tasks.
