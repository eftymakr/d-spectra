## Related Works

Generative retrieval (GR) has gained significant attention in information retrieval (IR) as an alternative to traditional index-retrieve pipelines \[[14](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/5beaeee1e27007bd12967480bb9c379cebd15e6e)\]. Unlike conventional methods that rely on separate indexing and retrieval stages, GR models directly map queries to document identifiers (docids) using a sequence-to-sequence framework \[[14](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/5beaeee1e27007bd12967480bb9c379cebd15e6e)\]. This paradigm has been explored in several recent works, including Differentiable Search Index (DSI), which encodes all documents into the model parameters and allows direct query-to-docid mapping \[[4](http://arxiv.org/abs/2202.06991)\]. However, most existing GR models are evaluated on static document collections, and their performance on dynamic corpora, where documents are continuously added, remains underexplored \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2206.02743)\].

Early efforts in GR focused on static collections, such as the DSI model, which uses a single Transformer to encode the entire corpus \[[4](http://arxiv.org/abs/2202.06991)\]. However, deploying DSI on dynamic corpora requires retraining the model, which is computationally expensive \[[9](http://arxiv.org/abs/2212.09744)\]. To address this, DSI++ was introduced, enabling continual learning by incrementally indexing new documents while preserving retrieval performance on previously indexed documents \[[9](http://arxiv.org/abs/2212.09744)\]. This work demonstrated that continual indexing can lead to forgetting, but strategies such as flatter minima and generative memory were proposed to mitigate this issue \[[9](http://arxiv.org/abs/2212.09744)\].

The design of docids plays a crucial role in GR performance. Numeric-based docids, such as those used in some early GR models, offer high efficiency but suffer from poor generalization over dynamic corpora due to overfitting on the training set \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2206.02743)\]. In contrast, text-based docids, which are more semantically aligned with language models, show superior performance, especially in dynamic settings \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2206.02743)\]. This aligns with findings from the NOVO paper, which proposed learnable and interpretable docids optimized for retrieval tasks \[[6](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/6682a3679a17d090830635f1c420126d20eae54a)\]. NOVO docids are generated as n-gram sets and can be trained to better capture semantic relationships between documents and queries \[[6](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/6682a3679a17d090830635f1c420126d20eae54a)\].

Several works have explored the trade-off between efficiency and effectiveness in GR. For example, the GenRet method learns to tokenize documents into short discrete representations (docids) using a discrete auto-encoding approach, enabling end-to-end modeling \[[8](http://arxiv.org/abs/2304.04171)\]. Similarly, the TOME model introduces a two-stage generation architecture that uses tokenized URLs as identifiers, improving both efficiency and performance \[[16](http://arxiv.org/abs/2305.11161)\]. These approaches highlight the importance of docid design in balancing the two competing objectives of GR.
