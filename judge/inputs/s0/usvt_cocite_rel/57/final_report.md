## Related Works

Generative retrieval (GR) has gained significant attention in information retrieval (IR) as an alternative to traditional index-retrieve pipelines \[[14](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/5beaeee1e27007bd12967480bb9c379cebd15e6e)\]. Unlike conventional methods that rely on separate indexing and retrieval stages, GR models directly map queries to document identifiers (docids) using a sequence-to-sequence framework \[[14](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/5beaeee1e27007bd12967480bb9c379cebd15e6e)\]. This paradigm has been explored in several recent works, including Differentiable Search Index (DSI), which encodes all documents into the model parameters and allows direct query-to-docid mapping \[[4](http://arxiv.org/abs/2202.06991)\]. However, most existing GR models are evaluated on static document collections, and their performance on dynamic corpora, where documents are continuously added, remains underexplored \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2206.02743)\].

Early efforts in GR focused on static collections, such as the DSI model, which uses a single Transformer to encode the entire corpus \[[4](http://arxiv.org/abs/2202.06991)\]. However, deploying DSI on dynamic corpora requires retraining the model, which is computationally expensive \[[9](http://arxiv.org/abs/2212.09744)\]. To address this, DSI++ was introduced, enabling continual learning by incrementally indexing new documents while preserving retrieval performance on previously indexed documents \[[9](http://arxiv.org/abs/2212.09744)\]. This work demonstrated that continual indexing can lead to forgetting, but strategies such as flatter minima and generative memory were proposed to mitigate this issue \[[9](http://arxiv.org/abs/2212.09744)\].

The design of docids plays a crucial role in GR performance. Numeric-based docids, such as those used in some early GR models, offer high efficiency but suffer from poor generalization over dynamic corpora due to overfitting on the training set \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2206.02743)\]. In contrast, text-based docids, which are more semantically aligned with language models, show superior performance, especially in dynamic settings \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2206.02743)\]. This aligns with findings from the NOVO paper, which proposed learnable and interpretable docids optimized for retrieval tasks \[[6](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/6682a3679a17d090830635f1c420126d20eae54a)\]. NOVO docids are generated as n-gram sets and can be trained to better capture semantic relationships between documents and queries \[[6](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/6682a3679a17d090830635f1c420126d20eae54a)\].

Several works have explored the trade-off between efficiency and effectiveness in GR. For example, the GenRet method learns to tokenize documents into short discrete representations (docids) using a discrete auto-encoding approach, enabling end-to-end modeling \[[8](http://arxiv.org/abs/2304.04171)\]. Similarly, the TOME model introduces a two-stage generation architecture that uses tokenized URLs as identifiers, improving both efficiency and performance \[[16](http://arxiv.org/abs/2305.11161)\]. These approaches highlight the importance of docid design in balancing the two competing objectives of GR.

The category "Empirical Evidence for Dynamic Corpora" highlights the challenges and advancements in generative retrieval (GR) models when applied to dynamic document collections. Existing GR models, which typically rely on static corpora, face significant performance degradation when new documents are continuously added, as they struggle to maintain relevance and avoid forgetting previously indexed content \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/2308.14968)\]. To address this, researchers have introduced continual learning frameworks such as CLEVER, which employs Incremental Product Quantization to efficiently encode new documents and a memory-augmented mechanism to retain knowledge from prior indexing stages \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/2308.14968)\]. Similarly, DSI++ introduces a continual learning challenge for differentiable search indices, demonstrating that continual indexing leads to considerable forgetting and proposing methods to mitigate this issue, such as optimizing for flatter minima and introducing a generative memory to supplement retrieval during indexing \[[9](http://arxiv.org/abs/2212.09744)\]. Additionally, RIPOR enhances generative retrieval by incorporating a prefix-oriented ranking optimization algorithm and constructing document IDs based on relevance associations between queries and documents, achieving substantial improvements on large-scale benchmarks \[[7](http://arxiv.org/abs/2311.09134)\]. These studies collectively emphasize the importance of designing GR models that can adapt to dynamic corpora while maintaining performance and avoiding catastrophic forgetting, thus paving the way for more robust and scalable retrieval systems in real-world applications \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/2308.14968)\]\[[7](http://arxiv.org/abs/2311.09134)\]\[[9](http://arxiv.org/abs/2212.09744)\].

### <a id='category-docid-design-and-its-impact-on-performance'></a>DocID Design and Its Impact on Performance: 7 papers found.
Document ID design significantly impacts the performance of generative retrieval models, as highlighted in multiple studies. Researchers have explored various approaches to document identifier (docid) representation, such as semantic-rich docids and learnable identifiers, to improve retrieval effectiveness. For instance, Ultron proposes a model-based indexer that encodes document knowledge into the model, using semantically meaningful docids to enhance retrieval quality \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/2208.09257)\]. Similarly, the Differentiable Search Index (DSI) introduces a paradigm where a single Transformer model maps queries directly to relevant docids, simplifying the retrieval process by encoding all corpus information into model parameters \[[4](http://arxiv.org/abs/2202.06991)\]. The Neural Optimized VOcabularial (NOVO) docids further advance this by generating unique n-gram sets that can be optimized through training, allowing for better semantic alignment and document relationships \[[6](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/6682a3679a17d090830635f1c420126d20eae54a)\]. GenRet introduces a discrete auto-encoding approach to learn semantic docids end-to-end, addressing the limitations of fixed rule-based identifiers \[[8](http://arxiv.org/abs/2304.04171)\]. The Semantic-Enhanced DSI (SE-DSI) model builds upon DSI by incorporating cognitive psychology-inspired strategies, such as elaborative descriptions and rehearsal contents, to improve document memorization and retrieval performance \[[13](http://arxiv.org/abs/2305.15115)\]. Additionally, TOME proposes a two-stage generation architecture using tokenized URLs as identifiers, aiming to bridge the gap between pre-training and fine-tuning \[[16](http://arxiv.org/abs/2305.11161)\]. PAG further enhances generative retrieval by introducing a set-based and sequential identifier design, leveraging both lexical tokens and relevance-based representations for improved performance \[[17](http://arxiv.org/abs/2404.14600)\]. These studies collectively emphasize the importance of docid design in generative retrieval, with semantic alignment, fine-grained representation, and lexical diversity being critical factors for success in dynamic corpora.
