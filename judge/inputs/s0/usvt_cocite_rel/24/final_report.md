## Related Works

Diffusion models have become a cornerstone in generative modeling, particularly for image and video synthesis, due to their ability to produce high-quality outputs through iterative noise estimation \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2302.04304)\]. However, the computational and memory demands of these models hinder their deployment on resource-constrained devices \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/2405.16005)\]. Post-training quantization (PTQ) has emerged as a promising technique to reduce model size and accelerate inference without retraining \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/2305.10657)\]. PTQ methods aim to compress models by reducing the bit-width of weights and activations, often achieving significant efficiency gains \[[4](http://arxiv.org/abs/2211.15736)\]. 

Early PTQ approaches for diffusion models, such as Q-Diffusion, focused on addressing the challenges posed by the multi-timestep pipeline and the bimodal activation distribution of the noise estimation network \[[5](http://arxiv.org/abs/2406.17343)\]. These methods introduced techniques like timestep-aware calibration and split shortcut quantization to maintain performance during quantization \[[5](http://arxiv.org/abs/2406.17343)\]. Similarly, PTQ4DiT was developed to handle the unique challenges of diffusion transformers (DiTs), including salient channels and temporal variability in activations \[[6](http://arxiv.org/abs/2306.02316)\]. This work proposed Channel-wise Salience Balancing (CSB) and Spearmen's $\rho$-guided Salience Calibration (SSC) to improve quantization accuracy \[[6](http://arxiv.org/abs/2306.02316)\]. 

For video generation, the challenges of quantization are further compounded by the need to maintain spatiotemporal coherence across frames \[[7](http://arxiv.org/abs/2310.03270)\]. Existing quantization methods for image generation models often fail to generalize to video tasks, leading to loss of information and misalignment between optimization objectives and video-specific requirements \[[8](http://arxiv.org/abs/2406.02540)\]. To address these issues, recent works such as ViDiT-Q and HQ-DiT have explored tailored quantization schemes for DiT models, achieving W8A8 and W4A8 precision with minimal performance degradation \[[9](http://arxiv.org/abs/2112.10752)\]. These methods focus on dynamic quantization intervals and efficient calibration strategies to preserve visual quality \[[9](http://arxiv.org/abs/2112.10752)\]. 

The Q-VDiT framework proposed in this work builds upon these advancements by introducing Token-aware Quantization Estimator (TQE) and Temporal Maintenance Distillation (TMD) to specifically address the challenges of video generation \[[10](http://arxiv.org/abs/2212.09748)\]. QE compensates for quantization errors in both token and feature dimensions, while TMD preserves spatiotemporal correlations between frames \[[10](http://arxiv.org/abs/2212.09748)\]. These innovations enable the framework to achieve a scene consistency of 23.40, outperforming current state-of-the-art quantization methods by 1.9$\times$ \[[10](http://arxiv.org/abs/2212.09748)\].

### <a id='category-post-training-quantization-techniques'></a>Post-Training Quantization Techniques: 16 papers found.
Post-training quantization (PTQ) has emerged as a critical technique for accelerating diffusion models while maintaining generation quality, particularly in resource-constrained environments \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2302.04304)\]. Traditional PTQ methods, however, face significant challenges when applied to diffusion models due to the multi-timestep pipeline and the bimodal activation distribution within the noise estimation network \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/2305.10657)\]. To address these issues, researchers have proposed specialized PTQ approaches tailored for diffusion models, such as timestep-aware calibration and split shortcut quantization \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2302.04304)\], which enable 4-bit quantization with minimal performance degradation \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/2305.10657)\]. Additionally, mixed-precision schemes and unified formulations for quantization noise have been introduced to mitigate the accumulation of quantization errors during the denoising process \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/2305.10657)\]. These methods have demonstrated promising results, achieving high-quality generation in both unconditional and text-guided settings \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2302.04304)\]. Furthermore, data-free and parameter-efficient fine-tuning frameworks, such as EfficientDM, have been developed to achieve QAT-level performance with PTQ-like efficiency, enabling 4-bit quantization with only a minor increase in FID score \[[7](http://arxiv.org/abs/2310.03270)\]. The success of these methods highlights the potential of PTQ in enabling efficient deployment of diffusion models on edge devices and in real-world applications \[[14](http://arxiv.org/abs/2111.13824)\].

### <a id='category-quantization-methods-for-diffusion-transformers'></a>Quantization Methods for Diffusion Transformers: 3 papers found.
Diffusion Transformers (DiTs) have emerged as a powerful architecture for image and video generation, offering scalability and quality improvements over traditional U-Net-based models \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/2405.16005)\]. However, their deployment on edge devices is hindered by high computational and memory demands, prompting research into efficient quantization techniques \[[5](http://arxiv.org/abs/2406.17343)\]. Post-Training Quantization (PTQ) has been explored as a viable solution, enabling model compression without retraining, but existing methods face challenges when applied to DiTs due to their unique architectural properties \[[8](http://arxiv.org/abs/2406.02540)\]. Specifically, DiTs exhibit spatial and temporal variance in activations and weights, leading to biased quantization and performance degradation \[[5](http://arxiv.org/abs/2406.17343)\]. To address these issues, several studies have proposed tailored quantization approaches, such as PTQ4DiT, which introduces Channel-wise Salience Balancing (CSB) and Spearmen's ρ-guided Salience Calibration (SSC) to mitigate quantization errors \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/2405.16005)\]. Similarly, Q-DiT integrates automatic quantization granularity allocation and sample-wise dynamic activation quantization to adapt to the variance in DiT models \[[5](http://arxiv.org/abs/2406.17343)\]. Additionally, ViDiT-Q is designed for text-to-image and video tasks, achieving W8A8 and W4A8 quantization with minimal quality loss \[[8](http://arxiv.org/abs/2406.02540)\]. These methods highlight the growing interest in developing quantization strategies specifically for DiTs, aiming to balance efficiency with the preservation of generation quality \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/2405.16005)\]\[[5](http://arxiv.org/abs/2406.17343)\]\[[8](http://arxiv.org/abs/2406.02540)\].
