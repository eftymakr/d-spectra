## Related Works

Large Language Models (LLMs) have been extensively studied for their ability to encode complex behaviors within their activation spaces, including refusal mechanisms. Prior research has explored how LLMs can be fine-tuned to align with specific safety and ethical guidelines, often relying on predefined templates or manual analysis to detect refusal behavior \[[8](http://arxiv.org/abs/2406.11717)\]. However, these approaches are limited by their dependence on model outputs and assumptions about the presence of specific refusal tokens \[[8](http://arxiv.org/abs/2406.11717)\]. 

Recent work has focused on improving the alignment of LLMs with human values through instruction tuning and reinforcement learning. For instance, Toolformer demonstrates that LLMs can be trained to use external tools via simple APIs, achieving competitive performance across a variety of downstream tasks without sacrificing core language modeling abilities \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/2302.04761)\]. This aligns with broader efforts to enhance LLMs' ability to perform complex reasoning and task execution, such as Multimodal-CoT, which integrates language and vision modalities to improve reasoning accuracy \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/2302.00923)\].

In the context of visual instruction tuning, LLaVA and Improved Baselines with Visual Instruction Tuning have shown that multimodal models can be effectively trained using visual instruction datasets, achieving strong performance on benchmarks such as ScienceQA and A-OKVQA \[[12](http://arxiv.org/abs/2304.08485)\]. These models leverage pre-trained vision and language encoders to generate responses grounded in visual and auditory information, demonstrating the potential of multimodal instruction tuning \[[4](http://arxiv.org/abs/2310.03744)\].

The challenge of identifying and steering refusal behaviors in LLMs has also been addressed through architectural innovations. For example, Flamingo enables few-shot learning by bridging pretrained vision-only and language-only models, allowing models to be rapidly adapted to novel tasks using only a handful of annotated examples \[[13](http://arxiv.org/abs/2204.14198)\]. Similarly, ReAct synergizes reasoning and acting in LLMs by generating both reasoning traces and task-specific actions in an interleaved manner, improving interpretability and trustworthiness \[[14](http://arxiv.org/abs/2210.03629)\].

COSMIC introduces an automated framework for direction selection that identifies viable steering directions and target layers using cosine similarity, independent of model outputs. This approach is particularly effective in adversarial settings and weakly aligned models, demonstrating robustness across a wide range of alignment conditions \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2303.18223)\]. By avoiding assumptions about the presence of specific refusal tokens, COSMIC provides a more general and reliable method for steering LLMs toward safer behavior \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2303.18223)\].

The category "Reasoning and Action Integration in LLMs" focuses on enhancing the ability of large language models to perform complex reasoning tasks and interact with external environments through action execution. Recent studies have explored methods to improve reasoning capabilities, such as chain-of-thought (CoT) prompting, which generates intermediate reasoning steps to guide model inference \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/2302.00923)\]. Additionally, multimodal approaches like Multimodal-CoT integrate text and visual information to enhance the quality of generated rationales and improve performance on tasks such as ScienceQA \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/2302.00923)\]. Furthermore, models like Toolformer demonstrate the potential of LLMs to use external tools via APIs, enabling them to perform tasks requiring arithmetic or factual lookup by leveraging simple demonstrations \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/2302.04761)\]. Another approach, ReAct, combines reasoning and action generation in an interleaved manner, allowing models to generate reasoning traces and task-specific actions while interacting with external sources like knowledge bases or environments \[[14](http://arxiv.org/abs/2210.03629)\]. These methods aim to improve the reliability, interpretability, and effectiveness of LLMs in real-world applications by integrating reasoning with actionable outputs.

### <a id='category-false-refusal-minimization-strategies'></a>False Refusal Minimization Strategies: 1 papers found.
Context: 
	Document 1: [Snippet]: «Conversational large language models are fine-tuned for both instruction-following and safety, resulting in models that obey benign requests but refuse harmful ones. While this refusal behavior is widespread across chat models, its underlying mechanisms remain poorly understood. In this work, we show that refusal is mediated by a one-dimensional subspace, across 13 popular open-source chat models up to 72B parameters in size. Specifically, for each model, we find a single direction such that erasing this direction from the model's residual stream activations prevents it from refusing harmful instructions, while adding this direction elicits refusal on even harmless instructions. Leveraging this insight, we propose a novel white-box jailbreak method that surgically disables refusal with minimal effect on other capabilities. Finally, we mechanistically analyze how adversarial suffixes suppress propagation of the refusal-mediating direction. Our findings underscore the brittleness of current safety fine-tuning methods. More broadly, our work showcases how an understanding of model internals can be leveraged to develop practical methods for controlling model behavior.»
