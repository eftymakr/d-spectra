## Related Works

Cross-domain Sequential Recommendation (CDSR) has gained significant attention in recent years due to its ability to alleviate data sparsity and cold-start problems by leveraging user behavior across multiple domains \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2304.03891)\]. Traditional approaches often rely on single-domain user preferences and then attempt to transfer knowledge to the target domain \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/2106.02768)\]. However, these methods typically ignore inter-sequence item relationships and suffer from the overlap dilemma, where they heavily depend on users with interactions across all domains, limiting their practicality \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/2105.03300)\]. Moreover, modeling complex transition patterns from mixed behavior sequences remains a challenge \[[4](http://arxiv.org/abs/2305.19860)\].

Several studies have explored the use of graph neural networks (GNNs) and attention mechanisms to better capture cross-domain interactions. For instance, DA-GCN introduces a domain-aware graph convolution network to learn user-specific node representations and incorporates attention mechanisms to guide message passing \[[5](http://arxiv.org/abs/2012.00485)\]. Similarly, C2DSR proposes a framework that simultaneously leverages intra- and inter-sequence item relationships to capture both single- and cross-domain user preferences \[[6](http://arxiv.org/abs/2103.01696)\]. The model employs a graph neural network to mine inter-sequence collaborative relationships and a sequential attentive encoder to capture intra-sequence patterns \[[6](http://arxiv.org/abs/2103.01696)\]. Additionally, the paper introduces a contrastive cross-domain infomax objective to enhance the correlation between single- and cross-domain user representations \[[6](http://arxiv.org/abs/2103.01696)\].

Other works have focused on dual learning mechanisms to improve cross-domain sequential recommendations. For example, DASL employs a dual learning framework that iteratively transfers information between two domains to enhance CTR prediction performance \[[7](http://arxiv.org/abs/2304.05027)\]. The model includes dual embedding and dual attention components that jointly establish a two-stage learning process \[[7](http://arxiv.org/abs/2304.05027)\]. Similarly, the mixed information flow network considers both behavioral and knowledge transfer to enrich sequence representations based on user preferences \[[8](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/96bfe33a2503e960153a934e10be91da4d8b13a6)\]. These models aim to address the limitations of traditional approaches by incorporating more comprehensive information flow mechanisms \[[8](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/96bfe33a2503e960153a934e10be91da4d8b13a6)\].

Recent advancements have also explored the use of large language models (LLMs) to enhance CDSR. LLMs are capable of capturing semantic relationships and reasoning about user preferences, making them promising for addressing the overlap dilemma and transition complexity \[[9](http://arxiv.org/abs/1804.06769)\]. For instance, BERT4Rec introduces a bidirectional encoder to model user behavior sequences, allowing for more accurate recommendations by capturing both left and right context \[[10](http://arxiv.org/abs/2311.04590)\]. Similarly, UniSRec proposes a universal sequence representation learning approach that leverages item descriptions to learn transferable representations across different recommendation scenarios \[[11](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/d1c7b8be5f3849a3fd4af780b58eb28355641206)\]. These models demonstrate the potential of LLMs in improving the generalization and performance of cross-domain recommendation systems \[[11](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/d1c7b8be5f3849a3fd4af780b58eb28355641206)\].

	Document 1: [Snippet]: «Modeling users' dynamic preferences from their historical behaviors is challenging and crucial for recommendation systems. Previous methods employ sequential neural networks to encode users' historical interactions from left to right into hidden representations for making recommendations. Despite their effectiveness, we argue that such left-to-right unidirectional models are sub-optimal due to the limitations including: \item unidirectional architectures restrict the power of hidden representation in users' behavior sequences; \item they often assume a rigidly ordered sequence which is not always practical. \end enumerate* To address these limitations, we proposed a sequential recommendation model called BERT4Rec, which employs the deep bidirectional self-attention to model user behavior sequences. To avoid the information leakage and efficiently train the bidirectional model, we adopt the Cloze objective to sequential recommendation, predicting the random masked items in the sequence by jointly conditioning on their left and right context. In this way, we learn a bidirectional representation model to make recommendations by allowing each item in user historical behaviors to fuse information from both left and right sides. Extensive experiments on four benchmark datasets show that our model outperforms various state-of-the-art sequential models consistently.»
