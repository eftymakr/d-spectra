## Related Works

Video super-resolution (VSR) has been a focal point in computer vision, with recent advances leveraging deep learning to address the challenges of temporal consistency and motion estimation \[[1](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/c6a8ff4d9dcdf9e5d82310b847af999ed6655f65)\]. Early methods often relied on explicit motion compensation, which is computationally expensive and prone to errors \[[1](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/c6a8ff4d9dcdf9e5d82310b847af999ed6655f65)\]. For instance, the Deep Video Super-Resolution Network Using Dynamic Upsampling Filters Without Explicit Motion Compensation introduced an end-to-end framework that generates dynamic upsampling filters and residual images, avoiding the need for explicit motion compensation \[[1](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/c6a8ff4d9dcdf9e5d82310b847af999ed6655f65)\]. This approach significantly improves the sharpness and temporal consistency of HR videos \[[1](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/c6a8ff4d9dcdf9e5d82310b847af999ed6655f65)\].

Several studies have explored spatio-temporal networks and motion compensation to enhance VSR performance. Real-Time Video Super-Resolution with Spatio-Temporal Networks and Motion Compensation proposed a novel joint motion compensation and video super-resolution algorithm that is more efficient than competing methods \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/1611.05250)\]. This method uses a fast multi-resolution spatial transformer module, enabling real-time processing while maintaining high accuracy \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/1611.05250)\]. Similarly, the Frame-Recurrent Video Super-Resolution framework introduced a recurrent structure that uses previously inferred HR estimates to super-resolve subsequent frames, naturally encouraging temporally consistent results \[[4](http://arxiv.org/abs/1801.04590)\]. This approach reduces computational cost by warping only one image per step and allows assimilation of a large number of previous frames without increased computational demands \[[4](http://arxiv.org/abs/1801.04590)\].

Temporal alignment remains a critical challenge in VSR, particularly when dealing with varying motion between frames. The Temporally-Deformable Alignment Network (TDAN) addresses this by aligning reference and supporting frames at the feature level without computing optical flow \[[5](http://arxiv.org/abs/1812.02898)\]. This method dynamically predicts offsets of sampling convolution kernels to transform supporting frames, improving alignment accuracy and reducing artifacts \[[5](http://arxiv.org/abs/1812.02898)\]. Similarly, the Enhanced Deformable Convolutional Networks (EDVR) tackle large motion alignment by using a Pyramid, Cascading, and Deformable (PCD) alignment module that operates at the feature level in a coarse-to-fine manner \[[6](http://arxiv.org/abs/1905.02716)\]. This module enables effective alignment of multiple frames with diverse motion and blur \[[6](http://arxiv.org/abs/1905.02716)\].

### <a id='category-video-super-resolution-techniques'></a>Video Super-Resolution Techniques: 18 papers found.
Video super-resolution (VSR) has seen significant advancements with the integration of deep learning techniques, particularly focusing on improving temporal consistency and computational efficiency. Early methods relied heavily on motion estimation and compensation, which often introduced artifacts and increased computational costs \[[1](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/c6a8ff4d9dcdf9e5d82310b847af999ed6655f65)\]. More recent approaches, such as spatio-temporal sub-pixel convolution networks, have effectively exploited temporal redundancies while maintaining real-time performance through early fusion, slow fusion, and 3D convolutions \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/1611.05250)\]. These methods also introduced joint motion compensation and video super-resolution algorithms that significantly improved efficiency and accuracy \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/1611.05250)\]. Another notable contribution is the Video Restoration Transformer (VRT), which utilizes parallel frame prediction and temporal reciprocal self attention (TRSA) to enhance feature alignment and fusion across multiple frames \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/2201.12288)\]. Similarly, the EDVR framework employs deformable convolutions and attention mechanisms to address large motions and improve fusion of diverse frames \[[6](http://arxiv.org/abs/1905.02716)\]. Recurrent-based methods, such as the proposed BasicVSR, have also gained traction by leveraging recurrent units and hidden state adaptation to enhance robustness and temporal consistency \[[8](http://arxiv.org/abs/2008.00455)\]. Additionally, the MuCAN network introduces a temporal multi-correspondence aggregation strategy and cross-scale nonlocal-correspondence aggregation to leverage self-similarity across frames \[[9](http://arxiv.org/abs/2007.11803)\]. These innovations collectively highlight the ongoing efforts to improve the accuracy, efficiency, and robustness of video super-resolution techniques.

[Snippet]: «Many interesting tasks in image restoration can be cast as linear inverse problems. A recent family of approaches for solving these problems uses stochastic algorithms that sample from the posterior distribution of natural images given the measurements. However, efficient solutions often require problem-specific supervised training to model the posterior, whereas unsupervised methods that are not problem-specific typically rely on inefficient iterative methods. This work addresses these issues by introducing Denoising Diffusion Restoration Models (DDRM), an efficient, unsupervised posterior sampling method. Motivated by variational inference, DDRM takes advantage of a pre-trained denoising diffusion generative model for solving any linear inverse problem. We demonstrate DDRM's versatility on several image datasets for super-resolution, deblurring, inpainting, and colorization under various amounts of measurement noise. DDRM outperforms the current leading unsupervised methods on the diverse ImageNet dataset in reconstruction quality, perceptual quality, and runtime, being 5x faster than the nearest competitor. DDRM also generalizes well for natural images out of the distribution of the observed ImageNet training set.»
