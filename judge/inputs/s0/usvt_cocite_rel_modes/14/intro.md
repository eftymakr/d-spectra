## Related Works

Video super-resolution (VSR) has been a focal point in computer vision, with recent advances leveraging deep learning to address the challenges of temporal consistency and motion estimation \[[1](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/c6a8ff4d9dcdf9e5d82310b847af999ed6655f65)\]. Early methods often relied on explicit motion compensation, which is computationally expensive and prone to errors \[[1](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/c6a8ff4d9dcdf9e5d82310b847af999ed6655f65)\]. For instance, the Deep Video Super-Resolution Network Using Dynamic Upsampling Filters Without Explicit Motion Compensation introduced an end-to-end framework that generates dynamic upsampling filters and residual images, avoiding the need for explicit motion compensation \[[1](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/c6a8ff4d9dcdf9e5d82310b847af999ed6655f65)\]. This approach significantly improves the sharpness and temporal consistency of HR videos \[[1](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/c6a8ff4d9dcdf9e5d82310b847af999ed6655f65)\].

Several studies have explored spatio-temporal networks and motion compensation to enhance VSR performance. Real-Time Video Super-Resolution with Spatio-Temporal Networks and Motion Compensation proposed a novel joint motion compensation and video super-resolution algorithm that is more efficient than competing methods \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/1611.05250)\]. This method uses a fast multi-resolution spatial transformer module, enabling real-time processing while maintaining high accuracy \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/1611.05250)\]. Similarly, the Frame-Recurrent Video Super-Resolution framework introduced a recurrent structure that uses previously inferred HR estimates to super-resolve subsequent frames, naturally encouraging temporally consistent results \[[4](http://arxiv.org/abs/1801.04590)\]. This approach reduces computational cost by warping only one image per step and allows assimilation of a large number of previous frames without increased computational demands \[[4](http://arxiv.org/abs/1801.04590)\].

Temporal alignment remains a critical challenge in VSR, particularly when dealing with varying motion between frames. The Temporally-Deformable Alignment Network (TDAN) addresses this by aligning reference and supporting frames at the feature level without computing optical flow \[[5](http://arxiv.org/abs/1812.02898)\]. This method dynamically predicts offsets of sampling convolution kernels to transform supporting frames, improving alignment accuracy and reducing artifacts \[[5](http://arxiv.org/abs/1812.02898)\]. Similarly, the Enhanced Deformable Convolutional Networks (EDVR) tackle large motion alignment by using a Pyramid, Cascading, and Deformable (PCD) alignment module that operates at the feature level in a coarse-to-fine manner \[[6](http://arxiv.org/abs/1905.02716)\]. This module enables effective alignment of multiple frames with diverse motion and blur \[[6](http://arxiv.org/abs/1905.02716)\].

These enhancements allow the model to exploit spatiotemporal information across misaligned video frames more effectively, leading to improved performance \[[11](http://arxiv.org/abs/2104.13371)\].
