## Related Works

3D human pose estimation has attracted significant research interest due to its applications in robotics, virtual reality, and human-computer interaction. Early approaches primarily relied on 2D pose estimation followed by a mapping to 3D space, often using regression or optimization techniques \[[5](http://arxiv.org/abs/1705.03098)\]. However, these methods suffer from limited accuracy due to depth ambiguity and self-occlusions \[[1](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/50167a9e6bdc1f64a58fe8a3c990105fec0bd2bc)\]. Recent works have focused on leveraging spatial and temporal relationships to improve estimation accuracy \[[1](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/50167a9e6bdc1f64a58fe8a3c990105fec0bd2bc)\]\[[8](http://arxiv.org/abs/1711.08585)\].

Spatial dependencies are crucial for modeling the human body's structure. For instance, \[[1](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/50167a9e6bdc1f64a58fe8a3c990105fec0bd2bc)\] proposed a graph-based method that incorporates domain knowledge about human body configurations into graph convolutional operations. This approach explicitly models spatial relationships and improves 3D pose estimation by capturing local and global interactions. Similarly, \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/1704.00159)\] introduced a structure-aware regression approach that uses bones instead of joints to represent pose, enabling the modeling of long-range interactions through a compositional loss function. This method significantly advances the state-of-the-art on Human3.6M and is competitive with state-of-the-art results on MPII \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/1704.00159)\].

Temporal consistency is another critical factor, especially when estimating 3D poses from video sequences. \[[8](http://arxiv.org/abs/1711.08585)\] utilized a sequence-to-sequence network with LSTM units to enforce temporal smoothness, leading to more coherent 3D pose estimates. Additionally, \[[9](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/30de7fecb146356d7ae6cafa072997674601e0f5)\] proposed an attention-based framework that exploits temporal contexts by adaptively identifying significant frames and tensor outputs, resulting in a 8% error reduction compared to state-of-the-art methods \[[9](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/30de7fecb146356d7ae6cafa072997674601e0f5)\].

Graph-based models have also been widely explored for their ability to model complex human body structures. \[[1](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/50167a9e6bdc1f64a58fe8a3c990105fec0bd2bc)\] introduced a graph convolutional network architecture that processes features across multiple scales, enabling the learning of both local and global representations. \[[14](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/442928cd95d7bca15800b29ce2e2f8db5461bd58)\] further improved upon this by proposing a Locally Connected Network (LCN) that outperforms GCNs in terms of representation power and generalization. This approach is less data hungry and generalizes well to unseen actions and datasets \[[14](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/442928cd95d7bca15800b29ce2e2f8db5461bd58)\].

Transformers have recently gained traction in 3D human pose estimation due to their ability to model long-range dependencies. \[[6](http://arxiv.org/abs/2103.10455)\] introduced PoseFormer, a purely transformer-based approach that models spatial and temporal correlations within and across frames, achieving state-of-the-art results on Human3.6M and MPI-INF-3DHP \[[6](http://arxiv.org/abs/2103.10455)\]. ### <a id='category-3d-human-pose-estimation-methods'></a>3D Human Pose Estimation Methods: 20 papers found.
The related works in 3D human pose estimation focus on addressing challenges such as depth ambiguity, self-occlusion, and temporal inconsistency, often leveraging graph-based models, transformers, and attention mechanisms to capture spatial and temporal dependencies \[[1](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/50167a9e6bdc1f64a58fe8a3c990105fec0bd2bc)\]. Early approaches relied on graph convolutional networks (GCNs) to model joint relationships, but they struggled with long-range dependencies due to limited receptive fields \[[15](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/0646857f867d9a624f2b53d1ffb4de99e225b2d3)\]. To overcome this, high-order GCNs were introduced, aggregating features from nodes at various distances to better capture complex interactions \[[21](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/2170b81eaac4095f5948b7922f29200a25db9b2b)\]. More recently, transformer architectures have been adapted for 3D pose estimation, enabling the modeling of both spatial and temporal correlations through self-attention mechanisms \[[6](http://arxiv.org/abs/2103.10455)\]. For instance, PoseFormer uses a spatial-temporal transformer to capture joint relations within frames and across time, achieving state-of-the-art results on benchmark datasets \[[6](http://arxiv.org/abs/2103.10455)\]. Similarly, MHFormer extends this idea by learning multiple plausible pose hypotheses and synthesizing the most accurate 3D pose through cross-hypothesis communication \[[12](http://arxiv.org/abs/2111.12707)\]. Attention-based frameworks have also been proposed to improve temporal coherence, such as the attentional mechanism in Attention3DHumanPose, which adaptively identifies significant frames and tensor outputs to enhance estimation accuracy \[[9](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/30de7fecb146356d7ae6cafa072997674601e0f5)\]. Additionally, methods like the U-shaped GCN incorporate motion loss to capture both short-term and long-term motion information, leading to smoother and more accurate 3D sequences \[[16](http://arxiv.org/abs/2004.13985)\]. These approaches collectively highlight the importance of modeling both local and global relationships, as well as temporal consistency, to achieve robust 3D human pose estimation from 2D inputs.

Others have explored the use of ordinal depth annotations to reduce the reliance on accurate 3D ground truth, demonstrating that Convolutional Networks (ConvNets) trained with such weaker supervision signals can achieve competitive performance \[[17](http://arxiv.org/abs/1805.04095)\]. These approaches collectively highlight the ongoing efforts to enhance the robustness and efficiency of 3D human pose estimation models.
