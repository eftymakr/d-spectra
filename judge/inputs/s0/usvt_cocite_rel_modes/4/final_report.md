## Related Works

Label noise is a pervasive issue in deep learning, affecting the performance of models trained on imperfectly annotated data. Several approaches have been proposed to address this challenge. Early work focused on modifying network architectures or estimating true labels from noisy ones \[[10](http://arxiv.org/abs/1712.09482)\]. For instance, \[[10](http://arxiv.org/abs/1712.09482)\] proposed loss functions that are inherently robust to label noise, showing that mean absolute error (MAE) can be used for risk minimization under noisy labels. This approach avoids the need for explicit label correction, relying instead on the robustness of the loss function itself. However, these methods often assume uniform noise distributions and may not generalize well to complex, real-world scenarios \[[15](http://arxiv.org/abs/1805.07836)\].

More recently, researchers have explored the use of semi-supervised learning to improve robustness against noisy labels. For example, \[[7](http://arxiv.org/abs/2002.07394)\] introduced DivideMix, a framework that leverages semi-supervised techniques to dynamically separate clean and noisy samples during training. This approach models the per-sample loss distribution using a mixture model, allowing the model to learn from both labeled and unlabeled data in a semi-supervised manner. Similarly, \[[11](http://arxiv.org/abs/1901.04215)\] proposed Co-teaching+, which builds upon the Co-teaching framework by incorporating disagreement-based learning to avoid confirmation bias. This method trains two diverged networks, where each network uses the dataset division from the other to improve robustness against noisy labels.

Another line of research focuses on loss correction and noise estimation. \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/1609.03683)\] introduced a loss correction approach that accounts for class-dependent label noise, proposing two procedures that amount to matrix inversion and multiplication, provided the probability of each class being corrupted is known. They also developed a method to estimate these probabilities, enabling an end-to-end framework for training robust models. This work was later extended in \[[14](http://arxiv.org/abs/2012.11854)\], which addressed instance-dependent label noise by leveraging second-order statistics to estimate noise rates and adjust the loss function accordingly. This approach allows for more accurate modeling of real-world label noise, which often depends on the difficulty of the task.

In addition to loss-based methods, some works have focused on reweighting examples to improve robustness. \[[12](http://arxiv.org/abs/1803.09050)\] proposed a meta-learning algorithm that learns to assign weights to training examples based on their gradient directions, without requiring additional hyperparameter tuning. This method can be applied to any deep network and has shown effectiveness in handling class imbalance and corrupted labels. ### <a id='category-label-noise-robust-deep-learning'></a>Label Noise Robust Deep Learning: 17 papers found.
The category "Label Noise Robust Deep Learning" encompasses a range of approaches aimed at improving the performance of deep neural networks in the presence of noisy labels. These methods address the challenge of training accurate models when a significant portion of the training data contains incorrect or corrupted labels. Several studies propose techniques to mitigate the impact of label noise, such as loss correction \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/1609.03683)\], joint neural embedding networks \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/1711.07131)\], and semi-supervised learning frameworks \[[7](http://arxiv.org/abs/2002.07394)\]. For instance, some works focus on estimating the probability of class-dependent label noise and correcting the loss accordingly \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/1609.03683)\], while others introduce novel loss functions that are inherently robust to label noise \[[10](http://arxiv.org/abs/1712.09482)\]. Additionally, methods like Co-teaching \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/1804.06872)\] and DivideMix \[[7](http://arxiv.org/abs/2002.07394)\] leverage the idea of training multiple models to collaboratively filter out noisy samples. Another line of research explores the use of graphical models and auxiliary information to infer clean labels from noisy ones \[[4](http://arxiv.org/abs/1706.00038)\]. Furthermore, some studies emphasize the importance of understanding the learning dynamics of deep networks under label noise, such as the memorization effects and the distinction between clean and noisy label learning \[[5](http://arxiv.org/abs/1806.02612)\]. These approaches highlight the diversity of strategies for combating label noise, ranging from modifying loss functions to leveraging semi-supervised learning and domain-specific knowledge \[[6](http://arxiv.org/abs/1703.02391)\]. Overall, the field is driven by the need for robust and scalable solutions that can effectively handle label noise in real-world applications.
