## Related Works

Vision Transformers (ViTs) have emerged as a powerful alternative to convolutional neural networks (CNNs) in computer vision tasks, leveraging the transformer architecture to effectively capture global context \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/2010.11929)\]. However, the deployment of ViTs on hardware platforms, especially FPGAs, remains challenging due to the complex arithmetic operations such as Softmax and LayerNorm, which are sensitive to model accuracy and difficult to map onto efficient hardware with low precision \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2208.05163)\]. Prior work has primarily focused on optimizing matrix multiplication operations on hardware while keeping these complex operations on the CPU, resulting in suboptimal performance due to communication overhead between the CPU and accelerator \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2208.05163)\].

Several studies have explored hardware acceleration for ViTs. Auto-ViT-Acc proposes an FPGA-aware acceleration framework with mixed-scheme quantization, achieving higher accuracy than state-of-the-art quantization methods while improving frame rates \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2208.05163)\]. ViTCoD introduces a co-design framework that integrates algorithmic pruning and hardware acceleration, achieving significant speedups by reducing dominant data movement costs \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/2210.09573)\]. I-ViT enables integer-only inference for ViTs by approximating non-linear operations with integer arithmetic, achieving comparable accuracy to full-precision models \[[4](http://arxiv.org/abs/2207.01405)\]. FQ-ViT addresses the challenges of post-training quantization for ViTs, reducing inference complexity while maintaining high accuracy \[[7](http://arxiv.org/abs/2111.13824)\]. Patch Slimming reduces computational costs by discarding redundant patches in a top-down manner, achieving over 45% FLOPs reduction with minimal accuracy loss \[[8](http://arxiv.org/abs/2106.02852)\]. HeatViT introduces hardware-efficient token pruning, achieving higher accuracy and significant computation reduction on various ViT models \[[9](http://arxiv.org/abs/2211.08110)\]. VAQF proposes a fully automatic framework for low-bit ViT acceleration on FPGAs, achieving real-time execution with minimal compilation time \[[10](http://arxiv.org/abs/2201.06618)\].

Efforts to improve ViT efficiency have also focused on architectural innovations. T2T-ViT improves training efficiency by incorporating a Tokens-to-Token transformation, reducing parameter count and MACs while improving accuracy \[[5](http://arxiv.org/abs/2101.11986)\]. LeViT combines principles from CNNs and transformers to achieve faster inference, outperforming existing convnets and vision transformers in the speed/accuracy tradeoff \[[6](http://arxiv.org/abs/2104.01136)\]. CvT introduces convolutions into ViTs to improve performance and efficiency, achieving state-of-the-art results with fewer parameters and lower FLOPs \[[12](http://arxiv.org/abs/2103.15808)\]. DynamicViT proposes a dynamic token sparsification framework, reducing FLOPs and improving throughput with minimal accuracy loss \[[13](http://arxiv.org/abs/2106.02034)\]. Swin Transformer introduces a hierarchical architecture with shifted windows, enabling efficient and effective dense prediction tasks \[[15](http://arxiv.org/abs/2103.14030)\].

### <a id='category-vision-transformer-architectures-and-optimizations'></a>Vision Transformer Architectures and Optimizations: 9 papers found.
Vision Transformer (ViT) architectures have gained significant attention for their ability to capture global context in computer vision tasks, achieving state-of-the-art performance \[[14](http://arxiv.org/abs/2103.00112)\]. However, their deployment on resource-constrained hardware remains challenging due to their high computational and memory demands \[[5](http://arxiv.org/abs/2101.11986)\]. Several works have focused on optimizing ViTs for efficient inference, particularly through quantization and hardware acceleration. For instance, I-ViT introduces an integer-only quantization scheme that enables ViTs to perform inference using integer arithmetic and bit-shifting, achieving comparable accuracy to full-precision models while improving inference speed \[[4](http://arxiv.org/abs/2207.01405)\]. Similarly, HeatViT proposes a hardware-efficient token pruning framework that dynamically identifies and consolidates non-informative tokens, reducing computation by up to 65.3% while maintaining high accuracy \[[9](http://arxiv.org/abs/2211.08110)\]. On the hardware front, an FPGA-aware acceleration framework with mixed-scheme quantization has been proposed, achieving up to 5.6x speedup over 32-bit floating-point baselines with minimal accuracy loss \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2208.05163)\]. Additionally, ViTCoD introduces an algorithm and accelerator co-design framework that prunes and polarizes attention maps, reducing dominant data movement costs and achieving speedups of up to 235.3× over general computing platforms \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/2210.09573)\]. These approaches highlight the ongoing efforts to address the computational bottlenecks of ViTs, particularly in complex operations like Softmax and LayerNorm, which are critical for maintaining model accuracy \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2208.05163)\].

### <a id='category-vision-transformer-architectures'></a>Vision Transformer Architectures: 5 papers found.
Vision Transformer (ViT) architectures have emerged as a promising alternative to traditional convolutional neural networks (CNNs) in computer vision, offering competitive performance while reducing computational demands \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/2010.11929)\]. Early works demonstrated that ViTs, when pre-trained on large datasets, could achieve excellent results on image classification tasks, often outperforming CNNs with fewer resources \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/2010.11929)\]. However, the high computational overhead of ViTs, particularly due to the use of multi-head self-attention modules, has posed challenges for efficient deployment \[[16](http://arxiv.org/abs/2203.08243)\]. To address this, recent research has focused on compression techniques such as pruning, layer skipping, and knowledge distillation, aiming to reduce model size and computational cost without significant accuracy loss \[[16](http://arxiv.org/abs/2203.08243)\]. Additionally, dynamic token sparsification frameworks have been introduced to progressively prune redundant tokens based on their importance, achieving substantial reductions in FLOPs and improved throughput while maintaining acceptable accuracy \[[13](http://arxiv.org/abs/2106.02034)\]. Furthermore, the Swin Transformer introduced a hierarchical architecture with shifted windows, enabling efficient modeling at multiple scales and demonstrating superior performance in dense prediction tasks \[[15](http://arxiv.org/abs/2103.14030)\]. These advancements highlight the ongoing efforts to optimize ViT efficiency, both in training and inference, while preserving their strong performance in vision tasks.

### <a id='category-others'></a>Others: 4 papers found.
Efforts to improve the efficiency of Vision Transformers (ViTs) have focused on reducing computational costs and enhancing inference speed without compromising accuracy. Several approaches have been proposed to address these challenges, including hybrid architectures that combine convolutional and transformer components, such as LeViT, which leverages attention-based mechanisms and activation maps with decreasing resolutions to achieve a favorable speed/accuracy trade-off \[[6](http://arxiv.org/abs/2104.01136)\]. Additionally, quantization techniques have been explored to reduce model complexity, with methods like Power-of-Two Factor (PTF) aiming to mitigate performance degradation in fully quantized ViTs by addressing inter-channel variation in LayerNorm inputs \[[7](http://arxiv.org/abs/2111.13824)\]. Similarly, a patch slimming approach has been introduced to remove redundant computations in ViTs by identifying and discarding ineffective patches, resulting in significant reductions in computational costs while maintaining performance \[[8](http://arxiv.org/abs/2106.02852)\]. Furthermore, hardware-specific optimizations, such as the use of FPGA platforms for ViT acceleration, have been proposed to improve throughput and reduce communication overhead between the CPU and accelerator \[[10](http://arxiv.org/abs/2201.06618)\]. These works collectively highlight the ongoing efforts to enhance the efficiency and practical deployment of ViTs through architectural innovations, quantization strategies, and hardware-specific optimizations.
