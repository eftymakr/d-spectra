## Related Works

Open-vocabulary object detection has emerged as a critical research direction, enabling detectors to recognize objects beyond the training vocabulary using natural language inputs \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2011.10678)\]. This capability is particularly valuable for real-world applications where the set of target objects is not known in advance \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2011.10678)\]. However, the effectiveness of such models is often limited by the quality and specificity of the user-defined vocabulary \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2011.10678)\]. Prior work has explored various strategies to enhance open-vocabulary detection, including leveraging vision-language pre-training \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2011.10678)\], using pseudo labels generated from image-caption pairs \[[10](http://arxiv.org/abs/2111.09452)\], and employing weakly supervised learning \[[23](http://arxiv.org/abs/1511.02853)\]. 

Several approaches have focused on improving the alignment between visual and textual representations. For instance, CLIP \[[22](http://arxiv.org/abs/2102.05918)\] and RegionCLIP \[[18](http://arxiv.org/abs/2112.09106)\] have demonstrated that pre-trained vision-language models can be adapted for object detection by aligning image regions with textual descriptions. These models achieve strong performance on zero-shot and open-vocabulary tasks, often outperforming traditional supervised methods \[[18](http://arxiv.org/abs/2112.09106)\]. Similarly, ViLD \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/2104.13921)\] and MDETR \[[11](http://arxiv.org/abs/2104.12763)\] have introduced methods that use vision-language knowledge distillation to train detectors that can detect objects described by arbitrary text inputs. ViLD, in particular, achieves significant improvements on benchmark datasets such as LVIS and COCO, even when trained with a strong teacher model like ALIGN \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/2104.13921)\].

Another line of research has focused on improving the scalability and efficiency of open-vocabulary detection. Detic \[[7](http://arxiv.org/abs/2201.02605)\] expands the vocabulary of detectors by training on image-level supervision, allowing them to detect tens of thousands of object categories without requiring extensive bounding box annotations. This approach simplifies the training process and improves performance on long-tail detection benchmarks \[[7](http://arxiv.org/abs/2201.02605)\]. Similarly, PromptDet \[[8](http://arxiv.org/abs/2203.16513)\] proposes a two-stage open-vocabulary object detector that uses regional prompt learning to align visual and textual features, enabling zero manual annotations \[[8](http://arxiv.org/abs/2203.16513)\]. The method is validated on challenging datasets like LVIS and MS-COCO, demonstrating superior performance over existing approaches \[[8](http://arxiv.org/abs/2203.16513)\].

Recent work has also explored the use of pseudo-labeling and self-training frameworks to improve open-vocabulary detection. For example, PB-OVD \[[10](http://arxiv.org/abs/2111.09452)\] generates pseudo bounding-box annotations from large-scale image-caption pairs, enabling detectors to be trained on a broader set of object categories. This approach significantly improves performance on benchmarks such as COCO and LVIS, outperforming state-of-the-art methods by a notable margin \[[10](http://arxiv.org/abs/2111.09452)\].
