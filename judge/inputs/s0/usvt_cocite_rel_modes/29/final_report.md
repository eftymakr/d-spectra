## Related Works

Maximum Inner Product Search (MIPS) has been a focal point in machine learning and information retrieval, particularly for high-dimensional data. Existing approaches to MIPS can be broadly categorized into two paradigms: those that rely solely on inner product (IP) similarity and those that reduce the problem to nearest neighbor search under the Euclidean metric via space projection. The former often faces challenges such as local optima and redundant computations \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/1405.5869)\]. The latter, while effective, can lead to topology destruction and information loss due to the inherent differences between IP and Euclidean metrics \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/1909.13459)\]. However, recent studies argue that there is no inherent binary opposition between these two metrics \[[3](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/25454f456c514219c9d6eeb42200ebbf378db9ca)\]. Instead, integrating both in the design of indexing and search algorithms can significantly enhance MIPS performance \[[4](http://arxiv.org/abs/1610.03317)\].

Several works have explored hashing-based methods for approximate MIPS. Asymmetric LSH (ALSH) was introduced as the first provably sublinear time algorithm for approximate MIPS, leveraging asymmetric transformations to convert the problem into approximate near neighbor search \[[5](http://arxiv.org/abs/1202.6101)\]. This approach was later improved with the introduction of Norm-Ranging LSH, which addresses the issue of long-tailed 2-norm distributions by partitioning the dataset into sub-datasets and building hash indexes independently \[[9](http://arxiv.org/abs/1809.08782)\]. Similarly, the SIMPLE-LSH method was proposed, but it was found to suffer from performance degradation in the presence of long-tailed data distributions \[[9](http://arxiv.org/abs/1809.08782)\]. These findings highlight the importance of addressing data distribution characteristics in hashing-based approaches \[[9](http://arxiv.org/abs/1809.08782)\].

Graph-based methods have also gained traction for efficient MIPS. The ip-NSW graph, an inner-product navigable small world graph, has achieved an order of magnitude speedup over existing baselines by leveraging the norm bias of the MIPS problem \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/1909.13459)\]. This bias implies that large norm items are more likely to be the result of MIPS, and ip-NSW exploits this by assigning higher in-degrees to such items \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/1909.13459)\]. The ip-NSW+ algorithm further improves performance by introducing an angular proximity graph to refine the search process \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/1909.13459)\]. Another graph-based method, the norm adjusted proximity graph (NAPG), was proposed to improve the selection of meaningful data points during graph construction \[[3](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/25454f456c514219c9d6eeb42200ebbf378db9ca)\]. These methods demonstrate the potential of graph-based indexing for MIPS, especially when tailored to data-specific characteristics \[[3](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/25454f456c514219c9d6eeb42200ebbf378db9ca)\].

Efforts have also been made to develop lightweight and efficient indexing strategies for large-scale MIPS. The ProMIPS method, for instance, uses a lightweight iDistance index and 2-stable random projections to achieve c-approximate MIPS

### <a id='category-maximum-inner-product-search-techniques'></a>Maximum Inner Product Search Techniques: 13 papers found.
The Maximum Inner Product Search (MIPS) problem has been extensively studied due to its critical role in recommendation systems, information retrieval, and high-dimensional similarity search \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/1405.5869)\]. Early approaches focused on hashing schemes, such as Locality Sensitive Hashing (LSH), but these were found insufficient for MIPS due to the non-metric nature of inner product \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/1405.5869)\]. Subsequent work introduced asymmetric hashing schemes, which transform inner products into near neighbor search problems, enabling sublinear time algorithms \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/1405.5869)\]. More recently, graph-based methods have gained traction, offering empirical advantages over hash-based and quantization-based approaches \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/1909.13459)\]. Techniques like the norm adjusted proximity graph (NAPG) improve graph construction by incorporating adjusting factors from sampled data, leading to more meaningful connections in the graph \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/1909.13459)\]. Additionally, budgeted MIPS algorithms, such as Greedy-MIPS, have been developed to balance search efficiency and quality, achieving significant speedups while maintaining high precision \[[3](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/25454f456c514219c9d6eeb42200ebbf378db9ca)\]. Theoretical advancements, including branch-and-bound and dual-tree algorithms, have also contributed to efficient MIPS by leveraging novel inner-product bounds and data structures like cone trees \[[4](http://arxiv.org/abs/1610.03317)\]. Furthermore, methods such as c-Approximate Maximum Inner Product (c-AMIP) have been proposed to reduce index space and improve efficiency by using lightweight iDistance indexes and random projections \[[6](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/f2171bc79fb72693f60c8e6b0dd547f7d4f850ac)\]. Hardware-efficient approaches, like blocked matrix multiply (BMM) and MAX-IMUS, have demonstrated superior performance in certain scenarios, while data-dependent optimizers like OPTIMUS select the best solver dynamically \[[7](http://arxiv.org/abs/2104.04406)\]. These developments highlight the ongoing evolution of MIPS techniques, addressing challenges such as computational complexity, accuracy, and scalability \[[8](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/0b2790c2c1e23e5535421696e1ec763bbc651bbd)\].

[Snippet]: «The inner-product navigable small world graph (ip-NSW) represents the state-of-the-art method for approximate maximum inner product search (MIPS) and it can achieve an order of magnitude speedup over the fastest baseline. However, to date it is still unclear where its exceptional performance comes from. In this paper, we show that there is a strong norm bias in the MIPS problem, which means that the large norm items are very likely to become the result of MIPS. Then we explain the good performance of ip-NSW as matching the norm bias of the MIPS problem — large norm items have big in-degrees in the ip-NSW proximity graph and a walk on the graph spends the majority of computation on these items, thus effectively avoids unnecessary computation on small norm items. Furthermore, we propose the ip-NSW+ algorithm, which improves ip-NSW by introducing an additional angular proximity graph. Search is first conducted on the angular graph to find the angular neighbors of a query and then the MIPS neighbors of these angular neighbors are used to initialize the candidate pool for search on the inner-product proximity graph. Experiment results show that ip-NSW+ consistently and significantly outperforms ip-NSW and provides more robust performance under different data distributions.»

### <a id='category-approximate-nearest-neighbor-search'></a>Approximate Nearest Neighbor Search: 3 papers found.
The category "Approximate Nearest Neighbor Search" encompasses several approaches aimed at improving efficiency in high-dimensional data retrieval. One method involves product quantization, which decomposes the space into low-dimensional subspaces and quantizes each separately, allowing for efficient distance estimation using short codes \[[12](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/4748d22348e72e6e06c2476486afddbc76e5eca7)\]. This technique has been shown to achieve high search accuracy, outperforming existing state-of-the-art approaches, especially when combined with inverted file systems \[[12](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/4748d22348e72e6e06c2476486afddbc76e5eca7)\]. Another approach leverages GPUs to enhance performance by optimizing algorithms for parallelism, such as $k$-selection, and applying them to brute-force, approximate, and compressed-domain search scenarios \[[16](http://arxiv.org/abs/1702.08734)\]. This GPU-based method achieves significant speedups, with implementations reaching up to 8.5 times faster than prior state-of-the-art solutions \[[16](http://arxiv.org/abs/1702.08734)\]. Additionally, navigable small world (NSW) graphs with controllable hierarchy (Hierarchical NSW, HNSW) offer a fully graph-based solution, enabling efficient and scalable search without reliance on additional structures \[[19](http://arxiv.org/abs/1603.09320)\]. These methods highlight the ongoing efforts to balance accuracy and efficiency in approximate nearest neighbor search, particularly in large-scale applications involving complex data types such as images and videos \[[12](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/4748d22348e72e6e06c2476486afddbc76e5eca7)\]\[[16](http://arxiv.org/abs/1702.08734)\]\[[19](http://arxiv.org/abs/1603.09320)\].
