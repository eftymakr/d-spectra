## Related Works

Brain tumor segmentation from 3D magnetic resonance images (MRIs) has been a focal point in medical image analysis, with numerous deep learning approaches proposed to improve accuracy and efficiency. Early methods relied on traditional image processing techniques, but these were limited in their ability to capture complex spatial relationships within brain tumors \[[13](http://arxiv.org/abs/1709.00382)\]. The advent of convolutional neural networks (CNNs) marked a significant advancement, enabling more accurate and automated segmentation \[[14](http://arxiv.org/abs/2011.11052)\]. The U-Net architecture, introduced by Ronneberger et al., became a cornerstone in biomedical image segmentation due to its symmetric encoder-decoder structure, which effectively captures context and enables precise localization \[[15](http://arxiv.org/abs/2103.04430)\]. This architecture has been widely adopted and modified for brain tumor segmentation tasks, with various improvements addressing issues such as class imbalance and overfitting \[[16](http://arxiv.org/abs/1811.02629)\].

Several studies have focused on enhancing the performance of U-Net-based models for brain tumor segmentation. For instance, the use of data augmentation and loss functions such as the dice loss has been shown to improve segmentation accuracy \[[17](http://arxiv.org/abs/2110.02519)\]. Additionally, the integration of variational auto-encoders (VAEs) into segmentation networks has been proposed to regularize the decoder and improve the quality of the segmented regions \[[18](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/b9c3631120c171570b94f946273b98c32505fc9a)\]. The BraTS challenge has played a crucial role in benchmarking these methods, with various teams achieving notable results \[[19](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/f28e387d4229c5f690ce4570a391c0f47e7155c7)\]. For example, the method proposed in \[[20](http://arxiv.org/abs/2107.02314)\] achieved high Dice scores for whole tumor, tumor core, and enhancing tumor, demonstrating the effectiveness of deep learning approaches in this domain.

The challenge of training deep networks on limited datasets has led to the exploration of transfer learning and pre-trained models. For instance, the use of pre-trained weights from ImageNet has been shown to improve the performance of 3D CNNs in brain tumor segmentation \[[21](http://arxiv.org/abs/1606.06650)\]. This approach has been further refined in recent works, such as the nnU-Net framework, which automatically configures the network for different segmentation tasks \[[22](http://arxiv.org/abs/1807.07716)\]. The integration of axial-coronal-sagittal convolutions and pre-trained weights has been proposed to reduce training time and improve model efficiency \[[23](http://arxiv.org/abs/2110.03352)\]. These strategies have been validated on benchmark datasets such as BraTS, where they demonstrated competitive performance compared to traditional ensemble methods \[[24](http://arxiv.org/abs/1812.11588)\].

In addition to architectural improvements, the use of attention mechanisms and hybrid models has also been explored. ### <a id='category-brain-tumor-segmentation-techniques'></a>Brain Tumor Segmentation Techniques: 14 papers found.
The related works in brain tumor segmentation focus on improving accuracy, efficiency, and adaptability of segmentation models, particularly in the context of 3D MRI data. Early approaches relied on manual delineation, which was time-consuming and error-prone \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/1810.11654)\]. With the advent of deep learning, methods such as semantic segmentation networks based on encoder-decoder architectures have become prevalent, with some achieving top performance in challenges like BraTS 2018 \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/1810.11654)\]. More recent studies have explored the use of nnU-Net, which has shown promising results through modifications in data augmentation, postprocessing, and region-based training, achieving top rankings in BraTS 2020 \[[6](http://arxiv.org/abs/2011.00848)\]. Additionally, there is a growing interest in lightweight and efficient models, such as HDC-Net, which reduces computational overhead by replacing 3D convolutions with a hierarchical decoupled convolution module \[[9](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/9bf1c55d5441b29acf35c2193342723187dbe896)\]. Transfer learning from 2D pre-trained models, such as EfficientNet, has also been investigated to improve performance in 3D segmentation tasks \[[14](http://arxiv.org/abs/2011.11052)\]. Furthermore, the integration of Transformer architectures, which leverage self-attention mechanisms for global feature modeling, has shown potential in capturing both local and global contexts in 3D medical images \[[15](http://arxiv.org/abs/2103.04430)\]. The BraTS challenges have played a crucial role in benchmarking these methods, highlighting the need for robust and adaptable algorithms that can handle the inherent variability of brain tumors \[[16](http://arxiv.org/abs/1811.02629)\]. The use of multi-modal data and the incorporation of clinical knowledge into segmentation models, such as in CKD-TransBTS, further demonstrates the ongoing efforts to enhance both accuracy and interpretability \[[30](http://arxiv.org/abs/2207.07370)\]. These advancements collectively underscore the evolving landscape of brain tumor segmentation, emphasizing the balance between model complexity, computational efficiency, and clinical applicability.

### <a id='category-brain-tumor-segmentation-research'></a>Brain Tumor Segmentation Research: 9 papers found.
The related works in brain tumor segmentation research focus on improving segmentation accuracy and efficiency through advanced neural network architectures and training strategies. Several studies have explored the use of convolutional neural networks (CNNs) tailored for medical imaging, such as the U-Net and its variants, which have become a foundational model for this task \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/1802.10508)\]. For instance, a dual pathway, 11-layer deep 3D CNN was proposed to handle the computational challenges of 3D medical scans, incorporating multi-scale processing and a 3D fully connected CRF for post-processing to reduce false positives \[[4](http://arxiv.org/abs/1603.05959)\]. Additionally, the use of small 3 × 3 kernels in CNNs has been shown to enable deeper architectures and reduce overfitting, with notable success in the BRATS 2013 and BRATS 2015 challenges \[[5](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/420f3f1078a6d8e0696572c032877079286051c6)\]. To address class imbalance and improve performance, some methods employ intensity normalization and data augmentation, further enhancing segmentation accuracy \[[5](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/420f3f1078a6d8e0696572c032877079286051c6)\]. Ensemble approaches, such as combining multiple models trained with different hyper-parameters, have also been used to reduce random errors and improve robustness \[[7](http://arxiv.org/abs/1812.01049)\]. Moreover, recent work has integrated human brain connectomics and parcellation data with deep learning models to enhance both segmentation and survival prediction \[[6](http://arxiv.org/abs/2011.00848)\]. These advancements highlight the ongoing efforts to improve segmentation accuracy, efficiency, and generalization across diverse tumor sub-regions and imaging modalities.
