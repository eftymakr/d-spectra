## Related Works

Unsupervised domain adaptation (UDA) for semantic segmentation has attracted significant research interest due to the high cost of obtaining pixel-level annotations for real-world data \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2004.07703)\]. Traditional UDA methods assume that the category settings between the source and target domains are known, which is often not the case in practical applications \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2004.07703)\]. This assumption leads to performance degradation when private classes exist in the target domain \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2004.07703)\]. Several works have addressed this issue by focusing on domain alignment and pseudo-label refinement \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/1909.13589)\]\[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/2007.08702)\].

A popular approach in UDA is adversarial learning, which aims to align the feature distributions between the source and target domains \[[4](http://arxiv.org/abs/1904.01886)\]. However, these methods often fail to account for category-level discrepancies, leading to misalignment of specific classes \[[5](http://arxiv.org/abs/1904.10620)\]. To address this, \[[6](http://arxiv.org/abs/2004.05498)\] introduced a category-level adversarial network that enforces local semantic consistency during global alignment. This approach improves alignment by adjusting the adversarial loss based on category-level alignment \[[6](http://arxiv.org/abs/2004.05498)\].

Another challenge in UDA is the reliability of pseudo-labels generated from the source domain \[[7](http://arxiv.org/abs/1809.09478)\]. These labels can be noisy due to domain shift, which can negatively impact training \[[7](http://arxiv.org/abs/1809.09478)\]. To mitigate this, \[[8](http://arxiv.org/abs/2003.03773)\] proposed uncertainty-aware consistency regularization, which leverages the latent uncertainty of target samples to improve knowledge transfer from a teacher model to a student model \[[8](http://arxiv.org/abs/2003.03773)\]. This method significantly outperforms existing approaches by improving performance on two benchmarks \[[8](http://arxiv.org/abs/2003.03773)\].

To further enhance pseudo-label quality, \[[9](http://arxiv.org/abs/2008.12197)\] introduced an instance adaptive self-training framework that improves the quality of pseudo-labels through a novel pseudo-label generation strategy and region-guided regularization \[[9](http://arxiv.org/abs/2008.12197)\]. This approach is particularly effective in reducing the domain gap between synthetic and real data \[[9](http://arxiv.org/abs/2008.12197)\].

Additionally, \[[10](http://arxiv.org/abs/1910.13049)\] proposed a category anchor-guided UDA model that explicitly enforces category-aware feature alignment to learn shared discriminative features and classifiers simultaneously \[[10](http://arxiv.org/abs/1910.13049)\]. This method uses category-wise centroids as anchors to guide feature alignment and improve classification accuracy \[[10](http://arxiv.org/abs/1910.13049)\].

Recent work has also explored the use of self-supervised learning to improve domain adaptation \[[11](http://arxiv.org/abs/1802.10349)\]. \[[12](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/3f9f801aec1f87ae81524f1928407673805927cb)\] introduced a method that leverages self-supervised learning to generate pseudo-labels and refine them through uncertainty estimation \[[12](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/3f9f801aec1f87ae81524f1928407673805927cb)\]. ### <a id='category-semantic-segmentation-domain-adaptation'></a>Semantic Segmentation Domain Adaptation: 22 papers found.
The category of semantic segmentation domain adaptation focuses on addressing the challenge of transferring knowledge from a labeled source domain to an unlabeled target domain without requiring explicit annotations in the target domain \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2004.07703)\]. Traditional methods often assume that the category settings between source and target domains are known, which is not always feasible in real-world applications \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2004.07703)\]. This assumption can lead to performance degradation when private classes exist in the target domain \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2004.07703)\]. To overcome these limitations, recent approaches have explored various strategies, such as self-supervised learning, adversarial training, and pseudo-label refinement \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/1909.13589)\]\[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/2007.08702)\]\[[4](http://arxiv.org/abs/1904.01886)\]. For instance, some methods use entropy minimization to balance the gradient of well-classified target samples \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/1909.13589)\], while others propose maximum squares loss to prevent the training process from being dominated by easy-to-transfer samples \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/1909.13589)\]. Additionally, methods like DACS introduce cross-domain mixed sampling to improve pseudo-label quality by combining labeled data with pseudo-labels \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/2007.08702)\]. Another approach, such as the Fourier Transform-based method, simplifies domain alignment by swapping the low-frequency spectrum between domains \[[6](http://arxiv.org/abs/2004.05498)\]. Furthermore, methods like CAG-UDA enforce category-aware feature alignment to learn shared discriminative features and classifiers simultaneously \[[9](http://arxiv.org/abs/2008.12197)\]. These techniques highlight the ongoing efforts to improve the generalization of semantic segmentation models across different domains, especially in synthetic-to-real scenarios \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2004.07703)\]\[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/1909.13589)\]\[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/2007.08702)\]\[[4](http://arxiv.org/abs/1904.01886)\]\[[6](http://arxiv.org/abs/2004.05498)\]\[[9](http://arxiv.org/abs/2008.12197)\].

[Snippet]: «We consider the problem of unsupervised domain adaptation in semantic segmentation. The key in this campaign consists in reducing the domain shift, i.e., enforcing the data distributions of the two domains to be similar. A popular strategy is to align the marginal distribution in the feature space through adversarial learning. However, this global alignment strategy does not consider the local category-level feature distribution. A possible consequence of the global movement is that some categories which are originally well aligned between the source and target may be incorrectly mapped. To address this problem, this paper introduces a category-level adversarial network, aiming to enforce local semantic consistency during the trend of global alignment. Our idea is to take a close look at the category-level data distribution and align each class with an adaptive adversarial loss. Specifically, we reduce the weight of the adversarial loss for category-level aligned features while increasing the adversarial force for those poorly aligned. In this process, we decide how well a feature is category-level aligned between source and target by a co-training approach. In two domain adaptation tasks, i.e., GTA5 -> Cityscapes and SYNTHIA -> Cityscapes, we validate that the proposed method matches the state of the art in segmentation accuracy.»

[Snippet]: «Fully convolutional models for dense prediction have proven successful for a wide range of visual tasks. Such models perform well in a supervised setting, but performance can be surprisingly poor under domain shifts that appear mild to a human observer. For example, training on one city and testing on another in a different geographic region and/or weather condition may result in significantly degraded performance due to pixel-level distribution shift. [Snippet]: «During the last half decade, convolutional neural networks (CNNs) have triumphed over semantic segmentation, which is a core task of various emerging industrial applications such as autonomous driving and medical imaging. However, to train CNNs requires a huge amount of data, which is difficult to collect and laborious to annotate. Recent advances in computer graphics make it possible to train CNN models on photo-realistic synthetic data with computer-generated annotations. Despite this, the domain mismatch between the real images and the synthetic data significantly decreases the models’ performance.
