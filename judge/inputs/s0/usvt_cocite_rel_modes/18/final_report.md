## Related Works

Anomaly detection in industrial settings has garnered significant attention due to its critical role in quality control and system reliability. Early approaches relied on rule-based methods and statistical models, which were limited in their ability to generalize across complex, dynamic environments \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2012.03586)\]. With the advent of deep learning, numerous methods have been proposed to improve detection accuracy and adaptability. 

One line of research focuses on process-based anomaly detection for Industrial Control Systems (ICSs). A systematic analysis of such schemes revealed that many are vulnerable to adversarial spoofing attacks, which can reduce recall and compromise detection reliability \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2012.03586)\]. This highlights the need for more robust models that can learn and enforce physical properties of the system \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2012.03586)\]. 

Unsupervised anomaly detection has also been extensively explored, particularly for 3D data. The MVTec 3D-AD dataset was introduced to evaluate models for detecting geometric anomalies in manufactured products \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/2112.09045)\]. This dataset includes precise ground-truth annotations, enabling rigorous evaluation of detection and localization performance \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/2112.09045)\]. Similarly, the MVTec AD dataset has been widely used to benchmark methods for image-based anomaly detection \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/2106.08265)\]. 

To address the cold-start problem in industrial anomaly detection, where models are trained only on normal data, PatchCore was proposed. This method uses a memory bank of representative patch features to achieve state-of-the-art performance on the MVTec AD benchmark, with an AUROC score of 99.6% \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/2106.08265)\]. Another approach, CFLOW-AD, introduces a real-time model based on conditional normalizing flows, achieving faster inference times and better localization performance than prior methods \[[4](http://arxiv.org/abs/2107.12571)\]. 

For time-series data, LSTM-based encoder-decoder models have shown robustness in detecting anomalies from both predictable and unpredictable time-series \[[7](http://arxiv.org/abs/1607.00148)\]. Similarly, MAD-GAN leverages Generative Adversarial Networks (GANs) to capture spatial-temporal correlations in multivariate time series, improving detection accuracy on real-world datasets like SWaT and WADI \[[13](http://arxiv.org/abs/1901.04997)\]. 

Recent advances in anomaly detection have also focused on localization. PaDiM introduces a patch distribution modeling framework that outperforms existing methods on both detection and localization tasks \[[6](http://arxiv.org/abs/2011.08785)\]. DRÆM, another recent approach, treats anomaly detection as a discriminative problem, achieving superior performance on the MVTec dataset \[[8](http://arxiv.org/abs/2108.07610)\]. 

Additionally, methods such as FastFlow and RIAD have been proposed to improve the efficiency and accuracy of anomaly detection by leveraging normalizing flows and inpainting techniques \[[10](http://arxiv.org/abs/2111.07677)\]\[[12](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/2c89b183df320c3ef698989bdc5d1d4731c4d65d)\]. ### <a id='category-industrial-anomaly-detection-methods'></a>Industrial Anomaly Detection Methods: 2 papers found.
In the realm of industrial anomaly detection, recent research has focused on developing robust and efficient methods for detecting anomalies in Industrial Control Systems (ICSs) \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2012.03586)\]. A systematic analysis of process-based anomaly detection schemes has revealed vulnerabilities in existing detectors, particularly their susceptibility to adversarial spoofing attacks such as Synthetic Sensor Spoofing \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2012.03586)\]. These attacks can significantly reduce the recall of anomaly detection systems, undermining their reliability in real-world scenarios. Additionally, sequence-to-sequence neural networks have been explored as a promising approach for anomaly detection, leveraging operational data to identify outliers and pinpoint attack points \[[5](http://arxiv.org/abs/1911.04831)\]. While these methods show promise, they often require extensive computational resources, limiting their deployment on resource-constrained embedded devices \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2012.03586)\]. The need for more efficient and reliable detection methods remains a critical challenge, especially for Small and Medium Enterprises (SMEs) that rely on cost-effective solutions for quality control in manufacturing \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2012.03586)\].

### <a id='category-anomaly-detection-localization'></a>Anomaly Detection Localization: 9 papers found.
In the realm of anomaly detection and localization, recent research has focused on developing efficient and accurate methods suitable for real-world industrial applications \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/2106.08265)\]. Many existing models, while effective, are often too large and computationally intensive for deployment on resource-constrained embedded devices, which is critical for Small and Medium Enterprises (SMEs) in intelligent manufacturing \[[12](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/2c89b183df320c3ef698989bdc5d1d4731c4d65d)\]. To address this challenge, approaches like PatchCore \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/2106.08265)\] and CFLOW-AD \[[4](http://arxiv.org/abs/2107.12571)\] have been proposed, offering competitive performance with reduced computational demands. PatchCore, for instance, uses a maximally representative memory bank of nominal patch-features to achieve state-of-the-art results on the MVTec AD benchmark with fast inference times \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/2106.08265)\]. Similarly, CFLOW-AD introduces a computationally efficient model based on conditional normalizing flows, achieving significant improvements in both detection and localization tasks \[[4](http://arxiv.org/abs/2107.12571)\]. Other methods, such as PaDiM \[[6](http://arxiv.org/abs/2011.08785)\], leverage pretrained convolutional neural networks and multivariate Gaussian distributions to model normal patterns, enhancing localization accuracy. Additionally, methods like FastFlow \[[10](http://arxiv.org/abs/2111.07677)\] and DRÆM \[[8](http://arxiv.org/abs/2108.07610)\] focus on improving the mapping of image features to tractable distributions, enabling robust anomaly detection without requiring extensive labeled data. These advancements highlight the growing emphasis on practical deployment and efficiency in anomaly detection systems \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/2106.08265)\]\[[4](http://arxiv.org/abs/2107.12571)\]\[[6](http://arxiv.org/abs/2011.08785)\]\[[8](http://arxiv.org/abs/2108.07610)\]\[[10](http://arxiv.org/abs/2111.07677)\].

The study was performed on a Secure Water Treatment testbed (SWaT) dataset, which represents a scaled-down version of a real-world industrial water treatment plant. We suggest a method for anomaly detection based on measuring the statistical deviation of the predicted value from the observed value. We applied the proposed method by using a variety of deep neural network architectures including different variants of convolutional and recurrent networks. The test dataset included 36 different cyber attacks. The proposed method successfully detected 31 attacks with three false positives thus improving on previous research based on this dataset. The results of the study show that 1D convolutional networks can be successfully used for anomaly detection in industrial control systems and outperform recurrent networks in this setting. The findings also suggest that 1D convolutional neural networks are effective at time series prediction tasks which are traditionally considered to be best solved using recurrent neural networks. This observation is a promising one, as 1D convolutional neural networks are simpler, smaller, and faster than the recurrent neural networks.»
