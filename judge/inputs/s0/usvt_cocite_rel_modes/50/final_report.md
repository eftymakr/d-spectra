## Related Works

The rapid growth of open-source machine learning (ML) resources has significantly impacted information retrieval (IR) research, as noted in several recent studies \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2305.11164)\]. While platforms like Hugging Face have become central to the ML community, they lack structured representations that enable advanced querying and analysis \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2305.11164)\]. This limitation has motivated researchers to explore novel approaches to enhance the management and utilization of ML resources.

Several studies have examined the characteristics and usage patterns of ML models on Hugging Face. For instance, \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/2311.13380)\] highlights the dominance of NLP in carbon emissions reporting and the correlation between model size and emissions. Similarly, \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/2401.13822)\] discusses the evolution and maintenance of ML models, emphasizing the need for better documentation and versioning practices. These findings underscore the importance of structured metadata for effective resource management.

In the realm of dataset documentation, \[[4](http://arxiv.org/abs/2303.02552)\] provides a comprehensive analysis of dataset cards on Hugging Face, revealing that while certain sections like Dataset Description are well-documented, others such as Considerations for Using the Data are underdeveloped. This highlights a gap in the documentation practices that could hinder the reproducibility and usability of datasets. Additionally, \[[5](http://arxiv.org/abs/2406.08205)\] discusses the challenges of model reuse, including issues related to provenance, reproducibility, and portability, which are critical for effective resource management.

The need for structured representations has also been addressed in the context of model reuse. \[[6](http://arxiv.org/abs/2402.07323)\] presents an empirical study of pre-trained model reuse, identifying challenges such as discrepancies between claimed and actual performance. These findings align with the need for a knowledge graph like HuggingKG to capture detailed relationships and attributes of ML resources.

### <a id='category-hugging-face-ecosystem-research'></a>Hugging Face Ecosystem Research: 9 papers found.
The Hugging Face ecosystem has been extensively studied for its role in machine learning (ML) research and development, with a focus on model and dataset management, carbon footprint analysis, and collaborative practices. Researchers have highlighted the lack of structured representations in existing platforms, which limits advanced querying and analysis of ML resources \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2305.11164)\]. This issue is addressed by HuggingKG, a knowledge graph built from Hugging Face data, enabling more sophisticated resource management and IR tasks \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2305.11164)\]. Studies have also examined the carbon emissions of ML models on Hugging Face, revealing that while there is a slight decrease in reported carbon footprints over time, the majority of emissions are still attributed to natural language processing (NLP) models \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2305.11164)\]. Additionally, the documentation practices of datasets on Hugging Face show significant variability, with some sections being more thoroughly addressed than others, such as the Considerations for Using the Data section \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/2311.13380)\]. The reuse of pre-trained models (PTMs) on Hugging Face has also been investigated, identifying challenges such as missing attributes, discrepancies between claimed and actual performance, and model risks \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/2401.13822)\]. These findings underscore the need for improved documentation, transparency, and standardization within the Hugging Face ecosystem \[[4](http://arxiv.org/abs/2303.02552)\]. The social and collaborative dynamics of Hugging Face have also been explored, revealing a core-periphery structure in developer networks and the dominance of a few companies in model adoption \[[6](http://arxiv.org/abs/2402.07323)\]. Overall, these studies collectively highlight the importance of structured data, transparency, and community engagement in advancing the Hugging Face ecosystem for ML research \[[7](http://arxiv.org/abs/2405.13058)\].

### <a id='category-hugging-face-model-research-trends'></a>Hugging Face Model Research Trends: 4 papers found.
The category "Hugging Face Model Research Trends" encompasses several key themes related to the development, maintenance, and reuse of machine learning models on the Hugging Face platform. Researchers have explored the growth and popularity of Hugging Face, identifying trends in ML domains, framework usage, and the evolution of tags and datasets \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/2311.13380)\]. Additionally, studies have highlighted challenges in model reuse, such as limited guidance for beginners, difficulties in understanding model outputs, and a lack of comprehensive documentation \[[8](http://arxiv.org/abs/2401.13177)\]. These issues are compounded by inconsistencies in release practices, including ambiguous naming conventions and incomplete training documentation, which hinder transparency and reproducibility \[[13](http://arxiv.org/abs/2409.10472)\]. Efforts to address these challenges include the introduction of semantic versioning and improved documentation standards, aiming to enhance model maintainability and user experience \[[13](http://arxiv.org/abs/2409.10472)\]. Furthermore, there is growing interest in integrating Hugging Face with tools from the Information Retrieval domain to facilitate more advanced analyses of large-scale textual corpora, such as through the development of GAIA Search, which enables efficient qualitative data analysis \[[14](http://arxiv.org/abs/2306.01481)\]. These trends underscore the evolving landscape of model management on Hugging Face, emphasizing the need for standardized practices and enhanced tooling to support both researchers and practitioners.

### <a id='category-ai-tools-and-software-engineering'></a>AI Tools and Software Engineering: 2 papers found.
The category "AI Tools and Software Engineering" encompasses research that bridges artificial intelligence with software development practices, focusing on the integration of AI models into broader software systems. Recent studies highlight the growing need for structured representations and knowledge graphs to manage the increasing complexity of open-source machine learning resources, such as those hosted on platforms like Hugging Face \[[12](http://arxiv.org/abs/2303.17580)\]. These platforms, while rich in models and datasets, often lack the ability to support advanced queries and analyses, such as tracing model evolution or recommending relevant datasets, due to the absence of structured data \[[12](http://arxiv.org/abs/2303.17580)\]. To address this, researchers have proposed the construction of knowledge graphs, such as HuggingKG, which captures domain-specific relations and textual attributes to enable more sophisticated resource management \[[12](http://arxiv.org/abs/2303.17580)\]. This approach aligns with broader efforts in software engineering to improve the dependability, safety, and maintainability of AI-based systems, which are increasingly prevalent in society \[[16](http://arxiv.org/abs/2105.01984)\]. While existing studies emphasize the importance of software testing and quality assurance in AI systems, challenges related to data management and system maintenance remain underexplored \[[16](http://arxiv.org/abs/2105.01984)\]. The integration of large language models (LLMs) as controllers to manage and coordinate diverse AI models, as seen in HuggingGPT, further illustrates the potential for AI to enhance software engineering practices by providing a unified interface for task planning and execution \[[12](http://arxiv.org/abs/2303.17580)\]. These developments underscore the evolving landscape of AI tools and software engineering, where the convergence of AI capabilities and software development methodologies is critical for advancing both research and practical applications.
