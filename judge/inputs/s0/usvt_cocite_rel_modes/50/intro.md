## Related Works

The rapid growth of open-source machine learning (ML) resources has significantly impacted information retrieval (IR) research, as noted in several recent studies \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2305.11164)\]. While platforms like Hugging Face have become central to the ML community, they lack structured representations that enable advanced querying and analysis \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2305.11164)\]. This limitation has motivated researchers to explore novel approaches to enhance the management and utilization of ML resources.

Several studies have examined the characteristics and usage patterns of ML models on Hugging Face. For instance, \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/2311.13380)\] highlights the dominance of NLP in carbon emissions reporting and the correlation between model size and emissions. Similarly, \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/2401.13822)\] discusses the evolution and maintenance of ML models, emphasizing the need for better documentation and versioning practices. These findings underscore the importance of structured metadata for effective resource management.

In the realm of dataset documentation, \[[4](http://arxiv.org/abs/2303.02552)\] provides a comprehensive analysis of dataset cards on Hugging Face, revealing that while certain sections like Dataset Description are well-documented, others such as Considerations for Using the Data are underdeveloped. This highlights a gap in the documentation practices that could hinder the reproducibility and usability of datasets. Additionally, \[[5](http://arxiv.org/abs/2406.08205)\] discusses the challenges of model reuse, including issues related to provenance, reproducibility, and portability, which are critical for effective resource management.

The need for structured representations has also been addressed in the context of model reuse. \[[6](http://arxiv.org/abs/2402.07323)\] presents an empirical study of pre-trained model reuse, identifying challenges such as discrepancies between claimed and actual performance. These findings align with the need for a knowledge graph like HuggingKG to capture detailed relationships and attributes of ML resources.

Furthermore, \[[7](http://arxiv.org/abs/2405.13058)\] discusses the importance of documentation quality in influencing the popularity of pre-trained models, reinforcing the argument for structured metadata. \[[8](http://arxiv.org/abs/2401.13177)\] highlights the challenges faced by developers in the open AI ecosystem, including imbalanced activity distributions and the core-periphery structure of collaboration, which could be mitigated with better resource organization.

The development of HuggingKG is also informed by the need for semantic versioning in model releases. \[[9](http://arxiv.org/abs/2307.14841)\] identifies inconsistencies in naming conventions and documentation practices, which could be addressed through a more formalized versioning system. This aligns with the goals of HuggingKG to provide a structured representation of ML resources.

In addition, \[[10](http://arxiv.org/abs/2405.16746)\] explores the use of large language models to analyze and construct the LLM4Code ecosystem, demonstrating the potential of advanced models in understanding and organizing complex ML resources.
