## Related Works

Federated Learning (FL) has emerged as a promising paradigm for training machine learning models on decentralized data while preserving privacy \[[4](http://arxiv.org/abs/1912.04977)\]. In FL, a central server coordinates the training of a global model using updates from multiple clients, each holding their own private data \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/1610.05492)\]. This approach is particularly useful in scenarios where data is distributed across mobile devices, IoT devices, or other edge computing environments \[[7](http://arxiv.org/abs/1602.05629)\]. However, FL faces several challenges, including the heterogeneity of client data distributions and the resulting fluctuations in global and local model parameters, which can negatively impact model accuracy \[[12](http://arxiv.org/abs/2102.02079)\].

Several works have addressed these challenges. For instance, FedProx \[[10](http://arxiv.org/abs/1812.06127)\] introduces a framework that generalizes and re-parameterizes FedAvg, allowing for more robust convergence in heterogeneous settings. This method is particularly effective in scenarios with non-identically distributed data and varying computational capabilities across devices. Similarly, SCAFFOLD \[[17](http://arxiv.org/abs/1910.06378)\] proposes a new algorithm that uses control variates to reduce the drift between client updates, leading to faster convergence and fewer communication rounds. These methods highlight the importance of addressing both statistical and system-level heterogeneity in FL.

Another key challenge in FL is the non-IID nature of data, which can significantly degrade model performance. The work in \[[13](http://arxiv.org/abs/1806.00582)\] demonstrates that federated learning accuracy can drop by up to 55% when data is highly skewed, and proposes a strategy to improve accuracy by sharing a small subset of data across clients. This approach has shown promising results, increasing accuracy by 30% on the CIFAR-10 dataset with only 5% globally shared data. Similarly, \[[12](http://arxiv.org/abs/2102.02079)\] emphasizes the challenges posed by non-IID data in FL and evaluates various algorithms, showing that no single method outperforms others in all cases.

To enhance model personalization, recent work has explored the integration of meta-learning techniques into FL. For example, \[[11](http://arxiv.org/abs/1909.12488)\] discusses how Federated Averaging can be interpreted as a meta-learning algorithm, and proposes methods to fine-tune global models for better personalization. This aligns with the broader goal of FL to provide models that are both globally effective and individually tailored.

### <a id='category-federated-learning-research'></a>Federated Learning Research: 6 papers found.
Federated Learning (FL) has emerged as a critical paradigm for training machine learning models while preserving data privacy, particularly in decentralized environments such as mobile devices and wearable systems \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/1812.01097)\]. FL enables the collaborative training of a global model without directly sharing raw data, which is essential for maintaining user privacy \[[7](http://arxiv.org/abs/1602.05629)\]. However, the heterogeneity of data across clients and the challenges of communication efficiency and model convergence have posed significant research hurdles \[[5](http://arxiv.org/abs/1905.10497)\]. To address these issues, researchers have proposed various approaches, including structured and sketched updates to reduce communication costs \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/1610.05492)\], as well as novel optimization algorithms like SCAFFOLD, which mitigates the drift between client models and improves convergence guarantees \[[17](http://arxiv.org/abs/1910.06378)\]. Additionally, fairness in model accuracy distribution across devices has been explored through methods like q-Fair Federated Learning (q-FFL), which ensures a more uniform performance across clients \[[5](http://arxiv.org/abs/1905.10497)\]. Furthermore, multi-task learning has been integrated into FL to handle statistical challenges, with systems-aware methods like MOCHA addressing communication costs and fault tolerance \[[18](http://arxiv.org/abs/1705.10467)\]. These advancements highlight the growing focus on improving both the efficiency and fairness of FL frameworks, aligning with the broader goal of deploying robust and scalable decentralized learning systems.

### <a id='category-federated-learning-research-trends'></a>Federated Learning Research Trends: 8 papers found.
Federated learning (FL) has emerged as a critical paradigm for training machine learning models while preserving data privacy, enabling collaborative learning across distributed clients without sharing raw data \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/2003.00295)\]. However, the heterogeneity of client data distributions poses significant challenges to model convergence and performance, as non-identically distributed data (non-IID) often leads to suboptimal results \[[5](http://arxiv.org/abs/1905.10497)\]. To address this, researchers have proposed various approaches, including adaptive optimization methods such as Adagrad, Adam, and Yogi, which have shown improved convergence and performance in federated settings \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/1812.01097)\]. Additionally, frameworks like FedProx have been introduced to handle both statistical and system heterogeneity by generalizing FedAvg and providing convergence guarantees under non-IID conditions \[[10](http://arxiv.org/abs/1812.06127)\]. Another approach, clustered FL (CFL), leverages geometric properties of the loss surface to group clients with similar data distributions, enabling more effective model training without requiring prior knowledge of the number of clusters \[[9](http://arxiv.org/abs/1910.01991)\]. Furthermore, methods such as ensemble distillation have been explored to enable flexible aggregation of heterogeneous client models, improving training efficiency and reducing communication rounds \[[14](http://arxiv.org/abs/2006.07242)\]. These advancements highlight the ongoing efforts to enhance the robustness and effectiveness of FL in the face of data heterogeneity, with a growing emphasis on adaptive strategies, clustering, and efficient aggregation mechanisms \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/2003.00295)\]\[[5](http://arxiv.org/abs/1905.10497)\]\[[9](http://arxiv.org/abs/1910.01991)\]\[[10](http://arxiv.org/abs/1812.06127)\]\[[14](http://arxiv.org/abs/2006.07242)\].

### <a id='category-others'></a>Others: 3 papers found.
Federated learning (FL) has gained significant attention as a decentralized approach to machine learning, enabling collaborative model training while preserving data privacy by keeping training data localized on client devices \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/1812.01097)\]. This paradigm is particularly beneficial for resource-constrained edge devices, such as mobile phones and IoT devices, as it allows them to contribute to a shared model without exposing sensitive data \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/1610.05492)\]. However, FL faces challenges when local data distributions are non-IID (independent and identically distributed), leading to reduced model accuracy due to weight divergence and statistical inconsistencies \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/1610.05492)\]. Existing frameworks often lack scalability and support for heterogeneous device environments, limiting their practical implementation \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/2003.00295)\]. Recent efforts, such as the development of Flower, aim to address these limitations by providing a comprehensive framework for large-scale FL experiments on diverse and heterogeneous edge devices \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/2003.00295)\]. These advancements highlight the growing interest in FL and its potential to overcome the privacy and efficiency challenges of traditional centralized learning approaches.

### <a id='category-federated-learning-personalization'></a>Federated Learning Personalization: 2 papers found.
FedCS \[[6](http://arxiv.org/abs/1804.08333)\] introduces an optimized FL protocol designed to address inefficiencies in traditional FL frameworks by managing client resources effectively. This protocol tackles the issue of heterogeneous client capabilities and network conditions, which can lead to prolonged training times. By solving a client selection problem under resource constraints, FedCS enables the server to aggregate more client updates, thereby accelerating model performance improvements. This approach is particularly relevant in mobile edge computing environments where clients may have varying computational resources and wireless channel qualities. In contrast, other works explore personalization in FL settings, such as leveraging Model Agnostic Meta Learning (MAML) to adapt global models to individual devices \[[11](http://arxiv.org/abs/1909.12488)\]. These studies highlight the importance of considering data heterogeneity and the need for efficient and effective methods to enhance model accuracy and personalization. Both FedCS and MAML-based approaches aim to improve FL by addressing specific challenges, such as resource management and model adaptation, which are critical for practical deployment in decentralized learning scenarios.
