## Related Works

Speech-driven 3D facial animation has been a focal point in computer vision and machine learning research, aiming to generate realistic facial expressions that match speech content and emotion. Early approaches relied on handcrafted features and rule-based systems, but recent advances have shifted toward data-driven methods using deep learning \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2112.05329)\]. These methods typically involve mapping audio signals to facial animations, often using pre-trained self-supervised audio models as encoders \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/2104.08223)\]. However, challenges persist, particularly in accurately capturing lip movements and facial expressions that align with speech content \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/2303.11089)\].

One notable work, **VOCA**, introduced a neural network that factors identity from facial motion, enabling realistic animation of a wide range of adult faces based on any speech signal \[[4](http://arxiv.org/abs/2104.07452)\]. This model was trained on a large 4D face dataset, allowing it to generalize to unseen subjects without retargeting \[[4](http://arxiv.org/abs/2104.07452)\]. Similarly, **FaceFormer** proposed a Transformer-based autoregressive model that encodes long-term audio context and autoregressively predicts a sequence of animated 3D face meshes, improving upon prior phoneme-level feature learning \[[5](http://arxiv.org/abs/2306.10799)\]. The use of self-supervised pre-trained speech representations was also emphasized to address data scarcity issues \[[5](http://arxiv.org/abs/2306.10799)\].

Another approach, **MeshTalk**, introduced a categorical latent space for facial animation that disentangles audio-correlated and audio-uncorrelated information using a novel cross-modality loss \[[6](http://arxiv.org/abs/1905.03079)\]. This enabled highly accurate lip motion and plausible animation of non-speech-related facial features like eye blinks and brow movements \[[6](http://arxiv.org/abs/1905.03079)\]. **EmoTalk** further extended this by proposing an end-to-end neural network to disentangle emotions from speech content, generating rich 3D facial expressions \[[7](http://arxiv.org/abs/2301.02379)\]. This work also addressed the scarcity of 3D emotional talking face data by leveraging facial blendshapes for plausible 3D face reconstruction \[[7](http://arxiv.org/abs/2301.02379)\].

**Audio-Driven Emotional Video Portraits (EVP)** decomposed speech into two decoupled spaces—duration-independent emotion and duration-dependent content—enabling dynamic 2D emotional facial landmarks to be deduced \[[8](http://arxiv.org/abs/1805.09488)\]. This was then used to generate high-quality video portraits with vivid emotional dynamics \[[8](http://arxiv.org/abs/1805.09488)\]. **SelfTalk** introduced a self-supervised commutative training diagram to learn 3D talking faces by involving cross-modal features exchange between audio, text, and lip shape \[[9](http://arxiv.org/abs/2104.11116)\]. This framework enabled more plausible lip shapes by leveraging knowledge from the lip-reading interpreter \[[9](http://arxiv.org/abs/2104.11116)\].

### <a id='category-3d-facial-animation-from-speech'></a>3D Facial Animation from Speech: 14 papers found.
In the field of 3D facial animation driven by speech, recent research has focused on improving the accuracy and realism of lip synchronization and facial expressions. Existing methods often rely on pre-trained self-supervised audio models as encoders, but these models struggle with phonetically similar syllables that have distinct lip shapes, leading to an averaging effect in lip motion generation \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2112.05329)\]. To address this, researchers have proposed various approaches, such as the Face-Former model, which uses a Transformer-based autoregressive structure to encode long-term audio context and predict 3D face meshes with improved accuracy \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2112.05329)\]. Similarly, the meshtalk method introduces a categorical latent space that disentangles audio-correlated and uncorrelated facial information, enabling more natural and realistic facial animations \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/2104.08223)\]. Additionally, the Emotional Video Portraits (EVP) system introduces techniques for synthesizing emotional dynamics in facial animations, decomposing speech into emotion and content spaces to generate more expressive and dynamic facial movements \[[4](http://arxiv.org/abs/2104.07452)\]. The VOCA model addresses data scarcity by introducing a 4D face dataset and a neural network that factors identity from facial motion, allowing for realistic animation of unseen subjects \[[6](http://arxiv.org/abs/1905.03079)\]. Other works, such as Imitator, focus on preserving identity-specific speaking styles by learning from short reference videos and optimizing for realistic facial expressions \[[12](http://arxiv.org/abs/2301.00023)\]. The Wav2Lip model improves lip synchronization for arbitrary identities by learning from a powerful lip-sync discriminator and introducing rigorous evaluation benchmarks \[[13](http://arxiv.org/abs/2008.10010)\]. These advancements highlight the ongoing efforts to enhance the realism, controllability, and generalizability of 3D speech-driven facial animation systems.

### <a id='category-speech-driven-3d-facial-animation'></a>Speech Driven 3D Facial Animation: 10 papers found.
Speech-driven 3D facial animation has been a focal point of recent research, aiming to generate realistic and emotionally expressive facial movements in response to speech signals \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/2303.11089)\]. Existing methods often rely on pre-trained self-supervised audio models as encoders, but these models struggle with phonetically similar syllables that have distinct lip shapes, leading to an averaging effect in lip motion generation \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/2303.11089)\]. To address this, researchers have proposed various approaches, such as the emotion disentangling encoder (EDE) which separates emotional and content components in speech signals \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/2303.11089)\], and SelfTalk, which employs a cross-modal network system with self-supervision to enhance lip movement accuracy \[[5](http://arxiv.org/abs/2306.10799)\]. Another study introduced a code query task in a learned codebook to reduce cross-modal mapping uncertainty and improve the vividness of generated facial motions \[[7](http://arxiv.org/abs/2301.02379)\]. Additionally, the work by \[[10](http://arxiv.org/abs/1807.07860)\] emphasized the importance of disentangled audio-visual representations to enable arbitrary-subject talking face generation with clearer lip motion patterns. Furthermore, EMOTE was introduced to explicitly control emotional expression while maintaining lip-sync, using decoupled losses for speech and emotion \[[15](http://arxiv.org/abs/2306.08990)\]. The use of diffusion models, such as FaceDiffuser, has also been explored to generate non-deterministic facial animations compatible with existing animation pipelines \[[21](http://arxiv.org/abs/2309.11306)\]. These approaches collectively highlight the challenges and advancements in achieving realistic and emotionally expressive 3D facial animations driven by speech.
