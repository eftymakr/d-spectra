## Related Works

Vectorized embeddings have become a cornerstone in modern recommender systems, enabling efficient user-item matching by capturing latent relationships in a compact form \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/1706.02216)\]. However, the computational and memory costs of maintaining dense embeddings have motivated research into representation binarization, which converts embeddings into compact binary sequences \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/1802.05814)\]. This approach has shown promise in optimizing both memory usage and computational overhead \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/1609.02907)\]. Despite these benefits, existing methods often focus on numerical quantization, which can lead to significant information loss and performance degradation \[[4](http://arxiv.org/abs/1806.01973)\]. 

Several studies have explored the use of graph-based models for recommendation tasks, leveraging the structural relationships between users and items \[[5](http://arxiv.org/abs/1905.08108)\]. For instance, GraphSAGE provides an inductive framework for generating embeddings of previously unseen nodes by aggregating features from a node's local neighborhood \[[6](http://arxiv.org/abs/1403.6652)\]. Similarly, GCN-based methods have been applied to large-scale recommender systems, where they combine efficient random walks and graph convolutions to generate embeddings that incorporate both graph structure and node features \[[7](http://arxiv.org/abs/1708.05031)\]. These models have demonstrated effectiveness in improving recommendation quality, especially in scenarios with massive graphs \[[8](http://arxiv.org/abs/1205.2618)\].

In the context of collaborative filtering, neural network-based approaches have been proposed to model user-item interactions more effectively. Neural Collaborative Filtering (NCF) introduces a multi-layer perceptron to learn the user-item interaction function, offering better performance than traditional matrix factorization methods \[[9](http://arxiv.org/abs/1708.04396)\]. Variational Autoencoders have also been extended to collaborative filtering, enabling the modeling of implicit feedback with a non-linear probabilistic framework \[[10](http://arxiv.org/abs/1905.07854)\]. These methods have shown significant improvements over state-of-the-art baselines, particularly in capturing complex interactions between users and items \[[11](http://arxiv.org/abs/1902.06236)\].

The integration of knowledge graphs into recommendation systems has also gained traction, as they provide rich side information that can enhance the modeling of user preferences \[[12](http://arxiv.org/abs/2001.00267)\]. Knowledge Graph Attention Networks (KGAT) explicitly model high-order relations in knowledge graphs, leading to more accurate and interpretable recommendations \[[13](http://arxiv.org/abs/1310.4546)\]. Additionally, methods like BiRank and TriRank have been developed to rank vertices in bipartite and n-partite graphs, respectively, by leveraging both graph structure and prior information \[[14](http://arxiv.org/abs/1708.05027)\]. These approaches demonstrate the potential of graph-based models in capturing collaborative signals that are often overlooked in traditional methods \[[15](http://arxiv.org/abs/1409.2944)\].

In summary, while existing approaches have made significant strides in improving recommendation accuracy and efficiency, there remains a need for more robust methods that minimize information loss during representation binarization. Our work builds on these prior contributions by introducing BiGeaR++, which explicitly mitigates information loss

### <a id='category-graph-neural-networks-research'></a>Graph Neural Networks Research: 3 papers found.
Graph Neural Networks (GNNs) have emerged as a powerful framework for learning node embeddings in large-scale graph-structured data, with applications ranging from social network analysis to biological systems \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/1706.02216)\]. Unlike traditional transductive methods that require all nodes to be present during training, inductive approaches like GraphSAGE enable the generation of embeddings for unseen nodes by leveraging local neighborhood information \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/1706.02216)\]. This is achieved through a function that aggregates and samples features from a node's immediate surroundings, allowing for generalization across evolving graphs \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/1706.02216)\]. Similarly, DeepWalk introduces a method for learning latent representations by treating random walks on graphs as sequences, akin to sentences in natural language processing, thereby capturing structural and semantic relationships \[[6](http://arxiv.org/abs/1403.6652)\]. This approach has been shown to outperform global methods, especially in scenarios with sparse labeled data \[[6](http://arxiv.org/abs/1403.6652)\]. Another notable work presents a scalable convolutional architecture for semi-supervised learning on graphs, based on localized approximations of spectral convolutions, which effectively encodes both local structure and node features \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/1609.02907)\]. These methods collectively highlight the importance of inductive learning, efficient aggregation mechanisms, and the ability to handle large-scale and evolving graph data, forming a foundational basis for modern GNN research.

### <a id='category-recommender-systems-research'></a>Recommender Systems Research: 7 papers found.
The related works in recommender systems research focus on improving collaborative filtering through advanced embedding techniques and graph-based models. Early approaches relied on matrix factorization, but recent work has shifted toward deep learning and graph neural networks to better capture user-item interactions and high-order connectivity \[[4](http://arxiv.org/abs/1806.01973)\]. For instance, the Neural Graph Collaborative Filtering (NGCF) framework integrates user-item graph structures into the embedding process, enabling more expressive modeling of collaborative signals \[[5](http://arxiv.org/abs/1905.08108)\]. Similarly, the Knowledge Graph Attention Network (KGAT) leverages knowledge graphs to model high-order relations between items and users, enhancing recommendation accuracy through attention mechanisms \[[10](http://arxiv.org/abs/1905.07854)\]. Additionally, the development of large-scale graph convolutional networks (GCNs) has enabled efficient embedding of massive graphs, as demonstrated in the Pinterest recommendation system, where GCN embeddings significantly improved recommendation quality \[[4](http://arxiv.org/abs/1806.01973)\]. Furthermore, the Bayesian Personalized Ranking (BPR) optimization criterion has been introduced to directly optimize for personalized ranking, surpassing traditional methods like matrix factorization and adaptive kNN \[[8](http://arxiv.org/abs/1205.2618)\]. These advancements highlight the growing emphasis on integrating graph structures, attention mechanisms, and deep learning to enhance collaborative filtering and achieve state-of-the-art performance \[[4](http://arxiv.org/abs/1806.01973)\]\[[5](http://arxiv.org/abs/1905.08108)\]\[[8](http://arxiv.org/abs/1205.2618)\].

### <a id='category-others'></a>Others: 6 papers found.
The category "Others" encompasses a variety of research topics that extend beyond traditional recommendation systems and graph-based methods, focusing on advanced techniques for learning and representing data. These include the use of bipartite graphs for ranking tasks, where methods like BiRank iteratively assign scores to vertices based on graph structure and prior information, leading to efficient and effective ranking \[[9](http://arxiv.org/abs/1708.04396)\]. Additionally, the integration of knowledge graphs (KGs) into recommender systems has been explored, emphasizing the importance of handling incomplete KGs by jointly learning recommendation models and KG completion \[[11](http://arxiv.org/abs/1902.06236)\]. Another area involves improving word representations through models like the continuous Skip-gram, which enhances the quality of distributed vectors and enables the learning of phrases \[[13](http://arxiv.org/abs/1310.4546)\]. Furthermore, models such as Factorization Machines (FMs) and their extensions, like Neural Factorization Machine (NFM), have been developed to handle sparse data by capturing both second-order and higher-order feature interactions \[[14](http://arxiv.org/abs/1708.05027)\]. Collaborative filtering (CF) has also been enhanced by incorporating auxiliary information and deep learning techniques, leading to models like Collaborative Deep Learning (CDL) that jointly learn from content and rating data \[[15](http://arxiv.org/abs/1409.2944)\]. Lastly, Graph Neural Networks (GNNs) have been analyzed for their expressive power, with theoretical frameworks developed to understand their limitations and enhance their performance in capturing complex graph structures \[[16](http://arxiv.org/abs/1810.00826)\]. These diverse approaches highlight the ongoing efforts to improve recommendation systems, natural language processing, and graph-based modeling through advanced learning techniques.
