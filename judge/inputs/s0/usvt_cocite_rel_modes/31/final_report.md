## Related Works

Unsupervised pre-training has emerged as a powerful paradigm for learning general-purpose representations across various domains, including computer vision and natural language processing \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/1807.03748)\]. In the context of DNA pre-training, prior work has explored the use of pure sequence-based models, but these approaches often fail to capture the complex regulatory signals that govern DNA function, such as chromatin accessibility and histone modifications \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/1905.09272)\]. This limitation highlights the need for more informative training signals that can better reflect the biological complexity of genomic data.

Recent advances in unsupervised learning have demonstrated that contrastive predictive coding can effectively learn useful representations from high-dimensional data by predicting future samples in latent space \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/1807.03748)\]. Similarly, self-supervised learning methods such as SimCLR and MoCo have shown that learning from unlabeled data through pretext tasks can yield representations that are competitive with fully supervised models \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/1805.01978)\]\[[4](http://arxiv.org/abs/1907.11692)\]. However, these methods often rely on large-scale data and specific architectural choices, which may not be suitable for the sparse and high-dimensional nature of genomic data \[[5](http://arxiv.org/abs/1505.05192)\].

Supervised learning, on the other hand, has been shown to provide more direct and effective training signals for tasks where labeled data is available. For example, in the field of image recognition, supervised contrastive learning has been demonstrated to outperform cross-entropy loss by pulling together clusters of points belonging to the same class while pushing apart clusters from different classes \[[6](http://arxiv.org/abs/2006.10029)\]. This approach has led to state-of-the-art results on benchmark datasets \[[7](http://arxiv.org/abs/2002.05709)\]. Similarly, in the domain of language modeling, BERT has achieved significant performance gains by pre-training deep bidirectional transformers on unlabeled text \[[8](http://arxiv.org/abs/1206.5538)\]. These models are then fine-tuned on downstream tasks with minimal additional effort \[[9](http://arxiv.org/abs/1611.09842)\].

In the context of genomic profile prediction, supervised training for predicting chromatin accessibility and other regulatory features has been shown to yield more effective representations than pure sequence pre-training \[[10](http://arxiv.org/abs/2003.04297)\]. This is because genomic profiles provide additional biological context that can improve the predictive power of DNA representations. Furthermore, the multi-species and multi-profile nature of genomic data requires models that can adapt to different data modalities and species-specific variations \[[11](http://arxiv.org/abs/1512.03385)\]. This has motivated the development of methods such as the SPACE model, which leverages a Mixture of Experts (MoE) architecture to better capture the relationships between DNA sequences across different species and genomic profiles \[[12](http://arxiv.org/abs/2104.14294)\].

Contrastive Predictive Coding \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/1807.03748)\] and its improvements \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/1905.09272)\] have been pivotal in advancing unsupervised learning by enabling the extraction of useful representations from high-dimensional data. These methods leverage probabilistic contrastive losses to train models that can predict future samples in latent space, making the learned representations highly informative for downstream tasks. Similarly, spatial context-based approaches \[[4](http://arxiv.org/abs/1907.11692)\] have shown promise by using within-image patch relationships to learn rich visual features, which can be used for unsupervised visual discovery and object detection. Additionally, methods like MoCo \[[16](http://arxiv.org/abs/1911.05722)\] and SimCLR \[[10](http://arxiv.org/abs/2003.04297)\] have demonstrated the effectiveness of contrastive learning frameworks, particularly when enhanced with design improvements such as MLP projection heads and data augmentation. These techniques have been further refined in Prototypical Contrastive Learning \[[13](http://arxiv.org/abs/2005.04966)\], which introduces prototypes to encode semantic structures into the embedding space, leading to significant improvements in low-resource transfer tasks. Furthermore, the use of deep residual networks \[[11](http://arxiv.org/abs/1512.03385)\] has enabled the training of substantially deeper models, which are more effective for complex visual recognition tasks. These advancements collectively highlight the importance of unsupervised pretraining and contrastive learning in improving representation learning, especially when combined with efficient architectures and task-specific fine-tuning.
