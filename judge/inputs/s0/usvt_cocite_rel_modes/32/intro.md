## Related Works

Recent advancements in large language models (LLMs) have emphasized the importance of understanding how feature representations evolve across layers, as this knowledge is critical for improving interpretability and robustness \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2307.09288)\]. Several studies have explored the dynamics of representation learning in LLMs, focusing on how layers contribute to specific functions or behaviors. For example, some works have identified critical layers linked to particular tasks or behaviors through data-dependent analyses of fine-tuned models \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/2109.01652)\]. However, these approaches are typically limited to post-hoc settings, as they rely on fine-tuned models rather than pre-fine-tuned ones \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/2305.18290)\].

In contrast, our work introduces a data-oblivious approach to identify intrinsic critical layers in pre-fine-tuned LLMs by analyzing representation dynamics using Centered Kernel Alignment (CKA) \[[4](http://arxiv.org/abs/2110.04366)\]. This method enables us to uncover patterns that are consistent across tasks for a given model, offering a more generalizable understanding of layer importance \[[5](http://arxiv.org/abs/2012.13255)\]. We show that layers with significant shifts in representation space are also those most affected during fine-tuning—a pattern that holds consistently across tasks \[[6](http://arxiv.org/abs/2106.10199)\].

Our spectral analysis further reveals that these shifts are driven by changes in the top principal components, which encode semantic transitions from rationales to conclusions \[[7](http://arxiv.org/abs/2106.09685)\]. This finding aligns with prior work on the intrinsic dimensionality of language models, which suggests that pre-trained models have a low intrinsic dimension, allowing for effective fine-tuning with relatively few parameters \[[8](http://arxiv.org/abs/2407.21783)\]. Additionally, this insight connects with studies on parameter-efficient transfer learning, where methods such as LoRA and adapters have been shown to achieve strong performance by modifying only a small subset of parameters \[[9](http://arxiv.org/abs/1802.05365)\].

We apply these findings to two practical scenarios: efficient domain adaptation and backdoor defense. For domain adaptation, we demonstrate that fine-tuning critical layers leads to greater loss reduction compared to non-critical layers \[[10](http://arxiv.org/abs/1902.00751)\]. This aligns with prior work on efficient fine-tuning methods, such as BitFit, which shows that modifying only bias terms can be competitive with full fine-tuning \[[11](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/1e077413b25c4d34945cc2707e17e46ed4fe784a)\]. Similarly, for backdoor defense, we find that freezing critical layers reduces attack success rates by up to 40% \[[12](http://arxiv.org/abs/2101.00190)\]. This result is consistent with findings from reinforcement learning from human feedback (RLHF) approaches, where aligning models with human preferences through reward modeling improves robustness \[[13](http://arxiv.org/abs/2001.08361)\].
