## Related Works

The growing adoption of cloud-native applications has led to increased reliance on container orchestration platforms such as Kubernetes to manage scalable and efficient deployments \[[1](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/18a720c86ed6e166c26f7d39490bfd11b93ea3ec)\]. While prior research has explored system-level optimization strategies for cost and resource efficiency in containerized systems \[[2](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/b5634b15badc73f3cda72b960fd51ef03ac1265d)\], the analysis of network costs in Kubernetes clusters remains underexplored \[[3](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/7c82fc45da1617fdd95fe1b03a2ebdaa6bae23d2)\]. This paper contributes to the emerging FinOps discipline by addressing the financial and operational aspects of managing network costs in cloud-native environments \[[4](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/0f4ac27e56efd714e3425bd9784eb76c7cd8c2a9)\].

Several studies have examined the performance and resource management capabilities of Kubernetes. For instance, \[[5](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/8eabcc47a99622184fc35b731992f10ff9f19444)\] investigates the modeling of performance and resource management in Kubernetes, highlighting the importance of efficient scheduling and resource allocation. Similarly, \[[6](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/d11e5c5c08af88ee8abb9ebaa166b1d6fd198501)\] focuses on Kubernetes' resource scheduling schemes, emphasizing the need for optimized scheduling algorithms to improve resource utilization and reduce latency. These studies provide foundational insights into the challenges of managing resources in Kubernetes clusters.

The use of Kubernetes for deploying and managing containerized applications has been widely studied. \[[7](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/da743c6e2fd08e2a04377bf7979f370753f39abe)\] discusses the benefits of using Kubernetes for automated container management, deployment, and scaling, while \[[8](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/8e853411a33651b57251f5f2bac46b774a180607)\] explores the role of Kubernetes in enabling high availability for microservice-based applications. These works underscore the importance of Kubernetes in modern cloud infrastructure and its ability to support scalable and resilient deployments.

In addition to performance and resource management, the security of Kubernetes deployments has been a focus of recent research. \[[9](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/14353c56e5a55a919fcad86b22ec0dc357171dbb)\] highlights the risks associated with misconfigured Kubernetes manifests and the need for adherence to security best practices. The paper also discusses potential exploits, such as denial of service attacks, that can arise from violations of these practices. \[[10](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/93b2353e44ec2cee51ea432602539fc096e75e0e)\] further expands on this by proposing a novel methodology for evaluating the security of Helm Charts, which are commonly used for deploying applications on Kubernetes. This work emphasizes the importance of secure deployment practices and the potential vulnerabilities that can arise from outdated or insecure configurations.

The scalability and availability of Kubernetes in edge computing environments have also been explored. \[[11](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/c5d1825555ad1c38501d67496e2cdc251d2d6de4)\] discusses the challenges of using Kubernetes for edge computing, noting that the strong consistency model of etcd can lead to increased latency and reduced throughput at larger cluster sizes. The paper proposes an eventually consistent approach to improve performance and scalability in edge environments.

Finally, the use of Kubernetes in specialized domains such as bioinformatics has been explored. \[[12](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/4d4080e1a2e26fe1ead433741602ab6eccc22b72)\] presents a case study on the use of Pachyderm, a workflow system built on Kubernetes, for managing complex data processing pipelines in bioinformatics. This work highlights the flexibility and scalability of Kubernetes in supporting advanced data science workflows.

### <a id='category-edge-computing-research'></a>Edge Computing Research: 2 papers found.
Edge computing research has increasingly focused on optimizing container orchestration systems like Kubernetes for performance-critical and dynamically scaled edge environments \[[14](http://arxiv.org/abs/2104.02423)\]. Traditional Kubernetes relies on etcd for strongly consistent cluster state management, but this leads to increased write request latency and reduced throughput at larger cluster sizes, negatively impacting availability and scalability \[[14](http://arxiv.org/abs/2104.02423)\]. To address these limitations, researchers have proposed shifting toward eventually consistent approaches, which enhance performance while maintaining the necessary functionality for Kubernetes \[[14](http://arxiv.org/abs/2104.02423)\]. Additionally, frameworks like Pachyderm have been adapted to support edge computing by integrating with Kubernetes, offering data versioning, pipeline management, and cloud-agnostic deployment capabilities \[[17](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/51e2cba5a013cbd11f2ab760a4e3906cfff44780)\]. These advancements highlight the growing need for optimized orchestration solutions that balance consistency with performance in edge environments.
