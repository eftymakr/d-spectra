## Related Works

Large language models (LLMs) have seen significant advancements in recent years, with a focus on improving their efficiency, scalability, and performance across diverse tasks. Early work on LLMs primarily focused on pre-training on large corpora and fine-tuning for specific tasks \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/2005.14165)\]. For instance, GPT-3 demonstrated strong few-shot learning capabilities, achieving competitive performance on various NLP tasks without any fine-tuning \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/2005.14165)\]. This was further extended by FLAN, which showed that instruction tuning significantly improves zero-shot performance on unseen tasks \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/2109.01652)\]. Instruction tuning involves fine-tuning models on a collection of tasks described via natural language instructions, leading to substantial improvements in performance across multiple benchmarks \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/2109.01652)\].

The training of large models also presents significant challenges, particularly in terms of computational resources and memory constraints. Megatron-LM introduced techniques for training multi-billion parameter models using model parallelism, enabling the training of models up to 8.3 billion parameters on 512 GPUs \[[4](http://arxiv.org/abs/1909.08053)\]. This work demonstrated the importance of careful architectural design and optimization to achieve state-of-the-art results on benchmark datasets \[[4](http://arxiv.org/abs/1909.08053)\].

Recent research has also explored the use of LLMs for code generation and mathematical reasoning. Codex, a GPT model fine-tuned on GitHub code, demonstrated impressive capabilities in writing Python code, solving 28.8% of problems on the HumanEval benchmark \[[10](http://arxiv.org/abs/2107.03374)\]. Similarly, GSM8K was introduced as a dataset of grade school math word problems, highlighting the limitations of current models in multi-step mathematical reasoning \[[13](http://arxiv.org/abs/2110.14168)\]. To address this, researchers proposed training verifiers to judge the correctness of model completions, significantly improving performance on GSM8K \[[13](http://arxiv.org/abs/2110.14168)\].

Instruction tuning has also been shown to enhance the performance of LLMs on a variety of tasks. WizardLM, for example, used an evolutionary instruction generation method to create complex instructions and fine-tune LLaMA, achieving performance comparable to ChatGPT on 17 out of 29 skills \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2304.12244)\]. This work demonstrated the potential of AI-evolved instructions for improving LLM performance \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2304.12244)\].

Furthermore, the development of efficient finetuning methods has enabled the training of large models on limited resources. QLoRA introduced a method for efficient finetuning of quantized LLMs, allowing the finetuning of a 65B parameter model on a single 48GB GPU while preserving full 16-bit finetuning performance \[[15](http://arxiv.org/abs/2305.14314)\]. However, manually creating such instruction data is very time-consuming and labor-intensive. Moreover, humans may struggle to produce high-complexity instructions. In this paper, we show an avenue for creating large amounts of instruction data with varying levels of complexity using LLM instead of humans. Starting with an initial set of instructions, we use our proposed Evol-Instruct to rewrite them step by step into more complex instructions. Then, we mix all generated instruction data to fine-tune LLaMA. We call the resulting model WizardLM. Human evaluations on a complexity-balanced test bed and Vicuna's testset show that instructions from Evol-Instruct are superior to human-created ones. By analyzing the human evaluation results of the high complexity part, we demonstrate that outputs from our WizardLM are preferred to outputs from OpenAI ChatGPT. In GPT-4 automatic evaluation, WizardLM achieves more than 90\% capacity of ChatGPT on 17 out of 29 skills. ### <a id='category-large-language-models-research'></a>Large Language Models Research: 8 papers found.
The category "Large Language Models Research" encompasses a range of advancements in training, fine-tuning, and evaluation of large-scale language models. Recent studies have demonstrated that scaling up language models significantly improves their few-shot and zero-shot learning capabilities, enabling them to perform well on diverse NLP tasks without explicit fine-tuning \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2304.12244)\]. For example, GPT-3, with 175 billion parameters, achieves strong performance on tasks such as translation, question-answering, and arithmetic reasoning, often rivaling or surpassing prior fine-tuning approaches \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2304.12244)\]. Instruction tuning has also been shown to enhance zero-shot performance by training models on tasks described via natural language instructions, leading to models like FLAN that outperform GPT-3 on several benchmarks \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/2109.01652)\]. Additionally, models like Llama 2, with parameters ranging from 7 billion to 70 billion, have been fine-tuned for dialogue use cases and show competitive performance against closed-source models \[[6](http://arxiv.org/abs/2307.09288)\]. The development of models like Codex, fine-tuned on code from GitHub, highlights the potential of large models in specialized domains such as programming \[[10](http://arxiv.org/abs/2107.03374)\]. Furthermore, research into tool integration, such as Toolformer, demonstrates that models can learn to use external APIs to enhance their reasoning and problem-solving abilities \[[12](http://arxiv.org/abs/2302.04761)\]. Despite these advances, challenges remain, including struggles with multi-step mathematical reasoning and the need for better verification mechanisms to ensure correctness \[[13](http://arxiv.org/abs/2110.14168)\]. These studies collectively underscore the ongoing efforts to improve the efficiency, accuracy, and versatility of large language models.

### <a id='category-others'></a>Others: 5 papers found.
The category "Others" encompasses a range of research efforts focused on advancing large language models (LLMs) through improved training methodologies, architectural innovations, and evaluation strategies. Recent work highlights the challenges of training very large transformer models, such as memory constraints and the need for efficient parallelization techniques \[[4](http://arxiv.org/abs/1909.08053)\]. Researchers have developed intra-layer model parallelism approaches that enable training models with billions of parameters using standard frameworks like PyTorch, demonstrating scalability up to 8.3 billion parameters across 512 GPUs \[[4](http://arxiv.org/abs/1909.08053)\]. Additionally, the Transformer architecture, which relies solely on attention mechanisms, has shown superior performance in sequence transduction tasks, achieving state-of-the-art results in machine translation and parsing \[[9](http://arxiv.org/abs/1706.03762)\]. The rapid evolution of LLMs has also led to systematic reviews of pre-training, post-training, utilization, and evaluation methods, emphasizing the need for new frameworks to understand their development and societal impact \[[11](http://arxiv.org/abs/2303.18223)\]. Instruction-tuning has emerged as a critical area, with studies exploring the effectiveness of various datasets in enhancing specific skills such as reasoning and multilinguality, while also highlighting the limitations of current evaluation methods \[[14](http://arxiv.org/abs/2306.04751)\]. Furthermore, recent advances in efficient fine-tuning, such as QLoRA, have enabled the training of massive models on limited hardware by using 4-bit quantization and low-rank adapters, achieving performance comparable to proprietary models \[[15](http://arxiv.org/abs/2305.14314)\]. These developments collectively underscore the ongoing efforts to improve the efficiency, performance, and practical applicability of large language models.
