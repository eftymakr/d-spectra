## Related Works

Deploying large language models (LLMs) on edge devices presents significant challenges due to limited storage, power, and computational resources \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2309.06180)\]. Prior work has focused on reducing memory usage, improving inference speed, and enabling efficient execution on resource-constrained hardware. For instance, PagedAttention and vLLM address memory fragmentation and improve throughput by efficiently managing the key-value cache \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2309.06180)\]. Similarly, AWQ introduces activation-aware weight quantization, enabling on-device deployment with up to 4x model size reduction and 3-4x acceleration \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/2306.00978)\]. These methods aim to reduce memory and computational demands, making LLMs more feasible for edge deployment.

Efforts to optimize attention mechanisms have also been crucial. ELSA proposes a hardware-software co-design solution that reduces the runtime and energy spent on self-attention by filtering out less relevant relations \[[3](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/5af69480a7ae3b571df6782a11ec4437b386a7d9)\]. FlashAttention-2 further improves attention efficiency by optimizing work partitioning and parallelism, achieving up to 2× speedup over its predecessor \[[10](http://arxiv.org/abs/2307.08691)\]. These techniques are essential for enabling efficient inference on devices with limited resources.

Quantization techniques such as SmoothQuant and OmniQuant have been developed to reduce memory and computational requirements while preserving accuracy \[[4](http://arxiv.org/abs/2211.10438)\]\[[11](http://arxiv.org/abs/2308.13137)\]. SmoothQuant enables 8-bit weight and activation quantization without accuracy loss, while OmniQuant introduces learnable components to optimize quantization parameters for diverse settings \[[11](http://arxiv.org/abs/2308.13137)\]. These methods are particularly relevant for edge deployment, where memory and power constraints are stringent.

Hardware accelerators have also played a critical role in enabling efficient LLM inference. DFX, a multi-FPGA appliance, achieves significant speedup and energy efficiency for transformer-based text generation \[[8](http://arxiv.org/abs/2209.10797)\]. Similarly, FTRANS leverages FPGA-based acceleration to reduce model size and improve performance, achieving up to 16× reduction in model size \[[16](http://arxiv.org/abs/2007.08563)\]. These hardware solutions complement software optimizations by providing dedicated hardware for LLM inference.

Inference with reference, as proposed by LLMA, further reduces computation by leveraging identical text spans between the decoding result and the reference \[[12](http://arxiv.org/abs/2304.04487)\]. This method enables lossless acceleration, achieving over 2× speedup in scenarios with significant overlap between reference and output. Similarly, Wanda introduces a pruning method that reduces model size without retraining, achieving competitive performance compared to magnitude pruning \[[18](http://arxiv.org/abs/2306.11695)\].

Overall, these works highlight the importance of combining algorithmic optimizations with hardware acceleration to enable efficient LLM deployment on edge devices. They provide a foundation for further research into scalable, energy-efficient, and accurate inference methods

[Snippet]: «High throughput serving of large language models (LLMs) requires batching sufficiently many requests at a time. However, existing systems struggle because the key-value cache (KV cache) memory for each request is huge and grows and shrinks dynamically. When managed inefficiently, this memory can be significantly wasted by fragmentation and redundant duplication, limiting the batch size. To address this problem, we propose PagedAttention, an attention algorithm inspired by the classical virtual memory and paging techniques in operating systems. On top of it, we build vLLM, an LLM serving system that achieves (1) near-zero waste in KV cache memory and (2) flexible sharing of KV cache within and across requests to further reduce memory usage. Our evaluations show that vLLM improves the throughput of popular LLMs by 2--4× with the same level of latency compared to the state-of-the-art systems, such as FasterTransformer and Orca. The improvement is more pronounced with longer sequences, larger models, and more complex decoding algorithms. vLLM's source code is publicly available at https://github.com/vllm-project/vllm.»

### <a id='category-transformer-acceleration-techniques'></a>Transformer Acceleration Techniques: 9 papers found.
The category "Transformer Acceleration Techniques" encompasses a range of approaches aimed at improving the efficiency and performance of transformer models, particularly in resource-constrained environments. These techniques focus on reducing the computational and energy costs associated with the self-attention mechanism, which is a key component of transformer models but also a major bottleneck due to its quadratic complexity \[[3](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/5af69480a7ae3b571df6782a11ec4437b386a7d9)\]. Various solutions have been proposed, including ELSA, which introduces an efficient approximation scheme to filter out less impactful relations, achieving a geomean speedup of 58.1× and significant energy savings \[[3](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/5af69480a7ae3b571df6782a11ec4437b386a7d9)\]. Similarly, Energon employs dynamic sparse attention and mix-precision filtering to reduce computation while maintaining accuracy, achieving up to 168× speedup and 10⁴× energy reduction over CPUs \[[13](http://arxiv.org/abs/2110.09310)\]. LLMA leverages reference-based optimization to speed up LLM inference by reusing identical text spans, offering over 2x speedup in scenarios with significant overlap between references and outputs \[[12](http://arxiv.org/abs/2304.04487)\]. NPE and Ftrans further explore FPGA-based solutions, with NPE achieving 4x lower power consumption than CPUs and 6x lower than GPUs for BERT, while Ftrans reduces model size by up to 16 times and improves performance and energy efficiency by 27.07× and 81×, respectively \[[15](http://arxiv.org/abs/2104.06535)\]\[[16](http://arxiv.org/abs/2007.08563)\]. SpAtten introduces token and head pruning strategies, along with quantization, to reduce DRAM access and improve speed and energy efficiency by up to 4059× compared to GPUs \[[19](http://arxiv.org/abs/2012.09852)\]. These methods collectively highlight the growing emphasis on algorithm-architecture co-design to address the challenges of deploying transformers on edge devices, ensuring both performance and energy efficiency \[[3](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/5af69480a7ae3b571df6782a11ec4437b386a7d9)\]\[[12](http://arxiv.org/abs/2304.04487)\]\[[13](http://arxiv.org/abs/2110.09310)\]\[[15](http://arxiv.org/abs/2104.06535)\]\[[16](http://arxiv.org/abs/2007.08563)\]\[[19](http://arxiv.org/abs/2012.09852)\].

The research also addresses critical challenges such as efficient scaling, model alignment, and the development of agentic reasoning capabilities, which are crucial for real-world deployment \[[22](http://arxiv.org/abs/2303.18223)\].
