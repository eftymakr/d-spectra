## Related Works

Fine-grained emotion classification in app reviews extends beyond traditional sentiment analysis, which primarily categorizes text into positive, negative, or neutral sentiments \[[1505.04657](http://arxiv.org/abs/1505.04657)\]. Capturing nuanced emotional states such as joy, anger, fear, or surprise is essential for a deeper understanding of user feedback, especially in software engineering where emotional cues can inform feature improvements and bug fixes \[[https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/695886bf2b7bf90e98e98f630b26dabcdbe78247](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/695886bf2b7bf90e98e98f630b26dabcdbe78247)\]\[[1703.02256](http://arxiv.org/abs/1703.02256)\]. Existing research highlights the value of app reviews as a rich source of user requirements and opinions, which can be mined to support various software engineering tasks including design evolution and debugging \[[https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/695886bf2b7bf90e98e98f630b26dabcdbe78247](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/695886bf2b7bf90e98e98f630b26dabcdbe78247)\]\[[https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/ff921a209cc9e1c2120669d3d81e627ca5e53572](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/ff921a209cc9e1c2120669d3d81e627ca5e53572)\].

Several datasets and annotation frameworks have been developed to facilitate emotion analysis in text, though few are tailored specifically to app reviews. The GoEmotions dataset provides a large-scale, manually annotated corpus of 58k Reddit comments labeled with 27 fine-grained emotions plus neutral, demonstrating the potential for multi-class emotion classification using transformer models such as BERT \[[2005.00547](http://arxiv.org/abs/2005.00547)\]. Similarly, EmoNet introduces a deep learning approach that achieves state-of-the-art accuracy in detecting 24 fine-grained emotions and models Plutchik’s primary emotion dimensions with even higher accuracy \[[https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/6a6f1b8ef3eefaf8027a76134859b44886a5f795](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/6a6f1b8ef3eefaf8027a76134859b44886a5f795)\]. These works underscore the importance of high-quality, fine-grained emotion annotations for effective model training and evaluation.

In the domain of app review analysis, several computational frameworks have been proposed to filter, classify, and summarize user feedback effectively. AR-Miner focuses on extracting informative reviews via topic modeling and ranking, helping developers prioritize relevant feedback \[[https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/ff921a209cc9e1c2120669d3d81e627ca5e53572](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/ff921a209cc9e1c2120669d3d81e627ca5e53572)\]. SOLAR further enhances this by integrating review helpfulness prediction with topic-sentiment modeling and multifactor ranking to summarize useful app reviews for release planning \[[2210.06235](http://arxiv.org/abs/2210.06235)\]. These tools generally focus on sentiment polarity or feature-oriented sentiment, yet they do not explicitly address the complexity of fine-grained emotion detection or the challenges in emotion annotation.

Challenges in annotating emotions in app reviews are well-documented. Annotation guidelines significantly influence the quality and utility of extracted app features and sentiments \[[1810.05187](http://arxiv.org/abs/1810.05187)\]. Iterative and carefully designed annotation processes improve consistency and reduce noise, which is crucial when dealing with subtle emotional nuances. Moreover, the complexity of emotional interpretation in natural language, compounded by figurative and metaphorical language use, limits the full automation of emotion annotation \[[2005.11882](http://arxiv.org/abs/2005.11882)\]\[[1703.02256](http://arxiv.org/abs/1703.02256)\]. Thus, manual annotation remains a gold standard, though labor-intensive.

The use of large language models (LLMs) for emotion and sentiment analysis has gained considerable attention. Studies show that LLMs perform well in simpler sentiment tasks and can reduce manual annotation efforts through few-shot learning, though they struggle with tasks requiring deep understanding or structured sentiment extraction \[[2305.15005](http://arxiv.org/abs/2305.15005)\]. Fine-tuning LLMs with human feedback, as demonstrated by models like InstructGPT, improves alignment with user intent and reduces toxic or untruthful outputs, suggesting promise for application in emotion annotation automation \[[2203.02155](http://arxiv.org/abs/2203.02155)\]. However, even with these advances, maintaining high agreement with human annotators in fine-grained emotion classification remains challenging due to the inherent subjectivity and subtlety of emotional expression \[[1505.04657](http://arxiv.org/abs/1505.04657)\]\[[2305.15005](http://arxiv.org/abs/2305.15005)\].

Traditional sentiment analysis methods, including lexical resources such as SentiWordNet, provide foundational tools for opinion mining by associating words with polarity scores and objectivity measures \[[https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/96761dc3d8adaeff44eb9c07501eb3109802ee4b](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/96761dc3d8adaeff44eb9c07501eb3109802ee4b)\]. However, these resources are limited in capturing the full spectrum of discrete emotions and their compositionality in phrases or sentences. Recursive deep models address compositionality by parsing sentiment at the phrase level, improving fine-grained sentiment detection accuracy and handling negation and scope effects \[[https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/687bac2d3320083eb4530bf18bb8f8f721477600](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/687bac2d3320083eb4530bf18bb8f8f721477600)\].

Finally, app review mining benefits from semi-automated approaches that leverage keyword extraction and active learning to reduce annotation costs while maintaining classification accuracy \[[1505.04657](http://arxiv.org/abs/1505.04657)\]\[[https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/3d1db23a8aa2eeb0cf3be91446d0296abecb8dc3](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/3d1db23a8aa2eeb0cf3be91446d0296abecb8dc3)\]. Combining topic modeling with sentiment analysis allows for feature-oriented sentiment ratings, giving developers richer insight than star ratings alone \[[https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/6fe8d604e8e19dc6ed738c8e58b7351aeeed515c](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/6fe8d604e8e19dc6ed738c8e58b7351aeeed515c)\]. Despite these advances, integrating fine-grained emotion classification into app review analysis remains an open challenge, necessitating tailored annotation frameworks, datasets, and evaluation metrics.

In summary, while broad sentiment analysis and some fine-grained emotion detection have been explored extensively in general text and social media, their application to app reviews is less mature. Existing tools and datasets provide valuable foundations, but the complexity of emotional interpretation in app reviews calls for structured annotation efforts and careful evaluation of LLM-based automation, as addressed in this study.
