## Related Works

Differential privacy (DP) has established itself as a fundamental framework for protecting individual privacy in statistical databases and data analysis \[[0803.3946](http://arxiv.org/abs/0803.3946)\]. It provides rigorous guarantees that the presence or absence of a single individual's data does not significantly affect the output of a computation, thus limiting privacy risks even in the presence of adversaries with arbitrary auxiliary information \[[0803.3946](http://arxiv.org/abs/0803.3946)\]\[[0811.2501](http://arxiv.org/abs/0811.2501)\]. Various relaxations and extensions of DP, such as approximate DP ((ε, δ)-DP) \[[0803.3946](http://arxiv.org/abs/0803.3946)\], concentrated DP \[[1605.02065](http://arxiv.org/abs/1605.02065)\]\[[1702.07476](http://arxiv.org/abs/1702.07476)\], and personalized DP \[[https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/e40f49994b0d5122e2c45ee04e38defb973a9d52](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/e40f49994b0d5122e2c45ee04e38defb973a9d52)\]\[[https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/f1ba5a27cf487335fc54c0dd38d54ccdbdade063](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/f1ba5a27cf487335fc54c0dd38d54ccdbdade063)\], have been proposed to balance privacy guarantees and utility.

Despite these theoretical strengths, practical implementations of DP mechanisms, especially in distributed or local models, face vulnerabilities due to unfaithful execution. In distributed settings, adversarial servers may sample noise incorrectly or generate correlated noise outputs, violating DP guarantees and undermining privacy \[[1909.09630](http://arxiv.org/abs/1909.09630)\]\[[1911.02046](http://arxiv.org/abs/1911.02046)\]. Local differential privacy (LDP), where users perturb data locally, strengthens trust by eliminating the need to trust a central server but introduces other challenges. LDP protocols are inherently vulnerable to manipulation by malicious clients who can skew aggregate results by sending crafted or falsified data \[[1909.09630](http://arxiv.org/abs/1909.09630)\]\[[2104.06569](http://arxiv.org/abs/2104.06569)\]\[[1911.02046](http://arxiv.org/abs/1911.02046)\]. These manipulation attacks can effectively obscure true data distributions or bias frequency estimations, highlighting critical security risks in LDP deployments \[[1909.09630](http://arxiv.org/abs/1909.09630)\]\[[1911.02046](http://arxiv.org/abs/1911.02046)\].

To mitigate such threats, verifiable differential privacy (VDP) mechanisms have been introduced, focusing on ensuring that the DP protocol is faithfully executed and that outputs are verifiably correct. A foundational approach leverages zero-knowledge proofs (ZKPs), cryptographic protocols enabling a prover to convince a verifier of correct computation without revealing sensitive data \[[2406.18940](http://arxiv.org/abs/2406.18940)\]\[[2104.06569](http://arxiv.org/abs/2104.06569)\]\[[1702.07476](http://arxiv.org/abs/1702.07476)\]. ZKP-based VDP schemes have been applied to both central and local models, enabling verification that noise addition and randomization steps comply with DP constraints \[[2104.06569](http://arxiv.org/abs/2104.06569)\]\[[1207.4305](http://arxiv.org/abs/1207.4305)\]. However, these ZKP approaches often incur significant computational and communication overhead, limiting practical scalability \[[2406.18940](http://arxiv.org/abs/2406.18940)\]\[[2104.06569](http://arxiv.org/abs/2104.06569)\].

Recent work has explored alternative cryptographic techniques for verifiable DP beyond ZKPs. For example, homomorphic encryption and secure multi-party computation (MPC) frameworks have been combined with DP to provide privacy-preserving and verifiable data aggregation protocols \[[https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/f1ba5a27cf487335fc54c0dd38d54ccdbdade063](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/f1ba5a27cf487335fc54c0dd38d54ccdbdade063)\]\[[1402.3329](http://arxiv.org/abs/1402.3329)\]. These methods offer formal verifiability guarantees but may still face efficiency challenges in large-scale or real-time settings.

In the local model, verifiable randomized response (VRR) protocols have been proposed to verify that clients correctly apply local randomization mechanisms, preserving local DP guarantees while preventing output manipulation \[[1207.4305](http://arxiv.org/abs/1207.4305)\]\[[1207.4262](http://arxiv.org/abs/1207.4262)\]. These approaches reduce the risk of malicious clients biasing the aggregated statistics, although achieving low communication and verification overhead remains an open challenge \[[2104.06569](http://arxiv.org/abs/2104.06569)\]\[[1207.4262](http://arxiv.org/abs/1207.4262)\].

More recent advances have focused on the shuffle model, which introduces an additional anonymization layer between clients and the server to amplify privacy while retaining utility \[[2406.18940](http://arxiv.org/abs/2406.18940)\]\[[1811.12469](http://arxiv.org/abs/1811.12469)\]. Verifiable DP mechanisms in the shuffle model have demonstrated improved efficiency and robustness against manipulation, balancing privacy, utility, and verifiability requirements \[[2406.18940](http://arxiv.org/abs/2406.18940)\].

Complementary to these efforts, there have been numerous studies on improving the utility and accuracy of DP mechanisms under realistic constraints. Adaptive personalized DP frameworks, such as AdaPDP, optimize noise addition based on user-specific privacy preferences and data distributions, enhancing data utility while maintaining rigorous privacy guarantees \[[https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/e40f49994b0d5122e2c45ee04e38defb973a9d52](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/e40f49994b0d5122e2c45ee04e38defb973a9d52)\]\[[https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/f1ba5a27cf487335fc54c0dd38d54ccdbdade063](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/f1ba5a27cf487335fc54c0dd38d54ccdbdade063)\]. Statistical and economic models have also been developed to guide the selection of privacy parameters (e.g., ε) balancing privacy risk and analytic accuracy \[[1402.3329](http://arxiv.org/abs/1402.3329)\]\[[2201.05964](http://arxiv.org/abs/2201.05964)\].

Finally, extensive surveys have examined the broad landscape of local differential privacy, its applications, and associated challenges, highlighting the importance of addressing both privacy guarantees and adversarial robustness in practical deployments \[[2008.03686](http://arxiv.org/abs/2008.03686)\].

The present work advances these lines of research by introducing Verifiable Distributed Differential Privacy (VDDP), a novel framework that explicitly incorporates verification mechanisms into distributed DP protocols to counter malicious server behaviors such as incorrect or correlated noise generation. Unlike prior ZKP-heavy approaches, the proposed mechanisms—including the Verifiable Distributed Discrete Laplacian Mechanism (VDDLM) and an optimized Verifiable Randomized Response (VRR) protocol—achieve substantial efficiency gains and reduced communication overhead while preserving strong verifiability and privacy guarantees, addressing critical gaps in existing verifiable DP solutions \[[2406.18940](http://arxiv.org/abs/2406.18940)\]\[[2104.06569](http://arxiv.org/abs/2104.06569)\]\[[1207.4305](http://arxiv.org/abs/1207.4305)\]\[[1207.4262](http://arxiv.org/abs/1207.4262)\]\[[2201.05964](http://arxiv.org/abs/2201.05964)\].
