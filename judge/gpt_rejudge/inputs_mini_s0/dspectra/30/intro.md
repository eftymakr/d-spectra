## Related Works

The problem of testing database transaction isolation has been extensively studied, focusing on the balance between isolation strength and system performance. It is well-established that verifying strong isolation levels, such as serializability, is NP-complete \[[1806.08416](http://arxiv.org/abs/1806.08416)\]. This computational hardness has shifted attention toward weak isolation levels, which offer more relaxed guarantees but allow polynomial-time testing, thus improving scalability \[[1806.08416](http://arxiv.org/abs/1806.08416)\]\[[2303.12606](http://arxiv.org/abs/2303.12606)\]. Despite this, existing weak isolation testers often exhibit large polynomial complexity, limiting their effectiveness on large-scale workloads typical of modern databases \[[https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/cbe191bda20907994d45bc835d20446968370add](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/cbe191bda20907994d45bc835d20446968370add)\]\[[2504.02344](http://arxiv.org/abs/2504.02344)\].

Several techniques have aimed to improve the efficiency of isolation testing. Tools like Elle leverage dependency graphs inferred from client-observed transactions to detect a broad range of anomalies, providing soundness and informative counterexamples \[[2003.10554](http://arxiv.org/abs/2003.10554)\]. Similarly, Emme introduces version certificate recovery, a white-box approach that efficiently checks histories involving predicate operations and achieves significant speedups over prior checkers like Elle \[[https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/04178561c9d8cd42904ed0a836e08e55b84703cf](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/04178561c9d8cd42904ed0a836e08e55b84703cf)\]. However, these tools either require internal database access or struggle with scalability for very large histories.

Black-box testing approaches have been developed to validate isolation guarantees without requiring database internals. Plume offers a complete, black-box checker for weak isolation levels by characterizing fine-grained transactional anomalous patterns and using vector clocks and tree clocks to accelerate checking \[[https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/cbe191bda20907994d45bc835d20446968370add](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/cbe191bda20907994d45bc835d20446968370add)\]. PolySI targets snapshot isolation with a black-box checker employing generalized polygraphs and SMT solvers, enabling sound and complete verification with practical optimizations to handle large workloads \[[2301.07313](http://arxiv.org/abs/2301.07313)\]. Viper further improves SI checking speed by introducing BC-polygraphs and heuristic pruning, supporting multiple SI variants while maintaining soundness and completeness \[[https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/9f96a48d2bc0e77004b32960495685be6c301c63](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/9f96a48d2bc0e77004b32960495685be6c301c63)\]. IsoVista enhances black-box checking by combining faithful isolation characterizations with efficient algorithms and user-friendly visualization of violations \[[https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/4f3081dbb68287f0d9d7bec3e7173e6c8ff035b9](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/4f3081dbb68287f0d9d7bec3e7173e6c8ff035b9)\].

Despite these advances, black-box checkers often face trade-offs between coverage, efficiency, and usability. To reduce overhead in both workload generation and verification, Mini-Transactions (MTs) have been proposed, which are short, semantically rich transactions that enable linear or quadratic time verification while capturing common isolation anomalies effectively. The MTC tool based on MTs demonstrates superior performance compared to randomized workload testing and state-of-the-art tools, detecting bugs across various isolation levels \[[2504.02344](http://arxiv.org/abs/2504.02344)\].

On the application side, MonkeyDB offers a mock storage system to test application robustness under weak isolation by randomly returning values from all possible reads allowed by the isolation level, providing comprehensive coverage of weak behaviors \[[https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/615ff2896b52bee2cbfd64ced324106a29b73397](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/615ff2896b52bee2cbfd64ced324106a29b73397)\]. Dynamic partial order reduction techniques further contribute to reducing the state space explored during correctness verification, offering sound and complete algorithms for popular weak isolation levels including Read Committed, Causal Consistency, and Snapshot Isolation \[[2303.12606](http://arxiv.org/abs/2303.12606)\].

Theoretical insights have also played a key role in guiding practical testing improvements. Recent work has established polynomial-time algorithms for Read Committed and Read Atomic isolation testing and linear-time algorithms for Causal Consistency under certain conditions \[[2003.10554](http://arxiv.org/abs/2003.10554)\]\[[https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/322ca3c1911230a84aab81b0f5ff8c2b543c56f7](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/322ca3c1911230a84aab81b0f5ff8c2b543c56f7)\]. These foundational results have encouraged the design of more scalable testers. Moreover, conditional lower bounds demonstrate that achieving sub-$O(n^{3/2})$ complexity for weak isolation testing between RC and CC is unlikely, emphasizing the optimality of efficient testers like AWDIT.

In summary, prior research spans from foundational complexity results and formal characterizations of isolation anomalies to practical black-box and white-box testing tools. While many state-of-the-art tools achieve soundness and completeness, their high polynomial complexity or reliance on database internals constrain their scalability. Approaches that combine efficient workload generation, compact history representations, and domain-specific optimizations have shown promise in bridging this gap, but challenges remain in testing very large, realistic workloads with minimal overhead and maximal coverage \[[https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/cbe191bda20907994d45bc835d20446968370add](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/cbe191bda20907994d45bc835d20446968370add)\]\[[https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/4f3081dbb68287f0d9d7bec3e7173e6c8ff035b9](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/4f3081dbb68287f0d9d7bec3e7173e6c8ff035b9)\]\[[2301.07313](http://arxiv.org/abs/2301.07313)\]\[[2504.02344](http://arxiv.org/abs/2504.02344)\]\[[2003.10554](http://arxiv.org/abs/2003.10554)\]\[[https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/9f96a48d2bc0e77004b32960495685be6c301c63](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/9f96a48d2bc0e77004b32960495685be6c301c63)\]\[[2303.12606](http://arxiv.org/abs/2303.12606)\]\[[https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/04178561c9d8cd42904ed0a836e08e55b84703cf](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/04178561c9d8cd42904ed0a836e08e55b84703cf)\].
