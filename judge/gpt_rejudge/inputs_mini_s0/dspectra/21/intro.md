## Related Works

Neural rendering has become a prominent approach for reconstructing 3D scenes from 2D images by learning implicit representations that encode geometry and appearance continuously across space \[[1906.01618](http://arxiv.org/abs/1906.01618)\]. These methods typically require multiple input views to optimize the scene representation, leveraging consistency across viewpoints to reduce uncertainty. However, the reconstruction quality is inherently limited by the number and diversity of input views, especially in complex or dynamic scenes where some viewpoints are occluded or missing \[[2007.11571](http://arxiv.org/abs/2007.11571)\]\[[1906.01618](http://arxiv.org/abs/1906.01618)\]. This motivates the use of data augmentation strategies to artificially increase view coverage.

Video frame interpolation (VFI) is a well-established technique aimed at synthesizing intermediate frames between consecutive video frames to increase temporal resolution and provide denser sampling of scene views. Early VFI methods relied on explicit motion estimation such as optical flow, followed by frame warping and fusion \[[1712.00080](http://arxiv.org/abs/1712.00080)\]\[[1708.01692](http://arxiv.org/abs/1708.01692)\]\[[1703.07514](http://arxiv.org/abs/1703.07514)\]. However, optical flow estimation is challenged by large displacement, occlusions, and complex motion patterns, often producing artifacts near motion boundaries \[[1712.00080](http://arxiv.org/abs/1712.00080)\]\[[1906.05928](http://arxiv.org/abs/1906.05928)\]. To overcome these limitations, deep learning approaches have integrated motion estimation and synthesis into end-to-end frameworks, improving robustness and quality \[[1712.00080](http://arxiv.org/abs/1712.00080)\]\[[2007.11762](http://arxiv.org/abs/2007.11762)\]\[[1708.01692](http://arxiv.org/abs/1708.01692)\]\[[1703.07514](http://arxiv.org/abs/1703.07514)\].

Recent advances in VFI have introduced architectures that better handle complex motion and occlusion. For example, Super SloMo jointly models bi-directional optical flow estimation and occlusion reasoning with visibility maps, enabling generation of multiple intermediate frames in one forward pass \[[1712.00080](http://arxiv.org/abs/1712.00080)\]. Similarly, multi-frame interpolation using pyramidal temporal networks and cubic motion models has been proposed to enhance temporal consistency and efficiency \[[2007.11762](http://arxiv.org/abs/2007.11762)\]. Transformer-based methods have also been introduced to capture long-range dependencies, allowing content-aware aggregation and multi-scale synthesis \[[2111.13817](http://arxiv.org/abs/2111.13817)\]. Kernel-based approaches estimate spatially adaptive convolutional filters per pixel to synthesize frames without explicit flow, improving accuracy and mitigating flow errors \[[1708.01692](http://arxiv.org/abs/1708.01692)\]\[[1703.07514](http://arxiv.org/abs/1703.07514)\]\[[2008.10680](http://arxiv.org/abs/2008.10680)\].

In addition to improving interpolation quality, some methods focus on computational efficiency and scalability to high resolutions. IM-Net formulates motion estimation as classification with a lightweight architecture, achieving real-time interpolation at HD resolutions \[[https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/089f2072c95c244f3b7f4df404562bb4afe24448](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/089f2072c95c244f3b7f4df404562bb4afe24448)\]. Splatting-based synthesis techniques have also been proposed to accelerate multi-frame interpolation while maintaining or surpassing state-of-the-art quality \[[2201.10075](http://arxiv.org/abs/2201.10075)\]. Efficient feature extraction methods reduce memory and computational overhead in 4K interpolation scenarios without relying on pretrained flow or synthesis networks \[[2211.14005](http://arxiv.org/abs/2211.14005)\].

Event-based cameras have been exploited to enhance frame interpolation by providing high temporal resolution motion cues, complementing frame-based methods to better handle non-linear motion and challenging lighting conditions \[[2203.17191](http://arxiv.org/abs/2203.17191)\]\[[https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/9c67428b034e6eb16184724c694a9cf9091bb962](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/9c67428b034e6eb16184724c694a9cf9091bb962)\]\[[2106.07286](http://arxiv.org/abs/2106.07286)\]. Weakly supervised training using events further alleviates the scarcity of high frame-rate data \[[https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/9c67428b034e6eb16184724c694a9cf9091bb962](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/9c67428b034e6eb16184724c694a9cf9091bb962)\].

In the context of neural rendering, few prior works have explored VFI as a data augmentation technique to enrich training data for 3D reconstruction. Interpolated frames can provide additional viewpoints, implicitly modeling camera motion and scene geometry, which serves as a strong prior to improve reconstruction quality, especially in dynamic scenes where certain views are missing or occluded \[[1711.07566](http://arxiv.org/abs/1711.07566)\]\[[https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/23c9b2b3d315dd81563f57bbec8fda28d53700a6](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/23c9b2b3d315dd81563f57bbec8fda28d53700a6)\]\[[1506.06825](http://arxiv.org/abs/1506.06825)\]\[[https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/d909acffb3f83eb140ac77b3b9f76c03cd995ee9](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/d909acffb3f83eb140ac77b3b9f76c03cd995ee9)\]. Methods like PS4PRO leverage diverse video datasets to train lightweight, high-quality VFI models that augment photo supervision in neural rendering pipelines, leading to enhanced reconstruction of both static and dynamic objects \[[https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/23c9b2b3d315dd81563f57bbec8fda28d53700a6](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/23c9b2b3d315dd81563f57bbec8fda28d53700a6)\]. This approach aligns with recent trends in combining implicit neural representations with learned priors for robust scene modeling \[[2203.00137](http://arxiv.org/abs/2203.00137)\]\[[1906.01618](http://arxiv.org/abs/1906.01618)\].

Moreover, meta-learning and test-time adaptation strategies have been proposed to specialize interpolation models to specific videos, improving performance without increasing model complexity \[[2004.00779](http://arxiv.org/abs/2004.00779)\]. Residue refinement and adaptive weighting mechanisms have been introduced to further reduce interpolation artifacts through improved optical flow and frame synthesis \[[https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/23c9b2b3d315dd81563f57bbec8fda28d53700a6](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/23c9b2b3d315dd81563f57bbec8fda28d53700a6)\]. Generalized deformable convolutions allow flexible motion sampling in space-time, enhancing interpolation quality in complex motion scenarios \[[2008.10680](http://arxiv.org/abs/2008.10680)\].

Complementary to VFI, learning-based view synthesis methods reconstruct novel views from sparse input images by estimating disparity and color sequentially, improving spatial resolution for light field cameras \[[1609.02974](http://arxiv.org/abs/1609.02974)\]. Neural sparse voxel fields and scene representation networks offer continuous 3D-structure-aware representations trained from posed images alone, providing powerful priors for novel view synthesis and 3D reconstruction \[[2007.11571](http://arxiv.org/abs/2007.11571)\]\[[1906.01618](http://arxiv.org/abs/1906.01618)\]. These methods benefit from augmented training data generated via VFI to overcome limited input views.

In summary, integrating video frame interpolation as a data augmentation tool in neural rendering addresses the critical challenge of limited multi-view coverage by synthesizing plausible intermediate views. Advances in deep learning-based VFI, including end-to-end flow estimation, kernel-based synthesis, temporal pyramidal networks, and transformer architectures, provide high-quality and efficient interpolation suitable for enhancing 3D reconstruction pipelines. The proposed PS4PRO model builds on these foundations by implicitly modeling camera motion and 3D geometry in a lightweight framework, enriching photo supervision and improving reconstruction performance across diverse scenes.
