## Related Works

Protein dynamics and conformational ensembles are fundamental to understanding biological function, yet traditional molecular dynamics (MD) simulations remain computationally expensive and limited by rare event sampling and long equilibration times \[[https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/2eb1ed9d4baf548391d8220c0a8a3d0be79845ed](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/2eb1ed9d4baf548391d8220c0a8a3d0be79845ed)\]. To address these challenges, recent research has focused on generative modeling approaches that efficiently learn distributions over protein conformations, enabling rapid sampling of protein structural ensembles.

Energy-based models (EBMs) have been explored to capture atomic-level protein conformations by directly modeling the energy landscape from crystallographic data without relying on handcrafted physical energy functions. Such EBMs have demonstrated performance comparable to state-of-the-art methods like Rosetta, successfully capturing key physicochemical properties relevant to protein design \[[2004.13167](http://arxiv.org/abs/2004.13167)\]. However, training EBMs can be challenging due to intractable normalization constants and optimization instabilities, for which improved contrastive divergence techniques and data augmentation have been proposed to stabilize training and enhance generation quality \[[2012.01316](http://arxiv.org/abs/2012.01316)\]\[[2101.03288](http://arxiv.org/abs/2101.03288)\].

Diffusion models, particularly those equivariant to spatial symmetries, have shown great promise in generating high-fidelity protein structures and ensembles. Approaches like ConfDiff incorporate force-guided networks that embed physical priors into SE(3) diffusion frameworks, yielding diverse yet physically plausible conformations that outperform prior diffusion-based methods by better preserving equilibrium distributions \[[2403.14088](http://arxiv.org/abs/2403.14088)\]. Similarly, EigenFold models protein structures as harmonic oscillator systems within a diffusion paradigm, enabling efficient sampling of conformational heterogeneity and uncertainty quantification \[[2304.02198](http://arxiv.org/abs/2304.02198)\]. Other diffusion-based methods such as the latent diffusion model by AIRS embed proteins into a condensed latent space with equivariant autoencoders to reduce modeling complexity while maintaining designability and diversity \[[2305.04120](http://arxiv.org/abs/2305.04120)\]. Generative models like Genie leverage equivariant denoising diffusion on oriented residue clouds to produce novel, diverse, and designable backbones, indicating strong capability in capturing the distribution of natural protein structure space \[[2301.12485](http://arxiv.org/abs/2301.12485)\].

Beyond structure-only generation, sequence-based diffusion models such as EvoDiff have expanded the design space by conditioning on evolutionary sequence data, allowing generation of proteins with disordered regions and functional motifs inaccessible to structure-only models \[[https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/f0f0b680ac74960922634e3da5cf1e6f6883c840](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/f0f0b680ac74960922634e3da5cf1e6f6883c840)\]. Protein language models like ProGen and ProGen2 have further pushed the boundaries by training on massive protein sequence datasets, enabling controllable protein generation with high fidelity and evolutionary diversity \[[2206.13517](http://arxiv.org/abs/2206.13517)\]\[[2004.03497](http://arxiv.org/abs/2004.03497)\]. These sequence models complement structure-based approaches by providing rich priors related to evolutionary constraints.

Generative modeling of protein conformational ensembles has also been advanced by flow-based and normalizing flow techniques. AlphaFlow and related SE(3) flow matching frameworks combine single-state predictors like AlphaFold with flow matching to sample diverse, sequence-conditioned conformations, effectively capturing conformational flexibility and positional distributions while converging faster than MD simulations \[[2402.04845](http://arxiv.org/abs/2402.04845)\]\[[2411.17196](http://arxiv.org/abs/2411.17196)\]\[[2310.02391](http://arxiv.org/abs/2310.02391)\]. Boltzmann generators represent another class of generative models trained to produce unbiased equilibrium samples of many-body systems by learning invertible transformations from latent Gaussian variables, enabling free-energy calculations and discovery of new conformational states without requiring predefined reaction coordinates \[[https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/2eb1ed9d4baf548391d8220c0a8a3d0be79845ed](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/2eb1ed9d4baf548391d8220c0a8a3d0be79845ed)\].

Several works highlight the importance of integrating physical knowledge into generative models to improve realism and sampling efficiency. For example, force-guided diffusion models explicitly incorporate physics-based forces to guide sampling toward equilibrium states \[[2403.14088](http://arxiv.org/abs/2403.14088)\], while energy-based alignment or calibration approaches seek to align generative outputs with energy differences from physical models to better balance conformational states. This integration addresses the known limitations of purely data-driven methods that may produce physically implausible conformations or biased ensembles.

Finally, ensemble generation methods like BioEmu demonstrate the capacity of deep generative models to produce thousands of independent samples from protein equilibrium ensembles rapidly, capturing functionally relevant conformational changes and thermodynamic properties validated by millisecond-scale MD and experiments \[[https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/8fa4f6b5232868c2e400670716540f6877068439](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/8fa4f6b5232868c2e400670716540f6877068439)\]. Similarly, idpGAN uses transformer-based generative adversarial networks trained on coarse-grained and atomistic simulations to predict conformational ensembles of intrinsically disordered peptides, showing transferability beyond training data \[[https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/21a4afa74caf78e28880b7fe4c30a09a5f9c14e3](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/21a4afa74caf78e28880b7fe4c30a09a5f9c14e3)\].

In summary, the landscape of protein conformational ensemble modeling is rapidly evolving, with energy-based and diffusion generative models at the forefront. These methods increasingly integrate physical supervision, either through force guidance, energy calibration, or structural priors, to overcome the limitations of standard generative objectives and achieve more accurate, diverse, and physically plausible protein ensemble generation \[[2402.04845](http://arxiv.org/abs/2402.04845)\]\[[https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/8fa4f6b5232868c2e400670716540f6877068439](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/8fa4f6b5232868c2e400670716540f6877068439)\]\[[2004.13167](http://arxiv.org/abs/2004.13167)\]\[[2305.04120](http://arxiv.org/abs/2305.04120)\]\[[https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/f0f0b680ac74960922634e3da5cf1e6f6883c840](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/f0f0b680ac74960922634e3da5cf1e6f6883c840)\]\[[2411.17196](http://arxiv.org/abs/2411.17196)\]\[[2304.02198](http://arxiv.org/abs/2304.02198)\]\[[https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/21a4afa74caf78e28880b7fe4c30a09a5f9c14e3](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/21a4afa74caf78e28880b7fe4c30a09a5f9c14e3)\]\[[2403.14088](http://arxiv.org/abs/2403.14088)\]\[[https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/2eb1ed9d4baf548391d8220c0a8a3d0be79845ed](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/2eb1ed9d4baf548391d8220c0a8a3d0be79845ed)\]\[[2310.02391](http://arxiv.org/abs/2310.02391)\].
