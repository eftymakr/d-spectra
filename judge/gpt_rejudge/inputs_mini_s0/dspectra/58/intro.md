## Related Works

Table discovery has gained increasing attention as the volume of tabular data repositories expands rapidly. Traditional table retrieval approaches primarily depend on matching query tables or keyword queries to identify relevant tables, which often results in large, imprecise result sets requiring extensive manual filtering. This challenge has motivated the introduction of methods that incorporate semantic and natural language (NL) understanding to enhance retrieval precision and user experience, directly motivating the new task of NL-conditional table discovery (nlcTD) proposed in this work \[[1208.4016](http://arxiv.org/abs/1208.4016)\].

Early efforts in enriching database systems with semantic capabilities have focused on latent table discovery, which aims to semantically connect unrelated tables beyond syntactic constraints of conventional query languages like SQL. This concept-driven framework enhances the ability to discover hidden relationships across disparate tables by leveraging semantic knowledge, thereby improving the informativeness and relevance of retrieved tables \[[1208.4016](http://arxiv.org/abs/1208.4016)\].

Dataset availability has been a critical enabler for progress in table discovery and related tasks. TableBank, a large-scale dataset consisting of 417K high-quality labeled tables extracted from Word and LaTeX documents, facilitates robust training and evaluation of image-based table detection and recognition techniques. TableBank’s scale and diversity help bridge the gap between synthetic or small datasets and real-world applications, underscoring the importance of comprehensive benchmarks for advancing table understanding systems \[[1903.01949](http://arxiv.org/abs/1903.01949)\].

Joint modeling of textual and tabular data representations has also emerged as a promising direction. TaBERT, a pretrained language model trained on 26 million tables and their English textual contexts, simultaneously learns representations of NL queries and structured tables. This approach improves semantic parsing tasks involving tables, such as question answering over tables and text-to-SQL translation, by enabling deeper integration of tabular structure and language context. TaBERT’s success in weakly-supervised semantic parsing highlights the benefits of unified text-table modeling for complex table-based queries \[[2005.08314](http://arxiv.org/abs/2005.08314)\].

While these works contribute valuable insights and resources, they primarily address related but distinct aspects of table understanding, such as semantic parsing, table structure recognition, and table detection in images. None explicitly target the problem of combining a query table with natural language conditions to refine table retrieval results, as formulated by the nlcTD task. Existing datasets often lack the rich annotations and diverse query types required to evaluate NL-conditional table discovery comprehensively.

Our proposed nlcTables dataset fills this gap by providing 627 diverse queries covering NL-only, union, join, and fuzzy conditions, alongside over 22,000 candidate tables and 21,200 relevance annotations. This benchmark enables rigorous assessment of table discovery methods under realistic, NL-augmented scenarios. Initial evaluations reveal significant performance gaps among state-of-the-art methods, underscoring the challenge and importance of advancing techniques specifically tailored for nlcTD.

In summary, prior research has laid foundational work in semantic table discovery, large-scale table detection, and joint text-table representation learning \[[1208.4016](http://arxiv.org/abs/1208.4016)\]\[[1903.01949](http://arxiv.org/abs/1903.01949)\]\[[2005.08314](http://arxiv.org/abs/2005.08314)\]. However, the unique demands of NL-conditional table discovery necessitate new benchmarks and methods, which our nlcTables dataset and task formulation explicitly address.
