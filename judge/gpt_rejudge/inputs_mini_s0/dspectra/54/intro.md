## Related Works

Graph Convolutional Networks (GCNs) have become a fundamental framework in collaborative filtering (CF) for recommender systems, leveraging the user-item bipartite graph structure to capture high-order connectivity and collaborative signals \[[1905.08108](http://arxiv.org/abs/1905.08108)\]. Early models like Neural Graph Collaborative Filtering (NGCF) explicitly propagate embeddings along the user-item graph to incorporate higher-order interactions, leading to improved user and item representations \[[1905.08108](http://arxiv.org/abs/1905.08108)\]. However, NGCF and similar models rely on embedding layers that map users and items to latent spaces, which may restrict the capacity to represent fine-grained local preference patterns and limit the diversity of signals extracted from raw interactions.

Addressing limitations in embedding capacity and over-smoothing, recent works have pursued simplified and refined graph convolution designs. For instance, LightGCN removes feature transformation and nonlinear activation layers from GCNs, retaining only neighborhood aggregation, which leads to a linear, more efficient model that outperforms NGCF by about 16% on average \[[2002.02126](http://arxiv.org/abs/2002.02126)\]. Similarly, LR-GCCF proposes a linear residual graph convolutional network structure that alleviates over-smoothing and facilitates training on large sparse graphs, emphasizing the importance of linearity and residual connections in CF \[[2001.10167](http://arxiv.org/abs/2001.10167)\]. RGCF further refines graph convolution by introducing symmetric normalization and embedding popularity features, enhancing expressiveness and robustness in sparse recommendation scenarios \[[2007.03383](http://arxiv.org/abs/2007.03383)\].

Beyond linear simplifications, other works focus on modeling heterogeneous and diverse user preferences. Disentangled Graph Collaborative Filtering (DGCF) disentangles user intents behind interactions by modeling multiple latent factors, yielding more interpretable and effective embeddings \[[2007.01764](http://arxiv.org/abs/2007.01764)\]. Multi-Component Graph Convolutional Collaborative Filtering (MCCF) decomposes user-item edges into multiple latent purchasing motivations, capturing complex user preferences beyond uniform edge semantics \[[1911.10699](http://arxiv.org/abs/1911.10699)\]. Furthermore, Interest-aware Message-Passing GCN (IMP-GCN) mitigates the over-smoothing problem by restricting message passing to subgraphs of users sharing similar interests, thus preventing the blending of dissimilar preferences \[[2102.10044](http://arxiv.org/abs/2102.10044)\].

Spectral methods provide a theoretical foundation for graph filtering and convolution, explaining many GCN mechanisms via spectral graph theory. Spectral Collaborative Filtering (SpectralCF) directly performs convolution in the spectral domain of user-item bipartite graphs, uncovering deep connectivity patterns and alleviating cold-start issues \[[1808.10523](http://arxiv.org/abs/1808.10523)\]. More recent studies highlight the limitations of linear graph convolutional filters in expressiveness and denoising capabilities, proposing generalized graph normalization (G2N) and individualized graph filters (IGF) to enhance spectral filtering power and generate more flexible embeddings \[[2406.08827](http://arxiv.org/abs/2406.08827)\]. Low-pass Collaborative Filters (LCF) also target noise reduction in large graphs by designing filters that remove exposure and quantization noise while preserving graph convolution effectiveness \[[2006.15516](http://arxiv.org/abs/2006.15516)\].

Chebyshev polynomial approximations have been employed for efficient and flexible graph filtering. Chebyshev filters provide a means to approximate non-linear spectral filters with lower computational cost and better localization properties compared to traditional polynomials \[[2108.05552](http://arxiv.org/abs/2108.05552)\]. These properties have been exploited in graph signal processing and image denoising tasks, demonstrating improved performance over simpler polynomial filters \[[2406.08827](http://arxiv.org/abs/2406.08827)\]. Such spectral filtering techniques inspire frameworks that go beyond linear graph convolution by enabling richer, non-linear filtering capable of capturing diverse preference patterns from the full frequency spectrum of interaction graphs.

Other notable approaches include Multi-Graph Convolution Collaborative Filtering (Multi-GCCF), which integrates multiple graphs—user-item, user-user, and item-item—to capture proximal and high-order relationships, thus enriching embeddings with diverse contextual information \[[2001.00267](http://arxiv.org/abs/2001.00267)\]. Deep item-based collaborative filtering methods leverage nonlinear neural networks to model higher-order and nonlinear item interactions, capturing complex decision-making patterns in user behavior \[[1811.04392](http://arxiv.org/abs/1811.04392)\]. Variational Autoencoders (VAEs) have also been applied to CF to model implicit feedback with probabilistic nonlinear embeddings, outperforming traditional linear factor models \[[1802.05814](http://arxiv.org/abs/1802.05814)\].

In summary, recent advancements in GCN-based CF have progressively moved toward simplifying graph convolutional operators to mitigate over-smoothing and improve scalability \[[2001.10167](http://arxiv.org/abs/2001.10167)\]\[[2002.02126](http://arxiv.org/abs/2002.02126)\]\[[2007.03383](http://arxiv.org/abs/2007.03383)\], incorporating spectral filtering perspectives for enhanced expressiveness and noise robustness \[[2406.08827](http://arxiv.org/abs/2406.08827)\]\[[2006.15516](http://arxiv.org/abs/2006.15516)\]\[[1808.10523](http://arxiv.org/abs/1808.10523)\], and modeling richer, multi-faceted user preferences via disentangled or multi-component frameworks \[[2007.01764](http://arxiv.org/abs/2007.01764)\]\[[1911.10699](http://arxiv.org/abs/1911.10699)\]. The proposed ChebyCF framework builds upon these insights by leveraging Chebyshev spectral filtering to approximate flexible nonlinear filters and utilizing raw user interaction histories rather than fixed embeddings, thereby addressing the identified bottlenecks in embedding capacity and neighborhood aggregation.
