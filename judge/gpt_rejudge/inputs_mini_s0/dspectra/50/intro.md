## Related Works

The proliferation of open-source machine learning (ML) resources, particularly models and datasets, has catalyzed advancements in information retrieval (IR) and ML research. Platforms like Hugging Face (HF) have become central hubs for sharing and collaborating on these resources, yet they often lack structured representations that facilitate advanced queries such as tracing model evolution and recommending related datasets \[[2405.13058](http://arxiv.org/abs/2405.13058)\]\[[2311.1338](http://arxiv.org/abs/2311.13380)\]. This limitation motivates the construction of knowledge graphs tailored to ML resource management.

Extensive studies have characterized the HF ecosystem, revealing its core-periphery social structure and highly skewed activity distribution, where a small fraction of models and developers dominate usage and collaboration \[[2405.13058](http://arxiv.org/abs/2405.13058)\]. These analyses underscore the challenge of organizing vast and heterogeneous resources in a way that supports effective retrieval and maintenance. Research on model evolution and maintenance on HF further highlights the dynamic nature of ML models, emphasizing the need for metadata and structured tracking of model updates, commit types, and lifecycle stages to support reuse and reproducibility \[[2311.1338](http://arxiv.org/abs/2311.13380)\]\[[2401.13177](http://arxiv.org/abs/2401.13177)\]. Such structured tracking is crucial for enabling traceability in knowledge graphs.

Carbon footprint reporting on HF models represents another dimension where structured data is sparse but increasingly important. Studies show only a small proportion of models provide emissions data, which is correlated with model size and domain, mainly natural language processing (NLP) \[[2305.11164](http://arxiv.org/abs/2305.11164)\]. The development of classification schemes for carbon efficiency and reporting practices demonstrates the potential benefits of embedding domain-specific attributes into knowledge representations to support sustainability-aware querying.

Documentation practices in HF, including dataset cards and model cards, have been systematically analyzed to understand content completeness and quality. Dataset cards often lack comprehensive sections, especially those detailing usage considerations, which hampers reproducibility and impacts user trust \[[2401.13822](http://arxiv.org/abs/2401.13822)\]\[[2502.04484](http://arxiv.org/abs/2502.04484)\]. Similarly, model documentation quantity has stagnated despite growing model uploads, creating challenges for effective selection and reuse \[[2401.13177](http://arxiv.org/abs/2401.13177)\]. These findings highlight the necessity of integrating rich textual attributes and metadata into knowledge graphs to improve discoverability and informed decision-making.

The reuse of pre-trained models (PTMs) on HF presents unique challenges related to provenance, reproducibility, and risk management \[[2303.02552](http://arxiv.org/abs/2303.02552)\]\[[2401.13177](http://arxiv.org/abs/2401.13177)\]. Empirical work has modeled PTM reuse decision processes and identified the need for attributes that capture model lineage and compatibility, which knowledge graphs can systematically encode to facilitate safe and efficient reuse.

On the platform design and empirical research front, HF has been evaluated for its suitability as a data source for empirical software engineering studies, with findings affirming its rich and diverse data amenable to large-scale analyses of AI development, maintenance, and social collaboration \[[2307.14841](http://arxiv.org/abs/2307.14841)\]\[[2402.07323](http://arxiv.org/abs/2402.07323)\]. This supports the feasibility of constructing large-scale, domain-specific knowledge graphs like HuggingKG from HF data.

Finally, benchmarking frameworks are critical for evaluating ML resources. While classic benchmarks like ImageNet have shaped computer vision research \[[1409.0575](http://arxiv.org/abs/1409.0575)\], current efforts emphasize multi-task benchmarks addressing resource recommendation, classification, and tracing tasks reflective of the open-source ecosystem’s complexity \[[2303.1758](http://arxiv.org/abs/2303.17580)\]. The integration of knowledge graphs with such benchmarks enhances task design by providing structured context and inter-resource relationships.

In summary, prior research converges on the importance of structured, comprehensive metadata and relational representations for managing ML models and datasets at scale. These insights inform the design of HuggingKG, which captures domain-specific relations and rich textual attributes to support advanced IR tasks, and HuggingBench, which leverages this structure for novel benchmarking in resource recommendation, classification, and tracing.
