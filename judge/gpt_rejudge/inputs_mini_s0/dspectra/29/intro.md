## Related Works

Maximum Inner Product Search (MIPS) is a critical problem in machine learning and information retrieval, particularly for high-dimensional data scenarios. Early work on MIPS focused on direct inner product similarity measures, but these approaches often suffered from local optima and redundant computations, limiting efficiency and scalability \[[1405.5869](http://arxiv.org/abs/1405.5869)\]. To address this, a common strategy was to reduce MIPS to Nearest Neighbor Search (NNS) under the Euclidean metric via space projection or transformation. However, such reductions frequently incur topology distortion and information loss, as the inner product does not satisfy the triangle inequality and differs fundamentally from Euclidean distance \[[1509.01469](http://arxiv.org/abs/1509.01469)\]\[[1410.5410](http://arxiv.org/abs/1410.5410)\].

The theoretical challenge of MIPS lies in bridging the gap between inner product similarity and metric-based search. Asymmetric Locality Sensitive Hashing (ALSH) pioneered this effort by introducing asymmetric transformations that convert the MIPS problem into approximate near neighbor search, enabling sublinear time algorithms with provable guarantees \[[1405.5869](http://arxiv.org/abs/1405.5869)\]\[[https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/92fb60fde7aa7c94a22571dec416d4a795548f67](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/92fb60fde7aa7c94a22571dec416d4a795548f67)\]. Subsequent improvements replaced these transformations with ones converting MIPS into approximate cosine similarity search, solved efficiently via signed random projections, thereby enhancing both theoretical bounds and empirical performance \[[1410.5410](http://arxiv.org/abs/1410.5410)\]\[[1908.08656](http://arxiv.org/abs/1908.08656)\]. Nonetheless, hashing methods can struggle with data exhibiting long-tailed norm distributions, which motivated the development of dataset partitioning schemes such as Norm-Ranging LSH to mitigate excessive normalization effects and improve query efficiency \[[1809.08782](http://arxiv.org/abs/1809.08782)\].

Quantization-based approaches have also been proposed to accelerate approximate MIPS by encoding database vectors into subspace codebooks optimized to minimize inner product quantization error. These methods avoid augmenting vectors into higher dimensions as required by some hashing schemes and can leverage training queries to further boost approximation accuracy \[[1509.01469](http://arxiv.org/abs/1509.01469)\]\[[https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/23cff8ecfc5feb4208dbb90ff3fa7a6adb214e9c](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/23cff8ecfc5feb4208dbb90ff3fa7a6adb214e9c)\]. Additive quantization techniques, such as Anisotropic Additive Quantization, extend this by incorporating score-aware loss functions that prioritize high inner product items, achieving lower approximation errors than product quantization \[[https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/23cff8ecfc5feb4208dbb90ff3fa7a6adb214e9c](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/23cff8ecfc5feb4208dbb90ff3fa7a6adb214e9c)\].

Graph-based methods have emerged as a powerful alternative paradigm for approximate MIPS, capitalizing on navigable small-world graph structures to efficiently traverse high-dimensional vector spaces. The inner-product Navigable Small World graph (ip-NSW) method exploits the norm bias intrinsic to MIPS—where vectors with larger norms tend to dominate results—to focus search on high-norm nodes, significantly speeding up retrieval while reducing unnecessary computations on smaller norm vectors \[[1909.13459](http://arxiv.org/abs/1909.13459)\]\[[https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/25454f456c514219c9d6eeb42200ebbf378db9ca](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/25454f456c514219c9d6eeb42200ebbf378db9ca)\]. Enhancements like ip-NSW+ integrate an angular proximity graph to better capture local neighborhood structure, leading to more robust performance across diverse datasets \[[1909.13459](http://arxiv.org/abs/1909.13459)\]\[[1507.05910](http://arxiv.org/abs/1507.05910)\].

Recent research has further refined graph-based indexing through norm-adjusted proximity graphs (NAPG), which adjust edge construction based on sampled data to improve the meaningfulness of graph connections for inner product retrieval \[[https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/25454f456c514219c9d6eeb42200ebbf378db9ca](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/25454f456c514219c9d6eeb42200ebbf378db9ca)\]. Other graph structures, such as Inner Product Delaunay Graphs (IPDG) and cone trees, have been proposed to transform MIPS into efficient graph search problems, demonstrating substantial speedups over naive linear search \[[https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/4d0dce74babcf393b5a4cbcb11c89ccca6a3b92b](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/4d0dce74babcf393b5a4cbcb11c89ccca6a3b92b)\]\[[1202.6101](http://arxiv.org/abs/1202.6101)\]. These graph methods emphasize the importance of data topology and metric interplay in guiding efficient traversal and candidate selection.

Budgeted MIPS settings, where computational resources are constrained, have been addressed by greedy algorithms and sampling-based methods like wedge and diamond sampling. Greedy-MIPS achieves significant speedups with strong precision guarantees by exploiting problem structure, while optimized wedge sampling algorithms provide a deterministic approach that balances speed and accuracy under tight computational budgets \[[1610.03317](http://arxiv.org/abs/1610.03317)\]\[[1908.08656](http://arxiv.org/abs/1908.08656)\].

More recent works focus on integrating metric perspectives within indexing and search algorithms to overcome the limitations of treating inner product and Euclidean metrics as mutually exclusive. For example, clustering-based approaches that employ spherical k-means have been adapted for approximate MIPS by leveraging connections between cosine similarity and inner product, enabling efficient candidate pruning and dynamic retrieval strategies \[[1507.05910](http://arxiv.org/abs/1507.05910)\]\[[2309.09013](http://arxiv.org/abs/2309.09013)\]. Similarly, probabilistic matrix factorization models have been restructured to be natively indexable, unifying inference and indexing to accelerate search without sacrificing accuracy \[[https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/386854741f828d30e25a5e01d75e3363d49cad1e](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/386854741f828d30e25a5e01d75e3363d49cad1e)\].

The nuanced interplay between inner product and Euclidean metrics motivates hybrid indexing structures that adapt search strategies based on data topology. The proposed Metric-Amphibious Graph (MAG) and Adaptive Navigation with Metric Switch (ANMS) algorithm embody this principle by dynamically switching between metrics during search to exploit their respective advantages. This approach aligns with findings that different indexing and search strategies offer distinct benefits depending on data distribution and topology, as captured by statistical indicators correlated with optimal parameter tuning \[[1909.13459](http://arxiv.org/abs/1909.13459)\]\[[https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/23cff8ecfc5feb4208dbb90ff3fa7a6adb214e9c](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/23cff8ecfc5feb4208dbb90ff3fa7a6adb214e9c)\]\[[https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/4d0dce74babcf393b5a4cbcb11c89ccca6a3b92b](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/4d0dce74babcf393b5a4cbcb11c89ccca6a3b92b)\].

In sum, MIPS research has progressed from purely inner product or Euclidean-based methods to more sophisticated frameworks that blend these metrics within hashing, quantization, graph-based, and clustering paradigms. This integration enables more adaptable, scalable, and efficient search algorithms capable of handling diverse real-world datasets with complex topologies.
