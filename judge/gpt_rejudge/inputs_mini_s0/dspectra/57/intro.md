## Related Works

Generative retrieval (GR) has emerged as a compelling alternative to traditional index-retrieve-rerank paradigms in information retrieval (IR), leveraging sequence-to-sequence models to generate document identifiers (docids) directly from queries. This paradigm encodes corpus knowledge within model parameters, enabling end-to-end retrieval without separate indices \[[2406.01197](http://arxiv.org/abs/2406.01197)\]\[[https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/5beaeee1e27007bd12967480bb9c379cebd15e6e](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/5beaeee1e27007bd12967480bb9c379cebd15e6e)\]\[[2203.00537](http://arxiv.org/abs/2203.00537)\]. Early GR methods typically utilize either text-based docids—such as titles, n-grams, or synthetic queries—or numeric-based docids derived from clustering or quantization \[[2406.01197](http://arxiv.org/abs/2406.01197)\]\[[2305.13859](http://arxiv.org/abs/2305.13859)\]\[[2305.18952](http://arxiv.org/abs/2305.18952)\]. Text-based docids benefit from semantic richness and alignment with pretrained language models, while numeric-based representations offer computational efficiency but often lack interpretability and semantic granularity \[[https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/6682a3679a17d090830635f1c420126d20eae54a](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/6682a3679a17d090830635f1c420126d20eae54a)\]\[[2305.13859](http://arxiv.org/abs/2305.13859)\]\[[2305.18952](http://arxiv.org/abs/2305.18952)\].

Despite promising results on static corpora, existing GR models largely assume fixed document collections, limiting their applicability to real-world scenarios where corpora evolve dynamically with frequent document additions and updates. Addressing this gap, recent work has focused on continual learning and adaptability of GR models in dynamic environments. CLEVER, for example, introduces a continual learning framework that incrementally updates docid encoding with low computational overhead and incorporates memory-augmented mechanisms to retain knowledge of both old and new documents, thus mitigating catastrophic forgetting \[[2308.14968](http://arxiv.org/abs/2308.14968)\]\[[2305.18952](http://arxiv.org/abs/2305.18952)\]. Empirical evidence demonstrates CLEVER’s effectiveness in maintaining retrieval performance over evolving corpora.

The choice and design of docids play a critical role in GR model generalization. Studies show that text-based docids, due to their semantic alignment with language model pretraining and lexical diversity, exhibit superior generalization to unseen documents in dynamic corpora compared to numeric-based docids, which tend to overfit to the initial training set and suffer performance degradation as the corpus changes \[[2308.14968](http://arxiv.org/abs/2308.14968)\]\[[2305.18952](http://arxiv.org/abs/2305.18952)\]. Techniques such as learning discrete document tokenizations via auto-encoding frameworks (e.g., GenRet) enable more semantically meaningful and compact docids, improving robustness and retrieval accuracy on both static and dynamic benchmarks \[[2304.04171](http://arxiv.org/abs/2304.04171)\]\[[2305.18952](http://arxiv.org/abs/2305.18952)\].

Beyond docid design, advancements in training strategies and model architectures have further enhanced GR effectiveness. Listwise generative retrieval models optimize relevance at the ranked list level rather than independently per docid, employing sequential learning processes and relevance calibration to better capture document relationships and graded relevance signals \[[2403.12499](http://arxiv.org/abs/2403.12499)\]\[[2409.18409](http://arxiv.org/abs/2409.18409)\]. Similarly, frameworks like RIPOR integrate prefix-oriented ranking optimization and relevance-aware docid construction, yielding substantial gains on large-scale benchmarks such as MS MARCO \[[2311.09134](http://arxiv.org/abs/2311.09134)\]. Term-Set Generation (TSGen) further innovates by representing docids as permutation-invariant sets of weighted terms, which increases decoding robustness and mitigates error propagation inherent in token-by-token generation \[[2305.13859](http://arxiv.org/abs/2305.13859)\].

Scalability remains a significant challenge for GR models. While GR performs competitively on small to medium corpora, scaling to millions of documents reveals limitations in model capacity and indexing strategies. Empirical studies on the MS MARCO passage corpus with millions of passages highlight the importance of synthetic queries for document representation and caution against naive parameter scaling, underscoring the need for more efficient architectures and indexing methods \[[2305.11841](http://arxiv.org/abs/2305.11841)\]\[[2311.09134](http://arxiv.org/abs/2311.09134)\].

Efforts to unify retrieval with generation in knowledge-intensive tasks have also been explored. CorpusLM proposes a unified model integrating generative retrieval, closed-book generation, and retrieval-augmented generation (RAG), leveraging ranking-oriented docid list generation and unsupervised docid understanding to enhance downstream task performance \[[2402.01176](http://arxiv.org/abs/2402.01176)\]. Similarly, few-shot indexing approaches eliminate costly retraining by prompting large language models to generate docid banks and constrain retrieval to these docids at inference, improving adaptability and efficiency on dynamic corpora \[[2408.02152](http://arxiv.org/abs/2408.02152)\].

Recent proposals seek to fully automate docid learning and retrieval in an end-to-end fashion. Auto Search Indexer (ASI) jointly optimizes semantic docid generation and document retrieval via encoder-decoder architectures, enabling automatic docid learning for existing and new documents without separate preprocessing, thus addressing the limitations of static docids and enhancing retrieval over dynamic corpora \[[2310.12455](http://arxiv.org/abs/2310.12455)\]. Neural Optimized VOcabularial (NOVO) docids offer another approach by learning interpretable n-gram based docids optimized through query denoising and retrieval tasks, which improves semantic relationships and retrieval accuracy \[[https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/6682a3679a17d090830635f1c420126d20eae54a](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/6682a3679a17d090830635f1c420126d20eae54a)\].

In summary, the landscape of generative retrieval research spans docid design, training methodologies, continual learning for dynamic corpora, and scalability considerations. The observed advantages of text-based docids—semantic alignment, fine granularity, and lexical diversity—support their superior performance in dynamic settings. Concurrently, numeric-based docids contribute efficiency but require further innovations to overcome generalization challenges. Hybrid and multi-docid strategies aim to combine the strengths of both types, advancing GR toward practical deployment in evolving real-world search systems \[[2308.14968](http://arxiv.org/abs/2308.14968)\]\[[2305.18952](http://arxiv.org/abs/2305.18952)\].
