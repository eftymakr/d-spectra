## Related Works

Multi-Domain Recommendation (MDR) has garnered significant attention as a method to leverage information across multiple domains to improve recommendation accuracy simultaneously \[[2309.06217](http://arxiv.org/abs/2309.06217)\]. Early Cross-Domain Recommendation (CDR) efforts focused on transferring knowledge from a source domain with richer data to a sparser target domain, often assuming domain-invariant user preferences or shared latent spaces \[[2103.01696](http://arxiv.org/abs/2103.01696)\]\[[1804.06769](http://arxiv.org/abs/1804.06769)\]. However, real-world domains frequently exhibit heterogeneous user behaviors, item characteristics, and distributional shifts, which challenge these assumptions and limit the effectiveness of uniform knowledge transfer \[[0902.3430](http://arxiv.org/abs/0902.3430)\]\[[1608.06019](http://arxiv.org/abs/1608.06019)\].

To address domain heterogeneity, recent MDR approaches incorporate both domain-shared and domain-specific components. For example, disentangling embeddings and alignment mechanisms have been used to separate inter-domain and intra-domain knowledge, enhancing knowledge transfer while preserving domain uniqueness \[[2308.05508](http://arxiv.org/abs/2308.05508)\]. Similarly, hierarchical hypergraph networks model multi-domain user-item interactions to capture high-order relationships and reduce domain discrepancy \[[2211.11191](http://arxiv.org/abs/2211.11191)\]. These methods aim to mitigate the adverse effects of domain conflicts and sparsity, which are central to the Negative Transfer Problem (NTP).

Another line of work tackles NTP explicitly by selectively transferring knowledge from related or similar domains. Methods such as domain-specific adapters dynamically generate parameters conditioned on domain characteristics, thereby improving flexibility and reducing interference among domains \[[2309.06217](http://arxiv.org/abs/2309.06217)\]. Frameworks that employ domain negotiation or domain regularization strategies further alleviate conflicts by balancing shared and private parameters and enforcing generalizability \[[2202.12524](http://arxiv.org/abs/2202.12524)\]. Likewise, dual-target CDR models leverage multi-source content and ratings to improve recommendation in both source and target domains concurrently, demonstrating the benefit of adaptive embedding sharing \[[https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/2b031c7170c9dccbe506f3d3a1d6104f65e835ae](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/2b031c7170c9dccbe506f3d3a1d6104f65e835ae)\].

Domain similarity or distance metrics have been introduced to measure domain relatedness and guide selective transfer. For instance, prototype-based or discrepancy distance measures have been used to quantify domain gaps and inform transfer decisions \[[0902.3430](http://arxiv.org/abs/0902.3430)\]\[[https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/e776b35d50f07998e0c4fdca6bfe1f21c364fddf](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/e776b35d50f07998e0c4fdca6bfe1f21c364fddf)\]. In multi-source domain adaptation, adversarial learning and domain aggregation networks align multiple source domains with the target by aggregating shared semantic features while preserving individual domain traits \[[2003.00820](http://arxiv.org/abs/2003.00820)\]. Such strategies are crucial for dynamically identifying beneficial domains and avoiding negative transfer from irrelevant or conflicting domains.

Graph-based approaches have also contributed by modeling user-item interactions across domains as unified graphs, enabling knowledge propagation and preference transfer. Preference Propagation GraphNet (PPGN) captures high-order graph structures to jointly optimize recommendations across domains \[[https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/24a473700894dbfab0bfd9dc3d542acfeef66bca](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/24a473700894dbfab0bfd9dc3d542acfeef66bca)\]. Similarly, domain alignment via random walks on user-item graphs enhances embedding similarity for shared users/items, facilitating cross-domain knowledge transfer \[[2308.05508](http://arxiv.org/abs/2308.05508)\].

Sequential and multi-scenario recommendation systems extend MDR by incorporating temporal and contextual dynamics. Multi-domain sequential recommendation models leverage domain-specific sequences and domain-to-domain adaptation mechanisms to supplement sparse histories and capture accurate user preferences \[[https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/756e3e6c0956d1daa19367eb13115151b20eeb7f](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/756e3e6c0956d1daa19367eb13115151b20eeb7f)\]. Prompt-enhanced paradigms have been proposed to efficiently fine-tune unified recommender backbones across multiple domains, balancing global commonality and domain distinctions with minimal parameter overhead \[[https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/884f8a29a7979a1f05223f432d9a149ac4dcc6b4](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/884f8a29a7979a1f05223f432d9a149ac4dcc6b4)\].

Transfer learning techniques underpin many of these advances, often employing neural architectures with cross-domain connections to enable dual knowledge transfer and complex interaction modeling \[[1804.06769](http://arxiv.org/abs/1804.06769)\]. Yet, the risk of negative transfer remains a persistent challenge, especially when domain conflicts are not explicitly modeled. Cooperative game-theoretic approaches have emerged to quantify negative transfer effects and dynamically weight domain contributions based on their marginal utility, thereby mitigating performance degradation \[[2311.13188](http://arxiv.org/abs/2311.13188)\].

Overall, the state-of-the-art in MDR and CDR highlights the importance of explicitly modeling domain relationships, selectively transferring knowledge based on domain similarity, and balancing shared and private components to avoid negative transfer. Despite these advances, scalable and lightweight solutions that dynamically select suitable source domains for each target remain underexplored. The proposed Similar Domain Selection Principle (SDSP) addresses this gap by introducing a prototype-based domain distance and dynamic domain selection mechanism, enabling effective and efficient alleviation of NTP in MDR scenarios.
