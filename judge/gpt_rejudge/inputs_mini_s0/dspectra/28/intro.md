## Related Works

Active learning (AL) is a well-established approach aimed at reducing annotation costs by selectively querying the most informative unlabeled samples, thereby improving model efficiency and accuracy \[[1804.08333](http://arxiv.org/abs/1804.08333)\]. Traditional AL methods, however, often struggle with limited data diversity and constrained annotation budgets, especially in distributed or large-scale settings \[[1909.06335](http://arxiv.org/abs/1909.06335)\]. Federated Active Learning (FAL) extends AL to a decentralized framework, enabling collaborative data selection and model training while preserving client data privacy \[[1909.06335](http://arxiv.org/abs/1909.06335)\]. Despite these advantages, existing FAL methods generally overlook the impact of client data heterogeneity and the resulting fluctuations in local and global models, which can significantly degrade performance \[[1910.01991](http://arxiv.org/abs/1910.01991)\].

Federated Learning (FL) itself faces challenges arising from heterogeneous data distributions across clients (non-IID data), system heterogeneity (varying client computation and communication capabilities), and limited communication bandwidth. Various strategies have been proposed to handle these issues. For example, client selection methods that dynamically choose clients based on resource availability or local loss have been shown to accelerate convergence and improve accuracy \[[1804.08333](http://arxiv.org/abs/1804.08333)\]\[[2010.01243](http://arxiv.org/abs/2010.01243)\]\[[2112.11256](http://arxiv.org/abs/2112.11256)\]. FedCS optimizes client selection under resource constraints to maximize the number of aggregated updates, thereby speeding up training in mobile edge environments \[[1804.08333](http://arxiv.org/abs/1804.08333)\]. Similarly, Power-of-Choice methods bias client selection toward those with higher local loss, achieving up to threefold faster convergence and improved test accuracy \[[2010.01243](http://arxiv.org/abs/2010.01243)\]. Adaptive client sampling further reduces wall-clock convergence time by addressing both system and statistical heterogeneity \[[2112.11256](http://arxiv.org/abs/2112.11256)\].

Data heterogeneity has also been tackled through clustering and personalization techniques. Clustered Federated Learning (CFL) groups clients with similar data distributions to train specialized models, yielding better performance than conventional FL in non-IID settings \[[1910.01991](http://arxiv.org/abs/1910.01991)\]\[[2006.04088](http://arxiv.org/abs/2006.04088)\]. Methods that keep a portion of model parameters client-specific while sharing generic parameters on the server have demonstrated improved handling of disjoint classes and multi-modal signals across clients \[[2011.06393](http://arxiv.org/abs/2011.06393)\]. Personalization approaches, such as those leveraging Model Agnostic Meta Learning (MAML), enable fast adaptation of global models to individual client distributions, improving local accuracy \[[1909.12488](http://arxiv.org/abs/1909.12488)\]. Other works address model calibration, revealing that classifier layers are disproportionately affected by data heterogeneity, and propose post-training calibration methods to improve classification performance \[[2106.05001](http://arxiv.org/abs/2106.05001)\].

In addition to addressing heterogeneity, several methods improve robustness and efficiency in FL. Techniques like SCAFFOLD use control variates to reduce client drift during local updates, enhancing convergence guarantees under data heterogeneity \[[1910.06378](http://arxiv.org/abs/1910.06378)\]. Communication-efficient FL algorithms combine partial asynchrony and compression to maintain convergence while reducing communication overhead \[[2206.10032](http://arxiv.org/abs/2206.10032)\]. Robust federated learning frameworks handle noisy and heterogeneous client data by aligning model feedback using public data, applying noise-tolerant loss functions, and weighting clients adaptively based on confidence \[[https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/1ace65d958cd27ae9ad857c49d8caa9aa5740526](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/1ace65d958cd27ae9ad857c49d8caa9aa5740526)\]. Frameworks like FLAME focus on multi-device environments, addressing both user and device heterogeneity with device selection and model personalization strategies \[[2202.08922](http://arxiv.org/abs/2202.08922)\].

Regarding the integration of active learning within FL, the literature is more limited but growing. Some works propose client selection strategies that consider data heterogeneity and loss correlations to improve FL convergence and personalization but do not explicitly incorporate active learning sample selection \[[https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/cc8379abcda8faa78e2c5e17deb96785ea447461](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/cc8379abcda8faa78e2c5e17deb96785ea447461)\]. Other approaches combine FL with data synthesis using generative adversarial networks (GANs) to alleviate non-IID issues by sharing synthetic data and pseudo labels, thereby enhancing consistency and model performance \[[2206.05507](http://arxiv.org/abs/2206.05507)\]. Federated semi-supervised learning methods introduce inter-client consistency losses to exploit unlabeled data distributed across clients, improving performance when labeled data are scarce \[[2006.12097](http://arxiv.org/abs/2006.12097)\].

Despite these advances, a key gap remains in effectively identifying the most informative samples for annotation in FAL, particularly under client heterogeneity and fluctuating model states. Current FAL methods often do not account for epistemic uncertainty variations across clients or dynamically calibrate decision boundaries to improve data selection. The proposed CHASe method addresses these challenges by tracking epistemic variations through inference inconsistency analysis, calibrating inaccurate models with alignment loss, and employing efficient data freeze/awaken mechanisms to optimize sample selection. This focus on epistemic uncertainty and model calibration to enhance FAL under heterogeneous federations represents a novel contribution beyond existing FL and FAL approaches \[[2006.12097](http://arxiv.org/abs/2006.12097)\].

In summary, while numerous works have tackled data and system heterogeneity, client selection, personalization, and robustness in federated learning, and some have explored active learning integration, no existing methods comprehensively address epistemic variation tracking and calibrated sample selection tailored for federated active learning. CHASe fills this gap by combining these aspects to improve both effectiveness and efficiency in heterogeneous federated settings.
