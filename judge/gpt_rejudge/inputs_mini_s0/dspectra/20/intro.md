## Related Works

Deep-unrolling and plug-and-play (PnP) methods have emerged as leading frameworks for solving inverse problems in imaging, including single-pixel imaging (SPI). These approaches synergize model-based optimization and deep learning, aiming to leverage the physical forward model while exploiting learned image priors for improved reconstruction.

PnP algorithms integrate advanced denoisers as implicit priors within iterative optimization schemes, providing flexibility to handle varying compression ratios without retraining. By plugging a pre-trained denoiser into a proximal gradient or ADMM framework, these methods achieve competitive performance with adaptability to different measurement conditions \[[1809.04693](http://arxiv.org/abs/1809.04693)\]\[[1704.03488](http://arxiv.org/abs/1704.03488)\]\[[2012.05703](http://arxiv.org/abs/2012.05703)\]\[[2410.02423](http://arxiv.org/abs/2410.02423)\]. However, PnP methods often encounter limitations in reconstruction accuracy and computational efficiency due to the decoupled nature of denoiser and data fidelity steps, and their reliance on heuristic parameter tuning \[[2109.14025](http://arxiv.org/abs/2109.14025)\]\[[2012.05703](http://arxiv.org/abs/2012.05703)\].

In contrast, deep algorithm unrolling transforms iterative optimization algorithms—such as half-quadratic splitting (HQS), ADMM, or proximal gradient methods—into multi-stage neural networks whose parameters are learned end-to-end from data. This approach bridges classical iterative solvers and deep networks by interpreting each iteration as a network layer, thus inheriting interpretability and convergence guarantees from the underlying algorithm while accelerating inference \[[1912.10557](http://arxiv.org/abs/1912.10557)\]\[[1902.05399](http://arxiv.org/abs/1902.05399)\]\[[1712.02862](http://arxiv.org/abs/1712.02862)\]\[[2109.14025](http://arxiv.org/abs/2109.14025)\]\[[https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/b954a94af132294a4a4e2efce15027758a535742](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/b954a94af132294a4a4e2efce15027758a535742)\]\[[1902.03493](http://arxiv.org/abs/1902.03493)\]\[[2108.06637](http://arxiv.org/abs/2108.06637)\]\[[1705.08041](http://arxiv.org/abs/1705.08041)\]. Unrolled networks often yield superior reconstruction accuracy and faster execution compared to PnP, as the learned parameters adapt to the specific inverse problem and data distribution. However, a major drawback is their sensitivity to measurement setups, requiring retraining or fine-tuning when compression ratios or forward models change, limiting their generalizability \[[2109.14025](http://arxiv.org/abs/2109.14025)\]\[[2107.11007](http://arxiv.org/abs/2107.11007)\].

Recent advances have sought to combine the flexibility of PnP methods with the accuracy and speed of unrolling. For instance, dynamic proximal unrolling networks incorporate a dynamic proximal mapping module whose parameters can be adjusted at inference time to adapt to varying sampling ratios and noise levels without retraining \[[2107.11007](http://arxiv.org/abs/2107.11007)\]. Similarly, training loss functions that encourage learned denoisers to approximate true proximal operators have been proposed, leading to proximal unrolling networks that inherit PnP flexibility while maintaining unrolling accuracy and efficiency \[[2109.14025](http://arxiv.org/abs/2109.14025)\].

The use of HQS and ADMM as unrolling backbones is widespread due to their clear splitting of data fidelity and regularization subproblems \[[1712.02862](http://arxiv.org/abs/1712.02862)\]\[[2109.14025](http://arxiv.org/abs/2109.14025)\]\[[https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/b954a94af132294a4a4e2efce15027758a535742](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/b954a94af132294a4a4e2efce15027758a535742)\]\[[1902.03493](http://arxiv.org/abs/1902.03493)\]. Networks based on these frameworks benefit from explicit incorporation of the forward model, reduced network complexity, and improved interpretability compared to black-box deep networks. These properties enable smaller networks with fewer parameters, reducing the demand for large training datasets and mitigating overfitting risks \[[1712.02862](http://arxiv.org/abs/1712.02862)\]\[[2108.06637](http://arxiv.org/abs/2108.06637)\]. Moreover, unrolled networks can be trained with weight sharing across iterations and can include numerical optimization blocks (e.g., conjugate gradient steps) to enforce data consistency efficiently \[[1712.02862](http://arxiv.org/abs/1712.02862)\]\[[1705.08041](http://arxiv.org/abs/1705.08041)\].

Unrolling has been successfully applied in diverse imaging domains, including blind image deblurring, magnetic resonance imaging (MRI), computed tomography (CT), and biological imaging, demonstrating state-of-the-art performance while providing uncertainty quantification capabilities for safety-critical applications \[[1902.05399](http://arxiv.org/abs/1902.05399)\]\[[https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/b954a94af132294a4a4e2efce15027758a535742](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/b954a94af132294a4a4e2efce15027758a535742)\]\[[2207.00698](http://arxiv.org/abs/2207.00698)\]\[[2108.06637](http://arxiv.org/abs/2108.06637)\]\[[2201.09375](http://arxiv.org/abs/2201.09375)\]\[[1709.01809](http://arxiv.org/abs/1709.01809)\]. Notably, unrolling models have been shown to outperform traditional iterative methods and direct deep learning approaches by exploiting the physical imaging model and learned priors jointly \[[1705.08041](http://arxiv.org/abs/1705.08041)\]\[[1704.06625](http://arxiv.org/abs/1704.06625)\]\[[1704.03264](http://arxiv.org/abs/1704.03264)\].

On the other hand, PnP frameworks have evolved to include tuning-free proximal algorithms that automatically select internal parameters via reinforcement learning, improving robustness and efficiency across varying imaging scenarios \[[2012.05703](http://arxiv.org/abs/2012.05703)\]. Recent developments also integrate generative models such as flow matching into PnP algorithms to enhance image restoration tasks like denoising, super-resolution, and inpainting, pushing the limits of PnP beyond classical denoiser priors \[[2410.02423](http://arxiv.org/abs/2410.02423)\].

The challenge of adapting unrolled networks to varying compression ratios is particularly relevant in SPI, where flexible, high-quality reconstruction is critical. Existing PnP methods excel in flexibility but are often slower and less accurate, whereas unrolling methods excel in speed and accuracy but lack adaptability \[[2109.14025](http://arxiv.org/abs/2109.14025)\]. The proposed proximal trajectory (PT) loss and efficient deep image restorer (DIR) network build upon these foundations to achieve both adaptability and performance, addressing a key limitation in current unrolling approaches.

In summary, the literature reveals a rich interplay between PnP and unrolling approaches. PnP offers flexibility with plug-in denoisers and scalable algorithms but can suffer in accuracy and speed. Deep unrolling provides interpretable, efficient, and high-performing networks tied closely to iterative solvers but struggles with adaptability to different compression ratios. Recent innovations focus on hybridizing these strengths through dynamic networks, proximal operator learning, and novel training losses, paving the way for flexible and efficient SPI reconstruction.
