## Related Works

Understanding representation dynamics across layers in large language models (LLMs) is crucial for improving their interpretability, robustness, and downstream adaptability. Prior work has largely focused on data-dependent analyses of fine-tuned models to identify layer-wise functional specialization, which restricts their applicability to post-hoc evaluation settings and specific tasks \[[1802.05365](http://arxiv.org/abs/1802.05365)\]\[[2109.01652](http://arxiv.org/abs/2109.01652)\]. Data-oblivious methods, such as Centered Kernel Alignment (CKA), have recently gained traction as tools to analyze intrinsic representation similarities and shifts without relying on labeled data, enabling the study of pre-fine-tuned models \[[1409.1556](http://arxiv.org/abs/1409.1556)\]\[[2307.10169](http://arxiv.org/abs/2307.10169)\]\[[2402.16363](http://arxiv.org/abs/2402.16363)\]. CKA has proven effective in revealing how layers evolve during training and how these changes correlate with behavioral shifts, providing a principled way to assess layer-wise representation changes \[[2109.01652](http://arxiv.org/abs/2109.01652)\]\[[2308.01825](http://arxiv.org/abs/2308.01825)\].

The concept of critical layers—those layers exhibiting significant representational shifts and sensitivity to fine-tuning—has been emphasized in several studies. These layers often correspond to semantic transitions within the model, such as moving from rationale generation to conclusion formation, and tend to dominate the model’s adaptation capacity \[[2305.1829](http://arxiv.org/abs/2305.18290)\]\[[2402.11651](http://arxiv.org/abs/2402.11651)\]\[[2309.12284](http://arxiv.org/abs/2309.12284)\]. Empirical evidence suggests that fine-tuning efforts concentrated on these critical layers yield disproportionately greater improvements in task performance, highlighting their importance for efficient domain adaptation \[[2012.13255](http://arxiv.org/abs/2012.13255)\]\[[2308.07633](http://arxiv.org/abs/2308.07633)\]. Conversely, freezing critical layers has been shown to mitigate adversarial influences such as backdoor attacks, reducing attack success rates substantially, thus enhancing model security \[[2002.05709](http://arxiv.org/abs/2002.05709)\]\[[1512.03385](http://arxiv.org/abs/1512.03385)\].

Spectral analysis of representation changes further elucidates the role of principal components in encoding semantic information within LLMs. Changes in top principal components often drive the most significant representational shifts across layers, marking semantic transitions that are consistent across tasks and model architectures \[[2309.12284](http://arxiv.org/abs/2309.12284)\]\[[2303.18223](http://arxiv.org/abs/2303.18223)\]. This spectral perspective aligns with findings from related work on mathematical reasoning and chain-of-thought fine-tuning, where augmented reasoning paths and alignment fine-tuning improve performance by emphasizing meaningful representational changes \[[2309.02144](http://arxiv.org/abs/2309.02144)\]\[[2308.01825](http://arxiv.org/abs/2308.01825)\]\[[2402.11651](http://arxiv.org/abs/2402.11651)\].

Related research has also explored fine-tuning dynamics from a broader perspective. Studies on scaling laws indicate that the amount of fine-tuning data interacts with model size and capacity to influence learning efficiency, with larger models showing distinct patterns in intrinsic dimensionality and parameter efficiency \[[2012.13255](http://arxiv.org/abs/2012.13255)\]\[[2402.17193](http://arxiv.org/abs/2402.17193)\]. Parameter-efficient transfer learning approaches, including prompt tuning and LoRA, offer alternative mechanisms to full-model fine-tuning, although their effectiveness varies with task and data scale \[[2110.04366](http://arxiv.org/abs/2110.04366)\]\[[2402.17193](http://arxiv.org/abs/2402.17193)\]. Moreover, recent advances in preference-oriented fine-tuning methods like Direct Preference Optimization (DPO) and ranking-based alignment integrate human feedback to further refine model behavior and reasoning abilities, underscoring the importance of targeted fine-tuning strategies \[[2305.1829](http://arxiv.org/abs/2305.18290)\]\[[2309.02144](http://arxiv.org/abs/2309.02144)\].

Model evaluation and interpretability remain central challenges, particularly given known biases in LLM-based evaluation frameworks. Calibration approaches combining multiple evidence and human-in-the-loop strategies have been proposed to mitigate evaluation biases, fostering more reliable assessment of model performance and interpretability \[[2305.17926](http://arxiv.org/abs/2305.17926)\]\[[2303.18223](http://arxiv.org/abs/2303.18223)\]. Such evaluation improvements complement layer-wise representation analyses by providing more trustworthy feedback for model tuning.

Finally, the integration of negative examples and failure trajectories during fine-tuning has emerged as a promising avenue to improve agent-like behaviors and reasoning robustness, suggesting that both successful and unsuccessful representations contribute valuable learning signals \[[2402.11651](http://arxiv.org/abs/2402.11651)\]. This insight supports the notion that understanding intrinsic layer dynamics—beyond merely successful outcomes—is essential for advancing LLM fine-tuning and adaptation.

In summary, our work builds upon and extends these lines of research by employing a data-oblivious CKA-based framework to identify intrinsic critical layers in pre-fine-tuned LLMs, linking representation shifts to fine-tuning sensitivity, spectral semantic transitions, and practical applications in domain adaptation and security.
