## Related Works

Fund allocation in financial contexts critically depends on accurate and reliable time series forecasting. Traditional approaches, such as prediction-only or Predict-then-Optimize (PnO), often suffer from goal misalignment, where forecasting accuracy does not directly translate to optimal allocation decisions \[[1703.07015](http://arxiv.org/abs/1703.07015)\]. This discrepancy is aggravated by the inherent uncertainty in state-of-the-art forecasting models, which can propagate errors and reduce allocation robustness \[[2403.07815](http://arxiv.org/abs/2403.07815)\].

The Predict-then-Optimize framework typically treats forecasting and optimization as separate stages, which can lead to suboptimal outcomes due to their decoupled objectives \[[2310.06625](http://arxiv.org/abs/2310.06625)\]. To overcome this, recent research has explored end-to-end training methodologies that jointly optimize forecasting and allocation objectives, thereby aligning the model’s predictions with downstream decision-making goals \[[1907.00235](http://arxiv.org/abs/1907.00235)\]\[[1912.09363](http://arxiv.org/abs/1912.09363)\]. However, many such approaches assume specific forecasting architectures, limiting their flexibility and applicability across diverse models \[[1809.04206](http://arxiv.org/abs/1809.04206)\].

Uncertainty quantification is another vital aspect in financial forecasting and allocation, as decisions must account for the risks associated with prediction errors. Approaches such as Monte Carlo dropout and Bayesian neural networks estimate forecasting uncertainty but often incur significant computational costs and may not generalize well across different models \[[1704.04110](http://arxiv.org/abs/1704.04110)\]\[[2211.14730](http://arxiv.org/abs/2211.14730)\]\[[2205.14415](http://arxiv.org/abs/2205.14415)\]. More recent methods, including conformal prediction and probabilistic forecasting frameworks like DeepAR, have shown promise in providing calibrated uncertainty estimates that can enhance decision-making under uncertainty \[[1704.04110](http://arxiv.org/abs/1704.04110)\]\[[2307.11494](http://arxiv.org/abs/2307.11494)\]\[[1711.11053](http://arxiv.org/abs/1711.11053)\].

Deep learning architectures remain the predominant choice for time series forecasting in finance, with various models developed to capture complex temporal dependencies and inter-series correlations. Convolutional and recurrent neural networks have been combined to extract both short-term local dependencies and long-term trends effectively \[[1703.07015](http://arxiv.org/abs/1703.07015)\]\[[1809.04206](http://arxiv.org/abs/1809.04206)\]. Transformer-based models have gained prominence due to their global attention mechanisms, yet they face challenges such as memory bottlenecks and sensitivity to non-stationarity in financial data \[[2310.06625](http://arxiv.org/abs/2310.06625)\]\[[1907.00235](http://arxiv.org/abs/1907.00235)\]\[[2205.14415](http://arxiv.org/abs/2205.14415)\]. Innovations like the iTransformer invert the attention mechanism to better capture multivariate correlations and improve scalability \[[2310.06625](http://arxiv.org/abs/2310.06625)\], while frameworks such as Non-stationary Transformers address the degradation in performance caused by distribution shifts in real-world financial time series \[[2205.14415](http://arxiv.org/abs/2205.14415)\]. Additionally, patching techniques and channel independence strategies have been proposed to reduce computational complexity and enhance long-term forecasting accuracy in Transformer models \[[2211.14730](http://arxiv.org/abs/2211.14730)\].

Representation learning methods have also been applied to disentangle seasonal and trend components in time series, improving forecasting robustness and interpretability—particularly important in financial applications where understanding underlying patterns is critical \[[2202.01575](http://arxiv.org/abs/2202.01575)\]. Graph neural networks further extend these capabilities by modeling inter-series correlations in the spectral domain, capturing complex relationships among multiple financial assets \[[2103.07719](http://arxiv.org/abs/2103.07719)\].

Probabilistic forecasting models such as DeepAR and diffusion-based models like TSDiff integrate uncertainty modeling with forecasting, enabling risk-aware predictions that can be leveraged for more informed fund allocation decisions \[[1704.04110](http://arxiv.org/abs/1704.04110)\]\[[2307.11494](http://arxiv.org/abs/2307.11494)\]. These models facilitate downstream tasks by producing predictive distributions rather than point estimates, aligning with the risk-aware objectives in financial allocations.

Recent advances also explore the use of large pretrained models and foundation models originally developed for natural language processing or computer vision. These models, including frozen pretrained Transformers and large language models (LLMs), have demonstrated strong zero-shot and few-shot capabilities for time series forecasting and financial data analysis, exhibiting an ability to reason over multi-modal inputs such as news and metadata alongside price series \[[2403.07815](http://arxiv.org/abs/2403.07815)\]\[[2306.11025](http://arxiv.org/abs/2306.11025)\]\[[2302.11939](http://arxiv.org/abs/2302.11939)\]. While promising, integrating these large models into allocation frameworks remains an emerging area of research.

In summary, existing literature reveals three main challenges addressed by the proposed RTS-PnO framework: (i) the misalignment between forecasting and allocation objectives characteristic of traditional PnO methods, (ii) the need for uncertainty calibration adaptable to arbitrary forecasting models, and (iii) the requirement for model-agnostic, flexible frameworks capable of leveraging advanced forecasting architectures. The RTS-PnO framework’s end-to-end training with objective alignment, adaptive uncertainty calibration, and model agnosticism builds upon and extends these prior efforts to deliver improved fund allocation performance in financial settings \[[1703.07015](http://arxiv.org/abs/1703.07015)\]\[[2403.07815](http://arxiv.org/abs/2403.07815)\]\[[2310.06625](http://arxiv.org/abs/2310.06625)\]\[[1907.00235](http://arxiv.org/abs/1907.00235)\]\[[1912.09363](http://arxiv.org/abs/1912.09363)\]\[[1809.04206](http://arxiv.org/abs/1809.04206)\]\[[1704.04110](http://arxiv.org/abs/1704.04110)\]\[[2211.14730](http://arxiv.org/abs/2211.14730)\]\[[2205.14415](http://arxiv.org/abs/2205.14415)\]\[[2307.11494](http://arxiv.org/abs/2307.11494)\]\[[2202.01575](http://arxiv.org/abs/2202.01575)\]\[[2306.11025](http://arxiv.org/abs/2306.11025)\].
