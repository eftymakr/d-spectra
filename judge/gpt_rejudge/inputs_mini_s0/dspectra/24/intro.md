## Related Works

Diffusion models have become a dominant approach in generative modeling, excelling in image and video synthesis tasks due to their high sample quality and flexibility \[[2105.05233](http://arxiv.org/abs/2105.05233)\]. Diffusion Transformers (DiTs), which replace traditional U-Net backbones with transformer architectures, have shown significant improvements in image and video generation capabilities, benefiting from the scalability and modeling power of transformers \[[2405.16005](http://arxiv.org/abs/2405.16005)\]\[[2406.17343](http://arxiv.org/abs/2406.17343)\]\[[2405.19751](http://arxiv.org/abs/2405.19751)\]. Despite these advances, the large parameter counts and intensive computations of DiTs pose severe challenges for deployment, especially on resource-constrained edge devices, motivating research into effective model compression and acceleration techniques such as quantization.

### Quantization of Diffusion Models

Post-training quantization (PTQ) is a widely adopted method to reduce the computational complexity and memory footprint of diffusion models by lowering the bit-width of weights and activations without retraining \[[2305.10657](http://arxiv.org/abs/2305.10657)\]\[[2302.04304](http://arxiv.org/abs/2302.04304)\]. However, naive PTQ approaches often degrade the quality of diffusion models due to the highly iterative denoising process and the temporal dynamics of activations across timesteps. Several works have addressed this by tailoring PTQ to the unique characteristics of diffusion models. For example, Q-Diffusion proposes timestep-aware calibration and split shortcut quantization to handle the evolving distributions of noise estimators over time, enabling 4-bit quantization with minimal quality loss \[[2302.04304](http://arxiv.org/abs/2302.04304)\]. PTQD further disentangles quantization noise into correlated and uncorrelated components and incorporates variance schedule calibration alongside mixed-precision strategies to mitigate error accumulation during sampling \[[2305.10657](http://arxiv.org/abs/2305.10657)\].

For vision transformers, which underpin DiTs, specific quantization challenges arise from inter-channel variability and the non-uniform distribution of activations. Methods like PTQ4DiT and Q-DiT introduce channel-wise salience balancing and dynamic activation quantization to address these issues, achieving effective 8-bit and even 4-bit quantization with preserved generation fidelity \[[2405.16005](http://arxiv.org/abs/2405.16005)\]\[[2406.17343](http://arxiv.org/abs/2406.17343)\]. HQ-DiT advances this further using hybrid 4-bit floating-point quantization and clipping range selection to minimize quantization error, realizing extremely low-precision inference with negligible impact on output quality \[[2405.19751](http://arxiv.org/abs/2405.19751)\].

### Quantization for Video Diffusion Transformers

Extending quantization techniques from image to video diffusion models introduces additional complexities. Video generation models must maintain temporal consistency and capture spatiotemporal correlations, which are not directly addressed by image-based quantization schemes. Video diffusion transformers process multi-frame inputs with temporal features that exhibit pronounced skewness and inter-channel asymmetries, complicating quantization level coverage \[[2406.0254](http://arxiv.org/abs/2406.02540)\]\[[2407.11585](http://arxiv.org/abs/2407.11585)\]. To overcome these challenges, QVD introduces High Temporal Discriminability Quantization (HTDQ) to preserve discriminative temporal features and Scattered Channel Range Integration (SCRI) to improve quantization level coverage across channels, achieving near-lossless performance on W8A8 settings and outperforming prior methods significantly on video fidelity metrics \[[2407.11585](http://arxiv.org/abs/2407.11585)\]. Similarly, ViDiT-Q designs an improved quantization scheme for DiT models applied to both image and video tasks, achieving 4- and 8-bit quantization with negligible degradation and practical speedups \[[2406.0254](http://arxiv.org/abs/2406.02540)\].

### Temporal Consistency and Optimization in Video Generation

Maintaining temporal coherence is a critical challenge for video diffusion models. Recent video generation frameworks employ architectural and training strategies to better model spatiotemporal dependencies. For instance, Lumiere uses a space-time U-Net architecture that synthesizes entire video sequences in a single forward pass, leveraging pretrained text-to-image models and multi-scale temporal processing to enhance consistency \[[2310.0327](http://arxiv.org/abs/2310.03270)\]. W.A.L.T employs a windowed attention mechanism optimized for spatial and temporal joint modeling, enabling photorealistic video synthesis with improved fidelity and coherence \[[2407.11585](http://arxiv.org/abs/2407.11585)\]\[[2310.0327](http://arxiv.org/abs/2310.03270)\]\[[2312.00845](http://arxiv.org/abs/2312.00845)\].

In the quantization context, approaches like Temporal Dynamic Quantization adapt quantization intervals dynamically based on timestep information, improving output quality without added inference overhead \[[2306.02316](http://arxiv.org/abs/2306.02316)\]. Moreover, knowledge distillation and fine-tuning methods such as EfficientDM incorporate quantization-aware training with parameter-efficient adapters and temporal learned step-size quantization to optimize low-bit diffusion models, balancing efficiency and generation fidelity \[[2310.0327](http://arxiv.org/abs/2310.03270)\].

### Summary and Positioning of Q-VDiT

Prior research has established foundational techniques for quantizing diffusion models and transformers, including adaptations for video generation and temporal dynamics. However, many existing methods focus primarily on image generation or fail to fully address the temporal dimension and token-level quantization granularity critical to video DiTs. Our Q-VDiT framework advances this frontier by proposing the Token-aware Quantization Estimator (TQE), which compensates quantization errors along both token and feature dimensions, and Temporal Maintenance Distillation (TMD), which preserves spatiotemporal correlations through optimization aligned with overall video context. This comprehensive approach enables effective low-bit quantization (W3A6) of video DiTs, setting a new benchmark in scene consistency and outperforming prior quantization methods by a significant margin \[[2407.11585](http://arxiv.org/abs/2407.11585)\]\[[2406.17343](http://arxiv.org/abs/2406.17343)\].
