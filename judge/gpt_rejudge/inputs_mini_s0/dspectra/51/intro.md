## Related Works

Federated Learning (FL) has emerged as a prominent distributed machine learning paradigm that enables collaborative model training while preserving user privacy by keeping data decentralized on client devices \[[2310.20448](http://arxiv.org/abs/2310.20448)\]\[[1511.03575](http://arxiv.org/abs/1511.03575)\]. FL’s decentralized nature inherently supports privacy, but it also introduces unique challenges when enforcing the “right to be forgotten” (RTBF), which mandates that users can request deletion of their data and its influence from trained models \[[2310.20448](http://arxiv.org/abs/2310.20448)\]\[[2401.05146](http://arxiv.org/abs/2401.05146)\]\[[2012.13891](http://arxiv.org/abs/2012.13891)\]. This requirement has led to the development of Federated Unlearning (FU), aiming to efficiently remove a client’s or specific data’s impact from a global model without costly retraining from scratch \[[1910.01991](http://arxiv.org/abs/1910.01991)\]\[[2012.13891](http://arxiv.org/abs/2012.13891)\]\[[https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/eadeffdec9fac8fd7f9aea732ca410eb082b7dcf](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/eadeffdec9fac8fd7f9aea732ca410eb082b7dcf)\].

Several FU methodologies have been proposed to address the unique constraints of FL. A common baseline is retraining from scratch after data removal, but this approach is often computationally prohibitive \[[https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/39da2d48f2132b919405e3bb6df6ece44ac6e15a](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/39da2d48f2132b919405e3bb6df6ece44ac6e15a)\]\[[2203.07320](http://arxiv.org/abs/2203.07320)\]\[[2012.13891](http://arxiv.org/abs/2012.13891)\]. Alternative approaches leverage stored historical updates or gradients to reconstruct an unlearned model more efficiently. For example, FedEraser reconstructs the unlearned global model by calibrating retained client updates, achieving up to a 4× speed-up compared to retraining \[[https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/eadeffdec9fac8fd7f9aea732ca410eb082b7dcf](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/eadeffdec9fac8fd7f9aea732ca410eb082b7dcf)\]\[[2012.13891](http://arxiv.org/abs/2012.13891)\]. Similarly, local unlearning combined with limited federated rounds, as proposed in \[[2207.05521](http://arxiv.org/abs/2207.05521)\], achieves comparable model performance with significantly lower overhead without requiring access to original data or full update histories.

Beyond efficiency, FU techniques also address security and privacy concerns. Recent studies highlight information leakage risks inherent in unlearning processes, where discrepancies between pre- and post-unlearning models may allow adversaries to infer sensitive client data \[[https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/a52c3ec7325e81bd87fdf8d74bf9acfd1e1ffe5f](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/a52c3ec7325e81bd87fdf8d74bf9acfd1e1ffe5f)\]\[[2404.09724](http://arxiv.org/abs/2404.09724)\]. To mitigate these risks, privacy-preserving FU frameworks have been developed, such as Starfish, which employs secure multi-party computation (2PC) to enable certified client removal while maintaining privacy guarantees \[[2404.09724](http://arxiv.org/abs/2404.09724)\]. Differential privacy has also been integrated into FU schemes like FedRecovery, which removes client influence via gradient residual adjustments combined with Gaussian noise, ensuring statistical indistinguishability from retrained models \[[https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/06edb1f6eadb6c141d720fcca4c854578dc5d670](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/06edb1f6eadb6c141d720fcca4c854578dc5d670)\]. These works underscore the critical balance between unlearning effectiveness, privacy protection, and computational efficiency.

Federated unlearning has been further specialized for various FL settings and data types. For instance, federated recommendation systems have adopted unlearning methods inspired by database rollback mechanisms to efficiently remove user contributions from on-device models while addressing storage limitations through update selection and negative sampling \[[2210.10958](http://arxiv.org/abs/2210.10958)\]. Feature-level unlearning frameworks, such as Ferrari, minimize feature sensitivity to erase sensitive, biased, or backdoor features in FL models without requiring participation from all clients, extending FU beyond sample or client removal \[[2205.12709](http://arxiv.org/abs/2205.12709)\]\[[2405.17462](http://arxiv.org/abs/2405.17462)\]. Knowledge distillation has also been leveraged in FU to restore model performance after subtracting client-specific contributions, facilitating practical and data-independent unlearning \[[2201.09441](http://arxiv.org/abs/2201.09441)\].

Verification of unlearning efficacy is another emerging focus area. Since unlearning may be incomplete or unverifiable, frameworks like VeriFi introduce mechanisms that fingerprint and check model changes to allow clients to actively verify their data's removal from the global model \[[2205.12709](http://arxiv.org/abs/2205.12709)\]. Verification methods based on backdoor triggers have also been proposed to statistically confirm unlearning, providing high-confidence deletion certifications while minimally impacting model utility \[[2003.04247](http://arxiv.org/abs/2003.04247)\]. These verification efforts are particularly relevant in Federated Online Learning to Rank (FOLTR), where dynamic user interactions and privacy concerns necessitate robust and transparent unlearning guarantees \[[https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/eadeffdec9fac8fd7f9aea732ca410eb082b7dcf](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/eadeffdec9fac8fd7f9aea732ca410eb082b7dcf)\]\[[2201.09441](http://arxiv.org/abs/2201.09441)\]\[[2003.04247](http://arxiv.org/abs/2003.04247)\].

The integration of FL with online learning to rank (OLTR) poses additional challenges. FOLTR frameworks optimize ranking models using implicit user feedback while preserving privacy through decentralized updates \[[2412.19069](http://arxiv.org/abs/2412.19069)\]. However, they face difficulties in ranking effectiveness, robustness to heterogeneous data distributions, and vulnerability to attacks, including those that exploit outdated or malicious client contributions. Recent research highlights the need for effective unlearning strategies within FOLTR to comply with RTBF legislation while maintaining system performance and user trust \[[https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/eadeffdec9fac8fd7f9aea732ca410eb082b7dcf](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/eadeffdec9fac8fd7f9aea732ca410eb082b7dcf)\]\[[2201.09441](http://arxiv.org/abs/2201.09441)\]\[[2412.19069](http://arxiv.org/abs/2412.19069)\].

Evaluation metrics for federated unlearning remain an open research problem. Existing unlearning assessments often rely on single metrics, which may inadequately capture the nuances of under-unlearning (failure to fully forget) or over-unlearning (excessive forgetting leading to performance degradation) \[[1910.01991](http://arxiv.org/abs/1910.01991)\]\[[2002.02730](http://arxiv.org/abs/2002.02730)\]. Recent studies advocate for multi-metric evaluation schemes that better reflect unlearning completeness and model utility preservation \[[1910.01991](http://arxiv.org/abs/1910.01991)\]. Such comprehensive assessments are essential for understanding trade-offs and guiding improvements in FU methods, especially in complex, real-world scenarios like FOLTR.

In summary, federated unlearning research has rapidly advanced across algorithmic design, privacy assurance, verification, and application domains. Existing works provide foundational FU methodologies that balance efficiency and effectiveness \[[1910.01991](http://arxiv.org/abs/1910.01991)\]\[[2207.05521](http://arxiv.org/abs/2207.05521)\]\[[https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/eadeffdec9fac8fd7f9aea732ca410eb082b7dcf](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/eadeffdec9fac8fd7f9aea732ca410eb082b7dcf)\], privacy-preserving mechanisms to mitigate leakage risks \[[https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/06edb1f6eadb6c141d720fcca4c854578dc5d670](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/06edb1f6eadb6c141d720fcca4c854578dc5d670)\]\[[2404.09724](http://arxiv.org/abs/2404.09724)\], specialized unlearning for recommendation and feature-level tasks \[[2205.12709](http://arxiv.org/abs/2205.12709)\]\[[2210.10958](http://arxiv.org/abs/2210.10958)\]\[[2405.17462](http://arxiv.org/abs/2405.17462)\], and verification frameworks to guarantee unlearning compliance \[[2205.12709](http://arxiv.org/abs/2205.12709)\]\[[2003.04247](http://arxiv.org/abs/2003.04247)\]. Nevertheless, challenges remain in unifying evaluation metrics and optimizing FU within dynamic settings such as FOLTR, motivating ongoing research efforts.
