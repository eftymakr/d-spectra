## Related Works

Deploying large language models (LLMs) on edge devices presents significant challenges due to the limited computational resources, memory capacity, power constraints, and latency requirements inherent to such environments \[[https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/ec93fc7ec82c65a7bf7213488327d391014539ed](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/ec93fc7ec82c65a7bf7213488327d391014539ed)\]. Addressing these challenges requires a multi-faceted approach combining model compression, algorithmic optimization, and hardware acceleration.

### Model Compression and Quantization

Model compression techniques, including pruning and quantization, are widely used to reduce LLM size and computational demands without significant performance degradation. Quantization methods convert model weights and activations into lower bit-width representations, such as INT8 or even lower precisions, to reduce memory footprint and improve inference efficiency. The LLM.int8() method demonstrates an 8-bit matrix multiplication technique that enables inference for models with up to 175 billion parameters on consumer-grade hardware without loss of accuracy \[[2208.07339](http://arxiv.org/abs/2208.07339)\]. Similarly, Activation-aware Weight Quantization (AWQ) identifies salient weights and applies activation distribution-aware quantization, achieving up to 4× size reduction and 3–4× acceleration across edge platforms, including resource-constrained IoT devices \[[2306.00978](http://arxiv.org/abs/2306.00978)\]. More aggressive mixed-precision schemes, such as the W2A8KV4 quantization (2-bit weights, 8-bit activations, 4-bit key-value cache), have been proposed to enable efficient long-context inference on hardware accelerators like FPGAs, balancing memory and bandwidth requirements \[[2505.03745](http://arxiv.org/abs/2505.03745)\]. These quantization methods are often combined with pruning strategies, including expert-level sparsification for Mixture-of-Experts (MoE) models, to further reduce model size and improve inference speed while maintaining accuracy \[[2402.14800](http://arxiv.org/abs/2402.14800)\].

### Algorithmic and System-Level Optimizations

Improving the efficiency of LLM inference also involves algorithmic innovations that reduce computational complexity. Techniques such as pruning, specialized attention mechanisms (e.g., Λ-shaped attention), and cache compression address the quadratic cost of attention and auto-regressive decoding bottlenecks \[[2505.03745](http://arxiv.org/abs/2505.03745)\]\[[2404.14294](http://arxiv.org/abs/2404.14294)\]. Memory management methods like PagedAttention optimize key-value cache usage by minimizing fragmentation and enabling cache sharing across requests, significantly increasing throughput by 2–4× compared to prior state-of-the-art systems \[[2309.06180](http://arxiv.org/abs/2309.06180)\]. Systems that manage memory hierarchies efficiently by leveraging flash storage and on-demand data loading ("windowing" and "row-column bundling") enable execution of LLMs larger than available DRAM, achieving 4–25× speedups in CPU and GPU environments \[[2312.11514](http://arxiv.org/abs/2312.11514)\]. Comprehensive surveys highlight these algorithmic and system-level innovations as critical to efficient LLM deployment in constrained settings \[[2402.16363](http://arxiv.org/abs/2402.16363)\]\[[2404.14294](http://arxiv.org/abs/2404.14294)\].

### Hardware Acceleration for Edge Deployment

Hardware accelerators tailored for LLM inference on edge devices are crucial to meeting latency and energy efficiency targets. FPGA-based accelerators are particularly promising due to their reconfigurability and energy efficiency. Designs such as SECDA-LLM enable streamlined FPGA accelerator creation for LLMs, achieving up to 11× latency reduction for small models on embedded boards \[[2408.00462](http://arxiv.org/abs/2408.00462)\]. Co-design methodologies that jointly optimize DNN architectures and FPGA hardware have demonstrated significant gains in throughput, energy efficiency, and latency reduction for edge inference tasks \[[https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/a821a00c54749a69a9d9fe0a7411de590d247715](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/a821a00c54749a69a9d9fe0a7411de590d247715)\]\[[1904.04421](http://arxiv.org/abs/1904.04421)\]. Domain-specific architectures like Google's TPU have shown the potential of custom ASICs for NN inference, delivering orders of magnitude improvements in performance-per-watt compared to CPUs and GPUs, though their large-scale deployment is currently limited to datacenter environments \[[1704.04760](http://arxiv.org/abs/1704.04760)\]. Surveys of existing hardware accelerators reveal a landscape of GPUs, FPGAs, and custom ASICs, each with trade-offs in performance, flexibility, and energy consumption, underscoring the importance of tailored solutions for edge LLM deployment \[[2401.09890](http://arxiv.org/abs/2401.09890)\].

### Privacy and Edge Computing Considerations

Deploying LLMs on edge devices also improves privacy by keeping sensitive data locally rather than transmitting it to cloud servers, an increasingly important consideration as LLM usage grows \[[https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/ec93fc7ec82c65a7bf7213488327d391014539ed](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/ec93fc7ec82c65a7bf7213488327d391014539ed)\]\[[1709.04061](http://arxiv.org/abs/1709.04061)\]. Frameworks for edge node resource management demonstrate that fog computing can reduce latency by up to 80% and data transfer to the cloud by 95%, highlighting the advantages of computation at the network edge \[[1709.04061](http://arxiv.org/abs/1709.04061)\]. Furthermore, efficient on-device LLM inference frameworks like TinyChat integrate quantization and optimized inference kernels to enable privacy-preserving, low-latency AI applications across a spectrum of edge hardware \[[2306.00978](http://arxiv.org/abs/2306.00978)\].

### Instruction Tuning and Model Fine-tuning

Improving the capabilities of LLMs via instruction tuning and alignment fine-tuning enhances performance on downstream tasks, enabling more efficient deployment by reducing the need for large-scale fine-tuning or multiple model variants \[[2109.01652](http://arxiv.org/abs/2109.01652)\]\[[2210.11416](http://arxiv.org/abs/2210.11416)\]\[[2309.02144](http://arxiv.org/abs/2309.02144)\]. Models like FLAN and WizardLM demonstrate that instruction-finetuned LLMs can generalize better and follow complex instructions, which is beneficial for edge applications requiring robust, general-purpose language understanding without frequent cloud interaction \[[2109.01652](http://arxiv.org/abs/2109.01652)\]\[[2304.12244](http://arxiv.org/abs/2304.12244)\].

### Summary

The related literature converges on a consensus that effective LLM deployment on edge devices necessitates a holistic approach integrating model compression, algorithmic innovations, and specialized hardware acceleration. Techniques such as quantization, pruning, and optimized memory management reduce the computational and storage demands, while FPGA and ASIC accelerators deliver the necessary throughput and energy efficiency. Real-world edge deployments also benefit from privacy improvements and system-level resource management frameworks. The proposed CLONE system builds on these foundations by integrating algorithm-hardware co-design, real-time energy optimization, and scalable hardware accelerator design to address the critical challenges outlined above.
