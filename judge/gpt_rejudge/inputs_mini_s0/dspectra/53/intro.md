## Related Works

Sequential recommendation has witnessed substantial progress through the integration of attention mechanisms, state-space models, and mixture-of-experts (MoE) frameworks to better capture user behavior dynamics and handle sequence variability.

### Attention Mechanisms in Sequential Recommendation

Early models predominantly relied on Markov Chains and Recurrent Neural Networks (RNNs) to capture sequential dependencies, with RNNs enabling longer-term semantics at the cost of computational complexity \[[1808.09781](http://arxiv.org/abs/1808.09781)\]. The advent of self-attention mechanisms, exemplified by SASRec, improved the ability to selectively focus on relevant past interactions while maintaining efficiency and handling both sparse and dense datasets \[[1808.09781](http://arxiv.org/abs/1808.09781)\]. However, these models often treated sequences as rigidly ordered without exploiting timestamp information. Extensions such as TiSASRec incorporated time intervals explicitly into self-attention, enhancing temporal context modeling and improving next-item predictions \[[https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/9c7a455b1e48d01a99e58e884a6c1acb75074ad0](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/9c7a455b1e48d01a99e58e884a6c1acb75074ad0)\]. Similarly, BERT4Rec introduced bidirectional self-attention to capture richer contextual representations by conditioning on both left and right contexts, overcoming limitations of unidirectional models \[[1904.06690](http://arxiv.org/abs/1904.06690)\]. Further refinements involved capturing temporal dynamics at multiple scales via multi-temporal embeddings and diverse attention heads, as in MEANTIME, which addressed limitations in positional embedding schemes by leveraging timestamp information and reducing information bottlenecks \[[2008.08273](http://arxiv.org/abs/2008.08273)\].

Feature-level attention has also been explored to capture transitions not only between items but also between their heterogeneous features, as demonstrated by FDSA, which applies separate self-attention blocks at item and feature levels to model complex sequential patterns \[[https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/63555b4f363ee72dd8d49d8deca68760542c0cd2](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/63555b4f363ee72dd8d49d8deca68760542c0cd2)\]. Category-aware models utilize item categories within self-attention frameworks to enhance dependency modeling and collaborative learning, thereby addressing sparsity issues by leveraging in-category transitions and user similarities \[[https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/455e11bde14967fc76d87d4649d821d2f27c3055](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/455e11bde14967fc76d87d4649d821d2f27c3055)\].

### State-Space Models and Temporal Dynamics

State-space models (SSMs) have been proposed to efficiently capture continuous-time dynamics and temporal evolution of user interests in sequential recommendation. Unlike discrete RNN or attention-based approaches, SSMs model the system's continuous state evolution, which is particularly suited for irregular time intervals in user interactions. SS4Rec integrates a time-aware SSM and a relation-aware SSM to jointly capture temporal dependencies and contextual relationships, allowing linear complexity processing of variable-length sequences and improved personalized recommendations \[[2502.08132](http://arxiv.org/abs/2502.08132)\]. Similarly, Time-LSTM enhances RNNs by incorporating time gates that model time intervals between interactions, effectively capturing both short-term and long-term user interests \[[https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/e1b93001202afd674cc3542cdb5599bff0d10157](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/e1b93001202afd674cc3542cdb5599bff0d10157)\].

These approaches demonstrate the importance of explicitly modeling temporal intervals and continuous evolution of user preferences, aligning with the use of state-space modeling to compress temporal dynamics and efficiently process sequences of variable lengths \[[https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/9c7a455b1e48d01a99e58e884a6c1acb75074ad0](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/9c7a455b1e48d01a99e58e884a6c1acb75074ad0)\]\[[2502.08132](http://arxiv.org/abs/2502.08132)\].

### Mixture-of-Experts for Diverse User Behavior Modeling

Modeling diverse and evolving user interests requires adaptive frameworks capable of handling heterogeneous behavioral patterns. Mixture-of-experts (MoE) architectures have been employed to address this challenge by routing different sequences or behaviors to specialized subnetworks or experts. This approach allows the system to focus on category-specific browsing patterns and diverse exploration behaviors within a unified framework. MoE frameworks have been shown to enhance recommendation accuracy by learning expert specialization and adaptive routing, as evidenced in recent works on multi-modal and multi-domain recommendation settings \[[https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/455e11bde14967fc76d87d4649d821d2f27c3055](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/455e11bde14967fc76d87d4649d821d2f27c3055)\]\[[2108.06479](http://arxiv.org/abs/2108.06479)\]\[[2208.04760](http://arxiv.org/abs/2208.04760)\].

Hierarchical attention networks have also been proposed to combine long-term and short-term preferences dynamically, integrating user-item interactions in a non-linear way to better reflect evolving user tastes \[[https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/0b42168f22713c48d8b0ce3bc8f680d347184d88](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/0b42168f22713c48d8b0ce3bc8f680d347184d88)\]. This complements MoE approaches by providing multi-level preference modeling that can be combined with expert routing strategies.

### Self-Supervised and Contrastive Learning Enhancements

To mitigate data sparsity and noise, self-supervised learning (SSL) and contrastive learning techniques have been integrated with sequential recommendation models. Methods like S3-Rec utilize mutual information maximization to learn robust representations from item, attribute, subsequence, and sequence-level correlations, improving performance especially with limited data \[[2008.07873](http://arxiv.org/abs/2008.07873)\]. CoSeRec applies contrastive SSL with robust data augmentations to enhance model robustness to sparse and noisy sequences \[[2108.06479](http://arxiv.org/abs/2108.06479)\]. CauseRec further improves robustness by synthesizing counterfactual user sequences, learning user representations that are less sensitive to noise and more reflective of indispensable behaviors \[[2109.05261](http://arxiv.org/abs/2109.05261)\].

### Summary

In summary, recent literature highlights three key directions that underpin the development of STAR-Rec: (1) preference-aware self-attention mechanisms that capture both similar and diverse item relationships with consideration of temporal and feature-level dynamics \[[1808.09781](http://arxiv.org/abs/1808.09781)\]\[[1904.06690](http://arxiv.org/abs/1904.06690)\]\[[2008.08273](http://arxiv.org/abs/2008.08273)\]\[[https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/9c7a455b1e48d01a99e58e884a6c1acb75074ad0](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/9c7a455b1e48d01a99e58e884a6c1acb75074ad0)\]\[[https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/63555b4f363ee72dd8d49d8deca68760542c0cd2](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/63555b4f363ee72dd8d49d8deca68760542c0cd2)\]\[[https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/455e11bde14967fc76d87d4649d821d2f27c3055](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/455e11bde14967fc76d87d4649d821d2f27c3055)\]; (2) state-space models that efficiently handle time-continuous, variable-length sequences and compress temporal dynamics for scalable recommendation \[[https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/9c7a455b1e48d01a99e58e884a6c1acb75074ad0](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/9c7a455b1e48d01a99e58e884a6c1acb75074ad0)\]\[[2502.08132](http://arxiv.org/abs/2502.08132)\]\[[https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/e1b93001202afd674cc3542cdb5599bff0d10157](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/e1b93001202afd674cc3542cdb5599bff0d10157)\]; and (3) mixture-of-experts frameworks that adaptively route diverse behavioral patterns to specialized experts, enabling nuanced modeling of heterogeneous user interests \[[https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/0b42168f22713c48d8b0ce3bc8f680d347184d88](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/0b42168f22713c48d8b0ce3bc8f680d347184d88)\]\[[https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/455e11bde14967fc76d87d4649d821d2f27c3055](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/455e11bde14967fc76d87d4649d821d2f27c3055)\]\[[2108.06479](http://arxiv.org/abs/2108.06479)\]\[[2208.04760](http://arxiv.org/abs/2208.04760)\]. Additionally, integrating self-supervised and contrastive learning methods addresses challenges related to data sparsity and noise \[[2108.06479](http://arxiv.org/abs/2108.06479)\]\[[2008.07873](http://arxiv.org/abs/2008.07873)\]\[[2109.05261](http://arxiv.org/abs/2109.05261)\], further enhancing robustness and generalization. STAR-Rec innovatively combines these elements within a sequence-level MoE framework, unifying temporal dynamics and attention to improve recommendation effectiveness across varied user behaviors and sequence lengths.
