## Related Works

Cross-Domain Sequential Recommendation (CDSR) has been recognized as a critical approach to alleviate data sparsity and cold-start issues by leveraging users' historical interactions across multiple domains \[[2401.04971](http://arxiv.org/abs/2401.04971)\]\[[2304.03891](http://arxiv.org/abs/2304.03891)\]. Traditional CDSR methods typically depend on overlapping users—those who interact in multiple domains—to learn cross-domain relationships and transfer knowledge effectively. However, this overlap assumption severely limits practical applicability since real-world scenarios often involve few or no shared users between domains, a challenge known as the overlap dilemma \[[2401.04971](http://arxiv.org/abs/2401.04971)\]\[[2311.04760](http://arxiv.org/abs/2311.04760)\]\[[2311.04590](http://arxiv.org/abs/2311.04590)\].

To address this, several studies have proposed frameworks to reduce reliance on overlapping users. Semi-supervised mapping approaches use both labeled overlapping users and unlabeled items to infer user preferences in cold-start domains, effectively improving recommendation accuracy with limited overlap \[[https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/f3b7672aaab5b65e1eb66aee6f3a5b95fb0c304f](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/f3b7672aaab5b65e1eb66aee6f3a5b95fb0c304f)\]. Dual learning and metric learning techniques enable bidirectional information transfer between domains, significantly reducing the need for large user overlaps while preserving latent relations in user-item interactions \[[2104.08490](http://arxiv.org/abs/2104.08490)\]\[[2106.02768](http://arxiv.org/abs/2106.02768)\]. Recent efforts also explore federated learning and privacy-preserving mechanisms to enable cross-domain recommendations without sharing sensitive user data, thus handling non-overlapping scenarios in a privacy-aware manner \[[2311.04760](http://arxiv.org/abs/2311.04760)\]\[[2311.04590](http://arxiv.org/abs/2311.04590)\].

Modeling the complex transition patterns inherent in mixed-domain sequential behaviors—referred to as transition complexity—remains another major challenge. Existing CDSR methods often focus on modeling single-domain sequences or rely on transferring knowledge via latent spaces, which may fail to capture intricate intra- and inter-sequence item relationships \[[2304.03891](http://arxiv.org/abs/2304.03891)\]\[[2209.10163](http://arxiv.org/abs/2209.10163)\]\[[https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/96bfe33a2503e960153a934e10be91da4d8b13a6](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/96bfe33a2503e960153a934e10be91da4d8b13a6)\]. Graph-based methods have been proposed to explicitly model cross-domain sequential transitions by constructing dynamic graphs that capture both local (intra-domain) and global (inter-domain) item transitions, combined with hybrid metric learning to improve representation quality and alleviate sparsity \[[2209.10163](http://arxiv.org/abs/2209.10163)\]\[[2206.08050](http://arxiv.org/abs/2206.08050)\]. Attention mechanisms, including dual-attentive sequential learning, have also been introduced to extract bidirectional user preferences and enhance cross-domain click-through rate predictions \[[2106.02768](http://arxiv.org/abs/2106.02768)\]\[[https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/44ecd5740798b8d90818fde07ea5bfceee22b83b](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/44ecd5740798b8d90818fde07ea5bfceee22b83b)\].

Another line of research tackles the challenges of item representation alignment across domains. Misaligned item embeddings can hinder the effectiveness of sequential modeling and user representation alignment. Adaptive partial alignment frameworks employ sequence-aware feature augmentation and spectrum filtering techniques to better align items across domains, thereby enhancing recommendation performance \[[2405.12473](http://arxiv.org/abs/2405.12473)\]\[[https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/6bceab2405e4bbd4dadc16e8b946aa12dbddefc7](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/6bceab2405e4bbd4dadc16e8b946aa12dbddefc7)\].

The recent advent of Large Language Models (LLMs) has opened new avenues for addressing both overlap and transition challenges in CDSR by leveraging their powerful semantic representation and reasoning capabilities. LLM-based frameworks generate semantically rich and domain-agnostic user and item embeddings by processing textual item descriptions and mixed-domain behavior sequences, overcoming the limitations of traditional ID-based embeddings \[[2310.14304](http://arxiv.org/abs/2310.14304)\]. Such models can bridge semantic gaps across domains and better capture user preferences from a holistic, semantic perspective \[[2305.19860](http://arxiv.org/abs/2305.19860)\]\[[https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/e33f51c13728bedeeb54a76b398949a381d6152f](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/e33f51c13728bedeeb54a76b398949a381d6152f)\]. To mitigate inherent LLM limitations like hallucination and handling large item corpora, methods have been proposed to generate pseudo cross-domain interactions and apply contrastive pre-training with meta-learning-based retrieval, enhancing robustness and relevance of recommendations \[[https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/e33f51c13728bedeeb54a76b398949a381d6152f](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/e33f51c13728bedeeb54a76b398949a381d6152f)\]\[[2406.03085](http://arxiv.org/abs/2406.03085)\].

Despite their promise, integrating LLMs into CDSR requires careful design to balance semantic understanding with sequential collaborative signals. Lightweight distillation techniques have been developed to reduce LLM size for efficient sequential recommendation without sacrificing performance, making them more practical for large-scale applications \[[2405.17890](http://arxiv.org/abs/2405.17890)\]. Furthermore, frameworks combining LLMs with graph neural networks and contrastive learning have been proposed to jointly capture intra- and inter-sequence item relations, improving cross-domain user preference modeling \[[2304.03891](http://arxiv.org/abs/2304.03891)\]\[[2406.03085](http://arxiv.org/abs/2406.03085)\].

Additionally, disentangling domain-shared and domain-specific preferences has been highlighted as crucial for effective knowledge transfer across domains. Disentangled representation learning with mutual-information-based regularizers helps separate shared information from domain-specific noise, thereby enhancing target domain recommendation accuracy \[[https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/fa36b8d483b06e0bce366970fde747761151f411](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/fa36b8d483b06e0bce366970fde747761151f411)\]. Optimal transport-based internal and external alignment methods have also been employed to model multi-interest user profiles and reduce domain discrepancies \[[https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/44ecd5740798b8d90818fde07ea5bfceee22b83b](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/44ecd5740798b8d90818fde07ea5bfceee22b83b)\].

In summary, existing CDSR research has extensively explored methods to mitigate the overlap dilemma and transition complexity through semi-supervised learning, dual learning, graph-based modeling, and attention mechanisms. The integration of LLMs represents a promising frontier, offering semantic-rich representations and reasoning capabilities that can overcome the limitations of traditional CDSR models. However, challenges remain in efficient LLM adaptation, item representation alignment, and balancing semantic and collaborative signals, motivating ongoing research in this area.
