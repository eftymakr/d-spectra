## Related Works

Cross-domain sequential recommendation (CDSR) extends traditional sequential recommendation by leveraging users' interaction sequences across multiple domains to improve prediction accuracy and alleviate data sparsity and cold-start issues \[[https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/a8f2764a76772ab957b31f5d7bb88527d46fbdc8](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/a8f2764a76772ab957b31f5d7bb88527d46fbdc8)\]\[[2304.03891](http://arxiv.org/abs/2304.03891)\]. Conventional sequential recommenders predominantly rely on self-attention-based Transformer architectures, such as SASRec and BERT4Rec, to capture long-term and complex user behavior patterns within single domains \[[1808.09781](http://arxiv.org/abs/1808.09781)\]\[[https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/5aed438fecf702ead9c0cddfa9a80bd00cff0138](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/5aed438fecf702ead9c0cddfa9a80bd00cff0138)\]. These models encode item sequences effectively but often assume a closed-world setting with fully overlapping users and stable data distributions, which limits their applicability in real-world open-world CDSR scenarios where user overlap is partial and data distribution shifts occur \[[2311.04760](http://arxiv.org/abs/2311.04760)\]\[[2311.04590](http://arxiv.org/abs/2311.04590)\].

To address cross-domain challenges, many CDSR approaches incorporate domain-specific modules or design transfer mechanisms for knowledge propagation between domains. For example, models like DREAM employ supervised contrastive learning and decoupled representations to jointly learn single- and cross-domain preferences, mitigating data sparsity and class imbalance \[[https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/96bfe33a2503e960153a934e10be91da4d8b13a6](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/96bfe33a2503e960153a934e10be91da4d8b13a6)\]. Other frameworks, such as DDGHM and C2DSR, exploit dual dynamic graphs or graph neural networks combined with contrastive objectives to capture intra- and inter-sequence item relationships, thereby improving cross-domain user preference modeling \[[2304.03891](http://arxiv.org/abs/2304.03891)\]\[[2209.10163](http://arxiv.org/abs/2209.10163)\]. Similarly, CD-ASR and DASL leverage attentive mechanisms and dual learning to integrate general and current user preferences or simultaneously transfer knowledge in both directions between source and target domains \[[https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/6dd0605f7de7581c69b4acf0ab78000748a09f3e](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/6dd0605f7de7581c69b4acf0ab78000748a09f3e)\]\[[2106.02768](http://arxiv.org/abs/2106.02768)\].

Despite these advances, many methods emphasize adding domain-aware modules or transfer components while treating the core self-attention mechanism as a fixed backbone. However, recent studies reveal that enhancing the self-attention module itself can lead to significant gains. Lightweight self-attentive networks (LSAN) reduce memory overhead and redundancy in multi-head attention, enabling efficient modeling of both global and local item dependencies \[[2108.11333](http://arxiv.org/abs/2108.11333)\]. Time interval-aware self-attention models (TiSASRec and Proxy-Aware CDSR) incorporate temporal gaps between interactions to refine sequence encoding and better capture user intent dynamics over time \[[https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/9c7a455b1e48d01a99e58e884a6c1acb75074ad0](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/9c7a455b1e48d01a99e58e884a6c1acb75074ad0)\]\[[https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/27670ed18e96550c674e022e284fc5356a7458c2](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/27670ed18e96550c674e022e284fc5356a7458c2)\]. Moreover, research into overcoming inherent issues in self-attention, such as oversmoothing, has led to novel methods like BSARec that integrate frequency domain features to maintain representational diversity and capture fine-grained sequential patterns \[[2312.10325](http://arxiv.org/abs/2312.10325)\].

Complementary to architectural improvements, data augmentation and contrastive learning strategies have been proposed to enhance representation quality and alleviate sparsity. CL4SRec introduces multi-task contrastive learning with data augmentations to extract richer user behavior patterns from limited sequences \[[https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/a8f2764a76772ab957b31f5d7bb88527d46fbdc8](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/a8f2764a76772ab957b31f5d7bb88527d46fbdc8)\]. ASReP augments short sequences using pseudo-prior items generated by a reverse-direction pretrained transformer, improving cold-start performance \[[2105.00522](http://arxiv.org/abs/2105.00522)\]. These self-supervised approaches provide stronger supervision signals and improve robustness against sparse interactions.

In cross-domain contexts, aligning user and item representations across heterogeneous domains is another critical challenge. Approaches like CA-CDSR propose adaptive partial alignment of item embeddings using sequence-aware feature augmentation and spectrum filtering, improving representation consistency and downstream recommendation quality \[[2405.12473](http://arxiv.org/abs/2405.12473)\]. Proxy-Aware CDSR generates universal item embeddings from textual descriptions to bridge domain gaps effectively \[[https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/27670ed18e96550c674e022e284fc5356a7458c2](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/27670ed18e96550c674e022e284fc5356a7458c2)\]. Graph-based methods such as TiDA-GCN model explicit cross-domain graph structures and incorporate time intervals into message passing to enhance shared-account recommendations \[[2206.08050](http://arxiv.org/abs/2206.08050)\].

Several efforts also focus on mitigating negative transfer and domain bias during cross-domain learning. The Pacer and Runner framework dynamically estimates domain-level negative transfer and adjusts gradient flows accordingly while maximizing mutual information between single- and cross-domain representations \[[2407.11245](http://arxiv.org/abs/2407.11245)\]. Similarly, AMID introduces a multi-interest debiasing mechanism and doubly robust estimator to handle open-world user overlap and distributional shifts \[[2311.04590](http://arxiv.org/abs/2311.04590)\].

Finally, multi-task and multi-target cross-domain recommendation frameworks like DTCDR and GA exploit shared embeddings, graph attention, and personalized training strategies to simultaneously improve recommendation accuracy across multiple domains, addressing dual- or multi-target scenarios beyond the traditional single-target setting \[[https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/2b031c7170c9dccbe506f3d3a1d6104f65e835ae](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/2b031c7170c9dccbe506f3d3a1d6104f65e835ae)\]\[[2108.07976](http://arxiv.org/abs/2108.07976)\].

In summary, CDSR research has explored diverse strategies including domain-aware modules, graph-based modeling, contrastive learning, temporal encoding enhancements, and representation alignment to improve performance. Nonetheless, few works focus on directly enhancing the self-attention mechanism from a multi-objective optimization perspective to balance cross-domain knowledge transfer and negative transfer mitigation. Our work fills this gap by proposing a Pareto-optimal self-attention mechanism that dynamically optimizes recommendation and cross-domain attention objectives, serving as a plug-and-play module compatible with existing transformer-based recommenders \[[https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/a8f2764a76772ab957b31f5d7bb88527d46fbdc8](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/a8f2764a76772ab957b31f5d7bb88527d46fbdc8)\]\[[1808.09781](http://arxiv.org/abs/1808.09781)\]\[[https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/96bfe33a2503e960153a934e10be91da4d8b13a6](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/96bfe33a2503e960153a934e10be91da4d8b13a6)\]\[[2304.03891](http://arxiv.org/abs/2304.03891)\]\[[2311.04590](http://arxiv.org/abs/2311.04590)\]\[[2312.10325](http://arxiv.org/abs/2312.10325)\]\[[2407.11245](http://arxiv.org/abs/2407.11245)\].
