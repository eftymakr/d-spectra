## Related Works

Neural retrieval models have significantly advanced the field of information retrieval, particularly for Web search, by learning complex semantic matching functions from large-scale data \[[2102.11903](http://arxiv.org/abs/2102.11903)\]. Traditional retrieval methods, such as BM25, rely on exact term matching, which can lead to vocabulary mismatch issues. Neural models, including dense retrieval frameworks, address these challenges by leveraging learned dense representations of queries and documents, often trained with large volumes of labeled query-document pairs \[[2004.04906](http://arxiv.org/abs/2004.04906)\]\[[2104.08051](http://arxiv.org/abs/2104.08051)\]\[[2010.08191](http://arxiv.org/abs/2010.08191)\]. However, obtaining such labeled data is costly and limits scalability.

To mitigate data scarcity, recent research has explored generating synthetic queries using large language models (LLMs), which serve as scalable substitutes for human-annotated queries \[[2102.11903](http://arxiv.org/abs/2102.11903)\]\[[1904.08375](http://arxiv.org/abs/1904.08375)\]. Synthetic query generation approaches range from sequence-to-sequence models that predict likely queries for documents \[[1904.08375](http://arxiv.org/abs/1904.08375)\] to frameworks that integrate dense retrieval and query generation in a unified pipeline \[[2112.04426](http://arxiv.org/abs/2112.04426)\]\[[2005.11401](http://arxiv.org/abs/2005.11401)\]. Despite these advances, synthetic queries often vary widely in quality, which can degrade downstream retrieval performance if noisy or irrelevant query-document pairs are included during training \[[2102.11903](http://arxiv.org/abs/2102.11903)\].

To address noise in synthetic data, traditional methods typically employ external neural re-rankers to filter out low-quality query-document pairs before using them for training retrieval models \[[2102.11903](http://arxiv.org/abs/2102.11903)\]. While effective, this two-stage process adds computational overhead and may not fully capture nuanced relevance signals. Recent work proposes more direct approaches that embed relevance signals within the training objective itself. For example, direct document relevance optimization (DDRO) aligns token-level generation with document-level relevance, bypassing explicit reward modeling and reinforcement learning, resulting in improved retrieval effectiveness and simpler training procedures \[[2102.11903](http://arxiv.org/abs/2102.11903)\].

Direct Preference Optimization (DPO) is a novel technique originally developed to align language models with human preferences by optimizing pairwise preference data through a classification loss rather than complex reinforcement learning pipelines \[[2305.1829](http://arxiv.org/abs/2305.18290)\]. DPO has demonstrated superior stability and performance in fine-tuning language models for tasks such as summarization and dialogue \[[2305.1829](http://arxiv.org/abs/2305.18290)\]. However, standard DPO can suffer from failure modes where the likelihood of preferred outputs decreases despite improving relative preference scores, especially when paired examples have low edit distances. To overcome this, DPO-Positive (DPOP) introduces a refined loss function that avoids such degradation and achieves state-of-the-art results across multiple benchmarks \[[2402.13228](http://arxiv.org/abs/2402.13228)\]. These advances highlight DPO’s potential for directly optimizing generation models toward relevance, making it a promising method for enhancing synthetic query generation in retrieval contexts.

The integration of DPO into the query generation process can be seen as a movement toward unifying ranking and generation objectives, reducing reliance on external re-rankers, and improving the overall quality of synthetic queries used in training retrieval models \[[2102.11903](http://arxiv.org/abs/2102.11903)\]\[[2305.1829](http://arxiv.org/abs/2305.18290)\]\[[2402.13228](http://arxiv.org/abs/2402.13228)\]. This approach aligns with recent trends in generative information retrieval, where models are optimized end-to-end to produce document identifiers or relevant outputs directly \[[2102.11903](http://arxiv.org/abs/2102.11903)\]\[[2504.05181](http://arxiv.org/abs/2504.05181)\].

Complementary to these developments, neural ranking models have evolved from traditional hand-crafted feature-based approaches to end-to-end trained deep architectures that learn to extract relevance signals from raw data \[[2102.11903](http://arxiv.org/abs/2102.11903)\]\[[1711.08611](http://arxiv.org/abs/1711.08611)\]\[[1706.06613](http://arxiv.org/abs/1706.06613)\]. Models such as DRMM and K-NRM emphasize relevance matching based on exact and soft term matches, highlighting the importance of capturing both lexical and semantic relationships for ad-hoc retrieval \[[1711.08611](http://arxiv.org/abs/1711.08611)\]\[[1706.06613](http://arxiv.org/abs/1706.06613)\]\[[1610.08136](http://arxiv.org/abs/1610.08136)\]. Similarly, hybrid models that combine local exact matches with distributed semantic representations have shown improved ranking performance by leveraging both lexical and semantic cues \[[1610.08136](http://arxiv.org/abs/1610.08136)\].

Dense retrieval models have also benefited from improved sampling strategies, such as hard negative mining and dynamic negative sampling, which enhance training stability and ranking quality \[[2104.08051](http://arxiv.org/abs/2104.08051)\]. Meanwhile, sparse and expansion-based neural models like SPLADE further improve retrieval effectiveness by combining lexical matching advantages with learned expansions \[[2109.10086](http://arxiv.org/abs/2109.10086)\]. These innovations demonstrate the multifaceted nature of neural retrieval, where training data quality, model architecture, and optimization techniques all critically influence performance.

Overall, the proposed framework builds upon these lines of research by employing DPO to directly optimize query generation toward producing high-quality queries that align with downstream retrieval objectives. This approach addresses the shortcomings of synthetic data noise filtering, leverages advances in direct preference optimization, and complements the broader evolution of neural retrieval models toward end-to-end, relevance-focused training \[[2102.11903](http://arxiv.org/abs/2102.11903)\]\[[2305.1829](http://arxiv.org/abs/2305.18290)\]\[[2402.13228](http://arxiv.org/abs/2402.13228)\]\[[1904.08375](http://arxiv.org/abs/1904.08375)\].
