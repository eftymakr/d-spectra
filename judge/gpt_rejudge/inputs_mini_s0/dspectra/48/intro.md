## Related Works

Neural retrieval has undergone significant transformation with the integration of transformer-based pre-trained language models (PLMs), which have substantially improved multilingual and cross-lingual retrieval capabilities. Models such as BERT and RoBERTa have been central to these advances by enabling richer contextualized representations of text, leading to state-of-the-art results on various benchmarks \[[1907.11692](http://arxiv.org/abs/1907.11692)\]\[[1810.04805](http://arxiv.org/abs/1810.04805)\]\[[1905.09217](http://arxiv.org/abs/1905.09217)\]. RoBERTa, in particular, demonstrated that careful hyperparameter tuning and extensive pretraining can significantly enhance performance beyond the original BERT model \[[1907.11692](http://arxiv.org/abs/1907.11692)\]. Moreover, multilingual pretrained models like mBERT and XLM-RoBERTa (XLM-R) have been employed for cross-lingual retrieval, leveraging shared semantic spaces across languages to support zero-shot and translate-train approaches \[[1911.02116](http://arxiv.org/abs/1911.02116)\]\[[2201.08471](http://arxiv.org/abs/2201.08471)\]\[[2007.01852](http://arxiv.org/abs/2007.01852)\]. XLM-R has shown notable improvements for low-resource languages due to large-scale multilingual pretraining on CommonCrawl data, demonstrating competitive results even against strong monolingual models \[[1911.02116](http://arxiv.org/abs/1911.02116)\].

Despite these advances, the application of dense retrieval models to low-resource and morphologically rich languages such as Amharic remains challenging. The scarcity of high-quality training data and the complexity of tokenizing morphologically rich languages limit the effectiveness of general multilingual models. Recent studies highlight that out-of-the-box multilingual dense retrievers often underperform on such languages, necessitating language-specific adaptations or data augmentation techniques to bridge this gap \[[2503.18570](http://arxiv.org/abs/2503.18570)\]\[[2408.11942](http://arxiv.org/abs/2408.11942)\]. The difficulty is compounded by vocabulary mismatch and insufficient language alignment in dense retrieval embeddings, which affects retrieval quality and cross-lingual transfer \[[2212.10448](http://arxiv.org/abs/2212.10448)\]\[[2408.11942](http://arxiv.org/abs/2408.11942)\].

To address these challenges, several works have explored Amharic-specific NLP resources and models. For example, a newly annotated Amharic Named Entity Recognition corpus and transformer-based recognizers demonstrate the benefits of language-specific fine-tuning and data balancing techniques such as SMOTE to mitigate data imbalance \[[2207.00785](http://arxiv.org/abs/2207.00785)\]. Similarly, dense retrieval tailored to Amharic has been investigated by constructing dedicated training datasets and evaluating dense versus sparse retrieval methods, confirming that dense retrieval approaches can outperform traditional lexical methods when properly adapted \[[2503.18570](http://arxiv.org/abs/2503.18570)\].

Dense retrieval architectures employing late interaction mechanisms, such as ColBERT, offer a promising balance between effectiveness and efficiency. ColBERT independently encodes queries and documents, allowing precomputation of document embeddings and fast token-level interaction during retrieval, thus reducing computational costs dramatically while maintaining or improving retrieval accuracy \[[2004.12832](http://arxiv.org/abs/2004.12832)\]\[[2112.01488](http://arxiv.org/abs/2112.01488)\]. Extensions like ColBERT-X leverage multilingual encoders like XLM-R to facilitate cross-language retrieval, showing improved performance in zero-shot and translate-train settings across multiple languages, though still facing challenges in extremely low-resource contexts \[[2201.08471](http://arxiv.org/abs/2201.08471)\]\[[2408.11942](http://arxiv.org/abs/2408.11942)\].

Training strategies for dense retrieval models also impact performance, especially in low-resource or zero-shot scenarios. Contrastive learning methods have been explored for unsupervised dense retriever pretraining, achieving competitive results on multilingual retrieval tasks without labeled data and improving cross-lingual transfer \[[2112.09118](http://arxiv.org/abs/2112.09118)\]. Parameter-efficient fine-tuning methods, such as adapters, have shown promise in cross-language dense retrieval by enabling language-specific adjustments without full model retraining, although some challenges remain in aligning retrieval-specific representations with language adapters \[[2212.10448](http://arxiv.org/abs/2212.10448)\].

In addition to model architecture and training, robust evaluation frameworks like BEIR provide heterogeneous benchmarks for zero-shot and out-of-distribution retrieval evaluation, highlighting the trade-offs between sparse, dense, and late interaction models in terms of efficiency and effectiveness \[[2104.08663](http://arxiv.org/abs/2104.08663)\]. These benchmarks reveal that while dense models excel with sufficient data, sparse models like BM25 remain strong baselines in low-resource or domain-shifted scenarios.

Finally, retrieval-augmented language models (e.g., REALM, RAG, RETRO) integrate retrieval directly into language model pretraining and generation, enhancing knowledge-intensive NLP tasks by combining parametric and non-parametric memory. These architectures underline the growing trend of coupling retrieval with large-scale pretrained models to improve factual accuracy and interpretability \[[2005.11401](http://arxiv.org/abs/2005.11401)\]\[[2002.08909](http://arxiv.org/abs/2002.08909)\]\[[2112.04426](http://arxiv.org/abs/2112.04426)\].

In summary, while multilingual transformer-based dense retrieval models have advanced cross-lingual retrieval, the unique challenges posed by low-resource, morphologically rich languages such as Amharic require language-specific model adaptation, tailored datasets, and efficient retrieval architectures like ColBERT. The work presented here further contributes by developing Amharic-specific dense retrieval models that outperform large multilingual baselines, confirming the importance of specialized modeling for effective retrieval in low-resource settings.
