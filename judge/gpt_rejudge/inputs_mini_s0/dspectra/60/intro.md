## Related Works

Model calibration is a critical aspect of machine learning, especially in ranking systems where accurate absolute predictions are as important as maintaining the correct relative order. Traditional ranking models prioritize the relative order of items but often overlook the calibration of the absolute prediction values, which can undermine downstream decision-making processes such as auctions or recommendation systems \[[https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/7f6bb146b9c1504ec77100e640d624bdc204f0ac](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/7f6bb146b9c1504ec77100e640d624bdc204f0ac)\]. Existing calibration methods for ranking models generally involve post-hoc transformations using predefined monotonic functions—commonly piece-wise linear or fixed-form mappings—that preserve order but lack sufficient expressiveness and flexibility to capture complex, non-linear calibration patterns \[[https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/7f6bb146b9c1504ec77100e640d624bdc204f0ac](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/7f6bb146b9c1504ec77100e640d624bdc204f0ac)\].

Recent advances have explored neural network-based calibrators to overcome these limitations. Monotonic neural networks, which guarantee order preservation by design, present a natural choice for calibration tasks requiring monotonic transformations. The Unconstrained Monotonic Neural Network (UMNN) architecture, in particular, enables learning arbitrary monotonic functions by ensuring positivity of derivatives via unconstrained neural networks, thus offering superior flexibility without sacrificing monotonicity \[[1908.05164](http://arxiv.org/abs/1908.05164)\]. UMNNs have demonstrated effectiveness in other domains such as density estimation and variational inference, showcasing their capacity to model complex monotonic relationships \[[1908.05164](http://arxiv.org/abs/1908.05164)\]. Leveraging UMNNs for calibration allows for more expressive, data-driven mappings that better adjust the raw model scores while preserving their ranking order.

Beyond monotonic neural networks, calibration in classification and ranking has been addressed through various strategies. Temperature scaling, a simple post-processing approach, adjusts confidence scores using a single scalar parameter and has proven effective for neural network calibration in classification tasks \[[1706.04599](http://arxiv.org/abs/1706.04599)\]. However, it lacks the ability to model more intricate calibration functions required in ranking contexts. More expressive post-hoc methods involving splines or kernel density estimators have been proposed to improve calibration accuracy and stability, though these methods often remain limited by fixed functional forms or require large hold-out calibration sets \[[2006.12800](http://arxiv.org/abs/2006.12800)\]\[[2003.07329](http://arxiv.org/abs/2003.07329)\]\[[1904.01685](http://arxiv.org/abs/1904.01685)\].

Other neural-network-based approaches focus on uncertainty estimation alongside calibration. Deep ensembles and Bayesian neural networks provide improved uncertainty quantification, which can indirectly improve calibration by capturing predictive confidence more accurately \[[1612.01474](http://arxiv.org/abs/1612.01474)\]\[[2006.12800](http://arxiv.org/abs/2006.12800)\]. Nevertheless, these methods introduce significant computational overhead and complexity, limiting their scalability in large-scale ranking systems \[[2012.07923](http://arxiv.org/abs/2012.07923)\]. Data augmentation techniques such as mixup have also been shown to improve calibration by smoothing label distributions and reducing overconfidence in neural networks, highlighting the impact of training strategies on calibration quality \[[1905.11001](http://arxiv.org/abs/1905.11001)\].

Calibration in ranking models presents unique challenges due to the invariance of many ranking losses to additive score shifts, which leads to scale ambiguity and instability during training \[[https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/7f6bb146b9c1504ec77100e640d624bdc204f0ac](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/7f6bb146b9c1504ec77100e640d624bdc204f0ac)\]. Addressing this, recent work introduces calibrated ranking losses that incorporate scale calibration during training rather than relying solely on post-hoc adjustments, resulting in more stable training and improved calibration performance \[[https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/7f6bb146b9c1504ec77100e640d624bdc204f0ac](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/7f6bb146b9c1504ec77100e640d624bdc204f0ac)\]. This approach aligns with the goal of learning flexible, monotonic calibration functions integrated within the training process, rather than applying fixed transformations after model convergence.

Additionally, recent studies have emphasized the importance of reliable calibration measures and evaluation protocols to accurately assess calibration performance. Common metrics like Expected Calibration Error (ECE) have known flaws, such as sensitivity to binning strategies and sample size, motivating the development of alternative metrics based on kernel density estimation or cumulative distribution comparisons \[[1904.01685](http://arxiv.org/abs/1904.01685)\]\[[2003.07329](http://arxiv.org/abs/2003.07329)\]. These advances enable more reliable calibration assessment, critical for validating novel flexible calibrators such as those based on UMNNs.

In summary, while traditional calibration methods rely on fixed, simple monotonic functions that constrain expressiveness, neural network-based monotonic calibrators like UMNNs offer a powerful alternative that can learn complex, flexible monotonic mappings essential for ranking calibration. Incorporating such flexible calibrators together with novel loss functions designed for smooth calibration conditions can significantly improve calibration quality and downstream utility in large-scale ranking systems \[[https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/7f6bb146b9c1504ec77100e640d624bdc204f0ac](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/7f6bb146b9c1504ec77100e640d624bdc204f0ac)\]\[[1908.05164](http://arxiv.org/abs/1908.05164)\]. Our work builds upon these foundations by introducing a UMNN-based calibrator optimized with a Smooth Calibration Loss, achieving superior calibration performance in both offline and large-scale online settings.
