## Related Works

Extrinsic calibration of radar and camera systems is a critical prerequisite for accurate sensor fusion in autonomous driving, yet it remains challenging due to radar data sparsity and height measurement uncertainty. Early calibration methods primarily relied on target-based setups, using specialized calibration boards or corner reflectors to estimate the rigid transformation between sensors. For instance, methods employing geometric calibration targets and reprojection error minimization have been proposed for radar-LiDAR-camera systems, involving multi-step optimization and radar cross section (RCS) pattern analysis to improve parameter estimation, particularly for elevation uncertainty \[[https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/4d4dbbcf27d991aeab43dafb82a105bb5512b799](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/4d4dbbcf27d991aeab43dafb82a105bb5512b799)\]\[[https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/0955326c67a947b5fac5fbae7238d04fc9adc251](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/0955326c67a947b5fac5fbae7238d04fc9adc251)\]. However, such approaches require controlled environments and are not suitable for online or dynamic calibration.

To overcome these limitations, recent work has shifted toward automatic, targetless calibration. One notable approach estimates poses by minimizing point-to-plane distances between multiple 3D LiDARs and radar scans, building a 3D map from LiDAR data and aligning radar scans without requiring overlapping fields of view \[[https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/0e88fc24ae7694d0b1a2fe78322936fc568bc7d2](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/0e88fc24ae7694d0b1a2fe78322936fc568bc7d2)\]. This method enables online calibration by using short vehicle drives rather than dedicated calibration setups. Similarly, continuous-time 3D radar-to-camera extrinsic calibration algorithms exploit radar velocity measurements and avoid the use of specialized retroreflectors, enhancing applicability in real-world conditions \[[2103.07505](http://arxiv.org/abs/2103.07505)\].

In parallel, sensor fusion research has contributed relevant insights for calibration. Radar-camera fusion methods for 3D object detection often incorporate spatial and contextual feature alignment techniques that implicitly rely on accurate extrinsic calibration. For example, proposal-level early fusion frameworks use cross-attention mechanisms to associate radar points with image proposals, improving detection robustness and accuracy \[[2209.06535](http://arxiv.org/abs/2209.06535)\]. Other approaches utilize bird’s-eye-view (BEV) representations to fuse radar and camera features effectively, with multi-modal deformable attention modules to handle spatial misalignment \[[2403.16440](http://arxiv.org/abs/2403.16440)\]\[[2307.10249](http://arxiv.org/abs/2307.10249)\]\[[2304.00670](http://arxiv.org/abs/2304.00670)\]. These fusion architectures demonstrate the importance of jointly modeling spatial correspondences between sensors, which is directly related to calibration quality.

Cross-modal feature matching techniques have also been explored to improve calibration and fusion robustness. Methods employing ray-constrained cross-attention exploit radar range measurements to resolve depth ambiguities in camera images, enhancing 3D object detection under sensor dropout scenarios \[[2210.09267](http://arxiv.org/abs/2210.09267)\]. Additionally, radar object detection networks supervised by camera-radar fused 3D localizations improve radar feature reliability for downstream tasks, indicating the benefit of integrated multi-sensor learning frameworks \[[2102.05150](http://arxiv.org/abs/2102.05150)\].

Noise and sparsity in radar data remain fundamental challenges. Some works address this by developing hybrid architectures combining point-based and grid-based processing to preserve fine-grained radar point cloud details while benefiting from convolutional feature extraction \[[2205.02111](http://arxiv.org/abs/2205.02111)\]. Graph-based neural networks for radar point-cloud recognition also show promise, although they currently lag behind LiDAR-based solutions in accuracy \[[https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/b5c3469beab918993c1c113cce97eb4e56b09df1](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/b5c3469beab918993c1c113cce97eb4e56b09df1)\]. Robust calibration methods must therefore incorporate mechanisms to handle radar measurement uncertainty and data sparsity.

Regarding supervision and robustness, recent fusion networks employ training strategies such as sensor dropout or noise-resistant matching to improve performance in adverse conditions and calibration stability \[[2210.09267](http://arxiv.org/abs/2210.09267)\]\[[2005.07431](http://arxiv.org/abs/2005.07431)\]. These strategies complement the development of adaptive fusion mechanisms that selectively integrate features from multiple perspectives or modalities, mitigating the impact of unreliable or uncertain inputs.

This paper’s proposed method builds upon these advances by introducing a Dual-Perspective representation that leverages both frontal and bird’s-eye views to exploit complementary height and spatial information. The Selective Fusion Mechanism aligns with the trend of adaptive fusion to reduce the effects of height uncertainty and data sparsity. Furthermore, the incorporation of Multi-Modal Cross-Attention for explicit location correspondence and a Noise-Resistant Matcher for enhanced supervision directly address the challenges highlighted in prior radar-camera calibration and fusion research. By enabling online, automatic geometric calibration without reliance on dedicated targets or offline procedures, this approach establishes a new benchmark consistent with the trajectory of prior work \[[https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/4d4dbbcf27d991aeab43dafb82a105bb5512b799](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/4d4dbbcf27d991aeab43dafb82a105bb5512b799)\]\[[2103.07505](http://arxiv.org/abs/2103.07505)\]\[[https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/0e88fc24ae7694d0b1a2fe78322936fc568bc7d2](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/0e88fc24ae7694d0b1a2fe78322936fc568bc7d2)\]\[[2210.09267](http://arxiv.org/abs/2210.09267)\]\[[2209.06535](http://arxiv.org/abs/2209.06535)\]\[[2304.00670](http://arxiv.org/abs/2304.00670)\].
