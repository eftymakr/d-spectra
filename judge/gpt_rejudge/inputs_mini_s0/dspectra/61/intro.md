## Related Works

Personalized outfit generation focuses on constructing sets of fashion items that are both compatible and aligned with individual user preferences. Early efforts in this domain emphasized compatibility modeling between items, often using visual and textual features to learn latent style spaces where compatible items cluster closely \[[1904.00741](http://arxiv.org/abs/1904.00741)\]\[[1803.09196](http://arxiv.org/abs/1803.09196)\]\[[1902.08009](http://arxiv.org/abs/1902.08009)\]. These methods typically leveraged deep neural networks or graph-based models to capture complex relationships among items within an outfit, enabling compatibility prediction and complementary item retrieval \[[1810.02443](http://arxiv.org/abs/1810.02443)\]\[[https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/4d431b88f7dfe1e75b0e23bb21fc9dd378b15cf2](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/4d431b88f7dfe1e75b0e23bb21fc9dd378b15cf2)\]\[[1902.08009](http://arxiv.org/abs/1902.08009)\]. However, such approaches often lacked explicit personalization, treating compatibility as a largely global aesthetic concept.

To address personalization, several frameworks incorporated user preferences into compatibility models by jointly modeling user-item interactions alongside item-item compatibility. For instance, GP-BPR introduced a personalized compatibility modeling scheme combining general compatibility with user-specific preferences, utilizing multimodal item representations to capture subjective tastes \[[https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/b1db62044adca574e4cf88b23c170f3cc089d513](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/b1db62044adca574e4cf88b23c170f3cc089d513)\]. Similarly, personalized outfit generation frameworks employed Transformer architectures to connect user preferences with outfit compatibility, demonstrating improvements in both personalization and compatibility metrics across large-scale datasets \[[1905.01866](http://arxiv.org/abs/1905.01866)\]. Attribute-wise interpretable models further enhanced this personalization by enabling fine-grained understanding of which fashion attributes contribute to compatibility or discordance for individual users \[[2008.01780](http://arxiv.org/abs/2008.01780)\]\[[https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/30fb5c24cc15eb8cde5e389bf368d65fb96513e4](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/30fb5c24cc15eb8cde5e389bf368d65fb96513e4)\].

Generative approaches have recently gained traction for outfit generation, moving beyond recommendation of existing items to synthesizing new fashion products tailored to users’ styles. Generative adversarial networks (GANs) have been applied to personalized fashion design, where latent user style vectors guide the generation of compatible items conditioned on query images, resulting in realistic and well-matched fashion items \[[https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/ca3ad34ff2652cd922c3476514a758674bc7e76b](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/ca3ad34ff2652cd922c3476514a758674bc7e76b)\]\[[1711.02231](http://arxiv.org/abs/1711.02231)\]. More advanced architectures, such as style-based GANs, improve the disentanglement of high-level attributes, enabling more controllable generation \[[1812.04948](http://arxiv.org/abs/1812.04948)\]. Diffusion models have also emerged as powerful generative frameworks, capable of parallel generation of multiple compatible fashion images with high fidelity and personalization, as evidenced by the DiFashion model applied to personalized outfit composition tasks \[[2402.17279](http://arxiv.org/abs/2402.17279)\].

Multi-modal data integration is critical in enhancing outfit generation and recommendation. Methods combining visual and textual information have improved outfit coherence and allowed conditioning on user queries or themes, such as occasion or style descriptions, enabling controllable and theme-consistent outfit generation \[[1906.07273](http://arxiv.org/abs/1906.07273)\]\[[1710.07346](http://arxiv.org/abs/1710.07346)\]\[[https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/4d431b88f7dfe1e75b0e23bb21fc9dd378b15cf2](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/4d431b88f7dfe1e75b0e23bb21fc9dd378b15cf2)\]. Such multimodal embeddings facilitate the alignment of item representations with user intents and outfit-level coherence.

Regarding model training paradigms, traditional supervised learning approaches rely heavily on annotated data with explicit compatibility labels or human-generated ratings, which can be costly and may limit diversity in generated outfits. Reinforcement learning from human feedback (RLHF) has been explored to fine-tune generation models by leveraging human preferences, although this approach can be complex and unstable \[[2305.18290](http://arxiv.org/abs/2305.18290)\]. Direct Preference Optimization (DPO) offers a promising alternative by framing the fine-tuning process as a classification problem using pairwise preference data, improving stability and efficiency without extensive reward engineering \[[2305.18290](http://arxiv.org/abs/2305.18290)\]. While DPO has been primarily studied in language models, its principles are transferable to fashion generative models to refine alignment with user preferences based on automatically generated multi-expert feedback covering quality, compatibility, and personalization aspects.

Complementary to these methods, explainable outfit recommendation advances have incorporated comment generation alongside outfit matching to provide interpretable feedback to users, improving trust and acceptance of recommendations \[[1806.08977](http://arxiv.org/abs/1806.08977)\]. Unsupervised style extraction techniques, such as bidirectional LSTMs combined with autoencoders, enable the discovery of latent style features from outfits, supporting flexible and interpretable style control in outfit generation \[[1807.03133](http://arxiv.org/abs/1807.03133)\]\[[1707.03376](http://arxiv.org/abs/1707.03376)\].

In summary, the landscape of personalized outfit generation encompasses compatibility modeling, personalized preference integration, generative model advancements, and novel training paradigms such as DPO. These approaches collectively aim to generate diverse, compatible, and user-aligned fashion outfits, addressing limitations of prior supervised and compatibility-only models.
