## Related Works

Protein dynamics are fundamental to understanding biological functions, traditionally studied through molecular dynamics (MD) simulations that are computationally intensive and often limited by rare event sampling and long equilibration times \[[https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/21a4afa74caf78e28880b7fe4c30a09a5f9c14e3](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/21a4afa74caf78e28880b7fe4c30a09a5f9c14e3)\]. The emergence of deep generative models, particularly diffusion-based frameworks, has transformed protein structure prediction and conformation sampling by learning distributions over crystallographic data, enabling faster and more diverse ensemble generation \[[2304.02198](http://arxiv.org/abs/2304.02198)\]\[[2209.15611](http://arxiv.org/abs/2209.15611)\]\[[2305.04120](http://arxiv.org/abs/2305.04120)\]. However, these data-driven methods face challenges in effectively integrating physical constraints, as standard energy-based objectives tend to create optimization complexity and intractability \[[https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/2eb1ed9d4baf548391d8220c0a8a3d0be79845ed](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/2eb1ed9d4baf548391d8220c0a8a3d0be79845ed)\].

Recent advances have addressed these challenges by combining physical principles with generative modeling. For example, force-guided SE(3) diffusion models incorporate physical prior knowledge directly into the generative process, improving the fidelity and diversity of sampled protein conformations. ConfDiff exemplifies this approach, using a force-guided network and data-driven score models to generate high-quality conformational ensembles surpassing prior methods \[[2403.14088](http://arxiv.org/abs/2403.14088)\]\[[2302.02277](http://arxiv.org/abs/2302.02277)\]. Similarly, FrameDiff and FoldFlow leverage SE(3)-equivariant flow and diffusion models to generate protein backbones with strong geometric and physical consistency, enabling stable training and diverse, designable structures without reliance on pretrained predictors \[[2302.02277](http://arxiv.org/abs/2302.02277)\]\[[2310.02391](http://arxiv.org/abs/2310.02391)\].

Diffusion models have been further specialized to capture protein conformational diversity and flexibility. EigenFold formulates protein structure prediction as a diffusion process over harmonic oscillator eigenmodes, enabling sampling of ensembles that reflect conformational heterogeneity, including fold-switching and ligand-induced changes \[[2304.02198](http://arxiv.org/abs/2304.02198)\]. The AlphaFlow and ESMFlow frameworks extend this approach by fine-tuning state-of-the-art single-structure predictors like AlphaFold under flow matching, achieving superior ensemble diversity and faster convergence to equilibrium properties than replicate MD trajectories \[[2402.04845](http://arxiv.org/abs/2402.04845)\].

Other generative models have incorporated broader physical realism and design constraints. The Boltzmann generator framework applies invertible neural networks to sample unbiased equilibrium states of many-body molecular systems from latent Gaussian distributions, efficiently overcoming rare-event sampling without predefined reaction coordinates \[[https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/2eb1ed9d4baf548391d8220c0a8a3d0be79845ed](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/2eb1ed9d4baf548391d8220c0a8a3d0be79845ed)\]. This approach offers a principled way to compute free-energy differences and explore metastable states in protein folding landscapes. Complementarily, DiG (Distributional Graphormer) employs deep neural networks inspired by thermodynamic annealing to transform simple latent distributions into equilibrium conformational ensembles conditioned on molecular descriptors, enabling fast and diverse sampling applicable to proteins and ligands \[[https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/1446b534858fc3fc6d9406920450b5c3ca87ee13](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/1446b534858fc3fc6d9406920450b5c3ca87ee13)\]\[[2306.05445](http://arxiv.org/abs/2306.05445)\].

Protein design efforts have also benefited from generative approaches that unify sequence and structure modeling. ProteinMPNN utilizes deep learning for robust sequence design on given backbones, outperforming classical physical methods and improving sequence recovery even under noisy structural models \[[https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/98926d43356e87c22c82efc132dcaaac1ff40ebe](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/98926d43356e87c22c82efc132dcaaac1ff40ebe)\]. Chroma introduces a diffusion-based generative model capable of sampling novel protein structures and complexes conditioned on functional or geometric constraints, leveraging Bayesian inference for flexible design with experimental validation confirming structural accuracy \[[https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/2cfc26b4af99f195b433fa7aa00f221b111c7cd4](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/2cfc26b4af99f195b433fa7aa00f221b111c7cd4)\].

Language models trained on large evolutionary-scale protein sequence data have further advanced structure prediction speed and accuracy. ESM (Evolutionary Scale Modeling) language models learn rich representations that enable atomic-level structure inference directly from primary sequences, facilitating rapid folding predictions at scale, including for orphan and fast-evolving proteins without homologs \[[https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/c49a0912595a1cc70aab63524f64ed08c92194a8](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/c49a0912595a1cc70aab63524f64ed08c92194a8)\]\[[https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/867d80c8779e1d301a5fc6e267e263f7e4c4c5c7](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/867d80c8779e1d301a5fc6e267e263f7e4c4c5c7)\].

Notably, recent methods have also emphasized zero-shot and simulation-free approaches to protein conformation sampling. Str2Str applies roto-translation equivariant score matching trained solely on crystal structures, enabling orders of magnitude faster sampling than traditional MD without relying on simulation data \[[2306.03117](http://arxiv.org/abs/2306.03117)\]. Similarly, latent diffusion models condense protein structure distributions into latent spaces for efficient generation of novel backbones with high designability and diversity \[[2305.04120](http://arxiv.org/abs/2305.04120)\]\[[2301.12485](http://arxiv.org/abs/2301.12485)\].

In summary, the integration of physical modeling, equivariant generative architectures, and deep learning has substantially advanced protein conformational ensemble generation. These methods address limitations of MD by providing rapid, diverse, and physically plausible samples, yet challenges remain in balancing computational efficiency with accurate energy-based supervision. The Energy-based Alignment (EBA) method proposed in this work builds on these foundations by efficiently calibrating generative models with physical feedback to improve the quality and physical realism of sampled ensembles.
