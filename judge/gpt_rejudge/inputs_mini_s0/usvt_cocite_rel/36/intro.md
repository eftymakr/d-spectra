## Related Works

Deploying large language models (LLMs) on resource-constrained edge devices poses significant challenges due to limited storage, compute capacity, power budgets, and strict latency requirements. Prior research has addressed these challenges through a combination of model compression, efficient inference algorithms, and specialized hardware acceleration, often employing a hardware-software co-design approach.

### Model Compression and Quantization

Model compression techniques such as pruning and quantization remain foundational for reducing the memory footprint and computation of LLMs. Wanda proposes a straightforward pruning method that removes weights based on their magnitude weighted by corresponding input activations, enabling sparsity without retraining and with minimal accuracy loss \[[2306.11695](http://arxiv.org/abs/2306.11695)\]. Quantization methods have also been extensively studied. LLM.int8() achieves 8-bit matrix multiplication for transformers by carefully isolating outlier features in mixed-precision, allowing large models (up to 175B parameters) to run with no performance degradation and halving memory usage \[[2208.07339](http://arxiv.org/abs/2208.07339)\]. Similarly, SmoothQuant introduces a training-free post-training quantization (PTQ) that shifts quantization difficulty from activations to weights, enabling 8-bit weight and activation quantization with up to 2× memory reduction and 1.56× speedup \[[2211.10438](http://arxiv.org/abs/2211.10438)\]. OmniQuant further enhances PTQ by making quantization parameters learnable and optimizing clipping and equivalent transformations, supporting extremely low-bit quantization (e.g., 4-bit weight and activation) while maintaining accuracy and improving inference speed \[[2308.13137](http://arxiv.org/abs/2308.13137)\]. SqueezeLLM combines sensitivity-based non-uniform quantization with a dense-and-sparse decomposition to achieve ultra-low precision (down to 3-bit) compression with reduced perplexity gap and up to 2.3× speedup \[[2306.07629](http://arxiv.org/abs/2306.07629)\]. AWQ focuses on on-device LLM compression by activation-aware weight quantization, reducing model size by 4× and accelerating inference by 3-4× on diverse edge platforms, balancing privacy and efficiency \[[2306.00978](http://arxiv.org/abs/2306.00978)\].

### Efficient Attention Mechanisms

The self-attention mechanism is a major computational bottleneck in LLMs due to its quadratic complexity with sequence length. Several algorithm-hardware co-designs target efficient attention computation. FlashAttention-2 improves upon FlashAttention by optimizing GPU thread-level parallelism and work partitioning, achieving 2× speedup over its predecessor and reaching up to 73% of theoretical peak FLOPs, facilitating faster training of GPT-style models \[[2307.08691](http://arxiv.org/abs/2307.08691)\]. ELSA proposes an approximate self-attention scheme combined with custom hardware, achieving a geometric mean speedup of 58.1× and drastic energy savings with minimal accuracy loss \[[https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/5af69480a7ae3b571df6782a11ec4437b386a7d9](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/5af69480a7ae3b571df6782a11ec4437b386a7d9)\]. Other sparse attention accelerators like SpAtten employ cascade token and head pruning to remove unimportant tokens and heads dynamically, reducing DRAM access by 10× and yielding speedups up to 347× over GPUs \[[2012.09852](http://arxiv.org/abs/2012.09852)\]. Energon utilizes dynamic sparse attention with multi-round filtering and a dedicated co-processor to deliver up to 168× speedup and significant energy reduction over CPU and GPU baselines \[[2110.09310](http://arxiv.org/abs/2110.09310)\]. Approximate attention acceleration approaches such as A³ leverage algorithmic approximation and hardware specialization to improve energy efficiency and speed \[[2002.10941](http://arxiv.org/abs/2002.10941)\].

### Hardware Acceleration on Edge Devices

FPGA-based accelerators have been widely explored for efficient LLM inference on edge platforms. NPE presents an FPGA overlay processor supporting a variety of NLP models with software-like programmability, achieving real-time conversational AI latency targets with substantially lower power consumption than CPUs and GPUs \[[2104.06535](http://arxiv.org/abs/2104.06535)\]. FTRANS demonstrates an FPGA acceleration framework employing block-circulant matrix-based compression and hardware optimizations, reducing model size up to 16× and achieving up to 81× energy efficiency compared to GPUs \[[2007.08563](http://arxiv.org/abs/2007.08563)\]. DFX uses multi-FPGA parallelism and custom instructions to accelerate GPT-2 inference end-to-end, delivering over 5× speedup and 4× energy efficiency improvements compared to NVIDIA V100 GPUs \[[2209.10797](http://arxiv.org/abs/2209.10797)\]. H3D-Transformer introduces a heterogeneous 3D computing platform combining compute-in-memory and digital tensor processors to boost energy efficiency by 2.6–3.1× compared to 7 nm TPUs for transformer models \[[https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/827b0b03f54ba43596193e5d47f1ff13b3afe293](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/827b0b03f54ba43596193e5d47f1ff13b3afe293)\]. These works highlight the benefits of co-designing algorithmic approximations with hardware capabilities to meet edge constraints.

### Memory and Runtime Optimizations

Memory management is critical for deploying LLMs with large KV caches during autoregressive decoding. PagedAttention addresses KV cache memory fragmentation and duplication through a paging-inspired attention algorithm and system design, achieving near-zero memory waste and 2–4× throughput improvement over state-of-the-art inference systems \[[2309.06180](http://arxiv.org/abs/2309.06180)\]. LLM in a flash explores running LLMs larger than available DRAM by storing parameters in flash memory and employing cost models to optimize data transfer and access patterns, achieving 4–25× speedups over naive approaches \[[2312.11514](http://arxiv.org/abs/2312.11514)\]. StreamingLLM tackles long-context streaming inference by leveraging the phenomenon of "attention sinks," enabling models to generalize to infinite sequence lengths efficiently without fine-tuning and providing up to 22× speedup over sliding window baselines \[[2309.17453](http://arxiv.org/abs/2309.17453)\]. LLMA improves decoding parallelism by leveraging references to reduce redundant computation, doubling inference speed in scenarios with overlapping context \[[2304.04487](http://arxiv.org/abs/2304.04487)\].

### Algorithm-Hardware Co-Design and Energy Efficiency

Several works emphasize the synergy of algorithmic and hardware innovation. DejaVu uses contextual sparsity prediction combined with asynchronous, hardware-aware implementation to reduce latency by over 2× compared to FasterTransformer and 6× over Hugging Face implementations without quality loss \[[2310.17157](http://arxiv.org/abs/2310.17157)\]. The surveyed hardware accelerators collectively demonstrate that co-design strategies—spanning pruning, quantization, sparse attention, memory optimization, and custom hardware—are essential to meet the stringent latency and energy constraints of edge LLM deployment \[[2401.09890](http://arxiv.org/abs/2401.09890)\]. Such approaches balance accuracy retention with throughput and power efficiency, aligning closely with the goals of CLONE.

In summary, the landscape of LLM deployment on edge devices encompasses a rich interplay of model compression, efficient attention computation, hardware specialization, and memory management techniques. The integration of these methods through algorithm-hardware co-design has proven effective for achieving fast, energy-efficient, and accurate LLM inference in constrained environments.
