## Related Works

Distributed training of large language models (LLMs) involves complex challenges in managing computation, memory, and communication across extensive hardware resources. Early research focused on scalable training methods and architectural innovations to enable models with billions of parameters. The Transformer architecture, which forgoes recurrence and convolutions in favor of attention mechanisms, laid the foundation for most large-scale LLMs by enabling efficient parallelization and superior performance on NLP tasks \[[1706.03762](http://arxiv.org/abs/1706.03762)\]. Subsequent works demonstrated that increasing model scale, such as training models with billions of parameters, leads to significant improvements in language understanding and generation capabilities \[[2005.14165](http://arxiv.org/abs/2005.14165)\]\[[1909.08053](http://arxiv.org/abs/1909.08053)\].

A key challenge in scaling LLMs is the efficient utilization of distributed computing resources. Model parallelism techniques have been developed to partition large models across many GPUs, addressing memory bottlenecks and maximizing throughput without requiring drastic changes to existing frameworks \[[1909.08053](http://arxiv.org/abs/1909.08053)\]. These approaches have enabled training models with billions of parameters on hundreds of GPUs, sustaining high computational efficiency. However, optimizing the interplay between computation and communication remains an open problem, especially given the heterogeneity of deployment environments.

Instruction tuning and fine-tuning methods have further advanced LLM capabilities by improving zero-shot and few-shot generalization without extensive task-specific data \[[2109.01652](http://arxiv.org/abs/2109.01652)\]\[[2304.12244](http://arxiv.org/abs/2304.12244)\]\[[2305.14314](http://arxiv.org/abs/2305.14314)\]. Instruction tuning, which involves fine-tuning on diverse tasks expressed as natural language instructions, has been shown to enhance performance across unseen tasks and reduce reliance on gradient updates during inference \[[2109.01652](http://arxiv.org/abs/2109.01652)\]. Similarly, self-generated instruction data and AI-driven instruction evolution have been used to fine-tune open models, yielding outputs competitive with proprietary systems \[[2304.12244](http://arxiv.org/abs/2304.12244)\]\[[2306.04751](http://arxiv.org/abs/2306.04751)\].

Performance modeling plays a crucial role in guiding optimization and deployment strategies for large-scale LLM training. Accurate models that predict runtime behaviors, resource utilization, and bottlenecks enable systematic exploration of configuration spaces and efficient scheduling. Prior works have explored performance characterization for both training and inference phases, including methods that optimize distributed inference latency and cost on heterogeneous hardware \[[2305.14314](http://arxiv.org/abs/2305.14314)\]\[[2302.04761](http://arxiv.org/abs/2302.04761)\]. These frameworks often rely on profiling and trace-driven techniques to build predictive models that support decision-making in complex environments.

Recent advances in self-supervised and verification-based learning approaches have also influenced LLM training efficiency by improving multi-step reasoning and robustness in output generation \[[2110.14168](http://arxiv.org/abs/2110.14168)\]. Verification methods that evaluate multiple candidate solutions and select the highest-quality output have demonstrated increased accuracy on challenging tasks such as math word problems, suggesting promising directions for incorporating feedback loops into training and inference workflows.

The development of efficient fine-tuning strategies for large, quantized models further complements performance modeling efforts. Techniques like QLoRA enable finetuning of very large models on limited hardware resources without sacrificing performance, thereby broadening accessibility and facilitating rapid iteration on model improvements \[[2305.14314](http://arxiv.org/abs/2305.14314)\].

Finally, large-scale evaluations and benchmarking frameworks have become essential for assessing LLM capabilities and guiding future research. Comprehensive multitask evaluations reveal persistent challenges in reasoning, factuality, and domain adaptation despite advances in scale and instruction tuning \[[2005.14165](http://arxiv.org/abs/2005.14165)\]\[[2009.033](http://arxiv.org/abs/2009.03300)\]\[[2303.08774](http://arxiv.org/abs/2303.08774)\]. Additionally, the use of LLMs themselves as judges to evaluate chatbots and language models offers scalable mechanisms to approximate human preferences and assess model quality in open-ended settings \[[2306.05685](http://arxiv.org/abs/2306.05685)\].

In summary, the landscape of LLM distributed training and optimization integrates advances in scalable architectures, instruction tuning, verification-based learning, fine-tuning methods, and performance modeling. Our work builds on these foundations by providing a trace-driven toolkit, Lumos, that accurately captures execution behaviors and predicts performance across diverse configurations and large-scale hardware setups, enabling more effective optimization and deployment of modern LLMs.
