## Related Works

Fund allocation in financial domains inherently depends on accurate time series forecasting, yet traditional approaches often face limitations due to misaligned objectives and forecasting uncertainty. The Predict-then-Optimize (PnO) paradigm, which sequentially applies forecasting followed by optimization, is widely used but suffers from goal mismatch because the forecasting loss does not necessarily translate into optimal allocation decisions \[[2205.14415](http://arxiv.org/abs/2205.14415)\]. Efforts to mitigate this include end-to-end frameworks that jointly align forecasting and allocation objectives, improving performance by integrating these tasks in a single model \[[2205.13504](http://arxiv.org/abs/2205.13504)\]\[[2210.02186](http://arxiv.org/abs/2210.02186)\]. However, many of these approaches rely on assumptions about the forecasting model's structure, reducing their generality and adaptability across diverse forecasting backbones \[[1704.0411](http://arxiv.org/abs/1704.04110)\].

Recent advances in time series forecasting have leveraged Transformer-based architectures, which excel at capturing long-range dependencies and complex temporal patterns. Variants such as PatchTST adopt channel-independent patching to enhance long-term forecasting accuracy and allow for effective self-supervised pretraining \[[2211.1473](http://arxiv.org/abs/2211.14730)\]. The Temporal Fusion Transformer (TFT) incorporates recurrent layers and interpretable attention mechanisms to handle multivariate inputs and multi-horizon forecasting with improved interpretability \[[1912.09363](http://arxiv.org/abs/1912.09363)\]. Autoformer introduces an Auto-Correlation mechanism coupled with progressive decomposition to overcome challenges in modeling intricate long-term temporal patterns, achieving state-of-the-art results on multiple benchmarks \[[2106.13008](http://arxiv.org/abs/2106.13008)\]. Similarly, iTransformer redesigns attention by inverting dimensions to better model multivariate series and improve performance on large lookback windows \[[2310.06625](http://arxiv.org/abs/2310.06625)\]. Despite these successes, some studies question the effectiveness of Transformers for time series forecasting, showing that simple linear models can outperform complex Transformer variants on long-term forecasting benchmarks, highlighting the challenges of temporal relation extraction inherent in self-attention mechanisms \[[2205.13504](http://arxiv.org/abs/2205.13504)\].

Uncertainty quantification remains critical in financial forecasting due to the high stakes of decision-making under risk. Classical methods like Monte Carlo dropout and Bayesian neural networks provide probabilistic forecasts but can be computationally expensive or model-specific \[[2310.01728](http://arxiv.org/abs/2310.01728)\]\[[2004.13408](http://arxiv.org/abs/2004.13408)\]. Conformal prediction techniques offer distribution-free uncertainty calibration, yet their adaptation to complex forecasting models is ongoing \[[2106.13008](http://arxiv.org/abs/2106.13008)\]. Hybrid models combining deep learning with classical statistical components facilitate uncertainty estimation while capturing complex global and local patterns, as demonstrated by Deep Factors \[[1905.12417](http://arxiv.org/abs/1905.12417)\]. Moreover, frameworks addressing non-stationarity in time series, such as Non-stationary Transformers, propose series stationarization and de-stationary attention modules to balance predictability and capture intrinsic bursty behaviors, significantly improving forecast accuracy \[[2205.14415](http://arxiv.org/abs/2205.14415)\].

The rise of large language models (LLMs) for time series analysis opens new avenues for financial forecasting. By framing time series forecasting as a next-token prediction task, LLMs like GPT-3 and LLaMA-2 have shown competitive zero-shot extrapolation capabilities, naturally modeling multimodal distributions and handling missing data without imputation \[[2310.0782](http://arxiv.org/abs/2310.07820)\]. Further, reprogramming LLMs with prompt-based techniques enhances their adaptability to time series forecasting tasks, achieving strong few-shot and zero-shot performance without modifying the backbone language models \[[2310.01728](http://arxiv.org/abs/2310.01728)\]\[[2310.04948](http://arxiv.org/abs/2310.04948)\]. In financial contexts, LLMs have been employed to integrate diverse multimodal signals such as textual news and metadata alongside price series, providing explainable forecasting and cross-sequence reasoning that improve prediction quality \[[2306.11025](http://arxiv.org/abs/2306.11025)\]. However, integrating LLMs effectively into allocation frameworks remains an open challenge.

General-purpose architectures beyond Transformers have also gained interest. TimesNet transforms 1D series into 2D tensors capturing multi-periodicity, enabling modeling of complex intraperiod and interperiod variations via 2D convolutions, achieving state-of-the-art results across forecasting, classification, and anomaly detection tasks \[[2210.02186](http://arxiv.org/abs/2210.02186)\]. All-MLP architectures like TSMixer exploit mixing operations along time and feature dimensions to efficiently extract cross-variate information and demonstrate superior performance on large-scale retail datasets \[[2303.06053](http://arxiv.org/abs/2303.06053)\]. SparseTSF offers a parameter-efficient solution for long-term forecasting by decoupling trend and periodicity via cross-period sparse forecasting, requiring fewer than 1,000 parameters while maintaining competitive accuracy \[[2405.00946](http://arxiv.org/abs/2405.00946)\].

Pretrained foundation models for time series are emerging to unify diverse tasks under a single architecture. The Frozen Pretrained Transformer (FPT) leverages models pretrained on natural language or image data, demonstrating state-of-the-art performance across forecasting, classification, anomaly detection, and few-shot learning tasks by bridging domain gaps via self-attention's PCA-like behavior \[[2302.11939](http://arxiv.org/abs/2302.11939)\]. Furthermore, decoder-only foundation models pretrained on large time-series corpora enable zero-shot forecasting across various temporal granularities and lookback lengths, showing competitive accuracy without task-specific tuning \[[2310.10688](http://arxiv.org/abs/2310.10688)\]. Prompt-based generative pretrained transformers like TEMPO incorporate decomposition and prompt design to enhance distribution adaptation and domain generalization, yielding superior zero-shot results on multiple benchmarks \[[2310.04948](http://arxiv.org/abs/2310.04948)\].

Overall, existing literature highlights the critical need for frameworks that integrate forecasting and allocation while addressing uncertainty and model adaptability. Most current methods either assume fixed forecasting models or lack uncertainty calibration tailored to financial decision-making. The proposed RTS-PnO framework advances this space by enabling end-to-end training with objective alignment, adaptively calibrating forecasting uncertainty, and remaining agnostic to the underlying forecasting architecture, thus addressing key gaps identified across forecasting and allocation research \[[2211.1473](http://arxiv.org/abs/2211.14730)\]\[[2205.14415](http://arxiv.org/abs/2205.14415)\]\[[2205.13504](http://arxiv.org/abs/2205.13504)\]\[[2310.06625](http://arxiv.org/abs/2310.06625)\]\[[2310.01728](http://arxiv.org/abs/2310.01728)\]\[[2310.0782](http://arxiv.org/abs/2310.07820)\]\[[2310.04948](http://arxiv.org/abs/2310.04948)\]\[[2302.11939](http://arxiv.org/abs/2302.11939)\].
