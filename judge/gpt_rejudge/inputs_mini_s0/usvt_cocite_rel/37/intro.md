## Related Works

Vision Transformers (ViTs) have established themselves as a compelling alternative to convolutional neural networks (CNNs) by leveraging self-attention mechanisms to capture global contextual information in images \[[2010.11929](http://arxiv.org/abs/2010.11929)\]. Unlike CNNs, ViTs process images as sequences of patches, enabling superior modeling of long-range dependencies, which has been shown to improve accuracy on diverse vision tasks \[[2010.11929](http://arxiv.org/abs/2010.11929)\]\[[2103.1403](http://arxiv.org/abs/2103.14030)\]. However, the inherent complexity of ViTs introduces significant challenges for efficient hardware deployment, particularly on resource-constrained platforms such as FPGAs.

### Hardware Acceleration Challenges and Approaches

The main bottlenecks in accelerating ViTs stem from the computationally intensive multi-head self-attention modules and complex nonlinear operations like Softmax and LayerNorm. These operations are particularly sensitive to quantization and precision scaling, which complicates their efficient mapping to fixed-point or integer hardware units without sacrificing model accuracy \[[2207.01405](http://arxiv.org/abs/2207.01405)\]\[[2111.13824](http://arxiv.org/abs/2111.13824)\]. Traditional FPGA accelerators often focus on optimizing matrix multiplications but leave Softmax and LayerNorm computations on the CPU, introducing communication overhead and limiting overall throughput \[[2208.05163](http://arxiv.org/abs/2208.05163)\]\[[2211.0811](http://arxiv.org/abs/2211.08110)\].

Several frameworks have been proposed to address these issues by integrating quantization and pruning techniques to reduce computational complexity while preserving accuracy. For instance, I-ViT introduces integer-only quantization schemes that approximate nonlinear functions such as Softmax and GELU via integer bit-shifting operations, enabling efficient integer arithmetic pipelines without floating-point operations. This approach achieves comparable accuracy to full-precision models and delivers substantial inference speedups on GPUs \[[2207.01405](http://arxiv.org/abs/2207.01405)\]. Similarly, FQ-ViT presents a fully quantized ViT approach that handles inter-channel variation and non-uniform attention map distributions through novel quantization techniques, achieving near lossless accuracy degradation with low-bit-width operations \[[2111.13824](http://arxiv.org/abs/2111.13824)\].

Token pruning methods have also been effective in reducing redundant computation by dynamically or statically discarding less informative tokens. DynamicViT employs a lightweight prediction module to prune tokens hierarchically during inference, achieving up to 40% throughput improvement with minimal accuracy loss \[[2106.02034](http://arxiv.org/abs/2106.02034)\]. HeatViT extends this concept to hardware-aware adaptive token pruning on FPGAs, combining 8-bit quantization and polynomial approximations of nonlinear functions to boost efficiency with minimal overhead \[[2211.0811](http://arxiv.org/abs/2211.08110)\]. Patch Slimming follows a top-down approach to remove patches with little impact on output features, reducing computational costs significantly without accuracy degradation \[[2106.02852](http://arxiv.org/abs/2106.02852)\]. These token and patch reduction strategies help alleviate the burden of the attention mechanism, which dominates ViT computation.

### Algorithm-Hardware Co-Design and Specialized Accelerators

Recent works emphasize co-design approaches that jointly optimize ViT algorithms and hardware architectures. ViTCoD proposes pruning and polarizing attention maps with fixed sparsity patterns, combined with learnable auto-encoder modules to trade expensive data movements for cheaper computations. This co-design enables a dedicated accelerator to exploit workload regularity and reduces data movement costs, resulting in speedups of up to 235× over CPU baselines and substantial improvements over prior accelerators \[[2210.09573](http://arxiv.org/abs/2210.09573)\]. Auto-ViT-Acc introduces a mixed-scheme quantization aware of FPGA constraints and accelerates ViTs by integrating quantization into the hardware design, achieving over 5× frame rate improvements with minor accuracy loss \[[2208.05163](http://arxiv.org/abs/2208.05163)\]. VAQF delivers a fully automatic software-hardware co-design framework to generate FPGA accelerators tailored to quantized ViTs, meeting target frame rates with optimized quantization precisions and hardware parameters \[[2201.06618](http://arxiv.org/abs/2201.06618)\].

Other architectural innovations aim to improve baseline ViT efficiency through structural design changes. CvT integrates convolutional token embeddings and convolutional projections into Transformer blocks, inheriting convolutional invariances while maintaining global attention, which improves performance and reduces FLOPs \[[2103.15808](http://arxiv.org/abs/2103.15808)\]. Swin Transformer introduces hierarchical representations with shifted windows to limit attention computations locally but maintain cross-window connections, achieving state-of-the-art results on classification and dense prediction tasks with linear complexity relative to image size \[[2103.1403](http://arxiv.org/abs/2103.14030)\]. Tokens-to-Token ViT progressively aggregates tokens to capture local structures better and reduce token sequence length, improving training efficiency and accuracy \[[2101.11986](http://arxiv.org/abs/2101.11986)\]. Transformer in Transformer (TNT) further decomposes patches into subpatches, applying nested attention to enhance feature richness \[[2103.00112](http://arxiv.org/abs/2103.00112)\].

### Unified Compression and Sparsity Techniques

Unified frameworks have emerged to compress ViTs by combining pruning, layer skipping, and knowledge distillation, optimizing for computational budgets while maintaining accuracy. Such methods have successfully halved FLOPs on DeiT-Tiny models with negligible accuracy loss \[[2203.08243](http://arxiv.org/abs/2203.08243)\]. Additionally, end-to-end sparsity exploration dynamically trains sparse subnetworks with learnable token selectors and structured pruning of self-attention heads, achieving significant FLOPs reduction and sometimes improving accuracy due to regularization effects \[[2106.04533](http://arxiv.org/abs/2106.04533)\].

### Summary

Despite these advances, an outstanding challenge remains in mapping the full ViT computational graph—including nonlinear operations such as Softmax and LayerNorm—onto efficient, low-precision hardware without offloading to CPUs. Prior works either approximate or quantize these operations but do not fully integrate them into FPGA accelerators, resulting in communication overhead and suboptimal utilization \[[2208.05163](http://arxiv.org/abs/2208.05163)\]\[[2207.01405](http://arxiv.org/abs/2207.01405)\]\[[2111.13824](http://arxiv.org/abs/2111.13824)\]\[[2211.0811](http://arxiv.org/abs/2211.08110)\]. The present work’s use of a novel Microscaling Integer (MXInt) arithmetic format to quantize ViTs and implement all operations directly on FPGA hardware provides a significant step forward, offering a compelling trade-off between accuracy, area efficiency, and speedup over existing floating-point and quantized FPGA accelerators.
