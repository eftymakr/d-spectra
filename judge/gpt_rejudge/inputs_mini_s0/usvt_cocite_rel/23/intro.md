## Related Works

Radar-camera calibration is a critical component in autonomous vehicle perception, enabling accurate sensor fusion for robust environment understanding. Traditional approaches primarily relied on target-based calibration methods, which require specialized calibration targets or controlled environments. For example, early extrinsic calibration methods often utilized calibration boards or corner reflectors to estimate the spatial transformation between sensors \[[2103.07505](http://arxiv.org/abs/2103.07505)\]\[[https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/4d4dbbcf27d991aeab43dafb82a105bb5512b799](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/4d4dbbcf27d991aeab43dafb82a105bb5512b799)\]. These methods, while effective under controlled conditions, are impractical for online or in-field calibration due to the need for dedicated setups and the inability to adapt to sensor misalignment or decalibration during operation.

To overcome these limitations, recent research has focused on targetless and automatic calibration methods. A prominent class of methods leverages data-driven or motion-based cues to estimate sensor poses without requiring explicit calibration targets. One such approach employs moving object tracking and detection across sensors to associate observations and recover extrinsic parameters online, providing robustness to decalibration during system operation \[[https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/c80265fa719eb85c8a56a6b36c74b99b793f39b2](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/c80265fa719eb85c8a56a6b36c74b99b793f39b2)\]\[[https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/90d807f4e4999443ce9910ba451675f3b9ce4ba0](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/90d807f4e4999443ce9910ba451675f3b9ce4ba0)\]. These methods exploit the fact that autonomous systems already perform object detection and tracking, enabling calibration without additional infrastructure.

Deep learning-based calibration techniques have also emerged, using convolutional neural networks to extract common features from radar and camera data for automatic rotational calibration \[[1904.08743](http://arxiv.org/abs/1904.08743)\]\[[2103.07825](http://arxiv.org/abs/2103.07825)\]. For instance, representation learning frameworks enable the discovery of cross-modal correspondences despite the sparse and noisy nature of radar data, improving robustness and precision in sensor registration \[[2103.07825](http://arxiv.org/abs/2103.07825)\]. Moreover, these methods often incorporate boosting or residual learning strategies to refine calibration estimates and generalize across sensor configurations.

Several works have addressed the unique challenges posed by radar data sparsity and measurement uncertainty, particularly in the elevation or height dimension. Methods combining radar cross-section (RCS) information with geometric constraints have been proposed to improve 6-DoF extrinsic calibration accuracy, by exploiting patterns in radar reflectivity correlated to sensor misalignments \[[https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/0955326c67a947b5fac5fbae7238d04fc9adc251](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/0955326c67a947b5fac5fbae7238d04fc9adc251)\]\[[2403.16440](http://arxiv.org/abs/2403.16440)\]. These approaches often involve multi-step optimization procedures designed to refine parameters with high uncertainty, such as vertical misalignment.

Spatiotemporal calibration methods extend this framework by jointly estimating spatial transformations and temporal offsets between radar and camera sensors, critical for synchronizing data streams in real-time applications. Such methods utilize ego-velocity measurements from radar and Gaussian process-based trajectory estimation to perform continuous-time joint optimization without specialized targets \[[https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/90d807f4e4999443ce9910ba451675f3b9ce4ba0](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/90d807f4e4999443ce9910ba451675f3b9ce4ba0)\]\[[2211.01871](http://arxiv.org/abs/2211.01871)\]. These techniques demonstrate improved calibration accuracy and temporal alignment, essential for robust sensor fusion in dynamic environments.

In terms of data representation and fusion strategies, radar-camera calibration benefits from multi-perspective approaches that reconcile the complementary characteristics of each sensor modality. Bird's-eye view (BEV) representations have been widely adopted for their robustness to height uncertainty and occlusions, facilitating fusion with camera image features extracted from frontal views \[[https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/a94f8a34a9c4fce90727f62d652f2420fd1021a2](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/a94f8a34a9c4fce90727f62d652f2420fd1021a2)\]\[[2403.07746](http://arxiv.org/abs/2403.07746)\]\[[2403.16440](http://arxiv.org/abs/2403.16440)\]. Recent fusion architectures employ cross-attention mechanisms to identify reliable correspondences between radar and camera features at multiple spatial resolutions, mitigating the impact of radar sparsity and enhancing detection performance \[[2403.07746](http://arxiv.org/abs/2403.07746)\]\[[https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/cc183eba49f926d84b3a401490c819be64b9b222](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/cc183eba49f926d84b3a401490c819be64b9b222)\]\[[2403.16440](http://arxiv.org/abs/2403.16440)\].

Additionally, methods leveraging velocity and optical flow information have been proposed to recover full-velocity radar returns by fusing radar Doppler measurements with camera motion cues, further enriching the feature space for calibration and object detection \[[2108.10637](http://arxiv.org/abs/2108.10637)\]. Such approaches address the incomplete velocity information inherent in radar data and improve temporal integration for dynamic scene understanding.

The proposed method advances this body of work by introducing a Dual-Perspective representation that harnesses both frontal and bird's-eye views to address radar height uncertainty and data sparsity. It incorporates a Selective Fusion Mechanism to adaptively combine reliable features from both views and a Multi-Modal Cross-Attention Mechanism to explicitly establish spatial correspondences through cross-modal matching. Additionally, the Noise-Resistant Matcher enhances robustness against sparse and noisy radar data, improving supervision during training. These innovations build upon and extend prior targetless, deep learning-based, and spatiotemporal calibration frameworks \[[https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/0955326c67a947b5fac5fbae7238d04fc9adc251](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/0955326c67a947b5fac5fbae7238d04fc9adc251)\]\[[1904.08743](http://arxiv.org/abs/1904.08743)\]\[[https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/c80265fa719eb85c8a56a6b36c74b99b793f39b2](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/c80265fa719eb85c8a56a6b36c74b99b793f39b2)\]\[[https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/90d807f4e4999443ce9910ba451675f3b9ce4ba0](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/90d807f4e4999443ce9910ba451675f3b9ce4ba0)\]\[[2103.07825](http://arxiv.org/abs/2103.07825)\]\[[2211.01871](http://arxiv.org/abs/2211.01871)\]\[[2403.07746](http://arxiv.org/abs/2403.07746)\]\[[2403.16440](http://arxiv.org/abs/2403.16440)\], setting a new benchmark for online radar-camera calibration in autonomous systems.
