## Related Works

Neural rendering has become a prominent approach for reconstructing 3D scenes from 2D images, leveraging implicit neural representations to jointly model scene geometry and appearance. Classical multi-view methods optimize scene representations from multiple input images but face limitations when the number of views is insufficient or when certain object viewpoints remain unobserved, especially in dynamic scenes \[[2106.10689](http://arxiv.org/abs/2106.10689)\]\[[2007.11571](http://arxiv.org/abs/2007.11571)\]. The quality of reconstruction is thus constrained by data completeness and diversity.

Video frame interpolation (VFI) has been extensively studied as a technique to synthesize intermediate frames between consecutive video frames, effectively increasing temporal resolution and providing more comprehensive scene coverage. Traditional VFI approaches often rely on two-step pipelines involving explicit motion estimation (e.g., optical flow) followed by pixel synthesis. However, these methods can suffer from errors due to inaccurate flow estimation, occlusions, large motions, or lighting variations \[[1703.07514](http://arxiv.org/abs/1703.07514)\]\[[https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/bb4a9650ca3946c70a7e92007cc61dc0dfd75522](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/bb4a9650ca3946c70a7e92007cc61dc0dfd75522)\]\[[1803.10967](http://arxiv.org/abs/1803.10967)\]. To overcome these challenges, recent deep learning-based methods integrate motion estimation and frame synthesis into a single end-to-end framework.

Early kernel-based interpolation methods formulated frame synthesis as adaptive convolutions over input frames, estimating spatially varying kernels that implicitly capture local motion and blending weights \[[1703.07514](http://arxiv.org/abs/1703.07514)\]\[[1708.01692](http://arxiv.org/abs/1708.01692)\]\[[https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/3a6e72172b7e1fb3b45f53e9aaf7d42e427c26de](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/3a6e72172b7e1fb3b45f53e9aaf7d42e427c26de)\]. These approaches benefit from end-to-end training on large video datasets without requiring ground-truth optical flow but often face trade-offs between kernel size, memory demands, and motion range. Deformable kernel methods extend this concept by learning pixel-wise offsets and masks, enabling the modeling of larger and more complex motion fields \[[https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/3a6e72172b7e1fb3b45f53e9aaf7d42e427c26de](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/3a6e72172b7e1fb3b45f53e9aaf7d42e427c26de)\]\[[1907.10244](http://arxiv.org/abs/1907.10244)\]. Other works have directly learned pixel motion representations, such as phase-based approaches, which handle subtle motions and lighting changes robustly but have limited applicability to large displacements \[[1804.00884](http://arxiv.org/abs/1804.00884)\]\[[https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/87b83fefb82216aad1c14cd1898d195722bcee42](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/87b83fefb82216aad1c14cd1898d195722bcee42)\].

More recent methods incorporate explicit optical flow estimation into their pipelines, often employing bi-directional flows and visibility maps to handle occlusions and improve frame fusion quality \[[1712.00080](http://arxiv.org/abs/1712.00080)\]\[[1803.10967](http://arxiv.org/abs/1803.10967)\]\[[1810.08768](http://arxiv.org/abs/1810.08768)\]. For example, Super SloMo uses a U-Net architecture to compute and refine optical flows and visibility masks, enabling high-quality interpolation of multiple intermediate frames \[[1712.00080](http://arxiv.org/abs/1712.00080)\]. Similarly, methods like BMBC and DAIN enhance flow accuracy through bilateral motion estimation or depth-aware flow projection, improving interpolation in challenging scenarios with occlusions and large object motions \[[2007.12622](http://arxiv.org/abs/2007.12622)\]\[[1904.00830](http://arxiv.org/abs/1904.00830)\].

To address the computational complexity and inefficiency of large models, dynamic architectures have been proposed that adapt their computational resources based on local motion magnitude, efficiently handling static and dynamic regions differently \[[https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/4b2ff4acdbefe5a0aafa8b989f28f220f9c750bb](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/4b2ff4acdbefe5a0aafa8b989f28f220f9c750bb)\]. Additionally, approaches like softmax splatting enable differentiable forward warping, improving interpolation quality and enabling arbitrary time-step synthesis \[[2003.05534](http://arxiv.org/abs/2003.05534)\].

The integration of VFI into neural rendering presents a promising data augmentation strategy to alleviate view sparsity. By generating intermediate frames between existing views, VFI enriches the training data, providing implicit priors on camera motion and scene geometry. Unsupervised and cycle-consistency-based training methods further enable adapting pre-trained interpolation models to new domains without additional ground-truth data \[[1906.05928](http://arxiv.org/abs/1906.05928)\]\[[https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/380f46cd24a15271036ff49e9e0d91f39f89d8b4](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/380f46cd24a15271036ff49e9e0d91f39f89d8b4)\]. This augmentation can improve neural rendering performance on both static and dynamic scenes by increasing the diversity and temporal coherence of input data.

Lightweight yet high-quality VFI models designed for high-resolution and real-time applications have emerged, such as IM-Net, which formulates motion estimation as a classification problem and achieves superior performance with low latency \[[https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/089f2072c95c244f3b7f4df404562bb4afe24448](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/089f2072c95c244f3b7f4df404562bb4afe24448)\]. Similarly, the proposed PS4PRO model fits into this line of research by training on diverse video datasets to implicitly encode camera movement and 3D geometry, acting as a learned world prior that enriches photorealistic rendering supervision.

Beyond interpolation, neural implicit scene representations such as Neural Sparse Voxel Fields (NSVF) and NeuS have demonstrated the ability to model 3D scenes from posed 2D images without explicit 3D supervision, leveraging differentiable volume rendering and implicit functions to achieve high-fidelity reconstructions \[[2007.11571](http://arxiv.org/abs/2007.11571)\]\[[2106.10689](http://arxiv.org/abs/2106.10689)\]. However, their performance depends heavily on input view diversity, which can be augmented through VFI-generated frames to provide more comprehensive scene coverage.

In summary, the intersection of video frame interpolation and neural rendering leverages advances in deep learning-based motion estimation, adaptive convolutional synthesis, and unsupervised training techniques to augment training data and improve 3D reconstruction quality. The proposed approach builds upon these foundations by introducing a lightweight, high-quality interpolation network that serves as an implicit prior, enabling enriched photorealistic supervision for neural rendering models and enhancing reconstruction performance in complex dynamic scenes.
