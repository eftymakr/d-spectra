## Related Works

Cross-domain sequential recommendation (CDSR) focuses on predicting user interactions across multiple domains by leveraging sequential behavioral data, addressing challenges such as data sparsity and cold-start problems \[[2304.03891](http://arxiv.org/abs/2304.03891)\]. Traditional sequential recommendation (SR) models often rely on Markov Chains or Recurrent Neural Networks (RNNs) to capture user behavior patterns, but these methods struggle with long-term dependencies or scalability \[[1808.09781](http://arxiv.org/abs/1808.09781)\]\[[1904.06690](http://arxiv.org/abs/1904.06690)\]. The self-attention mechanism, notably employed in Transformer-based models like SASRec and BERT4Rec, has become a dominant approach due to its ability to capture long-range dependencies and adapt to varying data sparsity levels efficiently \[[1808.09781](http://arxiv.org/abs/1808.09781)\]\[[1904.06690](http://arxiv.org/abs/1904.06690)\].

Early CDSR works mostly extended single-domain SR models by incorporating domain-specific modules or knowledge transfer techniques to handle cross-domain interactions. For example, CoNet proposed collaborative cross networks enabling dual knowledge transfer between domains via cross-layer mappings \[[1804.06769](http://arxiv.org/abs/1804.06769)\]. Similarly, BiTGCF exploited bi-directional transfer learning on graph collaborative filtering networks to fuse common and domain-specific user features \[[https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/d5630291a4ba3f6266c77e6d00920502f986afa6](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/d5630291a4ba3f6266c77e6d00920502f986afa6)\]. These approaches typically rely on overlapping users across domains to facilitate knowledge transfer, which can limit their applicability in real-world scenarios with sparse user overlap \[[2311.04590](http://arxiv.org/abs/2311.04590)\]\[[https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/27670ed18e96550c674e022e284fc5356a7458c2](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/27670ed18e96550c674e022e284fc5356a7458c2)\].

Recent research has addressed these limitations by designing models that more explicitly capture inter-domain relationships and sequential patterns. Graph neural network (GNN)-based methods, such as DA-GCN and TiDA-GCN, model users and items as graphs and incorporate domain-aware attention mechanisms and time interval information to better capture cross-domain and temporal dynamics in shared-account scenarios \[[2105.03300](http://arxiv.org/abs/2105.03300)\]\[[2206.08050](http://arxiv.org/abs/2206.08050)\]. Parallel architectures like π-Net and PSJNet handle shared-account cross-domain recommendation by splitting and joining role-specific representations and utilizing gating mechanisms to filter and transfer information across domains \[[https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/fa990aee9a8f157b5d393f5f3eaa014e1e5c67aa](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/fa990aee9a8f157b5d393f5f3eaa014e1e5c67aa)\]\[[1910.02448](http://arxiv.org/abs/1910.02448)\]. Reinforcement learning has also been applied to refine domain filtering and enhance user identification within shared accounts \[[2206.08088](http://arxiv.org/abs/2206.08088)\].

Another line of work focuses on integrating multi-objective and contrastive learning techniques to enhance cross-domain representation learning. Contrastive learning frameworks like C2DSR and MGCL leverage mutual information maximization across intra- and inter-domain views to better align user preferences and item relationships \[[2304.03891](http://arxiv.org/abs/2304.03891)\]\[[https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/d1c7b8be5f3849a3fd4af780b58eb28355641206](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/d1c7b8be5f3849a3fd4af780b58eb28355641206)\]. DREAM proposes decoupled representation learning with supervised contrastive learning and focal loss to jointly model single- and cross-domain preferences, addressing data sparsity and class imbalance \[[https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/96bfe33a2503e960153a934e10be91da4d8b13a6](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/96bfe33a2503e960153a934e10be91da4d8b13a6)\]. Similarly, Tri-CDR models triple sequences (source, target, and mixed behaviors) with triple cross-domain attention and contrastive learning to capture global and domain-specific preferences simultaneously \[[2304.05027](http://arxiv.org/abs/2304.05027)\].

Handling data sparsity and privacy concerns has also driven innovations. Federated learning frameworks such as FedDCSR disentangle domain-shared and domain-exclusive sequence features, learning richer representations while preserving user privacy \[[2309.08420](http://arxiv.org/abs/2309.08420)\]. Other approaches tackle non-overlapping domains by automated prompting and domain-invariant/domain-specific representation learning without requiring explicit domain alignment \[[2304.04218](http://arxiv.org/abs/2304.04218)\]. Adversarial learning methods, like RecGURU, unify latent user embeddings across domains to generate generalized user representations even with minimal user overlap \[[2111.10093](http://arxiv.org/abs/2111.10093)\].

Moreover, recent studies have emphasized the importance of improving the core self-attention mechanism within CDSR models. While many existing frameworks augment self-attention with domain-specific blocks or external modules, they often overlook the inherent capacity of self-attention to model complex item correlations \[[2012.00485](http://arxiv.org/abs/2012.00485)\]\[[2311.08272](http://arxiv.org/abs/2311.08272)\]. Efforts to enhance self-attention include mixed attention networks that combine local and global attention for domain-specific and cross-domain information fusion \[[2311.08272](http://arxiv.org/abs/2311.08272)\], and proxy-aware models that incorporate textual item descriptions and time-interval-aware attention to better represent cross-domain sequences \[[https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/27670ed18e96550c674e022e284fc5356a7458c2](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/27670ed18e96550c674e022e284fc5356a7458c2)\].

Multi-objective optimization has been explored to balance cross-domain knowledge transfer and mitigate negative transfer effects. Reinforcement learning approaches formulate domain filtering as hierarchical decision processes to adaptively revise transferred sequences \[[2206.08088](http://arxiv.org/abs/2206.08088)\], while multi-interest debiasing frameworks like AMID optimize recommendation objectives alongside debiasing objectives to handle open-world scenarios with partial user overlap \[[2311.04590](http://arxiv.org/abs/2311.04590)\]\[[2311.04760](http://arxiv.org/abs/2311.04760)\]. These strategies align with the notion of treating cross-domain learning as a multi-objective problem, facilitating complementary knowledge exchange while reducing interference.

In summary, the evolution of CDSR research reflects a trend from simple knowledge transfer between domains towards sophisticated architectures that jointly model intra- and inter-domain item relationships, user preferences, and temporal dynamics. Enhancing the self-attention mechanism itself, alongside multi-objective and contrastive learning strategies, emerges as a promising direction to improve CDSR performance without excessive model complexity or additional overhead \[[2304.03891](http://arxiv.org/abs/2304.03891)\]\[[2012.00485](http://arxiv.org/abs/2012.00485)\]\[[https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/96bfe33a2503e960153a934e10be91da4d8b13a6](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/96bfe33a2503e960153a934e10be91da4d8b13a6)\]\[[2311.08272](http://arxiv.org/abs/2311.08272)\]. Our work builds upon these insights by proposing a Pareto-optimal self-attention framework that dynamically balances domain-specific and cross-domain attention, enabling automated and efficient knowledge transfer in transformer-based CDSR models.
