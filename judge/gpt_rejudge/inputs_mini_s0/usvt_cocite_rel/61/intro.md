## Related Works

Personalized outfit generation has attracted considerable research interest as it involves constructing sets of compatible and personalized fashion items tailored to user preferences. Early works focused on modeling compatibility between fashion items using visual and contextual data. For instance, Siamese CNN architectures learned latent compatibility spaces by leveraging co-purchase or co-occurrence data from online platforms, capturing notions of complementary and substitute relationships across categories \[[1509.07473](http://arxiv.org/abs/1509.07473)\]\[[1506.04757](http://arxiv.org/abs/1506.04757)\]. Similarly, embedding-based approaches sought to jointly model item similarity and cross-category compatibility to facilitate outfit composition and recommendation \[[1803.09196](http://arxiv.org/abs/1803.09196)\]\[[1712.01262](http://arxiv.org/abs/1712.01262)\].

The challenge of outfit compatibility extends beyond pairwise item relations to modeling complex interactions within an entire outfit. Graph-based methods represent outfits as graphs where nodes correspond to item categories and edges model interactions, enabling richer compatibility learning via graph neural networks and attention mechanisms \[[1902.08009](http://arxiv.org/abs/1902.08009)\]. Transformer-based architectures have also been proposed to model relationships among unordered sets of fashion items, supporting scalable retrieval and compatibility prediction \[[https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/4d431b88f7dfe1e75b0e23bb21fc9dd378b15cf2](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/4d431b88f7dfe1e75b0e23bb21fc9dd378b15cf2)\]\[[https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/255f90cd765d90e602cd35ab7e22c3a8db1acefa](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/255f90cd765d90e602cd35ab7e22c3a8db1acefa)\]. These approaches improve upon sequence or pairwise methods by capturing global outfit coherence.

In addition to compatibility, style plays a critical role in outfit generation. Unsupervised style extraction techniques leverage latent style embeddings learned via autoencoders or topic models to represent global outfit characteristics, enabling generation conditioned on style themes and enhancing interpretability \[[1807.03133](http://arxiv.org/abs/1807.03133)\]\[[1707.03376](http://arxiv.org/abs/1707.03376)\]. Style2Vec and related vector representation models learn item embeddings from co-occurrence in style sets, capturing nuanced style semantics useful for personalized recommendations \[[1708.04014](http://arxiv.org/abs/1708.04014)\].

Personalization is often addressed by integrating user preferences into model training. Personalized Outfit Generation (POG) leverages large-scale user behavior data and Transformer architectures to connect user preferences for individual items and entire outfits, significantly improving recommendation compatibility and personalization metrics \[[1905.01866](http://arxiv.org/abs/1905.01866)\]. Other approaches incorporate collaborative filtering or augment feature representations with user-specific information to tailor recommendations \[[1810.02443](http://arxiv.org/abs/1810.02443)\]\[[1905.03703](http://arxiv.org/abs/1905.03703)\].

Generative models have recently been explored for fashion item and outfit generation. These models can synthesize new fashion items consistent with user taste, going beyond recommending from existing catalogs \[[1711.02231](http://arxiv.org/abs/1711.02231)\]\[[1712.01262](http://arxiv.org/abs/1712.01262)\]. However, generative methods often suffer from limited diversity and rely heavily on supervised learning with handcrafted reward functions. To address this, frameworks incorporating direct preference optimization (DPO) have been proposed, which fine-tune pretrained generative models via automatically generated multi-expert feedback covering quality, compatibility, and personalization dimensions. This approach avoids explicit reward design and enhances alignment with user preferences \[[1608.03016](http://arxiv.org/abs/1608.03016)\].

Multi-expert feedback mechanisms assess generated outfits from multiple perspectives to provide comprehensive and objective signals during model training. Prior works have emphasized the importance of evaluating outfits holistically, combining visual compatibility, style coherence, and personalization relevance to improve generation quality \[[1712.02662](http://arxiv.org/abs/1712.02662)\]\[[1904.00741](http://arxiv.org/abs/1904.00741)\]. Such feedback can be integrated into fine-tuning or reinforcement learning pipelines, including adaptations of reinforcement learning from human feedback (RLHF) techniques to fashion generation tasks \[[1506.04757](http://arxiv.org/abs/1506.04757)\]\[[1708.04014](http://arxiv.org/abs/1708.04014)\].

Multimodal data fusion, especially combining image and textual embeddings, further enriches outfit generation and recommendation systems by enabling semantic alignment between fashion items and user queries or style themes. Methods leveraging multimodal embeddings have demonstrated improved performance in generating outfits that satisfy both compatibility and user-specified constraints \[[1906.07273](http://arxiv.org/abs/1906.07273)\]\[[1812.01748](http://arxiv.org/abs/1812.01748)\]\[[1707.05691](http://arxiv.org/abs/1707.05691)\].

Finally, scalability and explainability are important in practical fashion recommendation systems. Recent advances employ attention mechanisms and submodular optimization to efficiently generate capsule wardrobes or scalable outfit recommendations while providing interpretable feedback to users and stylists \[[1712.02662](http://arxiv.org/abs/1712.02662)\]\[[https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/255f90cd765d90e602cd35ab7e22c3a8db1acefa](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/255f90cd765d90e602cd35ab7e22c3a8db1acefa)\]\[[1908.09493](http://arxiv.org/abs/1908.09493)\]. These systems aim to mimic expert stylists by balancing visual compatibility, versatility, and user preferences at scale.

In summary, personalized outfit generation has evolved from compatibility modeling and style representation to integrating user preferences and generative AI. The incorporation of direct preference optimization with multi-expert feedback and multimodal embeddings represents a promising direction to overcome limitations of supervised learning and enhance diversity, personalization, and compatibility in generated fashion outfits.
