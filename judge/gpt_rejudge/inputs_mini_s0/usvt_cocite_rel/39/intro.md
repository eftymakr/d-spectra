## Related Works

De novo genome assembly has evolved significantly with advances in sequencing technologies, yet it remains computationally intensive due to large data volumes, memory demands, and complex data dependencies \[[https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/d04b3cc774151260a59a5e6768b6cfae7fc3e512](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/d04b3cc774151260a59a5e6768b6cfae7fc3e512)\]. Traditional assembly methods, including overlap-layout-consensus (OLC) and de Bruijn graph approaches, have distinct advantages and limitations. OLC assemblers handle longer reads and small genomes effectively, while de Bruijn graph-based assemblers excel with massive short-read datasets typical of next-generation sequencing (NGS) \[[https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/7279acd9c61a8de019ea2d55d23124dfbb6b5269](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/7279acd9c61a8de019ea2d55d23124dfbb6b5269)\]. However, both approaches suffer from high memory consumption and computational overhead, especially when scaling to large eukaryotic genomes \[[1008.2555](http://arxiv.org/abs/1008.2555)\].

State-of-the-art assemblers have addressed scalability through distributed-memory parallelism. Tools like HipMER and MetaHipMer leverage supercomputing clusters to coassemble terabase-scale metagenomic datasets, offering improved genome completeness and reduced error rates compared to sample-by-sample assembly \[[https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/c0471dc9500e266c2b3669f724dbf816e8b51f11](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/c0471dc9500e266c2b3669f724dbf816e8b51f11)\]\[[https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/eff7a7b6916b7584b2184b3aa30601d158648be8](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/eff7a7b6916b7584b2184b3aa30601d158648be8)\]. Similarly, distributed-memory overlap detection and layout simplification algorithms, implemented in pipelines such as diBELLA 2D, achieve near-linear scaling and substantial runtime reductions for large genomes \[[2010.10055](http://arxiv.org/abs/2010.10055)\]. These frameworks utilize sparse matrix operations and transitive reduction on overlap graphs to efficiently manage memory and computation, yet they still contend with irregular data patterns and large memory footprints inherent to assembly graphs.

Long-read sequencing technologies (e.g., PacBio, Oxford Nanopore) have revolutionized assembly by producing reads exceeding 10 kb, facilitating more contiguous assemblies \[[https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/2b59fd18ada769ef76fb7fcc7382f063791abfab](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/2b59fd18ada769ef76fb7fcc7382f063791abfab)\]\[[1512.01801](http://arxiv.org/abs/1512.01801)\]. Assemblers like Canu apply adaptive k-mer weighting and repeat separation to handle noisy long reads, achieving high continuity and accuracy on complex genomes \[[https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/2b59fd18ada769ef76fb7fcc7382f063791abfab](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/2b59fd18ada769ef76fb7fcc7382f063791abfab)\]. Minimap2 and miniasm further accelerate mapping and assembly of these long reads without an initial error correction step, trading off higher consensus error rates for speed \[[1708.01492](http://arxiv.org/abs/1708.01492)\]\[[1512.01801](http://arxiv.org/abs/1512.01801)\]. However, these methods still demand considerable memory, and their performance is limited by memory-bound behavior and irregular data access.

Improving memory efficiency remains a central focus. SOAPdenovo2 enhances short-read assembly by reducing memory during graph construction and resolving repeats more effectively, leading to increased genome coverage and scaffold continuity \[[https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/f8d1052e2c9d1cdd63093427ccc9169e848d777d](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/f8d1052e2c9d1cdd63093427ccc9169e848d777d)\]. Succinct data structures, such as entropy-compressed de Bruijn graphs, have been proposed to reduce storage requirements by an order of magnitude, enabling human genome assembly on commodity hardware \[[1008.2555](http://arxiv.org/abs/1008.2555)\]. Parallel short-read assemblers like PASHA and SWAP-Assembler incorporate hybrid parallelism and asynchronous models to scale across thousands of cores, improving runtime and contig quality \[[https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/fe8d86bc5520b5049104ff58ee7e980982816ebf](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/fe8d86bc5520b5049104ff58ee7e980982816ebf)\]\[[https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/7bf2f1c55f90e11a4d1df4dd608ac621a08f8b33](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/7bf2f1c55f90e11a4d1df4dd608ac621a08f8b33)\]. Despite these advances, memory bottlenecks and irregular data structures continue to limit scalability and hardware utilization.

Hardware acceleration for genome assembly has been explored but remains nascent. While GPU-based approaches offer substantial speedups in certain pipeline stages, they face challenges from memory bandwidth limitations and data transfer overheads when applied to whole-genome assembly \[[https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/f27decfab0389e2003e7ca66260b34eb3c1b8827](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/f27decfab0389e2003e7ca66260b34eb3c1b8827)\]. Sparse matrix-based similarity search frameworks, such as PASTIS and GenomeAtScale, demonstrate the potential of distributed sparse computations for large-scale genomic analyses beyond assembly, including protein sequence alignment and Jaccard similarity computations \[[2009.14467](http://arxiv.org/abs/2009.14467)\]\[[1911.04200](http://arxiv.org/abs/1911.04200)\]. However, these do not directly address the core assembly bottlenecks.

Recent work on long-read assembly has also introduced locality-sensitive hashing (MHAP) combined with traditional assemblers to efficiently overlap noisy reads and generate high-quality near-complete eukaryotic assemblies \[[https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/618d82a202bb0408e388eba250c66c8a90700c81](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/618d82a202bb0408e388eba250c66c8a90700c81)\]. BELLA employs probabilistic models and sparse generalized matrix multiplication to enhance overlap detection precision and recall on long-read data, improving assembly contiguity \[[https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/f27decfab0389e2003e7ca66260b34eb3c1b8827](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/f27decfab0389e2003e7ca66260b34eb3c1b8827)\]. The Shasta toolkit integrates nanopore sequencing with rapid assembly and polishing algorithms, achieving high accuracy and speed by optimizing computational pipelines on single nodes \[[https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/f9d407ce133ec096ab4321e6d98e8f0081f5bdcc](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/f9d407ce133ec096ab4321e6d98e8f0081f5bdcc)\]. These advancements underscore the benefits of algorithm-hardware co-design but also highlight the need for specialized architectures tailored to the irregular and memory-intensive nature of assembly graphs.

The theoretical complexity of de novo assembly has been studied to better understand the interplay of repeat complexity, read length, and coverage, informing the design of more efficient algorithms \[[https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/d04b3cc774151260a59a5e6768b6cfae7fc3e512](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/d04b3cc774151260a59a5e6768b6cfae7fc3e512)\]. Furthermore, phased assembly graphs and haplotype-resolved assemblers like hifiasm leverage high-fidelity long reads to disentangle haplotypes, increasing assembly accuracy but also computational demands \[[https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/788f8a32ed116f928f3cab554c6efd55385f2a80](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/788f8a32ed116f928f3cab554c6efd55385f2a80)\].

Overall, while distributed and parallel software frameworks have substantially improved the scalability of de novo assembly, challenges persist in managing large, dynamic, and interdependent data structures with irregular access patterns. Existing solutions either require massive computational resources or fail to fully exploit hardware capabilities, motivating the development of near-memory processing and hardware-software co-design approaches to overcome memory bottlenecks and improve throughput \[[2010.10055](http://arxiv.org/abs/2010.10055)\]\[[https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/c0471dc9500e266c2b3669f724dbf816e8b51f11](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/c0471dc9500e266c2b3669f724dbf816e8b51f11)\]\[[https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/fe8d86bc5520b5049104ff58ee7e980982816ebf](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/fe8d86bc5520b5049104ff58ee7e980982816ebf)\].
