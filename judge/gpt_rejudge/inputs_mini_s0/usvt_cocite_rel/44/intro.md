## Related Works

Collaborative filtering (CF) remains a cornerstone of recommender systems, traditionally relying on matrix factorization and its variants to model user-item interactions. Early methods like Bayesian Personalized Ranking (BPR) explicitly optimized ranking objectives for implicit feedback, significantly improving personalized ranking tasks \[[1205.2618](http://arxiv.org/abs/1205.2618)\]. However, these approaches often struggled to capture complex, high-order relationships in interaction data.

The incorporation of graph-structured data into CF has led to substantial advances. Graph Neural Networks (GNNs), which aggregate and transform neighborhood information, have been widely adopted to model user-item bipartite graphs. Neural Graph Collaborative Filtering (NGCF) explicitly propagates embeddings through the user-item graph, capturing high-order connectivity and collaborative signals beyond simple feature mapping, resulting in improved recommendation accuracy \[[1905.08108](http://arxiv.org/abs/1905.08108)\]. Multi-Graph Convolution Collaborative Filtering (Multi-GCCF) further extends this idea by integrating multiple graphs—user-item, user-user, and item-item—to leverage diverse relational signals and better capture the intrinsic differences between user and item nodes \[[2001.00267](http://arxiv.org/abs/2001.00267)\]. These graph-based methods address sparsity and enrich representation learning but face challenges in scaling and information preservation during embedding.

Graph Convolutional Networks (GCNs) have been employed for semi-supervised classification on graph data, demonstrating the ability to learn representations encoding both node features and graph structure with linear complexity in edges \[[1609.02907](http://arxiv.org/abs/1609.02907)\]. Enhancements for web-scale recommender systems leverage efficient random walks combined with graph convolutions to generate embeddings from massive graphs containing billions of nodes and edges, as exemplified by Pinterest’s large-scale GCN recommender \[[1806.01973](http://arxiv.org/abs/1806.01973)\]. These methods highlight the importance of scalable graph embedding frameworks for practical recommendation.

Representation binarization has recently emerged to reduce memory and computational overhead, critical for efficient online inference. While binarized models have shown promise, many approaches focus primarily on numerical quantization without addressing the resultant information loss, which often causes performance degradation. Prior efforts in binarized collaborative filtering aim to distill ranking information into compact binary embeddings but tend to overlook mechanisms to explicitly mitigate information loss \[[1706.02216](http://arxiv.org/abs/1706.02216)\]. Similarly, loss-aware binarization techniques for deep networks propose proximal optimization to reduce accuracy drop but are generally not tailored to graph-based recommender systems \[[1905.08108](http://arxiv.org/abs/1905.08108)\].

In terms of embedding learning, variational autoencoders (VAEs) adapted for collaborative filtering model non-linear user-item interactions with probabilistic frameworks, offering improvements over linear factorization models \[[1802.05814](http://arxiv.org/abs/1802.05814)\]. Neural Collaborative Filtering (NCF) replaces traditional inner product interaction functions with multi-layer perceptrons to capture complex user-item relations, achieving better empirical results \[[1708.05031](http://arxiv.org/abs/1708.05031)\]. Additionally, knowledge graph-based methods incorporate rich side information by modeling high-order relations between items and their attributes, improving recommendation accuracy and interpretability, as demonstrated by KGAT and joint knowledge graph-recommender models \[[1905.07854](http://arxiv.org/abs/1905.07854)\]\[[1902.06236](http://arxiv.org/abs/1902.06236)\].

Graph representation learning methods such as DeepWalk also contribute by learning latent vertex embeddings via random walks treated as sentences, enabling scalable and effective social network representations, which can inform recommender systems \[[1403.6652](http://arxiv.org/abs/1403.6652)\]. Theoretical work on the expressive power of graph neural networks reveals limitations of popular GNN variants and proposes architectures as powerful as the Weisfeiler-Lehman test, underscoring the importance of architectural design in capturing graph structures \[[1810.00826](http://arxiv.org/abs/1810.00826)\].

Our work builds upon these advances by focusing specifically on graph representation binarization in collaborative filtering, addressing the critical issue of information loss during binarization through supervisory signals from pseudo-positive samples and embedding sample synthesis. This approach complements the propagation and aggregation mechanisms in graph-based CF models, while enhancing the efficiency and accuracy of binary embedding representations.
