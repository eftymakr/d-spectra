## Related Works

The burgeoning growth of open-source machine learning (ML) resources, including models and datasets, has catalyzed research in information retrieval (IR) and resource management. Hugging Face (HF), as one of the largest platforms hosting pretrained ML models and datasets, plays a pivotal role in this ecosystem. Despite its popularity, current platforms like HF lack structured representations that would enable advanced queries such as tracing model evolution, recommending relevant datasets, and managing resource dependencies effectively \[[2311.13380](http://arxiv.org/abs/2311.13380)\]\[[2303.02552](http://arxiv.org/abs/2303.02552)\]\[[2307.14841](http://arxiv.org/abs/2307.14841)\].

Several studies have examined aspects of ML resource organization on HF, highlighting challenges and opportunities. Repository mining efforts reveal that ML models on HF exhibit diverse maintenance and evolution patterns, with a need for improved metadata and documentation to support reuse and sustainability \[[2311.13380](http://arxiv.org/abs/2311.13380)\]\[[2406.08205](http://arxiv.org/abs/2406.08205)\]. The reuse of Pre-Trained Models (PTMs) has been extensively studied, uncovering challenges such as discrepancies between claimed and actual performance, missing provenance data, and difficulties in model comprehension, all of which hinder effective reuse and selection \[[2303.02552](http://arxiv.org/abs/2303.02552)\]\[[2401.13177](http://arxiv.org/abs/2401.13177)\]. These findings underscore the necessity of structured, machine-readable knowledge representations to facilitate better tracking and recommendation of ML resources.

Documentation practices on HF have also been investigated, particularly focusing on dataset cards and model cards. Large-scale analyses show heterogeneous completion rates and content quality in dataset documentation, with often insufficient coverage of usage considerations and limitations, which are critical for reproducibility and ethical deployment \[[2401.13822](http://arxiv.org/abs/2401.13822)\]\[[1810.03993](http://arxiv.org/abs/1810.03993)\]. Similarly, model cards are advocated as a standard for transparent reporting of model characteristics, including performance across demographic groups and intended use cases, to foster responsible AI development \[[1810.03993](http://arxiv.org/abs/1810.03993)\]. However, studies report that documentation quality strongly correlates with resource popularity, indicating a bias in available metadata that can impact IR and recommendation systems \[[2406.08205](http://arxiv.org/abs/2406.08205)\]\[[2401.13177](http://arxiv.org/abs/2401.13177)\].

The evolution and release practices of PTMs further complicate resource management. Empirical work reveals inconsistent and often ambiguous versioning schemes for PTM releases on HF, with many changes to model weights not reflected in naming or documentation, posing challenges for automatic tracking and dependency management \[[2409.10472](http://arxiv.org/abs/2409.10472)\]. These inconsistencies highlight the potential benefits of structured knowledge graphs that can encode relationships between base models, fine-tuned variants, and modifications such as quantization or pruning.

Benchmarking frameworks for ML models have been developed to evaluate performance and guide selection. Existing benchmarks often focus on specific tasks such as fake news detection or healthcare applications, emphasizing the need for comprehensive and multi-task evaluation frameworks to capture the diverse uses of open-source models \[[2405.13058](http://arxiv.org/abs/2405.13058)\]\[[2405.16746](http://arxiv.org/abs/2405.16746)\]. The integration of knowledge graphs with benchmarking can provide a richer context for resource recommendation and classification by linking model capabilities, training data, and usage scenarios.

Social and collaborative dynamics within the HF community also influence resource development and adoption. Studies of activity distribution show highly skewed usage and contribution patterns, with a small core of prolific developers and many isolated contributors, resembling open source software ecosystems \[[2405.13058](http://arxiv.org/abs/2405.13058)\]. This community structure impacts resource visibility and quality, reinforcing the importance of metadata and structured representations to surface valuable but less popular resources.

Recent efforts to combine IR techniques with HF resources illustrate the potential of interoperable tooling. For example, integrating Information Retrieval toolkits like Pyserini with HF enables scalable exploration and qualitative analysis of large NLP datasets, demonstrating the utility of structured access to textual metadata \[[2306.01481](http://arxiv.org/abs/2306.01481)\]. Similarly, approaches leveraging large language models (LLMs) as controllers to orchestrate multiple HF models for complex tasks point to the need for unified frameworks that integrate heterogeneous resources effectively \[[2303.17580](http://arxiv.org/abs/2303.17580)\].

In summary, existing literature underscores several key themes relevant to HuggingKG and HuggingBench. These include the critical need for structured, comprehensive metadata to enable advanced IR functions; challenges in documenting, versioning, and maintaining ML resources; the role of benchmarking for resource evaluation; and the social dynamics influencing resource sharing. The proposed knowledge graph approach builds on these insights by providing a large-scale, domain-specific structure capturing relations and attributes of ML models and datasets, thereby enabling novel IR tasks such as resource recommendation, classification, and evolution tracing that are currently underserved \[[2305.11164](http://arxiv.org/abs/2305.11164)\]\[[2311.13380](http://arxiv.org/abs/2311.13380)\]\[[2401.13822](http://arxiv.org/abs/2401.13822)\]\[[2303.02552](http://arxiv.org/abs/2303.02552)\]\[[2406.08205](http://arxiv.org/abs/2406.08205)\]\[[2405.13058](http://arxiv.org/abs/2405.13058)\]\[[2401.13177](http://arxiv.org/abs/2401.13177)\]\[[2307.14841](http://arxiv.org/abs/2307.14841)\]\[[2409.10472](http://arxiv.org/abs/2409.10472)\]\[[1810.03993](http://arxiv.org/abs/1810.03993)\].
