## Related Works

Maximum Inner Product Search (MIPS) is a fundamental problem in machine learning and information retrieval, especially when dealing with high-dimensional data. Early methods for MIPS often focused on directly using inner product (IP) similarity but faced challenges such as local optima and redundant computations \[[1405.5869](http://arxiv.org/abs/1405.5869)\]. To address efficiency, several studies attempted to reduce MIPS to more tractable problems like nearest neighbor search (NNS) under the Euclidean metric by applying space transformations. However, these transformations frequently distort the data topology, causing information loss and degraded search quality \[[1809.08782](http://arxiv.org/abs/1809.08782)\]\[[https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/d8aad5d9c2c336c550ca9621b73c7688f184d50b](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/d8aad5d9c2c336c550ca9621b73c7688f184d50b)\].

Hashing-based approaches were among the first to achieve sublinear time complexity for approximate MIPS. Asymmetric Locality Sensitive Hashing (ALSH) pioneered this direction by introducing asymmetric transformations that convert MIPS into approximate near neighbor search problems solvable by hashing \[[1405.5869](http://arxiv.org/abs/1405.5869)\]\[[1410.5410](http://arxiv.org/abs/1410.5410)\]. Subsequent improvements, such as Norm-Ranging LSH, tackled issues arising from norm distribution tails in real datasets by partitioning data and constructing multiple indices, yielding more efficient query processing \[[1809.08782](http://arxiv.org/abs/1809.08782)\]. Another hashing-based method, ProMIPS, leveraged 2-stable random projections and a lightweight iDistance index to balance search quality and efficiency, reducing index size and preprocessing costs \[[2104.04406](http://arxiv.org/abs/2104.04406)\].

Despite their theoretical appeal, hashing methods often require large storage for hash tables or long codes and can suffer from recall inefficiency in high dimensions. This limitation has motivated the rise of graph-based search techniques for MIPS, which have demonstrated superior empirical performance in many practical scenarios. One state-of-the-art method, the inner-product navigable small world graph (ip-NSW), exploits a norm bias inherent in MIPS: items with larger norms tend to dominate search results. ip-NSW constructs a proximity graph where high-norm items have greater connectivity, enabling efficient graph traversal that avoids unnecessary computations on low-norm items \[[1909.13459](http://arxiv.org/abs/1909.13459)\]\[[2104.04406](http://arxiv.org/abs/2104.04406)\]. Building on ip-NSW, ip-NSW+ introduces an additional angular proximity graph to better capture data geometry, leading to more robust and faster search performance across diverse datasets \[[1909.13459](http://arxiv.org/abs/1909.13459)\].

Other graph-based methods have explored novel indexing and search strategies. The Norm Adjusted Proximity Graph (NAPG) incorporates norm-based adjustment factors learned from sampled data to select more meaningful graph connections, improving MIPS retrieval efficiency \[[https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/25454f456c514219c9d6eeb42200ebbf378db9ca](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/25454f456c514219c9d6eeb42200ebbf378db9ca)\]. Similarly, the Inner Product Delaunay Graph (IPDG) transforms MIPS into a graph search problem, leveraging graph-theoretic properties for top-1 MIPS with enhanced efficiency \[[https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/4d0dce74babcf393b5a4cbcb11c89ccca6a3b92b](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/4d0dce74babcf393b5a4cbcb11c89ccca6a3b92b)\]. Hierarchical navigable small world graphs (HNSW) provide multi-layer graph structures that enable logarithmic scale search complexity and have been adapted successfully for MIPS \[[1603.09320](http://arxiv.org/abs/1603.09320)\].

Branch-and-bound algorithms and tree-based structures have also been proposed. Cone trees utilize inner-product bounds to prune the search space effectively and support efficient dual-tree algorithms for multiple queries, achieving significant query time improvements \[[1202.6101](http://arxiv.org/abs/1202.6101)\]. Budgeted MIPS methods, such as Greedy-MIPS, allow explicit control over the trade-off between search quality and computational cost, demonstrating remarkable speedups with high precision on large candidate sets \[[1610.03317](http://arxiv.org/abs/1610.03317)\].

Recent advances have sought to unify the inner product and Euclidean metric perspectives. While MIPS and Euclidean NNS have traditionally been treated as distinct problems, emerging research suggests that there is no strict dichotomy between these metrics. Integrating IP and Euclidean metrics in indexing and search algorithms can leverage the complementary strengths of both, adapting to the data topology for improved performance \[[https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/25454f456c514219c9d6eeb42200ebbf378db9ca](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/25454f456c514219c9d6eeb42200ebbf378db9ca)\]. This insight motivates dynamic or amphibious graph indexing techniques, which switch between metrics based on statistical indicators reflecting data characteristics, thereby enhancing adaptability and scalability \[[1603.09320](http://arxiv.org/abs/1603.09320)\]\[[https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/f2171bc79fb72693f60c8e6b0dd547f7d4f850ac](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/f2171bc79fb72693f60c8e6b0dd547f7d4f850ac)\]\[[https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/0b2790c2c1e23e5535421696e1ec763bbc651bbd](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/0b2790c2c1e23e5535421696e1ec763bbc651bbd)\].

Furthermore, exact MIPS methods have been optimized by leveraging hardware efficiencies and pruning. For example, blocked matrix multiply (BMM) and the MAX-IMUS algorithm exploit hardware-friendly operations combined with search space pruning to outperform traditional solvers on certain inputs, while an online optimizer (OPTIMUS) dynamically selects the best solver based on input properties \[[https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/0b2790c2c1e23e5535421696e1ec763bbc651bbd](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/0b2790c2c1e23e5535421696e1ec763bbc651bbd)\].

In addition to forward MIPS, reverse MIPS has been studied to identify users likely to be interested in specific items, with algorithms like Simpfer providing fast and exact solutions through efficient indexing and pruning \[[2110.07131](http://arxiv.org/abs/2110.07131)\].

Finally, diversity-aware MIPS extends the problem to incorporate recommendation list diversity, balancing inner product maximization with user satisfaction through greedy algorithms with early termination and skipping strategies \[[https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/87bfc511e4ef4a8aeddad903b06036dbe7177625](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/87bfc511e4ef4a8aeddad903b06036dbe7177625)\].

In summary, the landscape of MIPS research spans hashing, graph-based, tree-based, and hardware-optimized approaches. Recent trends emphasize hybrid metric integration and adaptive indexing strategies that respond to data topology, offering promising directions for scalable and efficient MIPS solutions.
