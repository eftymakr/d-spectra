## Related Works

Generative retrieval has recently emerged as a novel paradigm in information retrieval, where a single large-scale neural network replaces traditional search indices by directly generating document identifiers in an autoregressive manner conditioned on queries. This approach, often referred to as Differentiable Search Index (DSI), encodes the entire corpus within the model parameters, enabling end-to-end optimization and potentially reducing memory overhead compared to traditional index-retrieve pipelines \[[2202.06991](http://arxiv.org/abs/2202.06991)\]\[[2208.07652](http://arxiv.org/abs/2208.07652)\]\[[2206.02743](http://arxiv.org/abs/2206.02743)\]. Early works demonstrated that generative retrieval can outperform strong baselines such as dual encoder models and BM25, especially in zero-shot or low-resource settings, by learning to map queries directly to document identifiers \[[2202.06991](http://arxiv.org/abs/2202.06991)\]\[[2208.07652](http://arxiv.org/abs/2208.07652)\].

A key challenge in generative retrieval is defining effective document identifiers (docids). Conventional methods rely on fixed rule-based identifiers such as document titles or clustering-based embeddings, which may not capture full semantic information of documents. To address this, recent work has proposed learned tokenization schemes that generate short discrete docids through auto-encoding frameworks, enabling semantically rich and end-to-end optimized identifiers \[[2304.04171](http://arxiv.org/abs/2304.04171)\]. Such approaches have shown substantial improvements on standard benchmarks like NQ320K, MS MARCO, and BEIR datasets \[[2304.04171](http://arxiv.org/abs/2304.04171)\].

Another line of research seeks to improve the retrieval quality by generating multiple queries per document during indexing, thus mitigating the data distribution mismatch between indexing and retrieval phases. For example, the DSI-QG framework incorporates generated queries as surrogate document representations during indexing, which better aligns retrieval queries with the model’s learned associations, leading to significant gains in both mono-lingual and cross-lingual retrieval tasks \[[2206.10128](http://arxiv.org/abs/2206.10128)\]. Similarly, synthetic query generation has been identified as crucial for scaling generative retrieval to millions of passages, although challenges remain in effectively handling such large corpora \[[2305.11841](http://arxiv.org/abs/2305.11841)\].

Autoregressive generation of identifiers has also been extended to fine-grained retrieval tasks such as entity retrieval and evidence retrieval for fact verification. The GENRE system retrieves entities by autoregressively generating their unique names, reducing memory footprint and enabling effective cross-encoding of query and entity context \[[2010.00904](http://arxiv.org/abs/2010.00904)\]. Likewise, GERE applies a generative approach to retrieve evidence sentences for claim verification, dynamically selecting relevant evidence without relying on large static indexes or complex reranking pipelines \[[2204.05511](http://arxiv.org/abs/2204.05511)\].

Despite these advances, the autoregressive decoding process introduces limitations related to constraints and search algorithms. The use of constrained beam search for docid generation may not fully exploit the underlying relevance distributions, leading to suboptimal retrieval performance. Recent analyses have pointed out that the marginal distributions used in beam search decoding may not be ideal for generative retrieval, motivating research into alternative decoding and ranking strategies \[[2306.15222](http://arxiv.org/abs/2306.15222)\]. For instance, learning-to-rank frameworks integrated with generative retrieval models have been proposed to directly optimize passage ranking objectives, bridging the gap between generation likelihood and retrieval relevance \[[2306.15222](http://arxiv.org/abs/2306.15222)\].

Theoretical investigations into generative retrieval have examined the effects of constraints on error bounds, often expressed via Kullback-Leibler (KL) divergence between ground-truth and model-predicted marginal distributions. Such analyses provide a formal understanding of the performance limitations imposed by corpus-specific decoding constraints and the autoregressive generation paradigm \[[2202.06991](http://arxiv.org/abs/2202.06991)\]. Related work on KL divergence loss functions and convergence properties of Langevin algorithms offers insight into stable optimization approaches within this context \[[2010.00904](http://arxiv.org/abs/2010.00904)\]\[[2308.14968](http://arxiv.org/abs/2308.14968)\].

Moreover, memory and continual learning aspects have been explored to handle dynamic corpora where documents are incrementally added. Techniques like Incremental Product Quantization and memory-augmented learning mechanisms enable generative retrieval models to update docid representations efficiently while preserving previous knowledge, addressing practical deployment challenges \[[2308.14968](http://arxiv.org/abs/2308.14968)\].

Finally, neural architectures for generative retrieval continue to evolve. Sequence-to-sequence networks with prefix-aware, weight-adaptive decoders have been designed to improve recall by generating relevant docids directly from queries, coupled with query generation and semantic docid design to enhance performance \[[2206.02743](http://arxiv.org/abs/2206.02743)\]. Unified generative retrievers have also been proposed to handle heterogeneous retrieval tasks across different semantic granularities using prompt learning, achieving robust generalization and task-specific effectiveness within a single model \[[2304.14856](http://arxiv.org/abs/2304.14856)\].

Collectively, these studies delineate the current state of generative retrieval, highlighting its advantages over traditional methods, practical challenges in scaling and decoding, and theoretical foundations related to constrained autoregressive generation and beam search \[[2202.06991](http://arxiv.org/abs/2202.06991)\]\[[2208.07652](http://arxiv.org/abs/2208.07652)\]\[[2304.04171](http://arxiv.org/abs/2304.04171)\]\[[2206.10128](http://arxiv.org/abs/2206.10128)\]\[[2305.11841](http://arxiv.org/abs/2305.11841)\]\[[2010.00904](http://arxiv.org/abs/2010.00904)\]\[[2308.14968](http://arxiv.org/abs/2308.14968)\]\[[2206.02743](http://arxiv.org/abs/2206.02743)\]\[[2306.15222](http://arxiv.org/abs/2306.15222)\]\[[2304.14856](http://arxiv.org/abs/2304.14856)\]\[[2204.05511](http://arxiv.org/abs/2204.05511)\].
