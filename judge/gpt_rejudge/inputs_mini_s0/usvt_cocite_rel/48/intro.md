## Related Works

Neural retrieval has undergone significant progress with the integration of transformer-based pre-trained language models (PLMs), which have greatly enhanced multilingual and cross-lingual information retrieval (IR) capabilities. Models such as BERT and RoBERTa have become foundational due to their strong contextualized representations, enabling improved semantic matching beyond traditional lexical methods \[[2004.12832](http://arxiv.org/abs/2004.12832)\]\[[1810.04805](http://arxiv.org/abs/1810.04805)\]. However, the computational cost of applying such large-scale neural models to ad-hoc retrieval has motivated architectures like ColBERT, which utilize late interaction mechanisms to balance effectiveness and efficiency by independently encoding queries and documents before performing fine-grained token-level similarity computations \[[2004.12832](http://arxiv.org/abs/2004.12832)\]\[[2112.01488](http://arxiv.org/abs/2112.01488)\]. ColBERT and its improved variants (e.g., ColBERTv2) have demonstrated state-of-the-art performance while significantly reducing query latency and computational overhead \[[2004.12832](http://arxiv.org/abs/2004.12832)\]\[[2112.01488](http://arxiv.org/abs/2112.01488)\].

Cross-lingual retrieval, particularly for low-resource and morphologically rich languages, remains a challenging domain due to limited annotated data and the linguistic complexity involved. Multilingual PLMs such as multilingual BERT (mBERT) and XLM-RoBERTa (XLM-R) have achieved notable success in zero-shot and few-shot transfer scenarios by leveraging shared multilingual embeddings \[[1911.02116](http://arxiv.org/abs/1911.02116)\]\[[1901.07291](http://arxiv.org/abs/1901.07291)\]\[[2007.01852](http://arxiv.org/abs/2007.01852)\]. However, these models often struggle with low-resource languages due to training data imbalance and insufficient language-specific adaptation, which can lead to performance degradation in downstream retrieval tasks \[[2301.12566](http://arxiv.org/abs/2301.12566)\]\[[1911.02116](http://arxiv.org/abs/1911.02116)\]\[[2212.10448](http://arxiv.org/abs/2212.10448)\]. To mitigate this, approaches like adapter-based fine-tuning frameworks (e.g., MAD-X) and optimal transport distillation have been proposed to efficiently transfer monolingual retrieval models to new languages with minimal supervision, often relying only on parallel bitext data for distillation \[[2301.12566](http://arxiv.org/abs/2301.12566)\]\[[2005.00052](http://arxiv.org/abs/2005.00052)\]\[[2212.10448](http://arxiv.org/abs/2212.10448)\].

Dense retrieval, which maps queries and documents into continuous vector spaces, has been extensively studied for both monolingual and cross-lingual IR. Dense retrievers trained on large datasets like MS MARCO have outperformed traditional sparse models (e.g., BM25) by capturing semantic relevance more effectively \[[2004.04906](http://arxiv.org/abs/2004.04906)\]\[[2007.00808](http://arxiv.org/abs/2007.00808)\]\[[2211.14876](http://arxiv.org/abs/2211.14876)\]. However, dense retrieval models require substantial training data, which is scarce for many low-resource languages. Recent works have explored unsupervised contrastive learning methods and synthetic data generation to address this gap, showing improvements in cross-lingual robustness and transferability even when supervised data is minimal or unavailable \[[2112.09118](http://arxiv.org/abs/2112.09118)\]\[[2107.11976](http://arxiv.org/abs/2107.11976)\].

Language-specific pre-trained models have been shown to outperform multilingual models when sufficient monolingual data is available. For example, CamemBERT, a RoBERTa-based model trained solely on French data, achieves state-of-the-art performance on French NLP tasks despite using less data than multilingual counterparts, illustrating the benefits of language-specific adaptation \[[1911.03894](http://arxiv.org/abs/1911.03894)\]. Similarly, adapting transformer models to morphologically rich, low-resource languages like Amharic benefits substantially from tailored tokenization and dedicated training, which can mitigate issues caused by suboptimal multilingual tokenizers and data scarcity \[[2004.04906](http://arxiv.org/abs/2004.04906)\]\[[2103.11811](http://arxiv.org/abs/2103.11811)\].

Cross-lingual dense retrieval models such as ColBERT-X extend the ColBERT architecture by incorporating XLM-R encoders to support multilingual retrieval. These models can be trained in zero-shot or translate-train paradigms, where training data from high-resource languages is either directly used or augmented with machine translations, respectively. Such strategies have yielded significant improvements over lexical baselines and highlight the importance of cross-lingual representation learning in retrieval tasks \[[2201.08471](http://arxiv.org/abs/2201.08471)\]\[[2108.13897](http://arxiv.org/abs/2108.13897)\].

Benchmarking datasets and evaluation frameworks have been developed to facilitate research in low-resource IR. mMARCO, a multilingual version of the MS MARCO passage ranking dataset created via machine translation, enables the fine-tuning and evaluation of retrieval models across thirteen languages, demonstrating that multilingual models fine-tuned on translated data outperform monolingual or English-only baselines in zero-shot settings \[[2108.13897](http://arxiv.org/abs/2108.13897)\]. Additionally, the creation of datasets for named entity recognition and other NLP tasks in African languages has driven progress in language-specific model development, which is crucial for effective low-resource IR \[[2103.11811](http://arxiv.org/abs/2103.11811)\].

Finally, retrieval-augmented language models (e.g., REALM, RAG, RETRO) integrate explicit document retrieval with language model pre-training, enhancing the ability to access up-to-date factual knowledge and improving performance on knowledge-intensive tasks like open-domain question answering. These approaches underscore the potential for combining dense retrieval with generative language models to further advance IR capabilities in diverse languages and domains \[[2112.04426](http://arxiv.org/abs/2112.04426)\]\[[2005.11401](http://arxiv.org/abs/2005.11401)\]\[[2002.08909](http://arxiv.org/abs/2002.08909)\].

In summary, while multilingual pre-trained models have advanced cross-lingual retrieval, challenges remain for low-resource, morphologically complex languages such as Amharic. Language-specific dense retrieval models leveraging monolingual BERT and RoBERTa backbones, combined with efficient late interaction architectures like ColBERT, demonstrate substantial gains over multilingual baselines. These findings emphasize the importance of language-specific adaptation, efficient training methods, and the development of dedicated benchmarks to foster progress in low-resource IR \[[2004.12832](http://arxiv.org/abs/2004.12832)\]\[[2201.08471](http://arxiv.org/abs/2201.08471)\]\[[2004.04906](http://arxiv.org/abs/2004.04906)\]\[[2112.01488](http://arxiv.org/abs/2112.01488)\]\[[2108.13897](http://arxiv.org/abs/2108.13897)\]\[[2212.10448](http://arxiv.org/abs/2212.10448)\].
