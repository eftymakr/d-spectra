## Related Works

Neural retrieval models have become essential in modern information retrieval, particularly for Web search, due to their capacity to learn complex semantic patterns from data. However, their success hinges on large amounts of labeled query-document pairs, which are costly and labor-intensive to produce \[[1611.09268](http://arxiv.org/abs/1611.09268)\]. To mitigate this bottleneck, recent research has explored synthetic query generation using large language models (LLMs) as a scalable alternative \[[1611.09268](http://arxiv.org/abs/1611.09268)\]. While this approach leverages the abundance of web documents, the quality of synthetic queries can vary significantly, which negatively impacts downstream retrieval effectiveness \[[1611.09268](http://arxiv.org/abs/1611.09268)\].

Traditional methods to improve quality involve filtering noisy synthetic query-document pairs using signals from external re-rankers, such as BERT-based reranking models, which evaluate relevance scores to prune poor-quality samples \[[1611.09268](http://arxiv.org/abs/1611.09268)\]\[[1901.04085](http://arxiv.org/abs/1901.04085)\]. Although effective, these methods introduce computational overhead and may not capture nuanced preferences comprehensively \[[1611.09268](http://arxiv.org/abs/1611.09268)\]. Our approach diverges by integrating ranking signals directly into the query generation process through Direct Preference Optimization (DPO), a recent fine-tuning paradigm that aligns language models with human preferences by optimizing pairwise preference data without explicit reward modeling or reinforcement learning \[[2305.1829](http://arxiv.org/abs/2305.18290)\]\[[2402.13228](http://arxiv.org/abs/2402.13228)\]\[[2310.12036](http://arxiv.org/abs/2310.12036)\]. DPO simplifies training by replacing the complex reinforcement learning pipeline with a stable classification loss, producing models that better match human judgment in generation tasks \[[2305.1829](http://arxiv.org/abs/2305.18290)\]. Enhancements like DPO-Positive further address potential failure modes by ensuring the likelihood of preferred outputs does not degrade during fine-tuning, improving performance on benchmarks independent of the fine-tuning data \[[2402.13228](http://arxiv.org/abs/2402.13228)\].

DPO has demonstrated efficacy in aligning LLMs for various downstream tasks, including summarization, dialogue, and sentiment control, often outperforming traditional RLHF methods while being computationally lighter \[[2305.1829](http://arxiv.org/abs/2305.18290)\]\[[2204.05862](http://arxiv.org/abs/2204.05862)\]\[[2009.01325](http://arxiv.org/abs/2009.01325)\]. Its application to query generation for retrieval is novel but well-motivated given its ability to optimize models directly for preference-aligned outputs. Moreover, recent theoretical work proposes improvements to preference learning objectives beyond DPO, offering principled guarantees and empirical benefits, potentially guiding future refinements in this area \[[2310.12036](http://arxiv.org/abs/2310.12036)\].

Regarding synthetic data in retrieval training, dense retrieval models benefit significantly from enhanced negative sampling strategies to improve ranking robustness and stability. Techniques like Approximate Nearest Neighbor Contrastive Estimation (ANCE) dynamically select realistic hard negatives during training, aligning training and testing data distributions for superior retrieval accuracy \[[2007.00808](http://arxiv.org/abs/2007.00808)\]. Similarly, Stable Training Algorithm for dense Retrieval (STAR) and query-side ADORE sampling improve dense retriever training by addressing instability and optimizing ranking performance directly \[[2104.08051](http://arxiv.org/abs/2104.08051)\]. These advancements highlight the importance of careful training data curation and optimization strategies in neural retrieval.

Recent works also focus on end-to-end neural indexing and retrieval paradigms, such as the Differentiable Search Index (DSI), which unifies indexing and retrieval into a single transformer model. Methods like DSI-QG mitigate data distribution mismatches between indexing and retrieval by incorporating generated query candidates at indexing time, enhancing cross-lingual and monolingual retrieval performance \[[2206.10128](http://arxiv.org/abs/2206.10128)\]\[[2206.02743](http://arxiv.org/abs/2206.02743)\]. These paradigms underscore the trend toward more integrated and adaptive retrieval frameworks, which can benefit from improved query generation techniques.

Pretrained transformer architectures, especially BERT and its derivatives, remain foundational for passage re-ranking and document retrieval. Models leveraging contextualized embeddings, such as CEDR, combine pretrained language models with neural ranking architectures to significantly enhance ad-hoc retrieval effectiveness \[[1904.07094](http://arxiv.org/abs/1904.07094)\]\[[1901.04085](http://arxiv.org/abs/1901.04085)\]. Additionally, kernel-based neural ranking models like K-NRM optimize word-level soft matching through trainable embeddings, achieving robust end-to-end ranking improvements \[[1706.06613](http://arxiv.org/abs/1706.06613)\]. Combining local and distributed text representations, as in the Duet model, further improves retrieval by capturing both exact term matches and semantic relevance \[[1610.08136](http://arxiv.org/abs/1610.08136)\].

Finally, leveraging human preference data for training language models has evolved from reinforcement learning with human feedback (RLHF) to more direct and stable optimization methods like DPO, which eschew reward model fitting and reinforcement learning for simpler classification-based losses \[[2305.1829](http://arxiv.org/abs/2305.18290)\]\[[2204.05862](http://arxiv.org/abs/2204.05862)\]\[[2310.12036](http://arxiv.org/abs/2310.12036)\]. This shift allows for more efficient alignment with human judgments, reducing complexity and instability inherent in RLHF. Extensions incorporating self-rewarding contrastive prompt distillation demonstrate promising avenues for automatic preference data generation and alignment without human annotations, which could further enhance scalable query generation \[[2402.11907](http://arxiv.org/abs/2402.11907)\].

In summary, our work builds upon advances in preference-based LLM alignment via DPO, integrating ranking signals directly into synthetic query generation to improve downstream retrieval. This approach contrasts with prior reliance on external rerankers and complements recent progress in dense retrieval training, neural indexing, and transformer-based ranking architectures, collectively advancing the state of neural retrieval systems.
