## Related Works

Differential privacy (DP) has established itself as the standard framework for privacy-preserving data analysis, enabling statistical estimation and learning while protecting individual data contributions \[[https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/770ad70607ce60d8c411916129a423b5aad45e93](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/770ad70607ce60d8c411916129a423b5aad45e93)\]. The classical central DP model assumes a trusted data curator who adds calibrated noise to query results. In contrast, Local Differential Privacy (LDP) empowers users to perturb their own data before transmission, removing the need to trust the data collector but generally at the cost of utility and susceptibility to adversarial manipulation \[[https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/770ad70607ce60d8c411916129a423b5aad45e93](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/770ad70607ce60d8c411916129a423b5aad45e93)\]\[[1909.09630](http://arxiv.org/abs/1909.09630)\]\[[1911.02046](http://arxiv.org/abs/1911.02046)\].

Several works have demonstrated inherent vulnerabilities of LDP protocols to malicious participants who manipulate their randomized outputs to skew aggregate statistics, a form of data poisoning attack that undermines the accuracy and trustworthiness of the system \[[1909.09630](http://arxiv.org/abs/1909.09630)\]\[[1911.02046](http://arxiv.org/abs/1911.02046)\]\[[2104.06569](http://arxiv.org/abs/2104.06569)\]. These attacks exploit the plausible deniability offered by randomization mechanisms, allowing adversaries to bias frequency estimation or heavy hitter identification tasks. To mitigate such threats, recent research has proposed verifiable randomization mechanisms that enable the data collector to cryptographically verify the correctness of the randomization process executed by each user, thereby preventing manipulation attacks without assuming specific attacker models \[[2104.06569](http://arxiv.org/abs/2104.06569)\].

The notion of verifiable differential privacy (VDP) addresses a fundamental gap in DP deployments: the possibility of unfaithful execution by an untrusted server or data collector who might sample noise incorrectly or generate correlated noise to bias outputs while hiding behind the inherent randomness of DP mechanisms. Interactive proofs and zero-knowledge proofs (ZKPs) have been studied as tools to verify that DP algorithms have been executed correctly without revealing sensitive randomness \[[2208.09011](http://arxiv.org/abs/2208.09011)\]. These proofs ensure that the outputs released are both differentially private and reliably generated, eliminating the “smokescreen” effect that DP randomness can provide for malicious behavior. However, the reliance on ZKPs, while sufficient for verifiability, is not strictly necessary, as alternative cryptographic frameworks can also enforce compliance \[[2208.09011](http://arxiv.org/abs/2208.09011)\].

Efforts to combine cryptographic primitives with DP have yielded practical systems for secure and verifiable privacy-preserving data aggregation. For example, homomorphic encryption and secure multi-party computation (MPC) frameworks have been integrated with DP to enable verifiable and privacy-preserving computation without requiring a trusted party \[[https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/ac8be9c934d6ff9ae095ebde81c8d3a57f7b918d](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/ac8be9c934d6ff9ae095ebde81c8d3a57f7b918d)\]\[[2005.11753](http://arxiv.org/abs/2005.11753)\]\[[1911.06879](http://arxiv.org/abs/1911.06879)\]. These systems, however, often face challenges in scalability, performance, and complexity of security proofs.

In the local model, improvements in verifiable mechanisms have been proposed, such as Verifiable Randomized Response (VRR), which allows clients to confirm that their data was processed according to the DP protocol, ensuring local DP guarantees with verifiability \[[2104.06569](http://arxiv.org/abs/2104.06569)\]\[[1907.00782](http://arxiv.org/abs/1907.00782)\]\[[1712.01524](http://arxiv.org/abs/1712.01524)\]. Recent advances have achieved orders-of-magnitude improvements in communication cost and computational overhead for such verifiable LDP schemes, making them more practical for large-scale deployment \[[2104.06569](http://arxiv.org/abs/2104.06569)\].

Beyond local DP, hybrid models like the shuffle model have been explored to leverage anonymity to amplify privacy and improve accuracy, bridging the gap between local and central DP models without fully trusting the server \[[1811.12469](http://arxiv.org/abs/1811.12469)\]\[[1808.01394](http://arxiv.org/abs/1808.01394)\]\[[2107.11839](http://arxiv.org/abs/2107.11839)\]. These models benefit from cryptographic shuffling and permutation techniques to reduce noise while maintaining privacy, but do not directly address verifiable execution of DP mechanisms by potentially malicious servers.

Additionally, foundational work on frequency estimation and statistical queries under LDP has contributed to the understanding of noise calibration and accuracy improvements, including post-processing methods to enforce consistency and reduce error \[[1905.08320](http://arxiv.org/abs/1905.08320)\]\[[1907.00782](http://arxiv.org/abs/1907.00782)\]\[[1602.07387](http://arxiv.org/abs/1602.07387)\]. New distributions for noise addition, such as the discrete Gaussian, have been introduced to better fit integer-valued queries and improve practical applicability \[[2004.00010](http://arxiv.org/abs/2004.00010)\].

The present work advances this landscape by formalizing Verifiable Distributed Differential Privacy (VDDP), a framework that incorporates verification mechanisms directly into distributed DP protocols, addressing malicious server behavior in both local and central settings. It improves upon prior verifiable DP mechanisms by significantly enhancing proof generation efficiency and reducing communication and verifier overhead, specifically through the Verifiable Distributed Discrete Laplacian Mechanism (VDDLM) and an optimized VRR solution for local DP. These contributions respond to the critical need for trustworthy DP deployments in distributed environments, leveraging both cryptographic proof systems and novel algorithmic design to balance privacy, verifiability, and performance \[[2104.06569](http://arxiv.org/abs/2104.06569)\]\[[2208.09011](http://arxiv.org/abs/2208.09011)\].

Overall, the intersection of differential privacy, verifiability, and cryptographic techniques is an active and evolving research area, with ongoing efforts to reconcile strong privacy guarantees, resistance to adversarial manipulation, and practical efficiency in high-dimensional and repeated data collection scenarios \[[https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/770ad70607ce60d8c411916129a423b5aad45e93](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/770ad70607ce60d8c411916129a423b5aad45e93)\]\[[2005.11753](http://arxiv.org/abs/2005.11753)\]\[[2208.09011](http://arxiv.org/abs/2208.09011)\].
