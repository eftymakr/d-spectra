## Related Works

Understanding the evolution of feature representations across layers in large language models (LLMs) has been a central focus for advancing model interpretability and robustness. Initial studies predominantly employed data-dependent analyses on fine-tuned models to identify functionally important layers, limiting insights to post-hoc settings \[[2109.01652](http://arxiv.org/abs/2109.01652)\]. More recent work advocates for data-oblivious approaches, such as Centered Kernel Alignment (CKA), to analyze intrinsic layer-wise representation dynamics in pretrained models before fine-tuning \[[2110.04366](http://arxiv.org/abs/2110.04366)\]. CKA has been effective in quantifying similarity between neural representations, enabling the identification of layers that undergo significant shifts during training and fine-tuning \[[2012.13255](http://arxiv.org/abs/2012.13255)\]\[[2109.05687](http://arxiv.org/abs/2109.05687)\]. This line of inquiry reveals that certain layers, often termed "critical layers," exhibit pronounced changes in representation spaces and are pivotal for downstream task performance \[[2106.10199](http://arxiv.org/abs/2106.10199)\]\[[2106.09685](http://arxiv.org/abs/2106.09685)\].

The concept of critical layers aligns with findings from parameter-efficient transfer learning methods, which demonstrate that fine-tuning a small subset of parameters—often localized to specific layers—suffices for effective adaptation \[[2110.04366](http://arxiv.org/abs/2110.04366)\]\[[2106.09685](http://arxiv.org/abs/2106.09685)\]\[[1902.00751](http://arxiv.org/abs/1902.00751)\]\[[2304.01933](http://arxiv.org/abs/2304.01933)\]. Techniques such as adapter modules, LoRA, and BitFit focus on modifying only a fraction of model parameters, frequently corresponding to these critical layers, to achieve competitive or superior task performance compared to full fine-tuning with significantly fewer trainable parameters \[[2106.10199](http://arxiv.org/abs/2106.10199)\]\[[2106.09685](http://arxiv.org/abs/2106.09685)\]\[[1902.00751](http://arxiv.org/abs/1902.00751)\]\[[2304.01933](http://arxiv.org/abs/2304.01933)\]. This supports the hypothesis that critical layers encode core semantic and syntactic transitions essential for task adaptation, consistent with observations that fine-tuning these layers leads to greater loss reduction \[[https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/1e077413b25c4d34945cc2707e17e46ed4fe784a](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/1e077413b25c4d34945cc2707e17e46ed4fe784a)\]\[[2101.00190](http://arxiv.org/abs/2101.00190)\].

Spectral analyses of representation shifts have highlighted the role of top principal components in encoding semantic transitions, such as from rationales to conclusions, within LLMs \[[1802.05365](http://arxiv.org/abs/1802.05365)\]. These principal components capture the dominant modes of variation in layer-wise activations, linking changes in these components to functional transformations in model behavior \[[2012.13255](http://arxiv.org/abs/2012.13255)\]\[[1802.05365](http://arxiv.org/abs/1802.05365)\]. Similar phenomena have been observed in contextualized word representations, where deep internal states encode polysemous and context-dependent meanings critical for downstream tasks \[[1802.05365](http://arxiv.org/abs/1802.05365)\]. Understanding how these principal components evolve assists in designing fine-tuning strategies that focus on layers most responsible for semantic changes, enhancing both domain adaptation and robustness.

In the context of domain adaptation, selectively fine-tuning critical layers aligns with recent findings that larger models exhibit low intrinsic dimensionality during fine-tuning, allowing effective adaptation by optimizing fewer parameters concentrated in specific layers \[[2012.13255](http://arxiv.org/abs/2012.13255)\]\[[2402.17193](http://arxiv.org/abs/2402.17193)\]. This low-dimensional reparameterization underpins parameter-efficient fine-tuning techniques and explains why freezing non-critical layers can help reduce overfitting and improve generalization \[[2012.13255](http://arxiv.org/abs/2012.13255)\]\[[2402.17193](http://arxiv.org/abs/2402.17193)\]. Similarly, in adversarial robustness and backdoor defense, freezing critical layers has been shown to diminish attack success rates substantially, corroborating the idea that these layers are central to model behavior and vulnerability \[[2001.08361](http://arxiv.org/abs/2001.08361)\]\[[2304.06767](http://arxiv.org/abs/2304.06767)\].

Methods for aligning LLMs with human preferences and ensuring safe, controllable behavior also relate to fine-tuning dynamics at critical layers. Approaches such as Direct Preference Optimization (DPO) and Reward Ranked FineTuning (RAFT) leverage human feedback to guide model updates, often focusing on parameter-efficient or stable fine-tuning procedures that may implicitly affect critical layers \[[2305.18290](http://arxiv.org/abs/2305.18290)\]\[[2304.06767](http://arxiv.org/abs/2304.06767)\]\[[2406.11191](http://arxiv.org/abs/2406.11191)\]\[[1909.08593](http://arxiv.org/abs/1909.08593)\]. These techniques highlight the practical importance of understanding layer-wise representation changes for both performance and alignment.

Finally, scaling laws and empirical studies on fine-tuning reveal that the effectiveness of different fine-tuning methods, including full-model tuning and parameter-efficient tuning (PET) approaches like LoRA and prefix tuning, depends on model size, data availability, and task characteristics \[[2106.09685](http://arxiv.org/abs/2106.09685)\]\[[2304.01933](http://arxiv.org/abs/2304.01933)\]\[[2402.17193](http://arxiv.org/abs/2402.17193)\]\[[2104.08691](http://arxiv.org/abs/2104.08691)\]. These findings emphasize that critical layers identified by representation analysis can guide the choice of fine-tuning strategies tailored to the model scale and downstream task, improving efficiency and performance trade-offs.

In summary, the identification and analysis of critical layers through data-oblivious metrics like CKA, combined with spectral insights into principal components, provide a principled foundation for understanding and optimizing LLM fine-tuning. This body of work informs efficient domain adaptation, robustness to adversarial interventions, and alignment with human preferences, all key challenges in deploying LLMs effectively.
