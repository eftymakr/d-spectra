## Related Works

Active learning (AL) aims to reduce annotation costs by selectively querying the most informative unlabeled samples for labeling, thereby improving model efficiency \[[1912.04977](http://arxiv.org/abs/1912.04977)\]. However, conventional AL methods often struggle with limited data diversity and restricted annotation budgets, particularly in distributed environments where data is fragmented and privacy constraints prevent data sharing \[[1610.05492](http://arxiv.org/abs/1610.05492)\]. Federated Active Learning (FAL) extends AL to federated learning (FL) settings, enabling collaborative model training and data selection across clients without exposing raw local data \[[1602.05629](http://arxiv.org/abs/1602.05629)\]. Despite these advancements, existing FAL techniques frequently overlook the challenges posed by client heterogeneity—both in terms of non-identical data distributions (statistical heterogeneity) and variable system capabilities (systems heterogeneity)—which can lead to unstable model convergence and degraded accuracy \[[2102.02079](http://arxiv.org/abs/2102.02079)\]\[[1812.06127](http://arxiv.org/abs/1812.06127)\].

Federated learning itself has been extensively studied as a privacy-preserving distributed learning paradigm where multiple clients collaboratively train a shared model while keeping data decentralized \[[1912.04977](http://arxiv.org/abs/1912.04977)\]\[[1602.05629](http://arxiv.org/abs/1602.05629)\]. A core difficulty in FL is the handling of non-IID (non-independent and identically distributed) data across clients, which causes model divergence and accuracy degradation \[[2102.02079](http://arxiv.org/abs/2102.02079)\]\[[1806.00582](http://arxiv.org/abs/1806.00582)\]\[[1909.06335](http://arxiv.org/abs/1909.06335)\]. Various approaches have addressed this issue: FedProx modifies the Federated Averaging (FedAvg) algorithm to allow flexible computation on heterogeneous devices and improves convergence guarantees under statistical heterogeneity \[[1812.06127](http://arxiv.org/abs/1812.06127)\]. SCAFFOLD introduces control variates to reduce client drift caused by heterogeneous data, thus improving convergence speed and stability \[[1910.06378](http://arxiv.org/abs/1910.06378)\]. Adaptive federated optimization methods adapt classical optimizers such as Adam and Adagrad to the federated setting, mitigating tuning difficulties caused by data heterogeneity \[[2003.00295](http://arxiv.org/abs/2003.00295)\]. These methods improve general FL training but do not explicitly focus on data selection for annotation, which is critical in FAL.

To further cope with heterogeneity, personalized and clustered federated learning methods have been proposed. Clustered FL techniques, such as CFL and IFCA, partition clients into clusters based on similarity in data distributions or tasks, enabling specialized model training that improves performance in heterogeneous federated environments \[[1910.01991](http://arxiv.org/abs/1910.01991)\]\[[2006.04088](http://arxiv.org/abs/2006.04088)\]. Personalized FL approaches, including meta-learning adaptations, learn global models that can be quickly fine-tuned to individual clients’ data, addressing heterogeneity while maintaining privacy \[[1909.12488](http://arxiv.org/abs/1909.12488)\]\[[2102.07078](http://arxiv.org/abs/2102.07078)\]. However, these personalization strategies typically assume fully labeled data and do not integrate active learning mechanisms to optimize annotation efficiency.

Communication efficiency and client resource heterogeneity are additional challenges in FL. Strategies like structured and sketched updates reduce communication overhead by compressing model updates, supporting scalability to large client populations with limited bandwidth \[[1610.05492](http://arxiv.org/abs/1610.05492)\]\[[1602.05629](http://arxiv.org/abs/1602.05629)\]. Client selection algorithms such as FedCS and q-FedAvg dynamically choose clients based on system resources or fairness objectives to optimize training efficiency and fairness \[[1804.08333](http://arxiv.org/abs/1804.08333)\]\[[1905.10497](http://arxiv.org/abs/1905.10497)\]. While these methods improve FL training, they generally do not focus on the active selection of unlabeled samples for annotation.

The integration of active learning into FL remains nascent. Early FAL work has explored client-wise data selection strategies that consider heterogeneity in data distributions, such as correlation-based client selection and model distillation techniques to enable personalized federated models \[[2006.07242](http://arxiv.org/abs/2006.07242)\]\[[2007.1439](http://arxiv.org/abs/2007.14390)\]\[[1910.01991](http://arxiv.org/abs/1910.01991)\]. Ensemble distillation methods facilitate flexible model aggregation from heterogeneous client models, reducing communication rounds and improving robustness \[[2006.07242](http://arxiv.org/abs/2006.07242)\]. These works highlight the potential for combining active learning and FL but often neglect the dynamic behavior of model uncertainty and decision boundary shifts during training, which are crucial for identifying informative samples in federated settings.

Our work, CHASe, addresses this gap by explicitly modeling epistemic variation—the fluctuations of model uncertainty especially near decision boundaries—across training epochs to guide data selection in FAL. This approach builds upon insights into client heterogeneity and model divergence in FL \[[2102.02079](http://arxiv.org/abs/2102.02079)\]\[[1812.06127](http://arxiv.org/abs/1812.06127)\]\[[1910.06378](http://arxiv.org/abs/1910.06378)\] and leverages techniques for efficient communication and client management \[[1610.05492](http://arxiv.org/abs/1610.05492)\]\[[1804.08333](http://arxiv.org/abs/1804.08333)\]. By combining uncertainty tracking, decision boundary calibration, and efficient data subset sampling, CHASe advances the state of the art in Federated Active Learning, improving both annotation efficiency and model accuracy under heterogeneous federation scenarios.
