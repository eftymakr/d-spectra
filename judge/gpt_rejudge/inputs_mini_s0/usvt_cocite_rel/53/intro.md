## Related Works

Sequential recommendation has been extensively studied with a variety of deep learning architectures aimed at capturing the temporal dynamics and evolving preferences of users. Early methods such as Markov Chains (MCs) and Recurrent Neural Networks (RNNs) focus on modeling short-term and long-term dependencies, respectively, with MCs performing well on sparse datasets and RNNs excelling on denser data \[[1808.09781](http://arxiv.org/abs/1808.09781)\]. However, these approaches often struggle with long-range dependencies and efficient handling of variable-length sequences. To address these issues, self-attention mechanisms have been introduced, enabling models to capture long-term semantics adaptively, as demonstrated by SASRec \[[1808.09781](http://arxiv.org/abs/1808.09781)\], which balances the parsimony of MCs and the expressiveness of RNNs.

Building on self-attention, BERT4Rec employs bidirectional transformers with a Cloze objective to model user behavior sequences more effectively by conditioning on both left and right contexts, overcoming the limitations of unidirectional models \[[1904.06690](http://arxiv.org/abs/1904.06690)\]. Extensions to self-attention further incorporate temporal information explicitly, such as TiSASRec, which models time intervals between interactions to improve next-item prediction \[[https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/9c7a455b1e48d01a99e58e884a6c1acb75074ad0](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/9c7a455b1e48d01a99e58e884a6c1acb75074ad0)\]. Beyond item-level modeling, feature-level sequential patterns have also been explored; FDSA separately models item and feature transitions through deeper self-attention blocks, demonstrating the importance of capturing multi-level sequential dependencies \[[https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/63555b4f363ee72dd8d49d8deca68760542c0cd2](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/63555b4f363ee72dd8d49d8deca68760542c0cd2)\].

Self-supervised learning has emerged as a powerful paradigm to enhance representation learning for sequential recommendation, particularly under data sparsity. S3-Rec leverages mutual information maximization across various granularities (attribute, item, subsequence, sequence) to improve data representations via pre-training, leading to superior performance especially in limited data regimes \[[2008.07873](http://arxiv.org/abs/2008.07873)\]. Likewise, contrastive learning methods like DuoRec mitigate representation degeneration by enforcing uniform embedding distributions, enhancing item embedding quality and recommendation accuracy \[[2110.05730](http://arxiv.org/abs/2110.05730)\].

In parallel, graph neural networks (GNNs) have been applied to session-based recommendation to model complex item transitions beyond linear sequences. SR-GNN constructs session graphs to capture intricate item relationships and combines global preferences with session-specific interests through attention networks \[[1811.00855](http://arxiv.org/abs/1811.00855)\]. Similarly, convolutional models like Caser embed recent item sequences as latent images and apply convolutional filters to extract local sequential patterns, effectively capturing both general and sequential preferences \[[1809.07426](http://arxiv.org/abs/1809.07426)\]. Recent work also explores all-MLP architectures with learnable filtering in the frequency domain (FMLP-Rec), which improves noise robustness and computational efficiency, sometimes surpassing Transformer-based models \[[2202.13556](http://arxiv.org/abs/2202.13556)\]. 

Handling diverse user behaviors in sequential recommendation has been addressed by mixture-of-experts (MoE) frameworks. ComiRec captures multiple user interests from behavior sequences and uses a controllable aggregation module to balance accuracy and diversity, facilitating adaptive recommendation across focused and explorative user intents \[[2005.09347](http://arxiv.org/abs/2005.09347)\]. MoE architectures have proven effective in routing different behavioral patterns to specialized experts, improving model capacity to handle heterogeneous user interactions \[[1811.00855](http://arxiv.org/abs/1811.00855)\]\[[https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/2ca1abd015c72e7c04d63fa653b0a27d9592fc02](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/2ca1abd015c72e7c04d63fa653b0a27d9592fc02)\]\[[1809.07426](http://arxiv.org/abs/1809.07426)\]. This adaptive routing aligns with the design goals of STAR-Rec’s sequence-level MoE component.

State-space models (SSMs) offer a complementary approach for efficient modeling of variable-length sequences by compressing temporal dynamics into latent states, enabling linear complexity in sequence length \[[https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/9c7a455b1e48d01a99e58e884a6c1acb75074ad0](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/9c7a455b1e48d01a99e58e884a6c1acb75074ad0)\]. Such models naturally unify with attention mechanisms, where SSMs capture temporal evolution and attention models item relationships, providing a powerful framework for sequential recommendation \[[https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/9c7a455b1e48d01a99e58e884a6c1acb75074ad0](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/9c7a455b1e48d01a99e58e884a6c1acb75074ad0)\]. Although less explored than attention-based models, SSMs bring efficiency and temporal interpretability to sequential recommendation tasks.

Transfer learning and universal sequence representation learning have also gained attention. PeterRec introduces parameter-efficient transfer learning by injecting small adaptive networks into a frozen pre-trained backbone, enabling effective adaptation across multiple downstream recommendation tasks with limited data \[[https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/2ca1abd015c72e7c04d63fa653b0a27d9592fc02](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/2ca1abd015c72e7c04d63fa653b0a27d9592fc02)\]. UniSRec further generalizes this by leveraging item description texts and contrastive pre-training to obtain transferable sequence representations that perform well in cross-domain and cross-platform scenarios \[[2206.05941](http://arxiv.org/abs/2206.05941)\].

In summary, the evolution of sequential recommendation has moved from MCs and RNNs toward self-attention and Transformer-based models that better capture long-range dependencies and user intent. Enhancements through temporal modeling, feature-level attention, self-supervised learning, and contrastive regularization have improved representation quality and robustness. Graph and convolutional methods provide alternative sequence modeling paradigms. Mixture-of-experts frameworks enable adaptive handling of diverse user behaviors, while state-space models offer efficient temporal compression. Transfer learning approaches facilitate knowledge reuse across domains. STAR-Rec builds on these advances by integrating preference-aware attention, state-space modeling, and a mixture-of-experts architecture to address the challenges of sequence length variation and diverse interaction patterns effectively.
