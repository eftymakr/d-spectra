## Related Works

Deep learning methods for inverse imaging problems have broadly evolved into two prominent categories: plug-and-play (PnP) algorithms and deep unrolling (or unfolding) networks. PnP methods incorporate advanced denoisers as implicit priors within iterative optimization schemes, offering flexibility across varying problem settings such as noise levels and compression ratios (CRs) without retraining. Early works demonstrated the effectiveness of denoising networks as proximal operators, enabling PnP frameworks to solve diverse inverse problems like image deblurring and super-resolution \[[1704.03488](http://arxiv.org/abs/1704.03488)\]\[[1704.03264](http://arxiv.org/abs/1704.03264)\]\[[2008.13751](http://arxiv.org/abs/2008.13751)\]. However, PnP methods generally face challenges in reconstruction accuracy and computational speed compared to end-to-end learning methods.

Deep unrolling approaches address these limitations by transforming a fixed number of iterative optimization steps into a multi-stage neural network architecture trained end-to-end. This strategy combines the interpretability and domain knowledge of traditional iterative algorithms with the representation learning power of CNNs, resulting in faster inference and higher reconstruction accuracy \[[1902.05399](http://arxiv.org/abs/1902.05399)\]\[[1508.02848](http://arxiv.org/abs/1508.02848)\]\[[1611.03679](http://arxiv.org/abs/1611.03679)\]\[[1705.08041](http://arxiv.org/abs/1705.08041)\]\[[1409.2574](http://arxiv.org/abs/1409.2574)\]. For instance, unrolled algorithms based on half-quadratic splitting (HQS) or alternating direction method of multipliers (ADMM) have been successfully employed for image restoration tasks, offering a principled framework that incorporates explicit forward models and learned priors \[[1902.05399](http://arxiv.org/abs/1902.05399)\]\[[2003.10428](http://arxiv.org/abs/2003.10428)\]\[[1705.08041](http://arxiv.org/abs/1705.08041)\]\[[1707.06474](http://arxiv.org/abs/1707.06474)\]. These networks, however, generally require retraining or fine-tuning when the measurement conditions or compression ratios change, limiting their flexibility.

Several studies have sought to bridge the gap between PnP and unrolling methods. The use of fixed denoising networks as learned proximal operators within unrolled optimization frameworks has been explored to gain both interpretability and adaptability \[[1704.03488](http://arxiv.org/abs/1704.03488)\]\[[2008.13751](http://arxiv.org/abs/2008.13751)\]. This hybrid approach benefits from the modularity of PnP while retaining the end-to-end training advantages of unrolling. Moreover, recent advances propose training deep denoiser priors that generalize well across different noise levels and imaging tasks, further improving the robustness of PnP frameworks \[[1704.03264](http://arxiv.org/abs/1704.03264)\]\[[1608.03981](http://arxiv.org/abs/1608.03981)\]\[[2008.13751](http://arxiv.org/abs/2008.13751)\].

In the context of compressive sensing and single-pixel imaging, model-based deep learning methods have demonstrated remarkable performance gains. For example, MoDL introduces a model-based deep architecture that integrates CNN regularizers with data-consistency steps using conjugate gradient solvers, achieving high accuracy with fewer trainable parameters and fast convergence \[[1712.02862](http://arxiv.org/abs/1712.02862)\]. Similarly, networks inspired by iterative shrinkage-thresholding algorithms (ISTA) and approximate message passing (AMP) have been unrolled to form interpretable, efficient networks that outperform classical optimization methods in compressive image recovery \[[1704.06625](http://arxiv.org/abs/1704.06625)\]\[[https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/2cdc9bbde0f847094582b212f980fa4dbc48950d](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/2cdc9bbde0f847094582b212f980fa4dbc48950d)\]. These methods illustrate the power of combining algorithmic insights with deep learning to tackle challenging inverse problems.

Algorithm unrolling has also been applied extensively to blind image deblurring, where the network architecture mirrors iterative algorithms such as total variation regularization or nonlinear reaction diffusion models \[[1902.05399](http://arxiv.org/abs/1902.05399)\]\[[1508.02848](http://arxiv.org/abs/1508.02848)\]\[[https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/b954a94af132294a4a4e2efce15027758a535742](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/b954a94af132294a4a4e2efce15027758a535742)\]. These unrolled networks often achieve state-of-the-art performance while maintaining interpretability and computational efficiency. Notably, unrolling frameworks have been extended with novel loss functions and training strategies, such as proximal trajectory losses, to better approximate ideal proximal operators and improve generalization across varying acquisition conditions \[[2003.10428](http://arxiv.org/abs/2003.10428)\]\[[1705.08041](http://arxiv.org/abs/1705.08041)\].

Finally, the convergence and theoretical guarantees of PnP algorithms have been studied, including online and batch variants that accommodate large datasets and denoisers not necessarily corresponding to proximal operators \[[1809.04693](http://arxiv.org/abs/1809.04693)\]. These analyses enhance the understanding of PnP frameworks and support their application in large-scale imaging problems.

In summary, the current literature evidences a rich interplay between PnP and unrolling methodologies for inverse problems in imaging. PnP methods provide flexible, modular frameworks that adapt to varying measurement conditions, while unrolled networks offer superior accuracy and efficiency when trained for specific settings. Hybrid approaches that unify these paradigms by learning proximal operators within unrolled architectures present promising avenues to achieve both adaptability and high performance, particularly relevant for compressive sensing and single-pixel imaging tasks \[[1704.03488](http://arxiv.org/abs/1704.03488)\]\[[1712.02862](http://arxiv.org/abs/1712.02862)\]\[[1902.05399](http://arxiv.org/abs/1902.05399)\]\[[1704.03264](http://arxiv.org/abs/1704.03264)\]\[[1704.06625](http://arxiv.org/abs/1704.06625)\]\[[2003.10428](http://arxiv.org/abs/2003.10428)\]\[[1809.04693](http://arxiv.org/abs/1809.04693)\]\[[1705.08041](http://arxiv.org/abs/1705.08041)\]\[[2008.13751](http://arxiv.org/abs/2008.13751)\].
