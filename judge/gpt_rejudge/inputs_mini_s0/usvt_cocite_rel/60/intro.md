## Related Works

Model calibration has been extensively studied to improve the reliability of probabilistic predictions, particularly in classification and ranking tasks where the focus traditionally lies on relative order rather than accurate absolute values. Modern neural networks, despite achieving high predictive accuracy, are often poorly calibrated out-of-the-box, tending to produce overconfident predictions \[[1706.04599](http://arxiv.org/abs/1706.04599)\]. This has motivated the development of various calibration methods, many of which rely on post-hoc adjustment of predicted scores using predefined transformation functions such as Platt scaling or temperature scaling. These methods are typically simple, computationally efficient, and preserve the order of predictions but suffer from limited flexibility due to their fixed functional forms, restricting their ability to model complex calibration mappings \[[1706.04599](http://arxiv.org/abs/1706.04599)\]\[[1909.10155](http://arxiv.org/abs/1909.10155)\].

In the context of learning-to-rank, calibration is especially challenging because ranking objectives emphasize relative ordering and are often invariant to score shifts or scaling, resulting in predictions that lack meaningful absolute values \[[https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/7f6bb146b9c1504ec77100e640d624bdc204f0ac](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/7f6bb146b9c1504ec77100e640d624bdc204f0ac)\]\[[https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/d388760ce74792bb764ccf77ea8620ca77406e56](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/d388760ce74792bb764ccf77ea8620ca77406e56)\]. This invariance poses theoretical and practical challenges, including training instability and difficulties in downstream tasks such as auctions or recommendation systems that require well-calibrated absolute scores \[[https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/7f6bb146b9c1504ec77100e640d624bdc204f0ac](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/7f6bb146b9c1504ec77100e640d624bdc204f0ac)\]. Conventional calibration methods designed for classification do not directly address these ranking-specific issues, motivating research into scale calibration techniques tailored for ranking models. Recent work proposes incorporating calibration directly into the training process by designing calibrated ranking losses, which improve both ranking quality and score calibration simultaneously without additional post-processing \[[https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/7f6bb146b9c1504ec77100e640d624bdc204f0ac](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/7f6bb146b9c1504ec77100e640d624bdc204f0ac)\].

Neural network-based calibration approaches have gained attention for their capacity to learn complex, nonlinear transformations beyond fixed parametric forms. Monotonic neural networks, in particular, provide a promising avenue as they enforce order preservation while allowing flexible modeling of arbitrary monotonic functions \[[1905.11001](http://arxiv.org/abs/1905.11001)\]. This is crucial for ranking calibration, where maintaining relative order is mandatory but the transformation of prediction scores must be expressive enough to correct miscalibration effectively. Unconstrained Monotonic Neural Networks (UMNNs) have demonstrated strong theoretical and empirical properties for modeling monotonic functions flexibly and have been successfully applied to calibration tasks requiring such constraints \[[1905.11001](http://arxiv.org/abs/1905.11001)\]\[[1506.02142](http://arxiv.org/abs/1506.02142)\].

Beyond deterministic calibration, Bayesian and ensemble-based methods aim to quantify uncertainty and improve calibration by representing epistemic and aleatoric uncertainties explicitly. Bayesian neural networks and Monte Carlo dropout approximate Bayesian inference to provide uncertainty estimates but often incur significant computational overhead and complexity, limiting their practicality in large-scale ranking systems \[[1905.10713](http://arxiv.org/abs/1905.10713)\]\[[1506.02142](http://arxiv.org/abs/1506.02142)\]\[[1906.02530](http://arxiv.org/abs/1906.02530)\]\[[1703.04977](http://arxiv.org/abs/1703.04977)\]. Deep ensembles offer a simpler yet effective alternative, producing well-calibrated predictions and robustness to distributional shifts without requiring Bayesian machinery \[[1612.01474](http://arxiv.org/abs/1612.01474)\]\[[1906.02530](http://arxiv.org/abs/1906.02530)\]. However, these approaches primarily target uncertainty quantification rather than explicit calibration of score magnitudes.

Data augmentation techniques such as mixup training have also been shown to improve calibration by encouraging smoother decision boundaries and reducing overconfidence in neural networks \[[1905.11001](http://arxiv.org/abs/1905.11001)\]. Mixup’s label smoothing effect leads to better calibrated softmax outputs, which has been validated across large-scale image classification benchmarks. Nevertheless, these improvements are indirect and do not guarantee monotonicity or expressiveness needed for ranking calibration.

Several methods focus on post-hoc calibration that leverages discretization or binning strategies, such as histogram binning and Bayesian binning into quantiles (BBQ), which provide non-parametric calibration guarantees but can be sample inefficient or lack scalability \[[1909.10155](http://arxiv.org/abs/1909.10155)\]\[[https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/8d115c3b2ee80e0754360a154a9369bc1658b607](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/8d115c3b2ee80e0754360a154a9369bc1658b607)\]. Hybrid approaches, like scaling-binning calibrators, combine parametric scaling with binning to balance expressiveness and sample efficiency, achieving improved calibration error bounds with theoretical guarantees \[[1909.10155](http://arxiv.org/abs/1909.10155)\]. Similarly, field-aware calibration methods integrate domain-specific information to enhance calibration quality on sensitive input fields, showing improved negative log-likelihood and calibration error metrics \[[1905.10713](http://arxiv.org/abs/1905.10713)\].

In summary, while classical calibration methods offer simplicity and efficiency, their limited expressiveness restricts performance in complex ranking calibration scenarios. Bayesian and ensemble methods enhance uncertainty estimation but often at a computational cost. Neural network-based calibrators, particularly those enforcing monotonicity like UMNNs, provide a flexible and theoretically sound framework for learning expressive, order-preserving calibration functions. Incorporating novel loss functions that enforce smoothness or other calibration criteria further refines calibration quality. Our approach builds upon these advances by leveraging UMNNs coupled with a Smooth Calibration Loss to achieve superior calibration performance tailored to ranking models, addressing the limitations of existing methods \[[https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/7f6bb146b9c1504ec77100e640d624bdc204f0ac](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/7f6bb146b9c1504ec77100e640d624bdc204f0ac)\]\[[1905.11001](http://arxiv.org/abs/1905.11001)\]\[[1506.02142](http://arxiv.org/abs/1506.02142)\].
