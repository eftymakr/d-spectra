## Related Works

Zero-Knowledge Proofs (ZKPs) are foundational cryptographic protocols that enable one party to prove the validity of a statement without revealing any additional information beyond its correctness \[[https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/9ccf7b6cb32cf89752a35bd910555adac54773e0](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/9ccf7b6cb32cf89752a35bd910555adac54773e0)\]\[[https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/6bcd2606c8f0099a82bd03e520659058a5b12e51](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/6bcd2606c8f0099a82bd03e520659058a5b12e51)\]. This property has led to their increasing use in privacy-preserving applications such as blockchain, verifiable machine learning, and secure outsourced computation. Despite their promise, the computational complexity of ZKP proof generation remains a significant barrier to widespread adoption \[[https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/9ccf7b6cb32cf89752a35bd910555adac54773e0](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/9ccf7b6cb32cf89752a35bd910555adac54773e0)\].

A substantial body of research has focused on accelerating the two most computationally intensive primitives in ZKP protocols: Number Theoretic Transforms (NTT) and Multi-Scalar Multiplications (MSM). These operations dominate the runtime of popular zkSNARK protocols such as Groth16 and newer constructions. Several hardware-accelerated implementations have been proposed to address these bottlenecks.

GPU-based acceleration efforts include GZKP, which optimizes polynomial computations and MSMs to achieve up to 48× speedups over CPU baselines by leveraging cache-friendly memory access and fine-grained parallelism \[[https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/e149d93531d8c07cf43f6cae327a4795144d1304](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/e149d93531d8c07cf43f6cae327a4795144d1304)\]. cuZK further advances GPU acceleration by introducing a novel parallel MSM algorithm that achieves near-linear speedup compared to the classical Pippenger algorithm, along with parallelized matrix-vector multiplications and reduced CPU-GPU data transfer overhead, leading to up to 4.86× speedups on standard benchmarks \[[https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/e4a4608e8e35379e63b9df5bda1b7599ee8ab9ac](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/e4a4608e8e35379e63b9df5bda1b7599ee8ab9ac)\]. These works demonstrate the potential of GPUs for accelerating ZKP proof generation but still face challenges related to memory bandwidth and latency.

ASIC-based approaches provide even greater acceleration and energy efficiency. SZKP presents the first full-chip ASIC accelerator capable of executing entire ZKP proofs on-chip by exploiting structured dataflows for NTT and MSM operations. SZKP achieves over 400× speedup compared to CPU implementations and outperforms GPU solutions by an order of magnitude, highlighting the benefits of custom hardware for scalable ZKP acceleration \[[2408.05890](http://arxiv.org/abs/2408.05890)\]. Similarly, PipeZK adopts a pipelined architecture dividing the workload between specialized subsystems for polynomial computations and MSMs, achieving 10× speedup on cryptographic benchmarks and 5× on cryptocurrency workloads \[[https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/6ee84674b7c426849c3da56cb278c08eb7a903cf](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/6ee84674b7c426849c3da56cb278c08eb7a903cf)\].

Beyond generic ZKP acceleration, recent research has explored zero-knowledge proofs tailored to machine learning applications. zkCNN enables zero-knowledge proofs of convolutional neural network (CNN) predictions and accuracy with highly efficient protocols based on sumcheck and fast Fourier transform optimizations, supporting large-scale CNNs such as VGG16 with proof generation times under 90 seconds \[[https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/6336149196f02d0eb6c1a89bc6b662e4422bd794](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/6336149196f02d0eb6c1a89bc6b662e4422bd794)\]. Likewise, zero-knowledge proofs for decision tree predictions and accuracy have been proposed, achieving practical proof sizes and generation times for moderately complex models \[[https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/3becc2935d1f52c82ffd7216c28bc86f5e393208](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/3becc2935d1f52c82ffd7216c28bc86f5e393208)\]. These works illustrate the expanding scope of ZKPs into complex, real-world verification tasks.

Fully Homomorphic Encryption (FHE) shares a related goal of secure computation on encrypted data, and recent hardware accelerators such as F1 and CraterLake have demonstrated dramatic speedups (thousands-fold) over CPU implementations by specializing functional units for modular arithmetic and number-theoretic transforms, and by optimizing data movement \[[https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/b718a4b59ee6ee21c7c2ee1aa64085231853535e](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/b718a4b59ee6ee21c7c2ee1aa64085231853535e)\]\[[2109.05371](http://arxiv.org/abs/2109.05371)\]. Although FHE and ZKP have distinct cryptographic objectives, techniques from FHE hardware acceleration inform ZKP accelerator design, particularly regarding large-scale polynomial arithmetic and modular operations.

The HyperPlonk protocol represents a state-of-the-art ZKP construction combining universal, one-time trusted setup with succinct proof sizes, making it attractive for consensus-based public verification. Prior accelerators have largely targeted individual primitives rather than the entire protocol end-to-end \[[https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/9ccf7b6cb32cf89752a35bd910555adac54773e0](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/9ccf7b6cb32cf89752a35bd910555adac54773e0)\]. The zkSpeed accelerator described in this work advances the state-of-the-art by comprehensively accelerating the HyperPlonk protocol—including the SumCheck protocol and MSM computations—on a full-chip ASIC with 2 TB/s bandwidth and 366.46 mm² area. zkSpeed achieves geometric mean speedups of 801× over CPU baselines, substantially surpassing prior GPU and ASIC efforts \[[https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/9ccf7b6cb32cf89752a35bd910555adac54773e0](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/9ccf7b6cb32cf89752a35bd910555adac54773e0)\].

In summary, prior research has demonstrated significant performance gains in ZKP proof generation through hardware acceleration on GPUs and ASICs, focusing on key primitives such as NTT and MSM. However, most solutions either require application-specific trusted setups or produce larger proofs with higher verification costs, limiting their applicability in large-scale, publicly verifiable systems. The zkSpeed accelerator addresses these limitations by supporting HyperPlonk’s universal setup and small proof sizes while delivering unprecedented acceleration across the entire proof generation pipeline. This positions zkSpeed as a critical step toward practical, high-throughput deployment of ZKP protocols in privacy-preserving and verifiable computing applications \[[https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/9ccf7b6cb32cf89752a35bd910555adac54773e0](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/9ccf7b6cb32cf89752a35bd910555adac54773e0)\]\[[2408.05890](http://arxiv.org/abs/2408.05890)\]\[[https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/e149d93531d8c07cf43f6cae327a4795144d1304](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/e149d93531d8c07cf43f6cae327a4795144d1304)\]\[[https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/6ee84674b7c426849c3da56cb278c08eb7a903cf](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/6ee84674b7c426849c3da56cb278c08eb7a903cf)\]\[[https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/e4a4608e8e35379e63b9df5bda1b7599ee8ab9ac](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/e4a4608e8e35379e63b9df5bda1b7599ee8ab9ac)\]\[[https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/3becc2935d1f52c82ffd7216c28bc86f5e393208](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/3becc2935d1f52c82ffd7216c28bc86f5e393208)\]\[[https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/6336149196f02d0eb6c1a89bc6b662e4422bd794](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/6336149196f02d0eb6c1a89bc6b662e4422bd794)\]\[[https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/b718a4b59ee6ee21c7c2ee1aa64085231853535e](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/b718a4b59ee6ee21c7c2ee1aa64085231853535e)\]\[[2109.05371](http://arxiv.org/abs/2109.05371)\].
