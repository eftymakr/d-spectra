## Related Works

Table discovery is a foundational problem in data management, especially given the rapid growth of tabular data repositories. Traditional table retrieval methods generally rely on matching query tables or loosely defined keywords, which often culminates in extensive result sets requiring manual post-filtering by users. This limitation has spurred interest in more refined retrieval paradigms that incorporate natural language (NL) conditions to better capture user intent and reduce irrelevant results, motivating the newly proposed task of NL-conditional table discovery (nlcTD) \[[2005.08314](http://arxiv.org/abs/2005.08314)\]\[[https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/9d6931dd0ba9e492b0ceab00268ca4be62ef663a](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/9d6931dd0ba9e492b0ceab00268ca4be62ef663a)\].

Existing research in table discovery and related areas has made significant strides in dataset construction and model development. For example, datasets focusing on table detection and structure recognition have been introduced to support the development of robust models capable of handling diverse table formats and noisy real-world inputs \[[2005.08314](http://arxiv.org/abs/2005.08314)\]. Benchmarks like WikiTableQuestions and Spider have demonstrated the utility of joint modeling of natural language and tabular data, as exemplified by TaBERT, a pretrained language model that learns unified representations of NL sentences and structured tables, achieving state-of-the-art results in semantic parsing tasks \[[https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/bff7592f28354b3be632f4bf678b622f765a2b8d](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/bff7592f28354b3be632f4bf678b622f765a2b8d)\]. Such joint representations are critical for tasks requiring deep semantic alignment between query language and table content, a challenge directly relevant to nlcTD.

While table detection and recognition datasets provide foundational tools, they fall short of addressing retrieval conditioned on NL constraints. Related works in table-to-text generation and table-based question answering datasets emphasize the complexity of aligning NL queries with table semantics, highlighting the need for datasets that combine both structured and unstructured data modalities . These efforts underscore the challenges inherent in tasks where understanding both the tabular schema and the NL expression is crucial.

Furthermore, existing benchmarks have revealed substantial performance gaps in current table discovery techniques when evaluated under more realistic and demanding conditions involving NL constraints. This observation points to the insufficiency of current approaches that primarily focus on keyword or structural matching without leveraging richer semantic signals \[[2005.08314](http://arxiv.org/abs/2005.08314)\]\[[https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/9d6931dd0ba9e492b0ceab00268ca4be62ef663a](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/9d6931dd0ba9e492b0ceab00268ca4be62ef663a)\]. The integration of NL conditions into table discovery necessitates advanced models capable of reasoning over both the query table and the natural language specification, which remains an open research frontier.

The nlcTables dataset directly addresses these gaps by providing a large-scale, richly annotated benchmark consisting of 627 diverse queries that span NL-only, union, join, and fuzzy matching conditions, along with over 22,000 candidate tables and more than 21,000 relevance annotations. This comprehensive resource enables rigorous evaluation of NL-conditional table discovery models and fosters the development of novel retrieval algorithms that can better handle the semantic complexity inherent in this task \[[2005.08314](http://arxiv.org/abs/2005.08314)\].

In summary, prior works have established important foundational datasets and models for table detection, structure recognition, and semantic parsing over tables and NL. However, they do not fully address the challenge of combining query tables with nuanced NL conditions for retrieval. Our work builds upon these foundations by explicitly targeting the nlcTD task, supported by a novel benchmark that captures the complexity of real-world table discovery scenarios with NL refinement.
