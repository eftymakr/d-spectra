## Related Works

Federated Learning (FL) enables collaborative model training across decentralized clients while preserving data privacy by avoiding raw data sharing \[[2203.07320](http://arxiv.org/abs/2203.07320)\]. However, the emergence of privacy regulations such as the "right to be forgotten" (RTBF) has introduced the critical need for mechanisms allowing users to remove their data’s influence from trained models, giving rise to the subfield of Federated Unlearning (FU) \[[2310.20448](http://arxiv.org/abs/2310.20448)\]\[[https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/eadeffdec9fac8fd7f9aea732ca410eb082b7dcf](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/eadeffdec9fac8fd7f9aea732ca410eb082b7dcf)\]. FU aims to efficiently remove the contribution of specific clients or data points from a global FL model without costly retraining from scratch, which is often prohibitive in distributed settings.

### Federated Unlearning Techniques

Early federated unlearning methods primarily rely on retraining or approximate unlearning by manipulating model updates or gradients. For example, FedEraser leverages stored historical client updates at the server to reconstruct the unlearned model rapidly by subtracting targeted client influences, achieving up to 4× speedup over retraining \[[https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/eadeffdec9fac8fd7f9aea732ca410eb082b7dcf](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/eadeffdec9fac8fd7f9aea732ca410eb082b7dcf)\]. However, such approaches require storage of update histories, which may be impractical in resource-constrained or privacy-sensitive environments.

To address storage and privacy concerns, alternative methods avoid reliance on historical data. One technique performs local unlearning at the target client and then fine-tunes the global model with a few federated rounds involving remaining clients to restore performance, eliminating the need for server-side update storage or access to original training data \[[2207.05521](http://arxiv.org/abs/2207.05521)\]. Bayesian approaches such as BFU introduce an adaptive unlearning rate to balance forgetting and model accuracy, optimizing parameter sharing while maintaining privacy \[[https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/efab3b30d6f67f55c68f9dc7c65b2b54b1789030](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/efab3b30d6f67f55c68f9dc7c65b2b54b1789030)\]. Other approaches like subspace-based federated unlearning (SFU) remove client influence by projecting model updates orthogonally to the target client's gradient subspace, requiring no extra storage and incorporating differential privacy for representation matrices \[[2302.12448](http://arxiv.org/abs/2302.12448)\].

Recent works focus on achieving **exact** federated unlearning with provable guarantees. The FATS framework introduces a TV-stable FL algorithm enabling exact, communication-efficient unlearning with theoretical convergence guarantees under both client- and sample-level data removal \[[2401.11018](http://arxiv.org/abs/2401.11018)\]. Similarly, Exact-Fun employs quantized FL to facilitate exact and efficient unlearning with strong theoretical backing \[[https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/a5f39bcd6198b26997b77233ff81a14e6bc8a32d](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/a5f39bcd6198b26997b77233ff81a14e6bc8a32d)\]. Such approaches represent significant progress toward rigorous, auditable unlearning in FL.

### Unlearning Evaluation and Verification

Effective evaluation of unlearning remains a challenge. Most prior works assess unlearning via single metrics, often focusing on model utility or forgetting rate. However, these are insufficient to capture nuances such as under-unlearning (incomplete forgetting) or over-unlearning (excessive forgetting, harming retained knowledge). VeriFi is a pioneering framework that introduces *verifiable federated unlearning*, combining marking and checking strategies to fingerprint and detect the unlearning effect on the global model, enabling participants to independently verify compliance with RTBF \[[2205.12709](http://arxiv.org/abs/2205.12709)\]. This addresses the critical gap where unlearning processes may not be transparent or verifiable.

Complementing verification frameworks, recent studies propose multi-metric evaluation schemes that assess unlearning efficacy more comprehensively by integrating membership inference-based forgetting rates, model accuracy, and robustness against backdoor or malicious client attacks \[[2310.20448](http://arxiv.org/abs/2310.20448)\]\[[2401.11018](http://arxiv.org/abs/2401.11018)\]\[[2201.09441](http://arxiv.org/abs/2201.09441)\]. Such comprehensive evaluation is essential, especially in Federated Online Learning to Rank (FOLTR) systems, where the balance between privacy and ranking performance is delicate.

### Unlearning in Specialized Federated Settings

Vertical federated learning (VFL), where clients hold disjoint feature sets, introduces unique unlearning challenges. Methods tailored to VFL impose constraints on intermediate parameters and remove client updates with minimal retraining overhead, achieving over 4× efficiency improvements compared to full retraining \[[https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/4e26fc4fe687c7471e44f2a5e6feef0facfc4be1](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/4e26fc4fe687c7471e44f2a5e6feef0facfc4be1)\]. Feature-level unlearning frameworks like Ferrari optimize feature sensitivity to remove sensitive, biased, or backdoor features through Lipschitz continuity-based metrics, applicable in FL contexts \[[2405.17462](http://arxiv.org/abs/2405.17462)\].

Unlearning has also been explored to counter malicious client attacks in FL. FAST employs federated unlearning to eliminate malicious clients’ contributions effectively, accelerating unlearning by up to 16× while recovering model utility \[[https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/2f69d7e9a7428a7e31de83b993a2142d0c2cde67](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/2f69d7e9a7428a7e31de83b993a2142d0c2cde67)\]. Additionally, methods for unlearning backdoors introduced by adversaries extend unlearning capabilities to security-critical scenarios \[[2304.10638](http://arxiv.org/abs/2304.10638)\].

### Incentive and System Design for Federated Unlearning

Beyond algorithmic advances, incentive mechanisms addressing the cost and participation dynamics of unlearning have been proposed. These consider users’ privacy costs and data contributions to design strategies that retain valuable clients while minimizing unlearning overhead, employing evolutionary game theory and reinforcement learning to optimize client selection in unlearning phases \[[https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/a08f1c5c9f0bdb24f0dc75c0d969eb353899156b](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/a08f1c5c9f0bdb24f0dc75c0d969eb353899156b)\]\[[https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/6556402fc6d6a1ebcbf82eed07c64d89dd40f786](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/6556402fc6d6a1ebcbf82eed07c64d89dd40f786)\]. Moreover, asynchronous federated unlearning frameworks cluster clients to limit retraining scope and reduce communication costs, showing up to 85% performance improvements over previous methods \[[https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/70954dd11d1463bc665f803d84b15a4d83805ec0](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/70954dd11d1463bc665f803d84b15a4d83805ec0)\].

### Summary

Overall, federated unlearning research provides a rich set of methods ranging from approximate to exact unlearning with diverse efficiency, privacy, and verification guarantees. The field recognizes the necessity for multi-faceted evaluation metrics and verifiable unlearning to meet legal and practical demands. Emerging directions include privacy-preserving unlearning with cryptographic techniques \[[2404.09724](http://arxiv.org/abs/2404.09724)\], unlearning integrated within training processes \[[2405.15474](http://arxiv.org/abs/2405.15474)\], and specialized solutions for vertical FL and feature-level unlearning \[[https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/4e26fc4fe687c7471e44f2a5e6feef0facfc4be1](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/4e26fc4fe687c7471e44f2a5e6feef0facfc4be1)\]\[[2405.17462](http://arxiv.org/abs/2405.17462)\]. These developments form a robust foundation for advancing federated unlearning in privacy-critical applications such as Federated Online Learning to Rank.
