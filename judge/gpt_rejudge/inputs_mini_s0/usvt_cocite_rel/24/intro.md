## Related Works

Diffusion models have become a dominant approach for high-quality image and video generation, with diffusion transformers (DiTs) showing particularly strong performance due to their scalable architecture and ability to model complex dependencies in data \[[2212.09748](http://arxiv.org/abs/2212.09748)\]\[[2105.05233](http://arxiv.org/abs/2105.05233)\]. However, the large number of parameters and high computational complexity of DiTs limit their deployment on resource-constrained devices, motivating research into quantization techniques that reduce model size and speed up inference while maintaining generation quality \[[2405.16005](http://arxiv.org/abs/2405.16005)\]\[[2405.19751](http://arxiv.org/abs/2405.19751)\].

### Quantization of Diffusion Models

Post-Training Quantization (PTQ) has emerged as a favored method for compressing diffusion models without requiring retraining, thus offering efficiency in time and data usage \[[2302.04304](http://arxiv.org/abs/2302.04304)\]\[[2305.10657](http://arxiv.org/abs/2305.10657)\]\[[2211.15736](http://arxiv.org/abs/2211.15736)\]. However, directly applying conventional PTQ methods to diffusion models presents challenges. The iterative nature of diffusion sampling causes the noise estimation network’s output distributions to vary across timesteps, and certain network layers exhibit bimodal or spatially varying activation distributions, which complicate quantization \[[2302.04304](http://arxiv.org/abs/2302.04304)\]\[[2211.15736](http://arxiv.org/abs/2211.15736)\]\[[2306.02316](http://arxiv.org/abs/2306.02316)\]. To address these issues, timestep-aware calibration and split shortcut quantization have been proposed to better adapt PTQ to the multi-timestep structure of diffusion models \[[2302.04304](http://arxiv.org/abs/2302.04304)\]. Additionally, mixed-precision schemes have been introduced to allocate bitwidths dynamically across timesteps for optimal trade-offs between compression and fidelity \[[2305.10657](http://arxiv.org/abs/2305.10657)\].

For diffusion models with transformer backbones (DiTs), quantization is even more complex due to the presence of salient channels exhibiting extreme magnitude variations and temporal variability in activation distributions \[[2405.16005](http://arxiv.org/abs/2405.16005)\]\[[2406.17343](http://arxiv.org/abs/2406.17343)\]. Methods such as PTQ4DiT apply channel-wise salience balancing and salience calibration guided by correlation metrics to alleviate quantization errors \[[2405.16005](http://arxiv.org/abs/2405.16005)\]. Q-DiT further enhances this by automatically allocating quantization granularity across channels and employing sample-wise dynamic activation quantization to capture spatial and temporal variance, achieving low-bit quantization (down to W4A8) with minimal quality degradation \[[2406.17343](http://arxiv.org/abs/2406.17343)\]. Hybrid floating-point quantization (FP4) has also been introduced in HQ-DiT to better fit the data distribution of DiTs, reducing quantization error and preserving generation quality at 4-bit precision \[[2405.19751](http://arxiv.org/abs/2405.19751)\].

### Video Generation with Diffusion Transformers

Video generation imposes additional challenges beyond image generation, particularly the need to maintain temporal consistency and spatiotemporal correlations across frames. Recent video diffusion models extend image diffusion architectures by introducing temporal layers or 3D variational autoencoders to compress spatial and temporal dimensions jointly \[[2304.08818](http://arxiv.org/abs/2304.08818)\]\[[2408.06072](http://arxiv.org/abs/2408.06072)\]. Architectures like Latte leverage transformer blocks to model spatiotemporal token distributions in latent space, balancing quality and efficiency \[[2401.03048](http://arxiv.org/abs/2401.03048)\]. The W.A.L.T model adopts window attention mechanisms to jointly model spatial and temporal dependencies, facilitating photorealistic video synthesis \[[2306.02316](http://arxiv.org/abs/2306.02316)\].

While these models achieve impressive results, quantization methods tailored for them remain underexplored. ViDiT-Q explicitly addresses quantization for diffusion transformers in both image and video generation contexts, analyzing quantization errors and proposing a scheme that attains W4A8 and W8A8 quantization with negligible loss in visual quality and significant memory and latency improvements \[[2305.10657](http://arxiv.org/abs/2305.10657)\]. However, these approaches often overlook the unique temporal dynamics and inter-frame correlations critical for video generation.

### Addressing Temporal Consistency in Quantization

Maintaining temporal consistency during quantization requires strategies that go beyond static weight and activation quantization. Temporal dynamic quantization methods adjust quantization parameters based on timestep information, significantly improving output quality without additional inference overhead \[[2306.02316](http://arxiv.org/abs/2306.02316)\]. Distillation techniques have also been applied to reduce sampling steps and condense knowledge from full-precision models into quantized counterparts, thereby preserving frame coherence and video context \[[2210.03142](http://arxiv.org/abs/2210.03142)\].

Moreover, recent frameworks have begun integrating optimization-aware quantization with temporal maintenance mechanisms. EfficientDM, for example, introduces quantization-aware fine-tuning with temporal learned step-size quantization, achieving QAT-level performance with PTQ efficiency \[[2310.0327](http://arxiv.org/abs/2310.03270)\]. These methods underscore the importance of jointly optimizing quantization and temporal modeling to preserve high-fidelity video synthesis under low-bit constraints.

### Summary

Prior works on diffusion model quantization have established foundational techniques for compressing large generative models, addressing challenges such as varying activation distributions, timestep-dependent output shifts, and channel salience \[[2302.04304](http://arxiv.org/abs/2302.04304)\]\[[2405.16005](http://arxiv.org/abs/2405.16005)\]\[[2305.10657](http://arxiv.org/abs/2305.10657)\]\[[2406.17343](http://arxiv.org/abs/2406.17343)\]. Extensions to video diffusion transformers highlight the necessity of preserving temporal correlations and maintaining scene consistency across frames \[[2305.10657](http://arxiv.org/abs/2305.10657)\]\[[2306.02316](http://arxiv.org/abs/2306.02316)\]. Our proposed Q-VDiT framework builds upon these insights by introducing a Token-aware Quantization Estimator (TQE) that compensates quantization errors in token and feature dimensions, alongside Temporal Maintenance Distillation (TMD) to optimize spatiotemporal coherence explicitly. This represents a novel advancement in quantization methodology tailored specifically for video diffusion transformers, achieving superior scene consistency and efficiency.
