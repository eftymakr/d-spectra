## Related Works

Fine-grained emotion classification has gained increasing attention in natural language processing due to its ability to capture nuanced affective states beyond simple polarity (positive, negative, neutral). While sentiment analysis traditionally focuses on polarity detection, finer distinctions into discrete emotions such as those defined by Ekman or Plutchik’s models provide richer insights, especially in user-generated content like app reviews \[[https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/7d0531aaaa4dbf4033da964289843e7812262702](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/7d0531aaaa4dbf4033da964289843e7812262702)\]\[[https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/6a6f1b8ef3eefaf8027a76134859b44886a5f795](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/6a6f1b8ef3eefaf8027a76134859b44886a5f795)\]\[[2005.00547](http://arxiv.org/abs/2005.00547)\]. However, resources and datasets specifically targeted at fine-grained emotion detection in the domain of app reviews remain scarce, presenting a significant research gap this study addresses.

Several large-scale datasets have been developed for fine-grained emotion classification, often leveraging social media or narrative text. For example, GoEmotions introduced a large manually annotated dataset of Reddit comments labeled with 27 emotion categories plus neutral, demonstrating the feasibility of multi-class emotion detection with transformer-based models \[[2005.00547](http://arxiv.org/abs/2005.00547)\]. Similarly, EmoNet achieved state-of-the-art results on 24 fine-grained emotion types and Plutchik’s eight primary emotions using gated recurrent neural networks, emphasizing the advantage of large, well-annotated corpora for model training \[[https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/6a6f1b8ef3eefaf8027a76134859b44886a5f795](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/6a6f1b8ef3eefaf8027a76134859b44886a5f795)\]. These efforts highlight the importance of comprehensive annotation frameworks and large datasets to improve emotion recognition performance.

Annotation quality and the complexity of emotion labelling are well documented challenges. Studies have found that emotion annotation often suffers from low inter-annotator agreement due to the subjective and context-dependent nature of emotions \[[https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/7d0531aaaa4dbf4033da964289843e7812262702](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/7d0531aaaa4dbf4033da964289843e7812262702)\]\[[2205.01996](http://arxiv.org/abs/2205.01996)\]\[[1308.6297](http://arxiv.org/abs/1308.6297)\]. For example, EmoBank proposes a bi-perspectival approach distinguishing writer and reader emotions, improving annotation reliability and enabling better dimensional emotion modeling using Valence-Arousal-Dominance (VAD) representations \[[2205.01996](http://arxiv.org/abs/2205.01996)\]. Crowdsourcing methods for word-emotion lexicon creation also demonstrate that carefully designed annotation protocols can reduce noise and improve quality \[[1308.6297](http://arxiv.org/abs/1308.6297)\]. These findings underscore the necessity for clear guidelines and iterative annotation refinement used in this study’s framework.

In the context of mobile app reviews, understanding user emotions can provide valuable insights for software engineering tasks such as feature prioritization, bug detection, and user satisfaction analysis \[[1505.04657](http://arxiv.org/abs/1505.04657)\]\[[https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/695886bf2b7bf90e98e98f630b26dabcdbe78247](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/695886bf2b7bf90e98e98f630b26dabcdbe78247)\]\[[https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/925b94cf95648384da7789c98676c9122e070b87](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/925b94cf95648384da7789c98676c9122e070b87)\]\[[https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/6fe8d604e8e19dc6ed738c8e58b7351aeeed515c](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/6fe8d604e8e19dc6ed738c8e58b7351aeeed515c)\]. While many approaches focus on sentiment polarity or aspect-based sentiment analysis, fewer have addressed fine-grained emotion classification explicitly. Tools like AR-Miner and MARK facilitate mining informative reviews and keyword-based opinion extraction to assist developers in managing large volumes of noisy app reviews \[[https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/ff921a209cc9e1c2120669d3d81e627ca5e53572](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/ff921a209cc9e1c2120669d3d81e627ca5e53572)\]\[[1505.04657](http://arxiv.org/abs/1505.04657)\]. Frameworks combining topic modeling and sentiment analysis have also been proposed to identify feature-specific sentiments, but they usually operate at a coarser sentiment granularity \[[https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/6fe8d604e8e19dc6ed738c8e58b7351aeeed515c](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/6fe8d604e8e19dc6ed738c8e58b7351aeeed515c)\].

Recent advances in large language models (LLMs) have opened new possibilities for automating emotion annotation and classification with reduced manual effort. Investigations show that LLMs perform well on simpler sentiment analysis tasks and can maintain substantial agreement with human annotators in few-shot settings, though challenges remain for fully automating complex emotion interpretation due to subtlety and context dependence \[[2305.15005](http://arxiv.org/abs/2305.15005)\]. These observations align with our findings on the feasibility and limitations of employing LLMs for fine-grained emotion annotation in app reviews. Despite promising results, full automation is constrained by the inherent complexity of emotional expression in user-generated texts.

Moreover, earlier works have explored semantic orientation and polarity detection as foundational techniques for opinion mining, often employing unsupervised or lexicon-based methods to classify review sentiments \[[https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/9e7c7853a16a378cc24a082153b282257a9675b7](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/9e7c7853a16a378cc24a082153b282257a9675b7)\]\[[1509.07761](http://arxiv.org/abs/1509.07761)\]. While these methods provide baseline sentiment insights, fine-grained emotion classification requires more sophisticated models and richer annotation schemes to capture specific emotional states beyond polarity. Likewise, emotion detection in narrative domains, such as children’s fairy tales and literary texts, has demonstrated that machine learning approaches can classify emotional content effectively but are domain-specific and often not directly transferable to app reviews \[[https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/872352b0a53ab6cbb4420f81df64d215d86c7d9b](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/872352b0a53ab6cbb4420f81df64d215d86c7d9b)\]\[[1910.11769](http://arxiv.org/abs/1910.11769)\].

Finally, integrating fine-grained emotion categories into personality detection and user profiling further illustrates the utility of nuanced affective features, showing statistically significant improvements over coarse sentiment features \[[1309.6352](http://arxiv.org/abs/1309.6352)\]. This suggests that capturing subtle emotional distinctions in app reviews could similarly enhance understanding of user behavior and preferences.

In summary, while there is substantial work on sentiment analysis and some on fine-grained emotion detection in general domains, the specific challenges of fine-grained emotion classification in mobile app reviews remain underexplored. This study contributes by providing a structured annotation framework adapted to Plutchik’s taxonomy, a carefully developed dataset, and an empirical evaluation of LLM-based automation, thereby advancing the state of opinion mining in the app review context.
