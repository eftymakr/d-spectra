## Related Works

Graph Convolutional Networks (GCNs) have become a dominant framework for collaborative filtering (CF) in recommender systems by leveraging the graph structure of user-item interactions to capture complex relationships and high-order connectivity \[[1905.08108](http://arxiv.org/abs/1905.08108)\]\[[2002.02126](http://arxiv.org/abs/2002.02126)\]\[[1806.01973](http://arxiv.org/abs/1806.01973)\]. Early CF methods primarily relied on embedding layers to represent users and items, but these often produced latent spaces with limited capacity, neglecting potentially valuable local preference patterns \[[1905.08108](http://arxiv.org/abs/1905.08108)\]. Moreover, standard neighborhood aggregation schemes in GCNs typically employ linear and uniform operations that inadequately model diverse and fine-grained preference signals \[[1708.05031](http://arxiv.org/abs/1708.05031)\]\[[2002.02126](http://arxiv.org/abs/2002.02126)\].

Efforts to simplify and improve GCNs for recommendation have shown that nonlinearities and feature transformations, common in classical GCNs, contribute little to CF performance and may even degrade it due to training difficulties and over-smoothing effects \[[2002.02126](http://arxiv.org/abs/2002.02126)\]\[[2001.10167](http://arxiv.org/abs/2001.10167)\]\[[1902.07153](http://arxiv.org/abs/1902.07153)\]. LightGCN, a simplified linear GCN variant that focuses solely on neighborhood aggregation without nonlinearities or feature transformations, demonstrated substantial performance improvements and easier training \[[2002.02126](http://arxiv.org/abs/2002.02126)\]. UltraGCN further streamlines the process by approximating infinite-layer graph convolutions without explicit message passing, achieving better scalability and faster convergence with minimal performance loss \[[2110.15114](http://arxiv.org/abs/2110.15114)\]. These findings emphasize the importance of linear, neighborhood-based propagation while exposing limitations of fixed, shallow graph filters.

On the spectral graph theory front, graph filtering techniques have been proposed to enhance the expressiveness of GCNs by explicitly manipulating graph signals in the frequency domain. Spectral CF methods model user-item interactions as signals on bipartite graphs and apply spectral filters to better capture connectivity and proximity information \[[1808.10523](http://arxiv.org/abs/1808.10523)\]\[[https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/de2562febbdf3181834648d1fc679e2cb75aa973](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/de2562febbdf3181834648d1fc679e2cb75aa973)\]. Polynomial graph filters, such as those based on Chebyshev polynomials, have been particularly attractive due to their efficiency and ability to approximate flexible, nonlinear filters without costly eigendecompositions \[[1606.09375](http://arxiv.org/abs/1606.09375)\]\[[2106.10994](http://arxiv.org/abs/2106.10994)\]. Such polynomial filters enable capturing information across multiple frequency bands, addressing the limited capacity of standard embedding-based methods and linear filters.

Several recent works have explored the application of polynomial spectral filters in collaborative filtering. PolyCF formulates CF as a graph signal processing problem and employs polynomial filters to capture spectral features across eigenspaces, optimizing filter parameters jointly with ranking objectives to improve recommendation accuracy \[[https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/de2562febbdf3181834648d1fc679e2cb75aa973](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/de2562febbdf3181834648d1fc679e2cb75aa973)\]. This approach highlights the potential of spectral filters to recover missing interactions by approximating optimal response functions on the user-item graph.

In addition to spectral filtering, advances in graph neural architectures have integrated attention mechanisms to better distinguish the importance of neighbors and relations, further enhancing representation learning. Knowledge Graph Attention Networks (KGAT) model high-order relations and side information via attention-based embedding propagation, improving recommendation diversity and explainability \[[1905.07854](http://arxiv.org/abs/1905.07854)\]. Similarly, disentangled graph collaborative filtering techniques model diverse user intents by disentangling representations according to different preference factors, enabling finer-grained modeling beyond uniform neighborhood aggregation \[[2007.01764](http://arxiv.org/abs/2007.01764)\].

Self-supervised learning methods have also been introduced to improve robustness and accuracy of GCNs for recommendation, incorporating auxiliary tasks that encourage consistent node representations under graph perturbations and reducing popularity bias \[[2010.10783](http://arxiv.org/abs/2010.10783)\]\[[2112.08679](http://arxiv.org/abs/2112.08679)\]. These approaches complement spectral filtering by enhancing the quality of learned embeddings through better signal utilization.

While traditional embedding layers rely on learned latent vectors, some works argue for directly leveraging the raw interaction history as graph signals to better exploit the full frequency spectrum \[[1905.08108](http://arxiv.org/abs/1905.08108)\]. This aligns with spectral filtering perspectives, where capturing the entire spectrum of user-item interactions allows for more nuanced preference modeling than embeddings restricted by cutoff frequency or linear forms.

In summary, current research highlights two critical bottlenecks in GCN-based CF: limited embedding capacity and restricted linear neighborhood aggregation. Spectral graph filtering, especially via Chebyshev polynomial approximations, offers a promising solution by enabling flexible, nonlinear filter design that can leverage the full graph frequency spectrum efficiently \[[https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/de2562febbdf3181834648d1fc679e2cb75aa973](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/de2562febbdf3181834648d1fc679e2cb75aa973)\]\[[1606.09375](http://arxiv.org/abs/1606.09375)\]. Simplified GCN models like LightGCN and UltraGCN demonstrate the benefits of focusing on essential graph propagation without unnecessary complexity \[[2002.02126](http://arxiv.org/abs/2002.02126)\]\[[2110.15114](http://arxiv.org/abs/2110.15114)\]. Attention and disentanglement mechanisms further refine neighborhood modeling, while self-supervised learning improves robustness and representation quality \[[2007.01764](http://arxiv.org/abs/2007.01764)\]\[[1905.07854](http://arxiv.org/abs/1905.07854)\]\[[2010.10783](http://arxiv.org/abs/2010.10783)\]. Building on these insights, methods such as ChebyCF that combine spectral filtering with degree normalization and flexible nonlinear filters can overcome existing limitations to achieve state-of-the-art performance in collaborative filtering.
