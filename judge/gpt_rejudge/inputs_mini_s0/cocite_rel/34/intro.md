## Related Works

Fund allocation in financial domains typically relies on accurate time series forecasting to inform decision-making. Traditional approaches, including prediction-only and predict-then-optimize (PnO) frameworks, often suffer from a misalignment between the forecasting objectives and the optimization goals, leading to suboptimal allocation outcomes \[[https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/7b74990bff941aece41437ab9aa9c1f48a3fafca](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/7b74990bff941aece41437ab9aa9c1f48a3fafca)\]. This goal mismatch is further compounded by the uncertainty inherent in state-of-the-art (SOTA) time series forecasting models, which can propagate errors into downstream optimization tasks \[[2407.13278](http://arxiv.org/abs/2407.13278)\].

Classical automatic forecasting methods have leveraged innovations state space models and ARIMA-based stepwise algorithms to handle both seasonal and non-seasonal univariate series effectively \[[https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/7b74990bff941aece41437ab9aa9c1f48a3fafca](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/7b74990bff941aece41437ab9aa9c1f48a3fafca)\]. However, these methods are limited when applied to complex financial data with nonlinear and non-stationary patterns. The rise of deep learning has shifted focus towards models that can capture intricate temporal dependencies and nonlinearities. Architectures combining convolutional and recurrent neural networks (CNNs and RNNs) have been proposed to model both short- and long-term temporal patterns, improving predictive accuracy over classical methods \[[1703.07015](http://arxiv.org/abs/1703.07015)\]. Probabilistic forecasting models such as DeepAR have introduced autoregressive recurrent networks to produce uncertainty-aware predictions, crucial for risk-sensitive financial applications \[[1704.04110](http://arxiv.org/abs/1704.04110)\]. Extensions to multi-horizon probabilistic forecasting further address challenges like seasonality shifts and event spikes, enhancing real-world applicability \[[1711.11053](http://arxiv.org/abs/1711.11053)\].

Transformer-based architectures have recently gained prominence for time series forecasting due to their ability to model global dependencies. Innovations include patching techniques that segment series into subseries-level tokens, reducing computational complexity while preserving local semantics, as seen in PatchTST \[[2211.14730](http://arxiv.org/abs/2211.14730)\]. Autoformer integrates decomposition with an auto-correlation mechanism to progressively extract complex temporal components, achieving state-of-the-art long-term forecasting accuracy \[[2106.13008](http://arxiv.org/abs/2106.13008)\]. Similarly, Non-stationary Transformers address the challenge of distributional shifts by combining series stationarization with de-stationary attention mechanisms, significantly improving performance on non-stationary financial data \[[2205.14415](http://arxiv.org/abs/2205.14415)\]. The iTransformer architecture proposes an inverted attention mechanism focusing on variate-centric representations, enabling better utilization of multivariate time series and look-back windows \[[2310.06625](http://arxiv.org/abs/2310.06625)\]. Despite these advances, recent studies have questioned the efficacy of Transformers for long-term time series forecasting, showing that simple linear models can outperform sophisticated Transformer variants, highlighting the need for careful architectural considerations \[[2205.13504](http://arxiv.org/abs/2205.13504)\].

Beyond architectural designs, uncertainty quantification and calibration remain critical for reliable financial forecasting. Traditional Bayesian and Monte Carlo dropout methods offer uncertainty estimates but can be computationally burdensome and model-specific \[[1704.04110](http://arxiv.org/abs/1704.04110)\]\[[1711.11053](http://arxiv.org/abs/1711.11053)\]. Conformal prediction techniques have emerged as model-agnostic alternatives providing valid uncertainty quantification for time series forecasts, which is essential for risk-aware fund allocation \[[2406.16964](http://arxiv.org/abs/2406.16964)\]. The proposed framework’s adaptive uncertainty calibration aligns with this direction by allowing flexible integration with diverse forecasting models.

Foundation models and large pretrained transformers have been explored for general time series analysis and financial forecasting. Chronos tokenizes time series data into discrete vocabularies and leverages pretrained language models to improve zero-shot and transfer learning performance across diverse datasets \[[2403.07815](http://arxiv.org/abs/2403.07815)\]. Similarly, frameworks like Time-LLM and Frozen Pretrained Transformer (FPT) repurpose large language and vision models for time series tasks, demonstrating competitive or superior performance without modifying core transformer components \[[2310.01728](http://arxiv.org/abs/2310.01728)\]\[[2302.11939](http://arxiv.org/abs/2302.11939)\]. However, recent ablation studies caution that pretrained large language models (LLMs) may not inherently capture temporal dependencies better than simpler architectures for forecasting tasks, suggesting that their direct application to financial time series requires further investigation \[[2406.16964](http://arxiv.org/abs/2406.16964)\]. In finance specifically, LLMs have shown promise in explainable forecasting by integrating multimodal information such as price data and news, demonstrating improved reasoning and interpretability over classic econometric models \[[2306.11025](http://arxiv.org/abs/2306.11025)\].

Hybrid approaches that combine forecasting and optimization in an end-to-end manner have been gaining attention to overcome the limitations of decoupled PnO methods. Temporal Fusion Transformers (TFT) enable interpretable multi-horizon forecasting by combining recurrent and attention mechanisms with feature selection and gating layers, facilitating joint modeling of static and dynamic covariates \[[1912.09363](http://arxiv.org/abs/1912.09363)\]. Other methods integrate forecasting uncertainty directly into allocation decisions through unified loss functions or objective alignment, improving risk-aware decision-making \[[2211.14730](http://arxiv.org/abs/2211.14730)\]\[[2106.13008](http://arxiv.org/abs/2106.13008)\]\[[1704.04110](http://arxiv.org/abs/1704.04110)\]. However, many of these approaches assume specific forecasting models, limiting their adaptability across varied financial datasets \[[1703.07015](http://arxiv.org/abs/1703.07015)\]. The proposed RTS-PnO framework advances this field by supporting agnosticism with respect to forecasting models, adaptive uncertainty calibration, and objective alignment for end-to-end training, addressing both goal mismatch and uncertainty challenges inherent to financial fund allocation.

Finally, recent advances in multiscale temporal modeling, such as TimeMixer, exploit decomposable multiscale mixing to disentangle microscopic and macroscopic temporal variations, enhancing both short- and long-term forecasting accuracy with efficiency suitable for financial time series \[[2405.14616](http://arxiv.org/abs/2405.14616)\]. Spatio-temporal graph convolutional networks (STGCN) have also been employed to capture spatial and temporal dependencies, particularly in traffic-related financial time series, illustrating the benefit of domain-specific structural modeling \[[1709.04875](http://arxiv.org/abs/1709.04875)\].

In summary, the literature highlights the evolution from classical forecasting methods to advanced deep learning and transformer-based models, alongside growing attention to uncertainty modeling and end-to-end integration of forecasting and optimization. The proposed RTS-PnO framework contributes by combining these strengths into a risk-aware, model-agnostic predict-and-allocate approach particularly suited for complex financial time series.
