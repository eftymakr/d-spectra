## Related Works

Active learning (AL) techniques aim to reduce annotation costs by selectively querying the most informative unlabeled samples for labeling, thereby improving learning efficiency \[[1912.04977](http://arxiv.org/abs/1912.04977)\]. However, traditional AL methods often struggle with limited data diversity and annotation budgets, especially when applied in distributed settings where data is decentralized and privacy concerns prevent data sharing \[[1806.00582](http://arxiv.org/abs/1806.00582)\]. Federated Active Learning (FAL) extends AL to such distributed environments by enabling multiple clients to collaboratively select data and train models without sharing raw data, thus preserving privacy \[[1812.06127](http://arxiv.org/abs/1812.06127)\]. Despite these advances, current FAL methods typically overlook the heterogeneity of data distributions across clients and the resulting fluctuations in local and global model parameters, which can significantly degrade model accuracy and convergence \[[1804.08333](http://arxiv.org/abs/1804.08333)\].

The challenge of statistical heterogeneity (non-IID data) in federated learning (FL) is well-documented. Non-IID data negatively impacts model performance due to divergence in client updates and weight parameters \[[1806.00582](http://arxiv.org/abs/1806.00582)\]\[[1812.06127](http://arxiv.org/abs/1812.06127)\]. To mitigate this, approaches such as FedProx introduce proximal terms to stabilize convergence under heterogeneous data and variable client workloads, achieving more robust performance than FedAvg \[[1812.06127](http://arxiv.org/abs/1812.06127)\]. Similarly, client selection strategies that account for resource variability and communication constraints, such as FedCS, have been proposed to improve training efficiency in networks with heterogeneous client capabilities \[[1804.08333](http://arxiv.org/abs/1804.08333)\]. Adaptive client sampling methods further tackle both system and statistical heterogeneity by optimizing sampling probabilities to minimize convergence time \[[2112.11256](http://arxiv.org/abs/2112.11256)\]. These approaches highlight the importance of managing client heterogeneity to enhance federated optimization.

Addressing heterogeneity through model personalization and clustering has also been explored. Clustered Federated Learning (CFL) groups clients with similar data distributions to jointly train specialized models, outperforming standard FL in non-IID scenarios without modifying the FL protocol \[[1910.01991](http://arxiv.org/abs/1910.01991)\]\[[2006.04088](http://arxiv.org/abs/2006.04088)\]. Personalized FL methods based on meta-learning frameworks like MAML focus on learning a shared initialization that clients can quickly adapt to their local data, thereby enhancing accuracy on heterogeneous datasets \[[2002.07948](http://arxiv.org/abs/2002.07948)\]. Another personalization strategy involves learning a shared representation with client-specific classification heads, effectively separating shared and local components of the model and improving convergence under label distribution skew \[[2102.07078](http://arxiv.org/abs/2102.07078)\]. These methods demonstrate that personalization and representation learning can mitigate the adverse effects of heterogeneity in FL, although they generally do not incorporate active learning mechanisms for data selection.

On the active learning front within FL, recent works have studied the impact of non-IID label distributions on classification layers, noting that standard softmax classifiers suffer due to missing classes on clients \[[https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/e2232e7d10cabb0a767edb46ce3c8f1d9f7e5fc0](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/e2232e7d10cabb0a767edb46ce3c8f1d9f7e5fc0)\]\[[2106.05001](http://arxiv.org/abs/2106.05001)\]. Techniques such as restricted softmax \[[https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/e2232e7d10cabb0a767edb46ce3c8f1d9f7e5fc0](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/e2232e7d10cabb0a767edb46ce3c8f1d9f7e5fc0)\] and classifier calibration using virtual representations \[[2106.05001](http://arxiv.org/abs/2106.05001)\] have been proposed to improve local model accuracy and global aggregation under label skew. Meanwhile, federated semi-supervised learning methods incorporate unlabeled data by enforcing consistency across clients and decomposing parameters for labeled and unlabeled subsets, improving performance when labels are scarce \[[2006.12097](http://arxiv.org/abs/2006.12097)\].

Communication efficiency remains a critical concern in FL and FAL. Methods such as structured and sketched updates reduce uplink communication by compressing client updates \[[1610.05492](http://arxiv.org/abs/1610.05492)\]\[[1602.05629](http://arxiv.org/abs/1602.05629)\]. Adaptive federated optimizers that leverage techniques like Adam and Adagrad have also been shown to improve convergence speed and stability in heterogeneous settings \[[2003.00295](http://arxiv.org/abs/2003.00295)\]. Furthermore, benchmarks like LEAF provide standardized datasets and evaluation protocols to rigorously assess FL algorithms under realistic heterogeneity and scale \[[1812.01097](http://arxiv.org/abs/1812.01097)\].

While multiple FL methods address heterogeneity, personalization, and communication efficiency, integration of these with active learning for data selection is limited. Existing FAL methods often fail to explicitly account for the fluctuations in model uncertainty caused by client heterogeneity, which is critical for selecting informative samples. The proposed CHASe method fills this gap by focusing on epistemic variation—tracking inference inconsistencies across training epochs to identify highly uncertain samples near decision boundaries—and by calibrating classifiers to improve decision boundary alignment in heterogeneous federations. Additionally, CHASe enhances selection efficiency through a freeze-and-awaken sampling mechanism, addressing both effectiveness and communication constraints in FAL.

In summary, federated learning research has extensively explored handling non-IID data through proximal optimization \[[1812.06127](http://arxiv.org/abs/1812.06127)\], client selection \[[1804.08333](http://arxiv.org/abs/1804.08333)\]\[[2112.11256](http://arxiv.org/abs/2112.11256)\], clustering \[[1910.01991](http://arxiv.org/abs/1910.01991)\]\[[2006.04088](http://arxiv.org/abs/2006.04088)\], personalization \[[2002.07948](http://arxiv.org/abs/2002.07948)\]\[[2102.07078](http://arxiv.org/abs/2102.07078)\], and classifier calibration \[[https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/e2232e7d10cabb0a767edb46ce3c8f1d9f7e5fc0](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/e2232e7d10cabb0a767edb46ce3c8f1d9f7e5fc0)\]\[[2106.05001](http://arxiv.org/abs/2106.05001)\]. Active learning within FL remains nascent, with existing works addressing label skew and semi-supervised learning \[[https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/e2232e7d10cabb0a767edb46ce3c8f1d9f7e5fc0](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/e2232e7d10cabb0a767edb46ce3c8f1d9f7e5fc0)\]\[[2006.12097](http://arxiv.org/abs/2006.12097)\]\[[2106.05001](http://arxiv.org/abs/2106.05001)\] but lacking strategies to robustly select informative samples under heterogeneity. CHASe advances this space by integrating heterogeneity-aware uncertainty estimation and calibration for effective federated active learning.
