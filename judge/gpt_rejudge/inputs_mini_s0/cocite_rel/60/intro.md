## Related Works

Model calibration is crucial for ensuring that predicted probabilities or scores accurately reflect true correctness likelihoods, particularly in ranking models where relative orderings often dominate focus but absolute value accuracy is also essential. Early calibration methods commonly applied post-hoc transformation functions such as Platt scaling and temperature scaling, which adjust model outputs to better align predicted confidence with empirical accuracy. However, these methods typically rely on simple parametric forms and may lack sufficient flexibility to capture complex calibration mappings, especially in multi-class or ranking scenarios \[[1706.04599](http://arxiv.org/abs/1706.04599)\]\[[1910.12656](http://arxiv.org/abs/1910.12656)\]\[[2002.09437](http://arxiv.org/abs/2002.09437)\].

Traditional post-processing calibrators, including piece-wise linear or scalar temperature transformations, preserve the order of predictions but are constrained in expressiveness, limiting their ability to handle non-linear or dataset-specific calibration needs. This limitation is significant for ranking tasks that require both order preservation and precise probability calibration \[[1706.04599](http://arxiv.org/abs/1706.04599)\]\[[2003.06820](http://arxiv.org/abs/2003.06820)\]. More expressive calibration functions have been explored, but often at the cost of increased complexity or loss of order-preserving guarantees.

Neural network-based calibration methods offer greater representational power by learning complex, non-linear mappings from uncalibrated to calibrated outputs. Among these, monotonic neural networks have emerged as a promising approach for calibration because they enforce monotonicity, ensuring that the relative order of predictions is preserved while allowing flexible transformation functions. The Unconstrained Monotonic Neural Network (UMNN) architecture, in particular, models monotone functions by constraining only the positivity of the derivative, thus enabling arbitrary monotonic mappings with high modeling capacity. This approach overcomes the expressiveness limitations of previous order-preserving calibrators and has demonstrated effectiveness in complex calibration tasks \[[1908.05164](http://arxiv.org/abs/1908.05164)\].

Beyond monotonic neural networks, other advanced calibration techniques include Dirichlet calibration, which generalizes binary beta calibration to multi-class settings via a learnable transformation based on Dirichlet distributions, improving class-wise and overall expected calibration error metrics \[[1910.12656](http://arxiv.org/abs/1910.12656)\]. Additionally, intra order-preserving neural calibration functions have been proposed, which aim to preserve the top-k prediction ordering while offering flexibility in the calibration map. These methods have shown improved performance over classical temperature scaling and Dirichlet calibration, indicating the benefits of more expressive, learned calibrators in preserving ranking structure alongside calibration \[[2003.06820](http://arxiv.org/abs/2003.06820)\].

Bayesian deep learning methods have also been applied to uncertainty estimation and calibration, modeling epistemic and aleatoric uncertainties explicitly. While effective for providing uncertainty estimates, Bayesian neural networks typically incur higher computational costs and require modifications to standard training pipelines, limiting their scalability for large-scale ranking systems \[[1612.01474](http://arxiv.org/abs/1612.01474)\]\[[1902.02476](http://arxiv.org/abs/1902.02476)\]\[[1703.04977](http://arxiv.org/abs/1703.04977)\]. Alternative ensemble-based approaches, such as deep ensembles and stochastic weight averaging Gaussian (SWAG), provide scalable uncertainty estimation and improve calibration by approximating Bayesian posterior distributions without extensive overhead \[[1612.01474](http://arxiv.org/abs/1612.01474)\]\[[1902.02476](http://arxiv.org/abs/1902.02476)\]. However, these methods focus more on uncertainty quantification than explicit calibration of ranking predictions.

Data augmentation techniques like mixup training have been shown to improve calibration and predictive uncertainty by smoothing label distributions during training, reducing overconfidence in neural networks and enhancing robustness to out-of-distribution data. Mixup-trained models yield better-calibrated softmax outputs and lower miscalibration compared to standard training, highlighting the impact of training strategies on calibration quality \[[1905.11001](http://arxiv.org/abs/1905.11001)\].

Finally, recent work emphasizes the challenges of evaluating calibration under dataset shift and the limitations of popular calibration metrics such as expected calibration error (ECE). Adaptive binning, class-conditional calibration measures, and improved estimators from meteorology have been proposed to more accurately assess calibration, particularly in large-scale image classification tasks \[[1906.02530](http://arxiv.org/abs/1906.02530)\]\[[1904.01685](http://arxiv.org/abs/1904.01685)\]. Novel loss functions, like the Smooth Calibration Loss (SCLoss), have been introduced to directly optimize calibration objectives and encourage smoothness in calibration maps, further enhancing calibration performance when combined with flexible calibrators \[[1909.10155](http://arxiv.org/abs/1909.10155)\]\[[2002.09437](http://arxiv.org/abs/2002.09437)\].

In summary, while classical calibration methods provide baseline solutions, their limited expressiveness and fixed functional forms restrict their effectiveness in complex ranking calibration scenarios. Neural network-based monotonic calibrators, especially UMNNs, offer a compelling balance of flexibility and order preservation, enabling improved calibration performance. Coupled with advanced evaluation techniques and novel loss functions, these approaches represent the state-of-the-art in calibration for ranking models.
