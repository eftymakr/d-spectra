## Related Works

The development of effective DNA sequence representation learning has been strongly influenced by advances in unsupervised and self-supervised pre-training paradigms. Early works in unsupervised representation learning for high-dimensional data, including images, speech, and text, laid foundational concepts for contrastive learning techniques that have since been adapted for genomic data \[[1807.03748](http://arxiv.org/abs/1807.03748)\]\[[1905.09272](http://arxiv.org/abs/1905.09272)\]\[[2002.05709](http://arxiv.org/abs/2002.05709)\]\[[1911.05722](http://arxiv.org/abs/1911.05722)\]. Contrastive Predictive Coding (CPC) introduced latent space prediction using autoregressive models coupled with contrastive loss, enabling the capture of useful features without supervision \[[1807.03748](http://arxiv.org/abs/1807.03748)\]\[[1905.09272](http://arxiv.org/abs/1905.09272)\]. Similarly, instance discrimination methods that treat each data instance as its own class have demonstrated strong performance on visual tasks, emphasizing the power of discriminative unsupervised learning \[[1805.01978](http://arxiv.org/abs/1805.01978)\].

However, these unsupervised approaches, when applied directly to DNA sequences, face intrinsic limitations. DNA sequences alone provide incomplete information since biological functions are heavily influenced by regulatory genomic profiles such as chromatin accessibility, histone modifications, and transcription factor binding \[[https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/4f7d1e04c9d30ce12df3e085d10d70997e6ea1a5](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/4f7d1e04c9d30ce12df3e085d10d70997e6ea1a5)\]\[[https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/9d7af049cf76892dc1b5126c5a51cd09e08e3b5d](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/9d7af049cf76892dc1b5126c5a51cd09e08e3b5d)\]\[[1609.08663](http://arxiv.org/abs/1609.08663)\]. This has prompted a shift towards supervised training paradigms that incorporate genomic profile data, which provide richer contextual signals for learning more biologically meaningful representations \[[2205.13769](http://arxiv.org/abs/2205.13769)\]\[[1609.08663](http://arxiv.org/abs/1609.08663)\].

Supervised genomic profile prediction has shown clear advantages. By training models explicitly on multi-dimensional genomic data, these approaches better capture regulatory mechanisms and functional annotations, thereby improving downstream prediction tasks such as gene expression and variant effect estimation \[[2205.13769](http://arxiv.org/abs/2205.13769)\]. This aligns with findings that supervised pre-training with meaningful biological labels outperforms purely sequence-based unsupervised pre-training, which may neglect crucial epigenetic and regulatory information \[[https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/4f7d1e04c9d30ce12df3e085d10d70997e6ea1a5](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/4f7d1e04c9d30ce12df3e085d10d70997e6ea1a5)\]\[[2205.13769](http://arxiv.org/abs/2205.13769)\].

Multi-species and multi-profile scenarios introduce further complexity, as DNA regulatory landscapes vary across species and genomic contexts. To address this, recent works in other domains have leveraged Mixture of Experts (MoE) architectures, which dynamically route inputs to specialized subnetworks or experts, enabling models to capture heterogeneous patterns more effectively \[[2006.10029](http://arxiv.org/abs/2006.10029)\]\[[2005.04966](http://arxiv.org/abs/2005.04966)\]\[[2004.11362](http://arxiv.org/abs/2004.11362)\]. While MoE has been successfully applied in vision and language tasks to model multi-modal data and improve scalability, its application in genomics remains underexplored. Integrating MoE with supervised genomic profile prediction offers a promising avenue to model the relationships between species-specific sequences and diverse genomic profiles, facilitating richer DNA representations \[[2005.04966](http://arxiv.org/abs/2005.04966)\]\[[2004.11362](http://arxiv.org/abs/2004.11362)\].

Previous attempts combining supervised and self-supervised learning for genomic data have not fully exploited the multi-species and multi-profile nature essential for generalizable DNA representations \[[2205.13769](http://arxiv.org/abs/2205.13769)\]\[[1711.05415](http://arxiv.org/abs/1711.05415)\]. Furthermore, existing pretraining methods often rely on pure sequence data or single-task objectives, limiting their applicability across diverse genomic contexts \[[2502.03499](http://arxiv.org/abs/2502.03499)\]. The proposed SPACE framework addresses these gaps by leveraging MoE within a supervised genomic profile prediction setting, enabling adaptive expert collaboration across species and profiles to learn more effective and generalizable DNA representations.

In the broader context of representation learning, contrastive and supervised contrastive losses have been shown to improve clustering of semantically related samples and robustness to data variations, which are critical for genomic data characterized by high noise and biological variability \[[2002.05709](http://arxiv.org/abs/2002.05709)\]\[[2004.11362](http://arxiv.org/abs/2004.11362)\]. Advances in dense contrastive learning for pixel-level tasks also highlight the importance of learning spatially sensitive features, an insight that parallels the need to capture local genomic regulatory elements beyond global sequence features \[[2011.09157](http://arxiv.org/abs/2011.09157)\]\[[2205.13769](http://arxiv.org/abs/2205.13769)\].

Finally, the success of large-scale pretraining with architecture innovations such as residual networks and deep transformers underscores the importance of model capacity and design for genomic representation learning \[[1512.03385](http://arxiv.org/abs/1512.03385)\]\[[2502.03499](http://arxiv.org/abs/2502.03499)\]. Transformer-based genomic models pretrained on sequence data have achieved state-of-the-art performance on numerous tasks but still face challenges in incorporating multi-modal and multi-species information effectively, which the MoE-based supervised framework aims to overcome \[[2502.03499](http://arxiv.org/abs/2502.03499)\].

In summary, prior work has established the utility of both unsupervised contrastive learning and supervised genomic profile prediction for DNA representation. However, integrating multi-species genomic contexts with multi-profile data through adaptive architectures like MoE remains a novel and promising direction, as embodied by the SPACE framework.
