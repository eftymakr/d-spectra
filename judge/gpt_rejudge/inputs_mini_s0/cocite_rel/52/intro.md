## Related Works

Dense retrievers have emerged as a pivotal approach in information retrieval, leveraging pre-trained language models (PLMs) such as BERT and LLaMA to encode queries and documents into dense vectors for efficient semantic matching \[[1810.04805](http://arxiv.org/abs/1810.04805)\]. Typically, these models employ contrastive learning during fine-tuning to optimize the encoder for retrieval tasks, with shallow similarity functions like inner product used for ranking. The foundational BERT model introduced deep bidirectional Transformer representations, enabling strong performance across diverse NLP tasks, including retrieval-related tasks \[[1810.04805](http://arxiv.org/abs/1810.04805)\]. Subsequent work such as RoBERTa demonstrated that extended pre-training and careful hyperparameter tuning yield significant improvements, highlighting the crucial role of pre-training quality \[[1907.11692](http://arxiv.org/abs/1907.11692)\].

A growing body of research has investigated the relative contributions of pre-training versus fine-tuning in dense retrieval. Initial findings using the Dense Passage Retriever (DPR) framework with BERT-based encoders suggested that retrieval knowledge is primarily acquired during pre-training, with fine-tuning mainly adjusting activation patterns rather than fundamentally reorganizing model knowledge \[[2104.08253](http://arxiv.org/abs/2104.08253)\]\[[1907.11692](http://arxiv.org/abs/1907.11692)\]. This conclusion motivated further exploration of alternative architectures and representation strategies to assess its generality.

To this end, recent studies have compared the impact of different pooling strategies—such as using the [CLS] token versus mean pooling—and backbone architectures, including encoder-only models like BERT and decoder-only models like LLaMA \[[2002.08909](http://arxiv.org/abs/2002.08909)\]. These analyses reveal that while DPR-style fine-tuning on BERT confirms the dominance of pre-trained knowledge, models like Contriever, which employs mean pooling, and decoder-based LLaMA models exhibit more substantial fine-tuning effects, suggesting that fine-tuning can lead to meaningful knowledge reorganization in certain settings \[[2002.03932](http://arxiv.org/abs/2002.03932)\]. This nuanced view underscores that the interplay between pre-training and fine-tuning is model- and architecture-dependent.

The importance of specialized pre-training objectives tailored for retrieval has been emphasized in multiple works. Condenser introduced a novel architecture pre-trained to condition language model predictions on dense representations, significantly improving dense retrieval effectiveness by better aggregating information into embeddings \[[2104.08253](http://arxiv.org/abs/2104.08253)\]. Building on this, coCondenser further enhanced pre-training by incorporating corpus-level contrastive losses, addressing dense retrievers’ sensitivity to noisy data and batch size constraints, and matching state-of-the-art performance without heavy engineering \[[2108.05540](http://arxiv.org/abs/2108.05540)\]. Similarly, RetroMAE proposed a retrieval-oriented masked autoencoder pre-training strategy with asymmetric encoder-decoder masking, achieving competitive improvements across dense retrieval benchmarks \[[2205.12035](http://arxiv.org/abs/2205.12035)\].

Contrastive learning has been widely adopted to improve text and sentence embeddings for retrieval. SimCSE demonstrated that simple contrastive objectives with dropout-based augmentation can yield high-quality sentence embeddings in both unsupervised and supervised settings \[[2104.08821](http://arxiv.org/abs/2104.08821)\]. CLEAR extended contrastive learning to sentence representations with multiple augmentation strategies, further boosting robustness and downstream retrieval performance \[[2012.15466](http://arxiv.org/abs/2012.15466)\]. Unsupervised contrastive pre-training methods have also shown promise in enabling dense retrievers to perform well in zero-shot or low-resource conditions, outperforming traditional sparse methods like BM25 on diverse datasets \[[2112.09118](http://arxiv.org/abs/2112.09118)\].

Fine-tuning strategies remain critical for dense retrieval efficacy. RocketQA introduced optimized training with cross-batch negatives, denoised hard negatives, and data augmentation, resulting in state-of-the-art retrieval accuracy on MSMARCO and Natural Questions \[[2010.08191](http://arxiv.org/abs/2010.08191)\]. Other works have highlighted the importance of hard negative mining and dynamic negative sampling to stabilize training and improve ranking quality \[[2104.08051](http://arxiv.org/abs/2104.08051)\]. Moreover, TAS-Balanced demonstrated that efficient, topic-aware sampling and dual-teacher supervision can train dense retrievers effectively on limited hardware resources while outperforming traditional baselines \[[2104.06967](http://arxiv.org/abs/2104.06967)\].

Pre-training approaches that infuse external knowledge or leverage modular architectures have also been explored. K-Adapter proposes injecting factual and linguistic knowledge into fixed pre-trained backbones via adapters, enabling continual knowledge infusion without catastrophic forgetting and improving knowledge-driven retrieval tasks \[[2002.01808](http://arxiv.org/abs/2002.01808)\]. REALM introduces retrieval-augmented language model pre-training by integrating a latent knowledge retriever during training, which allows explicit document retrieval and substantially improves open-domain question answering performance \[[2002.08909](http://arxiv.org/abs/2002.08909)\].

Finally, the role of model architecture is increasingly recognized as pivotal. XLNet’s generalized autoregressive pretraining outperforms BERT on many language understanding tasks by modeling bidirectional context through permutation-based factorization \[[1906.08237](http://arxiv.org/abs/1906.08237)\]. The use of decoder-only models like LLaMA in retrieval scenarios opens new avenues, as their fine-tuning dynamics and knowledge acquisition differ from encoder-only architectures \[[2002.08909](http://arxiv.org/abs/2002.08909)\]\[[2002.03932](http://arxiv.org/abs/2002.03932)\]. Autoencoder-based models with weak decoders have been proposed to encourage encoders to learn better dense representations without decoder shortcuts, improving retrieval quality \[[https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/5be217c678916543884c354654263f27c0a6bd9f](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/5be217c678916543884c354654263f27c0a6bd9f)\]\[[2204.10641](http://arxiv.org/abs/2204.10641)\].

In summary, the literature highlights that while pre-training provides a strong foundation of retrieval knowledge in many dense retrievers, fine-tuning’s role varies significantly with architecture, pooling method, and pre-training objectives. This motivates comprehensive studies that extend beyond DPR and BERT to better understand dense retriever knowledge acquisition across diverse models and datasets.
