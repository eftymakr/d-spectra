## Related Works

Generative retrieval represents a significant shift from traditional retrieval paradigms by replacing explicit index structures with a single large-scale neural network that directly generates document identifiers (docids) for queries. This approach, pioneered by the Differentiable Search Index (DSI) framework, encodes the entire corpus within model parameters and performs retrieval via sequence-to-sequence generation of docids, thus unifying indexing and retrieval into an end-to-end differentiable process \[[2202.06991](http://arxiv.org/abs/2202.06991)\]\[[2206.10128](http://arxiv.org/abs/2206.10128)\]. DSI and its variants have demonstrated competitive or superior performance compared to strong baselines such as dual encoders and BM25, particularly on small to medium-sized corpora, and have shown promising generalization to zero-shot and cross-lingual retrieval tasks \[[2202.06991](http://arxiv.org/abs/2202.06991)\]\[[2206.10128](http://arxiv.org/abs/2206.10128)\].

Key challenges in generative retrieval include the design of document identifiers and the scalability of these models. Early generative retrieval models used fixed or heuristic docids such as document titles or clustered embeddings, which often failed to capture complete document semantics \[[2304.04171](http://arxiv.org/abs/2304.04171)\]. To address this, GenRet introduced an end-to-end learned tokenization scheme that produces semantically meaningful discrete docids via a discrete auto-encoder, improving retrieval performance and generalization to unseen documents \[[2304.04171](http://arxiv.org/abs/2304.04171)\]. Other works have proposed more flexible docid formulations, such as using all ngrams within passages as possible identifiers, enabling autoregressive models to generate and score these identifiers for retrieval, which improves passage-level retrieval and reduces memory footprint \[[2204.10628](http://arxiv.org/abs/2204.10628)\]. More recently, RIPOR proposed a prefix-oriented ranking optimization and constructed docids based on query-document relevance associations, yielding significant gains on large-scale benchmarks like MS MARCO \[[2311.09134](http://arxiv.org/abs/2311.09134)\].

Scalability to millions of passages remains a critical open challenge. While generative retrieval shows strong performance on corpora of up to 100k documents, scaling to larger corpora (e.g., millions of passages in MS MARCO) reveals limitations in naive parameter scaling and architectural modifications, as well as the importance of synthetic queries for indexing \[[2305.11841](http://arxiv.org/abs/2305.11841)\]. These findings underscore the need for refined training and indexing strategies to maintain efficacy at scale.

Another practical challenge is continual learning over dynamic corpora, where new documents are incrementally added. CLEVER introduced methods to incrementally update docid quantization and incorporate memory-augmented learning to prevent forgetting previously learned documents, enabling efficient and effective generative retrieval in evolving datasets \[[2308.14968](http://arxiv.org/abs/2308.14968)\]. This addresses a critical limitation of earlier generative retrieval models that assumed static corpora.

The decoding process, particularly beam search, is central to generative retrieval. Beam search is widely used to generate docids autoregressively under corpus-specific constraints, but it introduces biases and errors due to its reliance on marginal distributions at each step rather than joint distributions over sequences. Recent theoretical analyses have derived lower bounds on retrieval errors in terms of KL divergence between ground-truth and model-predicted step-wise marginals \[[2202.06991](http://arxiv.org/abs/2202.06991)\]. These studies reveal fundamental limitations of constrained autoregressive decoding and motivate research into alternative decoding and optimization strategies. Related work in sequence generation tasks has proposed novel beam search variants and joint modeling schemes to mitigate decoding inefficiencies and improve output quality \[[2308.14968](http://arxiv.org/abs/2308.14968)\]\[[2311.09134](http://arxiv.org/abs/2311.09134)\].

Complementary to generative retrieval, dense retrieval methods remain a strong baseline, leveraging learned dense embeddings and approximate nearest neighbor search. Techniques like ANCE address the training gap between negative samples and test distributions, significantly improving dense retrieval effectiveness with efficient training \[[2007.00808](http://arxiv.org/abs/2007.00808)\]\[[2004.04906](http://arxiv.org/abs/2004.04906)\]\[[2108.0554](http://arxiv.org/abs/2108.05540)\]. Retrieval-Augmented Generation (RAG) models combine parametric sequence generation with non-parametric dense retrieval to enhance factuality and specificity in knowledge-intensive NLP tasks, illustrating a hybrid approach bridging generative and traditional retrieval mechanisms \[[2005.11401](http://arxiv.org/abs/2005.11401)\].

Model-based retrieval approaches extend generative retrieval by incorporating two-stage architectures, tokenized URL docids, and training strategies to scale to larger corpora while mitigating training-inference mismatches. For example, TOME uses a two-stage generation process and tokenized URLs as identifiers to improve retrieval effectiveness on MS MARCO and Natural Questions \[[2305.11161](http://arxiv.org/abs/2305.11161)\]. Similarly, Neural Corpus Indexer (NCI) integrates prefix-aware decoding and query generation to optimize recall in an end-to-end manner \[[2206.02743](http://arxiv.org/abs/2206.02743)\].

Entity-centric retrieval models have leveraged autoregressive generation of unique entity names for retrieval, addressing limitations in memory footprint and negative sampling faced by dense entity retrieval methods. GENRE exemplifies this by generating entity identifiers token-by-token conditioned on context, enabling efficient and accurate retrieval across numerous knowledge-intensive tasks \[[2010.00904](http://arxiv.org/abs/2010.00904)\].

In summary, generative retrieval research has advanced rapidly, addressing key issues in docid design, scalability, continual learning, and decoding strategies. While it offers a promising paradigm that unifies indexing and retrieval, ongoing challenges remain in scaling to large dynamic corpora and overcoming inherent limitations of autoregressive decoding. Complementary advances in dense retrieval and hybrid retrieval-generation models continue to shape the broader landscape of information retrieval research \[[2202.06991](http://arxiv.org/abs/2202.06991)\]\[[2204.10628](http://arxiv.org/abs/2204.10628)\]\[[2305.11841](http://arxiv.org/abs/2305.11841)\]\[[2304.04171](http://arxiv.org/abs/2304.04171)\]\[[2308.14968](http://arxiv.org/abs/2308.14968)\]\[[2311.09134](http://arxiv.org/abs/2311.09134)\]\[[2206.02743](http://arxiv.org/abs/2206.02743)\]\[[2007.00808](http://arxiv.org/abs/2007.00808)\]\[[2305.11161](http://arxiv.org/abs/2305.11161)\]\[[2010.00904](http://arxiv.org/abs/2010.00904)\]\[[2004.04906](http://arxiv.org/abs/2004.04906)\]\[[2108.0554](http://arxiv.org/abs/2108.05540)\]\[[2206.10128](http://arxiv.org/abs/2206.10128)\]\[[2005.11401](http://arxiv.org/abs/2005.11401)\].
