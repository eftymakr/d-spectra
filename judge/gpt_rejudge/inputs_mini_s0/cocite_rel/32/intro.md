## Related Works

Understanding the evolution of feature representations across layers in large language models (LLMs) has become a crucial area for enhancing interpretability and robustness. Traditional analyses largely focus on data-dependent methods applied to fine-tuned models, which restricts insights to post-hoc evaluations and limit generalizability across tasks \[[2109.01652](http://arxiv.org/abs/2109.01652)\]. Recent efforts have shifted toward data-oblivious techniques that analyze representation dynamics in pretrained, unfine-tuned LLMs to identify intrinsic critical layers. Centered Kernel Alignment (CKA) has emerged as a powerful tool for this purpose, enabling quantification of representation similarity across layers and training stages without reliance on labeled data \[[2106.10199](http://arxiv.org/abs/2106.10199)\]\[[1902.00751](http://arxiv.org/abs/1902.00751)\].

The identification of critical layers—those layers that exhibit the most substantial representational changes during fine-tuning—has been linked to model sensitivity and downstream performance. Empirical studies demonstrate that fine-tuning predominantly affects a subset of layers, suggesting a hierarchical or modular internal structure within LLMs \[[2406.11191](http://arxiv.org/abs/2406.11191)\]\[[2402.17193](http://arxiv.org/abs/2402.17193)\]. This observation aligns with parameter-efficient fine-tuning methods such as LoRA and adapter modules, which focus updates on specific components or layers to achieve effective adaptation with fewer trainable parameters \[[2106.09685](http://arxiv.org/abs/2106.09685)\]\[[1902.00751](http://arxiv.org/abs/1902.00751)\]. These methods implicitly leverage the notion that some layers are more critical for task-specific knowledge integration, supporting the idea that targeting critical layers yields more efficient domain adaptation \[[2101.0019](http://arxiv.org/abs/2101.00190)\]\[[2110.04366](http://arxiv.org/abs/2110.04366)\].

Spectral analysis of representation shifts further reveals that changes in top principal components often encode meaningful semantic transitions, such as moving from rationale generation to conclusion formulation. Prior research in intrinsic dimensionality and low-rank adaptation has shown that pretrained models reside in a low intrinsic dimension, enabling fine-tuning to operate effectively in a compressed parameter space and that principal components capture key semantic structures \[[2012.13255](http://arxiv.org/abs/2012.13255)\]\[[2106.09685](http://arxiv.org/abs/2106.09685)\]. This understanding informs strategies for both effective fine-tuning and model robustness, including defenses against adversarial or backdoor attacks. For example, freezing critical layers during defensive training reduces attack success rates, corroborating earlier findings on the utility of layer freezing as a mitigation approach \[[2110.04366](http://arxiv.org/abs/2110.04366)\]\[[2403.13372](http://arxiv.org/abs/2403.13372)\].

The broader landscape of fine-tuning explores a variety of methods balancing full-model tuning and parameter-efficient tuning (PET), including prefix-tuning, BitFit, and Child-Tuning, which selectively update subsets of parameters or introduce small auxiliary modules to achieve effective adaptation with constrained computational resources \[[2109.05687](http://arxiv.org/abs/2109.05687)\]\[[2106.10199](http://arxiv.org/abs/2106.10199)\]\[[2101.0019](http://arxiv.org/abs/2101.00190)\]. These techniques highlight the importance of fine-tuning granularity and parameter allocation, which directly relate to the concept of critical layers exhibiting pronounced representational shifts.

In addition to representation analysis, preference learning and alignment methods have become increasingly important for controlling LLM behavior post-pretraining. Approaches like Direct Preference Optimization (DPO) simplify alignment by integrating reward modeling directly into fine-tuning, enhancing stability and performance without complex reinforcement learning pipelines \[[2305.1829](http://arxiv.org/abs/2305.18290)\]\[[2409.02795](http://arxiv.org/abs/2409.02795)\]. This body of work complements studies on representation dynamics by emphasizing how fine-tuning layers sensitive to human preference signals can improve model behavior effectively.

Large-scale instruction-tuning efforts, such as FLAN and OPT-IML, demonstrate that fine-tuning on diverse, instruction-based datasets substantially improves zero-shot generalization and robustness across tasks, reinforcing the importance of task-agnostic yet impactful fine-tuning strategies that may implicitly target critical layers \[[2109.01652](http://arxiv.org/abs/2109.01652)\]\[[2212.12017](http://arxiv.org/abs/2212.12017)\]. Moreover, scaling laws governing fine-tuning reveal that model size and fine-tuning data interplay with the number of updated parameters, affecting performance gains and optimal tuning strategies \[[2402.17193](http://arxiv.org/abs/2402.17193)\].

Finally, unified frameworks like LlamaFactory aim to simplify and standardize efficient fine-tuning methods across hundreds of LLM architectures, enabling systematic exploration of critical layer behavior and parameter-efficient adaptation techniques at scale \[[2403.13372](http://arxiv.org/abs/2403.13372)\]. These developments suggest a growing consensus that understanding and leveraging intrinsic critical layers through data-oblivious analysis methods, such as CKA and spectral decomposition, can drive both interpretability and practical improvements in LLM adaptation and security.
