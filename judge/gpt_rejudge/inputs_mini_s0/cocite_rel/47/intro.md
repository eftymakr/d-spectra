## Related Works

Multi-Domain Recommendation (MDR) aims to improve recommendation performance by leveraging knowledge across multiple domains, addressing the inherent data sparsity and cold-start issues in single-domain recommender systems \[[2103.01696](http://arxiv.org/abs/2103.01696)\]. Early cross-domain recommendation (CDR) methods primarily focused on transferring knowledge from a richer source domain to a sparser target domain, often assuming domain invariance or employing embedding and mapping techniques to align user or item representations between domains \[[1804.06769](http://arxiv.org/abs/1804.06769)\]\[[2203.16863](http://arxiv.org/abs/2203.16863)\]. However, these assumptions become limiting when domains differ significantly in user behavior or content, potentially causing negative transfer, where irrelevant or conflicting knowledge degrades performance \[[2211.11964](http://arxiv.org/abs/2211.11964)\]\[[https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/54dedd4d06f36a00cde7084c1cd1a92ad8cf9168](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/54dedd4d06f36a00cde7084c1cd1a92ad8cf9168)\].

To overcome these challenges, more advanced frameworks have been proposed. Dual-target CDR models simultaneously improve recommendations in both source and target domains by leveraging multi-source content and rating information with multi-task learning, thus enabling mutual knowledge transfer and better embedding sharing across domains \[[https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/2b031c7170c9dccbe506f3d3a1d6104f65e835ae](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/2b031c7170c9dccbe506f3d3a1d6104f65e835ae)\]\[[1910.05189](http://arxiv.org/abs/1910.05189)\]. Graph-based methods, such as Bi-directional Transfer Graph Collaborative Filtering (BiTGCF) and Preference Propagation GraphNet (PPGN), exploit high-order connectivity and user-item interactions within and across domains to facilitate effective knowledge transfer while capturing domain-specific and common features \[[https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/d5630291a4ba3f6266c77e6d00920502f986afa6](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/d5630291a4ba3f6266c77e6d00920502f986afa6)\]\[[https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/24a473700894dbfab0bfd9dc3d542acfeef66bca](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/24a473700894dbfab0bfd9dc3d542acfeef66bca)\]. These approaches emphasize modeling domain relationships more explicitly through unified graph structures or propagation mechanisms.

Regarding MDR, several methods have been developed to jointly train models across multiple domains, aiming to balance domain-shared and domain-specific knowledge. Probabilistic matrix factorization frameworks adaptively transfer knowledge by learning domain correlations and correcting domain biases \[[1203.3535](http://arxiv.org/abs/1203.3535)\]. Adversarial feature translation methods use generative adversarial networks to translate features between domains, capturing domain-specific and shared characteristics for better multi-domain representation learning \[[https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/7807d924fa46caec7b706b98156962caf8458d59](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/7807d924fa46caec7b706b98156962caf8458d59)\]. Hierarchical hypergraph networks model multi-domain user-item interactions as unified graphs with hyperedges to capture high-order cross-domain connections and eliminate domain discrepancies \[[2211.11191](http://arxiv.org/abs/2211.11191)\]. Additionally, model-agnostic frameworks like MAMDR address domain conflicts and overfitting by domain negotiation and regularization strategies, improving scalability and generalizability across thousands of domains \[[2202.12524](http://arxiv.org/abs/2202.12524)\].

The problem of negative transfer remains a critical focus in MDR research. Selective knowledge transfer mechanisms based on domain similarity or distance metrics have been proposed to mitigate this issue. For example, multi-target CDR methods like CAT-ART learn global and domain-specific user embeddings with attention mechanisms to transfer information adaptively across several domains, reducing negative transfer effects \[[2211.11964](http://arxiv.org/abs/2211.11964)\]. Similarly, graph-based multi-domain sequential recommendation models (e.g., MAGRec) learn domain-guided and general representations to alleviate negative transfer by capturing temporal intra- and inter-domain user interactions \[[2302.05990](http://arxiv.org/abs/2302.05990)\]. The proposed Similar Domain Selection Principle (SDSP) aligns with these efforts by dynamically measuring domain-level distances and selecting suitable domains for knowledge transfer, addressing the need for explicit domain gap modeling and domain-specific transfer in MDR.

Multi-task learning (MTL) techniques have also been extensively applied in MDR and CDR to jointly optimize recommendation tasks across domains while managing task conflicts. Progressive Layered Extraction (PLE) improves joint representation learning by progressively separating shared and task-specific components, reducing negative transfer between tasks \[[https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/0182197c3996d11867ef650b66a2ddf1efa6f631](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/0182197c3996d11867ef650b66a2ddf1efa6f631)\]. Multi-gate Mixture-of-Experts (MMoE) explicitly models task relationships and adapts expert contributions for each task, enhancing model trainability and performance in multi-task recommendation scenarios \[[https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/af8a8dcb74561d52d904f7bc4afcc747e079b702](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/af8a8dcb74561d52d904f7bc4afcc747e079b702)\]. These MTL approaches provide useful insights for MDR models aiming to handle diverse and potentially conflicting domain objectives.

Finally, shared-account cross-domain sequential recommendation addresses challenges where multiple users share a single account and interact across domains, requiring disentanglement of user behaviors and adaptive cross-domain feature transfer, as demonstrated by π-Net \[[https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/fa990aee9a8f157b5d393f5f3eaa014e1e5c67aa](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/fa990aee9a8f157b5d393f5f3eaa014e1e5c67aa)\]. This line of work highlights the complexity of user modeling in multi-domain settings and the importance of dynamic information filtering and transfer mechanisms.

In summary, existing MDR and CDR research has made significant progress in modeling domain relationships, mitigating negative transfer, and incorporating domain-specific features through various architectures including multi-task learning, graph neural networks, adversarial learning, and prototype-based or attention-driven transfer mechanisms. However, effectively and efficiently selecting suitable domains for knowledge transfer to avoid negative transfer, especially in large-scale and heterogeneous multi-domain environments, remains an open and active research challenge \[[2103.01696](http://arxiv.org/abs/2103.01696)\]\[[https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/2b031c7170c9dccbe506f3d3a1d6104f65e835ae](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/2b031c7170c9dccbe506f3d3a1d6104f65e835ae)\]\[[https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/d5630291a4ba3f6266c77e6d00920502f986afa6](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/d5630291a4ba3f6266c77e6d00920502f986afa6)\]\[[https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/24a473700894dbfab0bfd9dc3d542acfeef66bca](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/24a473700894dbfab0bfd9dc3d542acfeef66bca)\]\[[2211.11964](http://arxiv.org/abs/2211.11964)\]\[[2202.12524](http://arxiv.org/abs/2202.12524)\]\[[2211.11191](http://arxiv.org/abs/2211.11191)\]\[[2302.05990](http://arxiv.org/abs/2302.05990)\].
