## Related Works

Transformer-based pre-trained language models (PLMs) have revolutionized neural retrieval by providing rich contextualized representations that significantly improve multilingual and cross-lingual information retrieval (IR) tasks \[[2004.12832](http://arxiv.org/abs/2004.12832)\]. BERT and its variants like RoBERTa have been widely adopted for dense retrieval frameworks, leveraging their bidirectional language understanding capabilities to model fine-grained query-document relevance \[[1810.04805](http://arxiv.org/abs/1810.04805)\]\[[1905.09217](http://arxiv.org/abs/1905.09217)\]\[[1904.07094](http://arxiv.org/abs/1904.07094)\]. However, these models typically require large-scale annotated data, which is scarce for low-resource and morphologically rich languages such as Amharic, limiting their effectiveness in such settings \[[2503.18570](http://arxiv.org/abs/2503.18570)\].

Multilingual pretrained models such as multilingual BERT (mBERT) and XLM-RoBERTa (XLM-R) have been employed to bridge the gap for low-resource languages by enabling cross-lingual transfer and zero-shot retrieval capabilities \[[1911.02116](http://arxiv.org/abs/1911.02116)\]\[[2201.08471](http://arxiv.org/abs/2201.08471)\]\[[https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/4a78f23be51f314240d17fa0a340785ee88bf032](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/4a78f23be51f314240d17fa0a340785ee88bf032)\]. XLM-R, trained on over 100 languages, has demonstrated substantial improvements over mBERT on multiple cross-lingual benchmarks, particularly benefiting low-resource languages through large-scale multilingual pretraining \[[1911.02116](http://arxiv.org/abs/1911.02116)\]. Nevertheless, these models often suffer from imbalanced language representation during pretraining, which leads to performance degradation on underrepresented languages like Amharic \[[2301.12566](http://arxiv.org/abs/2301.12566)\]. Approaches such as adapter-based fine-tuning and modular architectures have been proposed to address this challenge by enabling parameter-efficient and language-specific adaptation without full model retraining \[[2005.00052](http://arxiv.org/abs/2005.00052)\]\[[2212.10448](http://arxiv.org/abs/2212.10448)\].

Late interaction retrieval models like ColBERT and its successors (e.g., ColBERTv2, ColBERT-X, and ColBERT-XM) have introduced multi-vector representations that decompose relevance matching at the token level, achieving strong trade-offs between effectiveness and efficiency in dense retrieval \[[2004.12832](http://arxiv.org/abs/2004.12832)\]\[[2112.01488](http://arxiv.org/abs/2112.01488)\]\[[2201.08471](http://arxiv.org/abs/2201.08471)\]\[[2402.15059](http://arxiv.org/abs/2402.15059)\]. These models allow pre-computation of document embeddings and use lightweight interaction mechanisms at query time, facilitating scalability to large corpora. Extensions like ColBERT-X and ColBERT-XM have incorporated cross-lingual pretrained encoders such as XLM-R to support multilingual and zero-shot retrieval scenarios, showing competitive results even for languages with limited labeled data \[[2201.08471](http://arxiv.org/abs/2201.08471)\]\[[2402.15059](http://arxiv.org/abs/2402.15059)\]. However, their space and computational footprints remain a concern, and adapting these models specifically to morphologically complex low-resource languages requires dedicated language-specific pretraining or fine-tuning.

The scarcity of high-quality labeled datasets for Amharic and other African languages has motivated the creation of new benchmarks and datasets to facilitate evaluation and training of retrieval models \[[https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/cf387294994faf86b3c8332ba72fc17b1442651c](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/cf387294994faf86b3c8332ba72fc17b1442651c)\]\[[2503.18570](http://arxiv.org/abs/2503.18570)\]. For example, AfriCLIRMatrix offers a large-scale cross-lingual IR test collection covering 15 African languages, including Amharic, enabling systematic assessment of retrieval methods on low-resource African languages \[[https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/cf387294994faf86b3c8332ba72fc17b1442651c](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/cf387294994faf86b3c8332ba72fc17b1442651c)\]. Similarly, recent efforts have focused on Amharic-specific NLP resources for tasks like named entity recognition (NER) and speech recognition, which are foundational for higher-level IR improvements \[[2103.11811](http://arxiv.org/abs/2103.11811)\]\[[2403.06354](http://arxiv.org/abs/2403.06354)\]\[[2207.00785](http://arxiv.org/abs/2207.00785)\]\[[2503.18485](http://arxiv.org/abs/2503.18485)\].

Language-specific pretraining on diverse and noisier corpora, such as OSCAR, has been shown to outperform multilingual embeddings trained on Wikipedia data by better capturing linguistic nuances of mid-resource languages, suggesting the importance of domain and language-tailored data even for contextual embeddings \[[2006.06202](http://arxiv.org/abs/2006.06202)\]. This finding underlines that monolingual or language-specific models can learn abstractions that generalize across tasks, sometimes rivaling or exceeding multilingual models on low-resource languages \[[1910.11856](http://arxiv.org/abs/1910.11856)\]. For Amharic, recent work has demonstrated the effectiveness of training monolingual RoBERTa and BERT models for dense retrieval, yielding significant improvements over large multilingual baselines in ranking metrics such as MRR and Recall \[[2503.18570](http://arxiv.org/abs/2503.18570)\].

Cross-lingual retrieval techniques have also explored the use of optimal transport distillation to transfer retrieval models from high-resource to low-resource languages using only bitext data, mitigating the reliance on large annotated retrieval corpora \[[2301.12566](http://arxiv.org/abs/2301.12566)\]. Additionally, retrieval-augmented generation (RAG) models combine parametric language models with non-parametric dense retrievers to improve knowledge-intensive tasks, which could be adapted for low-resource language scenarios by leveraging retrieval components tuned for specific languages \[[2005.11401](http://arxiv.org/abs/2005.11401)\].

In summary, while multilingual transformer-based retrieval models provide a strong foundation, their performance on low-resource, morphologically rich languages like Amharic is constrained by limited data and tokenization challenges. Language-specific dense retrieval models built on monolingual BERT and RoBERTa backbones, alongside late interaction architectures like ColBERT, demonstrate promising improvements by adapting to Amharic’s linguistic characteristics and optimizing model size and efficiency. The emergence of new datasets and adapter-based fine-tuning techniques further supports advancing retrieval effectiveness in such low-resource settings \[[2004.12832](http://arxiv.org/abs/2004.12832)\]\[[1911.02116](http://arxiv.org/abs/1911.02116)\]\[[2112.01488](http://arxiv.org/abs/2112.01488)\]\[[2201.08471](http://arxiv.org/abs/2201.08471)\]\[[2006.06202](http://arxiv.org/abs/2006.06202)\]\[[https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/cf387294994faf86b3c8332ba72fc17b1442651c](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/cf387294994faf86b3c8332ba72fc17b1442651c)\]\[[2005.00052](http://arxiv.org/abs/2005.00052)\]\[[2402.15059](http://arxiv.org/abs/2402.15059)\]\[[2301.12566](http://arxiv.org/abs/2301.12566)\]\[[2212.10448](http://arxiv.org/abs/2212.10448)\]\[[1910.11856](http://arxiv.org/abs/1910.11856)\]\[[2403.06354](http://arxiv.org/abs/2403.06354)\]\[[2207.00785](http://arxiv.org/abs/2207.00785)\]\[[2503.18570](http://arxiv.org/abs/2503.18570)\]\[[2503.18485](http://arxiv.org/abs/2503.18485)\].
