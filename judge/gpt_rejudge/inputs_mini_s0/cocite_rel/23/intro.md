## Related Works

The calibration of radar and camera sensors is a critical component for reliable multi-sensor fusion in autonomous driving and intelligent transportation systems. Traditional calibration methods often rely on physical targets or controlled environments, which limits their applicability in dynamic or real-world scenarios. Early approaches predominantly used target-based calibration, employing reflectors or calibration boards to estimate the rigid-body transform between sensors \[[2103.07505](http://arxiv.org/abs/2103.07505)\]\[[https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/0955326c67a947b5fac5fbae7238d04fc9adc251](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/0955326c67a947b5fac5fbae7238d04fc9adc251)\]\[[https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/0e88fc24ae7694d0b1a2fe78322936fc568bc7d2](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/0e88fc24ae7694d0b1a2fe78322936fc568bc7d2)\]. However, these methods require manual intervention and are not suitable for online or continuous calibration during vehicle operation.

To overcome these limitations, targetless and automatic calibration methods have been developed. For example, a continuous-time 3D radar-to-camera extrinsic calibration approach utilizes radar velocity measurements and does not require specialized calibration targets \[[2103.07505](http://arxiv.org/abs/2103.07505)\]. Similarly, automatic targetless calibration for multiple 3D LiDAR and radar sensors leverages point-to-plane distance minimization and radial velocity errors, enabling extrinsic calibration without overlapping fields of view or calibration targets \[[https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/0e88fc24ae7694d0b1a2fe78322936fc568bc7d2](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/0e88fc24ae7694d0b1a2fe78322936fc568bc7d2)\]. These methods highlight the growing trend toward autonomous calibration by exploiting sensor data characteristics rather than relying on external artifacts.

Deep learning has also been introduced to address the challenges of radar-camera calibration and fusion. Data-driven methods using convolutional neural networks have enabled automatic rotational calibration without dedicated targets by learning robust feature correspondences from radar and camera data \[[1904.08743](http://arxiv.org/abs/1904.08743)\]. Representation learning approaches further improve radar-camera association by extracting feature-level interactions and employing novel loss functions to handle noisy labels and complex driving scenes \[[2103.07825](http://arxiv.org/abs/2103.07825)\]. These learning-based frameworks enhance calibration robustness and generalization capabilities, especially in challenging environments with sparse radar data and depth ambiguities.

Sensor fusion techniques for radar and camera data have evolved in parallel with calibration methods, aiming to leverage complementary sensor strengths. Middle-fusion and early fusion strategies have been proposed to combine radar point clouds and camera images for improved 3D object detection and tracking. For instance, CenterFusion uses a frustum-based association to fuse radar detections with image features, achieving significant improvements in detection accuracy and velocity estimation \[[2011.04841](http://arxiv.org/abs/2011.04841)\]. Other fusion networks employ cross-attention mechanisms to exchange spatial and contextual information between modalities, leading to state-of-the-art performance on benchmark datasets \[[2209.06535](http://arxiv.org/abs/2209.06535)\]\[[https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/0681cea243e4fb4d5ba9d8d08bec107615714bb4](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/0681cea243e4fb4d5ba9d8d08bec107615714bb4)\]\[[2009.08428](http://arxiv.org/abs/2009.08428)\].

Addressing radar data sparsity and height uncertainty remains a key challenge. Recent works propose multi-view representations and attention-based fusion mechanisms to mitigate these issues. For example, the use of both frontal and bird’s-eye views provides complementary perspectives, where the frontal view contains height-sensitive features and the bird’s-eye view offers robustness to height uncertainty \[[2210.09267](http://arxiv.org/abs/2210.09267)\]. Cross-attention modules have been utilized to explicitly model spatial correspondences between radar and camera features, enhancing fusion accuracy and robustness under adverse conditions \[[2210.09267](http://arxiv.org/abs/2210.09267)\]\[[2209.06535](http://arxiv.org/abs/2209.06535)\].

Online calibration methods based on dynamic scene understanding have also gained attention. Tracking moving objects detected across sensors enables continuous calibration updates without requiring static calibration targets or dedicated maneuvers \[[https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/c80265fa719eb85c8a56a6b36c74b99b793f39b2](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/c80265fa719eb85c8a56a6b36c74b99b793f39b2)\]. Such approaches exploit data already present in perception pipelines, offering computationally efficient and practical solutions for long-term system reliability.

In summary, the presented approach advances the state of the art by combining a Dual-Perspective representation with a Selective Fusion Mechanism and Multi-Modal Cross-Attention, addressing key issues of radar height data sparsity and uncertainty. These innovations build upon and extend prior work in targetless calibration, deep learning-based feature matching, and robust sensor fusion, demonstrating superior performance on large-scale datasets such as nuScenes \[[2103.07505](http://arxiv.org/abs/2103.07505)\]\[[1904.08743](http://arxiv.org/abs/1904.08743)\]\[[https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/c80265fa719eb85c8a56a6b36c74b99b793f39b2](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/c80265fa719eb85c8a56a6b36c74b99b793f39b2)\]\[[2011.04841](http://arxiv.org/abs/2011.04841)\]\[[2210.09267](http://arxiv.org/abs/2210.09267)\]\[[https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/0e88fc24ae7694d0b1a2fe78322936fc568bc7d2](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/0e88fc24ae7694d0b1a2fe78322936fc568bc7d2)\]\[[2209.06535](http://arxiv.org/abs/2209.06535)\]\[[2103.07825](http://arxiv.org/abs/2103.07825)\].
