## Related Works

Neural rendering has recently become a central approach for reconstructing 3D scenes from 2D images by learning implicit representations that encode geometry and appearance continuously. These methods typically rely on multi-view inputs and optimize scene representations to minimize inconsistencies across views. However, the quality of reconstruction is often constrained by the number and diversity of input views, particularly for complex or dynamic scenes where some viewpoints are unobserved \[[2206.00665](http://arxiv.org/abs/2206.00665)\]\[[2104.10078](http://arxiv.org/abs/2104.10078)\]\[[2008.02268](http://arxiv.org/abs/2008.02268)\]. This limitation motivates the use of data augmentation techniques to synthetically increase viewpoint coverage and improve reconstruction fidelity.

Video frame interpolation (VFI) aims to synthesize intermediate frames between given consecutive frames, effectively enhancing temporal resolution and providing additional views that can augment neural rendering datasets. Early VFI methods relied on two-step processes involving motion estimation via optical flow followed by pixel synthesis, but suffered from errors in flow estimation, especially under large motions, occlusion, or lighting variations \[[1703.07514](http://arxiv.org/abs/1703.07514)\]\[[1803.10967](http://arxiv.org/abs/1803.10967)\]. Recent deep learning approaches unify motion estimation and synthesis within end-to-end frameworks, improving robustness and quality. For example, adaptive convolutional methods estimate spatially varying kernels to simultaneously capture motion and resampling weights, enabling accurate interpolation even in challenging scenarios \[[1703.07514](http://arxiv.org/abs/1703.07514)\]\[[1708.01692](http://arxiv.org/abs/1708.01692)\]\[[2011.01280](http://arxiv.org/abs/2011.01280)\].

Several works have advanced multi-frame interpolation by learning bi-directional optical flow and visibility maps, allowing variable-length intermediate frame synthesis while mitigating artifacts from occlusions and motion boundaries \[[1712.00080](http://arxiv.org/abs/1712.00080)\]\[[2007.11762](http://arxiv.org/abs/2007.11762)\]. Techniques such as softmax splatting have been proposed to handle forward warping conflicts differentiably, further improving interpolation quality \[[2003.05534](http://arxiv.org/abs/2003.05534)\]. Other approaches avoid explicit optical flow altogether by leveraging channel attention mechanisms or pixel-level feature reshaping, which extract motion cues implicitly to enhance interpolation in complex motion and occlusion settings \[[https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/bb4a9650ca3946c70a7e92007cc61dc0dfd75522](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/bb4a9650ca3946c70a7e92007cc61dc0dfd75522)\]. Quadratic motion models that incorporate acceleration have also been introduced to better approximate real-world non-linear motion trajectories, yielding improved frame synthesis \[[1911.00627](http://arxiv.org/abs/1911.00627)\]. 

Among interpolation methods, warping modules with adaptive collaboration of flows (AdaCoF) estimate both kernel weights and offset vectors per pixel, generalizing traditional warping and effectively handling complex motions \[[1907.10244](http://arxiv.org/abs/1907.10244)\]. Depth-aware interpolation techniques incorporate depth prediction to better resolve occlusions and prioritize sampling from closer objects, which enhances the accuracy of synthesized frames \[[1904.00830](http://arxiv.org/abs/1904.00830)\]. Moreover, cycle consistency losses and motion linearity regularization have been shown to improve interpolation reliability and training efficiency \[[https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/380f46cd24a15271036ff49e9e0d91f39f89d8b4](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/380f46cd24a15271036ff49e9e0d91f39f89d8b4)\].

In the context of neural rendering, frame interpolation serves as an effective data augmentation strategy by generating additional intermediate views that enrich supervision signals for 3D reconstruction. Interpolated frames implicitly encode camera motion and scene geometry, acting as a learned world prior and mitigating the view sparsity problem inherent in many datasets \[[1603.06041](http://arxiv.org/abs/1603.06041)\]\[[1702.02463](http://arxiv.org/abs/1702.02463)\]\[[2007.12622](http://arxiv.org/abs/2007.12622)\]. This augmentation improves reconstruction quality on both static and dynamic scenes. Lightweight yet high-quality interpolation networks trained on diverse video datasets can implicitly model 3D structure and camera trajectories, providing valuable augmented data for neural rendering pipelines \[[1702.02463](http://arxiv.org/abs/1702.02463)\]. 

Neural rendering approaches themselves have evolved to unify implicit surface representations with volumetric radiance fields, enabling joint surface and novel view synthesis without requiring explicit masks or dense 3D supervision \[[2106.10689](http://arxiv.org/abs/2106.10689)\]\[[2104.10078](http://arxiv.org/abs/2104.10078)\]. Techniques such as NeuS leverage signed distance functions combined with volume rendering to improve surface reconstruction fidelity, particularly in complex scenes with self-occlusion \[[2106.10689](http://arxiv.org/abs/2106.10689)\]. Meanwhile, sparse voxel-based neural representations accelerate rendering while maintaining high visual quality \[[2007.11571](http://arxiv.org/abs/2007.11571)\]. These advances benefit from augmented input data richness, which frame interpolation can help provide.

In summary, video frame interpolation has matured into a robust tool for generating high-quality intermediate frames, overcoming traditional optical flow limitations through end-to-end deep learning architectures and advanced motion modeling. When integrated as a data augmentation mechanism, it enhances the input diversity for neural rendering methods, addressing view sparsity and improving 3D reconstruction performance in challenging scenarios. The proposed PS4PRO model builds on these foundations by offering an efficient and effective interpolation approach that implicitly encodes camera and scene geometry priors, thus enriching photometric supervision for neural scene reconstruction.
