## Related Works

Neural retrieval models have become fundamental in information retrieval, especially for web search, due to their ability to learn dense semantic representations that outperform traditional sparse methods like BM25 \[[2004.04906](http://arxiv.org/abs/2004.04906)\]\[[2007.00808](http://arxiv.org/abs/2007.00808)\]. These models typically rely on large-scale labeled query-document pairs, which are expensive to collect. To mitigate this, synthetic query generation using large language models (LLMs) has emerged as a scalable alternative, leveraging unlabeled document corpora such as ClueWeb22 \[[1611.09268](http://arxiv.org/abs/1611.09268)\]. However, synthetic queries vary in quality, often introducing noise that can degrade downstream retrieval performance.

Existing approaches commonly address noisy synthetic data by filtering query-document pairs using external re-rankers to identify and discard low-quality samples \[[1611.09268](http://arxiv.org/abs/1611.09268)\]. While effective, this two-stage filtering approach adds computational overhead and may inadequately capture the subtlety of relevance signals. Alternative strategies include data augmentation and carefully crafted negative sampling to improve dense retriever training. For instance, techniques like RocketQA employ cross-batch negatives and denoised hard negatives to enhance dense passage retrieval effectiveness \[[2010.08191](http://arxiv.org/abs/2010.08191)\], while ANCE dynamically updates negative samples via approximate nearest neighbor search to better reflect true irrelevant documents \[[2007.00808](http://arxiv.org/abs/2007.00808)\]. These methods improve retrieval ranking but still depend on the quality of the training queries themselves.

Direct Preference Optimization (DPO) offers a promising paradigm shift by integrating preference learning directly into model fine-tuning without relying on explicit reward modeling or reinforcement learning. Originally proposed for aligning language models with human preferences, DPO optimizes a policy to prefer outputs with higher human-annotated scores through a stable, classification-based loss \[[2305.1829](http://arxiv.org/abs/2305.18290)\]\[[2310.12036](http://arxiv.org/abs/2310.12036)\]. This eliminates the need for complex reward models and unstable reinforcement learning procedures like PPO \[[1707.06347](http://arxiv.org/abs/1707.06347)\]. Recent extensions, such as DPO-Positive (DPOP), address failure modes of standard DPO by ensuring the likelihood of preferred outputs increases, leading to improved alignment and performance on benchmarks \[[2402.13228](http://arxiv.org/abs/2402.13228)\]. DPO’s ability to directly optimize pairwise preferences has been demonstrated in tasks including summarization, dialogue, and content generation \[[2009.01325](http://arxiv.org/abs/2009.01325)\]\[[2310.12036](http://arxiv.org/abs/2310.12036)\].

In retrieval, integrating DPO into query generation aligns the synthetic data generation process toward producing queries that directly improve downstream ranking performance. By optimizing query generation with ranking signals, models can better capture relevance without external rerankers, streamlining training and potentially enhancing robustness. This approach aligns with recent advances in generative retrieval, where models learn to generate document identifiers as retrieval targets and are further improved by learning-to-rank objectives to bridge the gap between generation and ranking \[[2306.15222](http://arxiv.org/abs/2306.15222)\]. Similarly, Differentiable Search Index (DSI) models unify indexing and retrieval in a single transformer-based architecture, with query generation strategies improving retrieval performance by better aligning queries and document identifiers \[[2305.15115](http://arxiv.org/abs/2305.15115)\]\[[2206.10128](http://arxiv.org/abs/2206.10128)\]\[[2206.02743](http://arxiv.org/abs/2206.02743)\].

Transformer-based rerankers and dense retrievers such as ColBERT and RepBERT have demonstrated significant improvements in passage ranking by leveraging contextualized embeddings and efficient late interaction mechanisms \[[2004.12832](http://arxiv.org/abs/2004.12832)\]\[[2006.15498](http://arxiv.org/abs/2006.15498)\]. These models benefit from high-quality query-document pairs during training, emphasizing the importance of query quality.

Finally, advances in learning from human preferences suggest that methods bypassing intermediate reward modeling stages and directly optimizing pairwise preferences, as done by DPO and its variants, yield more stable and effective fine-tuning outcomes \[[2305.1829](http://arxiv.org/abs/2305.18290)\]\[[2310.12036](http://arxiv.org/abs/2310.12036)\]. Moreover, recent works on automatic alignment without human preference data, using contrastive prompt distillation combined with DPO, highlight the potential for self-supervised preference optimization strategies \[[2402.11907](http://arxiv.org/abs/2402.11907)\].

In summary, our work builds on the foundation of dense retrieval and query generation using synthetic data, while innovatively applying DPO to integrate ranking signals directly into the query generation process. This approach contrasts with prior reliance on external rerankers and leverages recent advances in preference optimization and generative retrieval to enhance downstream retrieval effectiveness \[[1611.09268](http://arxiv.org/abs/1611.09268)\]\[[2305.1829](http://arxiv.org/abs/2305.18290)\]\[[2402.13228](http://arxiv.org/abs/2402.13228)\]\[[2010.08191](http://arxiv.org/abs/2010.08191)\]\[[2004.04906](http://arxiv.org/abs/2004.04906)\]\[[2004.12832](http://arxiv.org/abs/2004.12832)\]\[[2006.15498](http://arxiv.org/abs/2006.15498)\]\[[2009.01325](http://arxiv.org/abs/2009.01325)\]\[[2305.15115](http://arxiv.org/abs/2305.15115)\]\[[2310.12036](http://arxiv.org/abs/2310.12036)\]\[[2402.11907](http://arxiv.org/abs/2402.11907)\]\[[2306.15222](http://arxiv.org/abs/2306.15222)\]\[[2206.10128](http://arxiv.org/abs/2206.10128)\]\[[2206.02743](http://arxiv.org/abs/2206.02743)\]\[[2007.00808](http://arxiv.org/abs/2007.00808)\].
