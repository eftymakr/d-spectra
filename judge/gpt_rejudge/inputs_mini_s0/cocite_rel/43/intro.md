## Related Works

Distributed training of large language models (LLMs) involves significant complexity due to the interplay between model execution, system architectures, and optimization strategies. Early approaches in distributed deep learning prioritized scalability and communication efficiency, but often at the cost of high overhead and extensive code modifications. Horovod exemplifies a notable advancement by enabling efficient multi-GPU training through ring-allreduce communication and minimal code changes, thus reducing communication overhead and simplifying distributed training in frameworks like TensorFlow \[[1802.05799](http://arxiv.org/abs/1802.05799)\]. Despite these improvements, achieving high efficiency in large-scale LLM training remains challenging due to the intricate resource demands and diverse deployment setups.

Performance modeling and estimation serve a critical role in guiding optimization and deployment of LLMs. Existing work on distributed inference optimization highlights the importance of hardware-aware strategies to reduce latency and improve throughput, especially for memory-intensive models \[[2312.11514](http://arxiv.org/abs/2312.11514)\]. For example, techniques that leverage flash memory to handle model parameters exceeding DRAM capacity through on-demand paging have demonstrated significant speedups in inference on limited-memory devices by optimizing data transfer and access patterns \[[2312.11514](http://arxiv.org/abs/2312.11514)\]. Complementary system-level frameworks such as LLM-Pilot provide characterization and predictive modeling of LLM inference performance, enabling cost-performance tradeoff analyses across hardware configurations \[[2312.15234](http://arxiv.org/abs/2312.15234)\]. These efforts underscore the necessity of accurate, hardware-informed models for effective resource allocation and system tuning.

From a machine learning systems perspective, surveys on efficient generative LLM serving emphasize the breadth of algorithmic and system-level solutions required to meet the stringent demands of low-latency, high-throughput serving \[[2009.033](http://arxiv.org/abs/2009.03300)\]. These range from algorithmic modifications such as sparsity exploitation and adaptive context loading, to architectural changes that optimize communication and computation pipelines, reinforcing the multidimensional nature of efficiency challenges in real-world deployments.

Recent advances in training and fine-tuning LLMs also contribute indirectly to performance considerations. Instruction-tuning methods that leverage machine-generated data, exemplified by WizardLM and Orca, show improvements in model capabilities through complex reasoning and stepwise explanation imitation, which in turn influence training workload characteristics and resource utilization \[[2110.14168](http://arxiv.org/abs/2110.14168)\]\[[2304.12244](http://arxiv.org/abs/2304.12244)\]. Hybrid instruction tuning approaches, like those used in MAmmoTH, enhance model generality and accuracy in complex domains such as mathematics, potentially affecting training complexity and convergence profiles \[[2309.05653](http://arxiv.org/abs/2309.05653)\]. Moreover, foundational architectures such as the Transformer continue to underpin LLM scalability and parallelism, facilitating distributed training at scale \[[2307.09288](http://arxiv.org/abs/2307.09288)\].

While much progress has been made in individual aspects of distributed training, inference optimization, and model fine-tuning, a unified and accurate performance modeling toolkit that captures detailed execution behaviors across diverse LLM architectures and deployment configurations remains lacking. Lumos addresses this gap by providing a trace-driven performance modeling and estimation framework tailored for large-scale LLM training. Its ability to replay execution traces with low error and predict performance under new configurations enables systematic exploration and optimization of training strategies, surpassing prior work that either focuses on inference or lacks integration with detailed execution data \[[2303.18223](http://arxiv.org/abs/2303.18223)\].

In summary, prior research has established foundational methods for distributed training communication efficiency, hardware-aware inference optimization, and advanced LLM fine-tuning. However, the complexity of modern LLM training at scale necessitates precise performance modeling tools that integrate system-level traces for accurate estimation, a need that Lumos directly fulfills. This positions Lumos as a critical contribution to accelerating practical optimization and deployment of large-scale LLM training systems.
