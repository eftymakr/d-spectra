## Related Works

Cross-domain sequential recommendation (CDSR) has become a vital area of research aiming to leverage user interactions across multiple domains to alleviate data sparsity and cold-start issues inherent in single-domain sequential recommendation (SDSR) \[[2304.03891](http://arxiv.org/abs/2304.03891)\]\[[2103.01696](http://arxiv.org/abs/2103.01696)\]. The majority of CDSR methods build on transformer-based architectures, primarily employing self-attention mechanisms to capture dependencies within and across user behavior sequences \[[1808.09781](http://arxiv.org/abs/1808.09781)\]\[[1904.06690](http://arxiv.org/abs/1904.06690)\]. However, existing approaches frequently augment the transformer with domain-specific components or transfer modules to facilitate cross-domain knowledge transfer, which may suffer from limitations such as information leakage, reliance on overlapping users, and insufficient modeling of inter-sequence item relationships \[[2304.03891](http://arxiv.org/abs/2304.03891)\]\[[2311.04590](http://arxiv.org/abs/2311.04590)\]\[[https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/96bfe33a2503e960153a934e10be91da4d8b13a6](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/96bfe33a2503e960153a934e10be91da4d8b13a6)\].

Several works focus on explicitly modeling cross-domain user preferences by combining intra-sequence and inter-sequence item interactions. For example, C2DSR utilizes graph neural networks to capture inter-sequence item collaboration alongside sequential attentive encoding for intra-sequence patterns, jointly learning single- and cross-domain user representations enhanced by contrastive learning objectives \[[2304.03891](http://arxiv.org/abs/2304.03891)\]. Similarly, MGCL adopts multi-view graph contrastive learning to integrate intra-domain item transitions and inter-domain user preferences, improving the robustness of user representation learning in CDSR \[[https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/d1c7b8be5f3849a3fd4af780b58eb28355641206](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/d1c7b8be5f3849a3fd4af780b58eb28355641206)\]. DREAM further advances this direction by decoupling single- and cross-domain preferences through an extraction attention module and supervised contrastive learning, addressing class imbalance and data sparsity effectively \[[https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/96bfe33a2503e960153a934e10be91da4d8b13a6](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/96bfe33a2503e960153a934e10be91da4d8b13a6)\].

The challenge of shared accounts, where multiple users share one account across domains, introduces additional complexity in CDSR. Methods like DA-GCN and π-Net propose graph convolution networks with domain-aware attention and parallel information sharing to disentangle user behaviors and transfer cross-domain information adaptively \[[2105.03300](http://arxiv.org/abs/2105.03300)\]\[[https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/fa990aee9a8f157b5d393f5f3eaa014e1e5c67aa](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/fa990aee9a8f157b5d393f5f3eaa014e1e5c67aa)\]. PSJNet and O-SCDR address such challenges via split-join frameworks and reinforcement learning-based clustering to better identify latent users and filter domain-relevant information in shared-account scenarios \[[1910.02448](http://arxiv.org/abs/1910.02448)\]\[[https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/5a653a8060bf9f714866be2823cef71c0fbf9a34](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/5a653a8060bf9f714866be2823cef71c0fbf9a34)\].

Another key concern in CDSR is handling open-world assumptions, where non-overlapping users and domain distribution shifts occur. AMID proposes a multi-interest debiasing framework that models overlapping and non-overlapping user interests and employs a doubly robust estimator to mitigate biases arising from data distribution shifts \[[2311.04590](http://arxiv.org/abs/2311.04590)\]. EA-GCL targets inter-domain density bias by integrating external attention mechanisms and self-supervised learning to enhance robustness in cross-domain sequential behavior modeling \[[2310.04633](http://arxiv.org/abs/2310.04633)\].

To better exploit the temporal and contextual aspects of user behavior, some works incorporate time interval awareness and multi-objective optimization. Proxy-Aware CDSR models time intervals in attention mechanisms and employs contrastive learning to weigh auxiliary domain items for improved sequence representation \[[https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/27670ed18e96550c674e022e284fc5356a7458c2](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/27670ed18e96550c674e022e284fc5356a7458c2)\]. Recent models like Tri-CDR jointly model source, target, and mixed behavior sequences with triple cross-domain attention and contrastive learning to capture global user preferences and fine-grained domain differences \[[2304.05027](http://arxiv.org/abs/2304.05027)\]. Multi-objective perspectives have been explored to dynamically balance recommendation accuracy and cross-domain knowledge transfer, mitigating negative transfer and encouraging complementary knowledge exchange \[[https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/96bfe33a2503e960153a934e10be91da4d8b13a6](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/96bfe33a2503e960153a934e10be91da4d8b13a6)\].

In addition to attention enhancements, graph neural networks and metric learning approaches have shown effectiveness. DDGHM constructs dual dynamic graphs to capture intra- and inter-domain sequential transitions, combined with hybrid metric learning to align and preserve user-item representations across domains \[[2209.10163](http://arxiv.org/abs/2209.10163)\]. CoNet employs deep transfer learning with cross-network connections to enable bidirectional knowledge transfer, reducing training data requirements while improving recommendation accuracy \[[1804.06769](http://arxiv.org/abs/1804.06769)\].

Several works emphasize contrastive learning to strengthen representation learning in CDSR. CMVCDR introduces multi-view contrastive learning to jointly model general and current domain-invariant and domain-specific user interests, enhancing knowledge distillation while suppressing noise \[[https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/73b219f3c0ae43460267ba57eeeb4e5934bc1a6f](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/73b219f3c0ae43460267ba57eeeb4e5934bc1a6f)\]. Similarly, S3-Rec leverages mutual information maximization through self-supervised objectives to improve data representation and alleviate sparsity \[[2008.07873](http://arxiv.org/abs/2008.07873)\].

While transformer-based models such as SASRec and BERT4Rec have laid foundational work in sequential recommendation by effectively capturing long-term dependencies via self-attention, their direct application to CDSR is limited by challenges like domain shifts and sparse cross-domain interactions \[[1808.09781](http://arxiv.org/abs/1808.09781)\]\[[1904.06690](http://arxiv.org/abs/1904.06690)\]. Recent works specifically target improving self-attention mechanisms for CDSR by incorporating domain-aware attention, mixed attention layers, or proxy encoders to unify item representations across domains \[[2012.00485](http://arxiv.org/abs/2012.00485)\]\[[2311.08272](http://arxiv.org/abs/2311.08272)\]\[[https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/27670ed18e96550c674e022e284fc5356a7458c2](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/27670ed18e96550c674e022e284fc5356a7458c2)\]. However, many still rely heavily on overlapping users or complex domain-specific modules, limiting scalability and generalization.

Our work differs by focusing on enhancing the core self-attention module itself through a Pareto-optimal multi-objective formulation that dynamically balances recommendation accuracy and cross-domain attention minimization. This approach automates knowledge transfer, mitigates negative transfer, and facilitates complementary knowledge exchange without heavy reliance on additional domain-specific components or extensive hyperparameter tuning, thus providing a flexible and efficient plug-and-play solution compatible with existing transformer-based CDSR models.
