## Related Works

Federated Learning (FL) enables collaborative model training across decentralized clients while preserving data privacy by keeping raw data local \[[1912.04977](http://arxiv.org/abs/1912.04977)\]. The emergence of the "right to be forgotten" (RTBF) legislation has motivated the development of Federated Unlearning (FU), which aims to remove the influence of specific client data from an already trained global FL model without requiring full retraining \[[https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/39da2d48f2132b919405e3bb6df6ece44ac6e15a](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/39da2d48f2132b919405e3bb6df6ece44ac6e15a)\]\[[2401.05146](http://arxiv.org/abs/2401.05146)\]. This is particularly important in privacy-sensitive applications such as Federated Online Learning to Rank (FOLTR), where user data privacy and model correctness must be balanced.

### Federated Unlearning Approaches

A range of FU methods have been proposed, varying in their assumptions, efficiency, and guarantees. The most straightforward solution is retraining the model from scratch after removing a client’s data, which is often computationally prohibitive \[[https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/39da2d48f2132b919405e3bb6df6ece44ac6e15a](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/39da2d48f2132b919405e3bb6df6ece44ac6e15a)\]. To mitigate this, FedEraser stores historical client model updates to efficiently reconstruct an unlearned model by calibrating these updates, achieving up to 4× speed-up over retraining \[[https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/eadeffdec9fac8fd7f9aea732ca410eb082b7dcf](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/eadeffdec9fac8fd7f9aea732ca410eb082b7dcf)\]. Similarly, FRU applies a rollback mechanism to federated recommendation systems, selectively removing a user’s contribution by rolling back and calibrating updates, while addressing storage constraints through update selection and negative sampling \[[2210.10958](http://arxiv.org/abs/2210.10958)\].

Other methods avoid storing historical updates. For instance, a local unlearning plus few-shot retraining approach removes a client’s influence by locally unlearning at the client side and then performing limited federated rounds with remaining clients, achieving comparable performance to retraining without requiring server storage or global data access \[[2207.05521](http://arxiv.org/abs/2207.05521)\]. Subspace-based FU (SFU) uses gradient ascent in the orthogonal space of other clients’ input gradients to erase a target client’s contribution without additional storage, while preserving model accuracy \[[2302.12448](http://arxiv.org/abs/2302.12448)\].

Privacy preservation is a key concern. FedRecovery incorporates differential privacy and removes weighted sums of gradient residuals from the global model, providing indistinguishability guarantees without retraining or convexity assumptions \[[https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/06edb1f6eadb6c141d720fcca4c854578dc5d670](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/06edb1f6eadb6c141d720fcca4c854578dc5d670)\]. Starfish employs secure two-party computation to enable privacy-preserving FU, reducing the risk of information leakage during unlearning \[[2404.09724](http://arxiv.org/abs/2404.09724)\]. Meanwhile, VeriFi introduces verifiable federated unlearning by enabling users to verify the effectiveness of unlearning via marker-based fingerprinting and model checking, an important step towards trustworthy FU \[[2205.12709](http://arxiv.org/abs/2205.12709)\].

Some works focus on specific unlearning scenarios. Vertical federated unlearning on logistic regression removes client influence by subtracting client updates with minimal extra communication rounds, improving efficiency by over 400% compared to retraining \[[https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/4e26fc4fe687c7471e44f2a5e6feef0facfc4be1](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/4e26fc4fe687c7471e44f2a5e6feef0facfc4be1)\]. Federated feature unlearning frameworks like Ferrari minimize feature sensitivity to remove sensitive or biased features effectively, addressing feature-level privacy concerns \[[2405.17462](http://arxiv.org/abs/2405.17462)\]. Class-discriminative pruning approaches selectively forget entire classes by pruning channels related to target categories, improving unlearning speed by nearly 9–10× without accuracy loss \[[2110.11794](http://arxiv.org/abs/2110.11794)\].

### Evaluation and Verification of Unlearning

Evaluation of unlearning effectiveness remains challenging. Recent works emphasize the necessity of multiple, complementary metrics to assess under-unlearning (incomplete removal) and over-unlearning (excessive forgetting) \[[https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/a5f39bcd6198b26997b77233ff81a14e6bc8a32d](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/a5f39bcd6198b26997b77233ff81a14e6bc8a32d)\]. VeriFi further advances this by providing systematic verification methods to confirm whether unlearning requests have been properly executed \[[2205.12709](http://arxiv.org/abs/2205.12709)\]. Privacy risks from unlearning processes have also been identified, such as membership inference attacks exploiting model differences pre- and post-unlearning, underscoring the need for privacy-preserving unlearning designs \[[2005.02205](http://arxiv.org/abs/2005.02205)\].

### Incentives and Efficiency

Incentivizing clients to remain engaged and avoid unnecessary unlearning is critical for maintaining model utility. Mechanism design studies propose incentive frameworks balancing clients’ privacy costs and training contributions, reducing server overhead by up to 54% \[[https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/a08f1c5c9f0bdb24f0dc75c0d969eb353899156b](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/a08f1c5c9f0bdb24f0dc75c0d969eb353899156b)\]\[[https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/6556402fc6d6a1ebcbf82eed07c64d89dd40f786](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/6556402fc6d6a1ebcbf82eed07c64d89dd40f786)\]. Efficiency-oriented designs include asynchronous federated unlearning via clustered aggregation to localize retraining within clusters, reducing costs by up to 85% \[[https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/70954dd11d1463bc665f803d84b15a4d83805ec0](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/70954dd11d1463bc665f803d84b15a4d83805ec0)\], and communication-efficient algorithms that guarantee exact unlearning with provable bounds leveraging total variation stability \[[2401.11018](http://arxiv.org/abs/2401.11018)\].

### FU in Context of Federated Online Learning to Rank

Federated Online Learning to Rank (FOLTR) integrates FL principles with online learning to optimize ranking models based on implicit feedback, safeguarding user privacy \[[2209.02299](http://arxiv.org/abs/2209.02299)\]. Although prior work has explored federated unlearning broadly, little attention has been paid to systematic, verifiable unlearning within FOLTR, especially balancing privacy guarantees with ranking effectiveness. The need to manage unlearning in FL ranking models aligns with the general challenges addressed by FU methods, requiring tailored evaluation metrics and verification mechanisms to capture unlearning quality comprehensively \[[https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/a5f39bcd6198b26997b77233ff81a14e6bc8a32d](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/a5f39bcd6198b26997b77233ff81a14e6bc8a32d)\].

---

In summary, federated unlearning has evolved into a multifaceted field addressing efficient, privacy-preserving, and verifiable removal of client data from FL models. The surveyed techniques range from historical update calibration to differential privacy, secure computation, and pruning, each contributing different trade-offs between efficiency, privacy, and accuracy. Evaluation methodologies are expanding beyond single metrics to encompass nuanced assessments of unlearning success, critical for sensitive applications such as FOLTR.
