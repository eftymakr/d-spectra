## Related Works

Protein dynamics underpin many biological functions and are traditionally explored through molecular dynamics (MD) simulations, which, despite their accuracy, are computationally intensive and often limited by rare event sampling and long equilibration times \[[https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/2eb1ed9d4baf548391d8220c0a8a3d0be79845ed](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/2eb1ed9d4baf548391d8220c0a8a3d0be79845ed)\]\[[https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/4b841e742eb2626cb97b6e574685894cd97b2925](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/4b841e742eb2626cb97b6e574685894cd97b2925)\]. To address these challenges, recent advances have leveraged generative modeling frameworks, particularly diffusion-based models, to efficiently generate protein conformational ensembles and predict structures with improved computational efficiency.

Denoising diffusion probabilistic models have emerged as a powerful class of generative models for protein structure generation. These models simulate the folding process by progressively denoising a random unfolded state into a folded structure, capturing the inherent rotational and translational invariances of proteins \[[2209.15611](http://arxiv.org/abs/2209.15611)\]\[[2205.15019](http://arxiv.org/abs/2205.15019)\]. Extensions of these approaches incorporate physical principles to enhance fidelity. For example, ConfDiff integrates force-guided SE(3)-equivariant diffusion with data-driven score models to generate conformations with both high diversity and physical realism \[[2403.14088](http://arxiv.org/abs/2403.14088)\]. Similarly, FoldFlow utilizes flow-matching techniques over the SE(3) group to produce stable, diverse protein backbones with improved training efficiency compared to diffusion-based approaches \[[2310.02391](http://arxiv.org/abs/2310.02391)\]. FrameDiff further advances this by developing an SE(3)-equivariant diffusion framework specifically tailored for multi-frame protein backbone generation, achieving generalization beyond known structures \[[2302.02277](http://arxiv.org/abs/2302.02277)\].

Complementary to diffusion models, other generative frameworks have been explored to capture protein conformational heterogeneity. EigenFold employs a diffusion generative model based on harmonic oscillator representations of protein structures, enabling sampling of conformational ensembles with competitive accuracy (median TMScore 0.84) and providing enhanced uncertainty quantification compared to single-structure predictors \[[2304.02198](http://arxiv.org/abs/2304.02198)\]. BioEmu utilizes a generative deep learning system trained on extensive MD data and experimental protein stabilities to rapidly produce statistically independent samples from equilibrium ensembles, accurately reflecting free energy landscapes and enabling mechanistic insights \[[https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/8fa4f6b5232868c2e400670716540f6877068439](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/8fa4f6b5232868c2e400670716540f6877068439)\]. Boltzmann generators combine deep learning with statistical mechanics by learning invertible transformations between complex protein conformations and simple latent spaces, allowing one-shot unbiased sampling of equilibrium states and overcoming rare event sampling bottlenecks without requiring reaction coordinates \[[https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/2eb1ed9d4baf548391d8220c0a8a3d0be79845ed](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/2eb1ed9d4baf548391d8220c0a8a3d0be79845ed)\].

Efforts to incorporate physical energy considerations into generative models address the challenge of aligning generated ensembles with physically meaningful distributions. Standard energy-based objectives often lead to intractable optimization, motivating hybrid approaches. For instance, recent work integrates learned score functions with approximate force fields derived from diffusion models to simulate coarse-grained molecular dynamics without explicit force inputs during training, replicating equilibrium distributions and folding events \[[2302.00600](http://arxiv.org/abs/2302.00600)\]. Additionally, methods like Str2Str employ amortized denoising score matching trained on crystal structures to enable zero-shot conformation sampling independent of simulation data, achieving speedups over traditional MD simulations while preserving equivariance properties \[[2306.03117](http://arxiv.org/abs/2306.03117)\].

Protein structure prediction has also seen significant progress through large-scale language models trained on vast sequence databases, which implicitly learn structural and functional relationships. Models such as ESM-2 leverage evolutionary patterns to predict atomic-resolution structures orders of magnitude faster than state-of-the-art methods, enabling large-scale structural characterization of metagenomic proteins \[[https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/c49a0912595a1cc70aab63524f64ed08c92194a8](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/c49a0912595a1cc70aab63524f64ed08c92194a8)\]\[[https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/18a93dc1558bf9d7534d0b416633cebaf75c1145](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/18a93dc1558bf9d7534d0b416633cebaf75c1145)\]. AlphaFold2, while primarily focused on static structure prediction, has been adapted to probe conformational heterogeneity by manipulating input multiple sequence alignments to sample alternative protein states, revealing intrinsic flexibility encoded in sequence data \[[https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/4244065522c28c9a075d145ad1970a6fb7811f78](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/4244065522c28c9a075d145ad1970a6fb7811f78)\]\[[https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/ff9b84297f16760472c791456befb31ff1d224ef](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/ff9b84297f16760472c791456befb31ff1d224ef)\]\[[https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/74d37f4ad2c017067c4da89059be1eee823b5da9](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/74d37f4ad2c017067c4da89059be1eee823b5da9)\]. Further, approaches like AlphaFlow combine flow-based generative modeling with fine-tuned single-state predictors to generate diverse, physically plausible ensembles with faster convergence to equilibrium properties than replicate MD trajectories \[[2402.04845](http://arxiv.org/abs/2402.04845)\].

Generative models have also been extended to protein design, coupling structure generation with sequence design. ProteinMPNN exemplifies deep learning-based sequence design that robustly encodes structure-sequence relationships and improves upon traditional physics-based methods \[[https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/98926d43356e87c22c82efc132dcaaac1ff40ebe](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/98926d43356e87c22c82efc132dcaaac1ff40ebe)\]. Chroma advances this by integrating diffusion processes respecting polymer conformational statistics with neural architectures that enable conditioning on functional and structural constraints, facilitating the design of novel proteins and complexes with experimentally validated folding and expression properties \[[https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/2cfc26b4af99f195b433fa7aa00f221b111c7cd4](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/2cfc26b4af99f195b433fa7aa00f221b111c7cd4)\].

Recent frameworks such as Distributional Graphormer (DiG) represent a methodological leap by directly learning to transform simple latent distributions into equilibrium distributions conditioned on molecular descriptors, thereby enabling efficient generation of diverse conformations and estimation of state densities across molecular systems including proteins \[[2306.05445](http://arxiv.org/abs/2306.05445)\]\[[https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/1446b534858fc3fc6d9406920450b5c3ca87ee13](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/1446b534858fc3fc6d9406920450b5c3ca87ee13)\]. These approaches highlight the potential of combining data-driven generative models with thermodynamic principles to statistically characterize molecular ensembles.

Collectively, these works demonstrate a convergence of machine learning, physics-based modeling, and statistical mechanics to overcome the limitations of traditional MD simulations for protein conformational sampling. However, effectively aligning learned generative models with accurate physical energy landscapes remains an open challenge, motivating methods like Energy-based Alignment (EBA) that seek to calibrate generative outputs with energy-derived feedback to balance conformational states according to their thermodynamic relevance.
