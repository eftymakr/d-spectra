## Related Works

Zero-Knowledge Proofs (ZKPs) enable a prover to convince a verifier of the truth of a statement without revealing any additional information, making them foundational for privacy-preserving and verifiable computation systems \[[https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/b179cf70666c9f67fc8c330be3a55a5dd2266c2e](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/b179cf70666c9f67fc8c330be3a55a5dd2266c2e)\]. The theoretical basis of ZKPs lies in interactive proof systems with zero knowledge, where the proof reveals no knowledge beyond the validity of the statement, initially characterized in seminal works on knowledge complexity \[[https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/9ccf7b6cb32cf89752a35bd910555adac54773e0](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/9ccf7b6cb32cf89752a35bd910555adac54773e0)\]\[[https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/d5ccb8b351118b35b1a565ad27de4cd2ebdd0287](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/d5ccb8b351118b35b1a565ad27de4cd2ebdd0287)\]. Moreover, it has been established that all languages in NP admit zero-knowledge proof systems, although some protocols require trusted setups or incur large proof sizes \[[https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/6bcd2606c8f0099a82bd03e520659058a5b12e51](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/6bcd2606c8f0099a82bd03e520659058a5b12e51)\].

Among the most practical and widely studied ZKP constructions are zkSNARKs (zero-knowledge Succinct Non-interactive Arguments of Knowledge), known for their succinct proofs and efficient verification \[[https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/b179cf70666c9f67fc8c330be3a55a5dd2266c2e](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/b179cf70666c9f67fc8c330be3a55a5dd2266c2e)\]. However, zkSNARK proof generation remains computationally intensive, often dominated by operations such as large-scale matrix-vector multiplication, number-theoretic transforms (NTTs), and multi-scalar multiplications (MSMs) on elliptic curves \[[https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/e4a4608e8e35379e63b9df5bda1b7599ee8ab9ac](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/e4a4608e8e35379e63b9df5bda1b7599ee8ab9ac)\]\[[https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/6ee84674b7c426849c3da56cb278c08eb7a903cf](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/6ee84674b7c426849c3da56cb278c08eb7a903cf)\]. These computational bottlenecks have motivated extensive research into hardware acceleration techniques to improve prover performance.

GPU-based acceleration has emerged as a promising approach to reduce the overhead of ZKP proof generation. For example, GZKP exploits GPU parallelism with cache-friendly memory patterns and fine-grained task scheduling in MSMs, achieving up to 48× speedup over CPU baselines in cryptographic benchmarks and real-world workloads \[[https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/e149d93531d8c07cf43f6cae327a4795144d1304](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/e149d93531d8c07cf43f6cae327a4795144d1304)\]. Similarly, cuZK introduces a novel parallel MSM algorithm that approaches linear speedup over classical serial algorithms and overlaps CPU-GPU data transfer with computation, yielding up to 4.86× speedup on standard benchmarks and practical applications such as Filecoin \[[https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/e4a4608e8e35379e63b9df5bda1b7599ee8ab9ac](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/e4a4608e8e35379e63b9df5bda1b7599ee8ab9ac)\]. These advancements highlight the potential for GPUs to significantly accelerate critical ZKP primitives.

ASIC and specialized hardware accelerators have also been proposed. PipeZK employs a pipelined architecture with two subsystems optimized for polynomial computations and MSMs, using workload dispatch mechanisms and bandwidth-efficient modules. This design achieves 10× speedup on cryptographic benchmarks and 5× on Zcash workloads, demonstrating the viability of hardware specialization for ZKP acceleration \[[https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/6ee84674b7c426849c3da56cb278c08eb7a903cf](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/6ee84674b7c426849c3da56cb278c08eb7a903cf)\].

While these prior works have made significant progress in accelerating individual or subsets of ZKP primitives, many rely on either trusted setup procedures specific to each application or produce larger proofs with higher verification costs. This limits their applicability in scenarios where universal, one-time setups and small proof sizes are critical, such as publicly verifiable consensus systems \[[https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/b179cf70666c9f67fc8c330be3a55a5dd2266c2e](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/b179cf70666c9f67fc8c330be3a55a5dd2266c2e)\].

Recent research has begun addressing these challenges. The HyperPlonk protocol, for instance, supports one-time universal setup and generates small proof sizes, suitable for decentralized systems requiring efficient verification \[[https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/b179cf70666c9f67fc8c330be3a55a5dd2266c2e](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/b179cf70666c9f67fc8c330be3a55a5dd2266c2e)\]. However, end-to-end acceleration of HyperPlonk’s full protocol, including its SumCheck and MSM components, has remained unexplored until now.

Beyond cryptographic primitives, ZKPs have also been extended to complex domains such as verifiable machine learning. zkCNN demonstrates zero-knowledge proofs tailored for convolutional neural network (CNN) inference, employing sumcheck protocols optimized for fast Fourier transforms and convolutions with linear prover time. This approach supports large-scale networks like VGG16 and achieves proof generation times orders of magnitude faster than prior methods \[[https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/6336149196f02d0eb6c1a89bc6b662e4422bd794](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/6336149196f02d0eb6c1a89bc6b662e4422bd794)\]. Such applications underscore the broader impact of ZKPs in privacy-preserving AI and verifiable computation.

In summary, while previous efforts have advanced the acceleration of ZKP primitives through GPUs and ASICs, challenges remain regarding universal setup requirements, proof size, and verification efficiency. The work on zkSpeed represents a significant step forward by delivering a full-chip accelerator for HyperPlonk that addresses these issues, achieving geometric mean speedups exceeding 800× relative to CPU baselines through comprehensive hardware acceleration of both SumCheck and MSM operations \[[https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/b179cf70666c9f67fc8c330be3a55a5dd2266c2e](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/b179cf70666c9f67fc8c330be3a55a5dd2266c2e)\]. This integration of state-of-the-art ZKP protocol design with high-performance hardware marks an important milestone toward practical, scalable zero-knowledge proof systems.
