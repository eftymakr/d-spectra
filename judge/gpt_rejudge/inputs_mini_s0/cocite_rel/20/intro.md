## Related Works

Single-pixel imaging (SPI) inverse problems have been extensively studied through both traditional optimization and modern deep learning frameworks. Among these, plug-and-play (PnP) and deep unrolling approaches have emerged as leading techniques due to their complementary strengths.

PnP algorithms incorporate advanced denoisers as implicit regularizers within iterative schemes, allowing flexible adaptation to changes in compression ratios (CRs) without retraining. This plug-and-play nature stems from the modular decoupling of data fidelity and regularization terms, where the denoiser serves as a learned proximal operator \[[1704.03488](http://arxiv.org/abs/1704.03488)\]\[[1704.03264](http://arxiv.org/abs/1704.03264)\]\[[1809.04693](http://arxiv.org/abs/1809.04693)\]\[[2012.05703](http://arxiv.org/abs/2012.05703)\]. While PnP methods exhibit impressive versatility and scalability, especially with deep CNN denoisers, their reconstruction accuracy and computational speed can be limited compared to end-to-end trained models \[[1704.03488](http://arxiv.org/abs/1704.03488)\]\[[1704.03264](http://arxiv.org/abs/1704.03264)\]. Recent works have addressed issues such as parameter tuning through learned policy networks to improve efficiency and robustness across varying imaging conditions \[[2012.05703](http://arxiv.org/abs/2012.05703)\].

In contrast, deep unrolling (or unfolding) methods translate iterative optimization algorithms into multi-stage neural networks with trainable parameters, allowing end-to-end learning of both data consistency and regularization components. These methods typically achieve superior reconstruction accuracy and faster inference by leveraging learned proximal operators tailored to specific forward models \[[1902.05399](http://arxiv.org/abs/1902.05399)\]\[[1712.02862](http://arxiv.org/abs/1712.02862)\]\[[1611.03679](http://arxiv.org/abs/1611.03679)\]\[[1409.2574](http://arxiv.org/abs/1409.2574)\]\[[1912.10557](http://arxiv.org/abs/1912.10557)\]. For example, unrolled half-quadratic splitting (HQS) and alternating direction method of multipliers (ADMM) networks have demonstrated state-of-the-art performance in image deblurring and tomographic reconstruction by explicitly incorporating the physics of the forward operator and data fidelity terms \[[1902.05399](http://arxiv.org/abs/1902.05399)\]\[[1712.02862](http://arxiv.org/abs/1712.02862)\]\[[1707.06474](http://arxiv.org/abs/1707.06474)\]. The interpretability of unrolled networks is a key advantage, as each network layer corresponds to an iteration step of a known optimization algorithm, facilitating theoretical analysis and efficient training \[[1912.10557](http://arxiv.org/abs/1912.10557)\]\[[1808.10038](http://arxiv.org/abs/1808.10038)\].

However, a notable limitation of unrolled networks is their dependence on fixed measurement models or compression ratios; when these change, retraining or fine-tuning is often necessary, reducing flexibility in practical scenarios \[[1508.02848](http://arxiv.org/abs/1508.02848)\]\[[2003.10428](http://arxiv.org/abs/2003.10428)\]. To mitigate this, recent efforts have proposed hybrid frameworks and novel loss functions to improve generalizability across varying conditions. For instance, training with proximal trajectory losses encourages the learned network to approximate the proximal operator of an ideal regularizer, promoting robustness to changes in imaging parameters \[[1704.03488](http://arxiv.org/abs/1704.03488)\]. Similarly, unfolding strategies that incorporate weight sharing and smaller networks reduce overfitting and enable faster adaptation to new scenarios \[[1712.02862](http://arxiv.org/abs/1712.02862)\]\[[1912.10557](http://arxiv.org/abs/1912.10557)\]\[[1808.10038](http://arxiv.org/abs/1808.10038)\].

The integration of deep denoisers within model-based frameworks has been explored extensively. Plug-and-play schemes utilize CNN denoisers trained for Gaussian noise removal as generic priors, which can be embedded into iterative solvers for various inverse problems including deblurring, super-resolution, and compressed sensing \[[1704.03488](http://arxiv.org/abs/1704.03488)\]\[[1704.03264](http://arxiv.org/abs/1704.03264)\]\[[1809.04693](http://arxiv.org/abs/1809.04693)\]\[[1406.4175](http://arxiv.org/abs/1406.4175)\]. Notably, combining denoising networks with proximal splitting and variable splitting techniques improves convergence and reconstruction quality \[[1704.03488](http://arxiv.org/abs/1704.03488)\]\[[0912.3522](http://arxiv.org/abs/0912.3522)\]\[[1809.04693](http://arxiv.org/abs/1809.04693)\]. On the other hand, discriminative learning methods train networks end-to-end for specific tasks, offering faster inference but limited flexibility \[[1704.03264](http://arxiv.org/abs/1704.03264)\]\[[1608.03981](http://arxiv.org/abs/1608.03981)\].

Algorithm unrolling has been successfully applied beyond SPI, including blind image deblurring \[[1902.05399](http://arxiv.org/abs/1902.05399)\]\[[https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/b954a94af132294a4a4e2efce15027758a535742](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/b954a94af132294a4a4e2efce15027758a535742)\]\[[1612.02177](http://arxiv.org/abs/1612.02177)\], compressed sensing MRI \[[2006.15271](http://arxiv.org/abs/2006.15271)\], super-resolution \[[2003.10428](http://arxiv.org/abs/2003.10428)\]\[[https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/27fc88ebb3e325a062a6ff48a7a76611af1ecd5a](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/27fc88ebb3e325a062a6ff48a7a76611af1ecd5a)\], and robust principal component analysis for ultrasound imaging \[[1811.08252](http://arxiv.org/abs/1811.08252)\]. These applications highlight the versatility of unrolled architectures in modeling complex image priors and enforcing data fidelity. Moreover, unrolled networks often employ residual learning, batch normalization, and multi-scale processing to enhance training stability and performance \[[1608.03981](http://arxiv.org/abs/1608.03981)\]\[[1512.03385](http://arxiv.org/abs/1512.03385)\]\[[1802.01770](http://arxiv.org/abs/1802.01770)\]\[[1612.02177](http://arxiv.org/abs/1612.02177)\]. Recent advances also include continuous-depth models such as neural ordinary differential equations, which provide flexible, memory-efficient architectures for inverse problems \[[1806.07366](http://arxiv.org/abs/1806.07366)\].

In terms of balancing flexibility and accuracy, the proposed approach aligns with recent trends aiming to combine PnP’s adaptability with unrolling’s accuracy and speed. By designing efficient deep image restorers within HQS/ADMM unrolling frameworks and introducing generalized proximal trajectory losses, the approach addresses the key limitations of existing methods. This unification enables a single trained model to handle varying CRs flexibly, while maintaining or surpassing the reconstruction quality and inference speed of CR-specific unrolled networks \[[1704.03488](http://arxiv.org/abs/1704.03488)\].

In summary, the landscape of SPI inverse problem solvers has evolved through a synergy of traditional iterative optimization, plug-and-play denoising priors, and deep unrolling architectures. Each paradigm offers distinct advantages: PnP methods provide adaptability and modularity; unrolling methods deliver interpretability and high accuracy; and hybrid strategies seek to unify these strengths to achieve flexible, efficient, and robust image reconstruction \[[1704.03488](http://arxiv.org/abs/1704.03488)\]\[[1902.05399](http://arxiv.org/abs/1902.05399)\]\[[1712.02862](http://arxiv.org/abs/1712.02862)\]\[[1704.03264](http://arxiv.org/abs/1704.03264)\]\[[1809.04693](http://arxiv.org/abs/1809.04693)\]\[[1912.10557](http://arxiv.org/abs/1912.10557)\]\[[2012.05703](http://arxiv.org/abs/2012.05703)\].
