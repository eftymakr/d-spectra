## Related Works

Deploying large language models (LLMs) on edge devices remains a critical yet challenging task due to the constrained computational resources, limited memory capacity, and strict power budgets typical of such platforms. Addressing these challenges requires advances across model compression techniques, efficient inference algorithms, and specialized hardware accelerators, often through holistic algorithm-hardware co-design.

### Model Compression and Quantization

Model compression has been extensively explored to reduce the memory footprint and computational demands of LLMs for edge deployment. Quantization stands out as a prominent approach, with various methods developed to balance model size reduction and accuracy retention. Activation-aware Weight Quantization (AWQ) introduces a novel scheme that selectively protects important weights based on activation distribution, enabling up to 4× model size reduction and 3–4× inference speedup on resource-constrained devices without significant performance loss \[[2306.00978](http://arxiv.org/abs/2306.00978)\]. Similarly, SmoothQuant achieves efficient 8-bit quantization of both weights and activations through a mathematically equivalent transformation that migrates quantization difficulty from activations to weights, resulting in up to 2× memory reduction and 1.56× speedup with negligible accuracy degradation \[[2211.10438](http://arxiv.org/abs/2211.10438)\]. OmniQuant further advances post-training quantization by employing learnable weight clipping and equivalent transformations within a differentiable framework, enabling highly effective low-bit quantization (e.g., W4A4) for LLaMA-2 models while improving inference speed and memory efficiency \[[2308.13137](http://arxiv.org/abs/2308.13137)\].

Beyond quantization, pruning techniques have also been adapted for transformers on edge devices. Column balanced block pruning has been implemented on FPGAs to accelerate transformer inference, achieving over 10× speedup compared to CPUs and doubling the speed of GPUs \[[https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/d6eeb0ca9e2f34f2427866aa864d364ec78e6049](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/d6eeb0ca9e2f34f2427866aa864d364ec78e6049)\]. These compression methods, combined with quantization, form the foundation for reducing the resource demands of LLMs suitable for edge deployment.

### Efficient Attention and Inference Algorithms

The self-attention mechanism in transformers is a major bottleneck due to its quadratic complexity with respect to sequence length. Multiple works propose algorithmic optimizations to reduce this overhead. Energon leverages dynamic sparse attention with multi-round filtering to identify important query-key pairs, significantly cutting computational cost and enabling massive speedups (up to 168×) and energy reductions compared to CPUs and GPUs \[[2110.09310](http://arxiv.org/abs/2110.09310)\]. Similarly, FlashAttention-2 improves parallelism and work partitioning for GPU-based attention computation, doubling speed compared to its predecessor and achieving up to 73% theoretical FLOP utilization \[[2307.08691](http://arxiv.org/abs/2307.08691)\].

Streaming LLM techniques address the challenge of long-context inference by introducing attention sink tokens and efficient caching strategies, enabling models like Llama-2 to generalize to sequences exceeding millions of tokens without fine-tuning, while providing up to 22× speedups over recomputation baselines \[[2309.17453](http://arxiv.org/abs/2309.17453)\]. PagedAttention addresses the memory inefficiency of key-value cache management during LLM serving by applying virtual memory paging concepts, leading to 2–4× throughput improvements with near-zero memory waste \[[2309.06180](http://arxiv.org/abs/2309.06180)\]. These advances are crucial for maintaining low latency and energy consumption in always-on edge scenarios.

### Hardware Acceleration for Edge Deployment

FPGA-based accelerators have been widely explored due to their flexibility, energy efficiency, and suitability for low-latency applications on edge devices. Designs such as LlamaF demonstrate significant speedups (over 14×) and power efficiency improvements (6×) for running LLaMA models on embedded FPGA platforms by employing post-training quantization and optimized pipelined architectures \[[2409.11424](http://arxiv.org/abs/2409.11424)\]. The NPE overlay processor offers a programmable FPGA solution for transformer inference with 4× lower power consumption than CPUs and 6× lower than GPUs, enabling real-time conversational AI at the edge \[[2104.06535](http://arxiv.org/abs/2104.06535)\]. FPGA acceleration frameworks like FTRANS utilize block-circulant matrix compression and architecture-level optimizations, achieving up to 27× performance and 81× energy efficiency improvements over CPUs, and outperforming GPUs in energy efficiency by nearly 9× \[[2007.08563](http://arxiv.org/abs/2007.08563)\]. Multi-FPGA systems such as DFX achieve end-to-end GPT-2 inference with 5.6× speedup and nearly 4× energy efficiency gains compared to multiple GPUs, highlighting the scalability of FPGA-based solutions \[[2209.10797](http://arxiv.org/abs/2209.10797)\].

In-memory computing and heterogeneous 3D architectures have also been proposed to overcome memory bandwidth and energy challenges. X-Former hybridizes nonvolatile memory (NVM) and CMOS processing elements to accelerate transformer workloads with substantial latency and energy improvements over GPUs and traditional NVM accelerators \[[2303.07470](http://arxiv.org/abs/2303.07470)\]. The H3D-Transformer platform integrates 3D memory/logic hybrid cubes and approximate computing schemes, reaching 2.6–3.1× higher energy efficiency than state-of-the-art TPUs \[[https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/827b0b03f54ba43596193e5d47f1ff13b3afe293](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/827b0b03f54ba43596193e5d47f1ff13b3afe293)\]. Hardware-software co-design approaches, such as ELSA, approximate self-attention computations to reduce runtime and energy by orders of magnitude while preserving accuracy \[[https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/5af69480a7ae3b571df6782a11ec4437b386a7d9](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/5af69480a7ae3b571df6782a11ec4437b386a7d9)\]. These architectural innovations are critical for meeting the stringent latency and power constraints of edge deployments.

### System-Level Optimization and Co-Design

Recent research emphasizes the synergy between model-level algorithms and hardware-aware system design. Techniques like AWQ combined with optimized inference frameworks (TinyChat) translate theoretical quantization savings into real-world speedups on diverse edge platforms \[[2306.00978](http://arxiv.org/abs/2306.00978)\]. Efficient memory management systems such as vLLM utilize paging-inspired algorithms to minimize KV cache waste, enabling larger batch sizes and improved throughput on existing hardware \[[2309.06180](http://arxiv.org/abs/2309.06180)\]. DejaVu exploits contextual sparsity dynamically during inference to reduce latency by over 2× compared to state-of-the-art libraries without sacrificing model quality \[[2310.17157](http://arxiv.org/abs/2310.17157)\]. These examples illustrate the importance of co-designing algorithms and system-level optimizations for practical edge deployment.

### Summary

In summary, deploying LLMs on edge devices necessitates advances across compression, algorithmic efficiency, hardware acceleration, and system-level coordination. Quantization and pruning reduce model size and computation; optimized attention mechanisms and memory management improve inference latency and resource utilization; FPGA and emerging hardware architectures deliver energy-efficient acceleration; and co-designed algorithm-hardware frameworks enable practical, scalable deployments. The CLONE approach aligns with and extends these efforts by integrating real-time energy optimization and scalable hardware design tailored for edge platforms.
