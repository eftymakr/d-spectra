## Related Works

Sequential recommendation has attracted extensive research attention, focusing on modeling users’ dynamic preferences through various deep learning architectures and attention mechanisms. Early methods leveraged Markov Chains (MCs) and Recurrent Neural Networks (RNNs) to capture short- and long-term sequential dependencies, respectively. MC-based approaches are effective in sparse data settings due to their parsimony, while RNNs handle denser datasets but at higher computational costs \[[1808.09781](http://arxiv.org/abs/1808.09781)\]. Recent advances introduced self-attention mechanisms, such as SASRec, which balance these trade-offs by adaptively attending to relevant past actions, achieving superior performance and efficiency over traditional MC and RNN models \[[1808.09781](http://arxiv.org/abs/1808.09781)\].

Complementing self-attention, convolutional approaches like Caser embed recent item sequences into latent "images" and apply convolutional filters to capture local sequential patterns, effectively modeling both general preferences and recent dynamics \[[1809.07426](http://arxiv.org/abs/1809.07426)\]. However, both attention and convolutional models may overlook complex temporal signals inherent in user behaviors. Incorporating temporal information explicitly, including absolute time patterns and relative time intervals, has been shown to improve modeling of user dynamic preferences by capturing time-sensitive behavior and the influence of elapsed time between actions \[[https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/5574d6123deea94eba2cb35b2486851e826c16ce](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/5574d6123deea94eba2cb35b2486851e826c16ce)\]\[[2008.07873](http://arxiv.org/abs/2008.07873)\].

Despite the success of self-attention in modeling sequential dependencies, recent studies highlight their limitation as low-pass filters that inadequately capture high-frequency and periodic patterns in user behavior sequences. To address this, frequency domain methods have been proposed, such as FEARec, which enhance self-attention by learning both low- and high-frequency signals and periodic characteristics through frequency-domain attention and contrastive learning, resulting in significantly improved recommendation accuracy \[[2304.09184](http://arxiv.org/abs/2304.09184)\]. Similarly, filtering techniques inspired by signal processing have been integrated with simple all-MLP architectures (e.g., FMLP-Rec) to attenuate noise and enhance model robustness while maintaining low computational complexity \[[2202.13556](http://arxiv.org/abs/2202.13556)\].

Another key challenge in sequential recommendation is representing diverse user interests effectively. Multi-interest frameworks like ComiRec capture multiple latent user intents from behavior sequences and aggregate them controllably to balance accuracy and diversity, which is crucial for reflecting complex browsing patterns \[[2005.09347](http://arxiv.org/abs/2005.09347)\]. Hierarchical attention architectures also model evolving long-term and short-term user preferences separately, enabling dynamic user representations that better reflect the temporal evolution of interests \[[https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/0b42168f22713c48d8b0ce3bc8f680d347184d88](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/0b42168f22713c48d8b0ce3bc8f680d347184d88)\].

The bidirectional modeling of sequences, as exemplified by BERT4Rec, addresses limitations of unidirectional RNN-based models by allowing each item representation to incorporate context from both preceding and succeeding interactions. This bidirectional self-attention approach yields richer sequence embeddings and improves recommendation accuracy \[[1904.06690](http://arxiv.org/abs/1904.06690)\]. Feature-level modeling further advances this direction by capturing transition patterns not only between items but also between item features, leveraging deeper self-attention networks to enhance performance \[[https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/63555b4f363ee72dd8d49d8deca68760542c0cd2](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/63555b4f363ee72dd8d49d8deca68760542c0cd2)\].

Recent works also emphasize the importance of contrastive learning to address representation degeneration in sequential models. DuoRec applies contrastive regularization and model-level augmentation to improve the distribution of item embeddings and sequence representations, mitigating semantic collapse and boosting recommendation quality \[[2110.05730](http://arxiv.org/abs/2110.05730)\]. Self-supervised learning approaches, such as S3-Rec, leverage mutual information maximization among various data types (item, attribute, subsequence) to enhance data representations, particularly under data sparsity conditions \[[2008.07873](http://arxiv.org/abs/2008.07873)\].

The integration of mixture-of-experts (MoE) architectures offers a promising avenue to handle diverse behavioral patterns by routing distinct user sequences or subsequences to specialized experts. Although existing MoE models have primarily been applied in multi-domain or multi-task recommendation contexts, their adaptive routing capability aligns with STAR-Rec’s design goals to handle both focused and exploratory user behaviors effectively \[[2005.09347](http://arxiv.org/abs/2005.09347)\]\[[1904.06690](http://arxiv.org/abs/1904.06690)\]. Moreover, MoE frameworks have been shown to improve recommendation diversity and personalize recommendations by capturing heterogeneous user intents \[[2005.09347](http://arxiv.org/abs/2005.09347)\]\[[https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/0b42168f22713c48d8b0ce3bc8f680d347184d88](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/0b42168f22713c48d8b0ce3bc8f680d347184d88)\].

State-space models (SSMs) have recently gained attention for their ability to efficiently model temporal dynamics with linear complexity, enabling scalable processing of variable-length sequences. These models compress temporal information into latent states, complementing attention mechanisms that focus on item-level relationships. Theoretical work demonstrates that SSMs and attention mechanisms can be unified, where SSMs capture temporal compression and attention models diverse item relationships, paralleling the approach of STAR-Rec to synergistically combine these paradigms \[[2304.09184](http://arxiv.org/abs/2304.09184)\]\[[2202.13556](http://arxiv.org/abs/2202.13556)\].

Generative convolutional networks employing dilated convolutions and residual connections have also been proposed to model long-range dependencies efficiently, providing competitive baselines that balance model depth and training efficiency \[[1808.05163](http://arxiv.org/abs/1808.05163)\]. Meanwhile, session-based recommendation systems focus on short-term user intents in sessions, using hybrid attention to combine sequential behavior with current session goals, improving recommendation precision especially in cold-start or sparse-profile scenarios \[[1711.04725](http://arxiv.org/abs/1711.04725)\]\[[1511.06939](http://arxiv.org/abs/1511.06939)\].

In summary, the landscape of sequential recommendation research encompasses diverse modeling techniques including self-attention, convolutional networks, state-space models, multi-interest frameworks, and contrastive/self-supervised learning. STAR-Rec builds on these foundations by integrating preference-aware attention with state-space modeling within a sequence-level MoE framework, addressing key challenges of variable sequence lengths and diverse user interaction patterns while maintaining computational efficiency and modeling flexibility.
