## Related Works

Rehearsal-based methods have become a cornerstone in continual learning (CL) for mitigating catastrophic forgetting by retaining and retraining on a subset of past data alongside new task data \[[1811.11682](http://arxiv.org/abs/1811.11682)\]. These approaches typically store exemplars from previous tasks in episodic memory buffers and perform concurrent training on both new and old samples, effectively reducing forgetting and facilitating knowledge retention \[[1902.10486](http://arxiv.org/abs/1902.10486)\]\[[1809.05922](http://arxiv.org/abs/1809.05922)\]. Experience replay techniques, including simple rehearsal buffers, have been shown to substantially reduce forgetting and improve performance even without task identity information, highlighting their general applicability \[[1811.11682](http://arxiv.org/abs/1811.11682)\]\[[1902.10486](http://arxiv.org/abs/1902.10486)\]\[[1908.04742](http://arxiv.org/abs/1908.04742)\].

Despite their empirical success, concurrent rehearsal strategies—where old and new data are trained simultaneously—may not always be optimal. Recent theoretical and empirical investigations suggest that sequential rehearsal, wherein the model first trains on new data followed by revisiting past data in sequence, can yield improved generalization and reduced forgetting, particularly when tasks exhibit lower similarity \[[1705.08690](http://arxiv.org/abs/1705.08690)\]. This insight aligns with biological learning processes where sequential reactivation of memories supports consolidation, inspiring deep generative replay frameworks that simulate past data to prevent forgetting without storing raw data \[[1705.08690](http://arxiv.org/abs/1705.08690)\]\[[https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/7bfb4ef17eabec1acd266958bdb08622eebfbb05](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/7bfb4ef17eabec1acd266958bdb08622eebfbb05)\]\[[1710.10368](http://arxiv.org/abs/1710.10368)\]. Generative replay methods, integrating generative models with solvers, allow for sampling previous task data and interleaving it sequentially with new tasks, thereby mimicking hippocampal replay mechanisms to achieve continual learning \[[1705.08690](http://arxiv.org/abs/1705.08690)\]\[[https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/7bfb4ef17eabec1acd266958bdb08622eebfbb05](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/7bfb4ef17eabec1acd266958bdb08622eebfbb05)\].

Hybrid rehearsal strategies that combine the advantages of concurrent and sequential rehearsal have also been proposed. These methods adaptively select rehearsal modes based on task similarity, concurrently training similar tasks to leverage shared features and sequentially revisiting dissimilar tasks to minimize interference, improving both forgetting and generalization \[[https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/90e04f3ae23ca7df5f59b11453341e3db943b6f4](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/90e04f3ae23ca7df5f59b11453341e3db943b6f4)\]\[[2003.09553](http://arxiv.org/abs/2003.09553)\]\[[2112.10017](http://arxiv.org/abs/2112.10017)\]. Such hybrid approaches benefit from modular architectures or task-conditioned mechanisms that selectively reuse or instantiate task-specific modules, effectively balancing plasticity and stability \[[2012.12631](http://arxiv.org/abs/2012.12631)\]\[[1906.00695](http://arxiv.org/abs/1906.00695)\]\[[2003.09553](http://arxiv.org/abs/2003.09553)\]. This modularity enables efficient scaling to long task sequences while maintaining past knowledge and supporting forward and backward knowledge transfer \[[2012.12631](http://arxiv.org/abs/2012.12631)\]\[[2003.09553](http://arxiv.org/abs/2003.09553)\]\[[2112.10017](http://arxiv.org/abs/2112.10017)\].

Complementary to rehearsal-based strategies, regularization-based methods address catastrophic forgetting by constraining changes to important parameters. Elastic Weight Consolidation (EWC) and Memory Aware Synapses (MAS) estimate parameter importance to selectively slow learning on weights critical for previous tasks, inspired by synaptic consolidation in neuroscience \[[1612.00796](http://arxiv.org/abs/1612.00796)\]\[[1809.10635](http://arxiv.org/abs/1809.10635)\]\[[1711.09601](http://arxiv.org/abs/1711.09601)\]. While effective in certain scenarios, these approaches often require knowledge of task boundaries or identity and may underperform in class-incremental learning settings where task identity is unknown \[[1904.07734](http://arxiv.org/abs/1904.07734)\]\[[1809.10635](http://arxiv.org/abs/1809.10635)\]\[[1806.08568](http://arxiv.org/abs/1806.08568)\]. Other parameter isolation and architecture growth methods, such as Progressive Neural Networks and reinforced continual learning, alleviate forgetting by dedicating separate subnetworks or dynamically expanding architectures for new tasks \[[1805.12369](http://arxiv.org/abs/1805.12369)\]\[[1606.04671](http://arxiv.org/abs/1606.04671)\].

Experience replay combined with meta-learning techniques further improves continual learning by optimizing representations to minimize interference and maximize transfer across tasks. Meta-Experience Replay (MER) aligns gradients across new and old data to reduce forgetting and facilitate faster learning on new tasks \[[1810.11910](http://arxiv.org/abs/1810.11910)\]\[[1905.12588](http://arxiv.org/abs/1905.12588)\]. Additionally, controlled memory sampling strategies, such as maximally interfered retrieval, enhance replay efficiency by prioritizing the rehearsal of samples most susceptible to forgetting, resulting in improved retention with limited memory \[[1908.04742](http://arxiv.org/abs/1908.04742)\].

Recent advances also explore task-agnostic continual learning, where task identity is unavailable at test time. Approaches like task-conditioned hypernetworks and prompt-based continual learning dynamically generate task-specific parameters or prompts, enabling the network to recall task knowledge without explicit task labels \[[1906.00695](http://arxiv.org/abs/1906.00695)\]\[[2112.08654](http://arxiv.org/abs/2112.08654)\]. These methods demonstrate robustness and scalability in challenging benchmarks, including class-incremental scenarios with many tasks and limited memory \[[1906.00695](http://arxiv.org/abs/1906.00695)\]\[[2112.08654](http://arxiv.org/abs/2112.08654)\].

Theoretical analyses of rehearsal-based continual learning, particularly in overparameterized linear models, have begun to elucidate the fundamental trade-offs between forgetting and generalization. Such analyses reveal that sequential rehearsal outperforms concurrent rehearsal when tasks are less similar, with hybrid strategies offering intermediate benefits by adapting rehearsal style to task similarity \[[1705.08690](http://arxiv.org/abs/1705.08690)\]\[[1706.08840](http://arxiv.org/abs/1706.08840)\]. Empirical validations on deep neural networks confirm these theoretical insights, underscoring the importance of rehearsal scheduling and task similarity in rehearsal effectiveness \[[1705.08690](http://arxiv.org/abs/1705.08690)\]\[[1606.09282](http://arxiv.org/abs/1606.09282)\]\[[1706.08840](http://arxiv.org/abs/1706.08840)\]\[[1906.00695](http://arxiv.org/abs/1906.00695)\].

In summary, rehearsal-based continual learning encompasses a spectrum of strategies from concurrent to sequential rehearsal, with hybrid approaches emerging as promising solutions that balance stability and plasticity by leveraging task similarity. These methods, supported by theoretical and empirical studies, advance the understanding of rehearsal dynamics and inspire architectures and algorithms that more closely mimic human learning processes to effectively mitigate catastrophic forgetting.
