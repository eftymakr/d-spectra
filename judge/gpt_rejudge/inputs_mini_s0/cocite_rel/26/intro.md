## Related Works

Differential Privacy (DP) has become a foundational standard for privacy-preserving data analysis, with extensive study in both centralized and distributed settings \[[https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/e4ce10063cd25447dcde75c2d9ce327446ced952](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/e4ce10063cd25447dcde75c2d9ce327446ced952)\]. The classical DP framework involves perturbing query outputs by calibrated noise according to the function’s sensitivity to protect individual privacy \[[https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/e4ce10063cd25447dcde75c2d9ce327446ced952](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/e4ce10063cd25447dcde75c2d9ce327446ced952)\]. However, the assumption of faithful noise generation by a trusted server or data aggregator is often unrealistic, especially in distributed or untrusted environments. Servers or aggregators may deviate from prescribed mechanisms by sampling noise incorrectly or generating correlated noise, thereby compromising privacy guarantees without detection \[[2208.09011](http://arxiv.org/abs/2208.09011)\]. This vulnerability motivates the need for verifiable mechanisms that ensure DP compliance under adversarial conditions.

Recent research has begun to address these challenges by integrating cryptographic verification techniques with DP mechanisms. The introduction of verifiable differential privacy (VDP) leverages zero-knowledge proofs (ZKPs) to allow a verifier to confirm that noisy outputs are generated according to the agreed DP protocol without revealing any sensitive information \[[2208.09011](http://arxiv.org/abs/2208.09011)\]. These zero-knowledge frameworks provide strong assurances that randomness used to satisfy DP is correctly sampled, deterring malicious behavior by the server. However, while ZKPs are sufficient for verifiable DP, they are not strictly necessary; alternative cryptographic approaches that enforce honest execution via secure multi-party computation (MPC) or homomorphic encryption also exist \[[https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/ac8be9c934d6ff9ae095ebde81c8d3a57f7b918d](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/ac8be9c934d6ff9ae095ebde81c8d3a57f7b918d)\]\[[https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/d762c3b48d3949dc2cd803d955c3c983e7aa16ba](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/d762c3b48d3949dc2cd803d955c3c983e7aa16ba)\]. Such methods provide complementary trade-offs between computational overhead, communication costs, and trust assumptions.

In the distributed and local DP (LDP) models, where data is privatized at the client side before aggregation, vulnerabilities remain significant. LDP protocols are especially susceptible to manipulation by malicious clients injecting crafted inputs to skew frequency estimates or heavy hitter identification \[[1911.02046](http://arxiv.org/abs/1911.02046)\]\[[1909.09630](http://arxiv.org/abs/1909.09630)\]. These manipulation attacks exploit the local perturbation step, which inherently provides plausible deniability and complicates detection. To mitigate this, verifiable randomization mechanisms have been proposed that enable a data collector to verify that each client’s randomization step adheres to the protocol, effectively preventing output manipulation \[[2104.06569](http://arxiv.org/abs/2104.06569)\]. These mechanisms improve robustness and significantly reduce the impact of adversarial clients, while maintaining acceptable overhead.

The shuffle model, which introduces an anonymity layer by randomly permuting user messages before aggregation, offers a promising intermediate model between local and central DP, improving accuracy and privacy amplification \[[1911.06879](http://arxiv.org/abs/1911.06879)\]\[[1808.01394](http://arxiv.org/abs/1808.01394)\]\[[1811.12469](http://arxiv.org/abs/1811.12469)\]\[[2107.11839](http://arxiv.org/abs/2107.11839)\]. Although the shuffle model reduces trust requirements, it does not inherently solve verifiability issues. Recent works explore interactive proofs and verification techniques tailored to differentially private counting queries to address potential server misbehavior and ensure reliable outputs \[[2208.09011](http://arxiv.org/abs/2208.09011)\]. These interactive proofs combine public and private randomness to achieve zero-knowledge verifiability, revealing no sensitive randomness while guaranteeing adherence to DP mechanisms.

On the algorithmic front, mechanisms for private frequency estimation, histogram construction, and telemetry data collection under LDP have been refined with post-processing corrections and consistency constraints to improve utility \[[1712.01524](http://arxiv.org/abs/1712.01524)\]\[[1905.08320](http://arxiv.org/abs/1905.08320)\]\[[1602.07387](http://arxiv.org/abs/1602.07387)\]\[[https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/770ad70607ce60d8c411916129a423b5aad45e93](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/770ad70607ce60d8c411916129a423b5aad45e93)\]. These advances complement verification efforts by ensuring accurate statistics even under noise and potential adversarial influence. Furthermore, cryptographic frameworks such as Cryptε demonstrate how combining encrypted data processing with DP can yield high accuracy without trusted servers, highlighting the feasibility of secure and verifiable distributed DP systems \[[https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/ac8be9c934d6ff9ae095ebde81c8d3a57f7b918d](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/ac8be9c934d6ff9ae095ebde81c8d3a57f7b918d)\].

In summary, the literature converges on the critical insight that ensuring both privacy and verifiability in DP requires integrating cryptographic proofs, interactive verification protocols, and robust randomization mechanisms. This paper extends these efforts by proposing Verifiable Distributed Differential Privacy (VDDP), a framework that combines efficient verification with distributed noise generation to resist malicious behavior. Our contributions include novel, scalable mechanisms for verifiable discrete Laplacian noise and randomized response under local DP, achieving orders-of-magnitude improvements in proof generation and communication costs over prior work \[[2208.09011](http://arxiv.org/abs/2208.09011)\]\[[2104.06569](http://arxiv.org/abs/2104.06569)\]\[[https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/d762c3b48d3949dc2cd803d955c3c983e7aa16ba](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/d762c3b48d3949dc2cd803d955c3c983e7aa16ba)\]. These results advance the state-of-the-art by balancing efficiency, privacy, and verifiability in distributed DP settings.
