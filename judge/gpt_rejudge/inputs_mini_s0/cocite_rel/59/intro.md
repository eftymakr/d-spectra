## Related Works

Fairness in re-ranking and recommendation systems has attracted substantial attention due to the inherent trade-offs between ranking accuracy and fairness towards multiple stakeholders. Early research primarily focused on single-sided fairness concerns, either from the user or item perspective. For instance, user-oriented fairness studies have shown that recommender systems often favor active (advantaged) users over inactive (disadvantaged) ones, resulting in unequal recommendation quality that harms overall system performance \[[2104.10671](http://arxiv.org/abs/2104.10671)\]. Similarly, item fairness has been addressed through exposure fairness frameworks that ensure items receive exposure proportional to their merit, mitigating position bias and unfair opportunity distribution \[[1805.01788](http://arxiv.org/abs/1805.01788)\]\[[1802.07281](http://arxiv.org/abs/1802.07281)\].

Recognizing the two-sided nature of many platforms, recent works emphasize fairness simultaneously for consumers and producers. This dual perspective is crucial in marketplaces such as Amazon or Spotify, where maximizing customer satisfaction alone can lead to unfair exposure for producers, and vice versa \[[2201.0118](http://arxiv.org/abs/2201.01180)\]\[[2002.10764](http://arxiv.org/abs/2002.10764)\]. Approaches like FairRec guarantee max-min share (MMS) fairness for producers and envy-freeness up to one item (EF1) for consumers, balancing fairness on both sides with only marginal accuracy loss \[[2201.0118](http://arxiv.org/abs/2201.01180)\]\[[2002.10764](http://arxiv.org/abs/2002.10764)\]. Extensions such as CPFair integrate personalized user fairness with provider fairness in a joint optimization framework, improving fairness without sacrificing recommendation quality \[[1802.07281](http://arxiv.org/abs/1802.07281)\]. Likewise, P-MMF prioritizes providers in weak market positions through max-min fairness re-ranking, effectively balancing provider utility with user preferences in online settings \[[2110.15781](http://arxiv.org/abs/2110.15781)\].

The trade-off between fairness and accuracy, often viewed as a cost to consumers when fairness constraints are imposed on producers, has been studied through economic analogies. A taxation perspective models fairness constraints as item-level taxes redistributing ranking resources, revealing limitations in prior methods that lack continuity and controllability over accuracy loss \[[2404.17826](http://arxiv.org/abs/2404.17826)\]. Tax-rank addresses these issues by using utility-difference-based taxes optimized via optimal transport, providing smoother and more controllable fairness-accuracy trade-offs \[[2404.17826](http://arxiv.org/abs/2404.17826)\]. This economic viewpoint aligns with efforts to formalize fairness using welfare economics concepts such as Lorenz dominance, which ensures Pareto efficiency and improved utility for worse-off individuals in rankings \[[2110.15781](http://arxiv.org/abs/2110.15781)\].

Fairness-aware ranking frameworks also extend to group fairness constraints, where demographic parity or proportionality is enforced across ranked prefixes to prevent under-representation of protected groups \[[1706.06368](http://arxiv.org/abs/1706.06368)\]\[[1704.0684](http://arxiv.org/abs/1704.06840)\]. These methods balance diversity and fairness while maintaining utility, often via constrained optimization approaches that handle large numbers of fairness constraints efficiently \[[1712.09752](http://arxiv.org/abs/1712.09752)\]\[[1706.06368](http://arxiv.org/abs/1706.06368)\]\[[1704.0684](http://arxiv.org/abs/1704.06840)\]. In learning-to-rank scenarios with dynamic feedback, controllers have been proposed to enforce amortized group fairness over time, adapting ranking functions to evolving data while maintaining fairness guarantees \[[2005.14713](http://arxiv.org/abs/2005.14713)\].

Another significant direction is the amortization of individual fairness across multiple ranking instances, addressing position bias by ensuring attention received by items is proportional to their relevance over time \[[1805.01788](http://arxiv.org/abs/1805.01788)\]\[[2204.03046](http://arxiv.org/abs/2204.03046)\]. Approaches like VerFair improve the fairness-performance trade-off by leveraging consumer prior knowledge to allocate exposure more effectively, outperforming state-of-the-art algorithms at both individual and group levels \[[2204.03046](http://arxiv.org/abs/2204.03046)\].

While many fairness metrics exist, their reliance on single fairness measures can obscure the complex interplay between user utility and item fairness. Recent proposals emphasize the need for multi-dimensional evaluation frameworks that capture this trade-off comprehensively. For example, the Elastic Fairness Curve (EF-Curve) framework quantitatively analyzes algorithm performance across varying elasticity levels, measuring how fairness "taxes" on providers transfer to accuracy losses for consumers \[[1712.09752](http://arxiv.org/abs/1712.09752)\]. ElasticRank builds on this by adjusting inter-item distances in a curved space using elasticity calculations, effectively navigating the fairness-accuracy landscape with improved efficiency and effectiveness \[[1712.09752](http://arxiv.org/abs/1712.09752)\].

Beyond re-ranking algorithms, fairness in recommender systems has been addressed through fairness-aware learning objectives that mitigate bias in collaborative filtering and adapt to multiple fairness targets concurrently \[[1705.08804](http://arxiv.org/abs/1705.08804)\]. Multi-stakeholder fairness taxonomies have also been proposed to conceptualize fairness as a multisided construct, reflecting the diverse interests of users, providers, and platforms \[[1707.00093](http://arxiv.org/abs/1707.00093)\].

In summary, the literature reveals a rich landscape of fairness-aware ranking and recommendation techniques that balance competing goals of accuracy, user satisfaction, and item fairness. Economic principles such as resource allocation, max-min fairness, and taxation models provide valuable theoretical foundations for understanding and improving fairness-accuracy trade-offs. However, the complexity of these trade-offs necessitates evaluation frameworks like EF-Curve and algorithms like ElasticRank that explicitly consider elasticity to guide fair re-ranking in practice.
