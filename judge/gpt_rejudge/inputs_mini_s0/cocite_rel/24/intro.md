## Related Works

Diffusion models have advanced generative modeling significantly, particularly in image and video synthesis. Diffusion Transformers (DiTs) have emerged as powerful backbones, replacing traditional U-Net architectures with transformer-based latent diffusion models that operate on image or video tokens. DiTs, such as DiT-XL/2, achieve state-of-the-art results on class-conditional ImageNet benchmarks, demonstrating excellent scalability and quality \[[2212.09748](http://arxiv.org/abs/2212.09748)\]\[[2209.12152](http://arxiv.org/abs/2209.12152)\]. Extending diffusion transformers to video generation involves modeling spatiotemporal dependencies and handling a large number of tokens, as shown by methods like Latte and Video LDM, which introduce specialized temporal embeddings and transformer blocks to capture video dynamics efficiently \[[2401.03048](http://arxiv.org/abs/2401.03048)\]\[[2304.08818](http://arxiv.org/abs/2304.08818)\].

Despite their performance, DiTs face significant computational and memory costs, limiting their deployment on resource-constrained or edge devices. Quantization, especially post-training quantization (PTQ), is a widely studied compression technique that reduces model size and inference latency by lowering bit-widths of weights and activations \[[2106.08295](http://arxiv.org/abs/2106.08295)\]\[[1712.05877](http://arxiv.org/abs/1712.05877)\]. However, applying PTQ directly to diffusion models is challenging due to their multi-timestep denoising process, where activation distributions vary dynamically during sampling, and shortcut connections exhibit bimodal activation distributions \[[2302.04304](http://arxiv.org/abs/2302.04304)\]\[[2211.15736](http://arxiv.org/abs/2211.15736)\]. Conventional PTQ methods designed for static models struggle to maintain generation quality in this setting.

Recent works have begun to tailor quantization strategies specifically for diffusion transformers (DiTs). PTQ4DiT introduced channel-wise salience balancing and salience calibration to address extreme magnitude channels and temporal activation variability in DiTs, enabling 8-bit quantization and even 4-bit weight quantization with minimal quality loss \[[2405.16005](http://arxiv.org/abs/2405.16005)\]. Similarly, Q-DiT proposes automatic quantization granularity allocation and sample-wise dynamic activation quantization to handle spatial and temporal variance in activations and weights, achieving competitive fidelity on ImageNet and video benchmarks under low-bit settings \[[2406.17343](http://arxiv.org/abs/2406.17343)\]. ViDiT-Q extends these ideas to both image and video diffusion transformers, implementing efficient GPU kernels that yield significant memory savings and latency speedups while maintaining visual quality \[[2406.0254](http://arxiv.org/abs/2406.02540)\]. Complementarily, temporal dynamic quantization methods dynamically adapt quantization intervals based on timestep information, improving output quality without inference overhead \[[2306.02316](http://arxiv.org/abs/2306.02316)\].

Quantization-aware training (QAT) approaches, such as EfficientDM, attempt to bridge the gap between PTQ efficiency and QAT’s higher fidelity by introducing data-free fine-tuning with quantization-aware low-rank adapters, scale-aware optimization, and temporal learned step-size quantization, resulting in lower FID degradation at 4-bit precision with substantially reduced fine-tuning cost \[[2310.0327](http://arxiv.org/abs/2310.03270)\]. PTQD further improves PTQ for diffusion models by decomposing quantization noise and calibrating the variance schedule during denoising, employing mixed-precision schemes tailored for each denoising step to minimize quality loss \[[2305.10657](http://arxiv.org/abs/2305.10657)\].

In the video generation domain, maintaining temporal consistency across frames is critical and challenging. Models like Lumiere and W.A.L.T address this by incorporating space-time U-Net architectures and windowed attention mechanisms, respectively, to generate temporally coherent videos in a single pass or via spatiotemporal attention \[[2204.03458](http://arxiv.org/abs/2204.03458)\]\[[2306.02316](http://arxiv.org/abs/2306.02316)\]\[[2211.15736](http://arxiv.org/abs/2211.15736)\]. Latent video diffusion models further leverage pretrained image diffusion backbones and temporal alignment modules to efficiently extend image models to video, ensuring both temporal coherence and high resolution \[[2304.08818](http://arxiv.org/abs/2304.08818)\]\[[2311.15127](http://arxiv.org/abs/2311.15127)\]. ConsistI2V proposes spatiotemporal attention over initial frames and noise initialization strategies to preserve visual consistency in image-to-video generation \[[2402.04324](http://arxiv.org/abs/2402.04324)\]. Adaptive caching techniques, such as AdaCache, tackle inference efficiency for video DiTs by caching computations and dynamically allocating compute based on motion content, enabling substantial speedups without quality loss \[[2411.02397](http://arxiv.org/abs/2411.02397)\].

While quantization for vision transformers has progressed with methods addressing inter-channel variation, non-uniform attention distributions, and outliers in weight and activation statistics, these advances primarily target classification or language models \[[2111.12293](http://arxiv.org/abs/2111.12293)\]\[[2111.13824](http://arxiv.org/abs/2111.13824)\]\[[2106.14156](http://arxiv.org/abs/2106.14156)\]\[[2307.00331](http://arxiv.org/abs/2307.00331)\]. Such insights have informed quantization strategies for DiTs, given their transformer backbone and unique challenges from the diffusion process.

In summary, the state-of-the-art in quantizing diffusion transformers for video generation combines advances in temporal-aware quantization, dynamic calibration, and optimization strategies that preserve spatiotemporal correlations during inference. Our Q-VDiT framework extends this line of research by introducing a Token-aware Quantization Estimator (TQE) that compensates quantization errors across token and feature dimensions and a Temporal Maintenance Distillation (TMD) technique that explicitly preserves frame-wise spatiotemporal dependencies during optimization, thereby establishing new benchmarks in scene consistency and efficiency \[[2406.17343](http://arxiv.org/abs/2406.17343)\]\[[2406.0254](http://arxiv.org/abs/2406.02540)\]\[[2405.16005](http://arxiv.org/abs/2405.16005)\]\[[2310.0327](http://arxiv.org/abs/2310.03270)\].
