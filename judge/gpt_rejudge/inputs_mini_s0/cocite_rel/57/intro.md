## Related Works

Generative retrieval (GR) has emerged as a transformative paradigm in information retrieval by reframing the retrieval task as a sequence-to-sequence generation problem that directly predicts document identifiers (docids) from queries. This approach eliminates the need for traditional index-retrieve pipelines, enabling end-to-end optimization and potentially more seamless integration with large pretrained language models \[[2202.06991](http://arxiv.org/abs/2202.06991)\]\[[2206.02743](http://arxiv.org/abs/2206.02743)\]\[[2208.07652](http://arxiv.org/abs/2208.07652)\]. Early GR methods, such as Differentiable Search Index (DSI), demonstrated the feasibility of encoding entire corpora within the parameters of a single transformer model and generating docids autoregressively, achieving competitive retrieval performance on static corpora \[[2202.06991](http://arxiv.org/abs/2202.06991)\]\[[2206.10128](http://arxiv.org/abs/2206.10128)\]\[[2305.15115](http://arxiv.org/abs/2305.15115)\].

A fundamental design consideration in GR concerns the representation of docids. Two main categories have been explored: text-based docids, which use semantically meaningful tokens such as titles, n-grams, or generated queries, and numeric-based docids, which rely on fixed or learned discrete identifiers often derived from clustering or hashing \[[2202.06991](http://arxiv.org/abs/2202.06991)\]\[[2304.04171](http://arxiv.org/abs/2304.04171)\]\[[https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/6682a3679a17d090830635f1c420126d20eae54a](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/6682a3679a17d090830635f1c420126d20eae54a)\]. Text-based docids benefit from semantic alignment with pretrained language models, higher lexical diversity, and fine-grained representations, which collectively enhance generalization to unseen or newly added documents \[[2304.04171](http://arxiv.org/abs/2304.04171)\]\[[2305.15115](http://arxiv.org/abs/2305.15115)\]\[[https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/6682a3679a17d090830635f1c420126d20eae54a](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/6682a3679a17d090830635f1c420126d20eae54a)\]. In contrast, numeric-based docids offer computational efficiency but tend to overfit to the initial corpus distribution, leading to significant performance degradation when the document collection evolves dynamically \[[2308.14968](http://arxiv.org/abs/2308.14968)\]\[[2212.09744](http://arxiv.org/abs/2212.09744)\]\[[2305.11841](http://arxiv.org/abs/2305.11841)\]. This limitation is particularly acute in scenarios requiring continual indexing, where the model must incorporate new documents without retraining from scratch.

Addressing dynamic or evolving corpora in GR has recently attracted focused research attention. CLEVER introduced continual learning mechanisms specifically tailored for generative retrieval, incorporating incremental product quantization for efficient encoding of new documents and memory-augmented learning to mitigate forgetting of previously indexed documents \[[2308.14968](http://arxiv.org/abs/2308.14968)\]. Similarly, DSI++ tackled catastrophic forgetting in continual indexing by optimizing training dynamics toward flatter minima and employing generative memory to stabilize learning, significantly improving retrieval robustness over sequences of document additions \[[2212.09744](http://arxiv.org/abs/2212.09744)\]. These works highlight the challenges of maintaining retrieval quality in dynamic settings and emphasize the importance of specialized training and memory strategies.

Improving docid design has also been a key avenue for enhancing GR in dynamic corpora. GenRet proposed a discrete auto-encoding framework that learns semantic docids end-to-end, enabling improved generalization and retrieval effectiveness across diverse benchmarks, including unseen documents \[[2304.04171](http://arxiv.org/abs/2304.04171)\]. NOVO further advanced this direction by optimizing n-gram-based docids through query denoising and retrieval tasks, yielding interpretable and semantically rich identifiers that improve both retrieval accuracy and robustness \[[https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/6682a3679a17d090830635f1c420126d20eae54a](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/6682a3679a17d090830635f1c420126d20eae54a)\]. Other approaches, such as Auto Search Indexer (ASI), combine semantic indexing with end-to-end generative retrieval, jointly learning optimal docids for existing and new documents, which addresses limitations of fixed docid schemes \[[2310.12455](http://arxiv.org/abs/2310.12455)\].

Scaling GR to large corpora remains a significant hurdle. Studies show that naive scaling of model size or corpus size does not straightforwardly translate into retrieval improvements, underscoring the need for effective document representation and query generation strategies \[[2305.11841](http://arxiv.org/abs/2305.11841)\]\[[2311.09134](http://arxiv.org/abs/2311.09134)\]. RIPOR introduced a prefix-oriented ranking optimization and relevance-aware docid construction to boost large-scale generative retrieval performance, achieving substantial gains on standard benchmarks \[[2311.09134](http://arxiv.org/abs/2311.09134)\]. PAG proposed simultaneous decoding and set-based identifiers to improve both accuracy and efficiency of autoregressive docid generation \[[2404.14600](http://arxiv.org/abs/2404.14600)\].

Furthermore, query generation has emerged as a pivotal technique to bridge the distribution gap between indexing and retrieval. DSI-QG enhances classic DSI by indexing documents through multiple generated queries, thus better aligning training and inference conditions and improving retrieval performance, especially in cross-lingual and zero-shot scenarios \[[2206.10128](http://arxiv.org/abs/2206.10128)\]\[[2305.15115](http://arxiv.org/abs/2305.15115)\]. CorpusBrain demonstrated that pretraining generative retrieval models with synthetic query-document pairs significantly boosts retrieval quality and downstream task performance, even under low-resource conditions \[[2208.07652](http://arxiv.org/abs/2208.07652)\].

Lastly, unified frameworks such as UGR integrate multiple retrieval granularities (document, passage, sentence, entity) within a single generative model using prompt learning, enhancing generalization across diverse knowledge-intensive language tasks and domains \[[2304.14856](http://arxiv.org/abs/2304.14856)\]. This illustrates the growing trend toward versatile and adaptable GR architectures that can cope with evolving corpora and heterogeneous retrieval requirements.

In summary, recent research converges on several critical insights relevant to GR over dynamic corpora: (i) semantic and fine-grained text-based docids substantially improve generalization and robustness; (ii) continual learning techniques are essential to prevent forgetting and efficiently incorporate new documents; (iii) query generation plays a key role in mitigating distribution mismatch between indexing and retrieval; and (iv) joint optimization of docid design and retrieval models leads to superior end-to-end performance. These advances collectively motivate hybrid approaches that leverage the efficiency of numeric-based docids and the semantic richness of text-based ones to meet the demands of real-world dynamic retrieval systems \[[2308.14968](http://arxiv.org/abs/2308.14968)\]\[[2212.09744](http://arxiv.org/abs/2212.09744)\]\[[2310.12455](http://arxiv.org/abs/2310.12455)\]\[[2304.04171](http://arxiv.org/abs/2304.04171)\]\[[https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/6682a3679a17d090830635f1c420126d20eae54a](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/6682a3679a17d090830635f1c420126d20eae54a)\].
