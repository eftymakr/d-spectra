## Related Works

Dense retrieval has emerged as a vital paradigm in information retrieval, leveraging pre-trained language models (PLMs) such as BERT and LLaMA to encode queries and documents into dense vector representations, which are then compared via simple similarity measures like inner products \[[2408.06663v5](http://arxiv.org/abs/2408.06663v5)\]. The predominant approach involves a pre-train-then-fine-tune paradigm, where models are first pre-trained on large corpora and subsequently fine-tuned on task-specific data via contrastive learning \[[2408.06663v5](http://arxiv.org/abs/2408.06663v5)\]\[[2108.05652v1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2108.05652v1)\]. This framework has demonstrated strong empirical success but has also raised fundamental questions about the distinct roles and interplay of pre-training and fine-tuning in dense retriever performance.

A key insight from recent work is that much of the retrieval knowledge embedded in dense retrievers originates during pre-training, while fine-tuning mainly serves to adjust neuron activations without substantially reorganizing the underlying knowledge structure \[[2408.06663v5](http://arxiv.org/abs/2408.06663v5)\]. This observation was initially established in the context of BERT-based encoders with Dense Passage Retrieval (DPR), highlighting that fine-tuning mostly modulates model sensitivity to evaluation prompts and may cause some forgetting of domain knowledge absent in the fine-tuning data \[[2408.06663v5](http://arxiv.org/abs/2408.06663v5)\]. However, this claim’s generality remained underexplored, especially across different architectures, pooling strategies, and datasets.

Extending this line of inquiry, investigations comparing representation approaches—such as single CLS token embeddings versus mean pooling—and backbone architectures, including encoder-only models like BERT and decoder-only models like LLaMA, reveal nuanced differences. While BERT-based DPR models conform to the pattern where pre-training knowledge dominates and fine-tuning primarily adjusts activations, decoder-based models (e.g., LLaMA) and mean-pooled models (e.g., Contriever) exhibit greater fine-tuning influence on final retrieval effectiveness \[[2408.06663v5](http://arxiv.org/abs/2408.06663v5)\]. Moreover, evaluation across datasets such as MSMARCO and Natural Questions further underscores the variability in knowledge acquisition dynamics between pre-training and fine-tuning \[[2408.06663v5](http://arxiv.org/abs/2408.06663v5)\]\[[2403.01432v5](http://arxiv.org/abs/2403.01432v5)\].

Fine-tuning strategies themselves have been critically examined. For instance, employing very large dropout rates during fine-tuning has been shown to yield richer representations that improve out-of-distribution generalization beyond what ensembles and weight-averaging methods achieve, highlighting fine-tuning’s capacity to enhance robustness when carefully applied \[[2403.00946v3](http://arxiv.org/abs/2403.00946v3)\]. This supports the notion that fine-tuning can modulate model representations substantially, especially under specific regularization regimes. Additionally, fine-tuning combined with data augmentation can significantly boost performance in low-resource or domain-specific retrieval scenarios, indicating the importance of fine-tuning when training data is scarce or specialized \[[2403.01432v5](http://arxiv.org/abs/2403.01432v5)\]\[[2106.11251v2](http://arxiv.org/abs/2106.11251v2)\].

Pre-training techniques are also pivotal. Architectures like Condenser and coCondenser have been proposed to better align pre-training objectives with dense retrieval needs, learning to condense information into dense vectors more effectively and mitigating problems such as training noise sensitivity and the requirement for large batch sizes \[[2108.05540v1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2108.05540v1)\]\[[2104.08253v2](http://arxiv.org/abs/2104.08253v2)\]. Multi-task pre-training architectures, such as MASTER, integrate diverse objectives to enrich dense vector representations, leading to improved retrieval quality \[[2212.07841v2](http://arxiv.org/abs/2212.07841v2)\]. These advances underscore that high-quality pre-training is foundational to effective dense retrieval and can amplify fine-tuning benefits \[[2409.14683v1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2409.14683v1)\].

The choice of embedding and pooling strategies further impacts retrieval performance and knowledge retention. Studies comparing CLS token-based single-vector representations with multiple token-level embeddings indicate trade-offs between efficiency and effectiveness; multiple representations (e.g., ColBERT) generally yield higher retrieval effectiveness, especially for complex queries, albeit at increased computational cost \[[2108.06279v2](http://arxiv.org/abs/2108.06279v2)\]. Token pooling methods can reduce memory footprints with minimal performance degradation, improving practical applicability \[[2409.14683v1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2409.14683v1)\]. Meanwhile, in multi-domain and multi-modal settings, strategies such as ensembling multiple CLS tokens or specialized pooling (e.g., Group Generalized Mean) have been developed to better capture domain-specific or modality-specific nuances \[[2212.04114v1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2212.04114v1)\]\[[2309.04333v1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2309.04333v1)\].

Finally, retrieval augmentation methods like Retrieval-Augmented Generation (RAG) have been shown to outperform fine-tuning particularly for less popular or low-frequency knowledge, emphasizing the complementary role of retrieval mechanisms to fine-tuned knowledge in enhancing model coverage \[[2403.01432v5](http://arxiv.org/abs/2403.01432v5)\]. Improvements in retrieval accuracy, such as integrating control tokens with dense passage retrieval, also contribute to mitigating hallucination and improving response fidelity \[[2405.13008v1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2405.13008v1)\].

In summary, the interplay between pre-training and fine-tuning in dense retrievers is complex and dependent on model architecture, representation approach, dataset characteristics, and training strategies. While pre-training lays the foundational retrieval knowledge, fine-tuning serves as a critical modulator, particularly in architectures and settings beyond the canonical BERT-DPR framework. Advances in pre-training objectives, pooling strategies, and retrieval augmentation continue to enrich the dense retrieval landscape, informing best practices for building robust and effective retrieval systems.
