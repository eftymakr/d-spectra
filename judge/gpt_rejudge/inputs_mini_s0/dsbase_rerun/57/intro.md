## Related Works

Generative retrieval (GR) has emerged as a promising paradigm in information retrieval, shifting from traditional index-retrieve pipelines to models that generate document identifiers (docids) directly in an end-to-end manner \[[2304.04171v1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2304.04171v1)\]. This approach leverages autoregressive language models to predict relevant documents by generating discrete docids, enabling unified optimization of retrieval tasks. Early GR methods often relied on fixed, rule-based docids such as document titles or cluster centroids derived from embeddings, which limited their semantic expressiveness and adaptability \[[2305.18952v5](http://arxiv.org/abs/2305.18952v5)\]. To address this, GenRet proposed learning semantic docids via discrete auto-encoding, allowing for end-to-end tokenization of documents into short discrete identifiers that better capture semantic content, leading to state-of-the-art performance on benchmarks like NQ320K and MS MARCO \[[2305.18952v5](http://arxiv.org/abs/2305.18952v5)\].

Despite these advances, most GR models have been developed and evaluated on static corpora, assuming a fixed document collection. However, real-world applications frequently involve dynamic corpora where documents are continuously added or updated. The challenges of applying GR in such evolving environments have recently received attention. CLEVER introduced a continual learning framework for GR that incrementally indexes new documents using an adaptive product quantization codebook and memory-augmented learning to maintain knowledge of prior documents, addressing catastrophic forgetting and enabling efficient updates without exhaustive retraining \[[2308.14968v2](http://arxiv.org/abs/2308.14968v2)\]\[[2102.11903v2](http://arxiv.org/abs/2102.11903v2)\]. Similarly, empirical evaluations demonstrate that GR models can adapt more flexibly than dual-encoder approaches to evolving knowledge, exhibiting improved robustness and computational efficiency in dynamic settings \[[2304.04171v1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2304.04171v1)\].

A critical distinction in GR approaches lies in the design of docids. Text-based docids, which align more closely with pretrained language models’ semantic understanding, tend to generalize better to unseen or newly added documents due to their lexical richness and fine-grained semantic encoding \[[2305.18952v5](http://arxiv.org/abs/2305.18952v5)\]\[[2304.04171v1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2304.04171v1)\]. In contrast, numeric-based docids—often derived from clustering or hashing methods—offer high efficiency but suffer from overfitting to the initial corpus, resulting in significant performance drops in dynamic corpora \[[2308.14968v2](http://arxiv.org/abs/2308.14968v2)\]\[[2504.09935v1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2504.09935v1)\]. This phenomenon is attributed to the numeric docids’ limited lexical diversity and coarse granularity, which restrict their adaptability to new documents outside the training distribution \[[2504.09935v1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2504.09935v1)\]. Recent analyses have highlighted that fine-grained and semantically aligned docid designs are essential for effective GR in dynamic environments \[[2305.18952v5](http://arxiv.org/abs/2305.18952v5)\]\[[2304.04171v1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2304.04171v1)\].

To improve the alignment between token-level generation and document-level relevance, methods such as direct document relevance optimization (DDRO) have been proposed. DDRO optimizes the generation of docids directly against document-level relevance via pairwise ranking objectives, bypassing the complexity and instability of reinforcement learning approaches used previously \[[2504.05181v2](http://arxiv.org/abs/2504.05181v2)\]. This simplification leads to substantial improvements in retrieval effectiveness on standard datasets and suggests pathways for enhancing GR models’ performance without incurring heavy computational overhead.

Alternative GR frameworks have explored few-shot indexing to reduce training costs and improve adaptability. Few-Shot GR utilizes large language models (LLMs) prompted to generate docids for documents without requiring fine-tuning, creating a docid bank for retrieval that balances efficiency and effectiveness. This approach demonstrates superior performance relative to heavily trained GR models, highlighting the potential of leveraging pretrained LLM capabilities directly for dynamic indexing \[[2408.02152v3](http://arxiv.org/abs/2408.02152v3)\].

Beyond docid design and indexing strategies, research on integrating semantic search techniques into retrieval-augmented generation (RAG) systems has shown that hybrid retrievers combining dense and sparse representations can substantially improve retrieval accuracy at scale \[[2404.07220v2](http://arxiv.org/abs/2404.07220v2)\]. While these methods primarily target static corpora, their principles are relevant for extending GR models to dynamic corpora by enhancing the retrieval of relevant documents for generation tasks.

Overall, the literature converges on the importance of semantic and fine-grained docid representation, continual learning mechanisms for dynamic indexing, and direct optimization of relevance to advance GR models in dynamic environments. However, challenges remain in balancing efficiency with generalization, especially when extending GR systems to real-world, ever-changing document collections \[[2304.04171v1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2304.04171v1)\]\[[2308.14968v2](http://arxiv.org/abs/2308.14968v2)\]\[[2305.18952v5](http://arxiv.org/abs/2305.18952v5)\]\[[2504.09935v1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2504.09935v1)\].
