## Related Works

Fine-grained emotion classification in app reviews is a growing research area that extends beyond traditional sentiment analysis, which typically categorizes text as positive, negative, or neutral \[[2006.09739v1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2006.09739v1)\]\[[1703.02256v1](http://arxiv.org/abs/1703.02256v1)\]. Unlike coarse sentiment analysis, fine-grained emotion recognition aims to identify a broader spectrum of emotional states, such as joy, anger, fear, and anticipation, which provides more nuanced insights into user experiences \[[2108.07638v2](http://arxiv.org/abs/2108.07638v2)\]\[[2505.00013v1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2505.00013v1)\]. This granularity is essential for understanding complex user feedback and improving app development processes.

Several studies have explored datasets and annotation frameworks tailored for fine-grained emotions, though few focus explicitly on app reviews. For example, the creation of weakly supervised lexical datasets for fine-grained emotions in Portuguese exemplifies the challenges of low-resource environments and the importance of well-structured annotation guidelines \[[2108.07638v2](http://arxiv.org/abs/2108.07638v2)\]. Similarly, the adaptation of Plutchik's emotion taxonomy and other multidimensional emotion models has been used to improve classification granularity and interpretability \[[2505.00013v1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2505.00013v1)\]\[[2411.18162v1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2411.18162v1)\].

Automated emotion detection using machine learning, and more recently, large language models (LLMs), has shown promising results. Transformer-based models such as BERT and DeBERTa have been fine-tuned for fine-grained emotion classification, achieving high accuracy and F1-scores on diverse datasets, including social media and customer feedback texts \[[2108.07638v2](http://arxiv.org/abs/2108.07638v2)\]\[[2505.00013v1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2505.00013v1)\]. However, LLMs often struggle with nuanced emotional distinctions and exhibit lower performance on less frequent emotions, highlighting ongoing challenges in model robustness and class imbalance \[[2411.18162v1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2411.18162v1)\]\[[2505.00013v1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2505.00013v1)\]. Advanced methods like Emotion Context Learning (E-ICL) improve fine-grained emotion prediction by dynamically selecting semantically and emotionally accurate prototypes, mitigating interference from irrelevant categories without additional training \[[2406.02642v4](http://arxiv.org/abs/2406.02642v4)\].

In the context of app reviews, sentiment and emotion analysis have been pivotal in extracting actionable insights for developers. Early work demonstrated that sentiment extracted from millions of app reviews correlates with user satisfaction and app performance metrics \[[2006.09739v1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2006.09739v1)\]\[[1703.02256v1](http://arxiv.org/abs/1703.02256v1)\]. Beyond sentiment, ethical concerns and privacy issues expressed in reviews have been systematically analyzed using machine learning approaches, emphasizing the depth of user feedback beyond mere polarity \[[2401.11063v2](http://arxiv.org/abs/2401.11063v2)\]\[[2212.09289v4](http://arxiv.org/abs/2212.09289v4)\]. Techniques such as automated helpfulness prediction and topic-sentiment modeling have further refined how user reviews are summarized and prioritized for developers, facilitating release planning and feature improvements \[[2210.06235v1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2210.06235v1)\]\[[2103.15774v2](http://arxiv.org/abs/2103.15774v2)\]\[[2210.10223v4](http://arxiv.org/abs/2210.10223v4)\].

Annotation quality and guidelines critically impact the performance of supervised models for app review analysis. Studies reveal that refined annotation guidelines yield more informative and less noisy app feature extraction, improving model precision and recall \[[1810.05187v1](http://arxiv.org/abs/1810.05187v1)\]. Additionally, augmenting training data with cross-domain annotated reviews enhances recall but may reduce precision, indicating trade-offs in dataset composition \[[1810.05187v1](http://arxiv.org/abs/1810.05187v1)\].

Challenges specific to fine-grained emotion classification in app reviews include the complexity of emotional interpretation, inter-annotator variability, and the noisy nature of user-generated content \[[1703.02256v1](http://arxiv.org/abs/1703.02256v1)\]\[[2212.09289v4](http://arxiv.org/abs/2212.09289v4)\]. Attempts to fully automate emotion annotation with LLMs demonstrate substantial agreement with human annotators but also reveal the limitations of these models in capturing subtle emotional cues, necessitating iterative human-in-the-loop approaches \[[2108.07638v2](http://arxiv.org/abs/2108.07638v2)\]\[[2401.11063v2](http://arxiv.org/abs/2401.11063v2)\]\[[2406.02642v4](http://arxiv.org/abs/2406.02642v4)\]. Integrating sensory or neuro-cognitive data into transformer architectures has been proposed as a novel direction to enhance emotional sensitivity, suggesting potential future improvements for emotion classification models \[[2403.15574v1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2403.15574v1)\].

Overall, the convergence of structured annotation frameworks, advanced machine learning models, and domain-specific datasets forms the foundation for advancing fine-grained emotion classification in app reviews. However, the complexity of emotions expressed and the contextual nature of app feedback maintain significant challenges for achieving reliable full automation.
