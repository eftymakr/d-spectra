## Related Works

Neural retrieval models have become central to modern information retrieval (IR) systems, particularly in Web search, due to their capacity to learn complex semantic relationships directly from large-scale data. Traditional models relied on hand-crafted features for ranking, but recent advances employ deep learning architectures trained end-to-end to extract features from raw documents and queries, thus overcoming feature engineering limitations \[[2102.11903v2](http://arxiv.org/abs/2102.11903v2)\]. However, these models require large volumes of labeled query-document pairs, which are expensive and labor-intensive to obtain.

To alleviate this dependency, synthetic query generation using large language models (LLMs) has emerged as a scalable alternative, leveraging the abundance of unlabeled document corpora such as ClueWeb22. LLMs can generate diverse and contextually relevant queries that can serve as training data for retrieval models. Studies have shown that synthetic queries generated by LLMs tend to be more verbose and specific compared to template-based query generation methods, enabling better retrieval of relevant entities \[[2406.06729v1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2406.06729v1)\]. Nonetheless, synthetic queries often vary in quality, and noisy or irrelevant query-document pairs can degrade downstream retrieval performance.

Common approaches to mitigate noise include filtering synthetic query-document pairs via external re-rankers or separate relevance estimators. While such post-hoc filtering can improve training data quality, it incurs additional computational costs and may fail to fully capture the nuanced preferences of end-users \[[2102.11903v2](http://arxiv.org/abs/2102.11903v2)\]. To address these limitations, recent work proposes integrating ranking signals directly into the query generation process through Direct Preference Optimization (DPO), a preference-based alignment method that optimizes models by learning from pairwise preferences rather than explicit reward modeling or reinforcement learning \[[2102.11903v2](http://arxiv.org/abs/2102.11903v2)\]. This method has been successfully applied in other domains requiring alignment with human preferences, such as counter-speech generation, where DPO-aligned LLMs outperform supervised fine-tuning baselines in generating contextually appropriate and linguistically nuanced responses \[[2412.15453v1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2412.15453v1)\].

The use of synthetic data to enhance retrieval systems has also been explored in domain-specific contexts. For instance, in Vietnamese legal document retrieval, synthetic queries generated by LLMs have been effectively used to pre-train embedding-based bi-encoder and ColBERT models, resulting in significant accuracy improvements despite limited annotated data \[[2412.00657v1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2412.00657v1)\]. This highlights the broader applicability of synthetic queries in overcoming data scarcity challenges across specialized retrieval tasks.

Beyond query generation, advances in retrieval-augmented generation (RAG) demonstrate the importance of optimizing retrieval components jointly with generation models. Hybrid retrieval methods blending dense and sparse indexing techniques improve document selection accuracy, which subsequently benefits generative question-answering systems \[[2404.07220v2](http://arxiv.org/abs/2404.07220v2)\]. Optimization frameworks such as CARROT address retrieval chunk selection in RAG by modeling relationships among retrieved chunks and dynamically adapting retrieval strategies to query characteristics, yielding substantial gains in retrieval efficiency and effectiveness \[[2411.00744v2](http://arxiv.org/abs/2411.00744v2)\].

Research on query variation and robustness further underscores the challenges in retrieval pipelines. Studies reveal that retrieval models exhibit significant performance drops (~20%) when tested against syntactic and semantic query perturbations that preserve meaning, demonstrating brittleness to natural user query variability \[[2111.13057v3](http://arxiv.org/abs/2111.13057v3)\]. This suggests that generating synthetic queries which better reflect realistic user behavior and preferences is critical for robust retrieval.

Additionally, query recommendation systems have benefited from LLM-based generative approaches that do not require extensive query logs, addressing cold start problems and improving user engagement through more relevant and diverse suggestions \[[2405.19749v2](http://arxiv.org/abs/2405.19749v2)\]. Similarly, generating synthetic queries aligned with human preferences can enhance retrievability assessments, as studies find that artificial queries often fail to correlate well with real user query logs, indicating a need for more sophisticated query generation strategies \[[2404.09473v1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2404.09473v1)\].

In e-commerce search, generative retrieval frameworks incorporating preference optimization and constrained search have been developed to handle challenges such as noisy item titles and long-tail queries, improving interpretability and conversion rates by aligning model outputs with user click data \[[2407.19829v2](http://arxiv.org/abs/2407.19829v2)\]. These advances demonstrate the value of integrating preference signals into generative retrieval models for practical applications.

Lastly, the integration of human preference rationales into preference learning frameworks has shown to accelerate convergence and improve final model performance. Augmenting preference pairs with explanatory rationales aids in producing more robust models aligned with nuanced human judgments, which is relevant for direct preference optimization in query generation \[[2407.14477v4](http://arxiv.org/abs/2407.14477v4)\].

In summary, the literature demonstrates a clear trend toward leveraging large language models for synthetic query generation as a means to scale training data for neural retrieval models, while addressing quality and alignment challenges through preference-based optimization techniques like DPO. These approaches offer promising avenues to improve retrieval effectiveness without reliance on expensive labeled data or extensive post-hoc filtering, aligning model outputs more closely with human relevance judgments and real-world query distributions \[[2102.11903v2](http://arxiv.org/abs/2102.11903v2)\]\[[2406.06729v1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2406.06729v1)\]\[[2405.19749v2](http://arxiv.org/abs/2405.19749v2)\]\[[2412.00657v1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2412.00657v1)\]\[[2412.15453v1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2412.15453v1)\]\[[2407.14477v4](http://arxiv.org/abs/2407.14477v4)\].
