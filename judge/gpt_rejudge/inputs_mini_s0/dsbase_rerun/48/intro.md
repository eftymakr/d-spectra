## Related Works

Neural retrieval methods leveraging transformer-based pre-trained language models (PLMs) have substantially advanced multilingual and cross-lingual retrieval, particularly through dense retrieval techniques that embed queries and documents into continuous vector spaces for semantic matching \[[2108.06279v2](http://arxiv.org/abs/2108.06279v2)\]\[[2211.14876v1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2211.14876v1)\]. Two prominent dense retrieval architectures include single-representation models, which encode entire passages into one embedding (e.g., ANCE), and multiple-representation models, which represent each token separately to capture fine-grained interactions (e.g., ColBERT). Multiple-representation methods tend to outperform single-representation models in effectiveness metrics such as Mean Average Precision (MAP) and MRR@10, especially for complex or hard queries, though they incur higher computational costs \[[2108.06279v2](http://arxiv.org/abs/2108.06279v2)\]. The ColBERT late interaction mechanism, as employed in recent work, enables strong retrieval performance by balancing detailed token-level matching with efficiency \[[2108.06279v2](http://arxiv.org/abs/2108.06279v2)\]\[[2203.11163v2](http://arxiv.org/abs/2203.11163v2)\].

Despite these advances, the application of dense retrieval to low-resource languages like Amharic remains underexplored. Amharic, characterized by rich morphology and limited annotated data, presents unique challenges including data scarcity and suboptimal tokenization strategies \[[2503.18570v1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2503.18570v1)\]\[[2010.06447v2](http://arxiv.org/abs/2010.06447v2)\]. The scarcity of training data undermines the effectiveness of multilingual PLMs such as mBERT and XLM-RoBERTa, which generally perform well in cross-lingual tasks but often suffer when applied to languages with limited resources and complex morphology \[[2503.18570v1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2503.18570v1)\]\[[2105.05996v3](http://arxiv.org/abs/2105.05996v3)\]\[[2204.05814v1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2204.05814v1)\]. This limitation motivates the development of language-specific models and tokenization methods tailored to morphological characteristics, which have shown improved downstream task performance in morphologically rich languages like Hindi and Marathi \[[2504.10335v2](http://arxiv.org/abs/2504.10335v2)\]\[[2501.05260v1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2501.05260v1)\].

Tokenization plays a critical role in handling morphological complexity and improving representation quality for low-resource languages. Morphologically grounded tokenization approaches, such as morphology-aware segmentation and constrained Byte-Pair Encoding (BPE), have demonstrated gains in machine translation and language modeling tasks for Indian languages, highlighting the importance of linguistically informed subword units \[[2504.10335v2](http://arxiv.org/abs/2504.10335v2)\]. Similarly, for African low-resource languages, human-involved tokenization strategies that consider tonal and grammatical nuances have improved neural machine translation quality, underscoring the need for language-specific preprocessing \[[2103.08052v2](http://arxiv.org/abs/2103.08052v2)\]. These findings suggest that adapting tokenization for Amharic’s morphological structure could enhance dense retrieval model effectiveness.

Data scarcity in low-resource settings is often mitigated using transfer learning, data augmentation, and multi-lingual transfer techniques. Cross-lingual transfer using multilingual PLMs allows leveraging high-resource language data to improve performance in low-resource languages, though effectiveness varies depending on language family proximity and domain adaptation \[[2204.05814v1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2204.05814v1)\]\[[1804.07878v2](http://arxiv.org/abs/1804.07878v2)\]. Contrastive learning methods incorporating multiple positive samples, including typo-augmented queries, have been proposed to increase robustness and improve retrieval quality under noisy conditions \[[2403.10939v1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2403.10939v1)\]. Moreover, ensemble learning and mixture-of-experts frameworks have been explored to handle domain heterogeneity and improve retrieval accuracy by combining specialized models, which could be beneficial for domain-specific Amharic retrieval tasks \[[2403.13468v1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2403.13468v1)\]\[[2412.10008v1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2412.10008v1)\].

Benchmarking and evaluation datasets tailored for low-resource languages are crucial for systematic assessment and model development. Efforts to create Amharic-specific datasets, alongside openly released codebases and trained models, facilitate reproducibility and further research \[[2503.18570v1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2503.18570v1)\]. Comparable initiatives for other low-resource languages have shown that well-designed benchmarks enable more reliable comparisons between sparse and dense retrieval baselines, and foster improvements in retrieval effectiveness \[[2404.07220v2](http://arxiv.org/abs/2404.07220v2)\]\[[2411.00727v2](http://arxiv.org/abs/2411.00727v2)\]\[[2310.09765v2](http://arxiv.org/abs/2310.09765v2)\]. Additionally, research on optimizing dense retrieval efficiency, such as lexically-accelerated dense retrieval (LADR) and scaling with large language models (ScalingNote), addresses practical deployment challenges without sacrificing retrieval performance, which is vital for real-world applications in low-resource settings \[[2307.16779v1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2307.16779v1)\]\[[2411.15766v1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2411.15766v1)\].

In summary, while transformer-based dense retrieval has shown remarkable success in multilingual contexts, its adaptation to low-resource, morphologically rich languages like Amharic requires addressing challenges in tokenization, data scarcity, and model architecture. Language-specific pre-trained models, morphology-aware tokenization, and advanced contrastive and ensemble learning techniques are promising avenues to improve retrieval effectiveness. The integration of late interaction models such as ColBERT further enhances performance by capturing fine-grained semantic matches. The present work builds on these foundations by introducing Amharic-specific dense retrieval models that outperform multilingual baselines, supported by a comprehensive benchmark and publicly released resources \[[2108.06279v2](http://arxiv.org/abs/2108.06279v2)\]\[[2503.18570v1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2503.18570v1)\]\[[2504.10335v2](http://arxiv.org/abs/2504.10335v2)\]\[[2403.10939v1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2403.10939v1)\]\[[2103.08052v2](http://arxiv.org/abs/2103.08052v2)\]\[[2204.05814v1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2204.05814v1)\]\[[2307.16779v1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2307.16779v1)\].
