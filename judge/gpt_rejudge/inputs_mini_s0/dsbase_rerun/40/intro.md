## Related Works

Zero-Knowledge Proofs (ZKPs) enable a prover to convince a verifier of a statement's validity without revealing any additional information, providing privacy in applications such as blockchains, verifiable machine learning, and electronic voting \[[2412.12481v1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2412.12481v1)\]. Despite their growing importance, ZKPs have historically been hindered by the high computational cost of proof generation, limiting practical adoption \[[2412.12481v1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2412.12481v1)\]. Recent research has focused on accelerating key ZKP primitives, notably Multi-Scalar Multiplication (MSM) and Number Theoretic Transform (NTT), using specialized hardware such as FPGAs, GPUs, and ASICs, to reduce proof generation time.

One significant line of work is the FPGA-based acceleration of zk-SNARK provers, emphasizing MSM computations that dominate zk-SNARK performance. The if-ZKP framework uses Intel FPGA architectures and highly optimized modular arithmetic IP libraries to exploit parallelism in MSM, achieving 110× to 150× speedups over reference software implementations, including support for common elliptic curves like BLS12-381 and BN128 \[[2412.12481v1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2412.12481v1)\]. Complementing this, SZKP presents an ASIC design that accelerates the entire proof generation process, leveraging structured dataflows for both NTT and MSM operations. SZKP achieves conservative speedups exceeding 400× compared to CPU implementations and outperforms existing ASIC and GPU accelerators by factors of 3× and 12× respectively \[[2408.05890v1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2408.05890v1)\]. These works demonstrate the critical role of optimized MSM and NTT hardware acceleration in enabling scalable, high-throughput ZKP systems.

While these accelerators achieve notable improvements, many prior solutions focus on specific primitives rather than entire protocols. Additionally, some require trusted setups that limit their applicability in decentralized or multi-verifier environments, or produce larger proofs that increase verification overhead. The HyperPlonk protocol, which supports a one-time, universal setup alongside small proof sizes, addresses these limitations and is well-suited for publicly verifiable, consensus-based systems \[[2412.12481v1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2412.12481v1)\]. However, full-protocol hardware acceleration for HyperPlonk has not been extensively explored until the present work.

Beyond MSM and NTT, ZK-friendly cryptographic hash functions optimized for zero-knowledge contexts have also been investigated. AMAZE provides a hardware-optimized implementation of the MiMC hash function, a ZK-friendly primitive with simple algebraic structure that facilitates efficient ZKP computations. AMAZE targets resource-constrained edge devices, offering multiple power-performance tradeoffs and outperforming standard CPU implementations by over 13×, thus enabling practical ZK hashing in constrained environments \[[2411.06350v1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2411.06350v1)\]. Integrating such cryptographic building blocks into hardware accelerators is essential for end-to-end ZKP efficiency.

ZKPs have also been extended to complex applications such as verifiable machine learning. For example, zkDL presents efficient zero-knowledge proofs tailored for deep learning training, addressing challenges of non-linear computations typically difficult to verify succinctly. This line of work demonstrates the expanding applicability of ZKPs beyond simple statements to privacy-preserving AI \[[1801.06027v2](http://arxiv.org/abs/1801.06027v2)\]. Hardware acceleration methodologies like DNNExplorer facilitate customized AI accelerator design, which can synergize with ZKP frameworks requiring efficient neural network verifications \[[2104.02251v1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2104.02251v1)\].

In terms of hardware acceleration approaches, tools like DNNExplorer support automated benchmarking and design space exploration for AI accelerators, which is critical for identifying bottlenecks and optimizing performance and efficiency in domain-specific hardware platforms \[[2104.02251v1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2104.02251v1)\]. Similarly, LLM4SecHW explores the application of domain-specific large language models for hardware debugging, underscoring the importance of specialized tooling in hardware design workflows to improve accelerator reliability \[[2401.16448v1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2401.16448v1)\].

Quantum zero-knowledge proof systems represent a parallel research avenue, where the complexity of quantum states requires novel proof constructs, such as commit-and-open protocols and proofs of quantum knowledge, expanding the theoretical landscape of zero-knowledge proofs beyond classical systems \[[1911.07782v3](http://arxiv.org/abs/1911.07782v3)\]\[[0801.4917v1](http://arxiv.org/abs/0801.4917v1)\].

In summary, prior research has made significant progress in accelerating key ZKP primitives, optimizing ZK-friendly cryptographic components, and extending ZKPs to advanced applications like machine learning and quantum computing. However, many existing accelerators either target parts of the proof system or face tradeoffs in setup requirements and proof sizes. The zkSpeed accelerator advances the state-of-the-art by providing a full-chip architecture that accelerates the entire HyperPlonk protocol, including SumCheck and MSM operations, achieving speedups up to 801× over CPU baselines while maintaining small proof sizes and a universal setup, thus addressing critical scalability and applicability challenges in ZKP deployment \[[2412.12481v1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2412.12481v1)\].
