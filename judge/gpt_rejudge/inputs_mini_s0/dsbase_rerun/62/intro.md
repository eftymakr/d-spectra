## Related Works

Generative retrieval has recently emerged as a transformative paradigm in information retrieval, seeking to replace traditional search index data structures with a unified large-scale neural network. This approach enables direct generation of document identifiers conditioned on user queries via corpus-specific constrained decoding, promising improved efficiency and seamless integration with large language models (LLMs) \[[2504.05181v2](http://arxiv.org/abs/2504.05181v2)\]. The paradigm’s ability to generalize to out-of-distribution corpora has drawn significant research interest, focusing on both theoretical foundations and practical decoding strategies.

A key challenge in generative retrieval is the alignment between token-level generation and document-level relevance. Lightweight Direct Document Relevance Optimization (DDRO) addresses this by directly optimizing for document-level relevance through pairwise ranking, bypassing the complexity and instability of reinforcement learning-based reward modeling. DDRO demonstrated substantial gains in retrieval effectiveness on benchmarks such as MS MARCO and Natural Questions, highlighting the benefits of aligning generation objectives with relevance estimation \[[2210.02068v3](http://arxiv.org/abs/2210.02068v3)\]. Complementarily, methods like Nonparametric Decoding (Np Decoding) have extended generative models by integrating external memory through contextualized vocabulary embeddings, enhancing capacity and improving zero-shot retrieval performance across diverse datasets \[[2404.14600v1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2404.14600v1)\].

Decoding strategies, particularly beam search, are critical in generative retrieval. Prior work has identified inherent limitations of autoregressive beam search under constraints, noting that marginal token distributions often do not yield optimal document retrieval performance \[[2504.05181v2](http://arxiv.org/abs/2504.05181v2)\]. This observation aligns with innovations such as Planning Ahead in Generative Retrieval (PAG), which introduces simultaneous decoding of set-based and sequential document identifiers to better capture relevance signals, achieving significant improvements in accuracy and efficiency over standard beam search \[[2503.18050v1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2503.18050v1)\]. Similarly, constrained decoding techniques like (G)I-DLE leverage KL divergence minimization to preserve the model’s conditional output distribution while excluding undesirable tokens, reducing output variance and improving generation quality under constraints \[[1705.06979v2](http://arxiv.org/abs/1705.06979v2)\].

Retrieval-Augmented Generation (RAG) systems, which combine retrieval with generative LLMs, also intersect with generative retrieval research. Hybrid retrieval methods blending semantic search and query-based strategies have set new benchmarks on IR datasets, emphasizing the importance of effective retrieval components in scaled generative Q&A systems \[[2404.07220v2](http://arxiv.org/abs/2404.07220v2)\]. Learned sparse retrieval approaches, such as DiSCo, distill knowledge from multiple LLM teachers into efficient retrievers that unify retrieval and context modeling by distilling similarity scores rather than mere embeddings, improving both in-domain and out-of-domain conversational search tasks \[[2410.14609v2](http://arxiv.org/abs/2410.14609v2)\].

From a theoretical perspective, the analysis of errors induced by constraints in generative retrieval has focused on quantifying discrepancies between ground-truth and model-predicted step-wise marginal distributions via Kullback-Leibler (KL) divergence. Such analyses establish lower bounds on retrieval error attributable to constraint effects, providing a principled basis for understanding limitations in constrained autoregressive generation \[[2504.05181v2](http://arxiv.org/abs/2504.05181v2)\]. Broader studies on KL divergence-based losses have proposed generalized formulations to alleviate asymmetric optimization bias and incorporate class-wise global information, which may inform refined objectives for generative retrieval training \[[2010.10789v1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2010.10789v1)\]. Additionally, convergence properties of sampling algorithms under KL divergence, as studied in the context of Inexact Langevin Algorithms, contribute to understanding how generative models approximate target relevance distributions \[[2410.14609v2](http://arxiv.org/abs/2410.14609v2)\].

Cross-modality retrieval work also informs generative retrieval through joint embedding learning and pairwise ranking loss optimization, enabling retrieval across heterogeneous data types via maximally correlated projections \[[1705.06979v2](http://arxiv.org/abs/1705.06979v2)\]. While not directly focused on generative retrieval, these approaches share methodological parallels in optimizing retrieval objectives end-to-end.

In summary, existing literature converges on addressing the challenges of constrained decoding, relevance alignment, and generalization in generative retrieval. Advances in direct optimization methods, enhanced decoding strategies, hybrid retrieval systems, and theoretical grounding via KL divergence analysis collectively lay the foundation for future improvements in robustness and generalizability of generative retrieval models \[[2504.05181v2](http://arxiv.org/abs/2504.05181v2)\]\[[2210.02068v3](http://arxiv.org/abs/2210.02068v3)\]\[[2404.14600v1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2404.14600v1)\]\[[2503.18050v1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2503.18050v1)\]\[[1705.06979v2](http://arxiv.org/abs/1705.06979v2)\]\[[2410.14609v2](http://arxiv.org/abs/2410.14609v2)\]\[[2010.10789v1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2010.10789v1)\]\[[2404.07220v2](http://arxiv.org/abs/2404.07220v2)\].
