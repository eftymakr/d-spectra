## Related Works

Understanding the evolution of feature representations across layers in large language models (LLMs) is a critical avenue toward improving model interpretability, robustness, and efficient adaptation. Prior investigations predominantly relied on data-dependent analyses of fine-tuned models, which limited their applicability to post-hoc interpretability and did not reveal intrinsic properties of pre-trained LLMs \[[2502.16722v1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2502.16722v1)\]. Our approach, focusing on a data-oblivious analysis of pre-fine-tuned models using Centered Kernel Alignment (CKA), aligns with ongoing efforts to move beyond purely empirical observations toward theoretically grounded understanding of representation dynamics \[[1203.0550v3](http://arxiv.org/abs/1203.0550v3)\]\[[2408.08670v1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2408.08670v1)\].

Centered Kernel Alignment has emerged as a prominent tool to quantify similarity between kernel representations and to analyze how feature spaces evolve during training and fine-tuning. Originally developed for kernel learning and alignment tasks \[[1203.0550v3](http://arxiv.org/abs/1203.0550v3)\], CKA has been applied to study layer-wise representation similarity in neural networks, revealing important transitions in feature spaces. Extensions to Gaussian RBF kernels have further characterized CKA behavior in different bandwidth regimes, shedding light on the sensitivity of representation geometry to kernel parameters \[[2112.09305v1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2112.09305v1)\]. Our use of CKA to identify intrinsic critical layers in pre-fine-tuned LLMs builds on these advances by linking alignment shifts to layers most affected during fine-tuning.

Layer importance and sensitivity during fine-tuning have been highlighted in several studies across different model architectures. In transformer-based models, early layers tend to retain general-purpose features, while middle and later layers progressively specialize toward task-specific representations \[[2502.16722v1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2502.16722v1)\]. This layered progression has been observed both in language and vision transformers, motivating adaptive fine-tuning strategies that prioritize computational resources on critical layers \[[2408.08670v1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2408.08670v1)\]\[[2102.03983v1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2102.03983v1)\]. For instance, partial fine-tuning methods selectively update certain layers or apply differential learning rates to preserve transferability while enabling adaptation, demonstrating improvements in few-shot and domain adaptation scenarios \[[2102.03983v1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2102.03983v1)\]\[[2310.15105v4](http://arxiv.org/abs/2310.15105v4)\]. Our findings that layers exhibiting significant representation shifts during pre-fine-tuning also undergo larger fine-tuning updates corroborate these insights and suggest a principled basis for selective layer adaptation.

Spectral analysis of representation changes reveals that top principal components drive the observed shifts, encoding semantic transitions such as moving from rationale to conclusion stages in reasoning tasks. This aligns with prior work identifying principal components as carriers of semantic and syntactic information within deep models \[[2305.19798v2](http://arxiv.org/abs/2305.19798v2)\]. Understanding how these components evolve offers a mechanistic explanation for feature dynamics and guides the design of fine-tuning methods that emphasize critical semantic directions \[[2409.01035v5](http://arxiv.org/abs/2409.01035v5)\]. Moreover, kernel-based perspectives on fine-tuning dynamics, including Neural Tangent Kernel analyses, support the interpretation of fine-tuning as modifying specific functional subspaces within the model \[[2210.05643v4](http://arxiv.org/abs/2210.05643v4)\].

Practical applications of these insights include efficient domain adaptation and robustness enhancement. Fine-tuning critical layers yields greater loss reductions compared to non-critical layers, consistent with adaptive layer selection approaches that reduce computational overhead without sacrificing performance \[[2408.08670v1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2408.08670v1)\]\[[2210.05643v4](http://arxiv.org/abs/2210.05643v4)\]. Conversely, freezing these critical layers can mitigate backdoor attack success rates by up to 40%, echoing findings that selective freezing and parameter-efficient fine-tuning techniques improve model robustness against adversarial manipulations \[[2501.19389v4](http://arxiv.org/abs/2501.19389v4)\]\[[math/0206136v2](http://arxiv.org/abs/math/0206136v2)\]. Such dual utility underscores the importance of identifying intrinsic critical layers in pre-fine-tuned models.

Beyond representation analysis, recent work in parameter-efficient fine-tuning (PEFT) has explored methods such as LoRA and federated sketching LoRA to reduce resource demands during adaptation, particularly in heterogeneous or resource-constrained environments \[[2501.19389v4](http://arxiv.org/abs/2501.19389v4)\]\[[2409.01035v5](http://arxiv.org/abs/2409.01035v5)\]. These approaches complement our findings by emphasizing the role of targeted parameter updates aligned with task-specific directions. Adaptive compute allocation strategies that dynamically assign training budgets to layers further demonstrate that not all layers contribute equally during fine-tuning, reinforcing the rationale for focusing on critical layers \[[2408.08670v1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2408.08670v1)\].

Lastly, the broader trend of leveraging kernel methods for understanding and improving LLM fine-tuning is supported by theoretical and empirical studies showing that kernel-based dynamics underpin much of the observed adaptation behavior in large-scale models \[[1203.0550v3](http://arxiv.org/abs/1203.0550v3)\]\[[2210.05643v4](http://arxiv.org/abs/2210.05643v4)\]. Our contribution extends this kernel-centric view by applying CKA to pre-fine-tuned transformer layers, thereby identifying intrinsic properties predictive of fine-tuning sensitivity and downstream task adaptability.

In summary, our work situates itself at the intersection of kernel-based representation analysis, layer-wise understanding of transformer adaptation, and practical fine-tuning strategies aimed at efficiency and robustness. It complements and extends existing literature by providing a data-oblivious, theoretically motivated method to identify and exploit critical layers in LLMs prior to fine-tuning.
