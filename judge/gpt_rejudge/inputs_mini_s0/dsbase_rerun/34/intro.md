## Related Works

Fund allocation in financial domains critically depends on effective time series forecasting and the integration of uncertainty into decision-making processes. Traditional methods often adopt a Predict-then-Optimize (PnO) approach, where forecasting and optimization are treated as separate steps. While straightforward, such decoupled strategies suffer from a goal mismatch issue because the forecasting model is optimized for accuracy rather than the downstream allocation objective, leading to suboptimal decisions \[[2407.02641v1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2407.02641v1)\]\[[1810.08418v2](http://arxiv.org/abs/1810.08418v2)\]. This misalignment motivates the exploration of end-to-end frameworks that jointly optimize forecasting and allocation objectives to better align predictions with decision performance \[[2306.12086v2](http://arxiv.org/abs/2306.12086v2)\]\[[1211.4108v1](http://arxiv.org/abs/1211.4108v1)\].

Recent advances in time series forecasting have emphasized capturing inter-series relational structures and uncertainty estimation. Graph-based models leverage stochastic correlations to learn underlying dependencies among multiple time series, improving both accuracy and calibration of forecasts \[[2407.02641v1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2407.02641v1)\]. Similarly, probabilistic models using Implicit Quantile Networks or bivariate jump components have been proposed to better characterize the distributional properties and dependency structures in financial time series, such as electricity prices, enhancing the reliability of forecasts under uncertainty \[[2107.03743v1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2107.03743v1)\]\[[1810.08418v2](http://arxiv.org/abs/1810.08418v2)\].

Uncertainty calibration remains a key challenge. Bayesian approaches and Monte Carlo methods provide credible intervals but can be computationally expensive or limited to specific model families \[[2407.06567v3](http://arxiv.org/abs/2407.06567v3)\]\[[1809.09588v5](http://arxiv.org/abs/1809.09588v5)\]\[[2106.09305v3](http://arxiv.org/abs/2106.09305v3)\]. Alternative calibration techniques, including conformal prediction and adaptive uncertainty modeling, have been introduced to provide well-calibrated forecasts agnostic to the base forecasting model, which is critical for robust financial decision-making \[[2407.02641v1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2407.02641v1)\]\[[2407.06567v3](http://arxiv.org/abs/2407.06567v3)\]\[[2502.18410v2](http://arxiv.org/abs/2502.18410v2)\]. The RTS-PnO framework’s adaptive uncertainty calibration addresses this challenge by not assuming particular forecasting architectures, enhancing flexibility.

Deep learning architectures have been widely applied to financial time series due to their ability to model complex temporal dynamics. Novel architectures such as SCINet employ recursive downsample-convolve-interact layers to extract multi-resolution temporal features, outperforming traditional convolutional and Transformer-based models \[[2106.09305v3](http://arxiv.org/abs/2106.09305v3)\]. Likewise, the TSKANMixer model builds on MLP-Mixer architectures enhanced by Kolmogorov-Arnold Networks for improved spatial-temporal dependency modeling \[[2502.18410v2](http://arxiv.org/abs/2502.18410v2)\]. These models contribute to more accurate and expressive representations, which when combined with optimization-aware training, translate to improved allocation performance.

Self-supervised contrastive learning (SSCL) has also shown promise in enhancing time series representations by emphasizing relevant temporal patterns such as scale and periodicity, which are crucial for financial forecasting \[[2306.12086v2](http://arxiv.org/abs/2306.12086v2)\]. This aligns with the need for end-to-end training frameworks that can prioritize forecasting features most relevant for allocation tasks.

Large Language Models (LLMs) and foundation models have recently been explored for financial time series forecasting, demonstrating strong generalization capabilities even with limited data through few-shot or zero-shot learning \[[2410.19025v1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2410.19025v1)\]\[[2407.06567v3](http://arxiv.org/abs/2407.06567v3)\]. These models can capture complex temporal dependencies and multi-source information, which supports sophisticated decision-making in financial contexts. However, their integration into risk-aware allocation frameworks remains nascent, representing an opportunity for future research.

In financial applications, integrating forecasting and allocation is not only about accuracy but also about managing risks and nonlinearities inherent in financial systems. Econophysics-inspired models highlight the importance of capturing nonlinear dynamic volatilities and market shocks for reliable risk assessment \[[1211.4108v1](http://arxiv.org/abs/1211.4108v1)\]\[[2410.16214v1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2410.16214v1)\]. Multi-agent frameworks using LLMs have been proposed to incorporate systematic risk control and self-critiquing mechanisms for enhanced investment decisions, emphasizing risk-awareness in financial allocation \[[2407.06567v3](http://arxiv.org/abs/2407.06567v3)\].

Finally, evaluation metrics that consider both point-wise accuracy and the correlation structure between time series, such as the energy score, are critical for assessing joint forecast quality in multivariate financial settings \[[1810.08418v2](http://arxiv.org/abs/1810.08418v2)\]. The combination of precise uncertainty quantification, objective alignment through end-to-end training, and model-agnostic adaptability forms the foundation upon which RTS-PnO builds, addressing key gaps in existing literature.

In summary, the landscape of financial time series forecasting and fund allocation has evolved towards integrated, uncertainty-aware, and model-flexible approaches. RTS-PnO contributes to this trajectory by unifying end-to-end objective alignment with adaptive uncertainty calibration, applicable across diverse forecasting models and financial datasets \[[2407.02641v1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2407.02641v1)\]\[[2107.03743v1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2107.03743v1)\]\[[1810.08418v2](http://arxiv.org/abs/1810.08418v2)\]\[[2306.12086v2](http://arxiv.org/abs/2306.12086v2)\]\[[2410.19025v1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2410.19025v1)\]\[[2407.06567v3](http://arxiv.org/abs/2407.06567v3)\]\[[2106.09305v3](http://arxiv.org/abs/2106.09305v3)\]\[[2502.18410v2](http://arxiv.org/abs/2502.18410v2)\].
