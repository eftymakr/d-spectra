## Related Works

Active learning (AL) techniques have been extensively developed to reduce annotation costs by selectively querying the most informative samples for labeling, thereby improving model performance with fewer labeled instances \[[2302.08893v4](http://arxiv.org/abs/2302.08893v4)\]. Traditional AL methods, however, often face limitations in distributed settings due to restricted data diversity and annotation budgets. Federated Active Learning (FAL) has emerged as a promising paradigm that combines AL with federated learning (FL), enabling collaborative model training and data selection while preserving client data privacy \[[2408.09545v2](http://arxiv.org/abs/2408.09545v2)\]\[[2504.11216v2](http://arxiv.org/abs/2504.11216v2)\]. Despite this advancement, existing FAL approaches frequently overlook the critical issue of client data heterogeneity, which can cause fluctuations in both local and global model parameters and ultimately degrade overall accuracy.

The challenge of data heterogeneity—clients holding non-i.i.d. and imbalanced data distributions—is well recognized in FL research. FedDiverse, for instance, tackles statistical data heterogeneity by proposing a diversity-driven client selection algorithm that encourages collaboration among clients with complementary data distributions, improving model robustness and convergence speed with low communication overhead \[[2504.11216v2](http://arxiv.org/abs/2504.11216v2)\]. Similarly, frameworks such as the feature-oriented regulation method ($\Psi$-Net) address structural misalignment in heterogeneous federated settings by enforcing feature-level alignment across diverse neural network architectures, enhancing convergence and accuracy \[[1910.04956v2](http://arxiv.org/abs/1910.04956v2)\]. Other approaches propose client-specific model parameters or conditioning to tackle distributional differences, improving personalization in FL \[[2011.06393v1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2011.06393v1)\]\[[2007.04806v1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2007.04806v1)\].

Client adaptation techniques have also been proposed to address heterogeneity in FL by learning client-specific conditioning units, which help models better fit local data distributions, especially when these distributions vary widely \[[2007.04806v1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2007.04806v1)\]. Complementary to this, scheduling strategies like FedCompass address client imbalance by adaptively assigning training tasks based on clients’ computational capabilities, thus improving training efficiency and convergence \[[1910.04956v2](http://arxiv.org/abs/1910.04956v2)\]. While these methods focus on improving model training under heterogeneity, they typically do not integrate active learning mechanisms to selectively query informative samples for annotation.

Personalized federated learning methods such as WAFFLE and FedCor improve convergence and model performance via weighted averaging and correlation-based client selection, respectively, yet they do not explicitly incorporate active learning for sample selection \[[2011.06393v1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2011.06393v1)\]\[[2004.11794v2](http://arxiv.org/abs/2004.11794v2)\]. Likewise, model distillation methods for personalized FL enable clients to maintain distinct local models while contributing to a global consensus, offering privacy and performance benefits but without leveraging active learning strategies \[[2007.04806v1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2007.04806v1)\].

Integrating active learning into FL remains a nascent area. Most existing FAL methods do not explicitly model or track epistemic uncertainty or variation across training epochs, which is crucial for identifying samples near decision boundaries that could most improve model generalization. Furthermore, many FAL approaches lack mechanisms to calibrate decision boundaries in the presence of inaccurate local models caused by data heterogeneity. Techniques that combine uncertainty-based sampling with model calibration and client heterogeneity awareness are therefore needed.

In related domains, asynchronous and vertical FL methods have been proposed to enhance communication efficiency and handle feature-partitioned data across clients, which can indirectly influence data selection strategies by affecting model updates and privacy guarantees \[[2007.06081v1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2007.06081v1)\]\[[2011.06803v1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2011.06803v1)\]. Privacy-preserving FL research also emphasizes the importance of protecting sensitive information in distributed learning without sacrificing model accuracy and communication efficiency \[[2402.00342v2](http://arxiv.org/abs/2402.00342v2)\]. Moreover, methods addressing catastrophic forgetting in FL and robust aggregation against adversarial clients contribute to model stability when faced with diverse and potentially malicious data sources \[[1910.07796v1](http://arxiv.org/abs/1910.07796v1)\]\[[2006.13041v2](http://arxiv.org/abs/2006.13041v2)\].

Active learning in streaming data contexts offers insights into selecting informative samples in real-time, which can be adapted to federated settings where data and clients continuously evolve \[[2302.08893v4](http://arxiv.org/abs/2302.08893v4)\]. Finally, recent advances in client selection and sample querying that consider data distribution heterogeneity and model uncertainty provide a foundation for frameworks like CHASe, which specifically track epistemic variations, calibrate decision boundaries, and optimize data selection efficiency for heterogeneous federated active learning environments.

In summary, while significant progress has been made in addressing data heterogeneity, client adaptation, and privacy in federated learning, the integration of active learning strategies that explicitly account for client heterogeneity and model uncertainty remains underexplored. CHASe advances this field by focusing on epistemic variation tracking, boundary calibration, and efficient sample selection mechanisms tailored for heterogeneous federated learning settings, addressing a crucial gap in current literature.
