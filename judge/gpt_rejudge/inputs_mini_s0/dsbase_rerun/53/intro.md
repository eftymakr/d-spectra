## Related Works

Sequential recommendation has witnessed substantial progress through the integration of attention mechanisms and advanced sequence modeling techniques. Traditional approaches predominantly focus on transitional dependencies among user actions but often neglect temporal and contextual nuances, which are critical for capturing evolving user preferences. For example, the Contextualized Temporal Attention Mechanism explicitly models the influence of historical user actions by dynamically weighing their importance based not only on the action type but also on the time and context in which they occurred, thereby refining the representation of user interests over time \[[2404.13878v2](http://arxiv.org/abs/2404.13878v2)\]. This aligns with the proposed preference-aware attention in STAR-Rec, which similarly aims to capture both consistent item relationships and diverse user preferences.

Handling noisy or less informative historical interactions is another challenge addressed by recent work. Multi-level sequence denoising via cross-signal contrastive learning has been proposed to mitigate noise impact by combining soft and hard denoising strategies while extracting both short- and long-term user interests with target awareness \[[2404.13878v2](http://arxiv.org/abs/2404.13878v2)\]. Such approaches enhance the robustness of sequential models and can complement attention mechanisms that otherwise might overfit noisy signals.

State-space models (SSMs) have been gaining attention for their efficiency in processing variable-length sequences with linear complexity. Unlike self-attention-based models, which are computationally expensive for long sequences and hinder incremental inference, linear recurrent units and SSMs provide fast inference and scalable training while maintaining strong performance \[[2310.02367v2](http://arxiv.org/abs/2310.02367v2)\]. This makes SSMs suitable for capturing temporal dependencies through compressed latent states, as done in STAR-Rec. The theoretical unification of attention and SSMs leverages the strengths of both: attention models capture similarity and diversity in item relations, while SSMs model temporal dynamics effectively.

Incorporating external knowledge and side information is another dimension explored to enhance sequential recommendations. Knowledge-aware attentive models leverage knowledge graphs to enrich item representations by capturing higher-order entity relations, thereby improving the modeling of both short- and long-term user interests \[[2012.03323v3](http://arxiv.org/abs/2012.03323v3)\]. Similarly, leveraging heterogeneous metadata with multi-modal attention mechanisms has proven effective for cold-start problems, indicating the value of diverse auxiliary inputs \[[2109.11059v2](http://arxiv.org/abs/2109.11059v2)\]\[[2305.12976v1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2305.12976v1)\].

Mixture-of-experts (MoE) architectures address the heterogeneity of user behaviors by adaptively routing different interaction patterns to specialized expert modules. This paradigm enables models to capture both focused category-specific browsing and broad exploration behaviors, reflecting the diversity in user sequences. Efficient expert pruning and routing strategies have been developed to maintain computational feasibility while enhancing performance \[[2402.14800v2](http://arxiv.org/abs/2402.14800v2)\]\[[2410.17954v2](http://arxiv.org/abs/2410.17954v2)\]. The adaptive gating and routing principles in MoE frameworks underpin STAR-Rec’s sequence-level MoE design, which dynamically assigns behavioral patterns to experts, improving representation and recommendation quality.

Automated model design and hardware-aware optimization have been explored for recommender systems to jointly optimize architecture and system efficiency. AutoML-based supernet approaches search across a large space of operators and hardware configurations to yield models that balance performance and computational cost, achieving notable improvements in energy efficiency and FLOPs reduction \[[2411.07569v1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2411.07569v1)\]. Although not central to STAR-Rec’s algorithmic design, such advances are relevant for practical deployment and scalability considerations.

Lastly, efforts to incorporate dynamic user preferences using temporal attention and diffusion-aware models have been made in domains like news recommendation, where user interests and content popularity evolve rapidly. Models employing multi-level attention combined with temporal and social diffusion signals demonstrate the importance of fine-grained temporal modeling \[[2210.01942v1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2210.01942v1)\]. This reinforces the need for architectures like STAR-Rec that combine temporal dynamics and preference-aware attention to address diverse, time-varying user behaviors.

In summary, STAR-Rec synthesizes key advances from preference-aware attention mechanisms, efficient temporal modeling via state-space methods, and adaptive mixture-of-experts architectures. This integrated approach addresses critical limitations in handling diverse interaction patterns and variable-length sequences, positioning it as a comprehensive solution in sequential recommendation research.
