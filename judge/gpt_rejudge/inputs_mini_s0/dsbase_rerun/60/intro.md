## Related Works

Model calibration is critical for ensuring that predicted probabilities or scores accurately reflect true likelihoods, which is especially important in ranking models where both relative order and absolute accuracy matter. Traditional calibration methods often rely on predefined, order-preserving transformation functions such as piece-wise linear mappings to adjust predictions. While these methods maintain the relative order, their limited expressiveness restricts their ability to model complex, nonlinear calibration functions, thus constraining calibration performance in real-world applications \[[2406.08010v1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2406.08010v1)\].

Multi-objective approaches to calibrated ranking combine pointwise calibration losses with ranking losses to jointly optimize absolute value accuracy and relative ordering. However, these methods typically require aggregating the entire candidate list in a single mini-batch, which limits data shuffling and may degrade training effectiveness. Moreover, applying conflicting calibration and ranking objectives to a single prediction can lead to suboptimal trade-offs between calibration and ranking quality \[[2406.08010v1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2406.08010v1)\]. These challenges motivate the need for more flexible calibration functions that can model complex monotonic transformations without sacrificing ranking structure.

Neural network-based calibration methods have been explored to improve flexibility and modeling power. Monotonic neural networks, which enforce monotonicity by constraining weights or activation functions, enable invertible transformations suitable for calibration tasks. However, such constraints can limit expressiveness. The Unconstrained Monotonic Neural Network (UMNN) architecture addresses this by ensuring monotonicity through enforcing strict positivity of the derivative, realized by a free-form neural network producing positive outputs. This design supports learning arbitrary monotonic functions with greater expressiveness and has been demonstrated effective in density estimation and variational inference scenarios \[[1908.05164v3](http://arxiv.org/abs/1908.05164v3)\]. Applying UMNNs as calibrators relaxes the rigid forms of traditional methods, enabling more accurate adjustments of predictions while preserving order.

In personalized ranking contexts, calibration efforts have focused on post-processing score mappings using parametric functions such as Gaussian or Gamma calibrations to better estimate true preference probabilities without degrading ranking accuracy. These approaches, combined with unbiased empirical risk minimization frameworks, improve calibration performance on biased interaction datasets, highlighting the importance of accurate probability estimation beyond ranking order \[[2112.07428v2](http://arxiv.org/abs/2112.07428v2)\]. However, these parametric calibrators are typically limited in flexibility compared to neural approaches like UMNN.

Confidence calibration for multiclass classification has also been extensively studied, where maximum predicted class probabilities often poorly represent true correctness likelihoods. Novel methods have reframed multiclass calibration into binary surrogate tasks to enable efficient application of standard calibration techniques, enhancing calibration in large-class problems \[[2411.02988v2](http://arxiv.org/abs/2411.02988v2)\]. Though focused on classification, such insights underline the significance of tailored calibration losses and transformations to meet problem-specific constraints.

More broadly, surrogate loss functions for complex multiclass and subset ranking problems have been analyzed via the convex calibration dimension framework, which characterizes the minimal prediction space size needed for convex calibrated surrogates to exist. This theoretical lens reveals inherent limitations in designing convex calibration functions for ranking losses, reinforcing the need for flexible calibration architectures capable of overcoming these constraints \[[1408.2764v2](http://arxiv.org/abs/1408.2764v2)\].

In the realm of metric learning and ranking, structured ranking losses that incorporate all instances in a batch have been proposed to address slow convergence and limited structure in pairwise or triplet losses. These losses aim to preserve intraclass similarity structure rather than compressing it excessively, which can be viewed as a form of regularization that complements calibration objectives \[[1903.03238v8](http://arxiv.org/abs/1903.03238v8)\].

Finally, contrastive loss methods in natural language processing have highlighted the importance of data shuffling and batch-wise optimization for improving ranking and calibration of sentence embeddings, indicating that training dynamics are crucial for successful calibration in ranking models \[[2110.15725v1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2110.15725v1)\].

In summary, existing calibration methods for ranking models often trade-off between expressiveness and order preservation, with traditional parametric or piece-wise functions lacking flexibility and constrained neural methods limiting modeling power. The UMNN approach offers a promising alternative by combining monotonicity guarantees with high expressiveness. Furthermore, introducing novel calibration-specific loss functions, such as Smooth Calibration Loss, can enforce necessary calibration conditions to optimize these flexible models effectively. These developments build on and extend prior work in calibrated ranking, monotonic neural networks, surrogate loss theory, and ranking loss design, addressing key limitations in current calibration methodologies \[[2406.08010v1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2406.08010v1)\]\[[1908.05164v3](http://arxiv.org/abs/1908.05164v3)\]\[[2112.07428v2](http://arxiv.org/abs/2112.07428v2)\]\[[2411.02988v2](http://arxiv.org/abs/2411.02988v2)\]\[[1408.2764v2](http://arxiv.org/abs/1408.2764v2)\]\[[1903.03238v8](http://arxiv.org/abs/1903.03238v8)\]\[[2110.15725v1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2110.15725v1)\].
