## Related Works

Collaborative filtering (CF) remains a cornerstone technique for recommender systems, with recent advances leveraging graph-structured data and representation learning to capture complex user-item interactions. Traditional matrix factorization methods have evolved to incorporate implicit feedback, but often fall short in modeling high-order relationships and suffer from sparsity issues \[[2505.11552v3](http://arxiv.org/abs/2505.11552v3)\]\[[2011.06807v2](http://arxiv.org/abs/2011.06807v2)\]. Graph neural networks (GNNs) have emerged as a powerful framework to address these shortcomings by explicitly modeling user-item interactions as bipartite or heterogeneous graphs, enabling embedding propagation that captures collaborative signals more effectively \[[2505.11552v3](http://arxiv.org/abs/2505.11552v3)\]\[[2011.06807v2](http://arxiv.org/abs/2011.06807v2)\]\[[2001.00267v1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2001.00267v1)\].

Neural Graph Collaborative Filtering (NGCF) introduced embedding propagation on the user-item bipartite graph to capture high-order connectivity, explicitly injecting collaborative signals into embeddings \[[2505.11552v3](http://arxiv.org/abs/2505.11552v3)\]. Building on this idea, Heterogeneous Graph Collaborative Filtering (HGCF) further extends the graph structure by integrating user-user similarity edges alongside user-item interactions, which alleviates sparsity and enriches the learned representations \[[2011.06807v2](http://arxiv.org/abs/2011.06807v2)\]. Multi-Graph Convolution Collaborative Filtering (Multi-GCCF) unifies multiple graphs—user-item, user-user, and item-item—into the embedding process, modeling both high-order connectivity and proximal information, and explicitly differentiating between user and item nodes during graph convolution \[[2001.00267v1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2001.00267v1)\]. These multi-graph approaches demonstrate that incorporating diverse relational information significantly enhances recommendation performance and addresses limitations of solely bipartite graph modeling \[[2011.06807v2](http://arxiv.org/abs/2011.06807v2)\]\[[2001.00267v1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2001.00267v1)\].

Graph Signal Processing (GSP) methods complement GNN-based CF models by interpreting user-item interaction data in the spectral domain. GSPRec advances this direction by incorporating item-item proximity derived from user interaction sequences, which captures intermediate-frequency components often ignored by standard low-pass graph filters. This spectral filtering approach selectively amplifies community-level preferences and popularity trends, yielding richer item representations and improved recommendation accuracy \[[1711.00146v1](http://arxiv.org/abs/1711.00146v1)\]. The coupling of graph construction and filter design in GSPRec highlights the importance of integrating both structural and spectral perspectives for effective embedding learning \[[1711.00146v1](http://arxiv.org/abs/1711.00146v1)\].

Despite the effectiveness of graph-based methods, the high computational and memory costs of continuous embeddings pose challenges for real-time recommendation systems. Representation binarization addresses these challenges by encoding latent features into compact binary sequences, substantially improving inference efficiency. Binarized Collaborative Filtering (BiCF) leverages graph convolutional networks to distill ranking information into binary embeddings, enabling efficient online recommendation with significantly reduced resource consumption \[[1906.01829v1](http://arxiv.org/abs/1906.01829v1)\]. However, binarization often leads to information loss and optimization difficulties, which can degrade model performance.

To mitigate these issues, recent works have proposed methods to better preserve information during binarization. For example, the continuous relaxation framework with stochastic penalty converts binary constrained optimization into a continuous problem that is easier to solve using standard optimizers like SGD or Adam, improving training stability and embedding quality \[[1906.01829v1](http://arxiv.org/abs/1906.01829v1)\]. Although these approaches enhance efficiency, they generally overlook the explicit modeling of information loss and the use of supervisory signals from pseudo-positive samples or synthesized embeddings, which can further improve binarized representation fidelity.

The incorporation of supervisory signals in binarized CF is an emerging trend. Our proposed BiGeaR++ builds on this by introducing fine-grained inference distillation and embedding sample synthesis to explicitly reduce information loss across binarization stages. This aligns with the broader recognition in CF literature that leveraging richer supervisory signals and multiple graph relations improves embedding quality and recommendation effectiveness \[[1906.01829v1](http://arxiv.org/abs/1906.01829v1)\]\[[2011.06807v2](http://arxiv.org/abs/2011.06807v2)\]\[[2001.00267v1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2001.00267v1)\].

Additionally, optimization methods play a crucial role in training complex deep learning models for CF. Advanced optimization techniques such as the Inertial Newton Algorithm (INNA) exploit loss geometry for efficient convergence, while multitask learning frameworks enable simultaneous optimization of related objectives, which can be beneficial for multi-target recommender systems \[[1905.12278v6](http://arxiv.org/abs/1905.12278v6)\]\[[1902.09154v1](http://arxiv.org/abs/1902.09154v1)\]. These techniques complement graph-based embedding learning and binarization by improving training efficiency and generalization.

In summary, prior work has established the importance of graph-based modeling for capturing collaborative signals and mitigating sparsity in recommender systems, as well as the potential of embedding binarization for efficient deployment. However, most existing binarization methods do not fully address the information loss inherent in binary representation learning or leverage supervisory signals from pseudo-positive and synthetic samples. Our work extends these lines by proposing a framework that explicitly tackles these issues, significantly enhancing binarized graph collaborative filtering performance.
