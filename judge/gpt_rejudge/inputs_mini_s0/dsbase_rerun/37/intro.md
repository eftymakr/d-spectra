## Related Works

Vision Transformers (ViTs) have become prominent in computer vision due to their ability to capture global dependencies using self-attention mechanisms, offering advantages over convolutional neural networks (CNNs) \[[2106.02277v1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2106.02277v1)\]. However, the quadratic complexity of self-attention with respect to input sequence length imposes significant computational and memory demands, especially when handling high-resolution images. This challenge has motivated algorithmic innovations such as the Glance-and-Gaze Transformer, which combines linear-complexity global attention with convolutional local context modeling to reduce complexity while maintaining accuracy \[[2106.02277v1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2106.02277v1)\]. Similarly, methods that unify low-rank and sparse approximations of attention, like ViTALiTy, achieve linearized attention computation, enabling faster and more energy-efficient inference on hardware \[[2211.05109v1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2211.05109v1)\].

Hardware acceleration of ViTs on resource-constrained devices, particularly FPGAs, has gained increasing attention. HeatViT introduces an adaptive token pruning framework that dynamically removes non-informative tokens to reduce computation and improve latency without sacrificing accuracy, achieving significant speedups on embedded FPGA platforms \[[2211.08110v2](http://arxiv.org/abs/2211.08110v2)\]. ViTA proposes a configurable FPGA accelerator targeting edge deployment of various ViT models, employing pipeline and MLP optimizations to reach high hardware utilization and low power consumption \[[2302.09108v1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2302.09108v1)\]. These efforts focus primarily on accelerating matrix multiplications and token-level computations but often leave complex nonlinear operations like Softmax and LayerNorm on general-purpose processors, causing communication overhead and limiting end-to-end acceleration.

Softmax and LayerNorm are critical yet challenging components for hardware acceleration due to their nonlinear and precision-sensitive nature. Several software-hardware co-design approaches have been proposed to address these bottlenecks. Softermax introduces a hardware-friendly Softmax design by using low-precision computations combined with online normalization to reduce energy consumption with minimal accuracy loss \[[2103.09301v1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2103.09301v1)\]. SoftmAP further pushes integer-only Softmax implementations using in-memory compute architectures, achieving orders of magnitude improvements in energy-delay product on LLM inference \[[2411.17847v1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2411.17847v1)\]. FLASH-D presents a mathematically equivalent reformulation of FlashAttention that hides the Softmax division within other nonlinear functions, reducing hardware area and power without sacrificing numerical stability or accuracy \[[2505.14201v1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2505.14201v1)\]. These approaches illustrate the critical need for novel arithmetic and algorithmic designs to efficiently implement Softmax on hardware.

LayerNorm has been studied from a geometric and algebraic perspective, revealing that it applies a sequence of linear projection, nonlinear scaling, and affine transformations that constrain outputs to a hyperellipsoid surface in high-dimensional space. This insight can guide hardware-friendly approximations and optimizations for LayerNorm implementations \[[2405.04134v1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2405.04134v1)\]. Despite this, most ViT accelerators still execute LayerNorm on CPUs or GPUs due to its complexity and sensitivity to quantization.

Model quantization and hardware-aware algorithm design are essential for bridging accuracy and efficiency in ViT acceleration. Surveys on ViT quantization emphasize specialized low-precision formats and co-designed hardware architectures tailored to the unique characteristics of ViTs, enabling significant reductions in model size and computational cost \[[2405.00314v1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2405.00314v1)\]. Approximate multipliers such as L-Mul, designed for emerging data formats like 8-bit floating-point, demonstrate potential for energy-efficient neural network inference by leveraging FPGA-specific primitives \[[2412.18948v1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2412.18948v1)\].

Hardware designs targeting the Transformer architecture more broadly have demonstrated that sharing resources between multi-head attention and feedforward networks, combined with optimized nonlinear function implementations, can yield substantial speedups over GPU baselines \[[2009.08605v1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2009.08605v1)\]. Online normalization techniques further reduce memory accesses in Softmax computations, enhancing throughput on actual hardware \[[1805.02867v2](http://arxiv.org/abs/1805.02867v2)\]. Analog circuits for Softmax have also been explored, offering linear scaling with input size and promising accuracy within a few percentage points error margin \[[2305.13649v1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2305.13649v1)\].

In summary, existing works have addressed ViT acceleration through algorithmic innovations in attention approximation, token pruning, and quantization, as well as hardware designs focusing on matrix multiplication and limited nonlinear operations. However, fully mapping all ViT operations—including Softmax and LayerNorm—onto FPGA with high efficiency and accuracy remains an open challenge. The proposed Microscaling Integer (MXInt) arithmetic format and corresponding hardware optimizations offer a novel approach to this problem by enabling end-to-end low-precision ViT inference on FPGAs, overcoming the limitations of prior methods that rely on CPU offloading for complex operations.
