## Related Works

Cross-domain Sequential Recommendation (CDSR) addresses the challenge of leveraging user interactions across multiple domains to improve recommendation accuracy and alleviate data sparsity inherent in single-domain settings \[[2012.00485v3](http://arxiv.org/abs/2012.00485v3)\]. Central to CDSR is the need to model user preferences by capturing both intra-sequence and inter-sequence item relationships across domains. Conventional CDSR methods predominantly rely on overlapping users who have interactions in multiple domains to establish cross-domain item connections. However, this reliance limits their practical applicability since overlapped users often constitute only a small fraction of real-world user bases \[[2401.04971v4](http://arxiv.org/abs/2401.04971v4)\]\[[2311.04590v6](http://arxiv.org/abs/2311.04590v6)\]\[[2410.13588v1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2410.13588v1)\]. This "overlap dilemma" motivates research into CDSR techniques capable of handling non-overlapping user scenarios.

To address the overlap issue, several federated learning frameworks have been proposed. For example, FedDCSR disentangles user sequence representations into domain-shared and domain-exclusive features and employs contrastive learning to enhance domain-specific knowledge without sharing raw user data, thus preserving privacy and reducing reliance on overlapped users \[[2309.08420v7](http://arxiv.org/abs/2309.08420v7)\]. Similarly, the Federated Mixture-of-Expert (FMoE-CDSR) framework enables knowledge transfer in non-overlapped user settings by sharing only model parameters rather than user data, addressing privacy and data availability constraints \[[2503.13254v1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2503.13254v1)\]. Prompt learning-based approaches like PLCR also tackle non-overlapping domains by learning domain-invariant and domain-specific representations without explicit domain alignment constraints, enhancing performance in scenarios without shared users or items \[[2304.04218v1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2304.04218v1)\].

Another key challenge in CDSR is effectively modeling the complex transitions present in mixed domain behavior sequences. Users’ preferences evolve not only across items but also through domain shifts and feedback types, which traditional models often overlook. To capture such dynamics, methods like $\text{Transition}^2$ employ transition-aware graph encoders that integrate domain and feedback transitions, utilizing multi-head self-attention and contrastive learning to improve representation learning \[[2408.08209v2](http://arxiv.org/abs/2408.08209v2)\]. Dual dynamic graph frameworks further enhance CDSR by constructing local and global graphs to model both intra-domain and inter-domain sequential transitions, combined with hybrid metric learning to alleviate data sparsity \[[2209.10163v1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2209.10163v1)\]. Mixed Information Flow networks explicitly model both behavioral and knowledge flows across domains, enabling adaptive utilization of cross-domain information to enrich sequence representations and improve recommendation accuracy \[[2502.15694v2](http://arxiv.org/abs/2502.15694v2)\].

Attention mechanisms have been widely adopted to capture nuanced user preferences in CDSR. Mixed Attention Networks utilize local and global attention modules to extract domain-specific and cross-domain sequential patterns, incorporating item similarity and user group prototypes to enhance cross-domain interest modeling \[[2311.08272v1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2311.08272v1)\]. Hierarchical attention fusion approaches integrate multimodal information, such as visual and textual embeddings generated by frozen CLIP models, to mimic human cognitive processing and jointly learn preferences across domains \[[2504.15085v5](http://arxiv.org/abs/2504.15085v5)\]\[[2304.03891v1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2304.03891v1)\]. These multimodal techniques leverage semantic information to better understand item characteristics and user interests.

The integration of Large Language Models (LLMs) into sequential recommendation has recently gained traction due to their strong semantic understanding and generalization capabilities. However, naive LLM-based sequential recommenders face challenges such as limited textual context, incomplete utilization of conventional sequential patterns, and complexity issues \[[2406.11156v4](http://arxiv.org/abs/2406.11156v4)\]. Frameworks like DELRec distill sequential patterns from traditional models into soft prompts, enabling LLMs to better comprehend and apply behavioral knowledge for improved recommendations \[[2406.11156v4](http://arxiv.org/abs/2406.11156v4)\]. This semantic bridging is particularly promising for overcoming the overlap dilemma by capturing item relationships beyond explicit user-item interactions. Moreover, LLM-based unified item representation modules facilitate learning domain-invariant embeddings, while trainable adapters with contrastive regularization help adapt LLMs effectively to CDSR tasks.

Notably, recent surveys highlight the importance of multidimensional fusion techniques and auxiliary learning strategies in CDSR, emphasizing the shift from flat single-domain models to stereoscopic, multi-granularity user preference modeling \[[2401.04971v4](http://arxiv.org/abs/2401.04971v4)\]. Furthermore, methods that disentangle user preferences into shared and domain-specific components show promise in extracting transferable knowledge even when shared items and overlapped users are scarce \[[2410.20580v2](http://arxiv.org/abs/2410.20580v2)\]. Contrastive learning objectives have also been employed to maximize mutual information between single- and cross-domain representations, enhancing the correlation and robustness of learned user preferences \[[2304.03891v1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2304.03891v1)\].

Despite advances, CDSR still faces challenges in open-world scenarios marked by long-tailed and cold-start users, data distribution shifts, and semantic gaps between domains. Adaptive frameworks incorporating multi-interest modeling and bias reduction techniques have been proposed to improve robustness against such real-world complexities \[[2311.04590v6](http://arxiv.org/abs/2311.04590v6)\]. Meanwhile, integrating multimodal data and leveraging LLMs’ semantic reasoning remain promising directions to address transition complexity and overlap dilemmas simultaneously.

In summary, existing CDSR research spans federated privacy-preserving methods, graph and attention-based transition modeling, multimodal fusion, and emerging LLM-enhanced frameworks. These collectively aim to reduce reliance on overlapped users, capture complex user behavior transitions, and leverage semantic item relationships to improve cross-domain recommendation performance \[[2012.00485v3](http://arxiv.org/abs/2012.00485v3)\]\[[2406.11156v4](http://arxiv.org/abs/2406.11156v4)\]\[[2502.15694v2](http://arxiv.org/abs/2502.15694v2)\]\[[2309.08420v7](http://arxiv.org/abs/2309.08420v7)\]\[[2503.13254v1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2503.13254v1)\]\[[2401.04971v4](http://arxiv.org/abs/2401.04971v4)\]\[[2504.15085v5](http://arxiv.org/abs/2504.15085v5)\]\[[2209.10163v1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2209.10163v1)\]\[[2410.20580v2](http://arxiv.org/abs/2410.20580v2)\]\[[2311.08272v1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2311.08272v1)\]\[[2311.04590v6](http://arxiv.org/abs/2311.04590v6)\]\[[2304.03891v1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2304.03891v1)\]\[[2408.08209v2](http://arxiv.org/abs/2408.08209v2)\]\[[2304.04218v1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2304.04218v1)\]\[[2410.13588v1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2410.13588v1)\].
