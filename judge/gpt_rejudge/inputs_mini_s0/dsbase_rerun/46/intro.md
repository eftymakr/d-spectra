## Related Works

Cross-domain sequential recommendation (CDSR) focuses on predicting user behaviors by leveraging sequential interactions across multiple domains to alleviate data sparsity and cold-start issues inherent in single-domain settings \[[2405.12473v3](http://arxiv.org/abs/2405.12473v3)\]\[[2401.04971v4](http://arxiv.org/abs/2401.04971v4)\]. Early CDSR methods predominantly utilized transformer-based architectures with self-attention mechanisms to model item dependencies within sequences \[[2405.12473v3](http://arxiv.org/abs/2405.12473v3)\]\[[2212.04120v1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2212.04120v1)\]. Despite their effectiveness in capturing intra-sequence correlations, these models often struggle to fully exploit cross-domain knowledge due to domain shifts and the complexity of transferring information between heterogeneous domains \[[2012.00485v3](http://arxiv.org/abs/2012.00485v3)\]\[[2311.04590v6](http://arxiv.org/abs/2311.04590v6)\].

To address these challenges, various approaches have incorporated domain-specific modules or enhanced attention mechanisms. For instance, works have designed behavior and knowledge transfer units to govern the flow of cross-domain information, enabling selective enrichment of user sequence representations \[[2012.00485v3](http://arxiv.org/abs/2012.00485v3)\]. Similarly, hierarchical attention fusion methods integrate multimodal item features, such as visual and textual embeddings generated by frozen CLIP models, to better capture cross-domain user preferences \[[2502.15694v2](http://arxiv.org/abs/2502.15694v2)\]\[[2504.15085v5](http://arxiv.org/abs/2504.15085v5)\]. Other studies have proposed mixed attention networks that combine local and global attention to extract domain-specific and cross-domain sequential patterns, improving recommendation accuracy without relying heavily on overlapped users \[[2311.08272v1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2311.08272v1)\].

User feedback and transitions across domains have also been explored to refine CDSR models. Some methods model domain and feedback transitions using graph encoders with weighted edges to capture complex user behavior patterns and feedback signals, enhancing the representation learning for cross-domain recommendations \[[2408.08209v2](http://arxiv.org/abs/2408.08209v2)\]. Furthermore, modeling negative feedback in sequential recommendation has been shown to improve ranking performance by structuring true positive and skipped items differently in the embedding space \[[2409.07367v1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2409.07367v1)\].

Privacy concerns have led to the development of privacy-preserving CDSR frameworks, such as PriCDSR, which applies differential privacy mechanisms to protect users’ interaction sequences while maintaining recommendation effectiveness \[[2401.15369v1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2401.15369v1)\]. Another direction tackles the open-world assumption where users may not overlap fully across domains and data distributions shift over time. Adaptive frameworks employing multi-interest information modules and doubly robust estimators have been proposed to mitigate biases and improve generalization in such realistic settings \[[2311.04590v6](http://arxiv.org/abs/2311.04590v6)\].

From a modeling perspective, several works focus on improving the self-attention mechanism itself, recognizing its central role in sequential recommendation. Lightweight self-attentive networks have been proposed to compress embedding matrices and reduce redundancy in multi-head attention, enhancing efficiency without compromising accuracy \[[2410.21048v1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2410.21048v1)\]\[[2108.11333v1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2108.11333v1)\]. Methods like frequency-enhanced hybrid attention explicitly learn both low- and high-frequency patterns in the frequency domain to better capture periodic user behavior characteristics \[[2304.09184v3](http://arxiv.org/abs/2304.09184v3)\]. Additionally, approaches such as Primal-Attention reformulate self-attention using asymmetric kernel singular value decomposition, promoting low-rank structures and improved efficiency \[[2305.19798v2](http://arxiv.org/abs/2305.19798v2)\]. Hybrid frameworks combining Mamba with low-rank decomposed self-attention modules strike a balance between linear complexity and structural bias to refine user preference modeling \[[2407.13135v1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2407.13135v1)\].

Cross-domain learning has also been framed as a multi-objective optimization problem to reconcile competing tasks and enhance knowledge transfer. Multi-task learning methods optimize Pareto-optimal solutions to balance different domain objectives, addressing task conflicts inherent in CDSR \[[1810.04650v2](http://arxiv.org/abs/1810.04650v2)\]\[[2210.10309v1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2210.10309v1)\]. Reinforcement learning and contrastive learning techniques have been employed to decompose user evolving preferences and align representations across domains, further mitigating negative transfer \[[2302.03221v1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2302.03221v1)\]\[[2203.16942v1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2203.16942v1)\]. Automated prompting strategies and large language model (LLM) integrations have recently been introduced to handle non-overlapping user and item spaces and to semantically bridge domains with scalable adaptation \[[2304.04218v1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2304.04218v1)\]\[[2504.20859v1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2504.20859v1)\]\[[2504.18383v1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2504.18383v1)\].

Other notable contributions include graph-based solutions that incorporate time interval information and account-aware self-attention to capture domain-specific sequential patterns in shared-account CDSR scenarios \[[2206.08050v1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2206.08050v1)\]. Parallel split-join networks address the challenge of disentangling mixed user behaviors within shared accounts across domains \[[1910.02448v4](http://arxiv.org/abs/1910.02448v4)\]. Moreover, image fusion techniques enrich item embeddings with visual data, leveraging multi-attention layers to jointly learn intra- and inter-sequence interactions for improved CDSR \[[2502.15694v2](http://arxiv.org/abs/2502.15694v2)\]\[[2504.15085v5](http://arxiv.org/abs/2504.15085v5)\].

In summary, existing CDSR research spans various strategies: enhancing or augmenting self-attention mechanisms, incorporating domain-specific and multimodal information, modeling user and feedback transitions, ensuring privacy, and employing multi-objective optimization frameworks to enable effective and adaptive cross-domain knowledge transfer. Our work complements these efforts by directly enhancing the self-attention mechanism through a Pareto-optimal formulation that dynamically balances task objectives and cross-domain attention, providing a plug-and-play and computationally efficient solution that advances CDSR performance \[[2405.12473v3](http://arxiv.org/abs/2405.12473v3)\]\[[1810.04650v2](http://arxiv.org/abs/1810.04650v2)\]\[[2410.21048v1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2410.21048v1)\]\[[2304.09184v3](http://arxiv.org/abs/2304.09184v3)\].
