## Related Works

Fairness in re-ranking has been extensively studied, focusing on balancing the trade-off between ranking accuracy and fairness constraints. Initial efforts addressed provider-side fairness by introducing fairness-aware re-ranking algorithms that balance accuracy with provider coverage, while also accounting for personalized user tolerance towards diversity in recommendations \[[2404.17826v1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2404.17826v1)\]. This personalization recognizes that users differ in their receptivity to the inclusion of diverse providers, which mitigates the accuracy loss typically observed in fairness interventions.

Building on provider fairness, resource allocation frameworks have been proposed, such as Provider Max-min Fairness (P-MMF), which formulates provider fairness as a max-min fairness regularized optimization problem. This approach prioritizes improving the utility of the worst-off providers, thereby fostering a more stable market and outperforming proportion fairness metrics \[[1809.02921v2](http://arxiv.org/abs/1809.02921v2)\]. P-MMF dynamically allocates exposure slots in online recommendation settings, balancing user preferences and provider fairness with bounded regret and efficient computation \[[1809.02921v2](http://arxiv.org/abs/1809.02921v2)\].

A novel perspective conceptualizes fair re-ranking through the lens of economic taxation. This perspective models fairness constraints as item-level tax policies that redistribute ranking resources similarly to commodity taxes in economics. The Tax-rank method exemplifies this approach by imposing taxes based on utility differences between items and optimizing via the Sinkhorn algorithm for optimal transport. Tax-rank addresses key limitations in prior item-level tax policies by ensuring continuity (small changes in tax rates lead to small shifts in accuracy and fairness) and controllability over accuracy loss, thus providing a theoretically grounded and practically effective framework \[[2405.16120v2](http://arxiv.org/abs/2405.16120v2)\]. This economic analogy aligns with the paper’s focus on elasticity, emphasizing how fairness constraints on items translate into costs borne by users in terms of accuracy loss.

Two-sided fairness in recommender systems has also been explored, recognizing that these platforms mediate between consumers and producers. CPFair integrates fairness constraints for both consumer and producer sides within a joint optimization framework, improving fairness for both stakeholders without degrading recommendation quality \[[2306.01417v1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2306.01417v1)\]. Such multi-stakeholder approaches highlight the complexity and necessity of addressing fairness beyond a single dimension.

Addressing fluctuating user traffic and its impact on fairness and accuracy trade-offs, the BankFair model draws inspiration from the bankruptcy problem in economics. By employing the Talmud rule, BankFair balances fairness requirements over time, offsetting periods of low user traffic with those of abundance to maintain consistent user experience and long-term fairness \[[2405.16120v2](http://arxiv.org/abs/2405.16120v2)\]. This temporal dimension illustrates the challenges of ensuring fairness in dynamic, real-world environments.

Further, personalization in fairness-aware re-ranking has been extended across multiple fairness dimensions, considering individual user preferences and multiple sensitive attributes simultaneously. Opportunistic fairness approaches learn user-specific fairness preferences to enhance provider fairness, achieving superior trade-offs between accuracy and fairness relative to prior methods \[[2005.12974v1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2005.12974v1)\]. This multi-aspect personalization reflects growing recognition of heterogeneous fairness perceptions and constraints.

The trade-off between accuracy and fairness is also framed as an intrinsic tension in fair machine learning, where group similarity in outcomes for protected groups often conflicts with statistical accuracy. This fundamental trade-off, formalized through economic principles of elasticity, underpins the challenges in designing fair re-ranking algorithms \[[2306.01417v1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2306.01417v1)\]. Related theoretical work on fairness definitions and constraints, such as sufficiency and positive predictive value parity, further reveals complexities and sometimes counterintuitive outcomes when optimizing for fairness criteria alongside utility maximization \[[2206.02237v1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2206.02237v1)\].

Besides algorithmic advances, fairness evaluation frameworks have evolved to capture nuanced trade-offs. The Elastic Fairness Curve (EF-Curve) provides a systematic framework to evaluate algorithms across varying levels of elasticity, facilitating a rigorous comparative analysis of fairness-accuracy trade-offs and guiding algorithm selection based on elasticity characteristics \[[2405.16120v2](http://arxiv.org/abs/2405.16120v2)\]. This directly informs the development of ElasticRank, which adaptively adjusts inter-item distances in a curved space based on elasticity calculations to optimize fairness and accuracy simultaneously.

Additional work highlights the importance of fairness-aware data generation for benchmarking, using synthetic, privacy-preserving datasets to better understand fairness dynamics without compromising sensitive information \[[2409.14078v1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2409.14078v1)\]. Moreover, fairness evaluation methods extend to robustness analysis under data perturbations, identifying subgroup-specific vulnerabilities in models such as face recognition systems \[[2202.03586v2](http://arxiv.org/abs/2202.03586v2)\].

Finally, works addressing the sociotechnical and organizational contexts of fairness emphasize the need for interdisciplinary collaboration and interpretability in fairness solutions. Tools like CONFAIR enable configurable and interpretable fairness optimization incorporating user constraints, while studies on industry practices reveal the challenges and invisible labor involved in cross-functional fairness work \[[2111.08878v3](http://arxiv.org/abs/2111.08878v3)\]\[[2306.06542v1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2306.06542v1)\]. These perspectives underscore that fairness in re-ranking is not only a technical challenge but also a human-centered and organizational one.

In summary, the literature spans economic analogies, multi-stakeholder fairness, personalization, theoretical fairness constraints, dynamic and temporal fairness considerations, evaluation frameworks centered on elasticity, and practical implementation challenges. This body of work provides a rich foundation for advancing elastic fairness-aware re-ranking methods that systematically address the inherent trade-offs between accuracy and fairness.
