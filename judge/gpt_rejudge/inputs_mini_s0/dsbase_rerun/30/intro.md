## Related Works

The study of database transaction isolation testing has garnered significant attention due to the critical trade-offs between isolation strength, performance, and scalability. Early foundational work established that verifying strong isolation levels, such as serializability, is NP-complete, making testing computationally expensive for large workloads \[[2504.02344v1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2504.02344v1)\]. This complexity has driven research towards weak isolation levels, which relax strict consistency guarantees and admit more efficient testing algorithms. Recent theoretical advances have shown that key weak isolation levels—such as Read Committed (RC), Read Atomic (RA), and Causal Consistency (CC)—can be tested in polynomial time, thus enabling scalability improvements over strong isolation testing \[[2504.02344v1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2504.02344v1)\]\[[2501.14421v2](http://arxiv.org/abs/2501.14421v2)\].

Several black-box testing frameworks have been proposed to detect isolation anomalies by analyzing execution histories of database transactions under various isolation semantics. Traditional black-box checkers rely on generating large randomized workloads to stress test the database and verify if the resulting histories comply with specified isolation levels \[[2504.02344v1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2504.02344v1)\]\[[2103.02830v1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2103.02830v1)\]. While effective, these methods often incur prohibitive overheads during history generation and verification, especially under strong isolation guarantees. To mitigate this, Mini-Transactions (MTs) were introduced as a lightweight workload model that executes compact read-modify-write patterns, enabling faster history generation and more efficient verification algorithms that scale linearly or quadratically with the workload size \[[2504.02344v1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2504.02344v1)\]. This approach retains the ability to detect a wide range of anomalies, including those relevant to weak isolation levels.

In parallel, timestamp-based isolation checking methods have been developed to address the challenge of verifying ever-growing histories in online or continuous settings. CHRONOS and its online extensions AION and AION-SER enable efficient incremental checking of Snapshot Isolation (SI) and Serializability (SER) without the need to construct full dependency graphs of the entire history, achieving throughput rates suitable for production environments \[[2504.01477v1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2504.01477v1)\]. These techniques, however, primarily focus on SI and SER rather than the broader spectrum of weak isolation levels targeted by AWDIT.

Weak isolation levels are particularly prevalent in industrial-strength databases as defaults due to their performance advantages, despite their susceptibility to subtle anomalies \[[2501.14421v2](http://arxiv.org/abs/2501.14421v2)\]. Formal verification approaches based on separation logic have been proposed to reason rigorously about these weak levels, such as read uncommitted, read committed, and snapshot isolation, by defining modular specifications that are implementation-agnostic and mechanized in proof assistants \[[2501.14421v2](http://arxiv.org/abs/2501.14421v2)\]. These formal foundations support the development of sound testing and verification tools but do not directly address the runtime complexity of isolation checking.

Other frameworks like Coo employ partial order pair (POP) graphs to capture transaction conflicts and stateful commit/abort information, allowing for precise and cost-effective consistency checking across various isolation levels, including weaker ones. Coo also facilitates constructing inconsistent test cases to systematically evaluate database conformance to isolation guarantees \[[2206.14602v1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2206.14602v1)\]. While powerful, Coo’s complexity remains relatively high for large-scale workloads.

Testing under weak isolation is further complicated by the rarity and unpredictability of weak behaviors, which led to the design of mock storage systems such as MonkeyDB. MonkeyDB probabilistically explores the set of possible read values under various isolation semantics, providing comprehensive coverage of weak anomalies in a testing environment \[[2103.02830v1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2103.02830v1)\]. However, this approach focuses on exposing application-level robustness rather than optimizing isolation checking algorithms themselves.

Theoretical work on the complexity of weak isolation testing has provided conditional lower bounds, highlighting that certain weak isolation levels cannot be tested faster than roughly \(O(n^{3/2})\), where \(n\) is the history size \[[2504.02344v1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2504.02344v1)\]. This insight underscores the importance of designing testers that are optimal or near-optimal with respect to these complexity limits. Prior polynomial-time algorithms for testing RC, RA, and CC, while scalable compared to NP-hard strong isolation testers, still exhibit higher polynomial degrees that limit their application on very large histories \[[2504.02344v1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2504.02344v1)\]\[[2501.14421v2](http://arxiv.org/abs/2501.14421v2)\].

In summary, the landscape of isolation testing encompasses a spectrum of approaches—ranging from black-box randomized testing and efficient workload modeling with Mini-Transactions, to timestamp-based online checkers, formal verification frameworks, and probabilistic mock storage systems. Despite these advances, existing tools face challenges in handling large-scale histories efficiently, especially for weak isolation levels. The development of AWDIT addresses this gap by providing highly efficient, provably optimal algorithms for testing RC, RA, and CC with significantly improved time complexities and empirical performance over prior state-of-the-art methods \[[2504.02344v1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2504.02344v1)\].
