## Related Works

Single-pixel imaging (SPI) offers a compelling solution for imaging in spectral ranges beyond conventional focal plane arrays and under low-light conditions. Early SPI methods employed random or structured illumination patterns such as Hadamard and Fourier bases, which improved reconstruction quality and efficiency compared to random patterns \[[1706.08383v1](http://arxiv.org/abs/1706.08383v1)\]. Hadamard single-pixel imaging (HSI) and Fourier single-pixel imaging (FSI) represent two prominent approaches; FSI tends to be more measurement-efficient, while HSI exhibits greater robustness to noise \[[1706.08383v1](http://arxiv.org/abs/1706.08383v1)\]. However, traditional SPI techniques face challenges including lengthy data acquisition times and limited reconstruction fidelity.

To address these issues, recent works have incorporated deep learning, significantly advancing SPI reconstruction quality and speed. Deep learning-based SPI methods leverage convolutional neural networks (CNNs) and other architectures to directly map measurements to images or enhance intermediate reconstructions, supporting tasks such as super-resolution, imaging through scattering media, photon-level sensing, and optical encryption \[[2310.16869v2](http://arxiv.org/abs/2310.16869v2)\]. Despite their success, purely data-driven models often require extensive retraining or fine-tuning when imaging conditions, such as compression ratios (CRs), change.

Algorithm unrolling has emerged as a promising framework that bridges iterative optimization algorithms and deep neural networks, offering interpretable and efficient architectures with performance benefits \[[1912.10557v3](http://arxiv.org/abs/1912.10557v3)\]. Unrolling transforms iterative solvers into multi-stage networks where each stage corresponds to an algorithm iteration, enabling end-to-end training and often faster inference. This approach has been successfully applied to inverse problems in imaging including deblurring \[[2402.12872v2](http://arxiv.org/abs/2402.12872v2)\]\[[1902.03493v3](http://arxiv.org/abs/1902.03493v3)\], biomedical imaging \[[2108.06637v1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2108.06637v1)\]\[[2109.14025v1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2109.14025v1)\], and spectral image reconstruction \[[2008.11625v3](http://arxiv.org/abs/2008.11625v3)\]. A key advantage of unrolling lies in its interpretability and the ability to incorporate domain knowledge from optimization frameworks, though typical unrolling models often require retraining for different CRs or noise levels.

Plug-and-play (PnP) methods form another class of iterative algorithms for inverse problems, where proximal operators in optimization are implicitly replaced by powerful off-the-shelf deep denoisers. PnP algorithms offer flexibility to adapt to varying imaging conditions without retraining, as they decouple regularization from the data-fidelity term \[[2008.11625v3](http://arxiv.org/abs/2008.11625v3)\]. However, PnP methods may suffer from slower convergence and sometimes lower reconstruction accuracy compared to learned unrolled networks.

Recent efforts have sought to combine the strengths of unrolling and PnP strategies. For example, unrolling of the Half-Quadratic Splitting (HQS) algorithm with learned proximal operators has been shown to improve both convergence guarantees and reconstruction performance in deblurring tasks \[[2402.12872v2](http://arxiv.org/abs/2402.12872v2)\]. Incorporating a general proximal trajectory loss enables training of unrolled networks whose learned modules approximate ideal proximal operators, effectively bridging explicit regularization and learned priors.

In SPI specifically, deep learning approaches have been developed to address reconstruction speed and quality limitations. Deep networks trained end-to-end for SPI reconstruction achieve faster inference but lack flexibility when CRs vary \[[2310.16869v2](http://arxiv.org/abs/2310.16869v2)\]. Conversely, PnP methods maintain flexibility but may lag in speed and accuracy \[[2008.11625v3](http://arxiv.org/abs/2008.11625v3)\]. By unrolling HQS and ADMM algorithms with a learned deep image restorer (DIR) and employing a proximal trajectory loss, recent work demonstrates that unrolled networks can handle varying CRs with a single model, achieving superior accuracy and faster inference compared to CR-specific unrolling networks or PnP methods \[[2402.12872v2](http://arxiv.org/abs/2402.12872v2)\]\[[2310.16869v2](http://arxiv.org/abs/2310.16869v2)\].

Beyond SPI, algorithm unrolling has proven effective for various biomedical and spectral imaging modalities, exploiting iterative solver structures for robust and interpretable deep models \[[2108.06637v1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2108.06637v1)\]\[[2109.14025v1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2109.14025v1)\]\[[2008.11625v3](http://arxiv.org/abs/2008.11625v3)\]. Extensions to multi-target Lidar imaging using Bayesian unrolling and graph attention \[[2504.02480v2](http://arxiv.org/abs/2504.02480v2)\], as well as spectral imaging with diffractive lenses and learned reconstruction algorithms integrating alternating minimization and unrolling, highlight the broad applicability of these approaches \[[2008.11625v3](http://arxiv.org/abs/2008.11625v3)\].

In summary, the integration of deep learning with iterative optimization through algorithm unrolling and plug-and-play techniques has driven significant advancements in SPI inverse problem solving. Unrolling-based methods provide faster inference and enhanced accuracy but traditionally lack flexibility across CRs, while PnP methods offer adaptability with slower convergence. Novel proximal unrolling frameworks that learn explicit proximal operators and incorporate trajectory losses show promise in unifying these benefits, enabling flexible, accurate, and efficient SPI reconstruction within a single model \[[1706.08383v1](http://arxiv.org/abs/1706.08383v1)\]\[[1912.10557v3](http://arxiv.org/abs/1912.10557v3)\]\[[2402.12872v2](http://arxiv.org/abs/2402.12872v2)\]\[[2008.11625v3](http://arxiv.org/abs/2008.11625v3)\]\[[2310.16869v2](http://arxiv.org/abs/2310.16869v2)\].
