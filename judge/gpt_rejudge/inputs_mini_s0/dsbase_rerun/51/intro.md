## Related Works

Federated Learning (FL) enables collaborative model training across decentralized clients while preserving data privacy by keeping raw data local \[[2007.14390v5](http://arxiv.org/abs/2007.14390v5)\]. Its application to Online Learning to Rank (OLTR) has given rise to Federated Online Learning to Rank (FOLTR), which leverages implicit user feedback like clicks to continually update ranking models without compromising privacy \[[2012.13891v3](http://arxiv.org/abs/2012.13891v3)\]. Despite its promise, FOLTR faces challenges including ranking effectiveness, robustness to heterogeneous data distributions, vulnerability to attacks, and the need for mechanisms that support unlearning of client data upon request \[[2012.13891v3](http://arxiv.org/abs/2012.13891v3)\].

The legal imperative of the "right to be forgotten" has catalyzed extensive research on machine unlearning, which aims to remove the influence of specific data from trained models efficiently without full retraining. In federated settings, federated unlearning (FU) extends this concept to remove client or data contributions from a global FL model, maintaining privacy compliance and mitigating risks such as data poisoning \[[2401.11018v1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2401.11018v1)\]\[[2403.02437v3](http://arxiv.org/abs/2403.02437v3)\]\[[2401.05146v3](http://arxiv.org/abs/2401.05146v3)\]\[[2310.19218v5](http://arxiv.org/abs/2310.19218v5)\]. Unlike centralized machine unlearning, FU must handle challenges unique to distributed learning, including data heterogeneity, limited access to global data, communication constraints, and stochasticity in client updates \[[2403.02437v3](http://arxiv.org/abs/2403.02437v3)\]\[[2401.05146v3](http://arxiv.org/abs/2401.05146v3)\].

Several FU methods have been proposed with varying focuses. FedEraser reconstructs unlearned models by leveraging stored historical client updates, achieving significant speed-ups over retraining approaches, albeit requiring server storage and update calibration \[[2012.13891v3](http://arxiv.org/abs/2012.13891v3)\]. FedAU introduces an auxiliary unlearning module integrated during training, enabling efficient unlearning at multiple granularities without additional costly retraining steps \[[2405.15474v2](http://arxiv.org/abs/2405.15474v2)\]. Forgettable Federated Linear Learning approximates deep neural networks with linear models to certify unlearning efficiently and securely without further client communication \[[2306.02216v3](http://arxiv.org/abs/2306.02216v3)\]. FUSED addresses irreversibility and indiscriminate unlearning by selectively overwriting sensitive knowledge via sparse adapters, making unlearning reversible and cost-effective \[[2502.20709v1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2502.20709v1)\].

Evaluation of unlearning effectiveness remains an active area of research. Traditional black-box metrics such as membership inference attacks and output accuracy comparisons are insufficient to fully characterize residual information in intermediate model representations. To address this, metrics like the Information Difference Index (IDI) quantify mutual information retained in intermediate layers to provide a more comprehensive assessment of unlearning efficacy \[[2405.17878v3](http://arxiv.org/abs/2405.17878v3)\]. Complementary approaches propose multi-metric frameworks to verify unlearning precisely, especially in domain-specific and multi-domain federated scenarios where naive unlearning can lead to over-forgetting or degrade model performance in untargeted domains \[[2406.03078v1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2406.03078v1)\]\[[2401.05146v3](http://arxiv.org/abs/2401.05146v3)\]. Probabilistic verification mechanisms based on backdoor triggers have also been explored to certify compliance with unlearning requests with high confidence \[[2003.04247v2](http://arxiv.org/abs/2003.04247v2)\].

Federated unlearning must also contend with security challenges such as poisoning attacks that can persist through unlearning processes. Recent work identifies attack vectors targeting federated unlearning itself (e.g., BadUnlearn), and proposes robust defense frameworks like UnlearnGuard to filter malicious updates and ensure unlearning closely matches retraining-from-scratch outcomes \[[2501.17396v1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2501.17396v1)\]. Privacy-preserving mechanisms, including differential privacy and plausible deniability techniques, further contribute to securing unlearning in FL by preventing inference of client participation or data leakage during the unlearning process \[[2310.20448v4](http://arxiv.org/abs/2310.20448v4)\]\[[2410.09947v1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2410.09947v1)\].

Incentive mechanisms addressing user retention and the cost-benefit trade-offs of unlearning have been studied to sustain model quality and system participation, highlighting the economic and strategic dimensions of federated unlearning \[[2308.12502v2](http://arxiv.org/abs/2308.12502v2)\]\[[2312.01235v2](http://arxiv.org/abs/2312.01235v2)\]. Additionally, federated unlearning has been extended beyond supervised learning to unsupervised tasks such as federated clustering, where efficient unlearning mechanisms yield significant speed-ups compared to retraining \[[2210.16424v2](http://arxiv.org/abs/2210.16424v2)\].

Despite progress, several challenges persist in applying unlearning to FOLTR. Existing federated unlearning methods often lack comprehensive evaluation across multiple metrics, limiting insights into balancing under-unlearning (insufficient forgetting) and over-unlearning (excessive forgetting) scenarios in ranking contexts. Moreover, the domain-specific nuances of ranking models and the dynamics of online learning require tailored evaluation methodologies and unlearning strategies to maintain ranking effectiveness while ensuring verifiable data removal \[[2012.13891v3](http://arxiv.org/abs/2012.13891v3)\]\[[2402.01401v4](http://arxiv.org/abs/2402.01401v4)\]\[[2401.05146v3](http://arxiv.org/abs/2401.05146v3)\]. This motivates the need for rigorous comparative studies that systematically assess federated unlearning methods within FOLTR, leveraging multiple adapted and novel metrics to provide nuanced insights into their strengths and limitations.

In summary, this work situates itself at the intersection of federated unlearning research and privacy-preserving online learning to rank, building upon foundational FU frameworks \[[2401.11018v1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2401.11018v1)\]\[[2403.02437v3](http://arxiv.org/abs/2403.02437v3)\]\[[2012.13891v3](http://arxiv.org/abs/2012.13891v3)\]\[[2401.05146v3](http://arxiv.org/abs/2401.05146v3)\], advanced evaluation metrics \[[2406.03078v1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2406.03078v1)\]\[[2405.17878v3](http://arxiv.org/abs/2405.17878v3)\]\[[2003.04247v2](http://arxiv.org/abs/2003.04247v2)\], secure and efficient unlearning methods \[[2405.15474v2](http://arxiv.org/abs/2405.15474v2)\]\[[2306.02216v3](http://arxiv.org/abs/2306.02216v3)\]\[[2502.20709v1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2502.20709v1)\]\[[2501.17396v1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2501.17396v1)\], and the emerging domain of FOLTR \[[2012.13891v3](http://arxiv.org/abs/2012.13891v3)\]. By focusing on the comprehensive and verifiable assessment of unlearning strategies in FOLTR, it addresses critical gaps identified in the literature, advancing both theoretical understanding and practical deployment of privacy-compliant online ranking systems.
