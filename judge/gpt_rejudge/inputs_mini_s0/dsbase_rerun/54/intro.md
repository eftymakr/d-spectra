## Related Works

Graph Convolutional Networks (GCNs) have become foundational in collaborative filtering (CF) for recommender systems, exploiting the user-item bipartite graph structure to model complex preference patterns \[[1905.08108v2](http://arxiv.org/abs/1905.08108v2)\]\[[2007.03383v2](http://arxiv.org/abs/2007.03383v2)\]. Early CF methods primarily relied on embedding layers to represent users and items, but these embeddings often have limited capacity to capture nuanced local preferences, as they typically do not incorporate the full richness of interaction signals \[[1905.08108v2](http://arxiv.org/abs/1905.08108v2)\]\[[2007.03383v2](http://arxiv.org/abs/2007.03383v2)\]. Furthermore, standard neighborhood aggregation schemes in GCNs tend to apply uniform or linear message passing, which restricts the model’s ability to differentiate between diverse preference patterns at a finer granularity \[[2007.03383v2](http://arxiv.org/abs/2007.03383v2)\]\[[2004.14734v1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2004.14734v1)\].

To overcome these limitations, recent works have framed CF within graph signal processing (GSP) to utilize spectral graph filters. PolyCF, for example, models the CF task as graph signal filtering using polynomial graph filters, which can approximate an optimal polynomial response to recover missing interactions by capturing spectral features across multiple eigenspaces \[[2401.12590v2](http://arxiv.org/abs/2401.12590v2)\]. Similarly, Automatic Graph Convolutional Networks (AutoGCN) dynamically adjust the bandwidth of graph convolutional filters to capture a broader spectrum of graph signals beyond the typical low-pass filtering, addressing the limitation of fixed bandwidth filters \[[2107.04755v3](http://arxiv.org/abs/2107.04755v3)\]. These spectral approaches highlight the importance of leveraging the full frequency spectrum of graph signals, which conventional GCNs often neglect due to their reliance on low-pass filtering.

The use of Chebyshev polynomials as a means of approximating graph convolution filters has been well-established in graph signal processing. Chebyshev polynomial filters enable efficient and flexible approximation of non-linear graph filters without explicitly computing the graph Laplacian eigen-decomposition, thus improving computational efficiency and expressiveness \[[2008.00240v1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2008.00240v1)\]\[[1611.08706v1](http://arxiv.org/abs/1611.08706v1)\]. This approach has been employed in various contexts, including graph-based denoising and interpolation, demonstrating superior approximation and convergence properties compared to standard polynomial filters \[[2008.00240v1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2008.00240v1)\]\[[1611.08706v1](http://arxiv.org/abs/1611.08706v1)\]. Such filtered polynomial techniques provide a theoretical foundation for designing graph filters that can better capture complex user-item interaction patterns in recommender systems.

In the collaborative filtering domain, several graph-based models have sought to refine neighborhood aggregation to alleviate noise and over-smoothing issues inherent in deep GCNs. RGCF refines embedding construction by redesigning aggregation to reduce noise and redundancy from indiscriminate neighbor mixing, enhancing the expressiveness of learned representations while addressing over-smoothing \[[2007.03383v2](http://arxiv.org/abs/2007.03383v2)\]. Similarly, hierarchical GCN models aggregate multi-order neighborhood information separately before hierarchical fusion, combined with stochastic message dropout, to improve robustness and mitigate over-smoothing effects \[[2004.14734v1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2004.14734v1)\]. DropNode regularization also addresses over-smoothing by randomly removing nodes during training, which effectively reduces excessive feature smoothing and overfitting \[[2008.12578v2](http://arxiv.org/abs/2008.12578v2)\].

Beyond the spectral perspective, contrastive learning has been integrated with graph convolutional methods to enhance representation learning in CF. The Simple Contrastive Collaborative Filtering (SCCF) framework theoretically connects contrastive loss with graph convolution, revealing that graph convolutions are not strictly necessary to capture high-order connectivity and that they may exacerbate over-smoothing risks \[[2406.13996v2](http://arxiv.org/abs/2406.13996v2)\]. This insight motivates alternative ways to exploit graph structure without relying solely on neighborhood aggregation.

Recent advances also consider the dynamic and multi-behavior nature of user-item interactions. Partial Order Graph Convolutional Networks (POGCN) model multiple user behaviors as a partially ordered graph, effectively merging separate behavior graphs into a unified representation that respects behavior relations, thus improving multi-behavior CF performance at scale \[[2402.07659v2](http://arxiv.org/abs/2402.07659v2)\]. Temporal Graph Networks (TGNs) have been introduced to capture evolving user preferences over time, demonstrating improved adaptability in dynamic recommendation scenarios \[[2403.16066v2](http://arxiv.org/abs/2403.16066v2)\].

In addition, improving graph construction itself has been explored to enhance CF efficiency and accuracy. Graph learning methods aim to learn sparse yet high-quality user-item graphs that better capture intrinsic data structure, avoiding dense uniform neighborhood sizes typical in kNN-based CF and thus improving computational efficiency without sacrificing recommendation quality \[[2311.03316v2](http://arxiv.org/abs/2311.03316v2)\].

Finally, various graph filter design methods inspired by classical filter functions such as Butterworth, Chebyshev, and Elliptic functions have been developed to construct universal graph filters with precise frequency responses and low computational complexity, outperforming standard ARMA and polynomial filters \[[2203.14748v1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2203.14748v1)\]. These advancements provide a rich toolkit for designing graph convolutional filters tailored to capture complex spectral properties in recommender systems.

In summary, the limitations of embedding capacity and linear neighborhood aggregation in GCN-based CF have motivated the exploration of spectral graph filtering techniques, advanced polynomial approximations via Chebyshev interpolation, refined aggregation schemes to reduce noise and over-smoothing, and dynamic behavior modeling. Our proposed ChebyCF framework builds upon these foundations by leveraging Chebyshev polynomial interpolation to approximate flexible non-linear graph filters and incorporating spectral enhancements such as ideal pass filters and degree-based normalization to fully exploit the spectrum of user interaction signals for improved recommendation performance.
