## Related Works

Radar-camera calibration is a critical prerequisite for robust sensor fusion in autonomous driving, enabling complementary perception capabilities under various environmental conditions. Early work predominantly focused on **target-based calibration**, which requires specialized calibration objects such as corner reflectors to establish correspondences between radar and camera measurements. For example, a flexible and accurate 3D radar-camera extrinsic calibration method uses a single corner reflector on the ground to iteratively gather data and solve a perspective-n-point (PnP) problem, applying RANSAC and Levenberg-Marquardt optimization to enhance robustness and accuracy \[[1207.4147v1](http://arxiv.org/abs/1207.4147v1)\]. While effective, these methods depend on controlled environments and are not suitable for online or in-situ calibration.

To overcome these limitations, **targetless calibration methods** have gained traction, leveraging data-driven and deep learning approaches to extract common features from radar and camera data without requiring dedicated calibration targets. A notable approach employs deep learning to implicitly extract and match common features from raw radar Range-Doppler-Angle data and camera images, facilitating online extrinsic calibration. This method also uses RANSAC and nonlinear optimization to improve accuracy and robustness in real-world scenarios \[[2307.15264v1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2307.15264v1)\]. Similarly, a data-driven method for automatic rotational calibration uses coarse and fine convolutional neural networks to achieve precise sensor registration without calibration targets, demonstrating good generalization across sensor setups \[[1904.08743v2](http://arxiv.org/abs/1904.08743v2)\].

Another significant research direction involves **continuous-time and spatiotemporal calibration** approaches that do not rely on retroreflectors or special infrastructure. One method exploits radar ego-velocity measurements for continuous-time 3D radar-to-camera calibration, showing observability and effectiveness in both synthetic and real-world experiments \[[2103.07505v2](http://arxiv.org/abs/2103.07505v2)\]. Extending this concept, spatiotemporal calibration algorithms have been developed to jointly estimate spatial and temporal offsets between radar and camera sensors during operation, leveraging ego-motion cues to perform targetless calibration in arbitrary environments, achieving accuracy comparable to target-based methods \[[2211.01871v4](http://arxiv.org/abs/2211.01871v4)\].

Beyond calibration, **radar-camera fusion architectures for perception** have evolved to better leverage the heterogeneous sensor data. Fusion methods differ in when and how features are combined, with recent works emphasizing bird’s-eye view (BEV) representations to preserve spatial information. For instance, ClusterFusion extracts local spatial features directly from radar point cloud clusters before projecting onto the image plane, improving 3D object detection performance \[[2309.03734v2](http://arxiv.org/abs/2309.03734v2)\]. Similarly, RC-BEVFusion introduces a modular radar encoder integrated with camera-based BEV architectures, yielding significant performance gains on benchmarks such as nuScenes \[[2305.15883v2](http://arxiv.org/abs/2305.15883v2)\]. Additionally, fusion methods have been enhanced by explicit modeling of radar hit distributions conditioned on monocular camera detections to refine matching scores and enhance detection accuracy \[[2504.09086v1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2504.09086v1)\]. Cross-dataset studies further confirm that radar-camera fusion consistently outperforms single-sensor baselines, particularly when leveraging high-quality radar data and diverse camera training sets \[[2309.15465v1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2309.15465v1)\].

Several works address the challenges posed by **radar data sparsity and height measurement uncertainty**, which are central to reliable calibration and fusion. While radar provides robust range and velocity measurements, its sparse height information complicates direct use for calibration and 3D perception. Methods that combine multiple sensor perspectives and modalities to mitigate these challenges are emerging. For example, dual-view representations that combine frontal and bird’s-eye views can leverage the rich height cues from the frontal view alongside the robustness of bird’s-eye view features, enabling selective fusion mechanisms to weigh reliable features and reduce the impact of height uncertainty.

In summary, the presented method advances the state of the art by introducing a Dual-Perspective representation and a Selective Fusion Mechanism tailored to address radar height data sparsity and uncertainty, combined with a Multi-Modal Cross-Attention framework to explicitly establish cross-modal correspondences. This design builds upon foundational work in targetless calibration \[[2307.15264v1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2307.15264v1)\]\[[1904.08743v2](http://arxiv.org/abs/1904.08743v2)\], spatiotemporal calibration without targets \[[2103.07505v2](http://arxiv.org/abs/2103.07505v2)\]\[[2211.01871v4](http://arxiv.org/abs/2211.01871v4)\], and sophisticated radar-camera fusion strategies exploiting BEV and spatial features \[[2309.03734v2](http://arxiv.org/abs/2309.03734v2)\]\[[2305.15883v2](http://arxiv.org/abs/2305.15883v2)\]\[[2504.09086v1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2504.09086v1)\]\[[2309.15465v1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2309.15465v1)\]. The integration of noise-resistant matching further strengthens robustness against inherent radar measurement noise and sparsity, setting a new benchmark for online automatic geometric calibration.
