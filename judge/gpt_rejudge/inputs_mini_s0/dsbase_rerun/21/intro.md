## Related Works

Neural rendering has become a prominent approach for reconstructing 3D scenes from 2D images by leveraging implicit neural representations, which model continuous scene geometry and appearance conditioned on multi-view inputs \[[2203.00137v1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2203.00137v1)\]\[[2210.11170v2](http://arxiv.org/abs/2210.11170v2)\]. Traditional multi-view neural rendering methods optimize the scene representation based on the consistency of geometry and appearance across input views. However, the reconstruction quality is often constrained by the limited number of available views, particularly in complex or dynamic scenes where some object viewpoints are never captured \[[2309.07021v1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2309.07021v1)\]\[[1903.06708v2](http://arxiv.org/abs/1903.06708v2)\].

To mitigate this limitation, data augmentation techniques have been explored to increase viewpoint coverage. Video frame interpolation (VFI) is a natural candidate for this purpose because it synthesizes intermediate frames between existing video frames, effectively providing additional virtual viewpoints and temporal samples. VFI has been widely studied, evolving from classical motion estimation and optical flow methods to deep learning paradigms that jointly learn motion and synthesis in an end-to-end manner \[[1703.07514v1](http://arxiv.org/abs/1703.07514v1)\]\[[1708.01692v1](http://arxiv.org/abs/1708.01692v1)\]\[[2207.12305v1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2207.12305v1)\]. Early deep convolutional neural network (CNN) approaches estimated adaptive convolution kernels for each pixel to synthesize intermediate frames, enabling robustness against occlusion, blur, and lighting variations \[[1708.01692v1](http://arxiv.org/abs/1708.01692v1)\]\[[1703.07514v1](http://arxiv.org/abs/1703.07514v1)\]. More recent works have incorporated depth information and occlusion reasoning to improve interpolation results in cases of large motion and complex scenes \[[1904.00830v1](http://arxiv.org/abs/1904.00830v1)\].

Transformer-based architectures have also been introduced to address limitations in convolutional models, such as kernel weight sharing that can lead to detail loss. By leveraging flow-guided temporal self-attention, these methods capture long-range dependencies and better handle large motions, resulting in higher visual fidelity of interpolated frames \[[2307.16144v1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2307.16144v1)\]. Similarly, spectral-domain methods using adaptive Fourier neural operators have demonstrated scale-invariant interpolation capabilities, producing smooth and accurate frame synthesis across diverse datasets \[[2211.10791v2](http://arxiv.org/abs/2211.10791v2)\].

In the context of neural rendering, frame interpolation’s ability to generate additional views has been utilized as a data augmentation strategy to improve 3D reconstruction quality. Implicit neural representations conditioned on interpolated frames can serve as a form of learned prior that enriches photometric supervision, especially when original views are sparse or incomplete \[[2203.00137v1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2203.00137v1)\]. This approach aligns with recent findings that combining interpolation with neural fields can enhance space-time consistency and reconstruction fidelity in both static and dynamic scenes \[[2203.00137v1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2203.00137v1)\]\[[2309.07021v1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2309.07021v1)\].

Several studies have also addressed the challenge of training VFI models without extensive ground-truth data by employing unsupervised or self-supervised learning schemes such as cycle consistency and pseudo-supervised losses. These techniques enable frame interpolation models to adapt to new domains and video characteristics without requiring high frame-rate videos for training \[[1906.05928v3](http://arxiv.org/abs/1906.05928v3)\]. Moreover, perceptual quality metrics specifically designed for VFI have been developed to better correlate with human subjective assessments, addressing the inadequacies of traditional full-reference metrics in evaluating interpolation artifacts \[[2312.15659v1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2312.15659v1)\]\[[2210.01879v1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2210.01879v1)\]\[[2210.00823v3](http://arxiv.org/abs/2210.00823v3)\].

In addition to improving interpolation quality, the integration of VFI into neural rendering pipelines benefits from models that are lightweight yet capable of modeling camera motion and real-world 3D geometry implicitly. Such models act as implicit scene priors, facilitating the generation of photorealistic intermediate frames that provide richer supervision signals during 3D optimization \[[2203.00137v1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2203.00137v1)\]. Approaches that unify interpolation and prediction tasks have also been proposed, enabling flexible synthesis at arbitrary time steps, which is advantageous for dynamic scene representation \[[2503.02316v1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2503.02316v1)\].

Finally, neural rendering systems face challenges in complex dynamic scenes and large-scale environments, where multi-view data is scarce or incomplete. Online reconstruction methods segment scenes into object-centric volumes and leverage pose estimation to maintain consistency and enable dynamic object reconstruction \[[2503.18897v1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2503.18897v1)\]\[[1903.06708v2](http://arxiv.org/abs/1903.06708v2)\]. Incorporating video frame interpolation as a data augmentation step can complement these strategies by synthesizing novel views that help fill in missing angles and improve reconstruction completeness.

In summary, the combination of video frame interpolation and neural rendering leverages advances in deep learning-based interpolation methods—spanning CNNs, Transformers, and implicit neural operators—to generate high-quality intermediate frames that enhance 3D reconstruction under limited-view scenarios. This synergy addresses fundamental limitations in neural rendering, particularly for dynamic and complex scenes, by augmenting training data and enriching photometric constraints with interpolated views \[[1904.00830v1](http://arxiv.org/abs/1904.00830v1)\]\[[2307.16144v1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2307.16144v1)\]\[[2211.10791v2](http://arxiv.org/abs/2211.10791v2)\]\[[1906.05928v3](http://arxiv.org/abs/1906.05928v3)\]\[[2203.00137v1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2203.00137v1)\]\[[2207.12305v1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2207.12305v1)\]\[[2503.02316v1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2503.02316v1)\].
