## Related Works

Differential privacy (DP) has established itself as the standard for privacy-preserving data analysis, offering strong theoretical guarantees against information leakage even when adversaries possess arbitrary side information \[[0803.3946v4](http://arxiv.org/abs/0803.3946v4)\]. Traditional DP mechanisms rely on adding calibrated randomness, such as Laplace or staircase noise, to query outputs to protect individual data points while maintaining overall utility \[[1212.1186v3](http://arxiv.org/abs/1212.1186v3)\]. However, these mechanisms typically assume honest execution by the data curator, an assumption that is increasingly challenged in distributed and multi-party settings.

Recent research has highlighted vulnerabilities in distributed implementations of DP, where malicious servers or data providers can deviate from protocols by, for instance, sampling noise from incorrect distributions or generating correlated noise to manipulate aggregate results \[[2104.06569v2](http://arxiv.org/abs/2104.06569v2)\]. This manipulation threatens the integrity of DP guarantees, as malicious randomness can mask adversarial behavior and skew statistical estimates. To counter these threats, the concept of verifiable differential privacy (VDP) has emerged, which augments DP with cryptographic proofs that attest to faithful execution of privacy-preserving algorithms.

Verifiable Differential Privacy was initially formalized by requiring the data curator or server to produce zero-knowledge proofs (ZKPs) that confirm the output adheres to the DP noise distribution without revealing the noise itself \[[2208.09011v2](http://arxiv.org/abs/2208.09011v2)\]. This approach resolves the paradox of verifying randomized outputs without disclosing sensitive randomness, leveraging a combination of private and public randomness and computational assumptions to achieve practical verifiability \[[2208.09011v2](http://arxiv.org/abs/2208.09011v2)\]. While ZKPs have been shown to be sufficient for enforcing DP verifiability, they are not strictly necessary, as alternative cryptographic constructions can enforce compliance \[[2502.18535v2](http://arxiv.org/abs/2502.18535v2)\].

Several works have integrated ZKPs with DP to enhance trustworthiness in the distributed and federated learning contexts. For example, frameworks that combine ZKPs with DP have been proposed to certify aggregate statistics and machine learning outcomes without compromising privacy or model confidentiality \[[2502.18535v2](http://arxiv.org/abs/2502.18535v2)\]\[[2208.08662v1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2208.08662v1)\]. These frameworks address challenges such as proving correctness of randomized responses at the client side (local DP) and ensuring global model updates respect DP constraints. Efficient composable zero-knowledge proof systems, such as zksk, support these developments by enabling modular proof construction that simplifies implementation and reduces error-prone coding \[[1911.02459v2](http://arxiv.org/abs/1911.02459v2)\].

In the local differential privacy (LDP) model, which removes the need for a trusted curator by having individuals perturb their data before submission, verifiable mechanisms are crucial to prevent malicious clients from manipulating their randomized outputs. Recent work proposes verifiable randomized response (VRR) protocols that enable the data collector or verifier to confirm that clients apply the agreed noise mechanism without cheating, thus preserving the integrity of LDP processes \[[2104.06569v2](http://arxiv.org/abs/2104.06569v2)\]\[[2402.04840v3](http://arxiv.org/abs/2402.04840v3)\]. Improvements to VRR have achieved dramatic reductions in communication overhead and verifier computation, making verifiable LDP mechanisms more practical for large-scale deployment \[[2104.06569v2](http://arxiv.org/abs/2104.06569v2)\].

Beyond zero-knowledge proofs, other cryptographic approaches like secure multi-party computation (MPC) have been employed to enable verifiable DP in distributed scenarios. MPC-based protocols allow multiple parties to jointly compute DP outputs while preventing any single party from learning sensitive inputs and simultaneously proving correct noise application \[[2409.10667v2](http://arxiv.org/abs/2409.10667v2)\]\[[2208.08662v1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2208.08662v1)\]. These approaches aim to balance privacy, accuracy, and efficiency, often addressing the trade-offs between communication cost and computational overhead. Benchmarking studies have evaluated the efficiency of various secure noise sampling protocols in MPC, highlighting performance bottlenecks and resilience against Byzantine adversaries \[[2409.10667v2](http://arxiv.org/abs/2409.10667v2)\].

Randomized response and post-randomization methods have been revisited under a unifying lens that connects them with DP and t-closeness via principles of deniability and permutation, providing deeper theoretical insight into privacy guarantees and their calibration \[[1803.02139v1](http://arxiv.org/abs/1803.02139v1)\]. Such connections inform the design of more accurate and utility-preserving verifiable mechanisms, including recent advances that randomize indexes rather than values to improve LDP utility \[[2504.17523v1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2504.17523v1)\].

Finally, privacy amplification techniques such as shuffling have been shown to strengthen DP guarantees in local models by randomizing the order of data contributions, effectively reducing the privacy loss and enabling tighter privacy-utility trade-offs \[[2012.12803v3](http://arxiv.org/abs/2012.12803v3)\]. These theoretical advances support the design of distributed verifiable DP protocols by offering means to amplify privacy guarantees while maintaining verifiability.

The present work builds on this rich landscape by defining Verifiable Distributed Differential Privacy (VDDP), a framework that explicitly addresses adversarial manipulation in distributed settings via efficient verification. It advances verifiable DP by proposing novel mechanisms such as the Verifiable Distributed Discrete Laplacian Mechanism (VDDLM), which significantly improves proof generation efficiency, and an enhanced Verifiable Randomized Response (VRR) protocol that reduces overhead in local DP scenarios. These contributions leverage insights from prior verifiable DP and cryptographic frameworks, bridging gaps in scalability and practical deployment \[[2104.06569v2](http://arxiv.org/abs/2104.06569v2)\]\[[2208.09011v2](http://arxiv.org/abs/2208.09011v2)\]\[[2402.04840v3](http://arxiv.org/abs/2402.04840v3)\]\[[2208.08662v1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2208.08662v1)\].
