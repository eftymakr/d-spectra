## Related Works

Personalized outfit generation has attracted considerable research interest, focusing on constructing sets of compatible fashion items tailored to individual user preferences. Early works addressed outfit compatibility prediction and recommendation by modeling pairwise or global item relationships, often using deep learning-based compatibility scoring or graph neural networks to capture item interactions within outfits \[[2211.16353v1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2211.16353v1)\]\[[2005.12566v1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2005.12566v1)\]\[[2404.18040v1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2404.18040v1)\]. These methods laid the groundwork for recommending compatible clothing items but were limited in personalization and generative capabilities.

With the advent of generative AI, recent studies have shifted toward generative outfit recommendation (GOR), where models synthesize fashion items or entire outfits conditioned on user preferences and compatibility constraints. Approaches such as diffusion-based generative models (e.g., DiFashion) enable parallel generation of multiple fashion images aligned with user-specific conditions, improving fidelity, diversity, and personalization beyond static product datasets \[[2402.17279v3](http://arxiv.org/abs/2402.17279v3)\]. Similarly, GAN-based frameworks like COutfitGAN and OutfitGAN use semantic alignment and collocation classification to synthesize photo-realistic, compatible complementary items given partial outfits, enhancing visual authenticity and compatibility \[[2502.08674v1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2502.08674v1)\]\[[2502.06827v1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2502.06827v1)\]. FCBoost-Net further advances this by iteratively boosting compatibility of multiple synthesized outfit candidates to increase diversity and maintain outfit coherence \[[2502.00992v1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2502.00992v1)\].

Incorporating global style or theme conditions has been another important direction. Methods that embed textual or stylistic queries alongside image features enable coherent outfit generation matching user-specified themes or occasions, combining item-item compatibility with item-query coherence for controllable generation \[[1906.07273v2](http://arxiv.org/abs/1906.07273v2)\]\[[2203.16161v1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2203.16161v1)\]\[[1807.03133v3](http://arxiv.org/abs/1807.03133v3)\]. Transformer-based architectures (OutfitTransformer, History-Aware Transformers) capture higher-order relations among outfit items and user purchase histories to refine compatibility scoring and personalized recommendations \[[2407.00289v2](http://arxiv.org/abs/2407.00289v2)\]\[[2204.04812v2](http://arxiv.org/abs/2204.04812v2)\]. Graph-based models, including transformer-enhanced graph neural networks, also effectively model complex item relationships for seeded outfit generation tasks \[[2304.08098v1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2304.08098v1)\]\[[2005.12566v1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2005.12566v1)\]\[[2404.18040v1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2404.18040v1)\].

Complementary item retrieval and outfit completion tasks, which focus on recommending items to complete partial outfits, have benefited from category-aware subspace attention mechanisms and permutation-invariant neural set transformations, improving retrieval accuracy and scalability \[[2311.16630v1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2311.16630v1)\]\[[1912.08967v2](http://arxiv.org/abs/1912.08967v2)\]. These retrieval-focused approaches complement generative frameworks by enabling the selection of compatible items from existing inventories.

Personalization in fashion recommendation has been explored through leveraging shopper histories, user preferences, and multi-objective reward models. History-aware transformers and hierarchical fashion graph networks jointly model user-outfit and outfit-item relationships to optimize personalized compatibility and recommendation effectiveness \[[2407.00289v2](http://arxiv.org/abs/2407.00289v2)\]\[[2005.12566v1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2005.12566v1)\]. Reinforcement learning from human feedback (RLHF) and multi-expert feedback generation modules have been used to align generative models with subjective user preferences, addressing the challenges of defining explicit reward functions in fashion \[[2402.18571v3](http://arxiv.org/abs/2402.18571v3)\]\[[2502.19312v2](http://arxiv.org/abs/2502.19312v2)\]. Such modules often evaluate generated outfits from multiple perspectives—quality, compatibility, and personalization—to provide comprehensive feedback for model fine-tuning.

The integration of multi-modal data sources, including images, textual descriptions, and style embeddings, has been shown to improve the relevance and accuracy of generated outfits. Methods leveraging multi-modal embeddings and style extraction (e.g., bidirectional LSTM with autoencoders) allow more flexible control over generated outfits based on global style representations \[[1906.07273v2](http://arxiv.org/abs/1906.07273v2)\]\[[1807.03133v3](http://arxiv.org/abs/1807.03133v3)\]. These techniques facilitate generating outfits that are not only compatible but also aligned with nuanced user tastes.

Despite these advances, challenges remain in achieving diverse, personalized generation without extensive supervised training or handcrafted reward functions. The use of direct preference optimization (DPO), originally developed for aligning large language models with human preferences, offers a promising avenue for fine-tuning fashion generative models using automatically generated, multi-perspective feedback without designing task-specific rewards \[[2402.18571v3](http://arxiv.org/abs/2402.18571v3)\]\[[2502.19312v2](http://arxiv.org/abs/2502.19312v2)\]. This aligns with the recent trend toward preference-based fine-tuning, enabling models to better capture diverse and evolving user tastes while maintaining outfit compatibility.

In summary, the landscape of personalized outfit generation spans compatibility learning, generative modeling, style-conditioned synthesis, retrieval-based completion, and preference-driven fine-tuning. Integrating these elements—particularly through multi-expert, multi-modal feedback and preference optimization—represents the frontier for producing diverse, personalized, and compatible fashion outfit recommendations \[[2211.16353v1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2211.16353v1)\]\[[2402.17279v3](http://arxiv.org/abs/2402.17279v3)\]\[[1906.07273v2](http://arxiv.org/abs/1906.07273v2)\]\[[2502.00992v1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2502.00992v1)\]\[[2502.08674v1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2502.08674v1)\]\[[2311.16630v1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2311.16630v1)\]\[[1807.03133v3](http://arxiv.org/abs/1807.03133v3)\]\[[2304.08098v1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2304.08098v1)\]\[[2502.06827v1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2502.06827v1)\]\[[2407.00289v2](http://arxiv.org/abs/2407.00289v2)\]\[[2005.12566v1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2005.12566v1)\]\[[2204.04812v2](http://arxiv.org/abs/2204.04812v2)\]\[[2402.18571v3](http://arxiv.org/abs/2402.18571v3)\]\[[2502.19312v2](http://arxiv.org/abs/2502.19312v2)\].
