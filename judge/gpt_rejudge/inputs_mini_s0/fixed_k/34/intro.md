## Related Works

Fund allocation in financial domains critically relies on accurate time series forecasting and effective decision-making under uncertainty. Traditional approaches often separate forecasting and optimization, typified by prediction-only or Predict-then-Optimize (PnO) frameworks, which suffer from misaligned objectives and suboptimal performance \[[2307.08853](http://arxiv.org/abs/2307.08853)\]. This goal mismatch arises because forecasting models focus on minimizing prediction error, while optimization aims at maximizing allocation utility, leading to discrepancies between predicted and actionable outcomes \[[2311.13247](http://arxiv.org/abs/2311.13247)\]. Approaches that train forecasting and optimization components end-to-end to align objectives have shown promise but typically assume specific forecasting architectures, limiting their generality and adaptability across diverse financial contexts \[[2012.10988](http://arxiv.org/abs/2012.10988)\]\[[2410.1463](http://arxiv.org/abs/2410.14630)\]\[[2503.12648](http://arxiv.org/abs/2503.12648)\].

Uncertainty quantification and calibration in forecasting play a pivotal role in risk-aware financial decision-making. Conventional uncertainty estimation techniques, such as Bayesian neural networks and Monte Carlo dropout, provide probabilistic forecasts but often entail significant computational overhead and depend heavily on model-specific assumptions \[[2401.03006](http://arxiv.org/abs/2401.03006)\]\[[2307.11494](http://arxiv.org/abs/2307.11494)\]\[[2407.02641](http://arxiv.org/abs/2407.02641)\]. More recent advances in conformal prediction frameworks offer distribution-free, theoretically valid uncertainty sets for time series, including multi-step forecasts, thus enhancing calibration and interpretability in sequential decision tasks \[[2103.00264](http://arxiv.org/abs/2103.00264)\]\[[2407.0639](http://arxiv.org/abs/2407.06390)\]. Furthermore, post-hoc calibration methods that address domain shifts improve forecast reliability when market dynamics change, a frequent occurrence in financial time series \[[2012.10988](http://arxiv.org/abs/2012.10988)\].

In the financial forecasting literature, deep learning models have become prevalent due to their capacity to capture complex temporal dependencies. Hybrid architectures combining statistical methods like ARIMA with neural networks (e.g., hybrid ETS-ANN) have demonstrated improved forecasting accuracy over long periods in volatile markets such as equities and cryptocurrencies \[[2307.08853](http://arxiv.org/abs/2307.08853)\]. Models leveraging convolutional neural networks (CNNs) excel at modeling short-term local patterns, while transformer-based architectures capture long-range dependencies, enabling more nuanced intraday price movement predictions \[[2304.04912](http://arxiv.org/abs/2304.04912)\]. Diffusion models have recently emerged as powerful generative frameworks for probabilistic time series forecasting, offering strong performance in refinement and synthetic data generation, which can aid downstream financial models \[[2401.03006](http://arxiv.org/abs/2401.03006)\]\[[2307.11494](http://arxiv.org/abs/2307.11494)\]. Graph-based approaches that learn latent relational structures among multivariate financial time series further enhance forecasting accuracy and calibration by explicitly modeling inter-series dependencies and uncertainty \[[2407.02641](http://arxiv.org/abs/2407.02641)\].

Addressing data scarcity and heterogeneity is crucial for robust financial forecasting. Transfer learning techniques that leverage multi-source historical data have shown effectiveness in forecasting volatility for new financial instruments with limited records, outperforming models trained solely on target data \[[2503.12648](http://arxiv.org/abs/2503.12648)\]. Similarly, retrieval-augmented models incorporate cross-entity information to improve multi-horizon demand forecasting, demonstrating gains in large-scale financial datasets \[[2207.10517](http://arxiv.org/abs/2207.10517)\]. The use of large pretrained language models (LLMs) has also been explored for financial time series analysis, showing competitive performance even in low-data regimes through few-shot or zero-shot learning, though their integration into allocation frameworks remains nascent \[[2308.00065](http://arxiv.org/abs/2308.00065)\]\[[2410.19025](http://arxiv.org/abs/2410.19025)\].

Regularization strategies for learnable embeddings in time series models address the challenge of encoding unique local sequence dynamics without overfitting or reliance on sequence identifiers, thereby improving transferability and robustness—key attributes for financial applications with diverse asset behaviors \[[2410.1463](http://arxiv.org/abs/2410.14630)\]. Additionally, uncertainty-aware forecasting frameworks that jointly learn graph structures and uncertainty distributions yield more accurate and calibrated predictions, which are essential for downstream allocation decisions under risk \[[2407.02641](http://arxiv.org/abs/2407.02641)\].

Finally, real-world financial forecasting must contend with high-frequency and non-stationary data, as well as adversarial robustness concerns. Adaptive learning models using order book data have shown stability and resilience to non-stationarity in high-frequency markets \[[2103.00264](http://arxiv.org/abs/2103.00264)\], while robust optimization approaches defend against adversarial perturbations in financial classification tasks, ensuring model reliability amidst manipulated or noisy inputs \[[1811.11079](http://arxiv.org/abs/1811.11079)\]. The integration of these advances into predictive allocation models points to the importance of combining accurate forecasting, uncertainty quantification, and robust optimization in a unified framework.

The proposed RTS-PnO framework builds on this rich literature by offering an end-to-end risk-aware predict-and-allocate system that is agnostic to the underlying forecasting model, incorporates adaptive uncertainty calibration, and aligns forecasting with allocation objectives. This approach addresses the critical gaps in prior PnO frameworks, uncertainty handling, and model specificity, thereby advancing the state-of-the-art in financial fund allocation under uncertainty.
