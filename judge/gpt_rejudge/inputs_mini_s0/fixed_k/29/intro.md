## Related Works

Maximum Inner Product Search (MIPS) is a critical problem in machine learning and information retrieval, especially for high-dimensional data. Early MIPS approaches either directly leveraged inner product (IP) similarity or reduced MIPS to Nearest Neighbor Search (NNS) under Euclidean metrics via space transformations. Direct IP-based methods often suffer from issues such as local optima and redundant computations due to the lack of triangle inequality, while Euclidean reduction methods can distort the data topology and lose essential information, impacting accuracy and efficiency \[[2503.06882](http://arxiv.org/abs/2503.06882)\]\[[1909.13459](http://arxiv.org/abs/1909.13459)\].

Graph-based indexing and search have emerged as a powerful framework to address these challenges. The inner-product navigable small world graph (ip-NSW) represents a state-of-the-art technique that exploits the norm bias inherent in MIPS—items with large norms tend to dominate results—by constructing a proximity graph where these large-norm items have high in-degree, thus focusing computation on promising candidates and avoiding unnecessary exploration \[[1909.13459](http://arxiv.org/abs/1909.13459)\]. Building on ip-NSW, ip-NSW+ introduces an auxiliary angular proximity graph to refine candidate initialization, resulting in improved robustness and performance across varied data distributions \[[1909.13459](http://arxiv.org/abs/1909.13459)\]\[[2212.07551](http://arxiv.org/abs/2212.07551)\]. These methods highlight how integrating geometric insights about data norm and angular relationships can enhance graph-based MIPS.

Further refining graph-based approaches, recent work has proposed frameworks that equivalently cast MIPS as query-scaled nearest neighbor search without requiring space transformations, thus preserving topology and allowing the use of advanced graph pruning techniques to reduce redundant computations \[[2503.06882](http://arxiv.org/abs/2503.06882)\]. This includes innovations such as the Proximity Graph with Spherical Pathway (PSP) to mitigate clustering around large-norm vectors and Adaptive Early Termination (AET) to limit excessive search once accuracy plateaus. These enhancements yield significant speed-ups and index size reductions, with industrial-scale deployment confirming their practical viability \[[2503.06882](http://arxiv.org/abs/2503.06882)\].

Besides graph-based methods, hashing techniques have also been extensively studied for MIPS. Asymmetric Locality Sensitive Hashing (ALSH) was the first to provide a provably sublinear time algorithm for approximate MIPS by asymmetric transformations converting MIPS into approximate near neighbor search under Euclidean or cosine similarity \[[1410.541](http://arxiv.org/abs/1410.5410)\]\[[1405.5869](http://arxiv.org/abs/1405.5869)\]. Subsequent improvements proposed transformations that convert MIPS to approximate cosine similarity search, solvable efficiently with signed random projections, leading to better theoretical and empirical performance \[[1410.541](http://arxiv.org/abs/1410.5410)\]. However, hashing methods often require substantial preprocessing and large index sizes to maintain accuracy \[[2104.04406](http://arxiv.org/abs/2104.04406)\]\[[1809.08782](http://arxiv.org/abs/1809.08782)\].

Sampling-based and budgeted MIPS algorithms address scenarios with computational constraints. Greedy-MIPS offers a simple yet effective budgeted approach that balances search efficiency and quality by exploiting problem structure, achieving large speed-ups while maintaining high precision \[[1610.03317](http://arxiv.org/abs/1610.03317)\]. Wedge and diamond sampling schemes further extend these ideas, with wedge sampling shown to be competitive or superior under budget constraints, especially when enhanced with deterministic variants and algorithmic engineering techniques \[[1908.08656](http://arxiv.org/abs/1908.08656)\]. These methods emphasize trade-offs between computational budgets and retrieval accuracy.

Dimensionality reduction and adaptive coordinate sampling have also been explored. BanditMIPS employs a randomized adaptive coordinate sampling scheme inspired by multi-armed bandits, achieving complexity independent of vector dimensionality and yielding substantial speed improvements without preprocessing \[[2212.07551](http://arxiv.org/abs/2212.07551)\]\[[1812.0636](http://arxiv.org/abs/1812.06360)\]. Similarly, CEO-based methods use extreme order statistics to focus on informative dimensions, enabling sublinear MIPS with theoretical guarantees and practical variants that scale linearly in space \[[2012.11098](http://arxiv.org/abs/2012.11098)\].

Clustering-based methods utilize geometric partitioning to reduce search space by probing only a fraction of data shards. Spherical k-means and related clustering techniques have demonstrated efficiency and accuracy improvements for approximate MIPS, with recent work applying optimistic query routing principles to further reduce search cost by leveraging distributional moments of inner products within clusters \[[1507.0591](http://arxiv.org/abs/1507.05910)\]\[[2309.09013](http://arxiv.org/abs/2309.09013)\]\[[2405.12207](http://arxiv.org/abs/2405.12207)\]. This aligns with recent efforts to unify dense and sparse vector retrieval paradigms, showing clustering-based retrieval as an effective solution for sparse MIPS while suggesting novel indexing structures for mixed dense-sparse spaces \[[2309.09013](http://arxiv.org/abs/2309.09013)\]\[[2301.10622](http://arxiv.org/abs/2301.10622)\].

Recent studies have also addressed diversity-aware MIPS, proposing algorithms that balance relevance and result diversity by minimizing pairwise similarity among retrieved items, thereby enhancing user experience in recommendation scenarios \[[2402.13858](http://arxiv.org/abs/2402.13858)\]. Additionally, reverse MIPS considers identifying users likely to be interested in given items, introducing efficient indexing and filtering techniques to accelerate such queries dramatically \[[2110.07131](http://arxiv.org/abs/2110.07131)\].

Parameter tuning for graph-based MIPS remains a critical practical concern. Black-box optimization methods have been developed to tune off-the-shelf graph indexes across varying vector dimensions and database sizes, optimizing recall and throughput without manual intervention \[[2309.00472](http://arxiv.org/abs/2309.00472)\]. Furthermore, statistical indicators capturing data topology have been identified to guide parameter selection, improving adaptability and scalability of MIPS systems \[[2503.06882](http://arxiv.org/abs/2503.06882)\].

In summary, the landscape of MIPS research reveals a convergence toward hybrid methods that integrate inner product and Euclidean metric insights, advanced graph structures, adaptive search strategies, and principled parameter tuning. These developments motivate approaches like the Metric-Amphibious Graph (MAG) and Adaptive Navigation with Metric Switch (ANMS), which dynamically leverage both IP and Euclidean metrics tailored to data topology for superior performance \[[2503.06882](http://arxiv.org/abs/2503.06882)\].
