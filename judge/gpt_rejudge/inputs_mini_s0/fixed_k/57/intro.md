## Related Works

Generative retrieval (GR) has emerged as a compelling paradigm in information retrieval (IR), shifting from traditional index-retrieve pipelines to end-to-end models that generate document identifiers (docids) directly from queries \[[2304.04171](http://arxiv.org/abs/2304.04171)\]. This approach enables seamless integration with large language models (LLMs) and offers potential efficiency gains by replacing conventional search indexes with neural networks \[[2504.09935](http://arxiv.org/abs/2504.09935)\]. Early GR frameworks typically relied on fixed or rule-based docids, such as document titles or clustered embeddings, which often failed to capture full document semantics \[[2408.02152](http://arxiv.org/abs/2408.02152)\]. More recent work, such as GenRet, introduced learned tokenization methods that generate short discrete semantic docids via discrete auto-encoding, improving generalization and retrieval accuracy on standard benchmarks \[[2408.02152](http://arxiv.org/abs/2408.02152)\]. Similarly, Term-Set Generation (TSGen) proposes sets of weighted terms as docids along with permutation-invariant decoding to enhance robustness and recall, addressing limitations of sequential token generation in constrained auto-regressive decoding \[[2305.13859](http://arxiv.org/abs/2305.13859)\]\[[2504.09935](http://arxiv.org/abs/2504.09935)\].

Despite these advances, most GR systems have been developed and evaluated under static corpus assumptions, limiting their applicability in real-world scenarios where document collections evolve continuously. This dynamic nature raises critical challenges for GR models, such as the ability to incrementally index new documents without degrading performance on previously learned content. Recent studies have begun to address this gap by investigating GR over dynamic corpora. For instance, CLEVER introduces a continual learning framework that incrementally updates quantization codebooks for numeric docids with adaptive thresholds and employs memory-augmented mechanisms to mitigate forgetting of prior knowledge \[[2308.14968](http://arxiv.org/abs/2308.14968)\]. Empirical results show CLEVER’s effectiveness and efficiency in maintaining retrieval quality over evolving datasets \[[2308.14968](http://arxiv.org/abs/2308.14968)\].

Another line of research highlights the differential impact of docid design on dynamic corpus performance. Text-based docids—such as titles or n-grams—demonstrate superior generalization to unseen documents due to their semantic alignment with pretrained LLM knowledge, fine-grained representation, and high lexical diversity \[[2305.18952](http://arxiv.org/abs/2305.18952)\]\[[2408.02152](http://arxiv.org/abs/2408.02152)\]. In contrast, numeric-based docids, while computationally efficient, are prone to overfitting the initial corpus distribution, resulting in significant performance degradation as the corpus updates \[[2305.18952](http://arxiv.org/abs/2305.18952)\]. This overfitting arises because numeric docids often do not leverage semantic richness and are sensitive to corpus shifts, limiting adaptability in dynamic environments \[[2305.18952](http://arxiv.org/abs/2305.18952)\]\[[2504.09935](http://arxiv.org/abs/2504.09935)\].

Few-shot indexing methods further explore avoiding expensive retraining by prompting LLMs to generate docids in a non-parametric manner, creating a docid bank for the corpus upfront, and constraining retrieval to this bank at query time \[[2408.02152](http://arxiv.org/abs/2408.02152)\]. This approach balances efficiency and adaptability, showing improved retrieval performance over fully training-dependent GR models on dynamic collections. Additionally, direct document relevance optimization (DDRO) methods address token-level misalignment in docid generation by optimizing ranking objectives directly, bypassing the instability and complexity of reinforcement learning strategies \[[2504.05181](http://arxiv.org/abs/2504.05181)\]. DDRO achieves improved retrieval effectiveness on benchmarks like MS MARCO and Natural Questions, highlighting optimization techniques that could benefit dynamic corpus scenarios.

From a broader IR perspective, the adaptability of GR systems to dynamic corpora also ties into emerging frameworks on dynamic information retrieval, which emphasize multi-stage interactions, user feedback, and evolving search intents \[[1601.04605](http://arxiv.org/abs/1601.04605)\]\[[2407.11605](http://arxiv.org/abs/2407.11605)\]. Such frameworks underscore the importance of incorporating user interaction and continual learning mechanisms to maintain retrieval relevance over time. Furthermore, hybrid retrieval strategies blending semantic search indexes with generative components have demonstrated superior performance in large-scale and evolving corpora, suggesting that hybridization may complement pure GR approaches \[[2404.0722](http://arxiv.org/abs/2404.07220)\].

Finally, theoretical analyses reveal inherent constraints in the auto-regressive decoding process of GR models, including errors induced by constrained decoding and beam search strategies that may limit recall despite perfect top-1 precision \[[2504.09935](http://arxiv.org/abs/2504.09935)\]. These insights motivate further research into decoding algorithms and docid designs that better support generalization and robustness in dynamic retrieval contexts.

In summary, the literature underscores three critical themes for GR over dynamic corpora: (i) the semantic and lexical advantages of text-based docids for generalization and adaptability; (ii) the efficiency but limited robustness of numeric-based docids; and (iii) the promise of continual learning frameworks and few-shot indexing to balance adaptability and computational cost. Our work builds on these insights by systematically evaluating representative GR models on dynamic datasets and proposing a multi-docid design that synergizes the strengths of both numeric and text-based representations without retraining.
