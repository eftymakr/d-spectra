## Related Works

Sequential recommendation systems have witnessed significant advancements aimed at modeling user behavior dynamics and improving recommendation performance. A major research focus has been on the integration of attention mechanisms to capture evolving user preferences and item relationships. For example, models incorporating self-attention effectively distill informative patterns from user interaction sequences, but often face challenges in computational efficiency and handling long sequences \[[2310.02367](http://arxiv.org/abs/2310.02367)\]. To address temporal dynamics explicitly, time-aware self-attention mechanisms have been proposed that weigh historical actions based on their timestamps and context, thereby capturing nuanced user preferences over time \[[2208.1333](http://arxiv.org/abs/2208.13330)\]\[[2002.00741](http://arxiv.org/abs/2002.00741)\].

Beyond attention, state-space models (SSMs) have been explored for their efficiency in modeling temporal dynamics and processing variable-length sequences with linear complexity. These models compress temporal information into latent states, enabling scalable and effective sequence modeling \[[2310.02367](http://arxiv.org/abs/2310.02367)\]. The combination of attention with state-space modeling has been recognized as promising, as SSMs capture compressed temporal dependencies while attention mechanisms model both similar and diverse item relationships, aligning well with the theoretical underpinnings of STAR-Rec.

Handling diverse user behaviors and heterogeneous interaction patterns is another critical challenge. Mixture-of-Experts (MoE) architectures have demonstrated strong capacity to adaptively route different types of user preferences and item characteristics to specialized experts. For instance, hierarchical and facet-aware MoE models disentangle multiple preference facets and dynamically integrate expert outputs for improved sequential recommendation \[[2501.14269](http://arxiv.org/abs/2501.14269)\]\[[2411.01457](http://arxiv.org/abs/2411.01457)\]. Multi-level MoE frameworks have also been applied to multi-modal data and multi-task settings, effectively capturing complex user interests and enhancing recommendation accuracy \[[2109.05261](http://arxiv.org/abs/2109.05261)\]\[[2102.0695](http://arxiv.org/abs/2102.06950)\]. This adaptive routing aligns with STAR-Rec’s approach to managing focused category-specific browsing and diverse exploratory behaviors.

Several works address the evolving nature of user preferences by decomposing user behavior sequences into sub-sequences or independent preference components, often leveraging reinforcement learning to simulate preference evolution and allocate behaviors accordingly \[[2203.16942](http://arxiv.org/abs/2203.16942)\]. These approaches improve the modeling of inconsistent and volatile user interests, which complements the preference-aware attention mechanism in STAR-Rec that captures both similar and diverse item relationships.

Robustness to noisy and sparse user interactions is critical for reliable user representation learning. Counterfactual sequence synthesis and contrastive learning frameworks have been introduced to strengthen user embeddings by distinguishing indispensable from dispensable behavioral concepts, thus improving model stability and generalization \[[2109.05261](http://arxiv.org/abs/2109.05261)\]\[[2404.13878](http://arxiv.org/abs/2404.13878)\]. Incorporating negative feedback through contrastive learning has similarly enhanced session-based recommendations by explicitly modeling user skips and refining item embeddings \[[2409.07367](http://arxiv.org/abs/2409.07367)\].

Moreover, multi-objective optimization approaches have been proposed to simultaneously optimize diverse and sometimes conflicting recommendation goals, such as accuracy, diversity, and commercial metrics, via multi-gradient descent techniques. These frameworks underscore the complexity of real-world recommendation scenarios where multiple user and stakeholder objectives must be balanced \[[2001.00846](http://arxiv.org/abs/2001.00846)\].

In summary, recent research directions closely relate to STAR-Rec’s contributions: preference-aware attention aligns with time-aware and logical reasoning-based attention mechanisms \[[2208.1333](http://arxiv.org/abs/2208.13330)\]\[[2002.00741](http://arxiv.org/abs/2002.00741)\]; state-space models offer computationally efficient temporal dynamics modeling \[[2310.02367](http://arxiv.org/abs/2310.02367)\]; and mixture-of-experts architectures provide adaptive specialization to diverse user behaviors \[[2501.14269](http://arxiv.org/abs/2501.14269)\]\[[2411.01457](http://arxiv.org/abs/2411.01457)\]. Together, these advances form the foundation for STAR-Rec’s unified framework that synergistically integrates attention, state-space modeling, and mixture-of-experts to effectively handle sequence length variations and complex interaction patterns in sequential recommendation.
