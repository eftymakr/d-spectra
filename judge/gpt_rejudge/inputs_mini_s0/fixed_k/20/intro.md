## Related Works

Single-pixel imaging (SPI) leverages structured illumination and a single-pixel detector to capture images in spectral ranges beyond conventional focal plane arrays, such as terahertz or photon-level regimes. Traditional SPI methods often suffer from limited image quality and high sampling requirements, motivating the adoption of advanced reconstruction algorithms and deep learning techniques for improved performance \[[2310.16869](http://arxiv.org/abs/2310.16869)\]. Early SPI methods employed random or deterministic patterns like Hadamard and Fourier masks to optimize sampling efficiency and reconstruction fidelity \[[2310.16869](http://arxiv.org/abs/2310.16869)\].

Deep learning has been integrated into SPI to address the challenges of limited reconstruction quality and slow iterative algorithms. Notably, deep learning-based single-pixel imaging frameworks have been developed for various applications including super-resolution, imaging through scattering media, photon-level imaging, and optical encryption, demonstrating superior reconstruction quality and speed compared to classical methods \[[2310.16869](http://arxiv.org/abs/2310.16869)\]. These frameworks enable both image reconstruction and image-free sensing tasks, expanding the applicability of SPI.

In the realm of inverse imaging problems, two dominant classes of deep learning-based solvers have emerged: plug-and-play (PnP) methods and deep algorithm unrolling (or unfolding) approaches. PnP methods incorporate pre-trained denoisers as implicit regularizers within iterative optimization loops, allowing flexible adaptation to varying compression ratios (CRs) and noise levels without retraining. However, PnP techniques typically face limitations in reconstruction accuracy and computational speed due to iterative procedures and the inherent constraints of denoiser priors \[[2410.02423](http://arxiv.org/abs/2410.02423)\]. Conversely, deep unrolling methods convert a truncated iterative optimization algorithm into a multi-stage end-to-end trainable network, often achieving superior accuracy and faster inference times. The drawback lies in their sensitivity to changes in forward model parameters such as CR, necessitating fine-tuning or retraining for different sampling conditions \[[1902.03493](http://arxiv.org/abs/1902.03493)\]\[[2107.11007](http://arxiv.org/abs/2107.11007)\]\[[2402.12872](http://arxiv.org/abs/2402.12872)\].

Unrolling approaches have been extensively studied in imaging inverse problems such as deblurring, compressive imaging, and magnetic resonance imaging (MRI). For example, unrolled networks based on Half-Quadratic Splitting (HQS) demonstrate interpretability and convergence guarantees, while achieving competitive performance in image deconvolution \[[2402.12872](http://arxiv.org/abs/2402.12872)\]. Dynamic proximal unrolling networks (DPUNet) efficiently handle multiple compressive imaging modalities and varying sampling ratios within a single trained model by dynamically adjusting proximal operator parameters at inference \[[2107.11007](http://arxiv.org/abs/2107.11007)\]. Similarly, deep unrolling techniques that integrate alternating direction method of multipliers (ADMM) and proximal gradient frameworks have been applied in MRI reconstruction, showing improved generalization to different acquisition settings by decoupling regularization from the forward model \[[2211.16881](http://arxiv.org/abs/2211.16881)\]\[[2307.09818](http://arxiv.org/abs/2307.09818)\]\[[2502.11749](http://arxiv.org/abs/2502.11749)\].

Recent advances have focused on enhancing unrolled networks via attention mechanisms and adaptive thresholding to better exploit sparsity and low-rank priors in dynamic imaging, as demonstrated in networks like JotlasNet and DUS-Net for dynamic MRI reconstruction \[[2502.11749](http://arxiv.org/abs/2502.11749)\]\[[2307.09818](http://arxiv.org/abs/2307.09818)\]. Moreover, uncertainty quantification has been incorporated into deep unrolling frameworks using Bayesian neural networks, enabling the estimation of epistemic and aleatoric uncertainties alongside high-quality reconstructions, which is critical for safety-sensitive imaging applications \[[2207.00698](http://arxiv.org/abs/2207.00698)\].

PnP and unrolling methods have also been combined with generative modeling techniques such as flow matching to improve restoration tasks including denoising, inpainting, and super-resolution. The PnP-Flow framework alternates between gradient descent on data fidelity, reprojections onto learned generative paths, and denoising with pre-trained models, achieving state-of-the-art restoration performance while maintaining computational efficiency \[[2410.02423](http://arxiv.org/abs/2410.02423)\].

Algorithm unrolling has been recognized for bridging the gap between classical iterative algorithms and deep learning, offering interpretable, efficient, and high-performance network architectures for a variety of imaging inverse problems \[[1912.10557](http://arxiv.org/abs/1912.10557)\]. Theoretical efforts have addressed convergence guarantees for unrolled networks, ensuring that learned parameters do not violate the stability of the underlying optimization algorithms \[[2402.12872](http://arxiv.org/abs/2402.12872)\]\[[1711.07653](http://arxiv.org/abs/1711.07653)\]. Techniques such as operator sketching have been proposed to reduce computational and memory demands of unrolled networks in high-dimensional imaging tasks, further enhancing their practicality \[[2203.11156](http://arxiv.org/abs/2203.11156)\].

In single-pixel imaging specifically, deep learning has improved reconstruction quality by learning data-driven priors, while unrolling methods enable faster inference by approximating iterative solvers within trainable networks \[[2310.16869](http://arxiv.org/abs/2310.16869)\]. However, unrolled networks often lack flexibility across varying CRs, contrasting with the inherent adaptability of PnP methods. Bridging this gap, recent works have proposed proximal unrolling networks trained with generalized proximal trajectory loss functions that encourage learned deep image restorers (DIRs) to approximate ideal proximal operators. This approach combines the flexible CR handling of PnP methods with the superior accuracy and speed of unrolled networks, achieving a single model capable of adapting to varying CRs without retraining \[[2402.12872](http://arxiv.org/abs/2402.12872)\]\[[2211.16881](http://arxiv.org/abs/2211.16881)\].

In summary, the integration of deep learning with classical optimization frameworks through unrolling and PnP paradigms has revolutionized inverse imaging problems, including single-pixel imaging. Current research trends focus on enhancing adaptability, interpretability, and computational efficiency of these methods, as well as extending their theoretical foundations and uncertainty quantification capabilities for reliable and flexible image reconstruction.
