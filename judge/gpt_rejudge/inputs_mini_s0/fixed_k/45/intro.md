## Related Works

Fine-grained emotion classification extends beyond traditional sentiment analysis, which typically categorizes text into broad sentiment polarity classes such as positive, negative, or neutral \[[2005.11882](http://arxiv.org/abs/2005.11882)\]\[[2005.00547](http://arxiv.org/abs/2005.00547)\]. This finer granularity aims to capture a wide range of emotional states, including joy, anger, fear, surprise, and others, which provide richer insights into user experiences, especially in domains like app reviews where understanding nuanced user feedback is essential \[[2006.09739](http://arxiv.org/abs/2006.09739)\]\[[1703.02256](http://arxiv.org/abs/1703.02256)\].

Several datasets and annotation frameworks have been developed to support fine-grained emotion analysis. The GoEmotions dataset, for example, offers a large-scale annotated corpus of 58k Reddit comments labeled with 27 distinct emotion categories plus neutral, facilitating research on emotion detection across diverse contexts \[[2005.00547](http://arxiv.org/abs/2005.00547)\]. Similarly, fine-grained emotion classification in dialogues and metaphoric language has been explored, with datasets and models designed to capture subtle and mixed emotional states, including aesthetic emotions in poetry and emotions conveyed via metaphors in Chinese advertisements \[[2003.07723](http://arxiv.org/abs/2003.07723)\]\[[2505.13483](http://arxiv.org/abs/2505.13483)\]\[[2012.13624](http://arxiv.org/abs/2012.13624)\]. These works highlight the importance of well-defined annotation schemas and the complexity inherent in emotion labeling.

In the context of app reviews, research has mainly focused on sentiment polarity or coarse-grained emotion detection, often leveraging machine learning and NLP techniques to classify user feedback and extract actionable insights for developers \[[2006.09739](http://arxiv.org/abs/2006.09739)\]\[[1703.02256](http://arxiv.org/abs/1703.02256)\]\[[1810.05187](http://arxiv.org/abs/1810.05187)\]. However, the complexity and noisiness of app review data pose significant challenges for fine-grained emotion annotation and automated classification \[[2408.01063](http://arxiv.org/abs/2408.01063)\]\[[2212.09289](http://arxiv.org/abs/2212.09289)\]. Studies have shown that app reviews often contain multiple topics and sentiments within a single review, complicating feature extraction and sentiment detection tasks \[[2408.01063](http://arxiv.org/abs/2408.01063)\]\[[1908.10816](http://arxiv.org/abs/1908.10816)\]. Moreover, user reviews exhibit subjective bias and varied linguistic styles, requiring robust modeling approaches.

Large Language Models (LLMs), particularly transformer-based models such as BERT and GPT-4, have recently demonstrated promising capabilities in fine-grained emotion classification and annotation automation. LLMs can reduce manual annotation workload and achieve substantial agreement with human annotators, although discrepancies remain due to the subjective nature of emotions and contextual subtleties \[[2412.07906](http://arxiv.org/abs/2412.07906)\]\[[2104.05861](http://arxiv.org/abs/2104.05861)\]. GPT-4, for instance, has been used to assist annotation by flagging low-quality labels and improving downstream learning performance, underscoring the potential and limitations of LLMs in this domain \[[2412.07906](http://arxiv.org/abs/2412.07906)\]. Domain-adaptive pre-training of transformer models with app review corpora has further improved classification accuracy and efficiency, reinforcing the value of specialized models for app feedback analysis \[[2408.01063](http://arxiv.org/abs/2408.01063)\]\[[2104.05861](http://arxiv.org/abs/2104.05861)\].

Annotation quality and guidelines are critical for training reliable emotion classifiers. Research has shown that clearer, more structured annotation guidelines lead to less noisy and more informative extracted features, which benefit both manual and automatic classification efforts in app review mining \[[1810.05187](http://arxiv.org/abs/1810.05187)\]. Incorporating multi-task learning by modeling emotion definitions alongside prediction tasks has also enhanced fine-grained emotion classification performance and generalization across datasets \[[2107.12135](http://arxiv.org/abs/2107.12135)\]. Additionally, addressing class imbalance and label granularity remains an ongoing challenge in fine-grained emotion recognition \[[2411.0616](http://arxiv.org/abs/2411.06160)\]\[[2411.18162](http://arxiv.org/abs/2411.18162)\].

Beyond pure emotion classification, several studies integrate emotional analysis with other tasks such as feature extraction, privacy concern detection, explanation need identification, and summarization in app reviews, demonstrating the multifaceted utility of emotion-aware NLP systems in software engineering \[[2212.09289](http://arxiv.org/abs/2212.09289)\]\[[2307.04367](http://arxiv.org/abs/2307.04367)\]\[[2210.06235](http://arxiv.org/abs/2210.06235)\]. These approaches provide comprehensive insights that support app developers in prioritizing features, addressing user concerns, and improving user experience.

In summary, existing work establishes the foundation for fine-grained emotion classification in app reviews through annotated datasets, advanced modeling techniques, and LLM-assisted annotation. Nonetheless, challenges persist in handling the complexity of emotional interpretation, annotation subjectivity, and domain-specific nuances. This paper builds on these insights by adapting Plutchik’s taxonomy to app reviews, proposing structured annotation guidelines, and evaluating LLM-based automation, thereby contributing to more effective emotion analysis in user feedback.
