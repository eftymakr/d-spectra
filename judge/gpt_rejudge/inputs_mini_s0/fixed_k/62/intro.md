## Related Works

Generative retrieval has emerged as a novel paradigm that replaces traditional search index data structures with a large-scale neural network model, directly generating document identifiers (docids) via corpus-specific constrained decoding. This approach offers improvements in retrieval efficiency and seamless integration with generative large language models (LLMs) \[[2504.05181](http://arxiv.org/abs/2504.05181)\]. Central to generative retrieval is the concept of learned differentiable search indexes that enable end-to-end retrieval by autoregressively generating docids constrained to a corpus-specific prefix tree, ensuring legal outputs \[[2504.05181](http://arxiv.org/abs/2504.05181)\]\[[2010.10789](http://arxiv.org/abs/2010.10789)\]. However, these constraints introduce fundamental limitations affecting generalization and decoding quality.

Recent research has focused on overcoming these limitations from both theoretical and practical perspectives. Lightweight and Direct Document Relevance Optimization (DDRO) addresses token-level and document-level misalignment by optimizing docid generation directly through pairwise ranking, bypassing complex reinforcement learning procedures. DDRO achieves notable performance gains on benchmark datasets such as MS MARCO and Natural Questions, highlighting the importance of direct relevance alignment in generative retrieval \[[2502.03685](http://arxiv.org/abs/2502.03685)\].

Decoding strategies play a crucial role in generative retrieval performance. Beam search is the predominant method for constrained autoregressive decoding but is limited by its greedy nature and tendency to produce low-diversity outputs. Diverse Beam Search (DBS) introduces objectives to promote output diversity, improving exploration of the search space and yielding better top-1 results with minimal overhead \[[1610.02424](http://arxiv.org/abs/1610.02424)\]. Beyond beam search, Monte-Carlo Tree Search (MCTS) has been proposed both for machine translation decoding and retrieval chunk selection, balancing multiple constraints and optimizing retrieval combinations considering chunk correlations and non-monotonic utility \[[2104.05336](http://arxiv.org/abs/2104.05336)\]\[[2210.02068](http://arxiv.org/abs/2210.02068)\]. Notably, CARROT leverages MCTS to optimize chunk retrieval order and configuration for Retrieval-Augmented Generation (RAG), achieving up to 30% improvements over baselines \[[2210.02068](http://arxiv.org/abs/2210.02068)\]. These findings suggest that more sophisticated metric-driven and search-based decoding methods can significantly enhance generative retrieval and RAG systems.

Other approaches address the limitations of fixed parametric vocabularies in generative retrieval models. Nonparametric Decoding (Np Decoding) augments decoder vocab embeddings with external memory of contextualized embeddings, allowing models to utilize both parametric knowledge and nonparametric contextual cues. This method yields significant improvements across multiple retrieval datasets and demonstrates strong zero-shot capabilities, indicating enhanced generalization potential \[[2210.02068](http://arxiv.org/abs/2210.02068)\].

The integration of generative retrieval with retrieval-augmented systems has also been explored. Blended RAG combines semantic search with hybrid query-based retrievers, blending dense and sparse indexes to improve retrieval accuracy for large-scale corpora, surpassing fine-tuned baselines on datasets like Natural Questions and TREC-COVID \[[2404.0722](http://arxiv.org/abs/2404.07220)\]. This hybrid approach underscores the complementary nature of generative and traditional retrieval methods.

In the context of autoregressive decoding efficiency, recent methods such as Auto-Parallel Auto-Regressive decoding (APAR) enable LLMs to plan generation hierarchically and perform parallel decoding steps, reducing latency and computational costs substantially \[[2401.06761](http://arxiv.org/abs/2401.06761)\]. Similarly, the Planning Ahead in Generative Retrieval (PAG) method guides simultaneous decoding of set-based and sequential identifiers for documents, yielding large improvements in retrieval effectiveness and query latency speedup \[[2404.146](http://arxiv.org/abs/2404.14600)\]. These innovations highlight the ongoing efforts to overcome the inherent sequential bottleneck of autoregressive generation in retrieval.

From a theoretical standpoint, the limitations imposed by constraints and decoding algorithms on generative retrieval have been examined through probabilistic and divergence measures. The KL divergence between ground-truth and model-predicted step-wise marginal distributions provides a principled lower bound on retrieval error under constrained decoding \[[2504.05181](http://arxiv.org/abs/2504.05181)\]. This aligns with theoretical analyses on optimization landscapes and convergence guarantees for related sampling algorithms in generative modeling \[[1610.02424](http://arxiv.org/abs/1610.02424)\]. Furthermore, generalized Kullback-Leibler divergence losses have been proposed to mitigate asymmetric optimization biases and improve model prediction accuracy, which is relevant to constrained generation scenarios \[[2010.10789](http://arxiv.org/abs/2010.10789)\].

Collectively, these works elucidate the challenges and opportunities in generative retrieval, spanning constrained decoding methods, optimization strategies, model generalization, and integration with retrieval-augmented systems. They lay a foundation for advancing understanding of the limitations inherent in autoregressive generative retrieval and inspire future research towards more robust and generalizable retrieval paradigms \[[2504.05181](http://arxiv.org/abs/2504.05181)\]\[[2502.03685](http://arxiv.org/abs/2502.03685)\]\[[2210.02068](http://arxiv.org/abs/2210.02068)\]\[[2104.05336](http://arxiv.org/abs/2104.05336)\]\[[2404.146](http://arxiv.org/abs/2404.14600)\]\[[1610.02424](http://arxiv.org/abs/1610.02424)\]\[[2010.10789](http://arxiv.org/abs/2010.10789)\]\[[2404.0722](http://arxiv.org/abs/2404.07220)\]\[[2401.06761](http://arxiv.org/abs/2401.06761)\].
