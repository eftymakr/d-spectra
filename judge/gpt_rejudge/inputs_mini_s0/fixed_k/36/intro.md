## Related Works

Deploying large language models (LLMs) on resource-constrained edge devices presents significant challenges due to limited memory, compute capacity, and power availability. To overcome these barriers, research has focused on algorithmic model compression, hardware acceleration, and integrated co-design approaches that jointly optimize model efficiency and hardware utilization.

### Model Compression Techniques

Model compression is critical to fitting LLMs within the tight resource budgets of edge devices. Pruning and quantization are among the most effective strategies. Hardware-aware pruning methods dynamically exploit sparsity to reduce inference latency substantially, as demonstrated in CNN accelerators where pruning tailored to hardware scheduling yielded up to 45% faster inference \[[2408.14055](http://arxiv.org/abs/2408.14055)\]. Activation sparsity, which enforces zero outputs in feed-forward networks (FFNs), can reduce memory and computational demands by approximately 50% with negligible accuracy loss, offering a complementary compression axis to traditional methods \[[2412.12178](http://arxiv.org/abs/2412.12178)\]. Moreover, mixed-precision quantization schemes adapt bitwidths per layer or operation by modeling quantization as a stochastic process, enabling better accuracy-to-efficiency trade-offs suitable for edge hardware \[[2205.15437](http://arxiv.org/abs/2205.15437)\]. Advanced quantization techniques like W2A8KV4 (2-bit weights, 8-bit activations, 4-bit KV cache) have also been proposed to reduce bandwidth and memory overheads critical for long-sequence LLM inference \[[2505.03745](http://arxiv.org/abs/2505.03745)\]. Together, these approaches allow substantial model size and computation reductions while maintaining robust generation quality.

### Hardware Acceleration

Custom hardware accelerators, especially FPGA-based designs, have emerged as a promising solution to accelerate LLM inference on edge devices. FPGAs provide reconfigurability that supports model-specific optimizations, enabling improvements in throughput and energy efficiency compared to general-purpose processors \[[2408.00462](http://arxiv.org/abs/2408.00462)\]\[[2110.00478](http://arxiv.org/abs/2110.00478)\]. For example, integrating matrix multiplication accelerators with block floating-point quantization on FPGA platforms reduced token latency by 11× for smaller LLM variants \[[2408.00462](http://arxiv.org/abs/2408.00462)\]. Accelerator designs that incorporate speculative decoding improve efficiency further by reducing unnecessary computation during LLM generation \[[2412.19925](http://arxiv.org/abs/2412.19925)\]. Hardware/software co-design frameworks streamline development workflows, reducing design iteration times and enabling rapid performance tuning for edge deployments \[[2110.00478](http://arxiv.org/abs/2110.00478)\]\[[2104.02251](http://arxiv.org/abs/2104.02251)\]. Algorithm-hardware co-design efforts, such as AccLLM, combine pruning, quantization, and specialized attention mechanisms with FPGA-implemented reconfigurable computing engines to achieve over 4× energy efficiency and nearly 3× throughput improvements compared to prior art \[[2505.03745](http://arxiv.org/abs/2505.03745)\]. These innovations illustrate how tight integration between model compression and hardware design can unlock practical edge LLM deployments.

### Algorithm-Hardware Co-Design and System-Level Optimization

A growing body of work emphasizes holistic co-design strategies that jointly optimize model architectures, compression schemes, and hardware platforms to maximize efficiency on edge devices. Hardware-aware neural architecture search frameworks identify hybrid network architectures combining convolution and self-attention that match target device latency profiles across diverse hardware platforms \[[2408.15395](http://arxiv.org/abs/2408.15395)\]. Similarly, hardware-aware pruning and compression frameworks consider device variability and environmental factors to maintain consistent inference performance across device clusters \[[2501.1524](http://arxiv.org/abs/2501.15240)\]. Co-design methodologies such as SECDA facilitate rapid exploration of DNN accelerator designs on edge FPGAs, achieving up to 3.5× speedup and nearly 3× energy reduction over CPU-only baselines \[[2110.00478](http://arxiv.org/abs/2110.00478)\]. These efforts underscore the importance of integrated software-hardware pipelines that tailor both models and accelerators to edge constraints.

### Approximate and Specialized Computing Paradigms

Approximate computing techniques exploit the inherent error tolerance of AI workloads to reduce energy consumption without significant accuracy degradation. For explainable AI (XAI) tasks, approximate matrix computations combined with domain-specific algorithmic approximations have doubled energy efficiency on TPU-based edge devices \[[2504.17929](http://arxiv.org/abs/2504.17929)\]. Non-MAC-based hyperdimensional computing accelerators offer low-SWaP (size, weight, and power) alternatives capable of real-time reconfiguration and few-shot learning suitable for edge \[[2206.05128](http://arxiv.org/abs/2206.05128)\]. Furthermore, Mixture-of-Experts (MoE) LLMs benefit from expert-level sparsification and skipping strategies that reduce model size and speed up inference while maintaining task performance, allowing more scalable edge deployment \[[2402.148](http://arxiv.org/abs/2402.14800)\]. These specialized paradigms complement traditional compression and acceleration techniques.

### Challenges Beyond Performance

Deploying LLMs on edge devices also raises concerns regarding fairness, privacy, and bias. Studies show that edge-deployed LLMs exhibit increased bias tendencies compared to cloud or desktop deployments, necessitating mitigation strategies such as iterative feedback loops applying layer-wise constraints during inference to reduce bias by over 79% \[[2502.11349](http://arxiv.org/abs/2502.11349)\]. Additionally, secure hardware acceleration and encrypted computation frameworks are vital to preserving data privacy in edge environments \[[1801.0561](http://arxiv.org/abs/1801.05610)\]. These considerations extend the scope of design beyond hardware and efficiency toward trustworthy, responsible AI at the edge.

### Summary

In summary, the deployment of LLMs on edge devices relies on a synergistic combination of model compression (pruning, quantization, activation sparsity), specialized hardware acceleration (FPGA-based reconfigurable engines, speculative decoding), and algorithm-hardware co-design frameworks that jointly optimize models and platforms. Approximate computing and novel architectures further push the boundaries of energy efficiency and latency reduction. Concurrently, addressing fairness and privacy concerns remains crucial. The proposed CLONE system builds upon and advances these lines of work by integrating real-time energy optimization with scalable hardware accelerator design, achieving significant inference acceleration and energy savings on off-the-shelf edge platforms.
