## Related Works

Model calibration is critical for enhancing the reliability and interpretability of machine learning predictions, especially in ranking systems where the emphasis traditionally lies on preserving relative order rather than ensuring the accuracy of absolute prediction values. This section reviews relevant literature on calibration methods in ranking and classification contexts, identifies their limitations, and situates the contribution of Unconstrained Monotonic Neural Networks (UMNN) within this landscape.

Classical calibration approaches frequently rely on predefined transformation functions that preserve order, such as piece-wise linear mappings or parametric scaling methods like Platt scaling and isotonic regression. These methods are widely used in credit scoring and binary classification to adjust predicted probabilities to better align with observed outcomes \[[1710.08901](http://arxiv.org/abs/1710.08901)\]. While effective in many scenarios, these fixed-form calibrators often lack the flexibility to capture complex, nonlinear calibration mappings required in more intricate ranking tasks. Moreover, relying solely on rank-based metrics without proper calibration can lead to suboptimal downstream decisions where absolute prediction accuracy is essential \[[1710.08901](http://arxiv.org/abs/1710.08901)\]\[[2101.04356](http://arxiv.org/abs/2101.04356)\].

To overcome the expressiveness limitations of traditional methods, recent research has explored neural network-based calibrators that can learn complex, nonlinear mappings. Monotonic neural networks, which enforce strict monotonicity by constraining derivatives or weights, have emerged as a promising tool to maintain order-preservation while enhancing functional flexibility \[[1908.05164](http://arxiv.org/abs/1908.05164)\]\[[2106.03228](http://arxiv.org/abs/2106.03228)\]. The UMNN architecture, in particular, models arbitrary monotonic functions by ensuring positivity of the derivative through an unconstrained neural network, thereby removing restrictive assumptions on weight constraints while guaranteeing invertibility and monotonicity \[[1908.05164](http://arxiv.org/abs/1908.05164)\]. This capacity allows UMNNs to serve as powerful calibration functions that better adapt to data distributions without distorting the inherent order of predictions.

UMNNs have demonstrated effectiveness beyond calibration, such as in distributional reinforcement learning, where modeling continuous monotonic transformations of probability distributions is crucial \[[2106.03228](http://arxiv.org/abs/2106.03228)\]. These applications underscore UMNNs’ versatility in representing complex monotone mappings, which aligns with the requirement for flexible yet order-preserving calibrators in ranking systems. By integrating UMNNs as calibrators, the calibration framework gains higher modeling power compared to fixed-form functions, addressing a key limitation in prior calibration methods.

Complementary to the calibrator architecture, loss functions tailored to calibration objectives play a vital role. Conventional calibration losses often focus on pointwise probability alignment or rely on multi-objective optimization combining ranking and calibration losses, but face challenges in balancing these conflicting goals efficiently. Recent advances propose decoupling ranking and calibration losses and leveraging context features to allow ranking loss computation without sample aggregation, improving both calibration and ranking performance in industrial settings \[[2406.0801](http://arxiv.org/abs/2406.08010)\]. Additionally, optimization methods utilizing accuracy-uncertainty relationships and tailored losses such as accuracy versus uncertainty calibration loss (AvUC) have shown significant improvements in neural network calibration, emphasizing the importance of loss function design in calibration \[[2012.07923](http://arxiv.org/abs/2012.07923)\].

Uncertainty quantification is another dimension linked closely to calibration. Bayesian and stochastic neural networks provide uncertainty estimates alongside predictions, enabling more robust calibration and risk-aware ranking decisions \[[2402.07307](http://arxiv.org/abs/2402.07307)\]\[[2101.04356](http://arxiv.org/abs/2101.04356)\]. However, these approaches often introduce computational overhead, limiting their scalability in large-scale online systems \[[2301.04378](http://arxiv.org/abs/2301.04378)\]. Recent methods that combine parameter estimation techniques with specific uncertainty measures have also been evaluated for calibration quality, highlighting the need for practical and scalable calibration solutions \[[1911.00104](http://arxiv.org/abs/1911.00104)\].

Calibration in ranking models is particularly critical in domains like recommendation and conversational search, where both the relative order and confidence in predictions influence user experience and downstream decision-making. Studies demonstrate that well-calibrated neural rankers improve relevance estimation and enable uncertainty-aware ranking strategies that handle ambiguous or unanswerable queries better \[[2101.04356](http://arxiv.org/abs/2101.04356)\]. Nevertheless, existing calibration approaches for ranking often inadequately address the joint optimization of ranking quality and calibration accuracy, motivating novel frameworks that explicitly target both objectives \[[2406.0801](http://arxiv.org/abs/2406.08010)\].

Finally, broader calibration surveys and empirical comparisons of methods underscore that recent advances in neural architectures and training regimes play a significant role in calibration quality, sometimes surpassing earlier trends where model size and pretraining correlated negatively with calibration \[[2106.07998](http://arxiv.org/abs/2106.07998)\]\[[2303.10761](http://arxiv.org/abs/2303.10761)\]. These insights suggest that architecture choice, such as adopting UMNNs for monotonic calibrators, can substantially impact calibration outcomes.

In summary, existing calibration techniques for ranking and classification models have advanced from fixed-form transformations to neural network approaches with increasing flexibility. However, many still suffer from limited expressiveness or suboptimal loss function designs that hinder calibration performance in complex scenarios. The use of UMNNs as flexible monotonic calibrators represents a significant step forward by combining strong modeling capacity with guaranteed monotonicity, addressing the core limitations of previous methods. Coupled with novel calibration-specific loss functions and strategies to balance ranking and calibration objectives, this approach offers a powerful framework for enhanced calibrated ranking systems in real-world applications \[[1710.08901](http://arxiv.org/abs/1710.08901)\]\[[1908.05164](http://arxiv.org/abs/1908.05164)\]\[[2106.03228](http://arxiv.org/abs/2106.03228)\]\[[2406.0801](http://arxiv.org/abs/2406.08010)\]\[[2012.07923](http://arxiv.org/abs/2012.07923)\]\[[2106.07998](http://arxiv.org/abs/2106.07998)\]\[[2303.10761](http://arxiv.org/abs/2303.10761)\]\[[2101.04356](http://arxiv.org/abs/2101.04356)\].
