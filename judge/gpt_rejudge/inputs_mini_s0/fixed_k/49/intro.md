## Related Works

Neural retrieval models have become fundamental in modern information retrieval due to their capacity to learn intricate semantic representations from large-scale data, outperforming traditional lexical methods in many settings \[[2403.13468](http://arxiv.org/abs/2403.13468)\]\[[2112.05662](http://arxiv.org/abs/2112.05662)\]. However, their reliance on extensive labeled query-document pairs for training presents a significant bottleneck, given the high cost and effort involved in obtaining such annotations \[[2403.13468](http://arxiv.org/abs/2403.13468)\]. To address this, synthetic query generation using large language models (LLMs) has emerged as a scalable alternative, leveraging the abundant availability of web document collections like ClueWeb22 to produce pseudo-labeled data for training retrieval models \[[2403.13468](http://arxiv.org/abs/2403.13468)\]\[[2303.11648](http://arxiv.org/abs/2303.11648)\]\[[2412.03573](http://arxiv.org/abs/2412.03573)\]. Despite the scalability, synthetic queries often vary in quality, which can degrade downstream retrieval effectiveness if noisy or irrelevant queries are included during training \[[2403.13468](http://arxiv.org/abs/2403.13468)\].

Existing approaches typically mitigate this issue by applying external re-ranking models to filter out low-quality synthetic query-document pairs before training, thus imposing a two-stage pipeline that may be computationally expensive and potentially miss nuanced relevance signals \[[2403.13468](http://arxiv.org/abs/2403.13468)\]. In contrast, frameworks that directly integrate preference signals into the generation process, such as Direct Preference Optimization (DPO), offer a promising direction by optimizing query generation towards human-aligned relevance judgments without relying on separate filtering steps \[[2412.15453](http://arxiv.org/abs/2412.15453)\]\[[2410.0435](http://arxiv.org/abs/2410.04350)\]. DPO has been effectively employed in diverse tasks involving preference alignment, including multilingual counter-speech generation, where it aligns model outputs with human preferences through pairwise ranking, leading to improved contextual appropriateness and factual grounding \[[2412.15453](http://arxiv.org/abs/2412.15453)\]. Further refinements like Token-level Importance Sampling for DPO (TIS-DPO) have enhanced optimization efficiency by weighting token contributions according to their reward signals, resulting in better alignment with human preferences across harmlessness and helpfulness tasks \[[2410.0435](http://arxiv.org/abs/2410.04350)\].

The application of generative retrieval models, which formulate retrieval as a document identifier generation task, has also gained traction. Techniques such as Direct Document Relevance Optimization (DDRO) align token-level generation with document-level relevance via pairwise ranking, avoiding the complexities of reinforcement learning and demonstrating substantial improvements on benchmarks like MS MARCO and Natural Questions \[[2403.13468](http://arxiv.org/abs/2403.13468)\]\[[2407.19829](http://arxiv.org/abs/2407.19829)\]. These generative frameworks can complement preference-based optimization methods by simplifying alignment and enhancing retrieval quality.

Synthetic data generation for privacy-preserving retrieval systems is another active area, where differentially private language models create synthetic queries that maintain privacy guarantees while boosting retrieval quality—a complementary concern when scaling synthetic query generation \[[2305.05973](http://arxiv.org/abs/2305.05973)\]. Additionally, controllable query generation methods have been proposed to address retrievability bias in dense retrieval models by diversifying training queries along intent dimensions (narrow vs. broad), thereby improving entity discoverability and reducing bias in retrieval results \[[2303.11648](http://arxiv.org/abs/2303.11648)\]. These efforts highlight the importance of query quality and diversity in synthetic data to maximize retrieval performance.

The challenge of query generation quality is further compounded by observations that neural retrieval models struggle with lexical matching in out-of-domain and zero-shot scenarios, often failing to prioritize important terms effectively \[[2112.05662](http://arxiv.org/abs/2112.05662)\]. This insight underscores the necessity of optimizing synthetic query generation not just for fluency but for alignment with retrieval-relevant features, an objective that DPO-based frameworks directly target.

Finally, leveraging LLMs to generate more contextually aware and semantically rich queries has proven beneficial beyond retrieval alone. For example, in tool retrieval for complex multi-tool scenarios, LLM-generated queries improve retrieval relevance across both in-domain and out-of-domain cases, demonstrating the utility of query generation aligned with downstream retrieval objectives \[[2412.03573](http://arxiv.org/abs/2412.03573)\].

In summary, while previous methods rely heavily on filtering synthetic queries through external rerankers, integrating preference-based optimization methods like DPO into the query generation process enables direct alignment with relevance signals. This approach promises higher-quality synthetic queries and improved downstream retrieval performance, as demonstrated in generative retrieval contexts and multi-lingual alignment tasks. Complementary advances in query diversity, importance-weighted optimization, and privacy-preserving synthetic data further contextualize this direction within a broader landscape of retrieval research \[[2403.13468](http://arxiv.org/abs/2403.13468)\]\[[2412.15453](http://arxiv.org/abs/2412.15453)\]\[[2410.0435](http://arxiv.org/abs/2410.04350)\]\[[2305.05973](http://arxiv.org/abs/2305.05973)\]\[[2303.11648](http://arxiv.org/abs/2303.11648)\]\[[2112.05662](http://arxiv.org/abs/2112.05662)\]\[[2412.03573](http://arxiv.org/abs/2412.03573)\].
