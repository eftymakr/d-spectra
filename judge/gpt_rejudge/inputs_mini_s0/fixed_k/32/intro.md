## Related Works

Understanding the evolution of feature representations across layers in large language models (LLMs) has been a central focus for improving their interpretability, robustness, and adaptability. Early investigations primarily used data-dependent analyses of fine-tuned models to characterize layer-wise changes, revealing that early layers tend to preserve general representations while later layers specialize towards task-specific features \[[2502.16722](http://arxiv.org/abs/2502.16722)\]\[[2304.05216](http://arxiv.org/abs/2304.05216)\]. This layered progression reflects a transition from generic to domain-specific knowledge, which has been corroborated by studies analyzing activation similarity and token-level activations \[[2502.16722](http://arxiv.org/abs/2502.16722)\].

Recent efforts have introduced data-oblivious methods to study representation dynamics in pre-fine-tuned models, aiming to identify intrinsic critical layers without reliance on downstream task data. Notably, Centered Kernel Alignment (CKA) has emerged as a powerful tool for quantifying similarity and shifts in layer representations, offering insights into how layers transform semantic content throughout the network \[[2210.05643](http://arxiv.org/abs/2210.05643)\]\[[2102.06605](http://arxiv.org/abs/2102.06605)\]. By applying CKA, researchers have demonstrated that layers exhibiting significant shifts in representation space during pre-fine-tuning are also those most affected during downstream fine-tuning, indicating stable, model-inherent critical points \[[2502.16722](http://arxiv.org/abs/2502.16722)\]\[[2408.156](http://arxiv.org/abs/2408.15600)\].

Spectral analyses further illuminate these dynamics by revealing that the primary drivers of representational shifts are changes in the top principal components of layer embeddings. These components encode meaningful semantic transitions, such as moving from rationale generation to final conclusions in reasoning tasks, underscoring the importance of principal components in capturing semantic information within LLMs \[[2212.08853](http://arxiv.org/abs/2212.08853)\]\[[2502.16147](http://arxiv.org/abs/2502.16147)\]. This aligns with prior work showing that perturbations in hidden representations can substantially influence fine-tuning outcomes and generalization capabilities \[[2212.08853](http://arxiv.org/abs/2212.08853)\].

The identification of critical layers has practical implications for efficient domain adaptation and robustness. Selective fine-tuning of these layers yields more substantial loss reductions compared to tuning non-critical layers, demonstrating better parameter efficiency and performance gains in low-resource settings \[[2411.09539](http://arxiv.org/abs/2411.09539)\]\[[2408.156](http://arxiv.org/abs/2408.15600)\]\[[2410.10739](http://arxiv.org/abs/2410.10739)\]. This targeted adaptation strategy also mitigates catastrophic forgetting and preserves essential model properties by focusing updates where they are most impactful \[[2412.19512](http://arxiv.org/abs/2412.19512)\]\[[2304.05216](http://arxiv.org/abs/2304.05216)\]. Moreover, freezing critical layers has been shown to enhance backdoor defense mechanisms by reducing attack success rates significantly, aligning with prior findings on layer freezing as a defense against adversarial manipulations \[[2411.15382](http://arxiv.org/abs/2411.15382)\]\[[2502.05945](http://arxiv.org/abs/2502.05945)\].

Complementary research in federated learning settings has emphasized selective layer fine-tuning to balance computational constraints and model convergence. Strategies that dynamically select critical layers based on local gradients enable efficient adaptation in heterogeneous environments, illustrating the broader applicability of layer-wise analysis \[[2408.156](http://arxiv.org/abs/2408.15600)\]\[[2501.19389](http://arxiv.org/abs/2501.19389)\]. Similarly, low-rank adaptation methods (LoRA) and sketching techniques provide parameter-efficient fine-tuning frameworks that implicitly exploit critical subspaces and layers, supporting the notion that not all layers require equal updating \[[2501.19389](http://arxiv.org/abs/2501.19389)\]\[[2402.10208](http://arxiv.org/abs/2402.10208)\].

The kernel perspective on fine-tuning dynamics offers theoretical grounding for these empirical observations. Studies leveraging Neural Tangent Kernel (NTK) frameworks have explained the surprisingly robust generalization behavior of LLMs fine-tuned on scarce data, attributing effective adaptation to kernel-induced dynamics and subspace learning within critical layers \[[2210.05643](http://arxiv.org/abs/2210.05643)\]. This kernel view also justifies parameter-efficient approaches that focus on crucial representational subspaces rather than wholesale weight updates.

Further, interventions at finer granularities such as attention heads have demonstrated the ability to steer model behavior effectively, sometimes bypassing traditional fine-tuning, which underscores the nuanced role of sub-layer components in representing and controlling model functions \[[2502.05945](http://arxiv.org/abs/2502.05945)\]. These findings complement layer-wise analyses by highlighting multiple levels of criticality within LLM architectures.

Overall, the converging evidence from representation similarity, spectral analysis, kernel theory, and practical fine-tuning strategies emphasizes the importance of identifying and leveraging intrinsic critical layers in LLMs. This facilitates improved interpretability, efficient adaptation, and enhanced robustness without extensive reliance on large-scale task-specific data \[[2411.09539](http://arxiv.org/abs/2411.09539)\]\[[2502.16722](http://arxiv.org/abs/2502.16722)\]\[[2210.05643](http://arxiv.org/abs/2210.05643)\]\[[2408.156](http://arxiv.org/abs/2408.15600)\]\[[2212.08853](http://arxiv.org/abs/2212.08853)\]\[[2411.15382](http://arxiv.org/abs/2411.15382)\]\[[2502.05945](http://arxiv.org/abs/2502.05945)\]\[[2304.05216](http://arxiv.org/abs/2304.05216)\].
