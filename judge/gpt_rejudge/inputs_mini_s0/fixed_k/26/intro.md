## Related Works

Differential privacy (DP) is widely recognized as a rigorous privacy framework for statistical data analysis and machine learning, ensuring individual data points contribute limited information to published outputs \[[803.3946](http://arxiv.org/abs/0803.3946)\]. The foundational semantic understanding of DP was formalized through Bayesian adversarial inference models, which underpin guarantees against arbitrary side information held by attackers \[[803.3946](http://arxiv.org/abs/0803.3946)\]. Despite these strong theoretical guarantees, practical deployment, especially in distributed or local models, faces significant challenges due to potential adversarial behaviors by servers or clients that may manipulate noise generation or output data, thereby undermining privacy and utility.

To address these threats, research has explored verifiable differential privacy (VDP) mechanisms that enable external parties to verify that DP protocols are executed faithfully. One major approach involves integrating zero-knowledge proofs (ZKPs) to demonstrate compliance with DP mechanisms without revealing sensitive internal randomness \[[2208.09011](http://arxiv.org/abs/2208.09011)\]. This concept was developed to close a critical loophole where malicious servers could manipulate noisy outputs while masking such misbehavior as legitimate randomness \[[2208.09011](http://arxiv.org/abs/2208.09011)\]. Although ZKPs provide strong assurances and are sufficient to guarantee verifiability, recent analysis shows they are not strictly necessary, as alternative cryptographic and protocol-level techniques can also ensure trustworthy DP execution \[[2310.14848](http://arxiv.org/abs/2310.14848)\]\[[2208.09011](http://arxiv.org/abs/2208.09011)\].

In distributed settings, the local and shuffle models of DP have gained attention for their reduced trust assumptions. Local differential privacy (LDP) mechanisms privatize data at the client side, but are vulnerable to input and output manipulation attacks by malicious clients, who can skew aggregate statistics by deviating from agreed protocols \[[2406.1894](http://arxiv.org/abs/2406.18940)\]\[[2104.06569](http://arxiv.org/abs/2104.06569)\]. To mitigate this, verifiable local differential privacy (VLDP) schemes have been proposed that rely on input authentication and verifiable randomization mechanisms, preventing malicious clients from compromising the protocol while maintaining high efficiency and practicality even in the shuffle model \[[2406.1894](http://arxiv.org/abs/2406.18940)\]\[[2104.06569](http://arxiv.org/abs/2104.06569)\]. Such VLDP protocols often require only minimal client-server interactions and achieve strong security guarantees with low computational overhead \[[2406.1894](http://arxiv.org/abs/2406.18940)\].

Randomized response (RR) techniques remain central to LDP protocols, with ongoing improvements in utility and verifiability. The Verifiable Randomized Response (VRR) mechanism represents a critical advancement in ensuring clients can confirm their data is processed as intended under LDP constraints, reducing communication and verification costs substantially \[[2104.06569](http://arxiv.org/abs/2104.06569)\]\[[2504.17523](http://arxiv.org/abs/2504.17523)\]. Recent works have also introduced bipartite randomized response mechanisms and staircase randomized response variants to optimize utility for specific applications such as location-based services and discrete distribution estimation, balancing privacy and accuracy under local constraints \[[2209.15091](http://arxiv.org/abs/2209.15091)\]\[[2504.20926](http://arxiv.org/abs/2504.20926)\]\[[2102.12621](http://arxiv.org/abs/2102.12621)\]\[[2504.17523](http://arxiv.org/abs/2504.17523)\].

Beyond local models, secure multi-party computation (MPC) has been leveraged to realize distributed DP with rigorous privacy and correctness guarantees. MPC-based protocols enable collaborative noise sampling and aggregation without revealing private inputs, and recent benchmarks have highlighted efficiency gains in Byzantine-resilient secure sampling protocols for DP \[[2409.10667](http://arxiv.org/abs/2409.10667)\]. These cryptographic approaches complement verifiability goals by ensuring noise generation and aggregation are conducted honestly, mitigating adversarial influence on DP guarantees \[[2409.10667](http://arxiv.org/abs/2409.10667)\]. Similarly, homomorphic encryption and MPC have been combined with DP to achieve verifiable privacy-preserving data aggregation in distributed systems \[[2405.01678](http://arxiv.org/abs/2405.01678)\]\[[2208.08662](http://arxiv.org/abs/2208.08662)\].

The relationship between DP and verifiability extends to machine learning settings, where DP-trained models can be vulnerable to both privacy leakage and robustness degradation. Efforts to design verifiable decentralized machine learning frameworks incorporate zero-knowledge proofs to ensure model integrity and privacy without exposing sensitive data, addressing emerging trust issues in federated and decentralized learning \[[2310.14848](http://arxiv.org/abs/2310.14848)\]\[[2208.08662](http://arxiv.org/abs/2208.08662)\]. Moreover, the trade-offs between privacy, utility, and security robustness have been studied, revealing that DP mechanisms can sometimes increase vulnerability to adversarial attacks despite their privacy benefits \[[2012.07828](http://arxiv.org/abs/2012.07828)\].

Notably, optimal noise-adding mechanisms such as the staircase mechanism offer improved privacy-utility trade-offs over classical Laplacian noise, especially in moderate to low privacy regimes, which can be integrated into verifiable DP frameworks to enhance performance \[[1212.1186](http://arxiv.org/abs/1212.1186)\]. Additionally, gradual relaxation of privacy budgets via randomized response adaptations has been proposed to dynamically balance privacy and utility while maintaining verifiability \[[2401.13952](http://arxiv.org/abs/2401.13952)\].

The present work advances this corpus by defining Verifiable Distributed Differential Privacy (VDDP), which generalizes verifiable guarantees to a client-server-verifier architecture, addressing malicious noise sampling and correlation in distributed environments. Our proposed Verifiable Distributed Discrete Laplacian Mechanism (VDDLM) achieves orders-of-magnitude efficiency improvements in proof generation with minimal utility loss compared to previous mechanisms, while our enhanced VRR protocol under local DP significantly reduces communication and verification overheads \[[2406.1894](http://arxiv.org/abs/2406.18940)\]\[[2104.06569](http://arxiv.org/abs/2104.06569)\]\[[2208.09011](http://arxiv.org/abs/2208.09011)\]. This builds on and surpasses prior verifiable DP efforts by combining cryptographic verification with practical performance optimizations.
