## Related Works

Cross-domain sequential recommendation (CDSR) has garnered significant attention for its ability to leverage user interaction sequences from multiple domains to enhance recommendation accuracy and address data sparsity and cold-start issues \[[2304.03891](http://arxiv.org/abs/2304.03891)\]\[[2401.04971](http://arxiv.org/abs/2401.04971)\]. Early CDSR models predominantly utilized self-attention mechanisms within Transformer architectures to capture sequential dependencies, yet they often struggled with effectively integrating cross-domain signals due to domain distribution shifts and limited overlap among users \[[2304.03891](http://arxiv.org/abs/2304.03891)\]\[[1911.06478](http://arxiv.org/abs/1911.06478)\]. To ameliorate these issues, numerous approaches have incorporated domain-specific modules or transfer learning strategies that explicitly model domain-aware preferences or align user/item representations across domains \[[2405.12473](http://arxiv.org/abs/2405.12473)\]\[[2309.0842](http://arxiv.org/abs/2309.08420)\]\[[2404.00268](http://arxiv.org/abs/2404.00268)\].

A major challenge in CDSR lies in balancing the preservation of single-domain preferences with the effective transfer and fusion of cross-domain knowledge. Some works tackle this by employing graph neural networks to capture intra- and inter-sequence item relations, enhancing user representations through collaborative filtering and contrastive learning objectives \[[2304.03891](http://arxiv.org/abs/2304.03891)\]\[[2302.03221](http://arxiv.org/abs/2302.03221)\]\[[2209.10163](http://arxiv.org/abs/2209.10163)\]. Others focus on aligning item embeddings partially or adaptively to mitigate misalignment that hampers sequential modeling \[[2405.12473](http://arxiv.org/abs/2405.12473)\]. Additionally, disentangled representation learning has been proposed to separate domain-shared and domain-exclusive features, improving cross-domain generalization and privacy preservation, especially under federated learning settings \[[2309.0842](http://arxiv.org/abs/2309.08420)\]\[[2404.00268](http://arxiv.org/abs/2404.00268)\]\[[2401.15369](http://arxiv.org/abs/2401.15369)\].

While many CDSR frameworks augment the Transformer backbone with domain-specific attention layers or auxiliary modules, recent studies have identified inherent limitations in the vanilla self-attention mechanism. Notably, self-attention often acts as a low-pass filter, causing oversmoothing and neglecting high-frequency or local context information critical for fine-grained user preference modeling \[[2304.09184](http://arxiv.org/abs/2304.09184)\]\[[2312.10325](http://arxiv.org/abs/2312.10325)\]. To address this, methods such as frequency-enhanced hybrid attention networks and causal convolution-based self-attention have been developed to capture both global and local item transitions more effectively \[[2304.09184](http://arxiv.org/abs/2304.09184)\]\[[2211.01297](http://arxiv.org/abs/2211.01297)\]. Moreover, approaches that refine attention weights or introduce stochastic self-attention mechanisms have demonstrated improvements by better modeling uncertainty and higher-order item correlations within sequences \[[2410.21048](http://arxiv.org/abs/2410.21048)\]\[[2201.06035](http://arxiv.org/abs/2201.06035)\].

Beyond improving attention architectures, researchers have explored multi-objective and reinforcement learning paradigms to optimize CDSR models. These frameworks simultaneously optimize multiple recommendation objectives or incorporate domain filtering mechanisms to reduce negative transfer and enhance cross-domain knowledge exchange \[[2012.00485](http://arxiv.org/abs/2012.00485)\]\[[2311.0459](http://arxiv.org/abs/2311.04590)\]\[[2206.08088](http://arxiv.org/abs/2206.08088)\]. For instance, reinforcement learning has been applied to dynamically adjust domain information in shared-account scenarios, improving user identification and filtering irrelevant behaviors \[[2206.08088](http://arxiv.org/abs/2206.08088)\]. Multi-interest debiasing and contrastive infomax learning strategies further help in handling open-world assumptions and heterogeneous sequence features across domains \[[2309.0842](http://arxiv.org/abs/2309.08420)\]\[[2311.0459](http://arxiv.org/abs/2311.04590)\].

The integration of external knowledge and multimodal information is another emerging direction to enrich item and user representations. Visual and textual embeddings derived from pretrained models like CLIP have been employed to infuse semantic context into CDSR, facilitating the capture of cross-domain preferences from multimodal perspectives \[[2502.15694](http://arxiv.org/abs/2502.15694)\]\[[2504.15085](http://arxiv.org/abs/2504.15085)\]. Similarly, large language models (LLMs) have been introduced to provide unified semantic item representations and hierarchical user profiling, addressing challenges related to user overlap scarcity and complex transition patterns across domains \[[2504.18383](http://arxiv.org/abs/2504.18383)\].

Addressing privacy concerns and practical deployment considerations, recent work emphasizes privacy-preserving mechanisms and lightweight model designs. For example, differential privacy techniques have been incorporated into CDSR without compromising recommendation accuracy \[[2401.15369](http://arxiv.org/abs/2401.15369)\], and lightweight attention-enhanced graph convolutional networks have been proposed to reduce computational burdens while maintaining effectiveness \[[2302.03221](http://arxiv.org/abs/2302.03221)\]. Furthermore, plug-and-play frameworks that can seamlessly integrate with existing Transformer-based recommenders and require minimal hyperparameter tuning have been developed to facilitate practical adoption \[[2310.04633](http://arxiv.org/abs/2310.04633)\].

In summary, existing CDSR research spans a wide range of methodologies including enhanced self-attention mechanisms, graph-based modeling, multi-objective optimization, multimodal representation fusion, and privacy-aware federated learning. However, most approaches either augment the Transformer with additional domain-specific components or focus on external alignments, often overlooking the potential of directly improving the core self-attention module itself. The proposed AutoCDSR approach differentiates itself by formulating cross-domain learning as a multi-objective optimization problem that dynamically balances recommendation accuracy and cross-domain attention minimization, enabling automated and efficient knowledge transfer within a unified self-attention framework \[[2310.04633](http://arxiv.org/abs/2310.04633)\].
