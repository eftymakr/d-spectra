## Related Works

Active learning (AL) aims to reduce annotation costs by selectively querying the most informative unlabeled samples for labeling, thereby improving model performance with fewer labeled data points \[[2302.08893](http://arxiv.org/abs/2302.08893)\]. Traditional AL methods predominantly focus on static or pool-based settings, where selection occurs from a fixed dataset. However, in practical scenarios involving continuous data streams, online active learning methods have been developed to adaptively select informative samples in real time \[[2302.08893](http://arxiv.org/abs/2302.08893)\]. Despite these advances, applying AL in distributed environments introduces new challenges.

Federated Active Learning (FAL) extends AL to privacy-preserving, decentralized settings where clients collaboratively select and label data samples without sharing raw data. This paradigm addresses the high annotation cost and privacy concerns inherent in centralized AL but faces additional complexities due to the heterogeneity of client data and system constraints. Existing FAL methods often overlook the impact of non-i.i.d. (non-independent and identically distributed) data distributions across clients, which manifest as statistical heterogeneity, leading to fluctuating local and global model parameters and degraded accuracy \[[2504.01921](http://arxiv.org/abs/2504.01921)\]\[[2504.11216](http://arxiv.org/abs/2504.11216)\].

Numerous federated learning (FL) studies have tackled the challenges of data heterogeneity. FedDiverse proposes a diversity-driven client selection strategy that encourages collaboration among clients with complementary data distributions to mitigate the negative effects of data imbalance and spurious correlations \[[2504.11216](http://arxiv.org/abs/2504.11216)\]. Other methods, such as those employing knowledge distillation and symmetric loss functions, simultaneously address model and data heterogeneity to improve global model efficiency in medical imaging applications \[[2308.00155](http://arxiv.org/abs/2308.00155)\]. Additionally, client selection strategies that optimize for statistical diversity, such as entropy-based selection methods that maximize label distribution entropy, have been shown to enhance global model generalization and robustness \[[2411.0124](http://arxiv.org/abs/2411.01240)\]. These approaches improve convergence and performance but generally focus on client or model selection rather than actively selecting informative unlabeled data points.

Client heterogeneity extends beyond data distribution to include computational resources and network latencies, which significantly affect FL efficiency and convergence. FedCompass introduces a computing power-aware scheduler that adapts training workloads based on client capabilities, striking a balance between synchronous and asynchronous FL to reduce model staleness and improve convergence \[[2309.14675](http://arxiv.org/abs/2309.14675)\]. Similarly, methods addressing system heterogeneity propose asynchronous or semi-asynchronous training protocols that tolerate variable client participation and speeds without severely compromising model quality \[[2206.10032](http://arxiv.org/abs/2206.10032)\]\[[2007.06081](http://arxiv.org/abs/2007.06081)\]. While these methods improve training efficiency, their integration with active learning strategies remains underexplored.

Personalization strategies in FL address non-i.i.d. data by adapting models to individual client distributions. WAFFLE uses weighted averaging based on Euclidean distances between client updates for faster convergence and better personalization \[[2110.06978](http://arxiv.org/abs/2110.06978)\]. Other approaches leverage client-specific parameters or correlation-based selection to enhance model adaptation and convergence \[[1909.05784](http://arxiv.org/abs/1909.05784)\]\[[2411.0124](http://arxiv.org/abs/2411.01240)\]. However, these methods primarily focus on improving local model performance and do not directly guide the selection of unlabeled samples for annotation.

Semi-supervised and self-supervised learning extensions to FL address scenarios where labeled data are scarce or unavailable on clients. Frameworks such as Hetero-SSFL enable collaborative self-supervised representation learning across heterogeneous clients without requiring labeled data, improving robustness in practical settings \[[2205.12493](http://arxiv.org/abs/2205.12493)\]\[[2207.08187](http://arxiv.org/abs/2207.08187)\]. Federated semi-supervised learning approaches introduce inter-client consistency losses and parameter decompositions to leverage partially labeled or unlabeled client data effectively \[[2006.12097](http://arxiv.org/abs/2006.12097)\]. Nonetheless, these approaches do not specifically target the identification of samples with high informativeness or epistemic uncertainty for active labeling.

To handle non-i.i.d. data distributions, some works propose synthetic data generation using GANs to harmonize client data distributions, thus facilitating more consistent model aggregation and improving learning outcomes \[[2206.05507](http://arxiv.org/abs/2206.05507)\]. Others employ robust aggregation methods such as superquantile-based objectives to account for tail statistics in client error distributions, enhancing fairness and reliability under heterogeneity \[[2002.11223](http://arxiv.org/abs/2002.11223)\]. Although these techniques assist model robustness, they focus on model training rather than active data selection.

In summary, while federated learning research has extensively addressed data and system heterogeneity through client selection, personalization, asynchronous training, and semi-supervised learning, the integration of active learning principles—particularly those focusing on epistemic uncertainty and decision boundary calibration in heterogeneous federated settings—remains insufficiently explored. Existing FAL methods inadequately consider the oscillations in model predictions caused by client heterogeneity, limiting their effectiveness in selecting informative samples. The proposed CHASe method builds upon these foundations by explicitly tracking epistemic variations across training epochs, calibrating decision boundaries with alignment losses, and employing efficient data freeze-and-awaken mechanisms for subset sampling to improve both selection quality and computational efficiency in heterogeneous federations \[[2302.08893](http://arxiv.org/abs/2302.08893)\]\[[2504.01921](http://arxiv.org/abs/2504.01921)\]\[[2504.11216](http://arxiv.org/abs/2504.11216)\]\[[2411.0124](http://arxiv.org/abs/2411.01240)\]\[[2309.14675](http://arxiv.org/abs/2309.14675)\].
