## Related Works

Cross-domain Sequential Recommendation (CDSR) aims to improve recommendation accuracy by leveraging users’ sequential interactions across multiple domains, addressing challenges such as data sparsity and cold-start issues in single-domain systems. Traditional CDSR approaches usually rely heavily on overlapping users—those with interactions in multiple domains—to model cross-domain item relationships and transfer user preferences. This reliance limits applicability, as overlapping users often constitute a small fraction of the user base in real-world scenarios, leading to what is known as the overlap dilemma \[[2410.13588](http://arxiv.org/abs/2410.13588)\]\[[2311.0459](http://arxiv.org/abs/2311.04590)\]\[[2104.0849](http://arxiv.org/abs/2104.08490)\]. Moreover, effectively capturing the complex transition patterns from mixed-domain behavior sequences remains a key challenge for CDSR models \[[2304.03891](http://arxiv.org/abs/2304.03891)\]\[[2209.10163](http://arxiv.org/abs/2209.10163)\].

To mitigate these issues, various methods have been proposed. Graph-based models, such as dual dynamic graph frameworks, model intra- and inter-domain sequential transitions by constructing multi-level graphs and applying attention mechanisms to fuse domain information \[[2406.03085](http://arxiv.org/abs/2406.03085)\]\[[2209.10163](http://arxiv.org/abs/2209.10163)\]. Contrastive learning techniques have also been employed to better align cross-domain user preferences by maximizing the mutual information between single- and cross-domain representations, thereby enhancing knowledge transfer \[[2304.03891](http://arxiv.org/abs/2304.03891)\]\[[2309.0842](http://arxiv.org/abs/2309.08420)\]. However, these models often require carefully designed domain alignment modules and still depend on overlapping user data or auxiliary content to bridge domains \[[2304.04218](http://arxiv.org/abs/2304.04218)\]\[[2309.01343](http://arxiv.org/abs/2309.01343)\].

Addressing the overlap dilemma, recent studies have explored scenarios with non-overlapping users and items. Prompt learning-based frameworks like PLCR use automated prompting and pre-trained sequence encoders to learn domain-invariant and domain-specific features without explicit domain alignment constraints, supporting recommendation in fully non-overlapping cross-domain settings \[[2304.04218](http://arxiv.org/abs/2304.04218)\]. Federated learning approaches, such as FedDCSR and FMoE-CDSR, have been introduced to preserve data privacy while enabling knowledge transfer between domains without sharing raw user data, effectively handling sequence heterogeneity and non-overlapping user scenarios \[[2309.0842](http://arxiv.org/abs/2309.08420)\]\[[2503.13254](http://arxiv.org/abs/2503.13254)\]. Distributional preference matching techniques further model latent domain-invariant user preference distributions, facilitating alignment across non-overlapping domains by mapping to a shared latent space \[[2309.01343](http://arxiv.org/abs/2309.01343)\].

To tackle the complexity of modeling user behavior transitions, methods have incorporated multi-level attention mechanisms and hybrid metric learning. Models like C2DSR combine graph neural networks with sequential attentive encoders to jointly capture intra-sequence and inter-sequence item relationships, while hybrid metric training enhances user/item representations by balancing alignment and uniformity objectives \[[2304.03891](http://arxiv.org/abs/2304.03891)\]\[[2209.10163](http://arxiv.org/abs/2209.10163)\]. Other approaches introduce time interval information and domain-aware graph convolutional networks to better characterize temporal dynamics and cross-domain user-item interactions \[[2206.0805](http://arxiv.org/abs/2206.08050)\]. Moreover, reinforcement learning frameworks have been applied to filter irrelevant domain information and refine shared-account cross-domain recommendations by modeling latent user identities within mixed sequences \[[2206.08088](http://arxiv.org/abs/2206.08088)\].

The advent of Large Language Models (LLMs) has opened new avenues for CDSR by leveraging their strong semantic understanding and reasoning capabilities. Several works have integrated LLMs into sequential recommendation, either as pretrained embedding sources or as core components to capture semantic item relationships and user preferences. Initial experiments show that LLM-enhanced models outperform traditional baselines, especially in rating prediction and ranking tasks across various domain pairs \[[2411.19862](http://arxiv.org/abs/2411.19862)\]\[[2309.09261](http://arxiv.org/abs/2309.09261)\]. Frameworks like URLLM and LLM4CDR propose unified representation modules and context-aware prompt designs to facilitate seamless integration of structural user-item information and domain grounding, effectively addressing the overlap dilemma and transition complexity \[[2406.03085](http://arxiv.org/abs/2406.03085)\]\[[2503.07761](http://arxiv.org/abs/2503.07761)\]. LLM4CDR, in particular, highlights the benefits of leveraging large parameter LLMs with carefully constructed prompts and closely related source-target domain pairs, achieving significant improvements in recommendation accuracy \[[2503.07761](http://arxiv.org/abs/2503.07761)\].

To further enhance LLM-based CDSR, recent studies focus on adapting LLMs to sequential recommendation via distillation and auxiliary knowledge integration. DELRec distills sequential patterns from conventional SR models into LLMs using soft prompts, improving LLMs’ ability to utilize behavioral patterns \[[2406.11156](http://arxiv.org/abs/2406.11156)\]. DARec introduces dynamic adaptation mechanisms, including context masking and collaborative knowledge injection, to better capture intra-item relations and long-term collaborative knowledge within LLMs \[[2408.07427](http://arxiv.org/abs/2408.07427)\]. Additionally, SLMRec demonstrates that smaller distilled language models can retain comparable performance to large LLMs with much greater efficiency, making LLM-based CDSR more practical for large-scale industrial deployment \[[2405.1789](http://arxiv.org/abs/2405.17890)\].

Despite these advances, challenges remain in effectively modeling non-overlapping user behaviors, preserving privacy, and capturing complex cross-domain transitions without heavy reliance on explicit domain alignment or overlapping users. The integration of LLMs with contrastive learning, prompt engineering, and federated learning paradigms presents a promising direction to overcome these limitations by extracting richer semantic item relationships and user preferences from heterogeneous cross-domain data \[[2406.03085](http://arxiv.org/abs/2406.03085)\]\[[2503.07761](http://arxiv.org/abs/2503.07761)\]\[[2304.04218](http://arxiv.org/abs/2304.04218)\]\[[2309.0842](http://arxiv.org/abs/2309.08420)\]\[[2309.01343](http://arxiv.org/abs/2309.01343)\].

In summary, the landscape of CDSR research has evolved from overlap-dependent graph and metric learning methods to more flexible frameworks that handle non-overlapping scenarios and exploit the semantic power of LLMs. The proposed LLM4CDSR model builds upon these insights by unifying semantic item representations, contrastive adaptation, and hierarchical user profiling into a tri-thread framework, demonstrating state-of-the-art performance on multiple public datasets and advancing the practical applicability of CDSR systems.
