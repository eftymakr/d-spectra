## Related Works

Personalized outfit generation involves creating sets of fashion items that are both compatible and tailored to individual user preferences. Traditional fashion recommendation systems primarily focus on compatibility prediction and personalization separately, but recent works aim to unify these aspects for more effective outfit generation.

Early methods concentrated on compatibility learning by modeling item-item relations or outfit-level compatibility. For instance, Relation Networks (FashionRN) learn compatibility among an arbitrary number of items without requiring fixed item orders or rich semantic labels, achieving strong performance on compatibility prediction and fill-in-the-blank tasks \[[2005.06584](http://arxiv.org/abs/2005.06584)\]. Similarly, hierarchical graph-based approaches like Hierarchical Fashion Graph Network (HFGN) jointly model user-item and outfit-item relations to capture both compatibility and personalization, demonstrating significant improvements over previous methods \[[2005.12566](http://arxiv.org/abs/2005.12566)\]. Transformer-based models also contribute by encoding outfit-level representations with self-attention, facilitating compatibility prediction and complementary item retrieval \[[2204.04812](http://arxiv.org/abs/2204.04812)\]. Graph neural networks combined with Transformers (TGNN) further capture complex relations among clothing items, enabling seeded outfit generation and compatibility estimation \[[2304.08098](http://arxiv.org/abs/2304.08098)\].

Generative models have gained traction for their ability to synthesize fashion items and outfits beyond recommending from existing pools. Approaches such as DiFashion leverage diffusion models to generate multiple fashion images in parallel, conditioned on compatibility and personalization factors, achieving high fidelity and user-aligned outfits \[[2402.17279](http://arxiv.org/abs/2402.17279)\]. GAN-based frameworks like COutfitGAN and OutfitGAN focus on synthesizing compatible complementary items, employing semantic alignment and collocation classification to improve visual realism and outfit coherence \[[2502.08674](http://arxiv.org/abs/2502.08674)\]\[[2502.06827](http://arxiv.org/abs/2502.06827)\]. FCBoost-Net innovates by generating multiple diverse and compatible outfit options, iteratively boosting fashion compatibility without sacrificing diversity \[[2502.00992](http://arxiv.org/abs/2502.00992)\]. These generative frameworks address challenges in diversity and visual authenticity often encountered in earlier models.

Style and theme conditioning have been highlighted as critical for producing coherent and contextually relevant outfits. Several works incorporate style encoders or latent style spaces to generate outfits aligned with user-specified themes or occasions, improving the semantic coherence of generated outfits \[[2203.16161](http://arxiv.org/abs/2203.16161)\]\[[2205.00663](http://arxiv.org/abs/2205.00663)\]\[[1807.03133](http://arxiv.org/abs/1807.03133)\]\[[1906.07273](http://arxiv.org/abs/1906.07273)\]. For example, methods generating outfits conditioned on textual style queries combine item-item compatibility with item-query coherence, yielding tightly clustered and theme-consistent outfits validated through user studies \[[1906.07273](http://arxiv.org/abs/1906.07273)\]. Style extraction modules based on unsupervised learning further enable flexible outfit generation controlled by global style features \[[1807.03133](http://arxiv.org/abs/1807.03133)\].

Personalization remains a key challenge, especially in cold-start scenarios. Visual preference modeling approaches leverage a small set of user input images to capture preferences and recommend occasion-oriented outfits, outperforming traditional attribute prediction methods \[[2008.01437](http://arxiv.org/abs/2008.01437)\]. Large-scale datasets such as the iFashion and Polyvore-U datasets provide rich user interaction and outfit data, supporting the development and evaluation of personalized outfit generation models \[[1905.01866](http://arxiv.org/abs/1905.01866)\]\[[2402.17279](http://arxiv.org/abs/2402.17279)\]. Transformer architectures have been employed to connect user preferences on individual items and entire outfits, facilitating personalized generation at industrial scale \[[1905.01866](http://arxiv.org/abs/1905.01866)\].

Recent advances integrate multimodal data and human feedback to enhance recommendation quality and model alignment with user tastes. Frameworks combining visual and textual embeddings enable more accurate and relevant outfit generation by leveraging image captioning and multimodal large language models (MLLMs) to extract style and color attributes effectively \[[2409.1215](http://arxiv.org/abs/2409.12150)\]\[[2402.05941](http://arxiv.org/abs/2402.05941)\]. Furthermore, reinforcement learning from human feedback (RLHF) and direct preference optimization (DPO) methods, originally applied to language models, have been adapted to fashion generation. These approaches optimize models based on pairwise preference comparisons without explicit reward function design, improving personalization and compatibility in a scalable manner \[[2404.13846](http://arxiv.org/abs/2404.13846)\]\[[2502.0437](http://arxiv.org/abs/2502.04370)\]. Multi-expert feedback modules that assess quality, compatibility, and personalization across multiple perspectives provide comprehensive and objective signals for model fine-tuning, enhancing outfit generation performance \[[2402.17279](http://arxiv.org/abs/2402.17279)\].

Explainability and interpretability have also been explored, with models generating natural language comments alongside recommendations, thus offering user-understandable explanations for outfit matches \[[1806.08977](http://arxiv.org/abs/1806.08977)\]. Attribute-wise interpretable compatibility models identify discordant and harmonious features between items, aiding personalized recommendations with transparent reasoning \[[2008.0178](http://arxiv.org/abs/2008.01780)\].

In summary, the landscape of personalized outfit generation is shaped by advances in compatibility modeling, generative AI, style conditioning, and preference-based fine-tuning. Recent frameworks that integrate direct preference optimization and multi-expert feedback address limitations of supervised learning by refining pre-trained models in an end-to-end manner, yielding diverse, compatible, and user-aligned outfit recommendations \[[2402.17279](http://arxiv.org/abs/2402.17279)\]\[[2404.13846](http://arxiv.org/abs/2404.13846)\]\[[2409.1215](http://arxiv.org/abs/2409.12150)\]. These developments collectively push the field towards more flexible, interpretable, and personalized fashion AI systems.
