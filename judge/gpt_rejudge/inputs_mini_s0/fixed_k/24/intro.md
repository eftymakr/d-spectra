## Related Works

Diffusion transformers (DiTs) have become a leading architecture for image and video generation, demonstrating superior quality and scalability compared to traditional UNet-based diffusion models \[[2406.0254](http://arxiv.org/abs/2406.02540)\]. However, the high parameter count and computational complexity of DiTs present significant challenges for their deployment on resource-constrained devices, motivating research into efficient quantization methods that reduce model size and accelerate inference without sacrificing generation quality \[[2406.17343](http://arxiv.org/abs/2406.17343)\]\[[2505.18663](http://arxiv.org/abs/2505.18663)\].

Post-Training Quantization (PTQ) is a popular approach for compressing pretrained diffusion models, primarily explored in image generation contexts \[[2502.04056](http://arxiv.org/abs/2502.04056)\]. Nonetheless, extending PTQ to video DiTs introduces unique difficulties due to pronounced temporal dynamics and spatial variances in both weights and activations. For instance, existing quantization methods designed for image diffusion models often suffer from biased quantization errors and performance degradation when applied to video tasks \[[2505.18663](http://arxiv.org/abs/2505.18663)\]\[[2407.11585](http://arxiv.org/abs/2407.11585)\]. These challenges stem from the significant spatial and temporal variance in DiT activations, as well as inter-channel asymmetries that complicate uniform quantization schemes \[[2407.11585](http://arxiv.org/abs/2407.11585)\]\[[2502.15077](http://arxiv.org/abs/2502.15077)\].

Several works have proposed novel quantization techniques tailored to DiTs that address these issues. ViDiT-Q introduces a quantization scheme customized for both image and video DiTs, combining weight and activation quantization to achieve up to 2.5× memory savings and latency speedups with minimal visual quality loss \[[2505.18663](http://arxiv.org/abs/2505.18663)\]. Q-DiT advances this by integrating automatic quantization granularity allocation and sample-wise dynamic activation quantization, effectively managing spatial and temporal activation variance in DiTs and achieving state-of-the-art image and video quantization fidelity at low bit-widths (e.g., W4A8) \[[2407.11585](http://arxiv.org/abs/2407.11585)\]. Similarly, DVD-Quant proposes a data-free PTQ framework for video DiTs that eliminates calibration data dependency through bounded initialization and auto-scaling rotated quantization, enabling W4A4 quantization with negligible video quality degradation \[[2502.15077](http://arxiv.org/abs/2502.15077)\]. HQ-DiT explores hybrid floating-point quantization using 4-bit FP precision for both weights and activations, coupled with clipping range selection and identity transforms to mitigate outlier effects, achieving extremely low-bit quantization with minimal performance loss \[[2502.0759](http://arxiv.org/abs/2502.07590)\].

Temporal consistency is a crucial consideration in video generation and quantization. While many diffusion models generate frames independently or in short sequences, this often leads to temporal incoherence \[[2405.0315](http://arxiv.org/abs/2405.03150)\]\[[2210.02396](http://arxiv.org/abs/2210.02396)\]. Lumiere employs a space-time U-Net architecture that generates the entire video duration in a single pass, leveraging multi-scale spatial and temporal down/up-sampling to enhance global temporal coherence \[[2401.12945](http://arxiv.org/abs/2401.12945)\]. W.A.L.T utilizes window attention tailored for joint spatial and spatiotemporal modeling, enabling efficient and temporally consistent video generation at high resolutions \[[2312.06662](http://arxiv.org/abs/2312.06662)\]. Other methods, such as TaQ-DiT and TQ-DiT, focus explicitly on temporal variance aware quantization strategies that preserve denoising step-dependent activation distributions and reduce temporal quantization errors, achieving competitive performance in low-bit regimes \[[2502.04056](http://arxiv.org/abs/2502.04056)\]\[[2411.14172](http://arxiv.org/abs/2411.14172)\].

Recent efforts also focus on bridging the gap between quantization optimization objectives and the specific demands of video diffusion transformers. For example, QVD introduces High Temporal Discriminability Quantization (HTDQ) and Scattered Channel Range Integration (SCRI) to better capture temporal feature dynamics and improve quantization level coverage across channels in video diffusion models, yielding near-lossless W8A8 quantization results \[[2407.11585](http://arxiv.org/abs/2407.11585)\]. Hardware-friendly static quantization methods have been developed to support deployment on AI processors lacking dynamic quantization capabilities, using step-wise calibration and smooth quantization to maintain video quality comparable to full precision \[[2502.15077](http://arxiv.org/abs/2502.15077)\].

Despite these advances, challenges remain in jointly addressing quantization error compensation across token and feature dimensions and optimizing models to preserve spatiotemporal correlations essential for video generation. Our work builds upon these foundations by introducing Token-aware Quantization Estimator (TQE) and Temporal Maintenance Distillation (TMD) techniques, which explicitly compensate quantization errors at both token and feature granularity and enforce temporal consistency during optimization, respectively. This approach complements prior quantization strategies and addresses their limitations in video DiT contexts \[[2505.18663](http://arxiv.org/abs/2505.18663)\]\[[2407.11585](http://arxiv.org/abs/2407.11585)\]\[[2502.15077](http://arxiv.org/abs/2502.15077)\].

In summary, the literature has made significant progress in quantizing diffusion transformers for image and video generation, with various methods tackling spatial variance, temporal consistency, and calibration challenges. However, video generation demands specialized quantization frameworks that jointly consider token-level quantization error compensation and temporal optimization, which motivates the contributions of our Q-VDiT framework.
