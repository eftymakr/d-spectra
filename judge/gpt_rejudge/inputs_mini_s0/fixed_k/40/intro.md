## Related Works

Zero-Knowledge Proofs (ZKPs) have become foundational in privacy-preserving and verifiable computing, enabling a prover to convince a verifier of a statement's validity without revealing any additional information \[[2408.0589](http://arxiv.org/abs/2408.05890)\]. Despite their broad applicability in areas such as blockchain, secure voting, and verifiable machine learning, widespread adoption remains limited due to the intensive computational demands of proof generation \[[2408.0589](http://arxiv.org/abs/2408.05890)\]. Recent research efforts have targeted accelerating core ZKP primitives, particularly the SumCheck protocol and Multi-scalar Multiplications (MSMs), which are central to many state-of-the-art zkSNARKs and related protocols.

Hardware acceleration has been a primary approach to overcoming performance bottlenecks. SZKP introduces a scalable ASIC accelerator that fully integrates the proof generation pipeline on-chip by exploiting structured dataflows for both Number Theoretic Transforms (NTTs) and MSMs. This architecture achieves conservative speedups exceeding 400× over CPU implementations and outperforms prior ASIC and GPU solutions by factors of 3× and 12×, respectively \[[2408.0589](http://arxiv.org/abs/2408.05890)\]. Complementarily, if-ZKP presents an Intel FPGA-based design optimized for the MSM operation in zkSNARK provers. Leveraging the inherent parallelism of MSM and modular arithmetic IP cores, it attains 110× to 150× speedups over software baselines for common elliptic curves like BLS12-381 and BN128 \[[2412.12481](http://arxiv.org/abs/2412.12481)\]. These works demonstrate the critical impact of accelerating MSM, a computationally intensive and data-dependent primitive, but typically focus on individual components rather than complete protocol acceleration.

Middleware solutions like zkFi address the integration challenges of ZKPs in multi-chain ecosystems, providing privacy-preserving transaction capabilities while ensuring regulatory compliance. zkFi abstracts the complexities of zero-knowledge proofs via a modular SDK, lowering the barrier for developers to adopt ZKPs in domains such as decentralized finance, gaming, and e-commerce \[[2206.1335](http://arxiv.org/abs/2206.13350)\]. While this approach enhances usability, it does not directly tackle the underlying computational challenges of proof generation.

The HyperPlonk protocol, which zkSpeed targets, advances the field by supporting a one-time, universal trusted setup alongside succinct proof sizes. This combination is advantageous for consensus-based public verifiability where numerous verifiers demand efficient verification times \[[2408.0589](http://arxiv.org/abs/2408.05890)\]. Prior accelerators have primarily optimized either MSM or SumCheck components in isolation; zkSpeed distinguishes itself by accelerating the entire proof generation process, including both SumCheck and MSM, using a full-chip ASIC design with substantial bandwidth (2 TB/s) and chip area (366.46 mm²). This comprehensive approach achieves geometric mean speedups of 801× over CPU baselines, marking a significant performance leap \[[2408.0589](http://arxiv.org/abs/2408.05890)\].

Beyond hardware acceleration of classical ZKP operations, research has also extended zero-knowledge techniques into new application areas. zkDL proposes efficient zero-knowledge proofs for deep learning training, overcoming challenges related to non-arithmetic operations like ReLU activations through specialized circuit designs and aggregation strategies. This enables scalable, privacy-preserving verifications of large neural networks within seconds per training batch \[[2307.00521](http://arxiv.org/abs/2307.00521)\]. Although distinct in focus, zkDL underscores the versatility of ZKPs and the necessity of efficient proof systems for complex computations.

Additional efforts have addressed foundational aspects of ZKP design and implementation. The zksk Python library provides a composable framework for constructing sigma protocols, facilitating modular proof development without requiring new domain-specific languages \[[2411.0635](http://arxiv.org/abs/2411.06350)\]. Streaming zero-knowledge proofs extend ZKP applicability to data streams, enabling space-efficient, one-pass verifications with zero knowledge guarantees through algebraic and temporal commitment protocols \[[2307.16273](http://arxiv.org/abs/2307.16273)\]. Moreover, research into new cryptographic primitives such as ZK-friendly hash functions like MiMC has led to hardware frameworks like AMAZE, which optimize collision-resistant hashing on resource-constrained devices, thereby improving ZKP performance in edge environments \[[2012.10452](http://arxiv.org/abs/2012.10452)\].

Experimental explorations into zero-knowledge protocols have also investigated physical security assumptions, such as relativistic zero-knowledge proofs that leverage special relativity to enforce security without relying on computational hardness assumptions, expanding the conceptual foundations of ZKPs \[[2301.02161](http://arxiv.org/abs/2301.02161)\]. Formal methods have advanced zero-knowledge proof design for complex problems, exemplified by Crêpe, which addresses the PSPACE-complete problem of regular expression equivalence using coinductive proofs, demonstrating scalable verification of higher-complexity statements \[[1911.02459](http://arxiv.org/abs/1911.02459)\].

In summary, zkSpeed builds upon a rich ecosystem of ZKP research, combining scalable hardware acceleration of key primitives with a protocol supporting universal setup and succinct proofs. This work advances the state of the art by delivering full-protocol acceleration and substantial speedups crucial for practical deployment in publicly verifiable systems \[[2408.0589](http://arxiv.org/abs/2408.05890)\]. The landscape of ZKP research continues to evolve, balancing efficiency, usability, and broad applicability in diverse computational domains.
