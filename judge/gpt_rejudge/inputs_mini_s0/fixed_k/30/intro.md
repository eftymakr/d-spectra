## Related Works

Testing database transaction isolation has been a critical area of research due to the inherent trade-offs between strong isolation guarantees and system performance. It is well-established that verifying strong isolation levels such as serializability and strict serializability is NP-complete, motivating the investigation of weak isolation levels that offer a more tractable testing complexity \[[2504.02344](http://arxiv.org/abs/2504.02344)\]. Weak isolation levels, including Read Committed (RC), Read Atomic (RA), and Causal Consistency (CC), are widely adopted in production databases to balance concurrency and performance, yet they have been shown to be prone to subtle isolation bugs \[[2501.14421](http://arxiv.org/abs/2501.14421)\]\[[2503.06284](http://arxiv.org/abs/2503.06284)\].

Black-box isolation checking methods traditionally rely on stressing databases with randomized concurrent workloads and verifying transaction histories post-execution. While effective, these approaches often suffer from significant overhead during workload generation and history verification, especially for strong isolation levels \[[2504.02344](http://arxiv.org/abs/2504.02344)\]. To address this, recent work proposed Mini-Transactions (MTs), compact transactions that accelerate history generation and enable efficient verification algorithms with linear or quadratic complexity for strong isolation levels, thereby improving scalability and bug detection capability \[[2504.02344](http://arxiv.org/abs/2504.02344)\].

For weak isolation levels, recent theoretical advances have demonstrated that testing can be performed in polynomial time, contrasting with the hardness of strong isolation testing \[[2303.12606](http://arxiv.org/abs/2303.12606)\]\[[2501.14421](http://arxiv.org/abs/2501.14421)\]. Notably, the use of dynamic partial order reduction techniques enables sound, complete, and memory-efficient stateless model checking for a range of weak isolation levels, including RC, Snapshot Isolation (SI), and Serializability \[[2303.12606](http://arxiv.org/abs/2303.12606)\]. Similarly, formal frameworks based on separation logic have been developed to rigorously specify and verify weak isolation levels, providing mechanized proofs of correctness for multi-version concurrency control implementations and establishing formal relationships between isolation levels \[[2501.14421](http://arxiv.org/abs/2501.14421)\].

Despite these improvements, existing testers for weak isolation levels still incur high polynomial computational costs, limiting their usability for large-scale workloads typical of modern database systems \[[2103.0283](http://arxiv.org/abs/2103.02830)\]\[[2503.06284](http://arxiv.org/abs/2503.06284)\]. Tools like MonkeyDB employ logical specifications of isolation levels to simulate and test storage-backed applications, providing coverage of weak behaviors but still facing challenges with workload size and complexity \[[2103.0283](http://arxiv.org/abs/2103.02830)\].

Advances in timestamp-based techniques have introduced efficient offline and online checkers for SI and serializability, capable of processing histories with millions of transactions rapidly. These approaches avoid constructing full dependency graphs by incrementally maintaining timestamp orders and have been extended to support online, continuous checking at high transaction throughput rates \[[2504.01477](http://arxiv.org/abs/2504.01477)\]. Such methods highlight the potential for real-time isolation verification in production environments.

Frameworks like VerIso have contributed formal verification tools within theorem provers to model concurrency control protocols and identify design-level isolation bugs, emphasizing the importance of rigorous formalism alongside practical testing \[[2503.06284](http://arxiv.org/abs/2503.06284)\]. Meanwhile, consistency checking frameworks like Coo leverage partial order pair graphs to represent transaction conflicts and construct inconsistent test cases for accurate, cost-effective verification across multiple isolation levels, revealing discrepancies between claimed and actual database behaviors \[[2206.14602](http://arxiv.org/abs/2206.14602)\].

Research on concurrency control protocols, including optimistic schemes tailored for mobile and distributed databases, further illustrates the complexity of balancing isolation guarantees with practical constraints such as blocking, abort rates, and communication overhead \[[1112.2155](http://arxiv.org/abs/1112.2155)\]. Similarly, lock-free or version-based isolation approaches, as demonstrated in blockchain systems such as Hyperledger Fabric, offer promising avenues to reduce performance bottlenecks while maintaining snapshot isolation semantics \[[1911.12711](http://arxiv.org/abs/1911.12711)\].

Overall, the landscape of isolation testing and verification is characterized by a progression from costly black-box methods toward more efficient, formally grounded, and scalable techniques. However, the polynomial complexity of current testers for weak isolation levels continues to pose challenges for large-scale application, motivating the development of optimal algorithms with improved runtime guarantees and practical applicability \[[2504.02344](http://arxiv.org/abs/2504.02344)\]\[[2303.12606](http://arxiv.org/abs/2303.12606)\]\[[2503.06284](http://arxiv.org/abs/2503.06284)\].
