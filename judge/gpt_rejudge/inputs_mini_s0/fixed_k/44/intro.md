## Related Works

Collaborative filtering (CF) has witnessed significant advancements through the integration of graph-based models and representation learning techniques. Traditional matrix factorization methods, such as SVD++, leverage implicit feedback from user interactions to enhance recommendation accuracy but often fail to fully exploit the complex user-item relationships present in large-scale data \[[1803.03502](http://arxiv.org/abs/1803.03502)\]. To overcome these limitations, graph neural networks (GNNs) have been employed to explicitly model the user-item interaction graph, capturing high-order connectivity and collaborative signals more effectively. Notably, Neural Graph Collaborative Filtering (NGCF) propagates embeddings over the bipartite user-item graph, resulting in richer representations and improved recommendation performance \[[1905.08108](http://arxiv.org/abs/1905.08108)\]. Extensions such as Multi-Graph Convolution Collaborative Filtering (Multi-GCCF) further incorporate multiple graphs, including user-user and item-item similarity graphs, to refine embeddings and capture diverse relational information \[[2001.00267](http://arxiv.org/abs/2001.00267)\]. Despite these advances, GNN-based CF models often face challenges including over-smoothing, noise, and scalability issues, which have motivated alternative frameworks like SimRec that combine knowledge distillation and contrastive learning to preserve global collaborative signals while mitigating representation degradation \[[2303.08537](http://arxiv.org/abs/2303.08537)\].

In parallel, embedding binarization has emerged as a promising direction to reduce memory usage and computational cost in recommender systems. Existing binarization techniques primarily focus on numerical quantization of embeddings or network weights but often neglect the impact of information loss on model accuracy. For example, near-lossless binarization of word embeddings compresses real-valued vectors into compact binary codes with minimal semantic degradation (~2% accuracy loss), enabling faster vector operations and smaller memory footprints \[[1803.09065](http://arxiv.org/abs/1803.09065)\]. Similarly, loss-aware binarization methods for deep networks optimize binarized weights by directly minimizing loss via proximal Newton algorithms, demonstrating robustness across feedforward and recurrent architectures \[[1611.016](http://arxiv.org/abs/1611.01600)\]. In the domain of collaborative filtering, Binarized Collaborative Filtering leverages graph convolutional networks to distill ranking information into binary embeddings, enhancing online recommendation efficiency while addressing optimization challenges through a continuous relaxation of binary constraints \[[1906.01829](http://arxiv.org/abs/1906.01829)\]. However, these methods still face the dilemma of balancing efficient representation with preservation of information critical to recommendation quality.

Recent efforts have also explored the integration of graph signal processing and spectral methods to improve embedding quality. Adaptive spectral graph wavelet frameworks utilize power transforms and localized graph signal representations to learn discriminative user and item embeddings, effectively capturing both local and global graph structures for better recommendation outcomes \[[2312.03167](http://arxiv.org/abs/2312.03167)\]. Moreover, graph-based implicit feedback models extend the classical CF paradigm by incorporating item-side feedback and learning weighted contributions of implicit interactions, thereby enriching user and item representations beyond equal-weight assumptions \[[1803.03502](http://arxiv.org/abs/1803.03502)\].

Beyond model architecture, the interpretability and evaluation of graph embeddings have gained attention, highlighting the need for robust frameworks to understand the information encoded in learned representations and their relation to graph structure recovery \[[1910.03081](http://arxiv.org/abs/1910.03081)\]. Unified frameworks such as Connector provide systematic support for diverse graph embedding models, facilitating experimentation and comparative analysis across shallow and deep approaches \[[2304.13195](http://arxiv.org/abs/2304.13195)\].

Addressing the fundamental challenge of information loss during binarization, recent advances propose explicit mitigation strategies. For instance, the BiGeaR++ framework builds upon prior binarized collaborative filtering models by incorporating supervisory signals from pseudo-positive samples and embedding sample synthesis, alongside fine-grained inference distillation mechanisms. These components collectively reduce information degradation during binarization and enhance model expressiveness, achieving substantial improvements over predecessor methods \[[1906.01829](http://arxiv.org/abs/1906.01829)\]. Such approaches align with the trend of combining knowledge distillation and embedding synthesis to retain semantic fidelity in compressed representations.

Finally, the role of federated and distributed learning paradigms introduces additional complexity in representation aggregation and information preservation. Studies analyzing neural network parameter averaging from an information-theoretic perspective reveal the nuanced effects of aggregation on mutual information between representations and data, offering insights applicable to distributed CF systems employing binarized embeddings \[[1911.07652](http://arxiv.org/abs/1911.07652)\].

In summary, the landscape of collaborative filtering has evolved through graph-based modeling of user-item interactions, innovative binarization techniques aimed at efficiency, and strategies explicitly targeting information loss during compression. Our work situates itself within this context by proposing a robust binarized graph embedding framework that leverages supervisory signals and distillation to balance efficiency and accuracy, pushing the state-of-the-art in efficient collaborative filtering.
