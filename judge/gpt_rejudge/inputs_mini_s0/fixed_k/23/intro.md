## Related Works

Radar-camera calibration is a critical prerequisite for effective sensor fusion in autonomous driving systems, enabling robust perception under diverse environmental conditions. Traditional approaches primarily employed target-based calibration methods, which require specifically designed calibration targets such as corner reflectors and controlled experimental setups. For example, a method using a single corner reflector on the ground iteratively collects radar and camera data to solve the perspective-n-point (PnP) problem, leveraging RANSAC and Levenberg-Marquardt (LM) optimization for robustness and accuracy \[[2309.03734](http://arxiv.org/abs/2309.03734)\]. These target-based methods, while accurate, are often impractical for in-field or online calibration due to their operational complexity and infrastructure requirements \[[2309.03734](http://arxiv.org/abs/2309.03734)\]\[[2302.10511](http://arxiv.org/abs/2302.10511)\].

To overcome these limitations, recent research has shifted towards targetless calibration techniques that do not require dedicated calibration targets. One notable direction involves deep learning methods that extract common features from both radar and camera data for online extrinsic calibration. Such methods implicitly match objects detected by radar and camera through learned feature representations, enabling continuous calibration without manual intervention \[[2307.15264](http://arxiv.org/abs/2307.15264)\]. These approaches utilize robust optimization frameworks like RANSAC and LM to derive the extrinsic transformation matrix reliably in real-world conditions \[[2307.15264](http://arxiv.org/abs/2307.15264)\].

Spatiotemporal calibration methods have also been explored, focusing on simultaneous spatial and temporal alignment of radar and camera sensors. Some leverage radar ego-velocity measurements and motion analysis to perform in-situ calibration without specialized infrastructure, maintaining calibration accuracy comparable to target-based methods \[[2302.10511](http://arxiv.org/abs/2302.10511)\]. Continuous-time calibration frameworks further extend these concepts by utilizing radar velocity data to estimate extrinsic parameters dynamically, which is particularly important for 3D millimeter-wave radars emerging in autonomous vehicle applications \[[2103.07505](http://arxiv.org/abs/2103.07505)\].

Beyond calibration techniques, sensor fusion strategies have evolved to address the intrinsic challenges posed by radar data sparsity, height uncertainty, and measurement noise. Bird’s-Eye-View (BEV) representations have become popular for radar-camera fusion due to their robustness against perspective distortion and height uncertainty. Several recent works propose multi-level fusion architectures that combine radar and camera features in BEV space, enhancing 3D object detection performance. For instance, RCM-Fusion integrates radar-guided BEV encoding and instance-level refinement to reduce localization errors, achieving state-of-the-art results on public datasets such as nuScenes \[[2307.10249](http://arxiv.org/abs/2307.10249)\]. Similarly, RCBEVDet employs a dual-stream radar backbone and cross-attention fusion to align radar and camera BEV features effectively, improving detection speed and accuracy \[[2403.1644](http://arxiv.org/abs/2403.16440)\].

Other innovative fusion approaches include semantic alignment of radar features with multi-view camera images and transformer-based fusion mechanisms that enhance cross-modal correlation through attention modules \[[2302.10511](http://arxiv.org/abs/2302.10511)\]\[[2404.03015](http://arxiv.org/abs/2404.03015)\]. The Dual Perspective Fusion Transformer (DPFT) method, for example, leverages radar cubes and projection onto both camera and ground planes to preserve elevation information and improve robustness under adverse weather conditions \[[2404.03015](http://arxiv.org/abs/2404.03015)\]. Furthermore, ClusterFusion capitalizes on radar point cloud clustering and handcrafted feature extraction to retain spatial radar characteristics before projecting onto the image plane, mitigating information loss common in image-plane fusion \[[2309.03734](http://arxiv.org/abs/2309.03734)\].

Robustness against environmental and intrinsic disturbances remains a core challenge in radar-camera fusion. Methods like RobuRCDet introduce modules to compensate for radar point inaccuracies and adaptively fuse features based on camera signal confidence, demonstrating improved performance under noisy and adverse conditions \[[2502.13071](http://arxiv.org/abs/2502.13071)\]. Cross-domain spatial matching techniques further enhance fusion by transforming camera 2D features into 3D space and complementing them with radar data for more accurate 3D object representations \[[2103.07825](http://arxiv.org/abs/2103.07825)\].

Finally, advances in representation learning and data association have facilitated better radar-camera matching despite noisy radar measurements and depth ambiguities. Deep learning-based association models surpass traditional rule-based methods by learning feature-level interactions and global reasoning, achieving higher F1 scores and robustness in complex driving scenarios \[[2103.07825](http://arxiv.org/abs/2103.07825)\].

The proposed method builds upon this rich background by introducing a novel Dual-Perspective representation that exploits both frontal and bird’s-eye views, addressing height uncertainty and data sparsity challenges inherent in radar measurements. The inclusion of a Selective Fusion Mechanism and Multi-Modal Cross-Attention further refines correspondence matching, supported by a Noise-Resistant Matcher that enhances robustness during training. This approach advances beyond prior work by combining geometric calibration with deep feature fusion strategies, establishing a new benchmark for online automatic radar-camera calibration.
