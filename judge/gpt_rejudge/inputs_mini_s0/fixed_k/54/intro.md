## Related Works

Graph Convolutional Networks (GCNs) have become fundamental in collaborative filtering (CF) for recommendation systems by effectively capturing the structural information of user-item interaction graphs. Early works like Neural Graph Collaborative Filtering (NGCF) explicitly propagate embeddings over the user-item bipartite graph to inject collaborative signals and model high-order connectivity, significantly improving recommendation accuracy \[[1905.08108](http://arxiv.org/abs/1905.08108)\]. However, these models often rely on embedding layers that map user and item IDs into latent spaces, which may limit the capacity to express locally observed but valuable preference patterns \[[2007.03383](http://arxiv.org/abs/2007.03383)\]. Moreover, the common neighborhood aggregation mechanism tends to apply uniform or linear transformations, which restricts the ability to capture diverse and fine-grained preference signals \[[2001.00267](http://arxiv.org/abs/2001.00267)\].

To alleviate these limitations, several approaches have been proposed. Linear residual graph convolutional networks remove nonlinearities to ease training on large graphs and mitigate over-smoothing, improving scalability and performance \[[2001.10167](http://arxiv.org/abs/2001.10167)\]. Residual structures tailored for CF further improve training stability and expressiveness by separately aggregating multi-hop neighborhood messages and addressing the over-smoothing problem \[[1905.08108](http://arxiv.org/abs/1905.08108)\]\[[2004.14734](http://arxiv.org/abs/2004.14734)\]. Other GCN variants focus on refining embedding representations by incorporating node popularity features and reducing information redundancy in nonlinear graph convolution operations, thereby enhancing expressiveness especially in sparse recommendation scenarios \[[2007.03383](http://arxiv.org/abs/2007.03383)\].

Beyond purely spatial aggregation, spectral graph theory offers a principled framework for designing graph filters that operate in the graph frequency domain. Traditional GCNs predominantly function as low-pass filters, limiting their ability to leverage middle- and high-frequency components that could encode richer user preference patterns \[[2107.04755](http://arxiv.org/abs/2107.04755)\]. To overcome this, recent works design more flexible graph convolutional filters capable of capturing a broader spectrum of graph signals. For example, AutoGCN automatically adapts filter bandwidth to cover a wider frequency range, improving performance by capturing the full spectrum of graph signals \[[2107.04755](http://arxiv.org/abs/2107.04755)\]. Similarly, PolyCF introduces polynomial graph filters to approximate optimal spectral responses for recovering missing interactions, enabling more expressive collaborative filtering \[[2401.1259](http://arxiv.org/abs/2401.12590)\]. Analytical graph filters, such as GraphBio, provide interpretable and trainable low-pass filters optimized via graph topology learning, demonstrating robust performance in related tasks \[[2307.04384](http://arxiv.org/abs/2307.04384)\].

Chebyshev polynomial-based filters are particularly relevant for spectral graph convolution due to their efficient approximation of graph signal processing operations. Chebyshev recurrence allows scalable and numerically stable implementation of higher-order filters without explicit eigen-decomposition, facilitating flexible nonlinear spectral filtering \[[2001.10167](http://arxiv.org/abs/2001.10167)\]\[[1411.2389](http://arxiv.org/abs/1411.2389)\]. This approach has been successfully applied in graph image denoising, outperforming existing polynomial filters by leveraging Chebyshev and conjugate gradient methods \[[1509.01624](http://arxiv.org/abs/1509.01624)\]. In recommender systems, leveraging Chebyshev polynomial filters can enhance the model’s ability to capture complex, multi-frequency preference signals beyond simple low-pass filtering, addressing key bottlenecks in embedding capacity and neighborhood aggregation.

Other notable advancements include Multi-Graph Convolution Collaborative Filtering (Multi-GCCF), which integrates multiple graph structures (user-user, item-item, and user-item) to better exploit various relational signals \[[2001.00267](http://arxiv.org/abs/2001.00267)\], and methods that incorporate causal modeling within GCNs to capture deeper dependencies and improve embedding robustness \[[2307.04384](http://arxiv.org/abs/2307.04384)\]. Contrastive learning combined with graph convolution has also emerged as a promising paradigm, mitigating data sparsity and popularity bias by enforcing consistency between augmented graph views \[[2406.13996](http://arxiv.org/abs/2406.13996)\]\[[2209.02544](http://arxiv.org/abs/2209.02544)\]. Additionally, graph trend filtering and adaptive propagation strategies have been proposed to explicitly handle unreliable interactions and dynamically control message-passing depth, further enhancing robustness and expressiveness \[[2108.05552](http://arxiv.org/abs/2108.05552)\]\[[2002.10306](http://arxiv.org/abs/2002.10306)\].

Overall, these works highlight the importance of moving beyond fixed, linear, low-pass graph filters and simple embedding layers toward more flexible, spectrum-aware graph filtering techniques that can fully exploit the rich structure of user-item interactions. The proposed ChebyCF framework builds upon this foundation by utilizing Chebyshev interpolation to approximate nonlinear spectral graph filters with enhanced frequency coverage and normalization, directly processing raw user interaction signals without a learned embedding layer. This approach aligns with and extends prior efforts in spectral graph filtering and polynomial graph convolution for collaborative filtering \[[2401.1259](http://arxiv.org/abs/2401.12590)\]\[[2307.04384](http://arxiv.org/abs/2307.04384)\]\[[2001.10167](http://arxiv.org/abs/2001.10167)\]\[[2107.04755](http://arxiv.org/abs/2107.04755)\]\[[1411.2389](http://arxiv.org/abs/1411.2389)\]\[[1509.01624](http://arxiv.org/abs/1509.01624)\].
