## Related Works

Transformer-based pre-trained language models (PLMs) have significantly advanced multilingual and cross-lingual retrieval by learning contextualized semantic representations that improve over traditional lexical retrieval methods \[[2211.14876](http://arxiv.org/abs/2211.14876)\]\[[2010.10469](http://arxiv.org/abs/2010.10469)\]. Models like mBERT and XLM-RoBERTa (XLM-R) have been widely adopted for cross-lingual information retrieval (CLIR) due to their multilingual pretraining, enabling zero-shot transfer and translation-based training strategies. ColBERT-X, for instance, leverages XLM-R to support cross-language dense retrieval and shows substantial improvements over classical lexical baselines when trained on English data with translations \[[2201.08471](http://arxiv.org/abs/2201.08471)\]. However, these multilingual PLMs often underperform on low-resource and morphologically rich languages such as Amharic, primarily due to limited training data and linguistic complexity \[[2503.1857](http://arxiv.org/abs/2503.18570)\]\[[2408.11942](http://arxiv.org/abs/2408.11942)\]\[[2108.06279](http://arxiv.org/abs/2108.06279)\].

Low-resource languages face unique challenges in retrieval, including data scarcity and complex morphology, which hinder the effectiveness of multilingual models. Prior research has demonstrated that language-specific adaptation and dedicated datasets can improve retrieval performance. For Amharic, several efforts have focused on building annotated resources and models tailored to its linguistic characteristics. For example, an Amharic Named Entity Recognition (NER) corpus and transformer-based recognizers have been developed, achieving state-of-the-art results by leveraging transfer learning and handling class imbalance effectively \[[2207.00785](http://arxiv.org/abs/2207.00785)\]. Similarly, fine-tuning large language models such as LLaMA-2 on augmented Amharic datasets, including translated and multimodal data, has shown promise in enhancing language understanding and retrieval capabilities \[[2403.06354](http://arxiv.org/abs/2403.06354)\].

Dense retrieval methods, which encode queries and documents into dense vector spaces for semantic matching, have gained traction in low-resource settings. Studies comparing single representation models (e.g., ANCE) and multiple representation models (e.g., ColBERT) indicate that late interaction mechanisms, as in ColBERT, often yield better retrieval effectiveness, especially for complex queries, albeit with higher computational costs \[[2108.06279](http://arxiv.org/abs/2108.06279)\]. Applying these dense retrieval architectures to Amharic, with adaptations to pre-trained Amharic BERT and RoBERTa backbones, helps mitigate tokenization inefficiencies and morphological challenges \[[2503.1857](http://arxiv.org/abs/2503.18570)\]. Moreover, parameter-efficient transfer learning approaches using adapters have been proposed to adapt monolingual retrieval models to new languages, improving cross-lingual dense retrieval without full fine-tuning, although the best strategies for dense retrieval differ from other NLP tasks \[[2212.10448](http://arxiv.org/abs/2212.10448)\].

Data scarcity remains a critical bottleneck for low-resource retrieval. Synthetic data generation using large language models to create multilingual training datasets has emerged as a practical solution. The SWIM-IR dataset, which spans 33 languages including low-resource ones, employs a summarize-then-ask prompting strategy to generate informative queries, enabling effective fine-tuning of multilingual dense retrievers without human supervision \[[2311.058](http://arxiv.org/abs/2311.05800)\]. Similarly, translation and transliteration augmentation techniques have been explored to improve question answering and retrieval in low-resource languages by augmenting scarce datasets \[[2204.05814](http://arxiv.org/abs/2204.05814)\]. These methods highlight the importance of data composition and fine-tuning strategies to improve automatic speech recognition (ASR), retrieval, and question answering in Amharic and similar languages \[[2503.18485](http://arxiv.org/abs/2503.18485)\]\[[2303.0329](http://arxiv.org/abs/2303.03290)\].

Cross-lingual retrieval and question answering for Amharic have also been studied through curated datasets and model evaluations, revealing that while multilingual models provide a baseline, language-specific training and contrastive learning yield notable gains \[[2408.11942](http://arxiv.org/abs/2408.11942)\]\[[2204.05814](http://arxiv.org/abs/2204.05814)\]\[[2303.0329](http://arxiv.org/abs/2303.03290)\]. Additionally, speech emotion recognition research underscores the potential of cross-lingual and multilingual training to improve model robustness for Amharic despite limited native data \[[2307.10814](http://arxiv.org/abs/2307.10814)\].

Sparse retrieval methods and hybrid approaches have been investigated to balance efficiency and effectiveness. Techniques like Lexically-Accelerated Dense Retrieval (LADR) improve dense retrieval efficiency without sacrificing accuracy, which is crucial when deploying retrieval systems for low-resource languages with limited computational resources \[[2307.16779](http://arxiv.org/abs/2307.16779)\]. Further, combining dense retrieval with lexical retrieval in a blended fashion enhances retrieval quality, as demonstrated in retrieval-augmented generation systems \[[2404.0722](http://arxiv.org/abs/2404.07220)\].

Finally, the creation of benchmark datasets and public resources for Amharic is essential to advance research. The introduction of new Amharic QA datasets and open-source retrieval models provides standardized evaluation frameworks that facilitate systematic comparison of retrieval approaches and foster community development \[[2503.1857](http://arxiv.org/abs/2503.18570)\]\[[2303.0329](http://arxiv.org/abs/2303.03290)\]. These resources, alongside codebases and trained models, support deeper investigation into the interplay of morphology, tokenization, and retrieval effectiveness in low-resource language settings.

In summary, while multilingual dense retrieval models have advanced cross-lingual IR, their performance on Amharic is constrained by data availability and linguistic complexity. Language-specific dense retrieval models, adapter-based transfer learning, synthetic data generation, and late interaction architectures like ColBERT collectively represent promising directions to improve retrieval effectiveness for Amharic and other low-resource morphologically rich languages \[[2503.1857](http://arxiv.org/abs/2503.18570)\]\[[2207.00785](http://arxiv.org/abs/2207.00785)\]\[[2503.18485](http://arxiv.org/abs/2503.18485)\]\[[2403.06354](http://arxiv.org/abs/2403.06354)\]\[[2212.10448](http://arxiv.org/abs/2212.10448)\]\[[2201.08471](http://arxiv.org/abs/2201.08471)\]\[[2108.06279](http://arxiv.org/abs/2108.06279)\]\[[2311.058](http://arxiv.org/abs/2311.05800)\]\[[2204.05814](http://arxiv.org/abs/2204.05814)\]\[[2303.0329](http://arxiv.org/abs/2303.03290)\].
