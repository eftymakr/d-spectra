## Related Works

Radar-camera calibration and fusion have been extensively studied to enable robust perception in autonomous driving, with research addressing both extrinsic calibration and sensor data fusion. Traditional extrinsic calibration methods often rely on dedicated calibration targets and offline procedures, which are impractical for dynamic and long-term autonomous system operation \[[https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/0955326c67a947b5fac5fbae7238d04fc9adc251](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/0955326c67a947b5fac5fbae7238d04fc9adc251)\]\[[2103.07505](http://arxiv.org/abs/2103.07505)\]\[[https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/fa7f4199640500d48c0671181ea6a8dce3141123](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/fa7f4199640500d48c0671181ea6a8dce3141123)\]. Target-based approaches typically require special calibration targets and multi-step optimization to handle radar measurement sparsity and elevation uncertainty, as in the geometric constraint and radar cross section pattern exploitation methods \[[https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/0955326c67a947b5fac5fbae7238d04fc9adc251](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/0955326c67a947b5fac5fbae7238d04fc9adc251)\]. However, these methods are not suitable for online or targetless calibration scenarios.

Recent advances emphasize targetless and online calibration methods. One line of work proposes online calibration based on moving object tracking, leveraging detections and associations already present in autonomous perception pipelines to estimate calibration parameters without specialized targets \[[https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/c80265fa719eb85c8a56a6b36c74b99b793f39b2](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/c80265fa719eb85c8a56a6b36c74b99b793f39b2)\]. This approach detects decalibration quickly and estimates rotational parameters with low latency, demonstrating the feasibility of online calibration in real-world sensor setups. Complementing this, data-driven methods utilize deep learning to perform automatic rotational calibration without targets by extracting common features from radar and camera data \[[1904.08743](http://arxiv.org/abs/1904.08743)\]. These methods employ coarse-to-fine convolutional neural networks trained on real-world data to achieve precise sensor registration despite radar sparsity and measurement noise.

Sensor fusion approaches are closely related to calibration as accurate extrinsic parameters are critical for effective data alignment. Multi-modal fusion methods have evolved from late fusion (fusing outputs of single-sensor detectors) to early and feature-level fusion, which better exploit complementary sensor characteristics \[[2209.06535](http://arxiv.org/abs/2209.06535)\]\[[https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/a94f8a34a9c4fce90727f62d652f2420fd1021a2](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/a94f8a34a9c4fce90727f62d652f2420fd1021a2)\]\[[2304.00670](http://arxiv.org/abs/2304.00670)\]\[[2302.10511](http://arxiv.org/abs/2302.10511)\]. Early fusion methods transform image features into bird’s-eye-view (BEV) representations aligned with radar features, employing attention mechanisms to overcome spatial misalignment and height uncertainty \[[2304.00670](http://arxiv.org/abs/2304.00670)\]\[[2302.10511](http://arxiv.org/abs/2302.10511)\]. Semantic alignment and cross-attention transformers have been proposed to enhance correlation between radar and camera features, significantly improving 3D object detection performance on benchmarks such as nuScenes \[[2302.10511](http://arxiv.org/abs/2302.10511)\]. Feature-level fusion frameworks that combine multi-camera and multi-radar data in BEV space have also shown substantial gains in detection accuracy and robustness \[[https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/a94f8a34a9c4fce90727f62d652f2420fd1021a2](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/a94f8a34a9c4fce90727f62d652f2420fd1021a2)\].

Robustness to radar sparsity and height uncertainty remains a major challenge. Methods incorporating multi-perspective feature extraction, such as combining frontal and BEV views, can leverage the rich but height-sensitive frontal features with the more robust BEV features, reducing the impact of radar height measurement noise \[[2304.10410](http://arxiv.org/abs/2304.10410)\]\[[2304.00670](http://arxiv.org/abs/2304.00670)\]. Cross-modal attention mechanisms further improve correspondence matching between radar and image features, addressing the difficulties posed by radar’s sparse and noisy measurements \[[2304.00670](http://arxiv.org/abs/2304.00670)\]\[[2103.07825](http://arxiv.org/abs/2103.07825)\].

Deep learning-based calibration and fusion architectures have been proposed to handle imperfect sensor data and noisy labels. Techniques such as noise-resistant matching and representation learning with ordinal loss functions enhance robustness against label noise and improve association accuracy between radar points and camera detections \[[2005.07431](http://arxiv.org/abs/2005.07431)\]\[[2103.07825](http://arxiv.org/abs/2103.07825)\]. Moreover, continuous-time calibration methods exploit radar velocity measurements to circumvent the need for specialized reflectors, enabling 3D radar-camera extrinsic calibration in dynamic environments \[[2103.07505](http://arxiv.org/abs/2103.07505)\].

Datasets like nuScenes, which provide synchronized radar, camera, and LiDAR data with comprehensive annotations, have been instrumental in benchmarking both calibration and fusion methods \[[1903.11027](http://arxiv.org/abs/1903.11027)\]. The availability of such datasets has accelerated progress in data-driven approaches, allowing improved evaluation of methods under realistic driving scenarios.

In summary, existing studies have explored target-based and targetless extrinsic calibration, online calibration through object tracking, and deep learning techniques for automatic calibration and fusion. However, challenges such as radar sparsity and height uncertainty remain significant. The proposed approach advances the state of the art by introducing a Dual-Perspective representation that combines frontal and BEV features with a Selective Fusion Mechanism and Multi-Modal Cross-Attention, along with noise-resistant supervision strategies. This addresses key limitations of previous methods, providing more reliable and accurate online radar-camera calibration validated on large-scale datasets \[[https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/c80265fa719eb85c8a56a6b36c74b99b793f39b2](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/c80265fa719eb85c8a56a6b36c74b99b793f39b2)\]\[[1904.08743](http://arxiv.org/abs/1904.08743)\]\[[https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/0955326c67a947b5fac5fbae7238d04fc9adc251](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/0955326c67a947b5fac5fbae7238d04fc9adc251)\]\[[2103.07505](http://arxiv.org/abs/2103.07505)\]\[[2304.00670](http://arxiv.org/abs/2304.00670)\]\[[2005.07431](http://arxiv.org/abs/2005.07431)\]\[[2302.10511](http://arxiv.org/abs/2302.10511)\]\[[https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/fa7f4199640500d48c0671181ea6a8dce3141123](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/fa7f4199640500d48c0671181ea6a8dce3141123)\]\[[2103.07825](http://arxiv.org/abs/2103.07825)\]\[[1903.11027](http://arxiv.org/abs/1903.11027)\].
