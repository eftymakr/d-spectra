## Related Works

Neural retrieval methods leveraging transformer-based pre-trained language models (PLMs) have significantly advanced the state of multilingual and cross-lingual information retrieval (IR). Models such as BERT and its derivatives have been widely adopted due to their ability to capture deep contextualized representations, improving retrieval effectiveness over traditional lexical methods like BM25 \[[2004.12832](http://arxiv.org/abs/2004.12832)\]\[[1810.04805](http://arxiv.org/abs/1810.04805)\]\[[1905.09217](http://arxiv.org/abs/1905.09217)\]. However, these models often face computational challenges, especially when applied to large-scale retrieval tasks, motivating innovations such as ColBERT’s late interaction architecture which balances effectiveness and efficiency by encoding queries and documents separately and performing lightweight similarity computations at the token level \[[2004.12832](http://arxiv.org/abs/2004.12832)\]\[[2112.01488](http://arxiv.org/abs/2112.01488)\].

Multilingual pre-trained models such as multilingual BERT (mBERT) and XLM-RoBERTa (XLM-R) have shown strong cross-lingual transfer capabilities, enabling zero-shot and few-shot retrieval for multiple languages \[[1911.02116](http://arxiv.org/abs/1911.02116)\]\[[2201.08471](http://arxiv.org/abs/2201.08471)\]\[[1901.07291](http://arxiv.org/abs/1901.07291)\]\[[1910.11856](http://arxiv.org/abs/1910.11856)\]. XLM-R, pretrained on over 100 languages using large-scale CommonCrawl data, outperforms mBERT notably on low-resource languages, demonstrating substantial improvements in cross-lingual classification and question answering tasks \[[1911.02116](http://arxiv.org/abs/1911.02116)\]. This has motivated extensions like ColBERT-X, which adapts ColBERT with XLM-R encoders for cross-lingual dense retrieval, achieving significant gains over traditional lexical baselines especially for non-English languages \[[2201.08471](http://arxiv.org/abs/2201.08471)\]. Nonetheless, these multilingual models still suffer from performance gaps on morphologically rich and low-resource languages due to limited pretraining data and suboptimal tokenization strategies \[[2301.12566](http://arxiv.org/abs/2301.12566)\].

Addressing low-resource and morphologically complex languages remains a major challenge in neural IR. Language-specific adaptations, such as training monolingual or language-specialized models, have been shown to improve retrieval performance, especially when coupled with subword tokenization techniques that better capture morphological variations \[[1508.07909](http://arxiv.org/abs/1508.07909)\]. For instance, dense retrieval models trained on language-specific datasets outperform multilingual baselines on languages like Amharic by effectively leveraging pretrained BERT or RoBERTa backbones fine-tuned on limited but targeted corpora \[[2201.08471](http://arxiv.org/abs/2201.08471)\]\[[2301.12566](http://arxiv.org/abs/2301.12566)\]. Such approaches highlight the importance of tailoring tokenization and model architectures to linguistic characteristics to mitigate data scarcity issues.

Data scarcity in low-resource settings has also spurred research into synthetic data generation and knowledge distillation methods. Multilingual datasets created by machine translation, such as mMARCO, enable fine-tuning of multilingual rerankers and dense retrievers on translated passages, improving zero-shot retrieval effectiveness \[[2108.13897](http://arxiv.org/abs/2108.13897)\]. Similarly, knowledge distillation and optimal transport distillation techniques have been proposed to transfer retrieval knowledge from high-resource to low-resource languages while minimizing the need for large-scale parallel corpora, which are often unavailable \[[2301.12566](http://arxiv.org/abs/2301.12566)\]. These strategies are critical for improving retrieval models on underrepresented languages without requiring costly annotation efforts.

Dense retrieval architectures have further evolved with advances in training methodologies. Contrastive learning approaches, such as Approximate Nearest Neighbor Negative Contrastive Estimation (ANCE), address the challenge of selecting hard negative samples during training, significantly enhancing dense retriever performance and narrowing the gap with sparse or hybrid retrieval methods \[[2007.00808](http://arxiv.org/abs/2007.00808)\]. Moreover, compact model variants like DistilBERT and medium-sized RoBERTa models offer competitive accuracy with reduced computational footprints, enabling practical deployment in resource-constrained environments \[[1910.01108](http://arxiv.org/abs/1910.01108)\]. These developments are particularly relevant for low-resource language contexts where computational resources may be limited.

Benchmarking and evaluation frameworks have also been expanded to better assess the generalization and zero-shot abilities of retrieval models across diverse languages and domains. The BEIR benchmark compiles a heterogeneous set of datasets and retrieval tasks, providing a rigorous testbed for lexical, sparse, dense, and re-ranking methods in multilingual and cross-lingual scenarios \[[2104.08663](http://arxiv.org/abs/2104.08663)\]. Such benchmarks are essential for revealing the limitations of current models on low-resource and morphologically rich languages and guiding future research.

In summary, the integration of language-specific dense retrieval models, informed tokenization strategies, and advanced training techniques has led to notable improvements in multilingual and cross-lingual IR, yet challenges remain for low-resource languages like Amharic. The evidence underscores the need for tailored architectures and data-efficient training methods to overcome linguistic complexity and data scarcity, as demonstrated by recent work on Amharic-specific BERT and RoBERTa-based models and ColBERT late interaction architectures \[[2004.12832](http://arxiv.org/abs/2004.12832)\]\[[2201.08471](http://arxiv.org/abs/2201.08471)\]\[[2301.12566](http://arxiv.org/abs/2301.12566)\].
