## Related Works

Generative retrieval has emerged as a novel paradigm that replaces traditional search index data structures with a single large-scale neural network, enabling direct retrieval through the generation of document identifiers (docids) conditioned on a query \[[2305.11841](http://arxiv.org/abs/2305.11841)\]\[[2202.06991](http://arxiv.org/abs/2202.06991)\]. This approach, often referred to as a differentiable search index, eliminates the need for external indices and supports end-to-end optimization, offering potential benefits in efficiency and integration with generative large language models \[[2305.11841](http://arxiv.org/abs/2305.11841)\]\[[2208.07652](http://arxiv.org/abs/2208.07652)\]\[[2202.06991](http://arxiv.org/abs/2202.06991)\].

Early foundational work introduced the Differentiable Search Index (DSI), demonstrating that a Transformer model can memorize a corpus and retrieve documents by autoregressively generating docids, outperforming traditional dual-encoder baselines especially in zero-shot settings \[[2202.06991](http://arxiv.org/abs/2202.06991)\]. Building on this, subsequent studies have explored various tokenization schemes for docids, including fixed rule-based identifiers such as titles or clustered embeddings, which often fail to capture full semantic content \[[2304.04171](http://arxiv.org/abs/2304.04171)\]. To address this, GenRet proposed learning discrete semantic docids via an auto-encoding framework, improving retrieval generalization and performance on large-scale benchmarks like MS MARCO and BEIR \[[2304.04171](http://arxiv.org/abs/2304.04171)\].

Scaling generative retrieval to large corpora remains challenging. While initial methods demonstrated competitive results on datasets with up to 100k documents, recent efforts have extended evaluation to millions of passages, such as the full MS MARCO passage ranking task with 8.8 million passages \[[2305.11841](http://arxiv.org/abs/2305.11841)\]. These studies revealed key insights, including the critical role of synthetic queries for indexing, the limited gains from naively scaling model size, and the ineffectiveness of some architectural modifications when accounting for computational cost \[[2305.11841](http://arxiv.org/abs/2305.11841)\]. RIPOR introduced a novel prefix-oriented ranking optimization and relevance-based docid construction to improve large-scale retrieval performance, achieving substantial improvements over prior generative retrieval models on MS MARCO and TREC Deep Learning benchmarks \[[2311.09134](http://arxiv.org/abs/2311.09134)\].

Alternative approaches to docid generation have been proposed to circumvent structural constraints. For instance, SEAL utilizes all ngrams in a passage as potential identifiers, allowing autoregressive models to generate and score distinctive substrings without imposing hierarchical search space structures. This method outperforms previous autoregressive retrieval techniques and achieves strong results on knowledge-intensive benchmarks with a smaller memory footprint \[[2204.10628](http://arxiv.org/abs/2204.10628)\]. Similarly, GENRE reframes entity retrieval as autoregressive generation of entity names, effectively capturing fine-grained context-entity interactions and reducing memory requirements compared to dense embedding methods \[[2010.00904](http://arxiv.org/abs/2010.00904)\].

Continual learning is an emerging concern for generative retrieval, given the dynamic nature of many corpora. CLEVER addresses this by incrementally encoding new documents into docids using product quantization and a memory-augmented learning mechanism to prevent forgetting, enabling efficient updates without full retraining \[[2308.14968](http://arxiv.org/abs/2308.14968)\].

Generative retrieval methods have also been contextualized within the broader retrieval-augmented generation (RAG) framework, which combines parametric models and non-parametric memory for knowledge-intensive language tasks. RAG models leverage dense vector indexes alongside sequence-to-sequence generation to improve answer factuality and diversity \[[2005.11401](http://arxiv.org/abs/2005.11401)\]. However, retrieval quality remains a bottleneck as corpus size grows. Blended RAG enhances retrieval accuracy by integrating semantic search techniques, including dense and sparse vector indexes with hybrid query strategies, yielding superior results on diverse benchmarks \[[2404.0722](http://arxiv.org/abs/2404.07220)\].

Dense retrieval remains a strong alternative, employing learned dense vector representations for queries and passages via dual-encoder architectures. Techniques like ANCE improve dense retriever training by mining hard negatives using approximate nearest neighbor search, substantially enhancing retrieval accuracy and speed \[[2004.04906](http://arxiv.org/abs/2004.04906)\]\[[2007.00808](http://arxiv.org/abs/2007.00808)\]. Efficient training strategies such as TAS-Balanced further optimize dense retrievers for resource efficiency and balanced topic coverage \[[2104.06967](http://arxiv.org/abs/2104.06967)\].

The theoretical understanding of generative retrieval has also advanced. Recent work investigates the limitations imposed by corpus-specific constraints and decoding algorithms like beam search. Analyses show that constrained autoregressive generation introduces inherent error lower bounds related to the Kullback-Leibler divergence between ground-truth and model-predicted token distributions, and that marginal distributions used in beam search may not optimally reflect relevance probabilities \[[2305.11841](http://arxiv.org/abs/2305.11841)\]. These insights align with broader studies on KL divergence convergence and loss function design, which aim to improve model robustness and generalization \[[2007.00808](http://arxiv.org/abs/2007.00808)\]\[[2005.11401](http://arxiv.org/abs/2005.11401)\].

In addition, novel optimization frameworks like CARROT address retrieval cost constraints in RAG systems by adapting retrieval strategies to chunk redundancy and non-monotonic utility, demonstrating significant performance gains \[[2311.09134](http://arxiv.org/abs/2311.09134)\]. These contributions collectively advance both the theoretical foundations and practical implementations of generative retrieval, highlighting challenges and opportunities for improving generalization, scalability, and efficiency.
