## Related Works

Rehearsal-based methods have become a cornerstone in continual learning (CL) to mitigate catastrophic forgetting by storing a subset of past data and replaying it alongside new task data \[[1705.08690](http://arxiv.org/abs/1705.08690)\]. This concurrent rehearsal strategy, where old and new samples are trained together, is widely adopted due to its empirical effectiveness across various domains, including image classification and reinforcement learning \[[1705.08690](http://arxiv.org/abs/1705.08690)\]\[[1811.11682](http://arxiv.org/abs/1811.11682)\]. However, storing and replaying all previous data is often infeasible or memory-intensive, motivating research into more efficient and scalable rehearsal approaches \[[1705.08690](http://arxiv.org/abs/1705.08690)\]\[[1809.05922](http://arxiv.org/abs/1809.05922)\].

Inspired by biological memory systems, particularly the hippocampus’s generative replay and consolidation mechanisms, generative replay methods have been proposed to sample pseudo-data representing past tasks, thus avoiding explicit storage of original data \[[1705.08690](http://arxiv.org/abs/1705.08690)\]\[[https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/7bfb4ef17eabec1acd266958bdb08622eebfbb05](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/7bfb4ef17eabec1acd266958bdb08622eebfbb05)\]\[[1710.10368](http://arxiv.org/abs/1710.10368)\]. These approaches have demonstrated success in alleviating forgetting, especially in class-incremental scenarios where task identity is unknown at test time \[[1809.10635](http://arxiv.org/abs/1809.10635)\]. However, scaling generative replay to complex, large-scale tasks remains challenging \[[https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/7bfb4ef17eabec1acd266958bdb08622eebfbb05](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/7bfb4ef17eabec1acd266958bdb08622eebfbb05)\]\[[1710.10368](http://arxiv.org/abs/1710.10368)\].

Beyond concurrent rehearsal, the notion of sequential rehearsal—revisiting tasks one after another rather than mixing data concurrently—draws inspiration from human learning processes where sequential reactivation aids memory retention \[[1612.00796](http://arxiv.org/abs/1612.00796)\]\[[1902.10486](http://arxiv.org/abs/1902.10486)\]. Theoretical analyses in overparameterized linear models suggest that sequential rehearsal can yield superior generalization and less forgetting when tasks are dissimilar, compared to concurrent rehearsal \[[1612.00796](http://arxiv.org/abs/1612.00796)\]. This aligns with empirical observations where sequentially revisiting tasks leads to improved retention of task-specific knowledge \[[1612.00796](http://arxiv.org/abs/1612.00796)\]\[[1805.12369](http://arxiv.org/abs/1805.12369)\].

Hybrid approaches combining concurrent and sequential rehearsal have also been explored, leveraging concurrent training for similar tasks while sequentially revisiting less similar tasks to optimize the stability-plasticity trade-off \[[1801.01423](http://arxiv.org/abs/1801.01423)\]\[[https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/90e04f3ae23ca7df5f59b11453341e3db943b6f4](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/90e04f3ae23ca7df5f59b11453341e3db943b6f4)\]\[[1904.07734](http://arxiv.org/abs/1904.07734)\]. Such methods capitalize on the complementary benefits of both strategies, achieving better overall performance than either approach alone in deep neural networks \[[1904.07734](http://arxiv.org/abs/1904.07734)\]. Additionally, optimizing replay sample selection through metrics like maximal interference retrieval further enhances rehearsal efficacy by focusing on the most vulnerable samples \[[1908.04742](http://arxiv.org/abs/1908.04742)\].

Other prominent strategies to address catastrophic forgetting eschew explicit rehearsal in favor of regularization techniques that constrain updates to parameters critical for previous tasks. Elastic Weight Consolidation (EWC) and Synaptic Intelligence (SI) exemplify this approach by selectively slowing learning on important weights, inspired by synaptic consolidation in neuroscience \[[1612.00796](http://arxiv.org/abs/1612.00796)\]\[[https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/a99d857ecc78316a0d9a774972b775058d5644ca](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/a99d857ecc78316a0d9a774972b775058d5644ca)\]\[[1711.09601](http://arxiv.org/abs/1711.09601)\]. While effective, these methods often require careful hyperparameter tuning and may underperform compared to rehearsal-based methods when task boundaries are unclear or task similarity varies \[[1904.07734](http://arxiv.org/abs/1904.07734)\]\[[1708.02072](http://arxiv.org/abs/1708.02072)\].

Complementary to regularization and rehearsal, architectural methods such as Progressive Neural Networks and task-conditioned hypernetworks mitigate forgetting by dynamically expanding model capacity or generating task-specific weights, thus isolating task knowledge \[[1906.00695](http://arxiv.org/abs/1906.00695)\]\[[1606.04671](http://arxiv.org/abs/1606.04671)\]. These methods excel in preventing interference but can incur increased computational or memory costs.

Recent advances also include meta-learning frameworks that learn representations robust to forgetting and optimize the alignment of gradients to maximize transfer and minimize interference during continual learning \[[1905.12588](http://arxiv.org/abs/1905.12588)\]\[[1810.11910](http://arxiv.org/abs/1810.11910)\]. These approaches can be combined with rehearsal buffers to further improve performance in non-stationary environments.

Empirical studies emphasize that the effectiveness of various strategies depends heavily on the learning scenario, such as whether task identity is known at test time and the similarity between tasks \[[1904.07734](http://arxiv.org/abs/1904.07734)\]\[[1708.02072](http://arxiv.org/abs/1708.02072)\]. Rehearsal-based methods, especially those employing sophisticated sample management or hybrid training protocols, consistently provide strong baselines and state-of-the-art results across diverse benchmarks \[[1902.10486](http://arxiv.org/abs/1902.10486)\]\[[1908.04742](http://arxiv.org/abs/1908.04742)\]\[[1810.12488](http://arxiv.org/abs/1810.12488)\].

In summary, while concurrent rehearsal remains a widely used and effective approach, emerging theoretical and empirical evidence highlights the benefits of sequential and hybrid rehearsal strategies, particularly when tasks exhibit low similarity. Integrating insights from neuroscience-inspired mechanisms, generative replay, meta-learning, and architectural innovations continues to advance the field towards robust continual learning systems capable of mitigating catastrophic forgetting.
