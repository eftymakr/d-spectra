## Related Works

The complexity of testing transactional isolation in databases has been a significant research focus, especially given the trade-offs between strong isolation guarantees and system performance. It is well-established that verifying strong isolation levels such as serializability is NP-complete \[[1908.04509](http://arxiv.org/abs/1908.04509)\], motivating a shift toward weak isolation levels that offer more tractable verification while maintaining practical consistency guarantees \[[1302.0309](http://arxiv.org/abs/1302.0309)\]\[[1908.04509](http://arxiv.org/abs/1908.04509)\].

Early work characterized many weak isolation levels—such as Read Committed (RC), Read Atomic (RA), and Causal Consistency (CC)—and demonstrated that their verification can be achieved in polynomial time, contrasting with the NP-completeness results for snapshot isolation and serializability \[[1908.04509](http://arxiv.org/abs/1908.04509)\]. Despite these theoretical advances, existing testers often exhibit high polynomial complexity, hampering scalability to large, realistic workloads \[[https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/04178561c9d8cd42904ed0a836e08e55b84703cf](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/04178561c9d8cd42904ed0a836e08e55b84703cf)\]\[[https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/cbe191bda20907994d45bc835d20446968370add](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/cbe191bda20907994d45bc835d20446968370add)\].

Several black-box testing frameworks have been developed to detect isolation anomalies in production databases. Elle introduced an Adya-style dependency graph inference approach, capable of detecting a wide range of anomalies for diverse isolation levels but excluding predicates, which limits applicability in some workloads \[[2003.10554](http://arxiv.org/abs/2003.10554)\]. PolySI and Viper represent state-of-the-art black-box checkers for snapshot isolation, focusing on efficiency by novel graph representations and SMT solver optimizations, yet their focus is on snapshot isolation rather than weaker levels \[[2301.07313](http://arxiv.org/abs/2301.07313)\]\[[https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/9f96a48d2bc0e77004b32960495685be6c301c63](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/9f96a48d2bc0e77004b32960495685be6c301c63)\]. Plume extends black-box checking to weak isolation levels by leveraging modular anomalous pattern characterizations and advanced timestamp techniques, demonstrating improved accuracy and efficiency over prior tools \[[https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/cbe191bda20907994d45bc835d20446968370add](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/cbe191bda20907994d45bc835d20446968370add)\].

From a theoretical perspective, detailed studies have delineated the boundaries of testability for various isolation levels. For instance, read committed, read atomic, and causal consistency have been shown to admit polynomial-time verification algorithms, while stronger levels like snapshot isolation and serializability remain NP-hard to test \[[1908.04509](http://arxiv.org/abs/1908.04509)\]\[[https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/4f3081dbb68287f0d9d7bec3e7173e6c8ff035b9](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/4f3081dbb68287f0d9d7bec3e7173e6c8ff035b9)\]. Notably, recent work has developed polynomial-time algorithms for testing RC and RA with complexities around \(O(n^{3/2})\), and linear or near-linear algorithms for CC, emphasizing both efficiency and provable optimality \[[1908.04509](http://arxiv.org/abs/1908.04509)\]. These results have driven the design of highly efficient testers optimized for large histories and multiple concurrent sessions.

Complementing algorithmic developments, methods for improving the efficiency of isolation checking include leveraging version certificate recovery and expected serialization orders to handle predicate operations and accelerate checking \[[https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/04178561c9d8cd42904ed0a836e08e55b84703cf](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/04178561c9d8cd42904ed0a836e08e55b84703cf)\]. These techniques enhance the precision and runtime of checkers, reducing false positives and enabling detection of subtle anomalies missed by earlier tools \[[https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/04178561c9d8cd42904ed0a836e08e55b84703cf](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/04178561c9d8cd42904ed0a836e08e55b84703cf)\]\[[2003.10554](http://arxiv.org/abs/2003.10554)\]. Additionally, frameworks like IsoVista provide comprehensive black-box verification with superior efficiency and user-friendly interfaces for understanding and profiling isolation violations \[[https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/4f3081dbb68287f0d9d7bec3e7173e6c8ff035b9](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/4f3081dbb68287f0d9d7bec3e7173e6c8ff035b9)\].

Static analysis approaches have also contributed by encoding serializability and causal consistency checks into SMT solvers, enabling bounded and unbounded anomaly detection and providing concrete counterexamples to aid debugging \[[1806.08416](http://arxiv.org/abs/1806.08416)\]\[[https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/322ca3c1911230a84aab81b0f5ff8c2b543c56f7](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/322ca3c1911230a84aab81b0f5ff8c2b543c56f7)\]. These methods, while more computationally intensive, offer complementary guarantees and insights into transactional correctness under weak consistency.

The literature further addresses the challenges posed by weak isolation in application-level correctness. Tools like MonkeyDB and IsoDiff focus on stress-testing and debugging application anomalies introduced by weak isolation, employing systematic exploration of execution histories and anomaly patterns to facilitate developer understanding and mitigation \[[https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/615ff2896b52bee2cbfd64ced324106a29b73397](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/615ff2896b52bee2cbfd64ced324106a29b73397)\]\[[https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/405ae0ebf74cfbf5f2edec62e2966827dcdc9bef](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/405ae0ebf74cfbf5f2edec62e2966827dcdc9bef)\].

In summary, the field has seen substantial progress in understanding the complexity of isolation testing and developing practical black-box checkers for weak isolation levels. However, scalability to large, real-world workloads remains a challenge due to the polynomial complexity of existing algorithms. The present work builds on these foundations by offering a provably optimal, highly efficient tester for common weak isolation levels that significantly outperforms prior tools in both theoretical complexity and empirical runtime, addressing a crucial gap in scalable, precise isolation verification \[[1908.04509](http://arxiv.org/abs/1908.04509)\].
