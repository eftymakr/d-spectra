## Related Works

Vision Transformers (ViTs) have revolutionized computer vision by effectively capturing global context through self-attention mechanisms, outperforming traditional convolutional neural networks (CNNs) in many tasks \[[2010.11929](http://arxiv.org/abs/2010.11929)\]. The original ViT architecture demonstrates that pure transformer models can achieve competitive image classification results by operating directly on sequences of image patches without convolutional inductive biases \[[2010.11929](http://arxiv.org/abs/2010.11929)\]. However, their adoption in hardware acceleration, particularly on FPGAs, faces challenges due to the complexity of operations like Softmax and LayerNorm, which are sensitive to quantization and difficult to implement efficiently in low precision \[[2208.05163](http://arxiv.org/abs/2208.05163)\]\[[2210.09573](http://arxiv.org/abs/2210.09573)\].

Several algorithmic and architectural advances aim to improve ViT efficiency and hardware friendliness. Dynamic token sparsification methods such as DynamicViT progressively prune redundant tokens based on their importance scores, reducing FLOPs by up to 37% and improving throughput by over 40% with minimal accuracy loss \[[2106.02034](http://arxiv.org/abs/2106.02034)\]. Similarly, HeatViT proposes hardware-aware, adaptive token pruning combined with 8-bit quantization and polynomial approximations of nonlinear functions to reduce computation by up to 65% and achieve up to 4.9× speedup on FPGA platforms with trivial resource overheads \[[2211.0811](http://arxiv.org/abs/2211.08110)\]. These approaches highlight the importance of token-level sparsity and quantization for hardware efficiency.

Quantization of ViTs has been extensively studied to address the challenges posed by complex operators. FQ-ViT introduces post-training quantization methods that maintain accuracy within 1% degradation even for fully quantized models, using techniques such as the Power-of-Two Factor and Log-Int-Softmax to handle LayerNorm and attention maps efficiently, enabling 4-bit quantization with low inference complexity \[[2111.13824](http://arxiv.org/abs/2111.13824)\]. VAQF presents a fully automatic software-hardware co-design framework that jointly optimizes quantization precision and accelerator parameters on FPGA platforms, achieving real-time execution of ViT models at 6-8 bit activations with minimal accuracy loss \[[2201.06618](http://arxiv.org/abs/2201.06618)\]. Auto-ViT-Acc further explores mixed-scheme quantization on FPGA, demonstrating significant frame rate improvements with less than 1% accuracy drop compared to 32-bit floating-point accelerators \[[2208.05163](http://arxiv.org/abs/2208.05163)\].

Beyond quantization and pruning, architectural modifications have been proposed to better balance accuracy and efficiency. Models like Tokens-to-Token ViT (T2T-ViT) improve tokenization by recursively aggregating neighboring tokens to better capture local structures, reducing model complexity and improving accuracy when trained from scratch \[[2101.11986](http://arxiv.org/abs/2101.11986)\]. CvT introduces convolutions into the transformer backbone to bring shift and scale invariance, achieving state-of-the-art performance with fewer parameters and FLOPs than vanilla ViTs \[[2103.15808](http://arxiv.org/abs/2103.15808)\]. LeViT combines convolutional principles with transformers to optimize speed-accuracy trade-offs and significantly accelerate inference on CPUs \[[2104.01136](http://arxiv.org/abs/2104.01136)\]. Pyramid Vision Transformer (PVT) adapts spatial dimension reduction principles from CNNs to transformers, enabling efficient and high-resolution outputs suitable for dense prediction tasks \[[2102.12122](http://arxiv.org/abs/2102.12122)\]. CrossViT employs multi-scale tokens and cross-attention for better feature representation while maintaining computational efficiency \[[2103.14899](http://arxiv.org/abs/2103.14899)\]. Transformer in Transformer (TNT) enhances feature granularity by applying self-attention at both local patch and sub-patch levels, improving representation with marginal computational overhead \[[2103.00112](http://arxiv.org/abs/2103.00112)\].

Efforts to accelerate ViTs also focus on pruning and sparsity in the attention mechanism. ViTCoD proposes a co-design framework that prunes attention maps with fixed sparse patterns and leverages learnable auto-encoders to reduce expensive data movements, achieving speedups up to 235× over CPUs and significant gains over prior transformer accelerators \[[2210.09573](http://arxiv.org/abs/2210.09573)\]. Patch slimming methods remove less impactful patches layer-by-layer, reducing over 45% FLOPs with minimal accuracy drop \[[2106.02852](http://arxiv.org/abs/2106.02852)\]. End-to-end sparsity exploration integrates dynamic data and architecture sparsity to train sparse subnetworks that maintain or even improve accuracy while significantly reducing computational cost \[[2106.04533](http://arxiv.org/abs/2106.04533)\].

Unified compression frameworks combine pruning, layer skipping, and knowledge distillation to reduce ViT complexity in an end-to-end optimization manner, achieving substantial FLOPs reduction without accuracy loss on popular backbones like DeiT and T2T-ViT \[[2203.08243](http://arxiv.org/abs/2203.08243)\].

Despite these advances, existing hardware acceleration methods generally focus on optimizing matrix multiplications and rely on CPUs for complex operations such as Softmax and LayerNorm, incurring communication overhead and limiting throughput \[[2208.05163](http://arxiv.org/abs/2208.05163)\]\[[2210.09573](http://arxiv.org/abs/2210.09573)\]\[[2211.0811](http://arxiv.org/abs/2211.08110)\]. Hardware-friendly alternatives to Softmax, such as ConSmax and Softermax, propose algorithmic replacements that facilitate parallelization and low-precision computation but have not been widely adopted in ViT accelerators \[[2103.15808](http://arxiv.org/abs/2103.15808)\]\[[2104.01136](http://arxiv.org/abs/2104.01136)\].

In contrast, the presented work uniquely addresses the full hardware mapping of all ViT operations, including Softmax and LayerNorm, onto FPGA using a novel Microscaling Integer (MXInt) arithmetic format. This approach enables high area efficiency and accuracy preservation while achieving significant speedups over floating-point and prior quantized designs, representing a substantial step forward in ViT hardware acceleration.
