## Related Works

Dense retrieval models have emerged as a pivotal approach in information retrieval, leveraging large pre-trained language models (PLMs) such as BERT and LLaMA to encode queries and documents into dense vector representations that facilitate efficient similarity search via simple operations like inner products \[[2108.05540](http://arxiv.org/abs/2108.05540)\]. The foundational architectures typically involve dual-encoders trained with contrastive learning objectives to distinguish relevant from irrelevant text passages \[[2004.04906](http://arxiv.org/abs/2004.04906)\]. However, the training of dense retrievers is challenging due to issues such as sensitivity to noisy data and the need for large batch sizes to learn robust embedding spaces \[[2108.05540](http://arxiv.org/abs/2108.05540)\]\[[2007.00808](http://arxiv.org/abs/2007.00808)\].

Early dense retrieval frameworks like Dense Passage Retrieval (DPR) demonstrated strong retrieval effectiveness on open-domain QA tasks by fine-tuning BERT-based encoders with supervised contrastive learning \[[2004.04906](http://arxiv.org/abs/2004.04906)\]. Subsequent work, such as RocketQA, improved training efficiency and robustness by introducing optimized negative sampling strategies including cross-batch negatives and data augmentation, resulting in substantially better retrieval accuracy \[[2010.08191](http://arxiv.org/abs/2010.08191)\]. ANCE further contributed by dynamically constructing hard negatives through an approximate nearest neighbor index that evolves alongside training, addressing the discrepancy between training and inference distributions \[[2007.00808](http://arxiv.org/abs/2007.00808)\].

Recent advances have focused on improving pre-training methods tailored for dense retrieval. The Condenser architecture introduces a novel pre-training objective that conditions language model predictions on dense representations, effectively bridging the gap between standard masked language modeling and dense retrieval requirements \[[2104.08253](http://arxiv.org/abs/2104.08253)\]. Building upon this, coCondenser integrates unsupervised corpus-level contrastive loss to enhance passage embedding quality without relying on heavy data engineering or large batch sizes, achieving performance comparable to heavily engineered systems like RocketQA \[[2108.05540](http://arxiv.org/abs/2108.05540)\]. Other works emphasize the benefits of domain and task adaptive pre-training, showing that continuing pre-training on in-domain or task-specific corpora leads to significant performance gains in retrieval tasks \[[2107.13602](http://arxiv.org/abs/2107.13602)\]\[[2004.10964](http://arxiv.org/abs/2004.10964)\].

The role of fine-tuning relative to pre-training in dense retrieval has recently become a subject of scrutiny. Studies have argued that the bulk of retrieval knowledge is acquired during pre-training, and fine-tuning primarily adjusts neuron activations rather than fundamentally reorganizing or adding new knowledge \[[2104.08253](http://arxiv.org/abs/2104.08253)\]. This phenomenon was initially observed in BERT-based DPR models but has been less explored in other architectures and representation methods. Models employing mean pooling (e.g., Contriever) or decoder-only backbones like LLaMA exhibit different behaviors, with fine-tuning playing a more significant role in shaping retrieval effectiveness \[[2112.09118](http://arxiv.org/abs/2112.09118)\]. This suggests that the interplay between pre-training and fine-tuning is architecture- and representation-dependent.

Contrastive learning remains a central mechanism in both pre-training and fine-tuning dense retrievers. Approaches like SimCSE demonstrate that simple contrastive objectives with minimal data augmentation can yield strong and uniform sentence embeddings, improving downstream task performance \[[2104.08821](http://arxiv.org/abs/2104.08821)\]. Similarly, DeCLUTR applies deep contrastive learning unsupervisedly to enhance textual representations, closing the gap with supervised methods \[[2006.03659](http://arxiv.org/abs/2006.03659)\]. These findings support the utility of contrastive objectives in pre-training dense retrieval models, complementing recent advances in autoencoder-based designs that use weak decoders to avoid shortcut learning and encourage better encoder representations \[[https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/5be217c678916543884c354654263f27c0a6bd9f](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/5be217c678916543884c354654263f27c0a6bd9f)\]\[[2204.10641](http://arxiv.org/abs/2204.10641)\].

The effectiveness of negative sampling strategies is also well studied. Static hard negative sampling can cause instability in training, motivating algorithms like STAR and ADORE that introduce randomness or dynamically update negatives to stabilize and directly optimize ranking performance \[[2104.08051](http://arxiv.org/abs/2104.08051)\]. Knowledge distillation methods further enhance dense retrievers by transferring knowledge from complex cross-encoder teachers to efficient bi-encoder students, often utilizing in-batch negatives to improve representation learning \[[https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/2c74e7258e7c89ca2da1784fb0cb4106a23ac382](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/2c74e7258e7c89ca2da1784fb0cb4106a23ac382)\].

Lastly, retrieval-augmented language models such as REALM and RAG integrate retrieval components into the pre-training or fine-tuning processes, enabling models to access explicit non-parametric knowledge sources during inference and improving performance on knowledge-intensive tasks \[[2002.08909](http://arxiv.org/abs/2002.08909)\]\[[2005.11401](http://arxiv.org/abs/2005.11401)\]. These frameworks highlight the importance of modular knowledge retrieval in complementing PLM-based dense representations.

In summary, the literature underscores that while pre-training establishes a foundational retrieval knowledge base, fine-tuning’s impact varies with model architecture and representation strategy. Advances in pre-training objectives, contrastive learning, negative sampling, and knowledge distillation continue to shape the development of more robust and efficient dense retrievers.
