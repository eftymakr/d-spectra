## Related Works

Single-pixel imaging (SPI) inverse problems have been effectively addressed by two prominent classes of methods: deep-unrolling and plug-and-play (PnP) approaches. PnP methods incorporate off-the-shelf deep denoisers as implicit regularizers within iterative algorithms, offering flexibility in handling varying compression ratios (CRs) without retraining. However, this flexibility often comes at the expense of reconstruction accuracy and computational speed \[[1902.05399](http://arxiv.org/abs/1902.05399)\]\[[1809.04693](http://arxiv.org/abs/1809.04693)\]\[[1704.03264](http://arxiv.org/abs/1704.03264)\]. Conversely, deep-unrolling methods transform a truncated iterative optimization process into an end-to-end trainable network, achieving superior accuracy and faster inference but typically require fine-tuning or retraining when the CR changes, limiting their adaptability \[[1902.05399](http://arxiv.org/abs/1902.05399)\]\[[1705.08041](http://arxiv.org/abs/1705.08041)\]\[[https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/2cdc9bbde0f847094582b212f980fa4dbc48950d](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/2cdc9bbde0f847094582b212f980fa4dbc48950d)\].

Algorithm unrolling, which connects iterative optimization algorithms and neural network architectures, has gained significant interest for imaging inverse problems. This approach provides interpretable, efficient networks by unfolding classical iterative methods such as half-quadratic splitting (HQS) or alternating direction method of multipliers (ADMM) into a finite number of trainable layers \[[1912.10557](http://arxiv.org/abs/1912.10557)\]\[[1902.05399](http://arxiv.org/abs/1902.05399)\]\[[https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/b954a94af132294a4a4e2efce15027758a535742](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/b954a94af132294a4a4e2efce15027758a535742)\]. For instance, unrolling has been successfully applied to blind image deblurring \[[1902.05399](http://arxiv.org/abs/1902.05399)\]\[[https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/b954a94af132294a4a4e2efce15027758a535742](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/b954a94af132294a4a4e2efce15027758a535742)\], super-resolution \[[2003.10428](http://arxiv.org/abs/2003.10428)\]\[[https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/27fc88ebb3e325a062a6ff48a7a76611af1ecd5a](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/27fc88ebb3e325a062a6ff48a7a76611af1ecd5a)\], and compressive sensing \[[https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/2cdc9bbde0f847094582b212f980fa4dbc48950d](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/2cdc9bbde0f847094582b212f980fa4dbc48950d)\]\[[1704.06625](http://arxiv.org/abs/1704.06625)\], demonstrating improved convergence speed and accuracy compared to traditional iterative methods.

PnP frameworks leverage the modular nature of proximal algorithms by substituting the proximal operator with a learned denoiser, enabling flexibility to different forward models and noise levels without retraining \[[1704.03488](http://arxiv.org/abs/1704.03488)\]\[[1704.03264](http://arxiv.org/abs/1704.03264)\]\[[1809.04693](http://arxiv.org/abs/1809.04693)\]. Such methods have shown strong generalizability across inverse problems like image deconvolution and demosaicking while maintaining state-of-the-art performance \[[1704.03488](http://arxiv.org/abs/1704.03488)\]\[[1704.03264](http://arxiv.org/abs/1704.03264)\]. However, PnP methods often require many iterations to converge, making them slower than unrolled networks at inference time.

Several works have sought to bridge the gap between these paradigms. Unrolled networks with embedded denoiser priors combine the interpretability and efficiency of unrolling with the flexibility of learned priors \[[1705.08041](http://arxiv.org/abs/1705.08041)\]. For example, MoDL introduces a model-based deep learning framework that explicitly incorporates the forward model and uses CNN-based regularizers, achieving faster convergence and reduced training data requirements compared to direct inversion approaches \[[1712.02862](http://arxiv.org/abs/1712.02862)\]. Similarly, ISTA-Net and its variants adapt iterative shrinkage-thresholding algorithms into trainable networks with learned nonlinear transforms, improving both speed and accuracy for compressive sensing recovery \[[https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/2cdc9bbde0f847094582b212f980fa4dbc48950d](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/2cdc9bbde0f847094582b212f980fa4dbc48950d)\].

The proximal operator plays a crucial role in both model-based optimization and unrolling techniques. Recent studies have explored training deep neural networks to approximate proximal mappings, enabling learned iterative algorithms that are both interpretable and effective \[[1912.10557](http://arxiv.org/abs/1912.10557)\]\[[1705.08041](http://arxiv.org/abs/1705.08041)\]\[[https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/2cdc9bbde0f847094582b212f980fa4dbc48950d](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/2cdc9bbde0f847094582b212f980fa4dbc48950d)\]. The proposed concept of a proximal trajectory loss function aligns with this trend by encouraging the learned deep image restorer to approximate the proximal operator of an ideal explicit regularizer, thus combining explicit model knowledge with learned priors \[[1912.10557](http://arxiv.org/abs/1912.10557)\]\[[1705.08041](http://arxiv.org/abs/1705.08041)\].

In the context of image restoration beyond SPI, residual learning and batch normalization have been shown to enhance training efficiency and denoising performance, enabling single models to handle varying noise levels and tasks such as Gaussian denoising, super-resolution, and JPEG deblocking \[[1608.03981](http://arxiv.org/abs/1608.03981)\]\[[1502.03167](http://arxiv.org/abs/1502.03167)\]\[[1512.03385](http://arxiv.org/abs/1512.03385)\]. These insights have informed the design of efficient denoisers used within PnP and unrolling frameworks.

Furthermore, state-of-the-art deep networks for inverse problems often balance the trade-off between interpretability, flexibility, and computational efficiency. Learned Primal-Dual methods unroll primal-dual optimization algorithms with CNN-based proximal operators, achieving substantial performance gains in tomographic reconstruction without relying on initial reconstructions \[[1707.06474](http://arxiv.org/abs/1707.06474)\]. Similarly, LDAMP integrates denoising within approximate message passing algorithms to deliver fast and accurate compressive image recovery, illustrating the benefits of combining principled iterative algorithms with learned components \[[1704.06625](http://arxiv.org/abs/1704.06625)\]\[[1406.4175](http://arxiv.org/abs/1406.4175)\].

Overall, these advances demonstrate that integrating model-based insights with learned priors via unrolling or PnP approaches yields robust, interpretable, and efficient solutions for inverse imaging problems. The current work builds upon this foundation by proposing a unified proximal unrolling framework that flexibly adapts to varying CRs with a single model, enhancing both reconstruction accuracy and speed, thereby addressing the limitations of existing PnP and unrolling methods \[[1902.05399](http://arxiv.org/abs/1902.05399)\]\[[1912.10557](http://arxiv.org/abs/1912.10557)\]\[[1705.08041](http://arxiv.org/abs/1705.08041)\].
