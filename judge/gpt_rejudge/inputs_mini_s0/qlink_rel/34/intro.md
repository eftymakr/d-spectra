## Related Works

Fund allocation in financial domains often relies on accurate time series forecasting, but naive predict-then-optimize (PnO) approaches suffer from objective misalignment between forecasting accuracy and downstream allocation goals \[[1703.07015](http://arxiv.org/abs/1703.07015)\]. This goal mismatch is compounded by the uncertainty inherent in time series predictions from state-of-the-art models, which can degrade allocation performance if not properly accounted for \[[1912.09363](http://arxiv.org/abs/1912.09363)\]. Consequently, integrating forecasting and allocation into a unified framework with aligned objectives has become an active research area.

Traditional forecasting models such as autoregressive and Gaussian processes have limitations in capturing complex temporal dependencies, motivating the development of advanced deep learning architectures. Hybrid models combining convolutional and recurrent networks, like LSTNet, simultaneously capture short- and long-term temporal patterns, improving multivariate forecasting accuracy \[[1703.07015](http://arxiv.org/abs/1703.07015)\]. Recurrent neural networks have also been adapted for probabilistic multi-horizon forecasting, enabling robust uncertainty estimation useful for decision-making \[[1704.04110](http://arxiv.org/abs/1704.04110)\]\[[1711.11053](http://arxiv.org/abs/1711.11053)\]. More recent convolutional-interaction models like SCINet leverage downsampling properties of time series to enhance temporal representation and forecasting accuracy, often outperforming Transformer-based models across datasets \[[2106.09305](http://arxiv.org/abs/2106.09305)\].

Transformer architectures have gained substantial interest for long-term and multivariate time series forecasting due to their ability to model long-range dependencies and multivariate interactions \[[2202.07125](http://arxiv.org/abs/2202.07125)\]\[[2012.07436](http://arxiv.org/abs/2012.07436)\]. However, canonical Transformers face challenges such as quadratic time complexity, memory bottlenecks, and insensitivity to local temporal context, which can limit their effectiveness in time series domains \[[2205.13504](http://arxiv.org/abs/2205.13504)\]\[[1907.00235](http://arxiv.org/abs/1907.00235)\]. Specialized variants, including Informer and Autoformer, address these issues by introducing sparse attention mechanisms, generative decoders, and decomposition blocks to improve efficiency and accuracy for long sequence forecasting \[[2012.07436](http://arxiv.org/abs/2012.07436)\]\[[2106.13008](http://arxiv.org/abs/2106.13008)\]. Patch-based and channel-independent Transformer designs, as in PatchTST and iTransformer, further enhance scalability and variate-specific representation learning, leading to state-of-the-art forecasting performance \[[2211.14730](http://arxiv.org/abs/2211.14730)\]\[[2310.06625](http://arxiv.org/abs/2310.06625)\].

Uncertainty calibration remains critical for reliable forecasting in financial settings. Classical Bayesian and Monte Carlo dropout methods provide uncertainty estimates but can be computationally demanding and model-specific \[[1704.04110](http://arxiv.org/abs/1704.04110)\]\[[1905.10437](http://arxiv.org/abs/1905.10437)\]. Recent advancements in conformal prediction and adaptive calibration techniques offer more general and computationally efficient ways to quantify forecast uncertainty, essential for risk-aware allocation \[[1709.04875](http://arxiv.org/abs/1709.04875)\]. Furthermore, hybrid approaches that combine deep learning global models with local classical models balance data efficiency and uncertainty quantification, enabling improved probabilistic forecasting \[[1905.12417](http://arxiv.org/abs/1905.12417)\].

End-to-end training frameworks that directly align forecasting and optimization objectives have shown promise in mitigating the goal mismatch of traditional PnO methods \[[2205.13504](http://arxiv.org/abs/2205.13504)\]\[[2012.07436](http://arxiv.org/abs/2012.07436)\]. However, many existing approaches are tied to specific forecasting architectures, limiting their adaptability across diverse financial datasets or model types \[[2106.13008](http://arxiv.org/abs/2106.13008)\]. The emergence of foundation models and pretrained transformers adapted for time series analysis offers potential for model-agnostic frameworks that leverage generalized temporal and cross-variate patterns learned from large datasets \[[2302.11939](http://arxiv.org/abs/2302.11939)\]\[[2403.14735](http://arxiv.org/abs/2403.14735)\]. Such models can facilitate unified treatment of temporal forecasting and allocation, incorporating uncertainty calibration and objective alignment in a flexible manner.

In financial applications, deep learning models have been successfully applied to forecasting and allocation tasks, demonstrating improved performance over classical statistical methods \[[2004.13408](http://arxiv.org/abs/2004.13408)\]. Yet, integrating uncertainty-aware forecasting with adaptive allocation remains challenging, especially under varying data regimes and asset classes such as currency, stock, and cryptocurrency markets. The proposed RTS-PnO framework addresses these gaps by enabling end-to-end training with objective alignment, adaptive uncertainty calibration, and forecasting model agnosticism, thus advancing robust fund allocation strategies under uncertainty.

In summary, the literature spans advanced deep learning architectures for multivariate and long-term forecasting, uncertainty quantification techniques, and integrated optimization frameworks. The challenges of scalability, temporal locality, uncertainty calibration, and objective alignment motivate the development of flexible, risk-aware frameworks that generalize across forecasting models and financial domains \[[1703.07015](http://arxiv.org/abs/1703.07015)\]\[[1912.09363](http://arxiv.org/abs/1912.09363)\]\[[2205.13504](http://arxiv.org/abs/2205.13504)\]\[[2012.07436](http://arxiv.org/abs/2012.07436)\]\[[2106.13008](http://arxiv.org/abs/2106.13008)\]\[[2211.14730](http://arxiv.org/abs/2211.14730)\]\[[1704.04110](http://arxiv.org/abs/1704.04110)\]\[[2310.06625](http://arxiv.org/abs/2310.06625)\]\[[2302.11939](http://arxiv.org/abs/2302.11939)\]\[[2106.09305](http://arxiv.org/abs/2106.09305)\]\[[1905.12417](http://arxiv.org/abs/1905.12417)\]\[[2403.14735](http://arxiv.org/abs/2403.14735)\].
