## Related Works

Sequential recommendation has witnessed significant progress through diverse modeling techniques, primarily centered on capturing temporal dynamics, user preferences, and interaction patterns. Traditional approaches such as Markov Chains (MCs) and Recurrent Neural Networks (RNNs) laid the foundation for modeling sequential user behaviors, with MCs excelling in sparse data scenarios due to their parsimonious assumptions, and RNNs enabling the capture of longer-term dependencies in denser datasets \[[1808.09781](http://arxiv.org/abs/1808.09781)\]. However, these methods often struggle with efficiency and scalability for long sequences, motivating the adoption of attention-based models.

Self-attention mechanisms, initially popularized by Transformer architectures, have been increasingly applied to sequential recommendation due to their ability to directly model item relationships across varying sequence lengths without recurrent operations. For example, SASRec utilizes self-attention to balance the benefits of MCs and RNNs, enabling adaptive focus on relevant past interactions and outperforming many baseline models on both sparse and dense data \[[1808.09781](http://arxiv.org/abs/1808.09781)\]. Extensions such as TiSASRec incorporate time interval information between interactions into self-attention, improving temporal sensitivity and recommendation accuracy by modeling both absolute and relative time positions \[[https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/9c7a455b1e48d01a99e58e884a6c1acb75074ad0](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/9c7a455b1e48d01a99e58e884a6c1acb75074ad0)\]\[[https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/5574d6123deea94eba2cb35b2486851e826c16ce](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/5574d6123deea94eba2cb35b2486851e826c16ce)\]. BERT4Rec advances this line by employing bidirectional self-attention with a Cloze-style masking objective to capture richer contextual dependencies, overcoming limitations of unidirectional models \[[1904.06690](http://arxiv.org/abs/1904.06690)\].

Other architectures explore the integration of additional structural information. Convolutional models like Caser embed sequences as latent "images" and extract local sequential features via convolutional filters, effectively capturing both general user preferences and sequential patterns \[[1809.07426](http://arxiv.org/abs/1809.07426)\]. Deeper CNN-based generative models with dilated convolutions and residual blocks have also been proposed to efficiently model long-range dependencies without pooling, achieving competitive accuracy and faster training \[[1808.05163](http://arxiv.org/abs/1808.05163)\]. Meanwhile, hierarchical attention networks have been designed to separately model long-term and short-term user preferences, dynamically combining them for enhanced personalization \[[https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/0b42168f22713c48d8b0ce3bc8f680d347184d88](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/0b42168f22713c48d8b0ce3bc8f680d347184d88)\]. Graph Neural Networks (GNNs) extend the modeling of session-based recommendations by representing sessions as graphs to capture complex item transitions beyond simple sequences \[[1811.00855](http://arxiv.org/abs/1811.00855)\].

Addressing challenges from noisy and heterogeneous user behavior data, recent work introduced filter-enhanced multilayer perceptrons (MLPs) with learnable frequency-domain filtering to attenuate noise and improve generalization, outperforming traditional RNN, CNN, and Transformer baselines while maintaining lower computational complexity \[[2202.13556](http://arxiv.org/abs/2202.13556)\]. Moreover, feature-level self-attention networks incorporate item feature sequences alongside item sequences to capture richer transition patterns at the attribute level, leading to improved recommendations \[[https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/63555b4f363ee72dd8d49d8deca68760542c0cd2](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/63555b4f363ee72dd8d49d8deca68760542c0cd2)\]. Self-supervised learning frameworks leverage mutual information maximization among item, attribute, subsequence, and sequence data to mitigate data sparsity and strengthen representation learning \[[2008.07873](http://arxiv.org/abs/2008.07873)\].

Temporal dynamics remain a critical focus in sequential recommendation. Beyond simply modeling item order, approaches such as Contextualized Temporal Attention dynamically weigh historical behaviors by considering both temporal decay and contextual factors, allowing the model to adaptively emphasize influential past actions \[[2002.00741](http://arxiv.org/abs/2002.00741)\]. Similarly, the joint modeling of absolute and relative temporal patterns has been shown to enhance the understanding of user time-sensitive behaviors \[[https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/5574d6123deea94eba2cb35b2486851e826c16ce](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/5574d6123deea94eba2cb35b2486851e826c16ce)\].

To handle diverse user behavior patterns and the variability in sequence lengths, mixture-of-experts (MoE) architectures have been explored. These models route different types of user behaviors to specialized expert networks, effectively capturing heterogeneous browsing modes such as focused category-specific exploration and broad category exploration. Hierarchical and multi-modal MoE frameworks further demonstrate the benefits of adaptive expert routing for complex user preferences and multi-domain recommendation tasks \[[https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/0b42168f22713c48d8b0ce3bc8f680d347184d88](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/0b42168f22713c48d8b0ce3bc8f680d347184d88)\]\[[1811.00855](http://arxiv.org/abs/1811.00855)\]\[[2002.00741](http://arxiv.org/abs/2002.00741)\]. This adaptive routing aligns with the need for models that can efficiently process sequences of varying lengths while maintaining high recommendation accuracy.

In session-based recommendation scenarios, models like NARM and STAMP combine attention mechanisms with memory components to jointly model users’ sequential behaviors and current session intents, addressing the limitations of purely long-term or short-term modeling \[[1711.04725](http://arxiv.org/abs/1711.04725)\]\[[https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/fd3f489fc0438e500c0473af40dfebe4705df6d2](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/fd3f489fc0438e500c0473af40dfebe4705df6d2)\]. RNN-based session models have shown that sequence modeling of short sessions can outperform traditional item-to-item similarity methods, especially when enhanced with ranking loss functions to better fit recommendation objectives \[[1511.06939](http://arxiv.org/abs/1511.06939)\].

Overall, the evolution of sequential recommendation methods reflects a trend toward integrating multiple modeling paradigms—attention mechanisms, temporal modeling, state-space representations, convolutional and graph-based structures, and mixture-of-experts—to effectively capture the complex, dynamic, and heterogeneous nature of user behaviors. STAR-Rec’s combination of preference-aware attention with state-space modeling within a sequence-level MoE framework builds upon and extends these advancements by unifying temporal dynamics and diverse interaction patterns into a scalable and adaptable architecture \[[1808.09781](http://arxiv.org/abs/1808.09781)\]\[[https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/9c7a455b1e48d01a99e58e884a6c1acb75074ad0](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/9c7a455b1e48d01a99e58e884a6c1acb75074ad0)\]\[[2202.13556](http://arxiv.org/abs/2202.13556)\]\[[https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/0b42168f22713c48d8b0ce3bc8f680d347184d88](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/0b42168f22713c48d8b0ce3bc8f680d347184d88)\]\[[2002.00741](http://arxiv.org/abs/2002.00741)\].
