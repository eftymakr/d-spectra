## Related Works

Differential privacy (DP) has been established as the standard for privacy-preserving data analysis, enabling statistical queries and machine learning on sensitive datasets while providing formal privacy guarantees \[[https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/e4ce10063cd25447dcde75c2d9ce327446ced952](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/e4ce10063cd25447dcde75c2d9ce327446ced952)\]\[[https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/0023582fde36430c7e3ae81611a14e558c8f4bae](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/0023582fde36430c7e3ae81611a14e558c8f4bae)\]. The classical central DP model assumes a trusted server that aggregates data and adds calibrated noise proportional to the sensitivity of queries to mask individual contributions \[[https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/e4ce10063cd25447dcde75c2d9ce327446ced952](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/e4ce10063cd25447dcde75c2d9ce327446ced952)\]. However, this trust assumption is often unrealistic, motivating alternative models such as local differential privacy (LDP), where each user perturbs their data before transmission, thereby eliminating the need for a trusted aggregator \[[1606.05053](http://arxiv.org/abs/1606.05053)\]\[[https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/770ad70607ce60d8c411916129a423b5aad45e93](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/770ad70607ce60d8c411916129a423b5aad45e93)\]. This model has seen practical adoption in major industrial deployments, including telemetry collection and frequency estimation tasks \[[1606.05053](http://arxiv.org/abs/1606.05053)\]\[[1712.01524](http://arxiv.org/abs/1712.01524)\]\[[1907.00782](http://arxiv.org/abs/1907.00782)\].

Despite these advances, both central and local DP mechanisms are vulnerable to adversarial manipulation, especially in distributed settings where untrusted servers may deviate from protocols by sampling noise incorrectly or generating correlated noise, thus undermining privacy guarantees \[[1909.09630](http://arxiv.org/abs/1909.09630)\]\[[1911.02046](http://arxiv.org/abs/1911.02046)\]. Local DP protocols are particularly susceptible to manipulation attacks; small fractions of malicious users can substantially skew aggregated statistics, obscuring true data distributions \[[1909.09630](http://arxiv.org/abs/1909.09630)\]\[[1911.02046](http://arxiv.org/abs/1911.02046)\]. These vulnerabilities underscore the need for mechanisms that provide verifiability to ensure faithful execution of DP protocols.

To address verification, prior work has introduced the concept of verifiable differential privacy (VDP), which enhances traditional DP by enabling verification that the noise addition and protocol steps were performed correctly, often leveraging cryptographic tools such as zero-knowledge proofs (ZKPs) \[[1909.09630](http://arxiv.org/abs/1909.09630)\]\[[https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/d762c3b48d3949dc2cd803d955c3c983e7aa16ba](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/d762c3b48d3949dc2cd803d955c3c983e7aa16ba)\]. ZKPs have been shown to be sufficient for verifying DP compliance without revealing sensitive information, enabling trust in the correctness of outputs despite untrusted servers \[[1909.09630](http://arxiv.org/abs/1909.09630)\]\[[1606.05053](http://arxiv.org/abs/1606.05053)\]\[[1905.08320](http://arxiv.org/abs/1905.08320)\]\[[1808.01394](http://arxiv.org/abs/1808.01394)\]. However, ZKPs can incur significant computational and communication overhead, limiting their practicality in large-scale or resource-constrained environments \[[2104.06569](http://arxiv.org/abs/2104.06569)\]\[[https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/25b7fe2ac81189952c72c5b869a6be1c5fcf410c](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/25b7fe2ac81189952c72c5b869a6be1c5fcf410c)\].

Beyond zero-knowledge proofs, other cryptographic approaches such as secure multi-party computation (MPC) and homomorphic encryption have been explored to achieve verifiable DP in distributed systems \[[https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/ac8be9c934d6ff9ae095ebde81c8d3a57f7b918d](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/ac8be9c934d6ff9ae095ebde81c8d3a57f7b918d)\]\[[1712.01524](http://arxiv.org/abs/1712.01524)\]\[[https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/3f70dc09f979b102c010d4bb500ceb09f18e3b17](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/3f70dc09f979b102c010d4bb500ceb09f18e3b17)\]. MPC-based frameworks enable multiple non-colluding parties to jointly compute DP mechanisms over encrypted data without revealing inputs, thus providing both privacy and verifiability \[[https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/ac8be9c934d6ff9ae095ebde81c8d3a57f7b918d](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/ac8be9c934d6ff9ae095ebde81c8d3a57f7b918d)\]\[[1712.01524](http://arxiv.org/abs/1712.01524)\]. These systems achieve the “best of both worlds” by combining the accuracy of the central model with the trustlessness of the local model, though often at the cost of complexity and scalability \[[https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/ac8be9c934d6ff9ae095ebde81c8d3a57f7b918d](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/ac8be9c934d6ff9ae095ebde81c8d3a57f7b918d)\].

Recent research has also focused on enhancing the efficiency and usability of verifiable DP mechanisms. For example, improvements in verifiable randomized response (VRR) protocols under local DP have reduced communication costs and verifier overhead by orders of magnitude while maintaining rigorous privacy guarantees \[[2104.06569](http://arxiv.org/abs/2104.06569)\]\[[2005.11753](http://arxiv.org/abs/2005.11753)\]\[[https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/770ad70607ce60d8c411916129a423b5aad45e93](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/770ad70607ce60d8c411916129a423b5aad45e93)\]. Similarly, advances in distributed noise generation protocols have enabled scalable and robust DP implementations that resist manipulation without sacrificing utility \[[https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/3f70dc09f979b102c010d4bb500ceb09f18e3b17](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/3f70dc09f979b102c010d4bb500ceb09f18e3b17)\].

Additionally, the shuffled model of DP, which introduces an anonymity layer via random permutation of users’ messages before aggregation, provides a middle ground between local and central models, amplifying privacy guarantees and enabling more accurate statistics without requiring full trust in the server \[[1811.12469](http://arxiv.org/abs/1811.12469)\]\[[1808.01394](http://arxiv.org/abs/1808.01394)\]. This model demonstrates that anonymity can reduce the amount of noise needed and improve privacy-utility trade-offs.

The work presented in this paper builds upon these foundations by formalizing Verifiable Distributed Differential Privacy (VDDP), a framework that incorporates verification mechanisms into distributed DP protocols to detect and prevent malicious server behaviors such as incorrect noise sampling or correlated noise generation. Unlike prior approaches relying solely on ZKPs, the proposed definition and mechanisms demonstrate that ZKPs, while sufficient, are not strictly necessary for achieving verifiable DP. The proposed Verifiable Distributed Discrete Laplacian Mechanism (VDDLM) and improved VRR solutions significantly improve proof generation efficiency and reduce communication overhead compared to existing verifiable DP methods, enabling practical deployment in large-scale distributed systems.

By integrating insights from cryptographic verification, local and central DP models, and recent advances in distributed noise generation and manipulation resistance, this work advances the state of the art in trustworthy and efficient privacy-preserving data analysis under adversarial threats \[[1909.09630](http://arxiv.org/abs/1909.09630)\]\[[2104.06569](http://arxiv.org/abs/2104.06569)\]\[[2005.11753](http://arxiv.org/abs/2005.11753)\]\[[https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/ac8be9c934d6ff9ae095ebde81c8d3a57f7b918d](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/ac8be9c934d6ff9ae095ebde81c8d3a57f7b918d)\]\[[https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/d762c3b48d3949dc2cd803d955c3c983e7aa16ba](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/d762c3b48d3949dc2cd803d955c3c983e7aa16ba)\].
