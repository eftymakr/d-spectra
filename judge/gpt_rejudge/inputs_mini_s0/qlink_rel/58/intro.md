## Related Works

Table discovery is a fundamental task in data management, especially with the rapid growth of tabular data repositories. Traditional methods often rely on keyword queries or query tables to retrieve relevant tables, which typically results in large, imprecise result sets requiring extensive manual filtering. This challenge motivates the introduction of more advanced paradigms that incorporate natural language (NL) conditions to refine search results, as exemplified by the proposed NL-conditional table discovery (nlcTD) task \[[1802.06159](http://arxiv.org/abs/1802.06159)\].

A significant body of work has explored semantic matching between queries and tables to improve retrieval effectiveness. For example, models that represent both queries and tables in multiple semantic spaces—including sparse discrete and dense continuous embeddings—and use various similarity measures have demonstrated substantial improvements over baseline retrieval methods \[[1802.06159](http://arxiv.org/abs/1802.06159)\]. This approach enables richer semantic alignment beyond keyword overlaps, crucial for handling complex NL conditions.

Pretrained language models tailored for joint understanding of text and tables have also advanced the field. TaBERT, a pretrained model trained on millions of tables and their textual contexts, learns unified representations of NL sentences and structured tabular data. It has shown superior performance on semantic parsing benchmarks involving table questions and text-to-SQL tasks, indicating the effectiveness of leveraging large-scale tabular corpora for downstream table-related tasks \[[2005.08314](http://arxiv.org/abs/2005.08314)\]. Such models provide a strong foundation for interpreting NL queries in conjunction with table structures, a necessary capability for NL-conditional retrieval.

Semantic parsing on semi-structured tables, focusing on compositional question answering, further illustrates the complexity of reasoning required over tabular data. Challenges include managing a broad domain with an open-ended set of relations and handling deep logical compositionality, which leads to a combinatorial explosion of potential query interpretations. Logical-form driven parsing algorithms that incorporate strong typing constraints have achieved notable gains on large datasets of complex questions over Wikipedia tables, highlighting the importance of structured semantic understanding in table-oriented tasks \[[1508.00305](http://arxiv.org/abs/1508.00305)\].

Despite these advances, existing datasets and benchmarks primarily focus on tasks such as table retrieval based on keywords, question answering over tables, or table-to-text generation. There remains a scarcity of comprehensive datasets that combine the retrieval of relevant tables with rich NL conditions specifying operations like unions, joins, or fuzzy matches, as required by the nlcTD scenario. The nlcTables dataset addresses this gap by providing a large-scale, diverse benchmark with explicit NL conditions paired with query tables, 22,080 candidate tables, and over 21,000 relevance annotations. This dataset enables systematic evaluation of NL-conditional table discovery methods and highlights significant performance gaps in current state-of-the-art approaches, underscoring the need for novel techniques tailored to this complex task.

In summary, prior work on semantic matching, pretrained joint text-table models, and compositional semantic parsing provides a strong foundation for NL-conditional table discovery but falls short of addressing the full complexity of combining NL constraints with table queries for refined retrieval. The nlcTD task and its associated dataset extend this landscape by emphasizing the integration of structured query tables and nuanced NL conditions, fostering further research in this challenging area \[[1802.06159](http://arxiv.org/abs/1802.06159)\]\[[2005.08314](http://arxiv.org/abs/2005.08314)\]\[[1508.00305](http://arxiv.org/abs/1508.00305)\].
