## Related Works

The accurate modeling of protein dynamics is fundamental to understanding biological functions, yet traditional molecular dynamics (MD) simulations remain computationally expensive and often infeasible for extensive conformational sampling \[[https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/2eb1ed9d4baf548391d8220c0a8a3d0be79845ed](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/2eb1ed9d4baf548391d8220c0a8a3d0be79845ed)\]. To overcome these limitations, recent work has increasingly focused on generative modeling approaches, particularly diffusion-based frameworks, that learn distributions over protein structures from experimental data and simulations, enabling efficient generation of protein conformational ensembles.

Diffusion models have been effectively applied to protein structure generation, leveraging stochastic processes that denoise random initial states into physically plausible conformations. EigenFold exemplifies this approach by modeling protein structures as harmonic oscillators, enabling a cascading-resolution generative process along eigenmodes and achieving high accuracy on benchmark datasets while capturing conformational heterogeneity \[[2304.02198](http://arxiv.org/abs/2304.02198)\]. Similarly, ConfDiff integrates physical priors by incorporating force guidance within SE(3)-equivariant diffusion models, enhancing fidelity and diversity of sampled conformations and outperforming prior state-of-the-art methods \[[2403.14088](http://arxiv.org/abs/2403.14088)\]. These studies underscore the importance of embedding physical knowledge to improve the realism of generated ensembles.

Further innovations include FrameDiff and FoldFlow, which formulate diffusion and flow-matching generative models directly on the SE(3) group of rigid body motions, allowing invariant and equivariant modeling of protein backbones without reliance on pretrained predictors. FrameDiff generates designable monomers up to 500 amino acids, while FoldFlow introduces simulation-free and Riemannian optimal transport-augmented dynamics that improve training stability and sample diversity for proteins up to 300 residues \[[2302.02277](http://arxiv.org/abs/2302.02277)\]\[[2310.02391](http://arxiv.org/abs/2310.02391)\]. These frameworks demonstrate enhanced control over protein backbone generation, crucial for structure-based design.

Complementing these approaches, models like AlphaFlow leverage flow-based generative techniques fine-tuned from high-accuracy predictors (AlphaFold, ESMFold) to sample conformational landscapes conditioned on sequence. AlphaFlow achieves superior precision and ensemble diversity compared to AlphaFold with MSA subsampling, and can approximate equilibrium ensemble properties faster than replicate MD simulations, highlighting its potential as a computationally efficient alternative to physics-based methods \[[2402.04845](http://arxiv.org/abs/2402.04845)\].

Other frameworks, such as BioEmu and Distributional Graphormer (DiG), focus on learning equilibrium distributions of protein conformations. BioEmu generates thousands of statistically independent samples per hour on a single GPU by combining extensive MD trajectories and experimental stability data, achieving ensemble accuracy validated against millisecond-scale MD simulations \[[https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/8fa4f6b5232868c2e400670716540f6877068439](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/8fa4f6b5232868c2e400670716540f6877068439)\]. DiG employs a graph transformer to transform simple distributions into equilibrium ensembles conditioned on molecular descriptors, facilitating diverse conformation generation and state density estimation orders of magnitude faster than traditional simulations \[[https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/1446b534858fc3fc6d9406920450b5c3ca87ee13](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/1446b534858fc3fc6d9406920450b5c3ca87ee13)\]\[[2306.05445](http://arxiv.org/abs/2306.05445)\].

Generative adversarial networks (GANs) have also been explored for direct ensemble generation of intrinsically disordered peptides, demonstrating transferability to unseen sequences and the capacity to scale from coarse-grained to atomistic resolutions without explicit sampling \[[https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/21a4afa74caf78e28880b7fe4c30a09a5f9c14e3](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/21a4afa74caf78e28880b7fe4c30a09a5f9c14e3)\]. This approach complements diffusion-based methods by offering alternative architectures for conformational ensemble prediction.

AlphaFold2, while originally developed for static structure prediction, has been adapted to sample alternative protein conformations by manipulating input multiple sequence alignments (MSAs) through stochastic subsampling or in silico mutagenesis, enabling prediction of multiple biologically relevant conformational states absent from training data \[[https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/ff9b84297f16760472c791456befb31ff1d224ef](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/ff9b84297f16760472c791456befb31ff1d224ef)\]\[[https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/4244065522c28c9a075d145ad1970a6fb7811f78](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/4244065522c28c9a075d145ad1970a6fb7811f78)\]. AF-Cluster further extends this by clustering MSAs to sample metamorphic protein states, validated experimentally, illustrating how evolutionary information can guide ensemble prediction within existing frameworks \[[https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/6d464c44cad5eb182ae580adfff8624562e457b8](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/6d464c44cad5eb182ae580adfff8624562e457b8)\].

Integrating physical supervision into generative models remains a critical challenge. Boltzmann generators address this by learning invertible neural transformations between complex protein configurations and simpler latent spaces, enabling unbiased one-shot sampling of equilibrium states and efficient free-energy computations without prior reaction coordinate knowledge \[[https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/2eb1ed9d4baf548391d8220c0a8a3d0be79845ed](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/2eb1ed9d4baf548391d8220c0a8a3d0be79845ed)\]. Similarly, approaches combining score-based generative models with implicit force fields enable learning coarse-grained molecular dynamics without explicit force inputs, preserving equilibrium distributions and folding dynamics \[[2302.00600](http://arxiv.org/abs/2302.00600)\].

Finally, protein sequence design methods like ProteinMPNN complement structure generation by enabling robust sequence prediction conditioned on backbones, improving sequence recovery and experimental validation outcomes, which is pivotal in closing the loop between structure and function in protein design \[[https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/98926d43356e87c22c82efc132dcaaac1ff40ebe](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/98926d43356e87c22c82efc132dcaaac1ff40ebe)\].

Collectively, these works illustrate a vibrant research landscape combining deep generative modeling, physical principles, and evolutionary insights to efficiently and accurately generate protein conformational ensembles, informing the development of methods such as Energy-based Alignment (EBA) which seek to calibrate generative models using physical feedback for improved ensemble fidelity.
