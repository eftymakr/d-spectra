## Related Works

Graph Convolutional Networks (GCNs) have become a foundational technique in collaborative filtering (CF) for recommendation systems, effectively leveraging the user-item interaction graph structure to capture complex preference patterns \[[1905.08108](http://arxiv.org/abs/1905.08108)\]. Early methods like Neural Graph Collaborative Filtering (NGCF) explicitly propagate embeddings over the user-item bipartite graph, injecting collaborative signals through high-order connectivity modeling \[[1905.08108](http://arxiv.org/abs/1905.08108)\]. However, NGCF and similar GCN-based models often include feature transformations and nonlinear activations, which have been shown to contribute little or even negatively affect performance in recommendation tasks, increasing training difficulty \[[2002.02126](http://arxiv.org/abs/2002.02126)\]\[[2001.10167](http://arxiv.org/abs/2001.10167)\]. This insight motivated simplified models such as LightGCN, which retain only the essential neighborhood aggregation component, achieving significant performance gains and scalability improvements by linear embedding propagation without feature transformations or nonlinearities \[[2002.02126](http://arxiv.org/abs/2002.02126)\].

Further simplifications include linear residual graph convolutional networks (LR-GCCF), which alleviate over-smoothing and training challenges in deep GCNs by adopting residual connections and removing nonlinearities, enabling deeper and more efficient CF models \[[2001.10167](http://arxiv.org/abs/2001.10167)\]. UltraGCN takes this simplification further by approximating infinite-layer graph convolutions via constraint loss without explicit message passing, achieving both higher accuracy and substantial speedups over LightGCN \[[2110.15114](http://arxiv.org/abs/2110.15114)\]. These works highlight the importance of balancing model expressiveness, computational efficiency, and training stability in graph-based CF.

Traditional embedding-based CF approaches, including matrix factorization and neural collaborative filtering, rely on learned latent vectors for users and items but may suffer from limited capacity and fail to fully exploit local interaction patterns \[[1708.05031](http://arxiv.org/abs/1708.05031)\]. Recent advances have incorporated side information via knowledge graphs to enrich embeddings, with models like KGAT and RippleNet leveraging high-order relations in knowledge graphs through attentive embedding propagation or preference ripple propagation, respectively, enhancing recommendation quality and interpretability \[[1905.07854](http://arxiv.org/abs/1905.07854)\]\[[1803.03467](http://arxiv.org/abs/1803.03467)\]. However, these methods still depend on learned embeddings and linear or limited nonlinear propagation mechanisms.

Graph spectral filtering offers an alternative perspective by analyzing graph convolution as a form of signal processing on graphs, enabling more flexible and theoretically grounded filter design \[[1211.0053](http://arxiv.org/abs/1211.0053)\]\[[1312.6203](http://arxiv.org/abs/1312.6203)\]. Spectral collaborative filtering methods explicitly operate in the spectral domain of the user-item bipartite graph, capturing rich connectivity information beyond local neighborhoods and alleviating cold-start issues \[[1808.10523](http://arxiv.org/abs/1808.10523)\]. The use of polynomial approximations, especially Chebyshev polynomials, has been widely adopted to efficiently approximate spectral filters without costly eigendecompositions, enabling scalable and flexible nonlinear filtering on graphs \[[1606.09375](http://arxiv.org/abs/1606.09375)\]\[[2007.01764](http://arxiv.org/abs/2007.01764)\]. These polynomial filters can approximate arbitrary smooth graph filters, offering a principled way to go beyond the restricted linear forms common in standard GCNs.

Furthermore, recent work on self-supervised graph learning for recommendation has shown that augmenting supervised CF tasks with auxiliary self-supervised objectives improves robustness and accuracy, particularly on long-tail items, by enhancing node representation learning via contrastive mechanisms \[[2010.10783](http://arxiv.org/abs/2010.10783)\]. This line of research emphasizes the importance of robust and informative user-item representations beyond traditional embedding learning.

Our approach, ChebyCF, builds upon these insights by directly leveraging the user's raw interaction history without relying on learned embeddings, thus capturing the full spectrum of signals contained in the interaction data. By employing Chebyshev interpolation, ChebyCF effectively approximates flexible nonlinear graph filters, addressing the limitations of prior embedding-based and linear GCN methods \[[2002.02126](http://arxiv.org/abs/2002.02126)\]\[[2001.10167](http://arxiv.org/abs/2001.10167)\]\[[1808.10523](http://arxiv.org/abs/1808.10523)\]. Additionally, ChebyCF incorporates ideal pass filters and degree-based normalization, enhancing the expressiveness and stability of the filtering process, drawing on principles from spectral graph theory \[[1211.0053](http://arxiv.org/abs/1211.0053)\]\[[1312.6203](http://arxiv.org/abs/1312.6203)\]. Compared to prior GCN simplifications and spectral CF models, ChebyCF offers a principled framework to overcome the bottlenecks related to limited latent space capacity and restricted neighborhood aggregation, achieving state-of-the-art performance with efficient inference.

In summary, the evolution of graph-based collaborative filtering has progressed from embedding-centric GCNs with complex transformations \[[1905.08108](http://arxiv.org/abs/1905.08108)\]\[[1708.05031](http://arxiv.org/abs/1708.05031)\], through simplified and linearized GCN architectures enhancing scalability and performance \[[2002.02126](http://arxiv.org/abs/2002.02126)\]\[[2001.10167](http://arxiv.org/abs/2001.10167)\]\[[2110.15114](http://arxiv.org/abs/2110.15114)\], to spectral graph filtering approaches that offer flexible and theoretically grounded signal processing on user-item graphs \[[1606.09375](http://arxiv.org/abs/1606.09375)\]\[[2007.01764](http://arxiv.org/abs/2007.01764)\]\[[1808.10523](http://arxiv.org/abs/1808.10523)\]. ChebyCF advances this trajectory by integrating Chebyshev polynomial spectral filtering with novel normalization and pass filtering techniques, providing a robust and expressive framework that addresses key limitations in existing GCN-based CF models.
