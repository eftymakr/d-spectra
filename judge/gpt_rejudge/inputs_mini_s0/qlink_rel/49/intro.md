## Related Works

Neural retrieval models have become fundamental in information retrieval, particularly for Web search, due to their ability to capture semantic matching beyond lexical overlap \[[2206.02743](http://arxiv.org/abs/2206.02743)\]\[[2004.04906](http://arxiv.org/abs/2004.04906)\]. However, these models typically require large amounts of labeled query-document pairs for training, which are costly and labor-intensive to obtain \[[2206.02743](http://arxiv.org/abs/2206.02743)\]. To address this limitation, synthetic query generation using large language models (LLMs) has emerged as a scalable alternative, enabling the creation of large training corpora without manual annotation \[[2206.02743](http://arxiv.org/abs/2206.02743)\]\[[2010.08191](http://arxiv.org/abs/2010.08191)\]. Despite its promise, synthetic data often suffers from quality variability, which can degrade retrieval performance if noisy query-document pairs are used indiscriminately \[[2206.02743](http://arxiv.org/abs/2206.02743)\].

Existing approaches commonly mitigate this issue by employing external re-rankers to filter out low-quality synthetic pairs based on their estimated relevance scores \[[2206.02743](http://arxiv.org/abs/2206.02743)\]\[[1901.04085](http://arxiv.org/abs/1901.04085)\]. While effective, this strategy introduces significant computational overhead since re-ranking requires costly neural inference on large candidate sets. Moreover, reliance on separate re-rankers may fail to integrate ranking signals during query generation, limiting the potential for end-to-end optimization of query quality \[[2206.02743](http://arxiv.org/abs/2206.02743)\]. Our proposed framework leverages Direct Preference Optimization (DPO) to address these challenges by incorporating ranking preferences directly into the query generation process, thus enabling the model to learn to produce higher-quality queries that better align with downstream retrieval objectives \[[2206.02743](http://arxiv.org/abs/2206.02743)\].

DPO, originally developed to simplify reinforcement learning from human feedback (RLHF), bypasses the need for explicit reward modeling by directly optimizing a policy to match human preferences via a classification loss \[[2305.18290](http://arxiv.org/abs/2305.18290)\]\[[2310.12036](http://arxiv.org/abs/2310.12036)\]. This method improves training stability and efficiency compared to traditional RLHF approaches that rely on separate reward models and policy optimization algorithms such as PPO \[[2305.18290](http://arxiv.org/abs/2305.18290)\]\[[1707.06347](http://arxiv.org/abs/1707.06347)\]. DPO has demonstrated strong performance in aligning language models with human preferences in tasks including dialogue and summarization, suggesting its utility for optimizing generation tasks sensitive to quality and relevance criteria \[[2305.18290](http://arxiv.org/abs/2305.18290)\]\[[2009.01325](http://arxiv.org/abs/2009.01325)\]. Our work extends DPO’s application to the domain of synthetic query generation for retrieval, optimizing query-document pair relevance implicitly through preference signals.

The importance of effective training strategies in dense retrieval models is well documented. Techniques such as hard negative mining, dynamic negative sampling, and data augmentation have been shown to improve dense retriever robustness and ranking quality \[[2007.00808](http://arxiv.org/abs/2007.00808)\]\[[2104.08051](http://arxiv.org/abs/2104.08051)\]\[[2010.08191](http://arxiv.org/abs/2010.08191)\]. For instance, Approximate Nearest Neighbor Negative Contrastive Estimation (ANCE) dynamically updates negative samples using an ANN index to better approximate the distribution of irrelevant documents, closing the train-test discrepancy and boosting retrieval accuracy \[[2007.00808](http://arxiv.org/abs/2007.00808)\]. Similarly, RocketQA introduces denoised hard negatives and cross-batch negatives to enhance dense passage retrieval training, yielding state-of-the-art performance on MS MARCO and Natural Questions \[[2010.08191](http://arxiv.org/abs/2010.08191)\]. These methods highlight the critical role of training data quality and sampling strategies, reinforcing the value of integrating ranking signals during data generation as our approach does.

Complementary to query generation improvements, advances in neural re-ranking architectures like BERT-based models and late interaction frameworks such as ColBERT have significantly contributed to retrieval effectiveness \[[2004.12832](http://arxiv.org/abs/2004.12832)\]\[[1901.04085](http://arxiv.org/abs/1901.04085)\]. ColBERT’s late interaction enables efficient yet fine-grained similarity modeling between queries and documents, allowing pre-computation of document representations for scalable retrieval \[[2004.12832](http://arxiv.org/abs/2004.12832)\]. Passage re-rankers based on BERT further refine initial retrieval outputs, substantially improving ranking metrics on benchmark datasets \[[1901.04085](http://arxiv.org/abs/1901.04085)\]. While these models excel in re-ranking, their computational expense motivates upstream improvements in query quality to reduce reliance on costly re-ranking stages.

On the theoretical front, recent work has formalized learning from human preferences beyond the approximations in RLHF and DPO, proposing novel objectives that directly optimize pairwise preferences and provide performance guarantees \[[2310.12036](http://arxiv.org/abs/2310.12036)\]. This line of research underscores the potential of preference-based optimization frameworks for more principled and effective model alignment, supporting our motivation to apply DPO in query generation with integrated ranking signals.

In summary, while prior work has extensively explored dense retrieval training, synthetic query generation, and human preference alignment separately, our approach uniquely combines these strands by applying DPO to guide synthetic query generation towards improving downstream retrieval performance. This integration addresses the limitations of external re-ranking filters and leverages advances in preference optimization to enhance the quality and relevance of generated queries, ultimately benefiting large-scale neural retrieval systems \[[2206.02743](http://arxiv.org/abs/2206.02743)\]\[[2305.18290](http://arxiv.org/abs/2305.18290)\]\[[2007.00808](http://arxiv.org/abs/2007.00808)\]\[[2310.12036](http://arxiv.org/abs/2310.12036)\]\[[2004.12832](http://arxiv.org/abs/2004.12832)\]\[[2010.08191](http://arxiv.org/abs/2010.08191)\].
