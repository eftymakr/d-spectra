## Related Works

Federated Learning (FL) enables collaborative model training across decentralized clients while preserving data privacy by avoiding raw data sharing \[[2203.07320](http://arxiv.org/abs/2203.07320)\]. The growing enforcement of data privacy regulations, notably the "right to be forgotten" (RTBF), has driven research into Federated Unlearning (FU), which aims to remove the influence of specified clients or data from a trained global FL model efficiently and effectively without full retraining \[[2310.20448](http://arxiv.org/abs/2310.20448)\]\[[https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/39da2d48f2132b919405e3bb6df6ece44ac6e15a](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/39da2d48f2132b919405e3bb6df6ece44ac6e15a)\]\[[2401.05146](http://arxiv.org/abs/2401.05146)\].

Initial federated unlearning approaches mainly focused on client-level data removal by either retraining or approximating retraining. For example, methods such as rapid retraining with local unlearning followed by few communication rounds have shown promising efficiency while maintaining model utility, even without access to global data or model update histories \[[2203.07320](http://arxiv.org/abs/2203.07320)\]\[[2207.05521](http://arxiv.org/abs/2207.05521)\]. Other approaches trade off storage and computation by retaining historical client updates at the server to expedite unlearning, as in FedEraser, which achieves up to a 4× speed-up over retraining by reconstructing unlearned models using calibrated historical updates \[[https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/eadeffdec9fac8fd7f9aea732ca410eb082b7dcf](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/eadeffdec9fac8fd7f9aea732ca410eb082b7dcf)\]\[[2012.13891](http://arxiv.org/abs/2012.13891)\]. Bayesian federated unlearning frameworks introduce adaptive unlearning rates and parameter sharing to balance forgetting and accuracy retention \[[https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/efab3b30d6f67f55c68f9dc7c65b2b54b1789030](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/efab3b30d6f67f55c68f9dc7c65b2b54b1789030)\].

Beyond client unlearning, federated unlearning has expanded to support more granular requests, including sample-level and class-level unlearning, which is especially relevant in applications like Federated Online Learning to Rank (FOLTR) where user data contributions vary in scope \[[2401.05146](http://arxiv.org/abs/2401.05146)\]\[[2405.15474](http://arxiv.org/abs/2405.15474)\]\[[2110.11794](http://arxiv.org/abs/2110.11794)\]. Techniques such as class-discriminative pruning selectively remove category-specific information by quantifying channel importance, enabling efficient forgetting without global data access or complete retraining \[[2110.11794](http://arxiv.org/abs/2110.11794)\]. Knowledge distillation has also been utilized to restore model performance post-unlearning by transferring knowledge from the original to the unlearned model without requiring client participation \[[2201.09441](http://arxiv.org/abs/2201.09441)\].

Security and privacy considerations are critical in federated unlearning. Research has identified risks of privacy leakage from model discrepancies before and after unlearning, which can enable membership inference attacks \[[https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/a52c3ec7325e81bd87fdf8d74bf9acfd1e1ffe5f](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/a52c3ec7325e81bd87fdf8d74bf9acfd1e1ffe5f)\]\[[2005.02205](http://arxiv.org/abs/2005.02205)\]. To mitigate these risks, differential privacy mechanisms and privacy-preserving protocols such as two-party computation have been integrated into federated unlearning frameworks (e.g., Starfish), ensuring that unlearning is both effective and secure without requiring retraining \[[https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/06edb1f6eadb6c141d720fcca4c854578dc5d670](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/06edb1f6eadb6c141d720fcca4c854578dc5d670)\]\[[2404.09724](http://arxiv.org/abs/2404.09724)\]. Additionally, federated unlearning frameworks addressing malicious clients have been developed, such as FAST, which removes malicious updates and remedies model bias using benchmark datasets, achieving substantial speed-ups and robustness \[[https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/2f69d7e9a7428a7e31de83b993a2142d0c2cde67](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/2f69d7e9a7428a7e31de83b993a2142d0c2cde67)\].

Verifiable federated unlearning represents a recent focus area, addressing the challenge that unlearning effects may be obscured by contributions from remaining clients, thus complicating verification of data removal \[[2205.12709](http://arxiv.org/abs/2205.12709)\]. Frameworks like VeriFi introduce marking and checking mechanisms to fingerprint clients and quantify unlearning impact, enabling users to verify that their data has been effectively removed. These verification methods are crucial for compliance and trustworthiness in decentralized settings.

In federated recommendation systems, unlearning strategies such as FRU apply rollback mechanisms inspired by database transactions to efficiently remove user contributions, addressing the challenge of limited device storage for historical updates \[[2210.10958](http://arxiv.org/abs/2210.10958)\]. Feature-level unlearning frameworks like Ferrari optimize feature sensitivity to unlearn sensitive, biased, or backdoor features without requiring all clients to participate, expanding the scope of federated unlearning beyond client or sample removal \[[2201.09441](http://arxiv.org/abs/2201.09441)\]\[[2405.17462](http://arxiv.org/abs/2405.17462)\].

Efforts have also been made to improve unlearning efficiency by integrating unlearning into the training process itself. FedAU introduces an auxiliary unlearning module that enables concurrent and multi-granularity unlearning (sample, class, client) without additional retraining steps, demonstrating practical applicability in FL scenarios \[[2405.15474](http://arxiv.org/abs/2405.15474)\]. Other works propose training-agnostic methods such as momentum degradation (MoDe) combined with memory guidance to erase implicit knowledge and restore model performance, achieving significant acceleration over retraining \[[https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/eff2be3b3ccfb1fc0650432b9b611ebd11ba0feb](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/eff2be3b3ccfb1fc0650432b9b611ebd11ba0feb)\].

Theoretical advancements have introduced frameworks for exact federated unlearning with provable guarantees of indistinguishability between unlearned and retrained models, leveraging concepts like total variation stability to guide algorithm design and communication-efficient protocols for both client- and sample-level unlearning \[[2401.11018](http://arxiv.org/abs/2401.11018)\]. Quantized FL models have also been proposed to facilitate exact and efficient unlearning \[[https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/a5f39bcd6198b26997b77233ff81a14e6bc8a32d](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/a5f39bcd6198b26997b77233ff81a14e6bc8a32d)\].

Comprehensive surveys highlight the challenges, methodologies, and open research directions in federated unlearning, emphasizing the need for standardized evaluation metrics that consider under-unlearning and over-unlearning scenarios, as well as the interplay between unlearning efficacy, privacy preservation, and computational efficiency \[[2310.20448](http://arxiv.org/abs/2310.20448)\]\[[2401.05146](http://arxiv.org/abs/2401.05146)\]\[[2403.13682](http://arxiv.org/abs/2403.13682)\]\[[https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/8b4103d09a1cb32ffb4dfba90e04698ea553a80c](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/8b4103d09a1cb32ffb4dfba90e04698ea553a80c)\]. The current study’s focus on systematically analyzing unlearning capabilities with multiple adapted metrics aligns with this broader research context, addressing the limitations of prior works that rely on single evaluation metrics.

Overall, federated unlearning research is rapidly evolving, covering a spectrum of techniques from retraining approximations and pruning to privacy-preserving verification and efficient algorithmic designs. These developments pave the way for practical, secure, and verifiable unlearning in privacy-sensitive federated settings including Federated Online Learning to Rank.
