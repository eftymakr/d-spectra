## Related Works

Zero-Knowledge Proofs (ZKPs) enable one party to prove the validity of a statement to another without revealing any additional information, ensuring both privacy and integrity in verifiable computing \[[https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/f056118f41849a3688634e1f2fc73108fc660435](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/f056118f41849a3688634e1f2fc73108fc660435)\]. Despite their theoretical appeal and broad applicability—including blockchain, verifiable machine learning, and electronic voting—ZKPs face significant practical challenges due to the computational intensity of the proof generation process \[[https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/f056118f41849a3688634e1f2fc73108fc660435](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/f056118f41849a3688634e1f2fc73108fc660435)\]. This bottleneck mainly arises from large-scale polynomial computations and multi-scalar multiplications (MSMs), which are essential components of many modern ZKP protocols such as zkSNARKs and HyperPlonk.

Efforts to accelerate ZKP proof generation have increasingly leveraged specialized hardware, including GPUs and ASICs. The SZKP accelerator represents a landmark ASIC design that achieves over 400× speedup against CPU baselines by exploiting structured dataflows tailored for Number Theoretic Transforms (NTTs) and MSMs, two key bottlenecks in ZKP computations \[[https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/6ee84674b7c426849c3da56cb278c08eb7a903cf](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/6ee84674b7c426849c3da56cb278c08eb7a903cf)\]. SZKP is notable for fully integrating these primitives on-chip, reducing memory overheads and addressing the irregular memory access patterns characteristic of these algorithms. However, while SZKP demonstrates exceptional speedups, its design complexity and power consumption pose challenges for widespread deployment \[[https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/6ee84674b7c426849c3da56cb278c08eb7a903cf](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/6ee84674b7c426849c3da56cb278c08eb7a903cf)\].

GPU-based accelerators have also been extensively explored due to their programmable parallelism and accessibility. GZKP and cuZK exemplify state-of-the-art GPU implementations that optimize polynomial computations and MSMs with innovative memory access patterns and parallelization strategies. GZKP achieves up to 48× speedup on standard benchmarks by combining cache-friendly data layouts and fine-grained task scheduling to balance workloads during elliptic curve operations \[[https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/e149d93531d8c07cf43f6cae327a4795144d1304](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/e149d93531d8c07cf43f6cae327a4795144d1304)\]. Similarly, cuZK introduces a parallel MSM algorithm that attains near-linear speedup over traditional serial MSM approaches like Pippenger’s algorithm, alongside efficient overlap of CPU-GPU data transfers to reduce latency \[[https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/e4a4608e8e35379e63b9df5bda1b7599ee8ab9ac](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/e4a4608e8e35379e63b9df5bda1b7599ee8ab9ac)\]. cuZK reports speedups of up to nearly 5× versus prior GPU implementations, demonstrating the potential of GPU acceleration to narrow the prover performance gap.

While these works achieve significant acceleration of ZKP primitives, many focus on individual components rather than the entire proof generation pipeline. This limits their applicability in protocols requiring full-stack optimization for practical deployment. The recently proposed HyperPlonk protocol addresses some limitations of prior ZKPs by supporting a one-time universal trusted setup and producing small proof sizes suitable for publicly verifiable, consensus-based systems \[[https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/f056118f41849a3688634e1f2fc73108fc660435](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/f056118f41849a3688634e1f2fc73108fc660435)\]. However, accelerating HyperPlonk’s full protocol—including the SumCheck protocol and MSM operations—remains an open challenge.

Beyond general ZKP acceleration, there has been growing interest in zero-knowledge proofs tailored to machine learning applications. zkCNN and zero-knowledge decision tree protocols enable verifiable, privacy-preserving validation of neural network predictions and model accuracy without revealing model parameters. zkCNN, for example, leverages a novel sumcheck protocol for fast Fourier transforms and convolutions, achieving proof generation times over 1000× faster than existing schemes on large CNNs \[[https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/6336149196f02d0eb6c1a89bc6b662e4422bd794](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/6336149196f02d0eb6c1a89bc6b662e4422bd794)\]. Similarly, decision tree ZKPs efficiently prove prediction correctness and accuracy with manageable proof sizes and verifier times \[[https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/3becc2935d1f52c82ffd7216c28bc86f5e393208](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/3becc2935d1f52c82ffd7216c28bc86f5e393208)\]. These specialized ZKPs demonstrate the adaptability of zero-knowledge techniques to complex, non-arithmetic computations.

The proposed zkSpeed accelerator builds on the prior state of the art by delivering a full-chip architecture that accelerates the entire HyperPlonk protocol, including both SumCheck and MSM components, achieving geometric mean speedups of 801× over CPU baselines \[[https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/f056118f41849a3688634e1f2fc73108fc660435](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/f056118f41849a3688634e1f2fc73108fc660435)\]. zkSpeed’s design integrates high-bandwidth memory and carefully optimized dataflows to overcome the irregular access patterns and computational overheads that limit prior accelerators. This comprehensive approach addresses both the trusted setup and proof size trade-offs inherent in earlier solutions, making it highly suitable for large-scale, publicly verifiable systems.

In summary, hardware acceleration of ZKPs has evolved from accelerating individual primitives on GPUs and ASICs \[[https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/6ee84674b7c426849c3da56cb278c08eb7a903cf](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/6ee84674b7c426849c3da56cb278c08eb7a903cf)\]\[[https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/e149d93531d8c07cf43f6cae327a4795144d1304](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/e149d93531d8c07cf43f6cae327a4795144d1304)\]\[[https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/e4a4608e8e35379e63b9df5bda1b7599ee8ab9ac](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/e4a4608e8e35379e63b9df5bda1b7599ee8ab9ac)\] to full-protocol acceleration that balances setup assumptions, proof size, and performance \[[https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/f056118f41849a3688634e1f2fc73108fc660435](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/f056118f41849a3688634e1f2fc73108fc660435)\]. Concurrently, zero-knowledge proofs have expanded into machine learning domains, proving the correctness and accuracy of complex models without compromising privacy \[[https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/6336149196f02d0eb6c1a89bc6b662e4422bd794](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/6336149196f02d0eb6c1a89bc6b662e4422bd794)\]\[[https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/3becc2935d1f52c82ffd7216c28bc86f5e393208](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/3becc2935d1f52c82ffd7216c28bc86f5e393208)\]. zkSpeed represents a significant advancement in this trajectory, providing scalable, high-throughput proof generation for a state-of-the-art ZKP protocol with practical deployment implications.
