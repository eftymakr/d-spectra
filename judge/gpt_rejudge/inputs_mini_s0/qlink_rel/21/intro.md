## Related Works

Neural rendering methods have become prominent for reconstructing 3D scenes from 2D images by optimizing implicit scene representations that jointly model geometry and appearance \[[1906.01618](http://arxiv.org/abs/1906.01618)\]. These methods typically rely on multiple input views to reduce uncertainty in reconstruction; however, their performance deteriorates when input views are limited, especially in dynamic or complex scenes with occlusions or unobserved viewpoints \[[1506.06825](http://arxiv.org/abs/1506.06825)\]\[[1906.01618](http://arxiv.org/abs/1906.01618)\]. Data augmentation through video frame interpolation has thus been proposed to increase effective view coverage and improve reconstruction fidelity.

Video frame interpolation aims to synthesize intermediate frames between consecutive video frames, enhancing temporal resolution and enabling smoother scene representations. Classical approaches often decompose the problem into motion estimation (typically optical flow) and frame synthesis guided by flow, but these methods suffer from inaccuracies in flow estimation, especially under large motion, occlusions, and appearance changes \[[1703.07514](http://arxiv.org/abs/1703.07514)\]\[[1803.10967](http://arxiv.org/abs/1803.10967)\]\[[1603.08155](http://arxiv.org/abs/1603.08155)\]. Recent deep learning approaches have addressed these limitations by integrating motion estimation and synthesis into unified networks or replacing explicit flow estimation with learned convolutional operations.

Adaptive convolution-based methods treat frame interpolation as a spatially-adaptive convolution over input frames, estimating pixel-specific kernels that implicitly capture motion and synthesis coefficients \[[1703.07514](http://arxiv.org/abs/1703.07514)\]\[[1708.01692](http://arxiv.org/abs/1708.01692)\]\[[2011.01280](http://arxiv.org/abs/2011.01280)\]. These methods reduce reliance on explicit flow and can better handle occlusion and brightness changes. Similarly, context-aware synthesis approaches warp both frames and pixel-wise contextual features derived from pretrained networks, enabling more robust interpolation in occluded or complex motion regions \[[1803.10967](http://arxiv.org/abs/1803.10967)\]. Other approaches employ multi-frame interpolation strategies that jointly estimate intermediate flows and soft visibility maps to exclude occluded pixels, improving temporal consistency and quality for arbitrary numbers of interpolated frames \[[1712.00080](http://arxiv.org/abs/1712.00080)\].

Incorporating depth information has also enhanced interpolation quality by explicitly reasoning about occlusions and object proximity. Depth-aware interpolation methods use depth-guided flow projection layers to prioritize closer objects during warping, combined with hierarchical contextual feature extraction to improve frame synthesis \[[1904.00830](http://arxiv.org/abs/1904.00830)\]. Additionally, novel flow modeling techniques, such as quadratic interpolation exploiting acceleration information, facilitate curvilinear motion modeling beyond linear assumptions, further improving interpolation accuracy in real-world scenarios \[[1911.00627](http://arxiv.org/abs/1911.00627)\].

A variety of motion representation strategies have been explored to overcome flow estimation challenges. Phase-based interpolation methods estimate per-pixel phase decomposition instead of flow, which is more robust to lighting changes and motion blur but limited to small motions \[[1804.00884](http://arxiv.org/abs/1804.00884)\]\[[https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/87b83fefb82216aad1c14cd1898d195722bcee42](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/87b83fefb82216aad1c14cd1898d195722bcee42)\]. Other strategies replace optical flow estimation with channel attention mechanisms and feature reshaping operations (e.g., PixelShuffle) to implicitly model motion within deep feature maps, providing robustness to large motions and occlusions without explicit flow estimation \[[https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/bb4a9650ca3946c70a7e92007cc61dc0dfd75522](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/bb4a9650ca3946c70a7e92007cc61dc0dfd75522)\].

Recent advances include forward warping techniques like softmax splatting, which resolve conflicts arising from multiple source pixels mapping to the same target location and enable differentiable interpolation with arbitrary temporal offsets \[[2003.05534](http://arxiv.org/abs/2003.05534)\]. Moreover, motion estimation and compensation driven networks integrate optical flow and adaptive kernels in a joint differentiable warping layer, balancing computational efficiency and interpolation quality \[[1810.08768](http://arxiv.org/abs/1810.08768)\].

In the context of neural rendering, frame interpolation serves as an effective data augmentation tool by enriching training datasets with synthetic intermediate views, thus reducing view sparsity and improving reconstruction quality. Models trained on diverse video datasets implicitly learn camera motion and 3D scene priors, which act as implicit world priors during neural rendering optimization \[[1506.06825](http://arxiv.org/abs/1506.06825)\]. Lightweight yet high-quality interpolation networks have been proposed to balance computational cost and performance, facilitating practical integration with neural rendering pipelines \[[https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/089f2072c95c244f3b7f4df404562bb4afe24448](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/089f2072c95c244f3b7f4df404562bb4afe24448)\]. Furthermore, unsupervised cycle consistency losses and motion regularization have been employed to enhance interpolation models when explicit ground truth is unavailable, enabling adaptation to new domains and improving generalization \[[https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/380f46cd24a15271036ff49e9e0d91f39f89d8b4](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/380f46cd24a15271036ff49e9e0d91f39f89d8b4)\]\[[1906.05928](http://arxiv.org/abs/1906.05928)\].

Overall, the integration of advanced video frame interpolation techniques into neural rendering frameworks addresses the critical challenge of limited input views, particularly for dynamic and occluded scenes. By leveraging convolutional architectures that unify motion estimation and synthesis, depth-aware reasoning, and novel motion representations, these methods provide enriched photo supervision that significantly enhances 3D reconstruction fidelity.
