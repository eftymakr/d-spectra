## Related Works

Unsupervised and self-supervised learning paradigms have profoundly influenced representation learning across domains, including natural language processing, computer vision, and increasingly, genomics. Early unsupervised methods for learning DNA representations drew inspiration from general self-supervised approaches that leverage intrinsic data properties to create predictive tasks without explicit labels. For instance, Contrastive Predictive Coding (CPC) introduced a universal unsupervised learning framework that predicts future latent representations using contrastive loss, demonstrating effectiveness across speech, images, text, and reinforcement learning domains \[[1807.03748](http://arxiv.org/abs/1807.03748)\]\[[1905.09272](http://arxiv.org/abs/1905.09272)\]. Similarly, contrastive learning frameworks like SimCLR and Momentum Contrast (MoCo) have improved visual representation learning by emphasizing data augmentations, nonlinear projections, and large batch sizes to learn discriminative features from unlabeled images \[[2002.05709](http://arxiv.org/abs/2002.05709)\]\[[1911.05722](http://arxiv.org/abs/1911.05722)\]\[[2003.04297](http://arxiv.org/abs/2003.04297)\]. These methods have shown that contrastive objectives enable models to capture meaningful semantic structure and transfer well to downstream tasks, closing the gap with supervised learning \[[1905.09272](http://arxiv.org/abs/1905.09272)\]\[[2006.10029](http://arxiv.org/abs/2006.10029)\].

In natural language processing, large-scale pre-training of transformer architectures such as BERT and RoBERTa has established the power of unsupervised learning on vast unlabeled corpora, achieving state-of-the-art results on diverse benchmarks \[[1907.11692](http://arxiv.org/abs/1907.11692)\]\[[1810.04805](http://arxiv.org/abs/1810.04805)\]. Extensions like the Switch Transformer employ Mixture of Experts (MoE) to scale model capacity efficiently by dynamically routing inputs to specialized subnetworks, enabling training of trillion-parameter models with manageable computational cost \[[2101.03961](http://arxiv.org/abs/2101.03961)\]. MoE architectures have also been adapted in vision and multimodal tasks, improving model expressivity and specialization \[[https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/4f7d1e04c9d30ce12df3e085d10d70997e6ea1a5](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/4f7d1e04c9d30ce12df3e085d10d70997e6ea1a5)\]\[[2103.00020](http://arxiv.org/abs/2103.00020)\].

When applied to genomics, pure sequence-based unsupervised pre-training frameworks face challenges due to the limited information content in raw DNA sequences, which lack explicit context on regulatory mechanisms critical to gene function. Prior works have explored self-supervised tasks on DNA data, such as predicting sequence transformations or solving jigsaw puzzles, to learn semantic features without annotations, achieving competitive performance on classification and detection benchmarks \[[1803.07728](http://arxiv.org/abs/1803.07728)\]\[[1603.09246](http://arxiv.org/abs/1603.09246)\]. However, these approaches do not fully incorporate biological regulatory context, which is often captured by genomic profiles like chromatin accessibility, transcription factor binding, and histone modifications.

Recognizing this gap, recent research advocates for supervised learning on genomic profiles as a more effective paradigm for DNA representation learning. By training models to predict a diverse set of genomic profiles, these methods integrate functional regulatory information, producing representations that better reflect biological activity and generalize across tasks \[[2203.06041](http://arxiv.org/abs/2203.06041)\]\[[1905.09272](http://arxiv.org/abs/1905.09272)\]. This aligns with findings in other domains that supervised signals, especially those capturing rich contextual information, lead to more robust and transferable features than purely unsupervised objectives \[[1810.04805](http://arxiv.org/abs/1810.04805)\]\[[1910.10683](http://arxiv.org/abs/1910.10683)\].

Moreover, the multi-species and multi-profile nature of genomic data motivates architectures capable of modeling complex, heterogeneous relationships. Mixture of Experts models provide a compelling solution, allowing the dynamic allocation of specialized subnetworks (experts) to different species and profile contexts. Although MoE has been successfully applied in NLP and vision for capturing modality-specific or task-specific nuances \[[https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/4f7d1e04c9d30ce12df3e085d10d70997e6ea1a5](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/4f7d1e04c9d30ce12df3e085d10d70997e6ea1a5)\]\[[2101.03961](http://arxiv.org/abs/2101.03961)\], its integration with supervised genomic profile prediction for DNA representation learning remains novel. This combination enables the model to better exploit cross-species conservation and divergence patterns, enhancing predictive performance and representation quality \[[1910.10683](http://arxiv.org/abs/1910.10683)\].

In summary, prior works establish that unsupervised and self-supervised learning methods like CPC, SimCLR, and MoCo provide strong foundations for representation learning but have limitations when applied to DNA sequences alone due to lack of regulatory context. Supervised training on genomic profiles addresses these shortcomings by embedding functional information directly into the learned representations. Our approach advances the field by integrating supervised genomic profile prediction with a MoE framework tailored to multi-species and multi-profile data, thereby capturing complex biological relationships and achieving state-of-the-art DNA representation learning performance.
