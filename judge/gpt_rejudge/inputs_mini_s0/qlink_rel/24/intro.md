## Related Works

Diffusion models have become a dominant approach in generative modeling for both image and video synthesis, with diffusion transformers (DiTs) emerging as a state-of-the-art architecture that surpasses traditional U-Net backbones in image generation quality and scalability \[[2105.05233](http://arxiv.org/abs/2105.05233)\]\[[2212.09748](http://arxiv.org/abs/2212.09748)\]\[[2111.12293](http://arxiv.org/abs/2111.12293)\]\[[2106.14156](http://arxiv.org/abs/2106.14156)\]. DiTs leverage transformer architectures to model complex dependencies in latent or pixel spaces, achieving superior fidelity and diversity in generated samples \[[2212.09748](http://arxiv.org/abs/2212.09748)\]\[[2111.12293](http://arxiv.org/abs/2111.12293)\]. However, their large parameter counts and computational costs hinder deployment on resource-constrained platforms such as edge devices, necessitating effective model compression strategies like quantization \[[2302.04304](http://arxiv.org/abs/2302.04304)\]\[[2106.08295](http://arxiv.org/abs/2106.08295)\]\[[2111.12293](http://arxiv.org/abs/2111.12293)\].

Quantization, particularly post-training quantization (PTQ), is a widely used technique to reduce model size and accelerate inference by lowering the bit-width of weights and activations without requiring expensive retraining \[[2106.08295](http://arxiv.org/abs/2106.08295)\]\[[2305.10657](http://arxiv.org/abs/2305.10657)\]\[[2310.0327](http://arxiv.org/abs/2310.03270)\]. While PTQ has shown success in image classification and convolutional networks, applying it directly to diffusion models is challenging. Diffusion models’ iterative denoising process and time-dependent noise estimation networks introduce temporal variation in activation distributions, complicating calibration and quantization \[[2302.04304](http://arxiv.org/abs/2302.04304)\]\[[2305.10657](http://arxiv.org/abs/2305.10657)\]\[[2211.15736](http://arxiv.org/abs/2211.15736)\]\[[2306.02316](http://arxiv.org/abs/2306.02316)\]. Early PTQ methods for diffusion models often led to substantial degradation in sample quality or required careful timestep-aware calibration \[[2302.04304](http://arxiv.org/abs/2302.04304)\]\[[2305.10657](http://arxiv.org/abs/2305.10657)\]\[[2211.15736](http://arxiv.org/abs/2211.15736)\].

Recent advancements have addressed these challenges by tailoring quantization methods specifically for diffusion models. Q-Diffusion introduced timestep-aware calibration and split shortcut quantization to maintain performance at 4-bit precision for unconditional diffusion models and text-guided Stable Diffusion, marking a significant step toward low-bit quantization without retraining \[[2302.04304](http://arxiv.org/abs/2302.04304)\]. PTQD further improved PTQ by modeling quantization noise's correlation with diffusion noise and applying mixed-precision bitwidth selection per denoising step, achieving near-full-precision quality with up to 19.9× bit operation savings in latent diffusion models \[[2305.10657](http://arxiv.org/abs/2305.10657)\]. Temporal dynamic quantization methods have also emerged, dynamically adjusting quantization intervals based on timestep information to improve output quality without inference overhead \[[2306.02316](http://arxiv.org/abs/2306.02316)\].

Diffusion transformers pose additional unique quantization challenges due to their spatial variance in weights and activations and temporal variance across timesteps, as well as the presence of salient channels with extreme magnitudes \[[2111.12293](http://arxiv.org/abs/2111.12293)\]\[[2406.0254](http://arxiv.org/abs/2406.02540)\]. PTQ4DiT introduced Channel-wise Salience Balancing and Spearman’s ρ-guided Salience Calibration to redistribute channel magnitudes and dynamically adjust activation balancing over time, enabling 8-bit and 4-bit weight quantization of DiTs with minimal performance loss \[[2405.16005](http://arxiv.org/abs/2405.16005)\]. Q-DiT advanced this by combining automatic quantization granularity allocation and sample-wise dynamic activation quantization, significantly reducing FID scores when quantizing large DiT models on ImageNet \[[2406.17343](http://arxiv.org/abs/2406.17343)\]. HQ-DiT proposed a 4-bit floating-point hybrid quantization scheme with clipping range selection and identity transforms to better align quantization with DiT data distributions, achieving very low-precision quantization with negligible accuracy degradation \[[2405.19751](http://arxiv.org/abs/2405.19751)\]. ViDiT-Q extended these ideas to video diffusion transformers, addressing the multi-frame processing challenge and achieving efficient quantization with negligible visual quality loss and practical speedups \[[2406.0254](http://arxiv.org/abs/2406.02540)\].

In parallel, video generation with diffusion models has evolved by leveraging latent diffusion and transformer-based architectures to model spatiotemporal dependencies efficiently. Latent video diffusion models (Video LDMs) compress high-dimensional video data into lower-dimensional latent spaces, enabling high-resolution video synthesis with lower computational costs \[[2304.08818](http://arxiv.org/abs/2304.08818)\]\[[2311.15127](http://arxiv.org/abs/2311.15127)\]\[[2401.03048](http://arxiv.org/abs/2401.03048)\]. Models such as Lumiere and W.A.L.T incorporate spatial-temporal attention and windowed architectures to improve temporal coherence and scalability in video generation, relying on pretrained image diffusion backbones and multi-scale processing \[[2310.0327](http://arxiv.org/abs/2310.03270)\]\[[2112.10752](http://arxiv.org/abs/2112.10752)\]\[[2406.17343](http://arxiv.org/abs/2406.17343)\]\[[2111.13824](http://arxiv.org/abs/2111.13824)\]. Imagen Video further scales text-to-video generation by combining cascaded diffusion models with temporal and spatial super-resolution modules, utilizing progressive distillation for fast sampling \[[2210.02303](http://arxiv.org/abs/2210.02303)\].

Despite these advances, quantization methods developed for image diffusion or static DiTs do not fully address video-specific challenges, such as preserving temporal consistency and spatiotemporal correlations across frames during low-bit inference \[[2106.08295](http://arxiv.org/abs/2106.08295)\]\[[2406.0254](http://arxiv.org/abs/2406.02540)\]. Existing works highlight the need for quantization schemes and optimization strategies that explicitly consider the temporal dimension and video context to maintain generation quality. Our work builds upon these foundations by introducing a token-aware quantization estimator that compensates quantization errors in both token and feature dimensions, and a temporal maintenance distillation method that preserves spatiotemporal correlations during training. This dual approach enables high-fidelity, low-bit quantized video diffusion transformers, setting new benchmarks in scene consistency and efficiency \[[2210.17323](http://arxiv.org/abs/2210.17323)\].

In summary, the landscape of diffusion model quantization has progressed from general PTQ methods to sophisticated, model- and task-specific techniques addressing temporal dynamics, channel salience, and mixed-precision strategies. Simultaneously, video diffusion modeling has advanced through latent space representations and transformer architectures tailored for temporal coherence. Our proposed Q-VDiT framework integrates insights from these domains to overcome the unique challenges posed by video diffusion transformers under quantization constraints.
