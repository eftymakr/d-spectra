## Related Works

Generative retrieval (GR) has recently emerged as a transformative paradigm in information retrieval (IR), where models directly generate document identifiers (docids) for relevant documents conditioned on input queries, effectively unifying indexing and retrieval within a single neural model \[[2202.06991](http://arxiv.org/abs/2202.06991)\]\[[2206.02743](http://arxiv.org/abs/2206.02743)\]\[[2206.10128](http://arxiv.org/abs/2206.10128)\]. This approach contrasts with traditional index-retrieve pipelines by eliminating explicit indexes and enabling end-to-end optimization. Early seminal work on Differentiable Search Index (DSI) demonstrated that a single Transformer could learn to map queries to docids by encoding the entire corpus into model parameters, showing strong retrieval and generalization capabilities on static corpora \[[2202.06991](http://arxiv.org/abs/2202.06991)\]\[[2206.10128](http://arxiv.org/abs/2206.10128)\]. Subsequent improvements focused on optimizing docid representations and mitigating training-retrieval data mismatches using query generation to better align indexing and retrieval distributions \[[2206.10128](http://arxiv.org/abs/2206.10128)\]\[[2305.15115](http://arxiv.org/abs/2305.15115)\].

A critical dimension of GR research involves the design of document identifiers. Initial methods relied on fixed or rule-based docids such as document titles or clustering outputs, which often fail to capture semantic nuances \[[2304.04171](http://arxiv.org/abs/2304.04171)\]\[[https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/6682a3679a17d090830635f1c420126d20eae54a](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/6682a3679a17d090830635f1c420126d20eae54a)\]. To address this, approaches like GenRet proposed learning semantic docids via discrete auto-encoding frameworks, yielding fine-grained, semantically rich representations that improve generalization, especially on unseen documents \[[2304.04171](http://arxiv.org/abs/2304.04171)\]. Similarly, NOVO introduced learnable n-gram sets as interpretable docids optimized through query denoising and retrieval tasks, further enhancing semantic alignment between queries and documents \[[https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/6682a3679a17d090830635f1c420126d20eae54a](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/6682a3679a17d090830635f1c420126d20eae54a)\]. Such semantic-aware docids have been shown to outperform numeric or static identifiers, particularly in dynamic corpora settings where new documents are continuously added \[[2208.09257](http://arxiv.org/abs/2208.09257)\]\[[2304.04171](http://arxiv.org/abs/2304.04171)\]\[[https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/6682a3679a17d090830635f1c420126d20eae54a](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/6682a3679a17d090830635f1c420126d20eae54a)\].

While text-based semantic docids tend to improve retrieval robustness and generalization, numeric-based docids, often constructed through clustering or hashing, provide computational efficiency but are prone to overfitting to the initial corpus and suffer performance degradation on evolving document collections \[[2208.09257](http://arxiv.org/abs/2208.09257)\]\[[2305.11841](http://arxiv.org/abs/2305.11841)\]. This challenge highlights the trade-off between retrieval effectiveness and efficiency in GR systems operating over dynamic corpora. Strategies to mitigate these issues include multi-docid designs that combine numeric and text-based identifiers to leverage both efficiency and semantic richness, achieving improved performance without extensive retraining \[[2208.09257](http://arxiv.org/abs/2208.09257)\].

Addressing retrieval over dynamic corpora is a growing research focus. Most GR models are evaluated on static datasets, limiting their practical applicability. CLEVER introduced a continual learning framework for generative retrieval that incrementally indexes new documents with low computational cost, employing techniques like Incremental Product Quantization and memory-augmented learning to prevent catastrophic forgetting of previously indexed documents \[[2308.14968](http://arxiv.org/abs/2308.14968)\]. Similarly, DSI++ studied continual indexing and proposed training modifications and generative memory mechanisms to stabilize learning and reduce forgetting when updating the model with new documents \[[2212.09744](http://arxiv.org/abs/2212.09744)\].

Scaling generative retrieval to large corpora presents additional challenges. While initial GR studies focused on corpora up to 100k documents, recent work has evaluated models on millions of passages, revealing issues such as the inefficacy of naive model scaling and the critical role of synthetic queries as document representations during indexing \[[2305.11841](http://arxiv.org/abs/2305.11841)\]\[[2311.09134](http://arxiv.org/abs/2311.09134)\]. RIPOR further advanced large-scale generative retrieval by introducing prefix-oriented ranking optimization and document ID constructions based on relevance associations, leading to strong improvements on large benchmarks like MS MARCO \[[2311.09134](http://arxiv.org/abs/2311.09134)\].

Beyond docid design and dynamic corpus handling, learning to rank within generative retrieval has been proposed to bridge the gap between sequence generation objectives and final passage ranking metrics. LTRGR introduces a learning-to-rank training phase that optimizes autoregressive models directly for ranking targets without inference overhead, achieving state-of-the-art results among generative methods \[[2306.15222](http://arxiv.org/abs/2306.15222)\].

Other works have explored autoregressive generation of substrings as document identifiers, enabling flexible and scalable retrieval without forced hierarchical structures, which has yielded improvements in passage-level retrieval benchmarks \[[2204.10628](http://arxiv.org/abs/2204.10628)\]. Moreover, unified generative retrievers have been developed to handle multiple granularities (document, passage, sentence, entity) in knowledge-intensive language tasks via prompt learning, enhancing generalization and task adaptability \[[2304.14856](http://arxiv.org/abs/2304.14856)\].

In summary, the literature highlights three key themes relevant to generative retrieval over dynamic corpora: (i) the importance of semantic, fine-grained docid design for robust generalization \[[2304.04171](http://arxiv.org/abs/2304.04171)\]\[[https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/6682a3679a17d090830635f1c420126d20eae54a](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/6682a3679a17d090830635f1c420126d20eae54a)\]\[[2305.15115](http://arxiv.org/abs/2305.15115)\], (ii) the challenges and strategies for continual learning and incremental indexing to handle evolving corpora \[[2308.14968](http://arxiv.org/abs/2308.14968)\]\[[2212.09744](http://arxiv.org/abs/2212.09744)\], and (iii) the scalability and ranking optimization techniques necessary for large-scale deployment \[[2305.11841](http://arxiv.org/abs/2305.11841)\]\[[2311.09134](http://arxiv.org/abs/2311.09134)\]\[[2306.15222](http://arxiv.org/abs/2306.15222)\]. Our work builds upon these insights by systematically evaluating the impact of docid granularity and proposing a hybrid multi-docid model that balances semantic expressiveness and computational efficiency without additional retraining, addressing a critical gap in GR research for dynamic environments.
