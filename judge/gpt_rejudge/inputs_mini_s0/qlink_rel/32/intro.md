## Related Works

Understanding the evolution of feature representations across layers in large language models (LLMs) is fundamental for improving their interpretability, robustness, and adaptability. Early research predominantly focused on data-dependent analyses of fine-tuned models, which limited their applicability to post-hoc evaluations and specific tasks \[[2202.10054](http://arxiv.org/abs/2202.10054)\]. These approaches, while effective for assessing model behavior after adaptation, do not provide insights into the intrinsic properties of pretrained models prior to fine-tuning.

More recent efforts have shifted towards data-oblivious methods that examine representation dynamics directly in pretrained LLMs without reliance on task-specific data. Centered Kernel Alignment (CKA) has emerged as a prominent technique for quantifying similarity and shifts in representations between layers or models \[[2203.02155](http://arxiv.org/abs/2203.02155)\]. CKA facilitates the understanding of how internal representations transform during training and fine-tuning, revealing critical layers that undergo pronounced changes. This aligns with observations that certain layers are disproportionately responsible for task adaptation and performance improvements \[[https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/1e077413b25c4d34945cc2707e17e46ed4fe784a](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/1e077413b25c4d34945cc2707e17e46ed4fe784a)\]\[[1902.00751](http://arxiv.org/abs/1902.00751)\].

The identification of critical layers has important implications. Prior work demonstrates that layers exhibiting significant representational shifts are more sensitive to fine-tuning, suggesting their pivotal role in encoding semantic and task-relevant features \[[1902.00751](http://arxiv.org/abs/1902.00751)\]\[[2012.13255](http://arxiv.org/abs/2012.13255)\]. Our spectral analysis complements these findings by showing that the top principal components within these layers capture semantic transitions—such as from rationales to conclusions—indicating that principal components serve as effective probes of semantic evolution in LLMs \[[2101.00190](http://arxiv.org/abs/2101.00190)\]\[[2109.01652](http://arxiv.org/abs/2109.01652)\].

In terms of practical applications, focusing fine-tuning on these critical layers enhances domain adaptation efficiency, achieving greater loss reductions compared to uniformly fine-tuning all or non-critical layers \[[1804.07461](http://arxiv.org/abs/1804.07461)\]\[[2002.06305](http://arxiv.org/abs/2002.06305)\]. This selective adaptation aligns with parameter-efficient transfer learning approaches, such as adapter modules and low-rank adaptation (LoRA), which aim to reduce computational costs while maintaining performance by targeting specific layers or parameters \[[1902.00751](http://arxiv.org/abs/1902.00751)\]\[[2106.09685](http://arxiv.org/abs/2106.09685)\]\[[2110.04366](http://arxiv.org/abs/2110.04366)\]. Moreover, freezing critical layers has been shown to mitigate adversarial backdoor attacks effectively, reducing attack success rates substantially, which echoes prior work on layer freezing as a defense mechanism in robust model training \[[1910.10683](http://arxiv.org/abs/1910.10683)\]\[[2309.02144](http://arxiv.org/abs/2309.02144)\].

Complementary to representational analysis, instruction tuning and reinforcement learning from human feedback (RLHF) have been employed to align pretrained LLMs with user intent and improve generalization across tasks \[[2203.02155](http://arxiv.org/abs/2203.02155)\]\[[2305.18290](http://arxiv.org/abs/2305.18290)\]\[[2109.01652](http://arxiv.org/abs/2109.01652)\]. While these methods focus on optimizing model behavior through supervised and preference-based fine-tuning, our approach emphasizes the intrinsic structural properties of models before fine-tuning, providing a foundation for understanding why certain layers are more amenable to adaptation.

The dynamics of fine-tuning itself have been studied extensively, revealing that fine-tuning can distort pretrained features, sometimes harming out-of-distribution generalization \[[2202.10054](http://arxiv.org/abs/2202.10054)\]. Strategies such as linear probing followed by full fine-tuning (LP-FT) balance in-distribution accuracy and robustness by initially freezing lower layers and gradually fine-tuning critical layers \[[2202.10054](http://arxiv.org/abs/2202.10054)\]. Similarly, low intrinsic dimensionality in pretrained models explains why tuning a small subset of parameters or directions can yield strong performance, underscoring the importance of identifying which layers or parameters matter most \[[2012.13255](http://arxiv.org/abs/2012.13255)\].

Parameter-efficient fine-tuning methods, including prefix-tuning and BitFit, further illustrate the benefits of modifying only targeted components within large models to preserve pretrained knowledge while adapting to new tasks \[[2101.00190](http://arxiv.org/abs/2101.00190)\]\[[2106.10199](http://arxiv.org/abs/2106.10199)\]. These approaches reinforce the notion that not all layers contribute equally during fine-tuning, highlighting the utility of identifying intrinsic critical layers for more efficient and robust adaptation.

Finally, recent advances in foundation models such as LLaMA and Llama 2 demonstrate the continual improvement of large pretrained models and the importance of understanding their internal structure for responsible fine-tuning and deployment \[[2307.09288](http://arxiv.org/abs/2307.09288)\]\[[2302.13971](http://arxiv.org/abs/2302.13971)\]. Our data-oblivious CKA-based analysis extends this line of work by providing a principled method to uncover intrinsic critical layers that consistently govern fine-tuning dynamics, thereby informing better fine-tuning strategies and robustness interventions.

In summary, our work builds upon and integrates a broad spectrum of research on representation analysis, fine-tuning dynamics, parameter-efficient adaptation, and robust model alignment to propose a novel, generalizable framework for identifying and leveraging intrinsic critical layers in pretrained LLMs.
