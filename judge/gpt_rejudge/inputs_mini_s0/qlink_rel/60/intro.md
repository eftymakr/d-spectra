## Related Works

Model calibration is a critical area of research aimed at improving the reliability of machine learning predictions by aligning predicted confidence scores with true correctness likelihoods. This challenge is particularly pronounced in ranking models, which often emphasize relative orderings rather than the accuracy of absolute prediction values. Traditional calibration methods predominantly rely on predefined, order-preserving transformation functions, such as piece-wise linear mappings, to adjust original predictions while maintaining their rank structure. However, these fixed-form calibrators tend to lack the flexibility needed to model complex, nonlinear calibration functions, limiting their effectiveness in practical scenarios \[[2003.06820](http://arxiv.org/abs/2003.06820)\].

Post-hoc calibration techniques like temperature scaling have gained popularity due to their simplicity and effectiveness in calibrating deep neural networks without sacrificing accuracy \[[1706.04599](http://arxiv.org/abs/1706.04599)\]. Temperature scaling applies a single scalar parameter to logits before the softmax layer, correcting for overconfidence. While effective in many cases, its expressiveness is limited to scaling the softmax temperature and may not capture more intricate miscalibration patterns. More expressive post-hoc methods, such as Dirichlet calibration, extend beyond temperature scaling by learning a parametric transformation derived from Dirichlet distributions, enabling improved calibration in multiclass settings \[[1910.12656](http://arxiv.org/abs/1910.12656)\]. Nonetheless, these approaches typically impose structural constraints on the calibration function which may hinder their adaptability to complex data distributions.

Recent research has explored neural network-based calibrators to overcome such limitations. Neural architectures designed to learn monotonic functions have been proposed to ensure order preservation while enhancing flexibility \[[2003.06820](http://arxiv.org/abs/2003.06820)\]. The concept of intra order-preserving functions, which preserve the top-k prediction orderings, has been operationalized through neural networks that combine common components to model complex calibration mappings \[[2003.06820](http://arxiv.org/abs/2003.06820)\]. This framework aligns closely with the motivation behind using Unconstrained Monotonic Neural Networks (UMNNs), which can approximate arbitrary monotonic functions with high modeling capacity, thus relaxing the constraints of fixed-form calibrators and enabling more precise calibration in ranking tasks.

Beyond deterministic calibrators, Bayesian approaches have been employed to model predictive uncertainty and improve calibration. Dropout as approximate Bayesian inference allows extraction of uncertainty estimates from standard neural networks without significant computational overhead \[[1506.02142](http://arxiv.org/abs/1506.02142)\], while deep ensembles leverage multiple independently trained models to produce well-calibrated uncertainty estimates and robustness to distributional shift \[[1612.01474](http://arxiv.org/abs/1612.01474)\]. Although effective in uncertainty quantification, these methods are often computationally intensive, limiting their deployment in large-scale ranking systems.

Data augmentation techniques such as mixup training have also shown promise in improving calibration by smoothing label distributions and reducing overconfidence in predictions \[[1905.11001](http://arxiv.org/abs/1905.11001)\]. Mixup-trained models exhibit better-calibrated softmax outputs and are less susceptible to overconfident errors, especially on out-of-distribution samples. This suggests that training strategies influence calibration quality, complementing post-hoc calibration efforts.

Evaluation of calibration remains an active research focus. Common metrics like Expected Calibration Error (ECE) have known limitations, including sensitivity to binning schemes and sample size \[[1904.01685](http://arxiv.org/abs/1904.01685)\]. Adaptive binning and class-conditional calibration metrics have been proposed to yield more stable and informative assessments of calibration quality. Additionally, recent work highlights the importance of data efficiency and expressive power in calibration methods, advocating ensemble and compositional strategies (Mix-n-Match) that combine multiple calibrators to achieve improved performance without sacrificing accuracy \[[2003.07329](http://arxiv.org/abs/2003.07329)\].

In large-scale real-world applications, such as image classification and recommendation systems, calibrated models have demonstrated enhanced decision-making reliability and user experience \[[2106.07998](http://arxiv.org/abs/2106.07998)\]\[[1905.11001](http://arxiv.org/abs/1905.11001)\]. However, many existing methods either lack the flexibility to capture complex monotonic calibration functions or impose rigid constraints that limit their application in ranking contexts where both relative order and absolute calibration matter.

Our work builds upon these insights by employing UMNNs to learn unconstrained, highly expressive monotonic calibration functions that preserve order without sacrificing flexibility. Moreover, we introduce a novel Smooth Calibration Loss to optimize these networks toward ideal calibration states in a principled manner. This approach addresses the expressiveness limitations of prior fixed-form calibrators and complements recent advances in monotonic neural architectures for calibration \[[2003.06820](http://arxiv.org/abs/2003.06820)\]\[[2106.07998](http://arxiv.org/abs/2106.07998)\]. The combination of flexible function modeling and tailored loss design distinguishes our method in the landscape of calibration research, enabling improved calibration performance validated in both offline benchmarks and large-scale online ranking systems.
