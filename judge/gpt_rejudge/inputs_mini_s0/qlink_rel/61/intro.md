## Related Works

Personalized outfit generation is a challenging task that involves recommending or generating sets of fashion items that are both compatible and aligned with individual user preferences. Early works in fashion recommendation primarily focused on modeling compatibility between pairs or sets of items using visual and contextual features. For example, Siamese CNN architectures have been employed to learn latent spaces encoding compatibility across categories, emphasizing cross-category fit rather than mere visual similarity \[[1509.07473](http://arxiv.org/abs/1509.07473)\]. Subsequent methods extended these ideas to sequence modeling, treating outfits as ordered item sequences and leveraging bidirectional LSTMs to capture compatibility and style information, while also integrating semantic information such as category and attributes \[[1707.05691](http://arxiv.org/abs/1707.05691)\]\[[1807.03133](http://arxiv.org/abs/1807.03133)\].

Graph-based approaches have further advanced outfit compatibility modeling by representing outfits as graphs, where nodes correspond to item categories and edges encode interactions. Node-wise graph neural networks (NGNN) have been proposed to learn node representations and outfit compatibility scores, effectively capturing complex item relationships beyond pairwise compatibility \[[1902.08009](http://arxiv.org/abs/1902.08009)\]. Similarly, hierarchical fashion graph networks (HFGN) unify user, outfit, and item relationships, propagating embeddings in a hierarchical structure to jointly optimize compatibility and personalization \[[2005.12566](http://arxiv.org/abs/2005.12566)\]. Attention mechanisms have also been integrated to model user-specific preferences and item relevancy within outfits, exemplified by OutfitNet, which uses multiple instance learning to simultaneously learn item compatibility and user attention distributions \[[https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/762ec45448e915142f355848265d10dd55ab470f](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/762ec45448e915142f355848265d10dd55ab470f)\].

Personalization in outfit recommendation has been addressed by incorporating user preferences into compatibility modeling. Models such as FashionNet and GP-BPR combine deep feature extraction with personalized embedding fine-tuning to capture individual tastes alongside general compatibility patterns \[[1810.02443](http://arxiv.org/abs/1810.02443)\]\[[https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/b1db62044adca574e4cf88b23c170f3cc089d513](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/b1db62044adca574e4cf88b23c170f3cc089d513)\]. However, a key challenge remains in handling sparse user data, especially for new users with limited interaction history. To tackle this, methods employing learnable anchors characterize users in latent outfit spaces using a small number of representative vectors, enabling robust preference modeling even with scarce data \[[https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/44cfb82fb3703f7ef2949f1cd38733643ce93328](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/44cfb82fb3703f7ef2949f1cd38733643ce93328)\].

Generative models have recently gained attention for fashion outfit generation, as they can synthesize novel items or complete outfits tailored to users. Early works use generative adversarial networks (GANs) conditioned on user preferences and query items to produce compatible and personalized fashion images \[[https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/ca3ad34ff2652cd922c3476514a758674bc7e76b](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/ca3ad34ff2652cd922c3476514a758674bc7e76b)\]\[[1711.02231](http://arxiv.org/abs/1711.02231)\]. These models typically rely on supervised learning with explicit training signals and often struggle with diversity and generalization. To overcome these limitations, approaches have integrated compatibility family learning with conditional generative models, allowing multi-prototype compatibility representations that better capture the diversity of compatible items \[[1712.01262](http://arxiv.org/abs/1712.01262)\]. Style extraction modules and multimodal embeddings (combining text and image features) have been adopted to control the global style of generated outfits and enforce coherence with user-specified themes \[[1807.03133](http://arxiv.org/abs/1807.03133)\]\[[1906.07273](http://arxiv.org/abs/1906.07273)\]\[[2204.04812](http://arxiv.org/abs/2204.04812)\].

Direct preference optimization (DPO), originally developed in natural language processing for aligning model outputs with human preferences without handcrafted reward functions, has recently been proposed to fine-tune fashion generative models using automatically generated feedback \[[1905.01866](http://arxiv.org/abs/1905.01866)\]. This approach addresses issues of supervised learning dependence and limited feedback signals by optimizing models directly on user preference signals, thus improving personalization and compatibility simultaneously. Multi-expert feedback generation systems evaluate generated outfits from multiple perspectives—quality, compatibility, and personalization—to produce comprehensive and objective supervisory signals that guide the fine-tuning process \[[https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/b5f488dd55024f0db2cabb73b055002b5d995be9](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/b5f488dd55024f0db2cabb73b055002b5d995be9)\]. This mechanism contrasts with traditional reinforcement learning methods that rely on human or heuristic rewards, offering a scalable alternative for model refinement.

Complementary item retrieval and fill-in-the-blank (FITB) tasks are common evaluation benchmarks in this domain, assessing the model’s ability to recommend items that complete an outfit compatibly and according to user tastes \[[1912.08967](http://arxiv.org/abs/1912.08967)\]\[[1902.08009](http://arxiv.org/abs/1902.08009)\]\[[1707.05691](http://arxiv.org/abs/1707.05691)\]. Recent transformer-based architectures, such as OutfitTransformer, leverage self-attention mechanisms and task-specific tokens to learn outfit-level representations, achieving state-of-the-art results in compatibility prediction and complementary item retrieval \[[2204.04812](http://arxiv.org/abs/2204.04812)\]. Similarly, multimodal approaches that fuse visual and textual embeddings have demonstrated improved personalization and flexibility in outfit generation and recommendation \[[1711.02231](http://arxiv.org/abs/1711.02231)\].

In summary, research in personalized outfit generation and recommendation has evolved from compatibility modeling using deep metric and graph learning, through personalized embedding and attention mechanisms, to generative models enhanced by novel fine-tuning paradigms like DPO. These advancements collectively address challenges of compatibility, diversity, and personalization, while enabling scalable, user-aligned fashion outfit generation.
