## Related Works

Fund allocation in financial domains requires effective integration of forecasting and optimization to overcome the limitations of naive approaches such as prediction-only and decoupled Predict-then-Optimize (PnO) methods, which often suffer from goal misalignment between forecasting accuracy and allocation objectives \[[2503.06928](http://arxiv.org/abs/2503.06928)\]. Traditional PnO frameworks separate forecasting and optimization tasks, leading to suboptimal fund allocation due to their incompatible objectives \[[2308.11200](http://arxiv.org/abs/2308.11200)\]. To mitigate this, some recent studies propose end-to-end training frameworks that align forecasting and optimization, improving overall performance by jointly optimizing both tasks; however, these are typically tailored to specific forecasting models, limiting their generalizability \[[2304.04912](http://arxiv.org/abs/2304.04912)\]\[[2211.14730](http://arxiv.org/abs/2211.14730)\]\[[2405.13810](http://arxiv.org/abs/2405.13810)\].

Accurate time series forecasting in financial contexts remains challenging due to inherent uncertainty and complex temporal dependencies. Deep learning models such as Convolutional Neural Networks (CNNs) and Transformers have been leveraged to capture both short-term local patterns and long-term global dependencies in financial time series \[[2304.04912](http://arxiv.org/abs/2304.04912)\]. Hybrid architectures combining CNNs and Transformers have demonstrated improved forecasting accuracy in stock price movement prediction by modeling multi-scale temporal dependencies \[[2304.04912](http://arxiv.org/abs/2304.04912)\]. Transformer-based models also benefit from segmentation and patching techniques to reduce computational costs and extend attention to longer histories, as evidenced by PatchTST and GridTST models, which embed time series as segmented tokens or 2D grids to capture temporal and variate correlations effectively \[[2211.14730](http://arxiv.org/abs/2211.14730)\]\[[2405.13810](http://arxiv.org/abs/2405.13810)\]. Moreover, addressing non-stationarity in financial time series with advanced modules such as wavelet convolutions and period-aware attention further enhances forecasting robustness \[[2406.03710](http://arxiv.org/abs/2406.03710)\].

Uncertainty quantification is critical for reliable financial decision-making. Probabilistic forecasting approaches, such as residual boosting networks built on Recurrent Neural Networks (RNNs), provide confidence intervals for predictions, enabling risk-aware decisions \[[https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/151942b084ece11084dec2970598fd5d495e1bbc](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/151942b084ece11084dec2970598fd5d495e1bbc)\]. Calibration methods, including conformal prediction and Monte Carlo dropout, have been applied to estimate forecasting uncertainty but may be computationally demanding or model-specific \[[2503.12648](http://arxiv.org/abs/2503.12648)\]\[[https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/e11d20dbd5103c75c58a5f5f241f7df663aabb2d](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/e11d20dbd5103c75c58a5f5f241f7df663aabb2d)\]\[[1912.09363](http://arxiv.org/abs/1912.09363)\]\[[2412.09394](http://arxiv.org/abs/2412.09394)\]. Recent advances incorporate uncertainty-aware structures, such as stochastic correlation learning over graphs to improve calibration and accuracy in multivariate time series forecasting \[[2407.02641](http://arxiv.org/abs/2407.02641)\].

Financial applications increasingly explore large language models (LLMs) and foundation models for time series forecasting. LLMs demonstrate strong reasoning abilities by integrating heterogeneous data sources, including textual financial news and price histories, to generate explainable forecasts and capture cross-sequence dependencies \[[2306.11025](http://arxiv.org/abs/2306.11025)\]\[[2410.19025](http://arxiv.org/abs/2410.19025)\]. Despite the common belief that financial markets are near-random, LLMs have shown potential in extracting subtle inefficiencies and generating alpha in stock return predictions \[[2412.09394](http://arxiv.org/abs/2412.09394)\]. Foundation models pretrained on extensive time series data, such as Chronos, TimesFM, and Time-MoE, enable zero-shot or few-shot forecasting with competitive or superior performance compared to task-specific trained models, reducing the need for large labeled datasets \[[https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/e11d20dbd5103c75c58a5f5f241f7df663aabb2d](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/e11d20dbd5103c75c58a5f5f241f7df663aabb2d)\]\[[2410.09487](http://arxiv.org/abs/2410.09487)\]\[[2410.19025](http://arxiv.org/abs/2410.19025)\]. Additionally, transfer learning approaches that leverage data from related assets improve volatility forecasting for new financial instruments with limited history, addressing data scarcity issues prevalent in financial domains \[[2503.12648](http://arxiv.org/abs/2503.12648)\].

Hybrid and emerging architectures continue to advance time series forecasting performance and applicability in finance. SegRNN improves long-term forecasting by reducing recurrent iterations via segment-wise processing, offering efficiency gains suitable for resource-constrained scenarios like IoT but with implications for financial forecasting \[[2308.11200](http://arxiv.org/abs/2308.11200)\]. FFTNet integrates frequency- and time-domain features through a hybrid CNN and MLP model to capture cyclical trends and abrupt changes simultaneously, achieving state-of-the-art results on multiple datasets \[[https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/143da25b6b3215acc9909d9b43796cb9d24bdc49](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/143da25b6b3215acc9909d9b43796cb9d24bdc49)\]. Diffusion models augmented by retrieval mechanisms address instability in forecasting by guiding denoising with reference time series, enhancing performance on complex prediction tasks \[[2410.18712](http://arxiv.org/abs/2410.18712)\]. Graph neural networks (GNNs) model interdependencies between financial variables and time points, enabling improved forecasting and uncertainty estimation, although challenges remain in scalability and explainability \[[2410.22377](http://arxiv.org/abs/2410.22377)\]\[[2407.02641](http://arxiv.org/abs/2407.02641)\].

Interpretable forecasting frameworks such as Temporal Fusion Transformers (TFT) combine recurrent and attention mechanisms to provide both high accuracy and interpretability in multi-horizon forecasting, which is crucial for financial decision support \[[1912.09363](http://arxiv.org/abs/1912.09363)\]. Recent surveys emphasize the architectural diversity in deep learning for time series forecasting, highlighting the trend toward hybrid models that balance inductive biases and computational efficiency, while addressing challenges like distribution shifts, causality, and feature selection \[[https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/6cf145b7a156034a4c8957c16cedabbd22be7136](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/6cf145b7a156034a4c8957c16cedabbd22be7136)\].

In summary, existing works have explored various facets of time series forecasting and optimization in financial contexts, ranging from deep learning architectures, uncertainty calibration, transfer learning, to foundation and large language models. However, many approaches assume fixed forecasting models or lack integrated risk-aware mechanisms. The proposed RTS-PnO framework advances the state of the art by enabling end-to-end training with objective alignment, adaptive uncertainty calibration, and forecasting model agnosticism, thus addressing both goal mismatch and forecast uncertainty prevalent in financial fund allocation problems \[[2503.06928](http://arxiv.org/abs/2503.06928)\]\[[2304.04912](http://arxiv.org/abs/2304.04912)\]\[[2503.12648](http://arxiv.org/abs/2503.12648)\]\[[2412.09394](http://arxiv.org/abs/2412.09394)\]\[[2306.11025](http://arxiv.org/abs/2306.11025)\]\[[2406.03710](http://arxiv.org/abs/2406.03710)\]\[[2410.18712](http://arxiv.org/abs/2410.18712)\].
