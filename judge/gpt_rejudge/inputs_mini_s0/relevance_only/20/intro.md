## Related Works

Single-pixel imaging (SPI) has garnered considerable interest due to its capability to reconstruct images from measurements acquired by a single-pixel detector using structured illumination patterns. Early SPI methods primarily utilized random patterns, which often resulted in high sampling requirements and suboptimal reconstruction quality. Later, deterministic pattern designs such as Hadamard and Fourier masks were adopted to improve control over the imaging process and enhance reconstruction performance . These advances laid the groundwork for integrating learning-based methods to overcome inherent limitations of traditional SPI reconstruction.

Among contemporary approaches, plug-and-play (PnP) methods have become popular for solving inverse imaging problems, including SPI. PnP frameworks incorporate advanced denoisers—often deep convolutional neural networks (CNNs)—as implicit image priors within iterative optimization algorithms. This paradigm offers notable flexibility since a single pretrained denoiser can be plugged into various reconstruction tasks without retraining for each scenario. For instance, the use of deep denoisers within half-quadratic splitting (HQS) or alternating direction method of multipliers (ADMM) frameworks has achieved state-of-the-art results in image restoration tasks such as deblurring, super-resolution, and compressed sensing MRI \[[1809.04693](http://arxiv.org/abs/1809.04693)\]\[[2008.13751](http://arxiv.org/abs/2008.13751)\]. However, PnP methods typically face trade-offs between reconstruction accuracy and computational speed, as well as challenges in theoretical convergence guarantees, especially when denoisers do not correspond to proximal operators \[[1809.04693](http://arxiv.org/abs/1809.04693)\]\[[2012.05703](http://arxiv.org/abs/2012.05703)\]. Recent work has addressed some of these limitations by developing tuning-free PnP proximal algorithms that learn parameters automatically via reinforcement learning, improving robustness and performance across diverse imaging conditions \[[2012.05703](http://arxiv.org/abs/2012.05703)\].

In parallel, deep algorithm unrolling (or unfolding) has emerged as an effective strategy that bridges model-based iterative algorithms and data-driven neural networks. By truncating and unrolling iterative optimization schemes into multi-stage networks, unrolling approaches embed the physical forward model explicitly while learning regularization priors, resulting in interpretable and efficient architectures \[[1912.10557](http://arxiv.org/abs/1912.10557)\]\[[1705.08041](http://arxiv.org/abs/1705.08041)\]. These networks typically achieve superior reconstruction accuracy and faster inference compared to classical iterative methods and PnP algorithms. However, a common limitation is their reduced adaptability; unrolled networks often require retraining or fine-tuning when imaging parameters such as compression ratio (CR) or sampling patterns change \[[2306.06378](http://arxiv.org/abs/2306.06378)\]\[[2003.10428](http://arxiv.org/abs/2003.10428)\]. Notably, unrolling techniques have been successfully applied to various inverse problems, including blind and non-blind image deblurring \[[1902.05399](http://arxiv.org/abs/1902.05399)\]\[[1902.03493](http://arxiv.org/abs/1902.03493)\]\[[https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/b954a94af132294a4a4e2efce15027758a535742](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/b954a94af132294a4a4e2efce15027758a535742)\], dynamic MR imaging \[[2307.09818](http://arxiv.org/abs/2307.09818)\], and hyperspectral image deconvolution combining handcrafted and learned regularizers for enhanced performance \[[2306.06378](http://arxiv.org/abs/2306.06378)\]. Recent advances have also focused on ensuring convergence of unrolled networks, for example, via novel parametrization schemes in half-quadratic splitting unrolling, which preserve algorithmic convergence guarantees while maintaining high reconstruction quality .

Efforts to combine the advantages of PnP and unrolling approaches have been pursued, motivated by the complementary strengths of flexibility and accuracy. For example, online PnP algorithms using stochastic subsets of measurements have been proposed to scale PnP frameworks to large datasets while maintaining performance \[[1809.04693](http://arxiv.org/abs/1809.04693)\]. Similarly, dynamic proximal unrolling networks adapt proximal operators during inference, allowing a single trained model to handle a variety of measurement matrices and sampling ratios without retraining \[[2107.11007](http://arxiv.org/abs/2107.11007)\]. These methods improve flexibility and generalizability but often rely on carefully designed dynamic modules or policy networks to tune parameters adaptively \[[2107.11007](http://arxiv.org/abs/2107.11007)\]\[[2012.05703](http://arxiv.org/abs/2012.05703)\].

Another relevant direction includes hybrid optimization frameworks that integrate handcrafted regularizers with learnable priors within unrolled architectures. This synergy reduces the complexity of learned components and enhances reconstruction quality, as demonstrated in deep unrolling models for hyperspectral image restoration \[[2306.06378](http://arxiv.org/abs/2306.06378)\]. Furthermore, recurrent momentum acceleration modules have been integrated into unrolled networks to improve performance on nonlinear inverse problems, highlighting the potential of incorporating optimization-inspired techniques to boost convergence and robustness \[[2307.16120](http://arxiv.org/abs/2307.16120)\].

Recent work also explores novel loss functions and training strategies to better approximate proximal operators in unrolled networks. The introduction of general proximal trajectory (PT) loss functions to train deep image restorers within HQS and ADMM unrolling frameworks exemplifies such trends, aiming to learn restoration operators that generalize across varying compression ratios, thus combining the flexibility of PnP with the accuracy and speed of unrolling \[[1809.04693](http://arxiv.org/abs/1809.04693)\].

In summary, the literature on SPI inverse problem solvers emphasizes a dichotomy between PnP approaches, which are flexible but limited in accuracy and speed, and unrolling methods, which are accurate and efficient but less adaptable to changing acquisition settings. Recent advancements focus on bridging this gap by designing proximal unrolling networks that maintain adaptability across varying CRs without retraining, leveraging learned regularizers, dynamic parameter tuning, and convergence-guaranteed architectures. These developments have demonstrated superior performance in image reconstruction tasks and represent the state-of-the-art in combining model-based and learning-based paradigms \[[1809.04693](http://arxiv.org/abs/1809.04693)\]\[[2107.11007](http://arxiv.org/abs/2107.11007)\]\[[2012.05703](http://arxiv.org/abs/2012.05703)\]\[[2306.06378](http://arxiv.org/abs/2306.06378)\]\[[2008.13751](http://arxiv.org/abs/2008.13751)\].
