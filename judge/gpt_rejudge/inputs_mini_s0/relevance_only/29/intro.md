## Related Works

Maximum Inner Product Search (MIPS) is a critical problem in machine learning and information retrieval, especially for high-dimensional data settings. Traditional MIPS methods either directly utilize inner product (IP) similarity or reduce the problem to Nearest Neighbor Search (NNS) under the Euclidean metric through space transformations. However, direct IP-based methods often face challenges such as local optima and redundant computations due to the non-metric nature of the inner product, which violates the triangle inequality \[[2503.06882](http://arxiv.org/abs/2503.06882)\]\[[https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/25454f456c514219c9d6eeb42200ebbf378db9ca](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/25454f456c514219c9d6eeb42200ebbf378db9ca)\]. Conversely, reduction-based methods suffer from distortions in the vector space topology, leading to information loss and degraded search accuracy \[[2503.06882](http://arxiv.org/abs/2503.06882)\]\[[https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/25454f456c514219c9d6eeb42200ebbf378db9ca](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/25454f456c514219c9d6eeb42200ebbf378db9ca)\].

Recent research argues against a strict dichotomy between IP and Euclidean metrics, suggesting that a hybrid approach leveraging the strengths of both can improve MIPS performance. This perspective has motivated efforts to explore the theoretical and empirical interplay between these metrics, particularly in the context of graph-based indexing and search \[[2503.06882](http://arxiv.org/abs/2503.06882)\]\[[1909.13459](http://arxiv.org/abs/1909.13459)\]\[[https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/4d0dce74babcf393b5a4cbcb11c89ccca6a3b92b](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/4d0dce74babcf393b5a4cbcb11c89ccca6a3b92b)\].

Graph-based methods have become a dominant paradigm for MIPS due to their empirical efficiency and scalability. The inner-product Navigable Small World graph (ip-NSW) is a notable example that exploits the norm bias in MIPS, where vectors with larger norms dominate search results. This norm bias induces a graph structure where large-norm items have high in-degree, allowing efficient search by focusing computation on these influential nodes and pruning smaller norm items \[[1909.13459](http://arxiv.org/abs/1909.13459)\]. Enhancements such as ip-NSW+ further improve performance by incorporating an angular proximity graph to guide the search initialization, effectively combining angular and inner product information to balance exploration and exploitation in the graph \[[1909.13459](http://arxiv.org/abs/1909.13459)\].

Similarly, the Norm Adjusted Proximity Graph (NAPG) refines graph construction by adjusting edge connections based on sampled data to better capture meaningful relationships for inner product retrieval. This method demonstrates that careful graph design, which accounts for vector norms and angular relations, can significantly enhance search efficiency \[[https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/25454f456c514219c9d6eeb42200ebbf378db9ca](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/25454f456c514219c9d6eeb42200ebbf378db9ca)\]. Other proximity graph variants, such as Inner Product Delaunay Graphs (IPDG), also transform MIPS into graph traversal problems, benefiting from efficient neighbor identification without requiring space transformation \[[https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/4d0dce74babcf393b5a4cbcb11c89ccca6a3b92b](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/4d0dce74babcf393b5a4cbcb11c89ccca6a3b92b)\].

Learning-based and reinforcement learning approaches have also been proposed for MIPS. Reinforcement routing on proximity graphs uses an agent trained via reinforcement and imitation learning to navigate the graph optimally, particularly when ground truth queries are scarce, improving search accuracy and efficiency \[[2201.09290](http://arxiv.org/abs/2201.09290)\]. On the modeling side, Indexable Probabilistic Matrix Factorization (IPMF) integrates the indexing mechanism into the training phase of matrix factorization models, achieving sublinear search time and improved retrieval speed by coupling inference and indexing \[[https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/386854741f828d30e25a5e01d75e3363d49cad1e](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/386854741f828d30e25a5e01d75e3363d49cad1e)\].

Quantization and sampling techniques provide complementary strategies for approximate MIPS. Anisotropic Additive Quantization improves upon product quantization by learning codebooks that minimize inner product quantization error, achieving higher approximation accuracy with efficient retrieval \[[https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/23cff8ecfc5feb4208dbb90ff3fa7a6adb214e9c](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/23cff8ecfc5feb4208dbb90ff3fa7a6adb214e9c)\]. Budgeted MIPS algorithms address computational constraints by selectively sampling or greedily searching within a fixed operation budget, balancing speed and accuracy effectively \[[1908.08656](http://arxiv.org/abs/1908.08656)\]\[[1610.03317](http://arxiv.org/abs/1610.03317)\]. Wedge and diamond sampling schemes have been compared, with wedge sampling often yielding superior efficiency and accuracy for budgeted top-k MIPS \[[1908.08656](http://arxiv.org/abs/1908.08656)\].

High-dimensional MIPS has also seen advances through randomized algorithms like BanditMIPS, which adaptively subsample vector coordinates based on multi-armed bandit strategies, reducing dimensional dependency from \(O(\sqrt{d})\) to \(O(1)\) without preprocessing \[[2212.07551](http://arxiv.org/abs/2212.07551)\]. GPU-accelerated frameworks, such as GAIPS, further speed up MIPS by exploiting norm-based pruning and hybrid residue and hash-based techniques, outperforming existing GPU-based search tools \[[https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/90d3d3c77e3acb0f455278af86c316f0ab893f8f](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/90d3d3c77e3acb0f455278af86c316f0ab893f8f)\].

Beyond pure efficiency, recent studies address diversity in MIPS results. Diversity-aware MIPS formulations incorporate category preferences or similarity penalties to promote diverse and novel recommendations, using approximation algorithms with probabilistic guarantees or greedy heuristics integrated with early termination and skipping techniques \[[https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/92fb60fde7aa7c94a22571dec416d4a795548f67](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/92fb60fde7aa7c94a22571dec416d4a795548f67)\]\[[https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/87bfc511e4ef4a8aeddad903b06036dbe7177625](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/87bfc511e4ef4a8aeddad903b06036dbe7177625)\]\[[2402.13858](http://arxiv.org/abs/2402.13858)\]. These approaches reconcile relevance and diversity, which are often conflicting objectives in recommender systems.

Finally, exact MIPS solutions have been revisited with a focus on hardware efficiency and adaptive solver selection. Approaches like blocked matrix multiply (BMM) and the MAX-IMUS algorithm leverage hardware-friendly computations and pruning to accelerate exact search. The OPTIMUS framework dynamically selects the best solver based on input characteristics, outperforming prior methods significantly on average \[[https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/0b2790c2c1e23e5535421696e1ec763bbc651bbd](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/0b2790c2c1e23e5535421696e1ec763bbc651bbd)\].

In summary, these works collectively illustrate an evolving landscape where graph-based indexing, metric blending, adaptive search strategies, learning-based indexing, quantization, sampling, and diversity considerations are integrated to address the multifaceted challenges of MIPS. Our proposed Metric-Amphibious Graph (MAG) and Adaptive Navigation with Metric Switch (ANMS) algorithm build upon these insights by explicitly harnessing both inner product and Euclidean metrics in a unified graph-based framework, guided by statistical topology indicators for parameter tuning, to achieve superior search efficiency and adaptability \[[2503.06882](http://arxiv.org/abs/2503.06882)\].
