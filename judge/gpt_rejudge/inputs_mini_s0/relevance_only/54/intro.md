## Related Works

Graph Convolutional Networks (GCNs) have become foundational in collaborative filtering (CF) for recommendation systems by modeling user-item interactions as graphs and exploiting their structural information to capture complex preferences \[[1905.08108](http://arxiv.org/abs/1905.08108)\]\[[2002.02126](http://arxiv.org/abs/2002.02126)\]. Early GCN-based CF methods focus on embedding propagation through neighborhood aggregation to capture high-order connectivity, which improves recommendation accuracy over traditional matrix factorization \[[1905.08108](http://arxiv.org/abs/1905.08108)\]\[[2002.02126](http://arxiv.org/abs/2002.02126)\]\[[2001.10167](http://arxiv.org/abs/2001.10167)\]. However, these methods often rely on learned embeddings derived from static ID features, which limits the model’s capacity to capture fine-grained, locally observed preference patterns and reduces flexibility in representing diverse user-item relationships \[[https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/de2562febbdf3181834648d1fc679e2cb75aa973](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/de2562febbdf3181834648d1fc679e2cb75aa973)\]\[[2406.08827](http://arxiv.org/abs/2406.08827)\].

A well-recognized issue in GCN-based CF is the oversmoothing problem, where repeated neighborhood aggregation causes node embeddings to become indistinguishable, degrading performance especially in deep models \[[2001.10167](http://arxiv.org/abs/2001.10167)\]\[[2404.10321](http://arxiv.org/abs/2404.10321)\]\[[2004.14734](http://arxiv.org/abs/2004.14734)\]. Various approaches have been proposed to address this, including residual connections specific to CF \[[2001.10167](http://arxiv.org/abs/2001.10167)\], dropout of neighbor messages to enhance robustness \[[2004.14734](http://arxiv.org/abs/2004.14734)\], and clustering strategies to differentiate multiple user interests during convolution \[[2404.10321](http://arxiv.org/abs/2404.10321)\]. Additionally, the embedding layers in most GCN models have limited expressiveness, motivating alternative approaches that directly exploit interaction signals without heavy reliance on ID embeddings \[[https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/a194dae103ce4593a8e15ecb1c1385cef3c60004](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/a194dae103ce4593a8e15ecb1c1385cef3c60004)\].

From a graph signal processing perspective, spectral graph filtering has been explored to enhance CF models by operating directly in the frequency domain of user-item bipartite graphs. SpectralCF first introduced spectral convolution on the bipartite graph, revealing deeper connectivity information and alleviating the cold-start problem \[[1808.10523](http://arxiv.org/abs/1808.10523)\]. Subsequent works like PolyCF leverage polynomial graph filters to approximate optimal spectral responses for recovering missing interactions, demonstrating the benefits of flexible spectral filtering over standard GCNs \[[https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/de2562febbdf3181834648d1fc679e2cb75aa973](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/de2562febbdf3181834648d1fc679e2cb75aa973)\]\[[2401.12590](http://arxiv.org/abs/2401.12590)\]. However, many GCN-based methods act effectively as low-pass filters that limit the frequency spectrum utilized, cutting off useful middle- and high-frequency signals and thereby constraining the representation power \[[2406.08827](http://arxiv.org/abs/2406.08827)\]\[[2006.15516](http://arxiv.org/abs/2006.15516)\]\[[2107.04755](http://arxiv.org/abs/2107.04755)\].

To overcome the limited expressiveness imposed by linear and low-pass graph filters, researchers have investigated advanced spectral filter designs. Approaches such as BernNet use Bernstein polynomial approximations to learn arbitrary spectral filters, allowing richer filter shapes beyond simple low-pass behaviors \[[2106.10994](http://arxiv.org/abs/2106.10994)\]. Similarly, universal IIR graph filters based on classical analog filter functions (Butterworth, Chebyshev, elliptic) have been proposed to design more accurate and computationally efficient spectral filters \[[2203.14748](http://arxiv.org/abs/2203.14748)\]. CayleyNets further extend spectral filtering by employing complex rational functions to target specific frequency bands, enhancing localization and spectral adaptability \[[1705.07664](http://arxiv.org/abs/1705.07664)\]. These methods provide theoretical and practical foundations for non-linear, flexible filtering on graphs, which can better capture diverse spectral features in recommendation data.

In parallel, simplified GCN architectures like LightGCN remove feature transformation and nonlinear activation to focus purely on neighborhood aggregation, improving both training efficiency and recommendation performance \[[2002.02126](http://arxiv.org/abs/2002.02126)\]. Linear residual GCN models address oversmoothing by introducing residual connections suited for CF tasks \[[2001.10167](http://arxiv.org/abs/2001.10167)\]. Embedding-less frameworks have also been proposed, where raw interaction histories are directly processed through graph convolutions or contrastive learning objectives without relying on learned embeddings, thus exploiting full spectral signals and avoiding embedding bottlenecks \[[https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/a194dae103ce4593a8e15ecb1c1385cef3c60004](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/a194dae103ce4593a8e15ecb1c1385cef3c60004)\]\[[2406.13996](http://arxiv.org/abs/2406.13996)\].

Other advances include multi-graph and multi-relation approaches that integrate different user-user, item-item, and user-item graphs or heterogeneous edge types to better capture complex user preferences and latent purchasing motivations \[[2001.00267](http://arxiv.org/abs/2001.00267)\]\[[1911.10699](http://arxiv.org/abs/1911.10699)\]\[[https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/62f19dc672ca2cd55f1c2ce5e1116e5587186ba0](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/62f19dc672ca2cd55f1c2ce5e1116e5587186ba0)\]. These models emphasize the need for fine-grained aggregation mechanisms capable of differentiating multiple neighbor relations beyond direct interactions, which parallels the motivation to improve spectral filtering expressiveness and flexibility.

Finally, recent works propose normalization and filtering techniques to adapt the spectral distribution of graph signals, redistributing noise and enabling more effective denoising by graph filters without heavy supervision \[[2406.08827](http://arxiv.org/abs/2406.08827)\]. Individualized graph filters adapt to user-specific confidence in observed preferences, further enhancing expressive power in embedding generation \[[2406.08827](http://arxiv.org/abs/2406.08827)\]. These insights highlight the importance of spectral filter design and graph normalization in overcoming the limitations of traditional GCN-based CF methods.

In summary, the limitations of embedding layers and neighborhood aggregation in existing GCN-based CF approaches stem largely from simplistic linear, low-pass graph filters that restrict spectral utilization. Advances in spectral graph filtering, polynomial and rational filter approximations, embedding-less models, and multi-relation graph convolutions provide promising directions toward more expressive, flexible, and efficient collaborative filtering models that leverage the full frequency spectrum of user-item interaction graphs \[[https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/de2562febbdf3181834648d1fc679e2cb75aa973](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/de2562febbdf3181834648d1fc679e2cb75aa973)\]\[[2401.12590](http://arxiv.org/abs/2401.12590)\]\[[2406.08827](http://arxiv.org/abs/2406.08827)\]\[[1808.10523](http://arxiv.org/abs/1808.10523)\]\[[2001.00267](http://arxiv.org/abs/2001.00267)\]\[[https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/a194dae103ce4593a8e15ecb1c1385cef3c60004](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/a194dae103ce4593a8e15ecb1c1385cef3c60004)\]\[[2002.02126](http://arxiv.org/abs/2002.02126)\]\[[1705.07664](http://arxiv.org/abs/1705.07664)\]\[[2203.14748](http://arxiv.org/abs/2203.14748)\]\[[2106.10994](http://arxiv.org/abs/2106.10994)\]\[[2001.10167](http://arxiv.org/abs/2001.10167)\]\[[https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/62f19dc672ca2cd55f1c2ce5e1116e5587186ba0](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/62f19dc672ca2cd55f1c2ce5e1116e5587186ba0)\]\[[2404.10321](http://arxiv.org/abs/2404.10321)\].
