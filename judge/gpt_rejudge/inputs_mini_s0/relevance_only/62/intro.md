## Related Works

Generative retrieval (GR) has recently emerged as a transformative paradigm in information retrieval, aiming to replace traditional index-retrieve-rank pipelines with a single large-scale autoregressive model that directly generates document identifiers (docids) from queries. This approach leverages sequence-to-sequence models, typically Transformers, to consolidate corpus knowledge within model parameters, enabling end-to-end differentiable retrieval without explicit inverted indices or embedding databases \[[https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/5beaeee1e27007bd12967480bb9c379cebd15e6e](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/5beaeee1e27007bd12967480bb9c379cebd15e6e)\]. GR’s potential advantages include improved efficiency, seamless integration with large language models (LLMs), and adaptability to dynamic corpora \[[https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/5beaeee1e27007bd12967480bb9c379cebd15e6e](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/5beaeee1e27007bd12967480bb9c379cebd15e6e)\]\[[2305.18952](http://arxiv.org/abs/2305.18952)\].

Early foundational studies introduced the conceptual framework of generative retrieval, highlighting its ability to map queries directly to docids and consolidating retrieval into a generation task \[[https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/5beaeee1e27007bd12967480bb9c379cebd15e6e](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/5beaeee1e27007bd12967480bb9c379cebd15e6e)\]\[[2204.10628](http://arxiv.org/abs/2204.10628)\]. Techniques such as Differentiable Search Index demonstrated the feasibility of autoregressive retrieval models but revealed challenges when scaling beyond 100k documents, particularly concerning model size, training complexity, and retrieval accuracy on large corpora with millions of passages \[[2305.11841](http://arxiv.org/abs/2305.11841)\]. Synthetic query generation for indexing emerged as a critical technique to improve scaling, though naive parameter scaling showed diminishing returns \[[2305.11841](http://arxiv.org/abs/2305.11841)\].

Recent advances address GR’s training and indexing bottlenecks. Traditional approaches require costly fine-tuning to memorize query-docid associations, limiting adaptability to corpus updates. Few-shot indexing frameworks alleviate this by prompting pretrained LLMs to generate docids for documents without extensive retraining, thus improving efficiency and robustness in dynamic environments \[[2408.02152](http://arxiv.org/abs/2408.02152)\]\[[2305.18952](http://arxiv.org/abs/2305.18952)\]. These methods also explore one-to-many mappings from documents to identifiers, enhancing retrieval coverage and accuracy \[[2408.02152](http://arxiv.org/abs/2408.02152)\].

A major practical challenge lies in handling complex document structures and multi-faceted content, such as in book search scenarios. Generative retrieval frameworks tailored for hierarchical and lengthy documents employ outline-oriented encoding and data augmentation to preserve contextual and hierarchical information, achieving significant retrieval improvements over state-of-the-art baselines \[[2501.11034](http://arxiv.org/abs/2501.11034)\]. Similarly, extensions to multi-graded relevance settings have been proposed, where generative retrieval models learn to produce semantically distinct and relevance-graded docids through constrained contrastive training, accommodating nuanced relevance beyond binary labels \[[2409.18409](http://arxiv.org/abs/2409.18409)\].

The decoding process in generative retrieval, frequently conducted via beam search, is critical for retrieval quality but also a source of limitations. Beam search, while a standard decoding heuristic, optimizes marginal likelihoods token-by-token and may not align well with the global retrieval objective, leading to suboptimal generation and ranking errors \[[2502.03678](http://arxiv.org/abs/2502.03678)\]. This is particularly relevant when corpus-specific constraints are imposed during decoding to ensure generated docids belong to the corpus. Recent theoretical analysis derives lower bounds on retrieval error in terms of KL divergence between ground-truth and predicted step-wise marginal distributions, highlighting inherent limits imposed by constrained autoregressive decoding \[[https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/5beaeee1e27007bd12967480bb9c379cebd15e6e](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/5beaeee1e27007bd12967480bb9c379cebd15e6e)\].

Alternative decoding strategies have been explored to overcome these limitations. Reflection-window decoding and generation-refinement frameworks introduce iterative refinement and selective correction during sequence generation, balancing efficiency and optimality \[[2502.03678](http://arxiv.org/abs/2502.03678)\]\[[2502.19732](http://arxiv.org/abs/2502.19732)\]. Controlled decoding approaches leverage discrete autoregressive biasing and energy-based models to enforce constraints while maintaining fluency and accuracy, potentially applicable to constrained docid generation \[[2502.03685](http://arxiv.org/abs/2502.03685)\]. Moreover, forward-looking constrained decoding strategies enable corpus-informed pruning and relevance-aware generation, improving evidence retrieval quality in retrieval-augmented generation systems \[[2412.11919](http://arxiv.org/abs/2412.11919)\].

Beyond retrieval itself, integrating learning-to-rank objectives into generative retrieval training has shown promise in bridging the gap between autoregressive generation and ranking performance. Learning-to-rank frameworks optimize the generative model directly for passage ranking targets via rank losses, enhancing retrieval effectiveness without adding inference overhead \[[2306.15222](http://arxiv.org/abs/2306.15222)\]. Pretraining strategies, such as those employed by CorpusBrain, further improve GR model generalization across knowledge-intensive tasks, enabling zero- and low-resource adaptability \[[2208.07652](http://arxiv.org/abs/2208.07652)\].

Retrieval-augmented generation (RAG) and related paradigms complement generative retrieval by combining retrieval with generation for downstream tasks, though often at the cost of extra retriever components and increased complexity. Unified frameworks like RetroLLM integrate retrieval and generation with constrained decoding to generate fine-grained evidence directly, addressing hallucination and efficiency issues \[[2412.11919](http://arxiv.org/abs/2412.11919)\].

In summary, the body of related work on generative retrieval spans foundational modeling, scaling challenges, indexing strategies, decoding algorithms, structured document retrieval, multi-graded relevance, and integration with ranking objectives. Theoretical insights into constrained decoding errors and beam search suboptimality provide critical groundwork for advancing the robustness and generalization of generative retrieval systems \[[https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/5beaeee1e27007bd12967480bb9c379cebd15e6e](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/5beaeee1e27007bd12967480bb9c379cebd15e6e)\]\[[2305.11841](http://arxiv.org/abs/2305.11841)\]\[[2408.02152](http://arxiv.org/abs/2408.02152)\]\[[2502.03678](http://arxiv.org/abs/2502.03678)\]\[[2305.18952](http://arxiv.org/abs/2305.18952)\]\[[2501.11034](http://arxiv.org/abs/2501.11034)\]\[[2409.18409](http://arxiv.org/abs/2409.18409)\]\[[2306.15222](http://arxiv.org/abs/2306.15222)\]\[[2204.10628](http://arxiv.org/abs/2204.10628)\]\[[2502.03685](http://arxiv.org/abs/2502.03685)\]\[[2412.11919](http://arxiv.org/abs/2412.11919)\]\[[2208.07652](http://arxiv.org/abs/2208.07652)\].
