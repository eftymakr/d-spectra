## Related Works

Neural retrieval models have become central to modern information retrieval systems, especially for Web search, due to their ability to learn effective representations from large-scale data. Traditionally, these models rely on extensive labeled query-document pairs for training, which are costly and labor-intensive to obtain \[[2102.11903](http://arxiv.org/abs/2102.11903)\]. To circumvent this limitation, synthetic query generation using large language models (LLMs) has emerged as a scalable alternative. Synthetic queries can approximate real user queries, enabling the creation of large-scale training datasets without manual annotation \[[1904.08375](http://arxiv.org/abs/1904.08375)\]. However, synthetic queries often vary significantly in quality, which can degrade downstream retrieval performance if noisy or irrelevant queries are used for training \[[1904.08375](http://arxiv.org/abs/1904.08375)\]\[[2305.05973](http://arxiv.org/abs/2305.05973)\].

Existing methods commonly address this quality issue by filtering out low-quality query-document pairs using external re-rankers that assess relevance scores \[[1904.08375](http://arxiv.org/abs/1904.08375)\]. While effective, this two-stage approach introduces additional computational overhead and may not fully capture the nuanced alignment between query generation and retrieval objectives. In contrast, frameworks that integrate ranking signals directly into the query generation process offer a promising direction. Direct Preference Optimization (DPO) is one such method that directly aligns language models with human or proxy preferences via pairwise ranking without explicit reward modeling or reinforcement learning \[[2305.18290](http://arxiv.org/abs/2305.18290)\]\[[2503.11701](http://arxiv.org/abs/2503.11701)\]. This simplifies training pipelines and has demonstrated robustness and stability in preference alignment across various tasks \[[2305.18290](http://arxiv.org/abs/2305.18290)\]\[[2503.11701](http://arxiv.org/abs/2503.11701)\].

DPO’s effectiveness has been demonstrated in multiple domains, including multilingual counter-speech generation, where it aligns LLM outputs with human preferences to improve contextual appropriateness and adaptability \[[2412.15453](http://arxiv.org/abs/2412.15453)\]. Furthermore, token-level importance weighting extensions of DPO (e.g., TIS-DPO) have improved optimization efficiency by accounting for the differential impact of tokens within responses, leading to better alignment on helpfulness and harmlessness metrics \[[2410.04350](http://arxiv.org/abs/2410.04350)\]. These advances suggest that DPO can effectively optimize generative models to produce outputs better aligned with downstream task objectives, such as query generation for retrieval.

The use of synthetic queries extends beyond generation quality concerns to privacy-preserving retrieval system training. Differentially private language models have been employed to generate synthetic queries that maintain privacy guarantees while supporting downstream dense retrieval training, demonstrating the utility of synthetic data beyond mere scalability \[[2305.05973](http://arxiv.org/abs/2305.05973)\]. In parallel, weak supervision techniques that generate pseudo query-document pairs from unsupervised models like BM25 have shown that neural ranking models can benefit from large-scale, automatically labeled data, achieving significant gains over traditional lexical methods \[[1704.08803](http://arxiv.org/abs/1704.08803)\]. This indicates the broad potential of leveraging synthetic and weakly supervised data to improve retrieval effectiveness.

Dense retrieval models also benefit from improved sampling and training strategies, such as dynamic hard negative sampling and joint optimization of query encoding with product quantization, which enhance ranking performance and efficiency \[[2104.08051](http://arxiv.org/abs/2104.08051)\]\[[2108.00644](http://arxiv.org/abs/2108.00644)\]. These methods complement synthetic data approaches by refining how models learn from training signals.

Generative retrieval paradigms, which treat document retrieval as a document identifier generation task, have explored direct optimization strategies to better align token-level generation with document-level relevance, avoiding complex reinforcement learning procedures \[[2504.05181](http://arxiv.org/abs/2504.05181)\]. This approach resonates with the principle of integrating preference signals directly into model training, as done with DPO in query generation.

Overall, the integration of ranking or preference signals directly into the synthetic query generation process, as realized by DPO and related methods, represents a significant advancement over traditional filtering approaches. This integration not only improves the relevance of generated queries but also simplifies training and enhances downstream retrieval performance, as supported by empirical results on benchmarks like MS MARCO \[[2102.11903](http://arxiv.org/abs/2102.11903)\]\[[1904.08375](http://arxiv.org/abs/1904.08375)\]\[[2305.18290](http://arxiv.org/abs/2305.18290)\]. Future research directions include combining token-level importance weighting and rationale-augmented preference data to further improve learning efficiency and alignment robustness \[[2410.04350](http://arxiv.org/abs/2410.04350)\]\[[2407.14477](http://arxiv.org/abs/2407.14477)\].
