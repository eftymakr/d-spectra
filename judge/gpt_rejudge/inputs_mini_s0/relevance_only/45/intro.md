## Related Works

Fine-grained emotion classification extends beyond traditional sentiment analysis by capturing nuanced emotional states such as joy, anger, fear, and surprise, rather than just polarity (positive, negative, neutral) \[[2005.00547](http://arxiv.org/abs/2005.00547)\]\[[2005.11882](http://arxiv.org/abs/2005.11882)\]. This granularity is especially valuable in domains like app reviews, where understanding specific emotional responses can provide deeper insights into user experience and satisfaction \[[1703.02256](http://arxiv.org/abs/1703.02256)\]\[[https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/6fe8d604e8e19dc6ed738c8e58b7351aeeed515c](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/6fe8d604e8e19dc6ed738c8e58b7351aeeed515c)\]. However, fine-grained emotion classification in app reviews remains relatively underexplored compared to general sentiment analysis and opinion mining, which have been more extensively studied across social media and other textual domains \[[https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/33c95f4d13958e65e676a9cd64bd4d8df0fdb44d](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/33c95f4d13958e65e676a9cd64bd4d8df0fdb44d)\]\[[https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/e0a7e1c24edc628b093daf4dca1dca86a583c377](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/e0a7e1c24edc628b093daf4dca1dca86a583c377)\].

Several large-scale datasets have been developed to support fine-grained emotion detection in text. Notably, GoEmotions offers 58,000 Reddit comments labeled for 27 emotion categories, demonstrating the feasibility of fine-grained annotation and serving as a benchmark for emotion prediction models \[[2005.00547](http://arxiv.org/abs/2005.00547)\]. Similarly, EmoNet presents a dataset and models for 24 fine-grained emotion types, achieving high accuracy, and extends to dimensional emotion modeling aligned with Plutchik’s taxonomy \[[https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/6a6f1b8ef3eefaf8027a76134859b44886a5f795](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/6a6f1b8ef3eefaf8027a76134859b44886a5f795)\]. These datasets, while not specific to app reviews, provide foundational resources and methodologies applicable to the domain. Furthermore, fine-grained emotion annotation frameworks incorporating emotion definitions and appraisal theories have shown promise in improving classification performance and interpretability \[[2107.12135](http://arxiv.org/abs/2107.12135)\]\[[2206.05238](http://arxiv.org/abs/2206.05238)\].

The challenges of emotion annotation, particularly its subjective nature and the complexity of emotional expression in text, have been recognized as key hurdles. Traditional manual annotation is costly, time-consuming, and prone to low inter-annotator agreement due to subjective interpretation \[[https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/7d0531aaaa4dbf4033da964289843e7812262702](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/7d0531aaaa4dbf4033da964289843e7812262702)\]\[[1308.6297](http://arxiv.org/abs/1308.6297)\]\[[2411.06160](http://arxiv.org/abs/2411.06160)\]. Recent work advocates for multi-label annotation to capture mixed emotions and emotional intensity, as demonstrated in micro-emotion detection frameworks like the Emotion Quantization Network (EQN) \[[2411.06160](http://arxiv.org/abs/2411.06160)\] and aesthetic emotion annotation in poetry \[[2003.07723](http://arxiv.org/abs/2003.07723)\]. These approaches highlight the importance of detailed annotation guidelines and iterative human annotation processes to improve reliability and data quality \[[https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/7d0531aaaa4dbf4033da964289843e7812262702](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/7d0531aaaa4dbf4033da964289843e7812262702)\]\[[2003.07723](http://arxiv.org/abs/2003.07723)\]\[[1810.05187](http://arxiv.org/abs/1810.05187)\].

In the context of app reviews, emotion analysis has been primarily approached through sentiment and feature-oriented sentiment rating, focusing on polarity and coarse-grained emotion detection to aid developers in understanding user feedback \[[1703.02256](http://arxiv.org/abs/1703.02256)\]\[[https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/6fe8d604e8e19dc6ed738c8e58b7351aeeed515c](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/6fe8d604e8e19dc6ed738c8e58b7351aeeed515c)\]. Approaches combining topic modeling with sentiment analysis have been effective in extracting feature-specific emotional information from noisy user reviews, facilitating better prioritization of app improvements \[[https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/6fe8d604e8e19dc6ed738c8e58b7351aeeed515c](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/6fe8d604e8e19dc6ed738c8e58b7351aeeed515c)\]\[[https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/bbdeae12a2a5e760cfacfa91b5a183396d749b24](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/bbdeae12a2a5e760cfacfa91b5a183396d749b24)\]. However, limited work has addressed the development of fine-grained emotion taxonomies and annotation frameworks tailored specifically for app review texts, which exhibit unique challenges such as informal language, mixed sentiments, and domain-specific content \[[https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/e0a7e1c24edc628b093daf4dca1dca86a583c377](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/e0a7e1c24edc628b093daf4dca1dca86a583c377)\]\[[2408.01063](http://arxiv.org/abs/2408.01063)\].

The emergence of Large Language Models (LLMs), particularly transformer-based architectures like GPT-4, has introduced new opportunities and challenges in automated emotion annotation. These models demonstrate strong performance in emotion recognition tasks and can significantly reduce manual annotation workload \[[2412.07906](http://arxiv.org/abs/2412.07906)\]. Studies show that LLMs can flag low-quality labels, assist human annotators, and enhance downstream learning efficiency, although discrepancies between human and model perceptions of emotion remain, underscoring the continued need for human oversight \[[2412.07906](http://arxiv.org/abs/2412.07906)\]\[[2408.01063](http://arxiv.org/abs/2408.01063)\]. Encoder-only LLMs have also been leveraged for feature extraction in app reviews, improving precision and recall by fine-tuning with domain-specific data and annotation strategies \[[2408.01063](http://arxiv.org/abs/2408.01063)\].

Additional research in opinion mining within software engineering has highlighted the importance of structured annotation guidelines and the impact of annotation quality on model performance, which directly relates to the difficulty of reliably extracting emotions from app reviews \[[https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/e0a7e1c24edc628b093daf4dca1dca86a583c377](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/e0a7e1c24edc628b093daf4dca1dca86a583c377)\]\[[1810.05187](http://arxiv.org/abs/1810.05187)\]. Systematic literature reviews emphasize the need for domain-adapted datasets and tools that can cope with noisy, subjective user-generated content \[[https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/e0a7e1c24edc628b093daf4dca1dca86a583c377](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/e0a7e1c24edc628b093daf4dca1dca86a583c377)\]. Furthermore, automated techniques for detecting specific user needs and concerns, such as privacy or explanation needs, demonstrate the potential for fine-grained emotion and intent analysis to support targeted app improvements \[[2307.04367](http://arxiv.org/abs/2307.04367)\]\[[2212.09289](http://arxiv.org/abs/2212.09289)\].

In summary, while sentiment analysis and general emotion recognition have matured with substantial datasets and advanced models, fine-grained emotion classification in app reviews is still nascent. Existing work in emotion annotation, large-scale fine-grained datasets, and LLM-assisted annotation provides valuable foundations. However, domain-specific annotation frameworks and datasets that address the unique linguistic and emotional complexities of app reviews are essential to advance this field \[[https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/7d0531aaaa4dbf4033da964289843e7812262702](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/7d0531aaaa4dbf4033da964289843e7812262702)\]\[[2412.07906](http://arxiv.org/abs/2412.07906)\]\[[2005.00547](http://arxiv.org/abs/2005.00547)\]\[[2107.12135](http://arxiv.org/abs/2107.12135)\]\[[2411.06160](http://arxiv.org/abs/2411.06160)\]\[[2408.01063](http://arxiv.org/abs/2408.01063)\].
