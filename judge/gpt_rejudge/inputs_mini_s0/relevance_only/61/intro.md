## Related Works

Personalized outfit generation centers on constructing fashion ensembles that are both visually compatible and tailored to individual user preferences. Traditional fashion recommendation systems typically focus on item compatibility or user preference modeling separately, but recent research emphasizes the joint optimization of compatibility, personalization, and diversity in generated outfits.

Early works on outfit recommendation mainly addressed compatibility and personalization through deep learning architectures. For instance, FashionNet combines feature extraction via convolutional networks with compatibility matching networks, employing a two-stage training approach to embed personal preferences in compatibility models \[[1810.02443](http://arxiv.org/abs/1810.02443)\]. Similarly, GP-BPR introduces a personalized compatibility modeling framework that accounts for both general item-item compatibility and user-item interactions using multimodal fashion data \[[https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/b1db62044adca574e4cf88b23c170f3cc089d513](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/b1db62044adca574e4cf88b23c170f3cc089d513)\]. Methods based on graph neural networks have been proposed to model outfit compatibility as a whole, capturing relationships among items and user preferences effectively \[[https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/c16dbfb1303c5dce657e29a84a12ee8a43caedcd](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/c16dbfb1303c5dce657e29a84a12ee8a43caedcd)\]. Regional attention mechanisms have further refined compatibility prediction by focusing on important item regions influenced by user preferences \[[https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/c1edd7ae1b786fd77c5fb04bd287bcc158796d4c](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/c1edd7ae1b786fd77c5fb04bd287bcc158796d4c)\].

The use of Transformer architectures has advanced outfit compatibility learning by modeling sets of items without ordering bias. OutfitTransformer leverages self-attention to learn relationships among outfit items and supports complementary item retrieval, enhancing the scalability and accuracy of fashion recommendations \[[https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/4d431b88f7dfe1e75b0e23bb21fc9dd378b15cf2](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/4d431b88f7dfe1e75b0e23bb21fc9dd378b15cf2)\]. In large-scale industrial contexts, models like POG connect user preferences with outfit generation through Transformer architectures and have been successfully deployed in Alibaba’s iFashion platform, demonstrating improvements in both compatibility and personalization \[[1905.01866](http://arxiv.org/abs/1905.01866)\].

Generative models have gained prominence for their ability to synthesize new fashion items and complete outfits. OutfitGAN uses a generative adversarial network combined with a compatibility network to produce realistic and compatible fashion items tailored to customer tastes \[[https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/61c15a1723d210ac75824bf185b547cee37c70af](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/61c15a1723d210ac75824bf185b547cee37c70af)\]. Extensions include personalized fashion design frameworks that employ GANs conditioned on latent user style vectors and compatibility discriminators, yielding high-quality, personalized items \[[https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/ca3ad34ff2652cd922c3476514a758674bc7e76b](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/ca3ad34ff2652cd922c3476514a758674bc7e76b)\]. Diffusion models have recently been introduced for generative outfit recommendation, enabling parallel generation of multiple fashion images with high fidelity and personalization via condition-guided diffusion and classifier-free guidance \[[2402.17279](http://arxiv.org/abs/2402.17279)\].

Diversity and style control in outfit generation have been addressed through various approaches. FCBoost-Net uses a boosting-inspired framework to generate multiple diversified and collocated outfit sets, improving compatibility progressively while maintaining diversity \[[2502.00992](http://arxiv.org/abs/2502.00992)\]. Style extraction modules embedded in generative models enable control over global outfit themes, allowing the generation of outfits aligned with specific styles or occasions \[[1807.03133](http://arxiv.org/abs/1807.03133)\]\[[2205.00663](http://arxiv.org/abs/2205.00663)\]. Multimodal embeddings that jointly represent images and textual descriptions have been exploited to generate coherent outfits matching user queries or themes, combining item-item compatibility with item-query coherence \[[1906.07273](http://arxiv.org/abs/1906.07273)\].

The integration of multimodal data, especially combining visual and textual features, enhances personalization and the semantic understanding of fashion items. Decoding style through fine-tuned large language models (LLMs) with image captioning bridges the visual-textual gap, enabling personalized outfit recommendation that adapts to seasonal trends and user preferences via direct preference feedback loops \[[2409.12150](http://arxiv.org/abs/2409.12150)\]. Similarly, fashion recommendation systems are increasingly incorporating contextual factors like occasion and climate in addition to outfit synergies to enhance relevance \[[https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/908aee5be8e926b8d73e32695a53888374267739](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/908aee5be8e926b8d73e32695a53888374267739)\].

Direct preference optimization (DPO) has emerged as an effective fine-tuning strategy for aligning generative models with human preferences without relying on complex reinforcement learning or manually designed reward functions. Initially developed for language models to optimize pairwise preference data, DPO has demonstrated superior stability and performance compared to traditional RLHF methods \[[2305.18290](http://arxiv.org/abs/2305.18290)\]\[[2402.10571](http://arxiv.org/abs/2402.10571)\]. Though primarily explored in NLP and 3D generation domains \[[2502.04370](http://arxiv.org/abs/2502.04370)\], the principles of DPO provide a promising avenue for fashion generative models, enabling fine-grained controllability and preference alignment without handcrafted rewards.

Previous fashion recommendation frameworks often rely on supervised learning with labeled data or explicit reward signals, which can constrain diversity and generalization. Incorporating DPO enables learning from preference comparisons potentially generated automatically or from user interaction data, thus addressing the limitations of standard supervised paradigms. Multi-expert feedback modules that evaluate outfit quality, compatibility, and personalization from diverse perspectives further enrich the feedback signals for model refinement \[[2504.09915](http://arxiv.org/abs/2504.09915)\].

In summary, personalized outfit generation has evolved from compatibility and preference modeling using deep neural networks and Transformers to generative frameworks empowered by GANs and diffusion models. Incorporating multimodal data, style control, and diversity remains critical. Recent advances in direct preference optimization offer a flexible and efficient fine-tuning methodology that enhances personalization and compatibility alignment without the need for explicit reward engineering, positioning frameworks like FashionDPO at the forefront of this research area \[[2402.17279](http://arxiv.org/abs/2402.17279)\]\[[https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/61c15a1723d210ac75824bf185b547cee37c70af](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/61c15a1723d210ac75824bf185b547cee37c70af)\]\[[https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/ca3ad34ff2652cd922c3476514a758674bc7e76b](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/ca3ad34ff2652cd922c3476514a758674bc7e76b)\]\[[1905.01866](http://arxiv.org/abs/1905.01866)\]\[[https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/c16dbfb1303c5dce657e29a84a12ee8a43caedcd](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/c16dbfb1303c5dce657e29a84a12ee8a43caedcd)\]\[[https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/4d431b88f7dfe1e75b0e23bb21fc9dd378b15cf2](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/4d431b88f7dfe1e75b0e23bb21fc9dd378b15cf2)\]\[[2504.09915](http://arxiv.org/abs/2504.09915)\]\[[2502.00992](http://arxiv.org/abs/2502.00992)\]\[[2409.12150](http://arxiv.org/abs/2409.12150)\]\[[2305.18290](http://arxiv.org/abs/2305.18290)\].
