## Related Works

Neural rendering has become a pivotal approach for reconstructing 3D scenes from 2D images by optimizing implicit neural representations that encode geometry and appearance continuously across multiple views \[[2309.15164](http://arxiv.org/abs/2309.15164)\]. Traditional multi-view reconstruction techniques rely heavily on the availability of numerous input views, which limits reconstruction quality, especially in dynamic or complex scenes where some viewpoints remain unobserved \[[2503.18897](http://arxiv.org/abs/2503.18897)\]\[[2106.10689](http://arxiv.org/abs/2106.10689)\]. Addressing this view sparsity is critical, motivating the integration of data augmentation strategies such as video frame interpolation to synthesize intermediate views and enrich supervision signals for neural rendering.

Video frame interpolation (VFI) aims to generate intermediate frames between given consecutive frames, effectively increasing temporal resolution and enabling more comprehensive scene coverage. Classical VFI methods typically involve two distinct stages—motion estimation (often via optical flow) and frame synthesis—which can be prone to artifacts in scenarios with large motions, occlusions, or illumination changes \[[1703.07514](http://arxiv.org/abs/1703.07514)\]\[[1708.01692](http://arxiv.org/abs/1708.01692)\]. Recent advances employ end-to-end deep learning architectures that unify motion estimation and pixel synthesis, improving robustness and visual fidelity \[[1712.00080](http://arxiv.org/abs/1712.00080)\]\[[1703.07514](http://arxiv.org/abs/1703.07514)\]\[[https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/3ce9d75cd6e16bcca9c649a869070ece0ca4a7d7](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/3ce9d75cd6e16bcca9c649a869070ece0ca4a7d7)\]. For instance, the Super SloMo framework jointly estimates bi-directional flows and visibility maps to handle occlusion and achieve smooth multi-frame interpolation \[[1712.00080](http://arxiv.org/abs/1712.00080)\]. Similarly, adaptive convolutional kernel methods reduce parameter complexity while preserving interpolation quality \[[1708.01692](http://arxiv.org/abs/1708.01692)\]\[[1703.07514](http://arxiv.org/abs/1703.07514)\].

High-resolution and multi-frame interpolation present additional challenges in computational efficiency and accuracy. Lightweight feature extraction and dimensionality reduction techniques have been proposed to reduce memory demands while maintaining performance on 4K videos \[[2211.14005](http://arxiv.org/abs/2211.14005)\]\[[https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/089f2072c95c244f3b7f4df404562bb4afe24448](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/089f2072c95c244f3b7f4df404562bb4afe24448)\]. Multi-scale feature and correlation volume frameworks further enhance motion understanding, particularly in complex scenes with large displacements \[[https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/298286710941a42b399ab95f6a4367067e8cf18f](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/298286710941a42b399ab95f6a4367067e8cf18f)\]. Transformer-based models introduce content-aware aggregation and long-range dependencies, achieving superior interpolation quality by leveraging spatial-temporal self-attention \[[2111.13817](http://arxiv.org/abs/2111.13817)\]. Additionally, many-to-many splatting strategies and spatial selective refinement mechanisms enable efficient and accurate multi-frame interpolation by adapting computation to challenging regions \[[2310.18946](http://arxiv.org/abs/2310.18946)\].

Incorporating video frame interpolation into neural rendering pipelines addresses the limitation of sparse input views by generating synthetic intermediate frames that serve as augmented supervision. This approach implicitly models camera motion and scene geometry, enriching the training data and improving 3D reconstruction outcomes on both static and dynamic scenes \[[2203.00137](http://arxiv.org/abs/2203.00137)\]\[[2207.10985](http://arxiv.org/abs/2207.10985)\]. Methods like PS4PRO leverage diverse video datasets to learn implicit world priors, facilitating photorealistic rendering and optimization. Such interpolation-based augmentation complements neural implicit surface reconstruction techniques—including NeuS and its accelerated variant NeuS2—that aim for high-fidelity geometry recovery but remain constrained by input view scarcity \[[2106.10689](http://arxiv.org/abs/2106.10689)\]\[[2212.05231](http://arxiv.org/abs/2212.05231)\]. 

Beyond RGB-based interpolation, event-based frame interpolation leverages complementary motion cues from event cameras, enhancing performance in challenging lighting or motion conditions and further broadening data augmentation possibilities for neural rendering \[[2203.17191](http://arxiv.org/abs/2203.17191)\]\[[https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/9c67428b034e6eb16184724c694a9cf9091bb962](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/9c67428b034e6eb16184724c694a9cf9091bb962)\]. Unsupervised video interpolation techniques that rely on cycle consistency and pseudo-supervised losses also provide scalable ways to improve interpolation quality without requiring high frame-rate ground truth data \[[1906.05928](http://arxiv.org/abs/1906.05928)\]\[[1603.06041](http://arxiv.org/abs/1603.06041)\].

Recent neural 3D reconstruction approaches exploit priors and feature interpolation strategies to improve scene completeness and reconstruction accuracy. For example, feature grid interpolation and object libraries enable continuous updates of object-centric neural implicit models during online 3D scene reconstruction \[[2503.18897](http://arxiv.org/abs/2503.18897)\]. Generalizable neural fields trained with scene priors allow efficient reconstruction from fewer views without scene-specific retraining \[[2309.15164](http://arxiv.org/abs/2309.15164)\]. Regularization techniques incorporating depth and normal priors, along with exposure compensation, have been proposed to tackle large indoor scene reconstruction challenges \[[2309.07021](http://arxiv.org/abs/2309.07021)\].

Overall, the integration of advanced video frame interpolation techniques into neural rendering workflows addresses fundamental limitations in view availability. By generating high-quality, temporally consistent intermediate frames, these methods enrich photometric supervision, facilitate better geometry and appearance optimization, and improve reconstruction fidelity in complex and dynamic scenes. The lightweight and efficient architectures, such as PS4PRO, contribute to practical applicability in scalable neural rendering systems \[[2203.00137](http://arxiv.org/abs/2203.00137)\]\[[2211.14005](http://arxiv.org/abs/2211.14005)\]\[[1712.00080](http://arxiv.org/abs/1712.00080)\]\[[2310.18946](http://arxiv.org/abs/2310.18946)\].
