## Related Works

Protein dynamics are fundamental to biological function, yet their accurate characterization remains challenging due to the computational intensity of traditional molecular dynamics (MD) simulations \[[2402.04845](http://arxiv.org/abs/2402.04845)\]. Recent advances in generative modeling, especially denoising diffusion models, have shown promise in efficiently sampling protein conformational ensembles by learning distributions over experimentally derived structures \[[2004.13167](http://arxiv.org/abs/2004.13167)\]\[[2304.02198](http://arxiv.org/abs/2304.02198)\]. These approaches can generate diverse and physically plausible conformations faster than classical MD, addressing throughput limitations inherent in simulation-based methods \[[https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/8fa4f6b5232868c2e400670716540f6877068439](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/8fa4f6b5232868c2e400670716540f6877068439)\]\[[https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/21a4afa74caf78e28880b7fe4c30a09a5f9c14e3](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/21a4afa74caf78e28880b7fe4c30a09a5f9c14e3)\].

Energy-based modeling has played a significant role in capturing protein conformational landscapes. Atomic-level energy-based models (EBMs) trained solely on crystallographic data can achieve performance comparable to sophisticated physics-based energy functions like Rosetta, underscoring the potential of learned energy landscapes in protein design and prediction \[[2004.13167](http://arxiv.org/abs/2004.13167)\]. However, conventional energy objectives often lead to intractable optimization when integrated into generative frameworks, motivating alternative strategies to incorporate physical insights effectively \[[2503.14569](http://arxiv.org/abs/2503.14569)\].

Several recent works have integrated physical priors or guidance into deep generative models to improve the fidelity and realism of generated protein ensembles. Force-guided SE(3) equivariant diffusion models, such as ConfDiff, leverage physical force information to steer sampling towards equilibrium distributions, resulting in conformations with enhanced structural diversity and accuracy compared to unguided diffusion approaches \[[2403.14088](http://arxiv.org/abs/2403.14088)\]\[[2411.17196](http://arxiv.org/abs/2411.17196)\]. Similarly, P2DFlow employs SE(3) flow matching with a physically informed prior to generate ensembles that capture dynamic fluctuations consistent with MD data, highlighting the value of embedding physical laws within the generative process \[[2411.17196](http://arxiv.org/abs/2411.17196)\].

Diffusion-based frameworks have been further extended to model conformational heterogeneity and dynamics. EigenFold introduces a harmonic oscillator–based diffusion process decomposed along eigenmodes of the protein, enabling efficient sampling of structural ensembles that reflect intrinsic flexibility and conformational transitions \[[2304.02198](http://arxiv.org/abs/2304.02198)\]. DeepConformer and other diffusion generative models trained on MD trajectories have demonstrated the ability to reproduce both static and temperature-dependent ensemble properties, capturing experimentally observed variations without explicit physics simulations during training \[[https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/43389c0e79b5db0b210bef998f867910db9d8d44](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/43389c0e79b5db0b210bef998f867910db9d8d44)\]\[[https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/bcf4f0cd55f0888b80c2bf7d47969aa34b222a99](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/bcf4f0cd55f0888b80c2bf7d47969aa34b222a99)\]. More recent 4D diffusion models extend these capabilities by concurrently modeling temporal protein trajectories, improving the prediction of dynamic conformational changes over multiple time steps \[[2408.12419](http://arxiv.org/abs/2408.12419)\].

Alternative machine learning architectures have also contributed to conformational ensemble generation. Transformer-based diffusion and language models, such as Angular Deviation Diffuser and Structure Language Modeling (SLM), use geometric and sequence-conditioned latent representations to efficiently generate diverse protein conformations with improved runtime performance and interpretability \[[https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/5d2b69b7ba59017fbf7fed60ffe85165b3207e57](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/5d2b69b7ba59017fbf7fed60ffe85165b3207e57)\]\[[2410.18403](http://arxiv.org/abs/2410.18403)\]. These methods exemplify how integrating sequence and structural information can facilitate exploration of conformational space beyond static predictions.

The challenge of balancing physical realism and generative efficiency has motivated hybrid approaches combining AI and MD simulations. Few-shot fine-tuning of pre-trained generative samplers using short MD trajectories enables target-specific adaptation, enhancing sample quality while maintaining computational tractability \[[2402.10433](http://arxiv.org/abs/2402.10433)\]. Potential Score Matching further improves sampling accuracy by incorporating approximate potential energy gradients to debias generated distributions towards the Boltzmann equilibrium, addressing deficiencies in purely data-driven diffusion models \[[2503.14569](http://arxiv.org/abs/2503.14569)\].

Reviews emphasize the growing trend of integrating AI-driven generative modeling with traditional physical methods and experimental data to capture Boltzmann-weighted protein ensembles. These approaches promise to accelerate structural biology and drug discovery by providing scalable, accurate conformational sampling frameworks that complement or even supplant classical MD \[[https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/934892bbb74d9cac69061977f5937eba6fd8da52](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/934892bbb74d9cac69061977f5937eba6fd8da52)\]\[[https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/c7c22781ba155b04f7350d3b5be07dbfbfbabb1d](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/c7c22781ba155b04f7350d3b5be07dbfbfbabb1d)\]\[[2501.02680](http://arxiv.org/abs/2501.02680)\]\[[https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/a8953e3489f502ce2397b91b4e53430474416134](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/a8953e3489f502ce2397b91b4e53430474416134)\].

In summary, the field has witnessed significant progress in developing generative models that incorporate physical knowledge through energy-based objectives, equivariant architectures, force guidance, and hybrid simulation fine-tuning. These advances have collectively improved the accuracy, diversity, and efficiency of protein conformational ensemble generation, setting the stage for methods like Energy-based Alignment (EBA), which aim to calibrate generative models directly with physical energy feedback to balance conformational states effectively.
