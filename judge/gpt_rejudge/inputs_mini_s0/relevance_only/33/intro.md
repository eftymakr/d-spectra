## Related Works

Rehearsal-based methods are a cornerstone of continual learning (CL) approaches aimed at mitigating catastrophic forgetting by storing and revisiting a subset of past data alongside new task data during training \[[2104.07446](http://arxiv.org/abs/2104.07446)\]\[[2010.05595](http://arxiv.org/abs/2010.05595)\]\[[1902.10486](http://arxiv.org/abs/1902.10486)\]. The dominant paradigm involves concurrent rehearsal, where old and new samples are jointly trained, which has shown strong empirical performance across various benchmarks \[[2104.07446](http://arxiv.org/abs/2104.07446)\]\[[2010.05595](http://arxiv.org/abs/2010.05595)\]. However, despite their success, these methods have limitations, such as potential overfitting to the limited rehearsal buffer and challenges in generalization, especially when tasks differ substantially \[[2104.07446](http://arxiv.org/abs/2104.07446)\]\[[https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/8eaed8eafb25d5c41cf0fce53d276765ddded613](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/8eaed8eafb25d5c41cf0fce53d276765ddded613)\].

Recent theoretical advances have begun to elucidate the dynamics of rehearsal in overparameterized linear models, offering explicit characterizations of forgetting and generalization error under different rehearsal schemes \[[2302.05836](http://arxiv.org/abs/2302.05836)\]\[[2305.00316](http://arxiv.org/abs/2305.00316)\]. Notably, sequential rehearsal—training first on new task data followed by revisiting past data—has been theoretically shown to outperform concurrent rehearsal when task similarity is low, due to better navigation of the loss landscape and reduced interference between tasks \[[2302.05836](http://arxiv.org/abs/2302.05836)\]. This contrasts with the conventional assumption that concurrent rehearsal is always optimal and aligns with insights from neuroscience on memory consolidation and re-consolidation processes that occur sequentially \[[https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/8eaed8eafb25d5c41cf0fce53d276765ddded613](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/8eaed8eafb25d5c41cf0fce53d276765ddded613)\].

Hybrid rehearsal strategies that combine concurrent training for similar tasks and sequential revisiting for dissimilar tasks have been proposed and empirically validated, demonstrating improved forgetting mitigation and generalization compared to purely concurrent rehearsal \[[https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/8eaed8eafb25d5c41cf0fce53d276765ddded613](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/8eaed8eafb25d5c41cf0fce53d276765ddded613)\]\[[2307.02251](http://arxiv.org/abs/2307.02251)\]\[[2410.07812](http://arxiv.org/abs/2410.07812)\]. These approaches draw inspiration from brain-inspired models that distill robust features and re-consolidate memories, thereby addressing the limitations of rehearsal methods reliant on pre-trained oracle models or static memory buffers \[[https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/8eaed8eafb25d5c41cf0fce53d276765ddded613](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/8eaed8eafb25d5c41cf0fce53d276765ddded613)\]. Empirical studies with deep neural networks confirm that hybrid rehearsal can harness the benefits of both strategies, adapting dynamically to task relationships \[[https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/8eaed8eafb25d5c41cf0fce53d276765ddded613](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/8eaed8eafb25d5c41cf0fce53d276765ddded613)\]\[[2410.07812](http://arxiv.org/abs/2410.07812)\].

Beyond rehearsal, alternative CL methods have been explored. Regularization-based approaches such as Elastic Weight Consolidation (EWC) constrain important parameters to prevent forgetting, but often require careful tuning and may be less effective when tasks are highly dissimilar or when no rehearsal buffer is allowed \[[2209.08660](http://arxiv.org/abs/2209.08660)\]\[[2203.17269](http://arxiv.org/abs/2203.17269)\]. Knowledge distillation and parameter regularization techniques have also been investigated as rehearsal-free strategies, with mixed results depending on task complexity and model architecture \[[2203.17269](http://arxiv.org/abs/2203.17269)\]. Bayesian methods and task-conditioned hypernetworks offer principled frameworks to model task-specific parameters and uncertainty, enabling flexible continual learning without direct data rehearsal \[[2103.01133](http://arxiv.org/abs/2103.01133)\]\[[1906.00695](http://arxiv.org/abs/1906.00695)\]. Variational continual learning with temporal-difference objectives further improves stability by integrating multiple posterior estimates, mitigating compounding approximation errors \[[2410.07812](http://arxiv.org/abs/2410.07812)\].

Memory management and rehearsal buffer curation are critical to rehearsal efficiency. Gradient-matching coresets that select samples to closely approximate original training gradients improve rehearsal effectiveness without increasing memory size \[[2203.14544](http://arxiv.org/abs/2203.14544)\]. Prototype-guided replay and replay scheduling policies learned via reinforcement learning have also been proposed to optimize which samples or tasks to replay and when, balancing computational cost and forgetting \[[2209.08660](http://arxiv.org/abs/2209.08660)\]\[[2108.12641](http://arxiv.org/abs/2108.12641)\]. Furthermore, pseudo-rehearsal techniques generate synthetic data via generative models or self-synthesized rehearsal to alleviate data storage constraints, particularly in domains like large language models where original training data may be unavailable \[[1812.02464](http://arxiv.org/abs/1812.02464)\]\[[2403.01244](http://arxiv.org/abs/2403.01244)\].

The utility of pre-trained foundation models in continual learning is an active area of research. Exploiting latent representations and random projections can reduce forgetting without explicit rehearsal buffers, offering efficient alternatives that leverage pretrained feature spaces \[[2307.02251](http://arxiv.org/abs/2307.02251)\]\[[2205.00329](http://arxiv.org/abs/2205.00329)\]. Studies highlight the importance of robust feature learning—features less susceptible to adversarial perturbations—as a mechanism to enhance continual learning robustness and mitigate forgetting \[[2306.17091](http://arxiv.org/abs/2306.17091)\]\[[https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/8eaed8eafb25d5c41cf0fce53d276765ddded613](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/8eaed8eafb25d5c41cf0fce53d276765ddded613)\]. These findings underscore the role of feature quality and task similarity in rehearsal effectiveness.

Finally, the consideration of task similarity and sequence structure in continual learning is gaining attention. Models that dynamically detect task similarity to selectively transfer knowledge forward and backward have been introduced, addressing mixed sequences of similar and dissimilar tasks \[[2112.10017](http://arxiv.org/abs/2112.10017)\]. Repetition in data streams, common in real-world scenarios, also influences rehearsal dynamics, with ensemble-based and repetition-aware strategies showing promise \[[2405.04101](http://arxiv.org/abs/2405.04101)\]\[[2207.04543](http://arxiv.org/abs/2207.04543)\].

In summary, while concurrent rehearsal remains a widely adopted method in CL, theoretical and empirical evidence increasingly supports the benefits of sequential and hybrid rehearsal strategies, especially when accounting for task similarity. Advances in memory selection, synthetic data generation, and leveraging pre-trained models complement rehearsal approaches, broadening the toolkit for effective continual learning. These insights motivate the exploration of adaptive rehearsal schedules that align with task characteristics to better mitigate forgetting and improve generalization.
