## Related Works

The problem of testing transactional isolation levels in databases has received significant attention, especially due to the trade-offs between isolation strength, performance, and correctness. Strong isolation levels such as serializability (SER) and strict serializability are known to be NP-complete to verify, which has motivated research focus on weaker isolation levels that are both widely used in practice and amenable to more efficient testing \[[2504.02344](http://arxiv.org/abs/2504.02344)\]. Weak isolation levels, including Read Committed (RC), Read Atomic (RA), and Causal Consistency (CC), have recently been shown to admit polynomial-time testing algorithms, enabling scalability improvements over strong isolation checkers \[[https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/cbe191bda20907994d45bc835d20446968370add](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/cbe191bda20907994d45bc835d20446968370add)\].

Black-box testing techniques, which do not rely on access to internal database mechanisms, have been the dominant approach for detecting isolation bugs in production systems. Early tools focused on strong isolation levels, employing randomized workloads and complex dependency graph analysis, but suffered from high overhead and limited scalability \[[2504.02344](http://arxiv.org/abs/2504.02344)\]\[[2003.10554](http://arxiv.org/abs/2003.10554)\]. For example, Elle infers Adya-style dependency graphs from client observations to detect anomalies, providing sound and informative counterexamples but still facing efficiency challenges on large workloads \[[2003.10554](http://arxiv.org/abs/2003.10554)\]. Emme improves on Elle by exploiting version certificate recovery, enabling more efficient white-box checks including support for predicate operations, but remains less practical for very large histories \[[https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/04178561c9d8cd42904ed0a836e08e55b84703cf](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/04178561c9d8cd42904ed0a836e08e55b84703cf)\].

Snapshot Isolation (SI), a popular weak isolation level, has been the subject of several specialized checkers. PolySI uses generalized polygraphs and SMT solvers to achieve sound and complete black-box SI checking with domain-specific optimizations to prune constraints, outperforming prior work and scaling to large workloads \[[2301.07313](http://arxiv.org/abs/2301.07313)\]. Viper introduces BC-polygraphs—a novel transaction dependency representation—achieving sound, complete, and fast SI checking, including support for SI variants, and demonstrates significant speedups over baselines \[[https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/9f96a48d2bc0e77004b32960495685be6c301c63](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/9f96a48d2bc0e77004b32960495685be6c301c63)\]. Chronos and its online extensions, Aion and Aion-SER, leverage timestamp-based dependency tracking to enable scalable offline and online checking of SI and serializability, processing millions of transactions efficiently \[[2504.01477](http://arxiv.org/abs/2504.01477)\]. These efforts highlight the challenges and solutions specific to SI, a middle ground between strong and weak isolation.

For weaker isolation levels than SI, recent work has focused on efficiency and completeness in black-box checking. Plume characterizes weak isolation levels such as RC, RA, and CC using modular anomalous patterns and accelerates checking with vector and tree clock techniques. It detects known and novel isolation bugs in production databases and outperforms prior state-of-the-art weak isolation checkers \[[https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/cbe191bda20907994d45bc835d20446968370add](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/cbe191bda20907994d45bc835d20446968370add)\]. IsoVista offers a comprehensive black-box checking framework that covers multiple isolation levels with faithful characterizations, efficient checking, and user-friendly visualization of anomalies to aid debugging \[[https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/4f3081dbb68287f0d9d7bec3e7173e6c8ff035b9](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/4f3081dbb68287f0d9d7bec3e7173e6c8ff035b9)\]. IsoDiff targets the debugging of application-level anomalies under weak isolation by identifying representative subsets of anomalies and reducing false positives through correlation detection and developer feedback, balancing coverage and computation time effectively \[[https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/405ae0ebf74cfbf5f2edec62e2966827dcdc9bef](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/405ae0ebf74cfbf5f2edec62e2966827dcdc9bef)\].

Theoretical foundations underpinning these testing tools have been advanced by formalizing isolation levels in separation logic and reasoning frameworks. For instance, recent formalizations define weak isolation levels such as Read Uncommitted, Read Committed, and Snapshot Isolation in a modular way independent of implementation, with mechanized proofs of realizability and implication relations among them \[[2501.14421](http://arxiv.org/abs/2501.14421)\]. Additionally, model checking techniques using dynamic partial order reduction have been proposed to verify application correctness against weak isolation, providing sound and complete algorithms with polynomial memory consumption, further bridging theory and practice \[[2303.12606](http://arxiv.org/abs/2303.12606)\].

Moreover, testing frameworks that generate workloads reflecting weak isolation semantics have been developed. MonkeyDB provides a mock storage system that uses logical isolation specifications to generate possible read return values, enabling effective testing of application robustness under weak isolation with good coverage and completeness in the limit \[[2103.02830](http://arxiv.org/abs/2103.02830)\]\[[https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/615ff2896b52bee2cbfd64ced324106a29b73397](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/615ff2896b52bee2cbfd64ced324106a29b73397)\].

Despite these advances, existing testers for weak isolation levels still exhibit polynomial complexities that pose challenges for very large workloads typical in modern distributed databases. The recent work of AWDIT improves upon these by achieving sub-quadratic time complexities—$O(n^{3/2})$ for RC and RA, and $O(n \cdot k)$ for CC—while proving essential optimality under conditional lower bounds. This represents a significant step forward in scalability and efficiency over prior polynomial-time algorithms \[[https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/cbe191bda20907994d45bc835d20446968370add](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/cbe191bda20907994d45bc835d20446968370add)\]\[[https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/4f3081dbb68287f0d9d7bec3e7173e6c8ff035b9](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/4f3081dbb68287f0d9d7bec3e7173e6c8ff035b9)\]\[[2303.12606](http://arxiv.org/abs/2303.12606)\].

In summary, the literature demonstrates a progressive evolution from NP-complete strong isolation checking toward scalable polynomial-time weak isolation testing frameworks, with improvements in both theoretical characterization and practical efficiency. The integration of advanced dependency representations, formal methods, and workload generation techniques continues to drive the development of highly efficient and sound isolation testing tools suitable for the large-scale, high-throughput environments of modern databases \[[2504.02344](http://arxiv.org/abs/2504.02344)\]\[[https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/cbe191bda20907994d45bc835d20446968370add](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/cbe191bda20907994d45bc835d20446968370add)\]\[[2301.07313](http://arxiv.org/abs/2301.07313)\]\[[2003.10554](http://arxiv.org/abs/2003.10554)\]\[[https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/9f96a48d2bc0e77004b32960495685be6c301c63](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/9f96a48d2bc0e77004b32960495685be6c301c63)\]\[[https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/4f3081dbb68287f0d9d7bec3e7173e6c8ff035b9](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/4f3081dbb68287f0d9d7bec3e7173e6c8ff035b9)\]\[[https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/405ae0ebf74cfbf5f2edec62e2966827dcdc9bef](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/405ae0ebf74cfbf5f2edec62e2966827dcdc9bef)\]\[[2303.12606](http://arxiv.org/abs/2303.12606)\]\[[https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/04178561c9d8cd42904ed0a836e08e55b84703cf](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/04178561c9d8cd42904ed0a836e08e55b84703cf)\]\[[2504.01477](http://arxiv.org/abs/2504.01477)\]\[[2501.14421](http://arxiv.org/abs/2501.14421)\]\[[2103.02830](http://arxiv.org/abs/2103.02830)\]\[[https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/615ff2896b52bee2cbfd64ced324106a29b73397](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/615ff2896b52bee2cbfd64ced324106a29b73397)\].
