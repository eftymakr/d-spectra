## Related Works

Dense retrieval has become a fundamental approach in information retrieval, relying on pre-trained language models (PLMs) such as BERT and LLaMA to encode queries and documents into dense vector representations, which are then compared via similarity measures like inner product \[[2208.09846](http://arxiv.org/abs/2208.09846)\]. The effectiveness of dense retrievers hinges on the quality of text representations learned during pre-training and fine-tuning. A growing body of work has focused on understanding and improving the interplay between these two stages.

Early investigations into dense retrieval emphasize the importance of pre-training with architectures tailored for dense encoding. For instance, Condenser introduced a pre-training paradigm where language model predictions condition explicitly on dense representations, addressing the limitations of standard transformers for dense retrieval tasks \[[2104.08253](http://arxiv.org/abs/2104.08253)\]. Similarly, RetroMAE proposed a masked auto-encoder pre-training approach with asymmetric masking and model components, leading to improved retrieval performance on benchmarks such as BEIR and MS MARCO \[[2205.12035](http://arxiv.org/abs/2205.12035)\]. MASTER extended this line by integrating multiple pre-training tasks into a bottlenecked masked autoencoder framework, improving semantic compression and retrieval quality \[[2212.07841](http://arxiv.org/abs/2212.07841)\]. These efforts collectively highlight that pre-training can embed rich retrieval-relevant knowledge into encoders, which fine-tuning then leverages.

The discriminative power of learned representations has also been targeted through contrastive learning techniques. Contrastive pre-training frameworks, such as those using relevance-aware pseudo-positive pairs or span prediction, have been shown to suppress common word signals while emphasizing representative tokens, leading to better retrieval embeddings \[[2208.09846](http://arxiv.org/abs/2208.09846)\]\[[2306.03166](http://arxiv.org/abs/2306.03166)\]\[[2204.10641](http://arxiv.org/abs/2204.10641)\]. Moreover, unsupervised contrastive learning approaches have demonstrated strong retrieval results in zero-shot or low-resource settings, often outperforming traditional sparse methods like BM25 \[[2112.09118](http://arxiv.org/abs/2112.09118)\]. These works suggest that contrastive learning during pre-training is crucial to obtaining discriminative encoders that generalize well.

Fine-tuning, traditionally viewed as the primary mechanism for adapting PLMs to retrieval tasks, has been re-examined in recent studies. Some analyses argue that fine-tuning mainly adjusts activation patterns rather than reorganizing or injecting new retrieval knowledge, which is predominantly acquired during pre-training \[[2108.05540](http://arxiv.org/abs/2108.05540)\]\[[2010.02616](http://arxiv.org/abs/2010.02616)\]. For example, in BERT-based DPR models, fine-tuning alters neuron activations without substantial changes to the underlying representations, indicating that pre-trained knowledge forms the foundation of retrieval effectiveness \[[2108.05540](http://arxiv.org/abs/2108.05540)\]. However, this phenomenon does not universally apply; mean-pooled models like Contriever and decoder-based models such as LLaMA show greater fine-tuning impact on retrieval performance, suggesting architecture and representation strategy influence the role of fine-tuning \[[2002.08909](http://arxiv.org/abs/2002.08909)\]\[[2312.15503](http://arxiv.org/abs/2312.15503)\].

Further, the choice of representation pooling strategies affects dense retrieval. While [CLS]-token-based representations are widespread, alternatives such as aggregating token embeddings or sentence-level embeddings have been proposed to mitigate issues like representation anisotropy and information loss from truncation. Aggretriever, for instance, aggregates contextualized token embeddings to improve robustness without heavy fine-tuning overhead \[[2208.00511](http://arxiv.org/abs/2208.00511)\], and EASE-DR employs variational auto-encoder latent sampling with contrastive learning to create isotropic sentence embeddings, enhancing retrieval quality \[[https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/c7f8acb0bdae40eeb0505bf87744fa29270073ac](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/c7f8acb0bdae40eeb0505bf87744fa29270073ac)\].

The relationship between pre-training and fine-tuning is nuanced. Studies show that continual pre-training can improve a model’s latent knowledge, which fine-tuning then unveils, but fine-tuning may also lead to forgetting domain knowledge or reduce linguistic knowledge encoded during pre-training \[[2010.02616](http://arxiv.org/abs/2010.02616)\]\[[2408.06663](http://arxiv.org/abs/2408.06663)\]. Parameter-efficient fine-tuning methods like LoRA have been explored to stabilize fine-tuning and reduce computational cost, while maintaining or improving retrieval effectiveness \[[https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/2f184d2e7182dd69dfb708aa088ada7abc47597d](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/2f184d2e7182dd69dfb708aa088ada7abc47597d)\]. Additionally, retrieval-augmented generation (RAG) approaches combine retrieval with language models to inject knowledge dynamically, often outperforming unsupervised fine-tuning in knowledge-intensive tasks \[[2312.05934](http://arxiv.org/abs/2312.05934)\].

Large language models (LLMs) have been leveraged both as retrievers and as data augmentation tools for training smaller retrievers, bridging the gap between efficiency and generalization. Frameworks like DRAMA utilize pruned LLMs to generate diverse training data for smaller dense retrievers, improving multilingual and long-context retrieval capabilities \[[2502.18460](http://arxiv.org/abs/2502.18460)\]. ExpandR jointly optimizes LLM query expansions and retriever training, demonstrating that alignment between generation and ranking objectives enhances retrieval effectiveness \[[2502.17057](http://arxiv.org/abs/2502.17057)\]. Moreover, unsupervised adaptation methods such as Llama2Vec enable effective initialization of LLMs for dense retrieval via embedding-based auto-encoding and regression, achieving state-of-the-art results on various benchmarks \[[2312.15503](http://arxiv.org/abs/2312.15503)\].

In summary, recent advances underscore that while pre-training embeds essential retrieval knowledge, fine-tuning’s role varies with model architecture, representation method, and task. Contrastive learning, bottlenecked autoencoder structures, and innovative pooling strategies improve retrieval embeddings during pre-training. Meanwhile, fine-tuning may primarily adjust model activations in some settings but can also significantly shape retrieval capability in others, especially with decoder-based or mean-pooled models. This complex interplay motivates continued research into unified frameworks that integrate pre-training, fine-tuning, and retrieval augmentation for dense retrieval systems.
