## Related Works

Collaborative filtering (CF) has increasingly leveraged graph-based representation learning to capture complex user-item interactions beyond traditional matrix factorization methods. Early influential methods like SVD++ incorporated user-side implicit feedback to enhance recommendation accuracy but neglected item-side implicit signals and treated all implicit feedback uniformly, limiting expressiveness \[[1803.03502](http://arxiv.org/abs/1803.03502)\]. To overcome this, graph-based collaborative filtering methods model the bipartite user-item interaction graph directly, allowing flexible weighting of implicit feedback on both user and item sides, thereby improving recommendation quality, especially in sparse settings \[[1803.03502](http://arxiv.org/abs/1803.03502)\].

Graph Neural Networks (GNNs) have become central to modern CF by enabling high-order connectivity modeling and embedding propagation through user-item graphs. Neural Graph Collaborative Filtering (NGCF) explicitly propagates embeddings along the bipartite graph to inject collaborative signals, achieving notable improvements over prior methods \[[1905.08108](http://arxiv.org/abs/1905.08108)\]. Multi-Graph Convolution Collaborative Filtering (Multi-GCCF) further extends this by integrating multiple graph structures—user-item, user-user, and item-item—to incorporate both interaction and similarity information, leading to better embeddings and recommendation accuracy \[[2001.00267](http://arxiv.org/abs/2001.00267)\]. Variants such as Graph Variational Embedding Collaborative Filtering (GVECF) use variational graph auto-encoders for pre-training embeddings, enhancing feature propagation and achieving significant recall improvements \[[2311.11824](http://arxiv.org/abs/2311.11824)\]. Meanwhile, models like the Enhanced Sub-graph Reconstruction Graph Neural Network (SRCF) address the often overlooked homogeneous user-user and item-item relationships by reconstructing sub-graphs, which improves information transfer and model performance \[[https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/26484691528f38bae54e5ad6bcadf880de4f098d](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/26484691528f38bae54e5ad6bcadf880de4f098d)\].

Despite their success, GNN-based CF models face challenges like scalability, over-smoothing, and noise sensitivity. LightGNN introduces a pruning framework guided by hierarchical knowledge distillation to reduce graph complexity and embedding size drastically while maintaining performance, effectively addressing scalability and robustness issues \[[2501.03228](http://arxiv.org/abs/2501.03228)\]. Similarly, SimRec combines knowledge distillation with contrastive learning in a graph-less framework to preserve global collaborative signals and mitigate over-smoothing, demonstrating efficiency and strong recommendation results \[[2303.08537](http://arxiv.org/abs/2303.08537)\]. Spike Signal Embedding Perturbation (SEP) is another recent technique that injects spike-like noise into embeddings during training, improving robustness and performance compared to standard graph convolution approaches \[[https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/3c8fba26e95c922d2616ecfb891de2542d01aea8](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/3c8fba26e95c922d2616ecfb891de2542d01aea8)\].

Binarization of embeddings has attracted attention due to its potential to reduce memory footprint and speed up inference, which is vital for real-world recommender systems. Binarized Collaborative Filtering distills ranking knowledge from GNNs into binary embeddings via a continuous relaxation approach for efficient optimization, significantly improving online recommendation efficiency \[[1906.01829](http://arxiv.org/abs/1906.01829)\]. However, binarization often leads to information loss, which degrades recommendation quality. Near-lossless binarization methods have been proposed in the NLP domain to transform real-valued embeddings into compact binary vectors while preserving semantic information and incurring minimal accuracy loss (~2%), demonstrating the feasibility of efficient binary embeddings \[[1803.09065](http://arxiv.org/abs/1803.09065)\]. These insights motivate the need for methods that explicitly address information loss during binarization in collaborative filtering.

Contrastive learning has been integrated with graph CF to alleviate data sparsity and enhance embedding quality. Community-Enhanced Contrastive Learning (CECL) leverages community detection to guide positive and negative sampling based on semantic and global graph structures, outperforming random or augmentation-based sampling strategies and boosting recommendation accuracy by 8–12% \[[https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/bcf2b3bfd3f7df75c0590444ad531e3cf1f02bb1](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/bcf2b3bfd3f7df75c0590444ad531e3cf1f02bb1)\]. Such approaches highlight the importance of exploiting latent graph structures for more effective representation learning.

Additional advances in graph representation learning frameworks provide useful tools for CF research. Connector offers a unified platform supporting a broad range of graph embedding models, from shallow to deep, facilitating experimentation and benchmarking \[[2304.13195](http://arxiv.org/abs/2304.13195)\]. Methods like GraphSAGE enable inductive learning by generating embeddings for unseen nodes via neighborhood feature aggregation, enhancing generalization capabilities in dynamic recommendation scenarios \[[1706.02216](http://arxiv.org/abs/1706.02216)\].

Overall, prior work demonstrates that graph-based collaborative filtering methods significantly benefit from modeling high-order interactions and multi-graph structures, while binarization techniques promise efficiency gains but require careful handling of information loss. Knowledge distillation, contrastive learning, and perturbation methods have further improved embedding robustness and expressiveness. Our proposed BiGeaR++ builds on these insights by explicitly mitigating information loss through supervisory signals from pseudo-positive samples and enhanced inference distillation, achieving state-of-the-art performance in binarized graph collaborative filtering.
