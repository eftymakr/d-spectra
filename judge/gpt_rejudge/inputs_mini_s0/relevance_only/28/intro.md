## Related Works

Active learning (AL) has been widely studied as a technique to reduce annotation costs by selecting the most informative unlabeled data for labeling, improving model performance with fewer annotations. However, AL methods often face challenges due to limited data diversity and constrained annotation budgets, especially in distributed or resource-limited settings \[[2210.04505](http://arxiv.org/abs/2210.04505)\]. Federated Active Learning (FAL) extends AL by enabling collaborative data selection and model training across multiple clients while preserving data privacy. This distributed paradigm addresses data silos but introduces new challenges related to heterogeneity of client data distributions and system resources \[[2010.01243](http://arxiv.org/abs/2010.01243)\].

One of the core difficulties in FAL is managing the heterogeneity inherent in federated learning (FL). Data heterogeneity manifests as non-IID and imbalanced distributions across clients, which can degrade model accuracy and slow convergence \[[2210.04505](http://arxiv.org/abs/2210.04505)\]\[[2504.11216](http://arxiv.org/abs/2504.11216)\]\[[2011.06393](http://arxiv.org/abs/2011.06393)\]. System heterogeneity, including differences in computational resources and network latencies, further complicates training efficiency and client participation \[[2504.01921](http://arxiv.org/abs/2504.01921)\]\[[1804.08333](http://arxiv.org/abs/1804.08333)\]\[[2112.11256](http://arxiv.org/abs/2112.11256)\]. Addressing these heterogeneities requires adaptive client selection and sampling strategies that balance statistical diversity and system constraints.

Several works have tackled data heterogeneity in FL through client selection and sampling. FedDiverse proposes a diversity-driven client selection algorithm that encourages collaboration among clients with complementary data distributions, thereby improving model robustness and convergence \[[2504.11216](http://arxiv.org/abs/2504.11216)\]. Similarly, HiCS-FL employs hierarchical clustered sampling based on statistical heterogeneity measures derived from client updates to accelerate convergence in non-IID settings with lower computational overhead \[[2310.00198](http://arxiv.org/abs/2310.00198)\]. Other approaches, such as adaptive client sampling algorithms, minimize wall-clock convergence time by jointly considering system and statistical heterogeneity \[[2112.11256](http://arxiv.org/abs/2112.11256)\]. These methods, however, focus primarily on model training efficiency and convergence rather than on actively selecting the most informative unlabeled samples.

Client adaptation and personalization techniques have also been explored to cope with heterogeneity. WAFFLE uses weighted averaging based on the Euclidean distance between client updates to personalize models and enhance convergence under label skew and concept shift \[[2110.06978](http://arxiv.org/abs/2110.06978)\]. FLAME adopts a user-centered training approach tailored for multi-device environments, incorporating device-level heterogeneity and system efficiency to improve performance in human activity recognition tasks \[[2202.08922](http://arxiv.org/abs/2202.08922)\]. Meanwhile, model-agnostic meta-learning (MAML) frameworks have been applied to FL for fast adaptation to heterogeneous client data distributions \[[1909.12488](http://arxiv.org/abs/1909.12488)\]. Although these techniques improve personalized model quality, they do not directly address the challenge of data selection for annotation in FAL.

Integrating active learning mechanisms into FL is an emerging research area. Some approaches leverage client selection strategies to improve data diversity and model generalization. For example, FedEntOpt selects clients to maximize the entropy of the aggregated label distribution, ensuring exposure to diverse classes and improving accuracy, particularly under label skew and low participation rates \[[2411.01240](http://arxiv.org/abs/2411.01240)\]. FedCor employs correlation-based client selection to enhance convergence by considering loss correlations among clients \[[2206.10032](http://arxiv.org/abs/2206.10032)\]. Synthetic data generation using GANs has also been leveraged to mitigate non-IID effects by sharing differentially private synthetic data with confident pseudo-labels, leading to more consistent local models and improved global aggregation \[[2206.05507](http://arxiv.org/abs/2206.05507)\]. These efforts highlight the potential of combining active data selection and federated optimization, yet often do not explicitly measure or exploit epistemic uncertainty or model confidence variations during training.

Robustness to label noise and heterogeneity is another critical aspect. RHFL integrates noise-tolerant loss functions and client confidence re-weighting schemes to reduce the impact of noisy and heterogeneous clients, enhancing FL performance under adverse conditions \[[https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/1ace65d958cd27ae9ad857c49d8caa9aa5740526](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/1ace65d958cd27ae9ad857c49d8caa9aa5740526)\]. Self-supervised federated learning frameworks like Hetero-SSFL and related works recognize the challenge of unlabeled and heterogeneous data, proposing client-specific self-supervised models aligned via common datasets to enable task-independent representation learning without architectural constraints or labels \[[2205.12493](http://arxiv.org/abs/2205.12493)\]\[[2207.08187](http://arxiv.org/abs/2207.08187)\]. These methods open avenues for leveraging unlabeled data in FL but do not specifically target active data selection based on epistemic uncertainty.

Finally, client selection strategies that consider both data heterogeneity and system constraints have demonstrated substantial improvements in convergence time and training efficiency. For instance, FedCS actively manages client participation based on resource availability to accelerate FL training in mobile edge environments \[[1804.08333](http://arxiv.org/abs/1804.08333)\]. Power-of-Choice client selection biases participation toward clients with higher local loss, leading to faster convergence and improved accuracy \[[2010.01243](http://arxiv.org/abs/2010.01243)\]. Recent optimization-based client selection schemes solve runtime minimization problems to handle simultaneous data and latency heterogeneity, outperforming previous heuristics \[[2504.01921](http://arxiv.org/abs/2504.01921)\].

In summary, while extensive research addresses federated learning challenges related to data heterogeneity, client selection, personalization, robustness, and unlabeled data, there remains a notable gap in methods that explicitly integrate epistemic uncertainty-driven active data selection in heterogeneous federated settings. Existing FAL methods often overlook the dynamic fluctuations of local and global model parameters caused by heterogeneity, which are critical for identifying informative samples. The proposed CHASe framework fills this gap by focusing on tracking epistemic variations through inference inconsistency, calibrating decision boundaries, and enhancing data selection efficiency, thereby advancing federated active learning in heterogeneous environments.
