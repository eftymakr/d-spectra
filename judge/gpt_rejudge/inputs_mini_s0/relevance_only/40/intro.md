## Related Works

Zero-Knowledge Proofs (ZKPs) are foundational cryptographic protocols enabling a prover to convince a verifier of a statement’s validity without revealing any additional information. Their applications span blockchain privacy, verifiable machine learning, and electronic voting, but practical adoption is impeded by the computational intensity of proof generation, especially in state-of-the-art protocols such as zkSNARKs and Plonk variants \[[2408.05890](http://arxiv.org/abs/2408.05890)\]. The main computational bottlenecks in these protocols are polynomial operations, notably the Number Theoretic Transform (NTT) and Multi-Scalar Multiplication (MSM) on elliptic curves, as well as the SumCheck protocol in some constructions.

Significant research efforts have targeted accelerating these primitives using specialized hardware, including GPUs, ASICs, and FPGAs. SZKP presents a scalable ASIC accelerator architecture focusing on structured dataflows for both NTT and MSM, achieving over 400× speedup compared to CPU baselines and outperforming prior GPU and ASIC implementations \[[2408.05890](http://arxiv.org/abs/2408.05890)\]. This work highlights the importance of exploiting algorithmic regularity and memory access patterns to optimize throughput.

GPU-based accelerators such as GZKP and cuZK have also demonstrated substantial improvements. GZKP enhances memory access efficiency for polynomial operations and introduces a fine-grained parallelization scheme for MSM, achieving up to 48.1× speedup on standard cryptographic benchmarks and 17.6× on real-world workloads \[[https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/e149d93531d8c07cf43f6cae327a4795144d1304](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/e149d93531d8c07cf43f6cae327a4795144d1304)\]. Similarly, cuZK proposes a new parallel MSM algorithm that attains near-linear speedup over classical serial methods like Pippenger’s algorithm, alongside parallelization of matrix-vector multiplication and NTT, resulting in up to 4.86× speedup on benchmarks and practical blockchain applications \[[https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/e4a4608e8e35379e63b9df5bda1b7599ee8ab9ac](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/e4a4608e8e35379e63b9df5bda1b7599ee8ab9ac)\]. These GPU solutions emphasize balancing parallelism with memory bandwidth and latency considerations.

FPGA-based acceleration has also been explored, as in if-ZKP, which leverages Intel OneAPI and optimized modular arithmetic IP to accelerate MSM for elliptic curves like BLS12-381 and BN128, achieving 110× to 150× speedup over software \[[2412.12481](http://arxiv.org/abs/2412.12481)\]. The architecture exploits MSM’s inherent parallelism and uses a generic Jacobian coordinate system to maintain flexibility. This approach underscores the potential of reconfigurable hardware for scalable ZKP acceleration.

PipeZK introduces a pipelined architecture that decomposes large polynomial kernels and uses a dynamic work dispatch mechanism to mitigate resource underutilization and load imbalance. This design achieves 10× speedup on cryptographic benchmarks and 5× on applications such as Zcash, demonstrating the benefits of pipelining and workload balancing in hardware accelerators \[[https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/6ee84674b7c426849c3da56cb278c08eb7a903cf](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/6ee84674b7c426849c3da56cb278c08eb7a903cf)\]. ReZK further expands the concept of configurability, offering a reconfigurable accelerator that adapts its datapath to support different NTT/INTT sizes and MSM bit-widths, maximizing hardware reuse and efficiency \[[https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/8b12309fd3c5a1851ff8b6d9cfff018811d08d93](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/8b12309fd3c5a1851ff8b6d9cfff018811d08d93)\].

Beyond hardware acceleration, recent advances in ZKP protocols have focused on improving proof succinctness and reducing verification overhead. HyperPlonk, the protocol targeted by zkSpeed, supports a one-time, universal trusted setup and generates small proof sizes optimized for public, consensus-based systems, addressing limitations of earlier zkSNARK protocols that either required per-application trusted setups or produced larger proofs with expensive verification \[[2408.05890](http://arxiv.org/abs/2408.05890)\]. Prior works mainly accelerate individual primitives like SumCheck or MSM, but zkSpeed distinguishes itself by accelerating the entire HyperPlonk protocol end-to-end, obtaining geometric mean speedups of 801× over CPU baselines through a full-chip architecture with significant bandwidth (2 TB/s) and chip area (366.46 mm²) \[[2408.05890](http://arxiv.org/abs/2408.05890)\].

Other notable works extend ZKP applicability beyond traditional cryptographic settings. For example, zkDL applies zero-knowledge proofs to deep learning training, addressing the challenge of verifying non-linear computations in neural networks while preserving privacy \[[https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/8b12309fd3c5a1851ff8b6d9cfff018811d08d93](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/8b12309fd3c5a1851ff8b6d9cfff018811d08d93)\]. Furthermore, theoretical advances explore new intractable problems and commitment schemes supporting more secure or quantum-resistant ZKPs, such as those based on homomorphic commitment schemes enabling polynomial-time zero-knowledge proofs for NP-complete problems with minimal interaction rounds \[[2304.09784](http://arxiv.org/abs/2304.09784)\].

Comprehensive surveys of ZKP frameworks highlight the diverse landscape of open-source tools and their varying trade-offs in usability, performance, and supported proof systems. These surveys emphasize the difficulty developers face in selecting suitable frameworks and the need for integrated acceleration solutions to bridge the gap between theoretical protocols and practical deployment \[[2502.07063](http://arxiv.org/abs/2502.07063)\]. 

In summary, the body of work on accelerating ZKP proof generation spans multiple hardware platforms and algorithmic improvements, focusing heavily on optimizing NTT, MSM, and SumCheck computations. However, existing accelerators often focus on partial protocol components or require trusted setups that limit flexibility. zkSpeed’s contribution lies in providing an end-to-end accelerator for HyperPlonk that combines universal setup, small proof sizes, and comprehensive hardware acceleration, achieving unprecedented speedups and addressing practical deployment constraints in public verification scenarios \[[2408.05890](http://arxiv.org/abs/2408.05890)\]\[[https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/e149d93531d8c07cf43f6cae327a4795144d1304](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/e149d93531d8c07cf43f6cae327a4795144d1304)\]\[[https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/e4a4608e8e35379e63b9df5bda1b7599ee8ab9ac](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/e4a4608e8e35379e63b9df5bda1b7599ee8ab9ac)\]\[[https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/6ee84674b7c426849c3da56cb278c08eb7a903cf](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/6ee84674b7c426849c3da56cb278c08eb7a903cf)\]\[[2412.12481](http://arxiv.org/abs/2412.12481)\]\[[https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/8b12309fd3c5a1851ff8b6d9cfff018811d08d93](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/8b12309fd3c5a1851ff8b6d9cfff018811d08d93)\]\[[2304.09784](http://arxiv.org/abs/2304.09784)\]\[[2502.07063](http://arxiv.org/abs/2502.07063)\].
