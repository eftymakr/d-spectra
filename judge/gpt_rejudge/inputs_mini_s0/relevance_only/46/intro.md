## Related Works

Cross-domain sequential recommendation (CDSR) has gained significant attention as it leverages user interaction sequences from multiple domains to alleviate data sparsity and cold-start issues inherent in single-domain sequential recommendation (SDSR) \[[https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/27670ed18e96550c674e022e284fc5356a7458c2](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/27670ed18e96550c674e022e284fc5356a7458c2)\]\[[2401.04971](http://arxiv.org/abs/2401.04971)\]. The fundamental challenge of CDSR lies in effectively modeling user preferences across heterogeneous domains while mitigating negative transfer caused by domain discrepancies and data sparsity \[[2407.11245](http://arxiv.org/abs/2407.11245)\]\[[2311.04760](http://arxiv.org/abs/2311.04760)\]\[[2311.04590](http://arxiv.org/abs/2311.04590)\].

### Core Sequence Modeling and Self-Attention in CDSR

Transformer-based self-attention models like SASRec and BERT4Rec have become dominant in sequential recommendation due to their ability to capture complex item relationships within sequences \[[2410.21048](http://arxiv.org/abs/2410.21048)\]. However, many CDSR methods extend these frameworks by integrating domain-specific modules or transfer mechanisms rather than directly enhancing the self-attention mechanism itself \[[https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/96bfe33a2503e960153a934e10be91da4d8b13a6](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/96bfe33a2503e960153a934e10be91da4d8b13a6)\]\[[https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/10715bc315c7c1075b5bf35e603fc08aaf5dff36](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/10715bc315c7c1075b5bf35e603fc08aaf5dff36)\]\[[https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/6dd0605f7de7581c69b4acf0ab78000748a09f3e](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/6dd0605f7de7581c69b4acf0ab78000748a09f3e)\]\[[2304.03891](http://arxiv.org/abs/2304.03891)\]. These approaches often build separate single-domain preference models and then transfer knowledge via dedicated modules, which can limit the exploitation of cross-domain correlations and inter-sequence item relationships \[[2304.03891](http://arxiv.org/abs/2304.03891)\]\[[https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/4124740c5376b23d87a1d539051b3f350aeb4326](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/4124740c5376b23d87a1d539051b3f350aeb4326)\].

Recent studies argue for improving the core self-attention to better capture both intra- and inter-sequence dependencies in cross-domain settings. For example, graph neural networks (GNNs) combined with attention mechanisms have been employed to model collaborative item relationships across domains, enhancing the expressiveness of user representations \[[https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/10715bc315c7c1075b5bf35e603fc08aaf5dff36](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/10715bc315c7c1075b5bf35e603fc08aaf5dff36)\]\[[2304.03891](http://arxiv.org/abs/2304.03891)\]. Similarly, channel-enhanced and time-interval-aware attention mechanisms have been proposed to capture temporal and contextual nuances in user behavior sequences \[[https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/27670ed18e96550c674e022e284fc5356a7458c2](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/27670ed18e96550c674e022e284fc5356a7458c2)\]\[[https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/11da511e9baafc1dfdf58a287c1ead787c9345bc](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/11da511e9baafc1dfdf58a287c1ead787c9345bc)\]\[[2206.08050](http://arxiv.org/abs/2206.08050)\].

### Cross-Domain Knowledge Transfer and Negative Transfer Mitigation

A key issue in CDSR is negative transfer, where irrelevant or conflicting information from auxiliary domains degrades target domain performance. To address this, adaptive weighting strategies have been developed that estimate the degree of negative transfer per domain and modulate gradient flows accordingly \[[2407.11245](http://arxiv.org/abs/2407.11245)\]. Cooperative learning frameworks that jointly train single- and cross-domain models with auxiliary losses maximizing mutual information between their representations have shown effectiveness in balancing knowledge transfer \[[2407.11245](http://arxiv.org/abs/2407.11245)\]\[[https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/96bfe33a2503e960153a934e10be91da4d8b13a6](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/96bfe33a2503e960153a934e10be91da4d8b13a6)\]\[[2304.03891](http://arxiv.org/abs/2304.03891)\].

Partial alignment of item representations across domains, via adaptive spectrum filtering or shared proxy encoders using textual item descriptions, has also been explored to enhance the semantic consistency of cross-domain knowledge transfer \[[https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/27670ed18e96550c674e022e284fc5356a7458c2](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/27670ed18e96550c674e022e284fc5356a7458c2)\]\[[2405.12473](http://arxiv.org/abs/2405.12473)\]. Contrastive learning techniques are widely adopted to reinforce domain-invariant features and improve representation robustness \[[https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/96bfe33a2503e960153a934e10be91da4d8b13a6](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/96bfe33a2503e960153a934e10be91da4d8b13a6)\]\[[https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/11da511e9baafc1dfdf58a287c1ead787c9345bc](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/11da511e9baafc1dfdf58a287c1ead787c9345bc)\]\[[https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/73b219f3c0ae43460267ba57eeeb4e5934bc1a6f](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/73b219f3c0ae43460267ba57eeeb4e5934bc1a6f)\].

### Modeling User Interests and Multi-View Preferences

Capturing both general user interests from source domains and current preferences in the target domain is critical. Attention-based models that aggregate multi-domain user representations via hierarchical gating or contrastive multi-view learning have been proposed to disentangle domain-invariant and domain-specific preferences \[[https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/6dd0605f7de7581c69b4acf0ab78000748a09f3e](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/6dd0605f7de7581c69b4acf0ab78000748a09f3e)\]\[[https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/4124740c5376b23d87a1d539051b3f350aeb4326](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/4124740c5376b23d87a1d539051b3f350aeb4326)\]\[[https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/73b219f3c0ae43460267ba57eeeb4e5934bc1a6f](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/73b219f3c0ae43460267ba57eeeb4e5934bc1a6f)\]. These methods improve recommendation accuracy by dynamically weighing the contribution of each domain’s user behavior.

Large language models (LLMs) have recently been introduced to CDSR to leverage rich semantic information for bridging domain gaps and modeling user preferences from a textual and behavioral perspective \[[2501.19232](http://arxiv.org/abs/2501.19232)\]\[[https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/e33f51c13728bedeeb54a76b398949a381d6152f](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/e33f51c13728bedeeb54a76b398949a381d6152f)\]\[[2504.18383](http://arxiv.org/abs/2504.18383)\]\[[2406.03085](http://arxiv.org/abs/2406.03085)\]. LLM-based frameworks utilize pretrained semantic embeddings and contrastive pre-training strategies to generate pseudo cross-domain interactions and enhance knowledge transfer, addressing challenges with non-overlapping user behaviors and cold-start scenarios.

### Reinforcement Learning and Multi-Agent Frameworks

Reinforcement learning (RL) techniques have been applied to optimize cross-domain knowledge transfer by selecting valuable source domain interactions adaptively. Multi-agent RL frameworks coordinate multiple agents to select relevant items from source domains, optimizing the transfer process and improving performance in sparse target domains \[[https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/5a653a8060bf9f714866be2823cef71c0fbf9a34](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/5a653a8060bf9f714866be2823cef71c0fbf9a34)\]\[[https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/c13603992b90529918bd170e2f7a10858a340d43](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/c13603992b90529918bd170e2f7a10858a340d43)\].

### Privacy and Open-World Considerations

Privacy-preserving CDSR models have been developed that incorporate differential privacy mechanisms to protect user identity and interaction order information while maintaining recommendation quality \[[2401.15369](http://arxiv.org/abs/2401.15369)\]. Additionally, open-world CDSR approaches address real-world challenges such as long-tailed and cold-start users by adapting to distribution shifts and considering both overlapping and non-overlapping user sets \[[2311.04760](http://arxiv.org/abs/2311.04760)\]\[[2311.04590](http://arxiv.org/abs/2311.04590)\].

### Summary

While most existing CDSR methods enhance performance by augmenting transformer architectures with domain-specific modules or transfer units, recent advances emphasize improving the core self-attention mechanism to better capture cross-domain correlations and mitigate negative transfer \[[https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/27670ed18e96550c674e022e284fc5356a7458c2](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/27670ed18e96550c674e022e284fc5356a7458c2)\]\[[2407.11245](http://arxiv.org/abs/2407.11245)\]\[[https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/10715bc315c7c1075b5bf35e603fc08aaf5dff36](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/10715bc315c7c1075b5bf35e603fc08aaf5dff36)\]\[[2304.03891](http://arxiv.org/abs/2304.03891)\]. Multi-objective optimization, contrastive learning, and adaptive alignment strategies have emerged as effective paradigms for balancing knowledge transfer and domain-specific learning \[[2407.11245](http://arxiv.org/abs/2407.11245)\]\[[https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/96bfe33a2503e960153a934e10be91da4d8b13a6](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/96bfe33a2503e960153a934e10be91da4d8b13a6)\]\[[https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/73b219f3c0ae43460267ba57eeeb4e5934bc1a6f](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/73b219f3c0ae43460267ba57eeeb4e5934bc1a6f)\]. The incorporation of semantic information through LLMs and reinforcement learning-based selection further pushes the frontier of robust and practical CDSR systems \[[2501.19232](http://arxiv.org/abs/2501.19232)\]\[[https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/e33f51c13728bedeeb54a76b398949a381d6152f](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/e33f51c13728bedeeb54a76b398949a381d6152f)\]\[[2504.18383](http://arxiv.org/abs/2504.18383)\]\[[2406.03085](http://arxiv.org/abs/2406.03085)\].

Our approach aligns with these directions by directly enhancing the self-attention mechanism via a Pareto-optimal multi-objective framework, automating knowledge transfer while minimizing negative transfer across domains, thus providing a flexible and efficient plug-and-play solution for transformer-based CDSR models.
