## Related Works

Federated Learning (FL) enables collaborative training of machine learning models across decentralized clients, preserving data privacy by keeping raw data local and exchanging only model updates \[[2012.13891](http://arxiv.org/abs/2012.13891)\]\[[2403.02437](http://arxiv.org/abs/2403.02437)\]. This paradigm is particularly relevant in privacy-sensitive domains where data cannot be centralized, such as search ranking systems optimized through Federated Online Learning to Rank (FOLTR) \[[https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/a52c3ec7325e81bd87fdf8d74bf9acfd1e1ffe5f](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/a52c3ec7325e81bd87fdf8d74bf9acfd1e1ffe5f)\]. FOLTR integrates implicit user feedback to continually adapt ranking models without exposing raw user data, addressing privacy concerns while enhancing personalization \[[https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/a52c3ec7325e81bd87fdf8d74bf9acfd1e1ffe5f](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/a52c3ec7325e81bd87fdf8d74bf9acfd1e1ffe5f)\]. However, this decentralized nature complicates compliance with emerging privacy regulations like the "right to be forgotten" (RTBF), which mandates the removal of a user's data influence from trained models \[[2401.05146](http://arxiv.org/abs/2401.05146)\]\[[2310.20448](http://arxiv.org/abs/2310.20448)\]\[[https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/39da2d48f2132b919405e3bb6df6ece44ac6e15a](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/39da2d48f2132b919405e3bb6df6ece44ac6e15a)\].

Federated Unlearning (FU) has emerged as a crucial extension of FL to fulfill RTBF requirements, aiming to efficiently remove the impact of specific clients or data samples from global models without costly retraining from scratch \[[2310.20448](http://arxiv.org/abs/2310.20448)\]\[[2012.13891](http://arxiv.org/abs/2012.13891)\]\[[2207.05521](http://arxiv.org/abs/2207.05521)\]. Unlike centralized machine unlearning, FU must handle challenges unique to distributed settings, including data heterogeneity, limited data accessibility, and the necessity to maintain model utility and security after unlearning \[[2403.02437](http://arxiv.org/abs/2403.02437)\]\[[2310.20448](http://arxiv.org/abs/2310.20448)\]\[[2207.05521](http://arxiv.org/abs/2207.05521)\]. Various FU approaches have been proposed, categorized broadly into exact unlearning methods that guarantee precise removal but are computationally intensive, and approximate methods which trade off strict guarantees for efficiency \[[https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/c78de63d34189a0d6b7a6407781415cbe024e9af](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/c78de63d34189a0d6b7a6407781415cbe024e9af)\]\[[https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/06edb1f6eadb6c141d720fcca4c854578dc5d670](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/06edb1f6eadb6c141d720fcca4c854578dc5d670)\].

Several strategies focus on client-level unlearning by leveraging stored historical updates to reconstruct models excluding targeted clients, such as FedEraser, which achieves significant speed-ups over full retraining by calibrating and rolling back parameter updates \[[2012.13891](http://arxiv.org/abs/2012.13891)\]\[[https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/eadeffdec9fac8fd7f9aea732ca410eb082b7dcf](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/eadeffdec9fac8fd7f9aea732ca410eb082b7dcf)\]. Other methods, including FedRecovery, employ differential privacy techniques to statistically indistinguishably erase a client’s influence without retraining or requiring convexity assumptions, enhancing privacy guarantees \[[https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/06edb1f6eadb6c141d720fcca4c854578dc5d670](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/06edb1f6eadb6c141d720fcca4c854578dc5d670)\]. Recent innovations like forgettable federated linear learning (F²L²) provide certified unlearning by approximating nonlinear deep models linearly, enabling unlearning without client participation or additional storage \[[2310.20448](http://arxiv.org/abs/2310.20448)\].

Vertical federated unlearning methods address unlearning in multi-party scenarios with vertically partitioned data, using constrained intermediate parameter updates to efficiently remove client data influence while preserving model accuracy \[[https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/4e26fc4fe687c7471e44f2a5e6feef0facfc4be1](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/4e26fc4fe687c7471e44f2a5e6feef0facfc4be1)\]. Feature-specific unlearning has also gained attention, where frameworks like Ferrari minimize feature sensitivity to selectively forget sensitive or malicious features, addressing concerns around biased or backdoored data \[[2405.17462](http://arxiv.org/abs/2405.17462)\].

A major challenge in FU lies in balancing the triad of privacy, accuracy, and efficiency. Studies have shown that unlearning often incurs model performance degradation or increased computational costs, necessitating evaluation frameworks that consider multiple metrics beyond simple accuracy drop or time overhead \[[2310.19218](http://arxiv.org/abs/2310.19218)\]\[[https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/c78de63d34189a0d6b7a6407781415cbe024e9af](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/c78de63d34189a0d6b7a6407781415cbe024e9af)\]\[[2412.00126](http://arxiv.org/abs/2412.00126)\]. The OPENFEDERATEDUNLEARNING framework exemplifies such efforts by benchmarking FU methods on their algorithmic efficiency, privacy preservation, and impact on model performance \[[2310.19218](http://arxiv.org/abs/2310.19218)\]. Moreover, enhancing unlearning verification has been identified as critical to ensure trustworthiness; frameworks like VeriFi enable participants to actively verify that their data influence has been removed, addressing the potential cancellation of unlearning effects by remaining clients’ contributions \[[2205.12709](http://arxiv.org/abs/2205.12709)\].

Security concerns during FU are also prominent, as unlearning processes can inadvertently leak information or be exploited by malicious clients injecting backdoors. Defensive mechanisms leveraging secure multiparty computation, such as Starfish, have been proposed to preserve privacy during the unlearning process and certify client removal without exposing sensitive information \[[2404.09724](http://arxiv.org/abs/2404.09724)\]\[[https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/a52c3ec7325e81bd87fdf8d74bf9acfd1e1ffe5f](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/a52c3ec7325e81bd87fdf8d74bf9acfd1e1ffe5f)\]. Furthermore, plausible deniability models have been introduced to allow servers to credibly deny a client’s participation, reducing the need for direct parameter modification while maintaining differential privacy guarantees \[[2410.09947](http://arxiv.org/abs/2410.09947)\].

Incentivizing client participation and retention during federated learning and unlearning is another emerging research direction. Unlearning requests can reduce global model utility, and motivating valuable clients to remain engaged is essential to maintain performance. Game-theoretic and reinforcement learning approaches have been explored to design incentive mechanisms that balance user privacy preferences and system costs \[[2308.12502](http://arxiv.org/abs/2308.12502)\]\[[https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/a08f1c5c9f0bdb24f0dc75c0d969eb353899156b](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/a08f1c5c9f0bdb24f0dc75c0d969eb353899156b)\]\[[https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/6556402fc6d6a1ebcbf82eed07c64d89dd40f786](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/6556402fc6d6a1ebcbf82eed07c64d89dd40f786)\].

Despite advances, FU methods often face difficulties in managing unlearning accuracy and efficiency in non-IID data settings typical of FL, as well as in multi-domain contexts where unlearning may disproportionately affect certain model layers or domains \[[2406.03078](http://arxiv.org/abs/2406.03078)\]\[[2403.02437](http://arxiv.org/abs/2403.02437)\]. Consequently, recent research calls for domain-aware unlearning strategies and standardized evaluation metrics to better quantify unlearning efficacy and model robustness \[[2406.03078](http://arxiv.org/abs/2406.03078)\]\[[https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/c78de63d34189a0d6b7a6407781415cbe024e9af](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/c78de63d34189a0d6b7a6407781415cbe024e9af)\].

Finally, the integration of FU into FOLTR remains nascent. While FOLTR frameworks have demonstrated privacy-preserving ranking based on implicit feedback, systematic evaluation of federated unlearning strategies within this context—especially under multiple complementary metrics addressing under- and over-unlearning scenarios—has been limited \[[https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/a52c3ec7325e81bd87fdf8d74bf9acfd1e1ffe5f](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/a52c3ec7325e81bd87fdf8d74bf9acfd1e1ffe5f)\]. The current study builds on this landscape by rigorously assessing multiple unlearning strategies tailored for FOLTR, employing newly proposed metrics for a comprehensive and verifiable evaluation.
