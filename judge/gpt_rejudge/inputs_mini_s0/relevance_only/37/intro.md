## Related Works

Vision Transformers (ViTs) have revolutionized computer vision by leveraging self-attention mechanisms to capture global image context, outperforming traditional convolutional neural networks (CNNs) in various tasks \[[2205.11397](http://arxiv.org/abs/2205.11397)\]. Despite their success, ViTs pose significant challenges for hardware acceleration due to their complex operations such as Softmax and LayerNorm, which are computationally intensive and sensitive to quantization, complicating efficient low-precision hardware implementation \[[2405.00314](http://arxiv.org/abs/2405.00314)\]. Existing accelerator designs often focus on parallelizing matrix multiplications while offloading these nonlinear operations to CPUs, resulting in costly CPU-accelerator communication and suboptimal performance \[[2403.14047](http://arxiv.org/abs/2403.14047)\]\[[2210.09573](http://arxiv.org/abs/2210.09573)\].

### Model Compression and Quantization for ViTs

Model compression techniques, including pruning and quantization, have been extensively studied to reduce ViT complexity for hardware deployment. Token pruning methods dynamically remove or consolidate less informative tokens, significantly lowering computational cost while maintaining accuracy. For example, HeatViT introduces a hardware-efficient adaptive token pruning framework that inserts a learnable token selector before transformer blocks, achieving up to 4.89× speedup on FPGA with minimal accuracy loss \[[2211.08110](http://arxiv.org/abs/2211.08110)\]. Similarly, integrated static weight pruning and dynamic token pruning combined with hardware-aware training and balanced parallelism have shown up to 3.4× computation reduction with acceptable accuracy trade-offs \[[2403.14047](http://arxiv.org/abs/2403.14047)\]. 

Quantization approaches transform high-precision weights and activations into low-bit representations, greatly reducing memory and compute demands. Q-ViT demonstrates a fully quantized low-bit ViT with an information rectification module to mitigate quantization distortion in attention maps, yielding better accuracy than prior methods and a theoretical 6.14× acceleration on DeiT-S \[[2210.06707](http://arxiv.org/abs/2210.06707)\]. Trio-ViT removes Softmax to enable efficient post-training quantization for convolution-transformer hybrid ViTs, achieving up to 7.3× FPS speedup and improved DSP efficiency on FPGA \[[2405.03882](http://arxiv.org/abs/2405.03882)\]. BSViT employs dynamic patch and weight bit-group quantization combined with a bit-serial accelerator, improving energy efficiency by up to 3.69× compared to state-of-the-art \[[https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/b2e51759f910101691039846bec4401a85b53309](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/b2e51759f910101691039846bec4401a85b53309)\]. These quantization efforts underline the importance of hardware-friendly algorithm design aligned with accelerator architectures \[[2405.00314](http://arxiv.org/abs/2405.00314)\].

### Hardware Architectures for ViT Acceleration

A variety of FPGA-based accelerator designs have been proposed to address ViTs’ computational and memory bottlenecks. HDViT utilizes a heterogeneous architecture with multiple specialized processing engines and hybrid dataflow mapping to match operator types, achieving up to 3.5× speedup and nearly full hardware utilization \[[https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/0ade4d722e48379761d17cf1efc05200315c27d9](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/0ade4d722e48379761d17cf1efc05200315c27d9)\]. ME-ViT introduces a single-load memory-efficient processing element that minimizes off-chip memory traffic by loading parameters once and storing intermediate results on-chip, leading to up to 17.89× memory bandwidth improvement and over 5× throughput increase on FPGA \[[2402.09709](http://arxiv.org/abs/2402.09709)\]. ViTA targets resource-constrained edge devices with a head-level pipeline and MLP optimizations, achieving nearly 90% hardware utilization at low power consumption \[[2302.09108](http://arxiv.org/abs/2302.09108)\]. 

For ViTs with hybrid convolution-transformer architectures, accelerators have been designed to exploit operation diversity and dataflow optimizations. An FPGA-based reconfigurable accelerator for EfficientViT supports lightweight convolutions and attention with time-multiplexed pipelined dataflow, attaining 780 GOPS throughput and 105 GOPS/W energy efficiency on Xilinx ZCU102 FPGA \[[2403.20230](http://arxiv.org/abs/2403.20230)\]. Trio-ViT’s dedicated accelerator similarly focuses on hybrid ViTs, co-designing quantization and hardware for improved performance \[[2405.03882](http://arxiv.org/abs/2405.03882)\].

Algorithm-hardware co-design frameworks have also demonstrated significant gains. ViTCoD prunes and polarizes attention maps into fixed sparse and dense patterns to reduce computations and data movements, integrating on-chip encoder/decoder engines for pipeline efficiency. This approach achieves speedups up to 235× over CPUs and notable improvements compared to prior accelerators \[[2210.09573](http://arxiv.org/abs/2210.09573)\]. CHOSEN offers a compilation and hardware optimization stack employing multi-kernel designs, approximate nonlinear functions, and an efficient compiler for design space exploration, improving throughput by over 1.4× for DeiT models \[[2407.12736](http://arxiv.org/abs/2407.12736)\].

### Addressing Complex Arithmetic Operations on Hardware

Softmax and LayerNorm operations are critical yet hardware-challenging components in ViTs. Some efforts target hardware-friendly alternatives to Softmax. For instance, ConSmax replaces standard Softmax with differentiable normalization parameters that enable parallelization without accuracy loss \[[2405.00314](http://arxiv.org/abs/2405.00314)\]. Softermax designs low-precision softmax computations with online normalization to reduce energy consumption significantly \[[2405.00314](http://arxiv.org/abs/2405.00314)\]. However, these approaches often remain algorithmic or limited to CPUs, lacking full hardware mapping.

In contrast, existing accelerators typically exclude such complex operations from FPGA mapping, leading to communication overhead and underutilization \[[2403.14047](http://arxiv.org/abs/2403.14047)\]\[[2210.09573](http://arxiv.org/abs/2210.09573)\]. The proposed work’s novelty lies in leveraging a new arithmetic format, Microscaling Integer (MXInt), to quantize ViTs and fully map all operations, including Softmax and LayerNorm, onto FPGA hardware. This holistic mapping addresses previous limitations by enabling custom datapath designs tailored to MXInt arithmetic, yielding substantial speedups and high area efficiency within 1% accuracy loss.

### Summary

Overall, the literature on ViT acceleration reveals a trend towards integrated algorithm-hardware co-design incorporating quantization, pruning, and custom accelerator architectures to overcome ViTs’ computational and memory challenges \[[2503.02891](http://arxiv.org/abs/2503.02891)\]\[[2405.00314](http://arxiv.org/abs/2405.00314)\]\[[2309.02031](http://arxiv.org/abs/2309.02031)\]. While many works optimize matrix multiplication and prune tokens or weights, they often leave complex nonlinear operations offloaded to CPUs, incurring overhead. Hardware architectures vary from heterogeneous multi-engine designs to memory-efficient single-load processing elements, achieving substantial speedups and efficiency gains \[[https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/0ade4d722e48379761d17cf1efc05200315c27d9](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/0ade4d722e48379761d17cf1efc05200315c27d9)\]\[[2402.09709](http://arxiv.org/abs/2402.09709)\]. Emerging quantization schemes and Softmax alternatives improve hardware friendliness but rarely achieve full operation mapping on FPGA \[[2405.03882](http://arxiv.org/abs/2405.03882)\]\[[2210.06707](http://arxiv.org/abs/2210.06707)\]\[[2405.00314](http://arxiv.org/abs/2405.00314)\]. The proposed MXInt-based accelerator builds on these insights by enabling comprehensive mapping of all ViT operations onto FPGA with new arithmetic formats and hardware optimizations, marking a significant advancement in ViT hardware acceleration.
