## Related Works

Model calibration has become increasingly important in machine learning, particularly for ranking models where the focus traditionally lies on preserving the relative order of predictions rather than ensuring the accuracy of absolute predicted scores. Accurate calibration is essential in many real-world applications such as advertising and recommendation systems, where calibrated scores directly impact downstream decisions like bidding or user engagement \[[2406.08010](http://arxiv.org/abs/2406.08010)\]\[[https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/7f6bb146b9c1504ec77100e640d624bdc204f0ac](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/7f6bb146b9c1504ec77100e640d624bdc204f0ac)\].

Traditional calibration techniques often rely on predefined transformation functions to adjust raw model outputs while maintaining their order. Commonly used transformations include piece-wise linear or monotonic functions, which enforce order preservation but suffer from limited expressiveness and flexibility. This restricts their ability to capture complex nonlinear calibration mappings that arise in practical scenarios \[[2406.08010](http://arxiv.org/abs/2406.08010)\]\[[https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/7f6bb146b9c1504ec77100e640d624bdc204f0ac](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/7f6bb146b9c1504ec77100e640d624bdc204f0ac)\]. Moreover, existing multi-objective calibrated ranking frameworks typically apply ranking and pointwise losses on a single prediction, resulting in suboptimal trade-offs between ranking quality and calibration accuracy \[[2406.08010](http://arxiv.org/abs/2406.08010)\].

To overcome these limitations, recent research has explored neural network-based calibration methods that can learn flexible, nonlinear transformations. Monotonic neural networks have been proposed as powerful tools for this purpose because they guarantee monotonicity, preserving the relative order of predictions while allowing rich modeling capacity \[[1908.05164](http://arxiv.org/abs/1908.05164)\]. The Unconstrained Monotonic Neural Network (UMNN) architecture, in particular, achieves this by parameterizing monotonic functions through neural networks constrained only by positive derivatives, thus significantly increasing expressiveness without restrictive weight or activation constraints \[[1908.05164](http://arxiv.org/abs/1908.05164)\]\[[2106.03228](http://arxiv.org/abs/2106.03228)\]. UMNNs have demonstrated success in density estimation and distributional reinforcement learning tasks by effectively modeling continuous monotonic functions \[[1908.05164](http://arxiv.org/abs/1908.05164)\]\[[2106.03228](http://arxiv.org/abs/2106.03228)\].

In the context of ranking calibration, the use of UMNNs is especially relevant as they enable learning arbitrary monotonic calibration functions that can flexibly adjust scores without distorting their order. This contrasts with conventional calibration methods that are limited to fixed forms and thus may fail in complex ranking scenarios \[[2406.08010](http://arxiv.org/abs/2406.08010)\]\[[https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/7f6bb146b9c1504ec77100e640d624bdc204f0ac](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/7f6bb146b9c1504ec77100e640d624bdc204f0ac)\]. Incorporating UMNNs as calibrators addresses the expressiveness gap and aligns with theoretical insights on scale calibration in ranking models, which highlight the importance of adjusting absolute values without compromising ranking performance \[[https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/7f6bb146b9c1504ec77100e640d624bdc204f0ac](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/7f6bb146b9c1504ec77100e640d624bdc204f0ac)\].

Beyond monotonic neural networks, other calibration strategies include Bayesian methods that provide probabilistic uncertainty estimates, improving calibration quality but often at the cost of increased computational complexity and scalability challenges in large systems \[[1807.00263](http://arxiv.org/abs/1807.00263)\]. Post-hoc calibration methods, such as temperature scaling or spline-based recalibration, offer simpler alternatives but may lack flexibility or require held-out calibration sets \[[2006.12800](http://arxiv.org/abs/2006.12800)\]\[[2002.09437](http://arxiv.org/abs/2002.09437)\]. Recent approaches also emphasize consistency-based calibration, leveraging model output stability under input perturbations to enhance uncertainty calibration without additional data \[[2410.12295](http://arxiv.org/abs/2410.12295)\].

In ranking-specific contexts, frameworks like the Self-Boosted Calibrated Ranking (SBCR) method combine pointwise and ranking losses with novel training strategies to improve both calibration and ranking quality simultaneously, highlighting the trade-offs and challenges in multi-objective calibration \[[2406.08010](http://arxiv.org/abs/2406.08010)\]. Other works propose calibrated ranking losses integrated during training to avoid instability caused by translation-invariance of ranking losses and to achieve better scale calibration \[[https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/7f6bb146b9c1504ec77100e640d624bdc204f0ac](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/7f6bb146b9c1504ec77100e640d624bdc204f0ac)\].

Overall, while a range of calibration methods exist, many exhibit limitations in flexibility, expressiveness, or scalability when applied to ranking models requiring both well-calibrated absolute scores and preserved ranking order. The introduction of UMNN-based calibrators offers a promising direction by relaxing constraints on the calibration function form while guaranteeing monotonicity, thus improving calibration effectiveness in complex ranking scenarios. Our work builds on these advances by employing UMNNs coupled with a novel Smooth Calibration Loss to optimize calibration quality, validated by both offline benchmarks and large-scale online deployment \[[2406.08010](http://arxiv.org/abs/2406.08010)\]\[[1908.05164](http://arxiv.org/abs/1908.05164)\]\[[2410.12295](http://arxiv.org/abs/2410.12295)\]\[[1807.00263](http://arxiv.org/abs/1807.00263)\].
