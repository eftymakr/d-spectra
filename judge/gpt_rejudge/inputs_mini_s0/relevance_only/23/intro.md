## Related Works

Radar-camera calibration is a critical component for robust sensor fusion in autonomous driving, enabling complementary perception capabilities under diverse environmental conditions. Traditional calibration techniques have predominantly relied on target-based methods, which require artificial calibration targets and controlled environments. Such methods, while accurate, are often impractical for in-field or online calibration due to their operational complexity and dependency on specialized setups \[[https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/6aa3d55fc03c97e5e7b7385437e473caa2acb0c8](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/6aa3d55fc03c97e5e7b7385437e473caa2acb0c8)\]\[[2103.07505](http://arxiv.org/abs/2103.07505)\]. For example, a method using a single corner reflector combined with perspective-n-point (PnP) problem solving and nonlinear optimization demonstrates high accuracy but necessitates manual target placement and iterative data collection \[[https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/6aa3d55fc03c97e5e7b7385437e473caa2acb0c8](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/6aa3d55fc03c97e5e7b7385437e473caa2acb0c8)\].

To overcome these limitations, recent research has focused on targetless calibration approaches that can operate during system deployment without dedicated targets. A pioneering method employs deep learning to extract common features from raw radar Range-Doppler-Angle data and camera images, implicitly matching identical objects across modalities for online extrinsic calibration. This approach improves robustness and accuracy by leveraging feature correspondences without explicit target detection \[[https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/fa7f4199640500d48c0671181ea6a8dce3141123](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/fa7f4199640500d48c0671181ea6a8dce3141123)\]. Similarly, other targetless frameworks exploit the radar's ability to measure ego-velocity relative to a fixed frame for spatiotemporal calibration, thereby enabling infrastructure-free, in-situ calibration that is resilient to environmental constraints \[[2305.15883](http://arxiv.org/abs/2305.15883)\]\[[2103.07505](http://arxiv.org/abs/2103.07505)\].

Height uncertainty and radar data sparsity are persistent challenges in radar-camera calibration. Radar measurements lack fine elevation resolution and are sparse compared to dense camera images, complicating direct feature association and spatial alignment \[[https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/0955326c67a947b5fac5fbae7238d04fc9adc251](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/0955326c67a947b5fac5fbae7238d04fc9adc251)\]\[[2304.10410](http://arxiv.org/abs/2304.10410)\]. To address these, recent fusion-based perception models integrate bird’s-eye view (BEV) representations with perspective views to leverage the complementary strengths of both. BEV offers robust spatial localization less sensitive to height ambiguity, while frontal views provide rich semantic and height cues \[[2509.05785](http://arxiv.org/abs/2509.05785)\]\[[2304.00670](http://arxiv.org/abs/2304.00670)\]. Fusion mechanisms employing cross-attention and contrastive learning have been proposed to align radar and camera features precisely, mitigating spatial misalignment and domain gaps between modalities \[[2504.16368](http://arxiv.org/abs/2504.16368)\]\[[2210.09267](http://arxiv.org/abs/2210.09267)\].

Cross-modal feature-level fusion frameworks have shown significant improvements in 3D object detection and calibration tasks. For instance, methods like RCAlign use dual-route alignment with contrastive learning to enhance radar-camera feature congruence, improving detection accuracy and robustness \[[2504.16368](http://arxiv.org/abs/2504.16368)\]. Others utilize ray-constrained cross-attention to resolve depth ambiguity by constraining camera features along radar measurement rays, enhancing geometric correspondences and robustness to sensor dropout \[[2210.09267](http://arxiv.org/abs/2210.09267)\]. These advances highlight the importance of explicitly modeling spatial correspondences and uncertainty during fusion.

Calibration methods that simultaneously consider spatial and temporal alignment further improve fusion reliability. Continuous-time calibration approaches leverage radar velocity data and temporal synchronization without requiring retroreflectors, offering flexibility in dynamic environments \[[2103.07505](http://arxiv.org/abs/2103.07505)\]\[[2305.15883](http://arxiv.org/abs/2305.15883)\]. Moreover, automatic targetless calibration for multi-sensor systems integrating radars, cameras, and LiDARs has been explored by minimizing geometric error metrics between sensor data and environmental maps, supporting robustness even without overlapping fields of view \[[https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/0e88fc24ae7694d0b1a2fe78322936fc568bc7d2](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/0e88fc24ae7694d0b1a2fe78322936fc568bc7d2)\]\[[https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/0955326c67a947b5fac5fbae7238d04fc9adc251](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/0955326c67a947b5fac5fbae7238d04fc9adc251)\].

Recent research also emphasizes the need to handle radar data sparsity directly by processing raw radar signals (e.g., range-Doppler spectra) and integrating them with camera features in the BEV domain for enhanced spatial representation and detection performance \[[2411.13311](http://arxiv.org/abs/2411.13311)\]\[[https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/a94f8a34a9c4fce90727f62d652f2420fd1021a2](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/a94f8a34a9c4fce90727f62d652f2420fd1021a2)\]. Multilevel fusion strategies that exploit complementary attributes at semantic and spatial levels further enhance perception robustness, particularly under adverse weather conditions \[[https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/d2745dfb48594ed2b1f74b583893c2106ce55006](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/d2745dfb48594ed2b1f74b583893c2106ce55006)\].

In summary, the proposed method advances the state of the art by introducing a Dual-Perspective representation that combines frontal and bird’s-eye views to mitigate radar height uncertainty and leverage complementary spatial features. The Selective Fusion Mechanism selectively integrates reliable features from both views, while the Multi-Modal Cross-Attention mechanism explicitly establishes location correspondences across modalities. This approach builds upon and extends existing targetless calibration frameworks \[[https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/fa7f4199640500d48c0671181ea6a8dce3141123](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/fa7f4199640500d48c0671181ea6a8dce3141123)\]\[[https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/6aa3d55fc03c97e5e7b7385437e473caa2acb0c8](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/6aa3d55fc03c97e5e7b7385437e473caa2acb0c8)\]\[[2305.15883](http://arxiv.org/abs/2305.15883)\], cross-attention-based fusion models \[[2504.16368](http://arxiv.org/abs/2504.16368)\]\[[2210.09267](http://arxiv.org/abs/2210.09267)\], and spatiotemporal calibration techniques \[[2103.07505](http://arxiv.org/abs/2103.07505)\]\[[2305.15883](http://arxiv.org/abs/2305.15883)\], addressing key challenges in radar-camera geometric calibration for autonomous systems.
