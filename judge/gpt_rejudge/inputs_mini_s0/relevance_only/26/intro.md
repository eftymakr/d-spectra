## Related Works

Differential privacy (DP) has become the foundational framework for privacy-preserving data analysis, providing formal guarantees that individual data contributions remain confidential despite statistical releases \[[2008.03686](http://arxiv.org/abs/2008.03686)\]. However, the integrity of DP mechanisms in practice, especially in distributed or decentralized settings, is vulnerable to malicious behaviors by servers or participants that can undermine privacy or utility. Key threats include incorrect noise sampling, correlated noise generation, and manipulation of inputs or outputs, which can skew results or compromise privacy guarantees \[[2406.18940](http://arxiv.org/abs/2406.18940)\]\[[1911.02046](http://arxiv.org/abs/1911.02046)\]\[[2104.06569](http://arxiv.org/abs/2104.06569)\].

To mitigate such threats, the concept of verifiable differential privacy (VDP) has been introduced, incorporating cryptographic verification techniques to ensure that DP protocols are executed faithfully. Early approaches leveraged zero-knowledge proofs (ZKPs) to prove the correctness of noise addition and protocol adherence without revealing sensitive data, thus enabling verifiable outputs that uphold DP guarantees \[[2208.09011](http://arxiv.org/abs/2208.09011)\]\[[2406.18940](http://arxiv.org/abs/2406.18940)\]. While ZKPs are sufficient for verifiability, recent research shows they are not always necessary, as alternative cryptographic frameworks can also enforce compliance with DP protocols efficiently \[[2406.18940](http://arxiv.org/abs/2406.18940)\]\[[https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/ac8be9c934d6ff9ae095ebde81c8d3a57f7b918d](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/ac8be9c934d6ff9ae095ebde81c8d3a57f7b918d)\].

In the local differential privacy (LDP) model, where data is perturbed at the client side before aggregation, verifiability is particularly challenging due to distributed trust assumptions. Several works have addressed malicious client behaviors, such as input and output manipulation attacks that can poison aggregate statistics by skipping perturbation or submitting crafted data \[[https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/375e25dfc061ec12c2c870a2177d7c2abaa5f36e](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/375e25dfc061ec12c2c870a2177d7c2abaa5f36e)\]\[[1911.02046](http://arxiv.org/abs/1911.02046)\]\[[2104.06569](http://arxiv.org/abs/2104.06569)\]. Interactive verifiable protocols have been developed to confirm that clients correctly apply LDP mechanisms, often using interactive frameworks or zero-knowledge proofs optimized for numeric mean estimation and frequency estimation tasks \[[https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/35f195703efe69345d6405c5f691f2037475bb15](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/35f195703efe69345d6405c5f691f2037475bb15)\]\[[https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/375e25dfc061ec12c2c870a2177d7c2abaa5f36e](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/375e25dfc061ec12c2c870a2177d7c2abaa5f36e)\]\[[1905.06361](http://arxiv.org/abs/1905.06361)\].

The shuffle model, which adds an anonymity layer between clients and aggregators, also benefits from verifiable privacy mechanisms. Recent work proposes efficient verifiable LDP protocols in the shuffle model that prevent poisoning attacks while maintaining practical runtimes and minimal client-server interactions \[[2406.18940](http://arxiv.org/abs/2406.18940)\].

Beyond zero-knowledge proofs, cryptographic tools such as secure multi-party computation (MPC) and homomorphic encryption have been integrated with DP to enhance trust and privacy in distributed data collection. These approaches enable secure computation on encrypted or secret-shared data, allowing DP noise to be generated and verified within a distributed framework without a trusted server \[[https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/ac8be9c934d6ff9ae095ebde81c8d3a57f7b918d](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/ac8be9c934d6ff9ae095ebde81c8d3a57f7b918d)\]\[[2409.10667](http://arxiv.org/abs/2409.10667)\]\[[https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/35f195703efe69345d6405c5f691f2037475bb15](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/35f195703efe69345d6405c5f691f2037475bb15)\]\[[1911.01402](http://arxiv.org/abs/1911.01402)\]. For example, Cryptε demonstrates how two non-colluding servers can jointly execute DP programs on encrypted data, combining the accuracy of the central model with the privacy guarantees of the local model \[[https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/ac8be9c934d6ff9ae095ebde81c8d3a57f7b918d](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/ac8be9c934d6ff9ae095ebde81c8d3a57f7b918d)\]. Benchmarking secure noise sampling protocols under MPC further highlights the trade-offs between efficiency, security assumptions, and utility in such distributed DP systems \[[2409.10667](http://arxiv.org/abs/2409.10667)\].

Personalized differential privacy (PDP) and input-discriminative LDP extend the DP model by recognizing that different users or data types may require varying privacy levels. These approaches adapt noise injection or perturbation mechanisms to individual privacy preferences or input sensitivities, improving utility while maintaining rigorous privacy guarantees \[[1911.01402](http://arxiv.org/abs/1911.01402)\]\[[https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/e40f49994b0d5122e2c45ee04e38defb973a9d52](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/e40f49994b0d5122e2c45ee04e38defb973a9d52)\]\[[https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/f1ba5a27cf487335fc54c0dd38d54ccdbdade063](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/f1ba5a27cf487335fc54c0dd38d54ccdbdade063)\].

More recent advancements focus on improving the efficiency and robustness of verifiable DP mechanisms. For instance, verifiable randomized response (VRR) protocols have been optimized to reduce communication overheads and verifier costs by orders of magnitude, making verifiable LDP more practical for large-scale deployments \[[2406.18940](http://arxiv.org/abs/2406.18940)\]. Similarly, verifiable discrete Laplacian mechanisms have achieved significant improvements in proof generation efficiency while incurring minimal additional error compared to prior state-of-the-art methods \[[2406.18940](http://arxiv.org/abs/2406.18940)\].

Interactive proofs for DP counting queries address the challenge of verifying that DP noise was generated correctly without revealing the noise itself, mixing private and public randomness to enable verifiability with theoretical guarantees \[[2208.09011](http://arxiv.org/abs/2208.09011)\]. This line of work complements verifiable DP frameworks by providing formal tools to detect malicious manipulation of DP outputs.

In summary, the literature spans a broad spectrum of verifiable DP techniques, ranging from zero-knowledge proof-based protocols and interactive verification to cryptographic frameworks leveraging MPC and homomorphic encryption. These efforts collectively address the vulnerabilities of DP implementations in distributed settings and improve trust, efficiency, and utility. The proposed notion of Verifiable Distributed Differential Privacy (VDDP) and its efficient realizations, such as VDDLM and improved VRR, advance this landscape by offering scalable and practical verification mechanisms tailored to distributed client-server-verifier architectures \[[2406.18940](http://arxiv.org/abs/2406.18940)\].
