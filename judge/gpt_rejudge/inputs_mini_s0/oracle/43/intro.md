## Related Works

The training of large language models (LLMs) in distributed environments has driven extensive research into scalable parallelism strategies, hardware architectures, and performance modeling techniques to overcome challenges posed by model size, sequence length, and system complexity. Early foundational work introduced the Transformer architecture, which replaced recurrent and convolutional networks with attention mechanisms, enabling more parallelizable and efficient training of sequence models \[[1706.03762](http://arxiv.org/abs/1706.03762)\]. This architectural innovation set the stage for scaling LLMs to hundreds of billions of parameters, as seen in models like PaLM and Megatron-Turing NLG (MT-NLG) 530B, which leverage massive TPU and GPU clusters alongside advanced parallelism and data curation techniques to achieve state-of-the-art performance across diverse NLP tasks \[[2204.02311](http://arxiv.org/abs/2204.02311)\]\[[2201.1199](http://arxiv.org/abs/2201.11990)\].

Hardware innovations have been critical to enabling such scale. Google's TPU v4 supercomputer incorporates optical circuit switches for dynamic interconnect reconfiguration, improving scalability, power efficiency, and performance, and integrates specialized SparseCores to accelerate embedding-based workloads efficiently \[[2304.01433](http://arxiv.org/abs/2304.01433)\]. TPU v4’s energy-optimized design yields significant energy and carbon emission reductions compared to traditional data centers, highlighting the importance of hardware-software co-design for sustainable LLM training.

Parallelism strategies have evolved to address growing model complexity and longer input sequences. Traditional data, tensor, and pipeline parallelism techniques effectively scale batch size, model width, and depth, respectively, but struggle with memory constraints imposed by long sequences. To address this, sequence parallelism methods have been proposed to split sequences across devices, enabling training on much longer contexts than previously feasible. For example, sequence parallelism combined with ring-style communication enables handling sequences exceeding 100,000 tokens, significantly surpassing prior sparse attention approaches \[[2105.1312](http://arxiv.org/abs/2105.13120)\]. DeepSpeed-Ulysses advances this concept by providing a scalable, communication-efficient sequence parallelism method that maintains constant communication overhead as both sequence length and device count increase proportionally, achieving faster training on ultra-long sequences \[[2309.14509](http://arxiv.org/abs/2309.14509)\]. Complementarily, Ring Attention with Blockwise Transformers distributes attention computation and communication to support near-infinite context lengths without approximation or additional overhead \[[2310.01889](http://arxiv.org/abs/2310.01889)\].

Automatic and hybrid parallelism frameworks have also gained prominence. Galvatron introduces an automated search combining multiple parallelism dimensions to optimize distributed training plans dynamically, outperforming prior approaches constrained to limited parallelism types \[[2211.13878](http://arxiv.org/abs/2211.13878)\]. Similarly, GSPMD provides a compiler-based system that infers tensor partitioning from minimal user annotations, enabling scalable parallelization across thousands of TPU cores and facilitating training of trillion-parameter models \[[2105.04663](http://arxiv.org/abs/2105.04663)\].

Performance modeling and optimization tools are essential for navigating this complex design space. Daydream proposes a fine-grained dependency graph-based simulation framework to predict the runtime impact of DNN optimizations, allowing practitioners to estimate the efficacy of changes before deployment \[[2006.03318](http://arxiv.org/abs/2006.03318)\]. Such predictive tools are crucial given the high cost and complexity of LLM training setups.

Together, these advances in hardware, parallelism, and performance modeling form the foundation for tools like Lumos, which leverages trace-driven performance estimation to accurately capture LLM execution behavior and predict training performance across diverse configurations. By combining detailed runtime traces with comprehensive modeling, Lumos supports efficient exploration and optimization of large-scale LLM training environments, addressing gaps left by prior work in precise performance estimation and flexible prediction \[[2304.01433](http://arxiv.org/abs/2304.01433)\]\[[2204.02311](http://arxiv.org/abs/2204.02311)\]\[[2201.1199](http://arxiv.org/abs/2201.11990)\]\[[2105.04663](http://arxiv.org/abs/2105.04663)\]\[[2105.1312](http://arxiv.org/abs/2105.13120)\]\[[2309.14509](http://arxiv.org/abs/2309.14509)\]\[[2211.13878](http://arxiv.org/abs/2211.13878)\]\[[2310.01889](http://arxiv.org/abs/2310.01889)\]\[[2006.03318](http://arxiv.org/abs/2006.03318)\].
