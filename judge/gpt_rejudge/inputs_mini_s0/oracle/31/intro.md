## Related Works

Recent progress in DNA representation learning has been driven by advances in pre-training paradigms, particularly unsupervised and self-supervised approaches. Early works focused on unsupervised pre-training of DNA sequences, modeling raw nucleotide data to learn general sequence representations without explicit supervision \[[2411.11158](http://arxiv.org/abs/2411.11158)\]. However, pure sequence-based models often struggle to capture the complex regulatory mechanisms governing gene expression, as these depend heavily on contextual genomic profiles such as chromatin accessibility and histone modifications \[[2306.15794](http://arxiv.org/abs/2306.15794)\]. This limitation has motivated the exploration of supervised training paradigms that directly predict genomic profiles from DNA sequences, thereby integrating functional regulatory information into the learned representations \[[2411.11158](http://arxiv.org/abs/2411.11158)\]\[[2306.15794](http://arxiv.org/abs/2306.15794)\].

Self-supervised genomic language models have been proposed to learn from large unlabeled genome data, inspired by successes in natural language processing (NLP). For instance, models based on Transformer architectures have been adapted to genomics to predict chromatin states or gene expression from DNA sequences \[[2411.11158](http://arxiv.org/abs/2411.11158)\]. However, standard Transformer models face challenges scaling to the full length of genomic sequences due to quadratic attention complexity and the necessity of tokenization or fixed k-mer segmentation, which can lose single nucleotide resolution critical for capturing subtle variants such as SNPs \[[2306.15794](http://arxiv.org/abs/2306.15794)\]. To overcome these bottlenecks, HyenaDNA introduced a novel approach leveraging implicit convolutions and long-range sequence modeling up to 1 million tokens at single-nucleotide resolution, significantly outperforming prior Transformer-based DNA models while reducing computational cost \[[2306.15794](http://arxiv.org/abs/2306.15794)\].

In parallel, Mixture of Experts (MoE) architectures have emerged as a powerful method to increase model capacity efficiently by sparsely activating subsets of parameters for each input, enabling scaling to trillions of parameters without proportional computational overhead \[[2101.03961](http://arxiv.org/abs/2101.03961)\]\[[1701.06538](http://arxiv.org/abs/1701.06538)\]. Originally demonstrated in NLP tasks such as language modeling and machine translation, MoE layers selectively route inputs to specialized expert subnetworks, improving performance and training efficiency \[[1701.06538](http://arxiv.org/abs/1701.06538)\]. The Switch Transformer simplified MoE routing mechanisms, reducing communication and computational costs while enabling large-scale multilingual pretraining \[[2101.03961](http://arxiv.org/abs/2101.03961)\]. Despite their success in NLP and vision domains, MoE methods have been underexplored in genomics.

Our work addresses this gap by integrating MoE into supervised genomic profile prediction, exploiting the multi-species and multi-profile nature of genomic data. This approach allows the model to dynamically learn species- and profile-specific experts that collaboratively enhance DNA representation learning. Prior studies have suggested that supervised training on genomic profiles yields more biologically meaningful embeddings than purely unsupervised sequence pretraining \[[2411.11158](http://arxiv.org/abs/2411.11158)\]. By combining this with MoE, our model can better capture complex relationships across diverse genomic contexts and regulatory mechanisms, leading to improved performance on downstream tasks.

Moreover, existing foundational genomic models often do not explicitly leverage cross-species or multi-profile information jointly, which is critical given the evolutionary conservation and divergence of regulatory elements. Our Species-Profile Adaptive Collaborative Experts (SPACE) framework addresses this by learning specialized expert networks that adaptively collaborate based on species and profile inputs, enhancing model expressivity and generalization.

In summary, prior works have demonstrated the limitations of pure sequence-based unsupervised pretraining and highlighted the benefits of supervised genomic profile prediction for DNA representation learning \[[2411.11158](http://arxiv.org/abs/2411.11158)\]\[[2306.15794](http://arxiv.org/abs/2306.15794)\]. MoE architectures have proven effective for large-scale, efficient model scaling in NLP \[[2101.03961](http://arxiv.org/abs/2101.03961)\]\[[1701.06538](http://arxiv.org/abs/1701.06538)\], but have yet to be fully applied in genomics. Our work bridges these advances, proposing a novel supervised, multi-species, multi-profile MoE framework that achieves state-of-the-art results, thus advancing the frontier of DNA representation learning.
