## Related Works

Cross-domain sequential recommendation (CDSR) has gained significant attention due to its ability to leverage user interactions across multiple domains to enhance prediction of future behaviors \[[2012.00485](http://arxiv.org/abs/2012.00485)\]\[[2311.08272](http://arxiv.org/abs/2311.08272)\]\[[2304.03891](http://arxiv.org/abs/2304.03891)\]\[[2407.11245](http://arxiv.org/abs/2407.11245)\]. At its core, sequential recommendation relies heavily on modeling user behavior sequences to capture temporal dynamics and evolving preferences. Early methods employed Markov Chains and Recurrent Neural Networks (RNNs) to model these sequences, with RNNs enabling longer-term dependencies, particularly in denser datasets \[[1808.09781](http://arxiv.org/abs/1808.09781)\]\[[1511.06939](http://arxiv.org/abs/1511.06939)\]. However, these approaches often struggled with scalability and efficiency.

The introduction of self-attention mechanisms and Transformer architectures revolutionized sequential recommendation by enabling the modeling of long-range dependencies without recurrence or convolution \[[1706.03762](http://arxiv.org/abs/1706.03762)\]. Transformer-based models such as SASRec and BERT4Rec effectively capture user behavior patterns through self-attention, with BERT4Rec further enhancing sequence representation via bidirectional encoding and masked item prediction \[[1808.09781](http://arxiv.org/abs/1808.09781)\]\[[1904.0669](http://arxiv.org/abs/1904.06690)\]. These models have demonstrated superior performance and efficiency compared to traditional RNN-based approaches \[[1808.09781](http://arxiv.org/abs/1808.09781)\]\[[1904.0669](http://arxiv.org/abs/1904.06690)\].

In the context of CDSR, existing models predominantly build upon Transformer-based frameworks and attempt to improve performance by integrating domain-specific modules or components into the architecture \[[2012.00485](http://arxiv.org/abs/2012.00485)\]\[[2311.08272](http://arxiv.org/abs/2311.08272)\]. For example, mixed information flow networks incorporate behavior and knowledge transfer units to dynamically enrich sequence representations across domains \[[2012.00485](http://arxiv.org/abs/2012.00485)\]. Similarly, Mixed Attention Network (MAN) employs local and global attention mechanisms to separately extract domain-specific and cross-domain signals, utilizing item similarity and user group prototypes to facilitate knowledge transfer \[[2311.08272](http://arxiv.org/abs/2311.08272)\]. Despite such advancements, these methods often rely heavily on the presence of overlapping users across domains, limiting their applicability in scenarios with sparse or non-overlapping user sets \[[2311.08272](http://arxiv.org/abs/2311.08272)\]\[[2304.03891](http://arxiv.org/abs/2304.03891)\].

Beyond architecture enhancements, recent studies have emphasized the importance of effectively modeling inter- and intra-sequence item relationships to better capture cross-domain user preferences. Contrastive learning frameworks, such as C²DSR, leverage graph neural networks to mine collaborative relationships between items across sequences and employ contrastive objectives to maximize mutual information between single- and cross-domain representations, leading to more precise user modeling \[[2304.03891](http://arxiv.org/abs/2304.03891)\]. Additionally, cooperative learning paradigms have been proposed to mitigate negative transfer—where knowledge from unrelated domains degrades performance—by adaptively weighting losses and maximizing mutual information between single-domain and cross-domain tasks \[[2407.11245](http://arxiv.org/abs/2407.11245)\].

Data sparsity and cold-start challenges remain critical in CDSR. Methods like Sparse-Interest Network (SINE) address this by inferring sparse, multi-interest embeddings to capture diverse user intentions from a large pool of conceptual prototypes, thereby improving recommendation accuracy in sparse data scenarios \[[2102.09267](http://arxiv.org/abs/2102.09267)\]. Other approaches focus on session-based recommendations where user histories are short or unavailable, applying attention mechanisms to capture both sequential behavior and main user intent within sessions \[[1511.06939](http://arxiv.org/abs/1511.06939)\]\[[1711.04725](http://arxiv.org/abs/1711.04725)\].

While many CDSR models enhance the self-attention module by injecting domain-specific components, less attention has been given to improving the core self-attention mechanism itself. Foundational work on the Transformer architecture demonstrated the power and efficiency of pure attention models without recurrence or convolutions, revealing their ability to learn complex dependencies in sequential data \[[1706.03762](http://arxiv.org/abs/1706.03762)\]. Subsequent advances such as Longformer extended attention mechanisms to handle very long sequences efficiently, combining local and global attention to scale linearly with sequence length \[[2004.0515](http://arxiv.org/abs/2004.05150)\]. These developments highlight the potential for more efficient and effective attention mechanisms within sequential recommendation.

Recent works have also explored multi-objective optimization frameworks in CDSR to balance transfer and domain-specific learning. For instance, methods that dynamically adjust training objectives to mitigate negative transfer and encourage complementary knowledge exchange have shown promising results in improving cross-domain recommendation accuracy \[[2407.11245](http://arxiv.org/abs/2407.11245)\]. This perspective aligns with the formulation of CDSR as a multi-objective problem, optimizing recommendation performance while controlling cross-domain attention interactions.

In summary, the literature on sequential and cross-domain sequential recommendation has evolved from Markov and RNN models to Transformer-based architectures with sophisticated attention mechanisms. Current CDSR approaches often augment Transformers with domain-aware modules or knowledge transfer units but face limitations due to reliance on overlapping user data and potential negative transfer. Emerging trends emphasize the enhancement of the self-attention mechanism itself and the application of multi-objective optimization to dynamically balance cross-domain knowledge transfer, providing a promising direction for scalable and effective CDSR systems \[[1808.09781](http://arxiv.org/abs/1808.09781)\]\[[1706.03762](http://arxiv.org/abs/1706.03762)\]\[[2102.09267](http://arxiv.org/abs/2102.09267)\]\[[1904.0669](http://arxiv.org/abs/1904.06690)\]\[[2012.00485](http://arxiv.org/abs/2012.00485)\]\[[2311.08272](http://arxiv.org/abs/2311.08272)\]\[[2304.03891](http://arxiv.org/abs/2304.03891)\]\[[2004.0515](http://arxiv.org/abs/2004.05150)\]\[[2407.11245](http://arxiv.org/abs/2407.11245)\].
