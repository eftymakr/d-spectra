## Related Works

Neural rendering has become a prominent approach for reconstructing 3D scenes from 2D images by optimizing implicit representations of geometry and appearance across multiple views. Neural Radiance Fields (NeRFs) exemplify this paradigm, learning continuous functions to represent scenes and enabling novel view synthesis from arbitrary camera poses \[[2302.12231](http://arxiv.org/abs/2302.12231)\]. Despite their success, NeRFs and similar methods are fundamentally limited by the availability and density of input views, which is especially problematic in dynamic or complex scenes containing occlusions or unseen angles \[[2302.12231](http://arxiv.org/abs/2302.12231)\]\[[2403.05907](http://arxiv.org/abs/2403.05907)\]. This scarcity can result in under-constrained geometry and appearance reconstructions, leading to artifacts and reduced fidelity.

To mitigate these limitations, recent research has explored learning priors and augmenting data to improve reconstruction robustness. Diffusion models have been employed to regularize NeRF training by learning priors over RGBD patches, which help constrain geometry and color fields under sparse-view conditions, enhancing reconstruction quality and generalization \[[2302.12231](http://arxiv.org/abs/2302.12231)\]. Similarly, hybrid scene representations that integrate LiDAR geometry priors have been proposed to accelerate training and improve novel view synthesis in autonomous driving scenarios \[[2403.05907](http://arxiv.org/abs/2403.05907)\]. These approaches highlight the importance of incorporating additional priors and data augmentation to overcome sparse-view challenges.

Video frame interpolation (VFI) is a well-studied technique that synthesizes intermediate frames between existing video frames to improve temporal resolution and scene coverage. Classical VFI methods primarily rely on optical flow estimation and motion compensation but often struggle with large motions, occlusions, and lighting variations \[[1904.0083](http://arxiv.org/abs/1904.00830)\]. Deep learning-based VFI approaches have advanced the field by learning motion and appearance patterns directly from data, offering improved interpolation quality and efficiency \[[2506.01061](http://arxiv.org/abs/2506.01061)\]. Techniques such as Many-to-Many splatting enable efficient multi-frame interpolation by warping pixels in a bidirectional and context-aware manner, reducing artifacts and computational overhead \[[2204.03513](http://arxiv.org/abs/2204.03513)\]. Real-time methods like RIFE use neural networks to estimate intermediate flows end-to-end, achieving state-of-the-art performance with significantly faster speeds compared to prior work \[[2011.06294](http://arxiv.org/abs/2011.06294)\]. Additionally, attention-based modules that separately extract motion and appearance features have been introduced to improve both interpolation accuracy and computational efficiency \[[2303.0044](http://arxiv.org/abs/2303.00440)\].

Incorporating frame interpolation into neural rendering pipelines has emerged as an effective data augmentation strategy. Synthetic intermediate views generated by VFI can enrich sparse datasets, providing additional perspectives and implicitly encoding camera motion and scene geometry information. This augmentation helps alleviate the view scarcity problem, improving 3D reconstruction quality for both static and dynamic scenes. Prior work has demonstrated that such augmentation leads to enhanced photo supervision, thereby enabling more accurate and consistent neural scene representations \[[2409.02048](http://arxiv.org/abs/2409.02048)\]. Lightweight yet high-quality interpolation models trained on diverse video datasets enable effective implicit world priors, further boosting neural rendering performance without significantly increasing computational cost \[[2409.02048](http://arxiv.org/abs/2409.02048)\].

Beyond frame interpolation, other neural rendering advancements focus on improving high-resolution reconstruction and handling dynamic scenes. For instance, methods combining multilayer perceptrons with convolutional layers address parameter efficiency and misalignment issues caused by moving objects or calibration errors, recovering more high-frequency details in the rendered scenes \[[2211.09682](http://arxiv.org/abs/2211.09682)\]. Video diffusion models have also been adapted to synthesize novel views leveraging coarse 3D clues, enabling high-fidelity and consistent novel view synthesis from sparse inputs \[[2409.02048](http://arxiv.org/abs/2409.02048)\]. These complementary directions underscore the growing trend toward integrating learned priors, efficient interpolation, and hybrid representations to overcome the intrinsic limitations of sparse multi-view data.

In summary, the integration of video frame interpolation with neural rendering addresses key challenges in 3D reconstruction from sparse and dynamic data by generating additional intermediate views and embedding implicit priors about scene geometry and camera motion. Lightweight and efficient interpolation models, combined with diffusion-based priors and hybrid scene representations, collectively contribute to improving reconstruction fidelity, generalization, and computational efficiency in neural rendering tasks \[[2409.02048](http://arxiv.org/abs/2409.02048)\]\[[2302.12231](http://arxiv.org/abs/2302.12231)\]\[[2506.01061](http://arxiv.org/abs/2506.01061)\]\[[1904.0083](http://arxiv.org/abs/1904.00830)\]\[[2204.03513](http://arxiv.org/abs/2204.03513)\]\[[2011.06294](http://arxiv.org/abs/2011.06294)\]\[[2303.0044](http://arxiv.org/abs/2303.00440)\]\[[2403.05907](http://arxiv.org/abs/2403.05907)\]\[[2211.09682](http://arxiv.org/abs/2211.09682)\].
