## Related Works

Neural retrieval methods leveraging transformer-based pre-trained language models (PLMs) have significantly advanced multilingual and cross-lingual information retrieval (IR) by learning dense text representations that improve semantic matching beyond sparse lexical methods \[[2004.04906](http://arxiv.org/abs/2004.04906)\]\[[2112.09118](http://arxiv.org/abs/2112.09118)\]. However, their effectiveness on low-resource, morphologically rich languages such as Amharic remains underexplored due to challenges in data scarcity, suboptimal tokenization, and limited language-specific resources \[[https://aclanthology.org/2021.mrl-1.11/](https://aclanthology.org/2021.mrl-1.11/)\]\[[1906.01502](http://arxiv.org/abs/1906.01502)\]\[[2012.15613](http://arxiv.org/abs/2012.15613)\].

Multilingual models like multilingual BERT (mBERT) and XLM-RoBERTa (XLM-R) have become standard baselines for cross-lingual retrieval and transfer learning. mBERT is trained on 104 languages from monolingual corpora and demonstrates zero-shot cross-lingual transfer capabilities, including across different scripts, though its effectiveness varies with language similarity and typology \[[1906.01502](http://arxiv.org/abs/1906.01502)\]. XLM-R, trained on over 100 languages using extensive CommonCrawl data, outperforms mBERT notably on low-resource languages by balancing model capacity and training data scale, achieving strong results on cross-lingual benchmarks such as XNLI and MLQA \[[1911.02116](http://arxiv.org/abs/1911.02116)\]. Despite these advances, both models can suffer from performance degradation on morphologically complex, low-resource languages due to vocabulary and tokenizer limitations \[[2012.15613](http://arxiv.org/abs/2012.15613)\]. Research indicates that language-specific tokenizers and pre-training data size play critical roles in narrowing the performance gap between multilingual and monolingual models \[[2012.15613](http://arxiv.org/abs/2012.15613)\].

To address these limitations, recent work has explored the development of language-specific dense retrieval models fine-tuned on low-resource languages. For instance, Arctic-Embed 2.0 presents an open-source suite of multilingual text embedding models optimized for retrieval quality and efficiency, outperforming prior models on both English and multilingual benchmarks while supporting compressed embeddings through Matryoshka Representation Learning \[[2412.04506](http://arxiv.org/abs/2412.04506)\]. However, Arctic-Embed 2.0’s large model size (568M parameters) and multilingual focus may limit efficiency and effectiveness for specific languages like Amharic.

Dense retrieval models trained with contrastive learning have also shown promise for unsupervised and supervised IR, improving retrieval accuracy and cross-lingual transfer even with limited labeled data \[[2112.09118](http://arxiv.org/abs/2112.09118)\]\[[2007.00808](http://arxiv.org/abs/2007.00808)\]. Techniques such as Approximate Nearest Neighbor Negative Contrastive Estimation (ANCE) improve training by selecting realistic hard negatives, bridging the gap between training and testing distributions, and boosting dense retriever performance close to or surpassing traditional sparse methods like BM25 \[[2007.00808](http://arxiv.org/abs/2007.00808)\]. Furthermore, late interaction models like ColBERT enable efficient fine-grained matching by combining dense embeddings with token-level interactions, achieving state-of-the-art retrieval effectiveness \[[2004.04906](http://arxiv.org/abs/2004.04906)\]. These models are particularly valuable for morphologically rich languages where exact lexical matches are sparse or unreliable.

The challenges of low-resource IR extend beyond model architectures to data availability. The scarcity of large labeled datasets for Amharic and similar languages constrains supervised training and evaluation. Efforts to develop domain-specific datasets, including large-scale Amharic news classification corpora and ad hoc IR test collections, help fill this gap and support benchmarking retrieval models under realistic conditions \[[2103.05639](http://arxiv.org/abs/2103.05639)\]\[[https://doi.org/10.1007/978-3-030-58219-7_5](https://doi.org/10.1007/978-3-030-58219-7_5)\]. Additionally, pre-processing tools and embedding models tailored to Amharic have been investigated to improve downstream task performance \[[https://aclanthology.org/2021.winlp-1.5/](https://aclanthology.org/2021.winlp-1.5/)\].

Beyond Amharic, massively multilingual models such as SERENGETI have been developed to cover hundreds of African languages, demonstrating improved performance on natural language understanding tasks through extensive coverage and language genealogy-informed training \[[2212.10785](http://arxiv.org/abs/2212.10785)\]. These models highlight the importance of language-specific adaptation and resource investment to overcome low-resource challenges.

In summary, while multilingual transformer-based retrieval models have advanced cross-lingual IR, their application to Amharic faces unique hurdles including tokenization inefficiencies, limited training data, and morphological complexity. Language-specific dense retrieval models based on Amharic-pretrained BERT and RoBERTa backbones, combined with advanced training methods such as contrastive learning and late interaction architectures, offer promising directions to improve retrieval effectiveness over strong multilingual baselines \[[2402.05672](http://arxiv.org/abs/2402.05672)\]\[[2412.04506](http://arxiv.org/abs/2412.04506)\]\[[2004.04906](http://arxiv.org/abs/2004.04906)\]\[[2112.09118](http://arxiv.org/abs/2112.09118)\]. The creation of dedicated datasets and benchmarks further supports rigorous evaluation and progress in low-resource IR research \[[2103.05639](http://arxiv.org/abs/2103.05639)\]\[[https://doi.org/10.1007/978-3-030-58219-7_5](https://doi.org/10.1007/978-3-030-58219-7_5)\].
