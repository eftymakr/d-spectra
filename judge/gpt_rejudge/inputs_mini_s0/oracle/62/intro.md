## Related Works

Generative retrieval (GR) has recently emerged as an innovative paradigm in information retrieval, where a single large-scale neural model replaces traditional search index data structures by directly generating document identifiers (docids) through autoregressive decoding conditioned on user queries. This end-to-end differentiable search index approach offers advantages in efficiency and integration with large language models (LLMs) for knowledge-intensive tasks \[[2404.14851](http://arxiv.org/abs/2404.14851)\]\[[2202.06991](http://arxiv.org/abs/2202.06991)\]\[[2310.12455](http://arxiv.org/abs/2310.12455)\]\[[2312.11036](http://arxiv.org/abs/2312.11036)\]. GR models encode the entire corpus within their parameters, enabling retrieval by generating relevant docids without relying on explicit indexes or embeddings \[[2202.06991](http://arxiv.org/abs/2202.06991)\]\[[2401.10487](http://arxiv.org/abs/2401.10487)\].

Early foundational work introduced the Differentiable Search Index (DSI), which formulates retrieval as a text-to-text mapping from queries to docids learned by a single Transformer model \[[2202.06991](http://arxiv.org/abs/2202.06991)\]. Subsequent studies refined how to represent docids, exploring semantic identifiers rather than simple integers, and mitigating distributional mismatches between indexing and retrieval queries via query generation techniques \[[2206.10128](http://arxiv.org/abs/2206.10128)\]\[[2305.15115](http://arxiv.org/abs/2305.15115)\]. These improvements enhance document memorization and relevance matching, demonstrating that generative retrieval can outperform strong baselines such as BM25 and dense retrieval models on small to medium-scale corpora \[[2202.06991](http://arxiv.org/abs/2202.06991)\]\[[2305.15115](http://arxiv.org/abs/2305.15115)\]\[[2401.10487](http://arxiv.org/abs/2401.10487)\].

Several approaches have addressed the challenge of defining effective document identifiers, a critical factor influencing retrieval performance. Traditional fixed or rule-based docids (e.g., titles or clustering results) often fail to capture the semantic richness of documents. To overcome this, learning-based tokenization methods have been proposed to produce discrete, semantically meaningful docids in an end-to-end manner using autoencoding frameworks \[[2304.04171](http://arxiv.org/abs/2304.04171)\]. Other works integrate relevance information when constructing docids, leveraging quantized relevance-based document representations to better align generation probability with document relevance \[[2311.09134](http://arxiv.org/abs/2311.09134)\]\[[2404.146](http://arxiv.org/abs/2404.14600)\]. These methods have yielded significant gains on benchmarks like NQ320k and MS MARCO, especially in unseen document retrieval scenarios \[[2304.04171](http://arxiv.org/abs/2304.04171)\]\[[2311.09134](http://arxiv.org/abs/2311.09134)\].

The intersection between generative retrieval and dense retrieval paradigms has been rigorously analyzed. Generative retrieval with atomic docids has been shown to be equivalent to single-vector dense retrieval, while hierarchical or multi-vector generative retrieval analogizes hierarchical tree search or multi-vector dense retrieval frameworks \[[2404.00684](http://arxiv.org/abs/2404.00684)\]\[[2306.11397](http://arxiv.org/abs/2306.11397)\]. This theoretical unification explains the shared mechanisms underlying relevance scoring, such as the computation of similarity as sums of dot products and alignment matrices between query and document representations. Such insights have inspired hybrid models combining generative and dense components to leverage both deep interaction and scalability \[[2401.10487](http://arxiv.org/abs/2401.10487)\].

Memory capacity and scalability remain key challenges for generative retrieval. Since GR models store corpus knowledge in parameters, increasing corpus size degrades fine-grained memorization and complicates index updating for dynamic corpora. To address these issues, frameworks combining generative and dense retrieval have been proposed, using generative models for coarse cluster retrieval and dense methods for fine intra-cluster matching \[[2401.10487](http://arxiv.org/abs/2401.10487)\]. Incremental learning methods further enable efficient indexing of new documents without full retraining, employing partial quantization updates and memory-augmented learning to preserve old knowledge \[[2308.14968](http://arxiv.org/abs/2308.14968)\]\[[2307.10323](http://arxiv.org/abs/2307.10323)\]. Few-shot indexing techniques utilizing LLM prompting have also been introduced to create docid banks without expensive model training \[[2408.02152](http://arxiv.org/abs/2408.02152)\].

Beam search, a common decoding strategy in generative retrieval, has been scrutinized for its limitations. While it enables efficient search among candidate docids, beam search relies on marginal likelihoods that may not align with the optimal retrieval objective, potentially causing suboptimal ranking and error accumulation \[[2006.15408](http://arxiv.org/abs/2006.15408)\]. Recent theoretical analyses have explored the Bayesian optimality of tree-based retrieval models under beam search, revealing intrinsic gaps between model predictions and retrieval performance. These findings motivate novel training algorithms to bridge the discrepancy between training and inference under beam search \[[2006.15408](http://arxiv.org/abs/2006.15408)\]. Additionally, constrained generation techniques aim to enforce corpus-specific constraints during decoding, improving the reliability of generated docids \[[2404.14851](http://arxiv.org/abs/2404.14851)\].

The robustness and generalization of generative retrieval models on out-of-distribution (OOD) corpora remain under active investigation. Empirical studies indicate that current GR models struggle with query variations, unseen query types, and corpus expansions, highlighting the need for improved generalization strategies \[[2412.18768](http://arxiv.org/abs/2412.18768)\]. Some recent efforts propose learning-to-rank frameworks tailored for generative retrieval, directly optimizing the model toward final passage ranking objectives rather than intermediate docid generation likelihoods, thereby enhancing overall retrieval effectiveness \[[2306.15222](http://arxiv.org/abs/2306.15222)\].

Finally, unified frameworks that jointly model generative retrieval and downstream tasks such as grounded answer generation have been developed. By integrating retrieval and generation into a single encoder-decoder architecture with shared parameters and cross-task connectors, these models achieve superior end-to-end performance on benchmarks like MS MARCO and Natural Questions \[[2312.11036](http://arxiv.org/abs/2312.11036)\]. This integration reflects the broader trend of synthesizing classical IR methods with generative language models to create domain-expert systems capable of justifying their outputs via retrieved evidence \[[2105.02274](http://arxiv.org/abs/2105.02274)\]\[[2312.11036](http://arxiv.org/abs/2312.11036)\].

In summary, the landscape of generative retrieval research encompasses theoretical analyses of autoregressive decoding and beam search, innovations in docid representation and indexing, hybrid retrieval architectures combining generative and dense methods, continual learning for dynamic corpora, and unified retrieval-generation frameworks. These advances collectively deepen our understanding of the limitations and potentials of generative retrieval, guiding future work toward more robust, scalable, and generalizable neural retrieval systems \[[2404.14851](http://arxiv.org/abs/2404.14851)\]\[[2202.06991](http://arxiv.org/abs/2202.06991)\]\[[2304.04171](http://arxiv.org/abs/2304.04171)\]\[[2404.00684](http://arxiv.org/abs/2404.00684)\]\[[2306.11397](http://arxiv.org/abs/2306.11397)\]\[[2408.02152](http://arxiv.org/abs/2408.02152)\]\[[2306.15222](http://arxiv.org/abs/2306.15222)\]\[[2312.11036](http://arxiv.org/abs/2312.11036)\]\[[2206.10128](http://arxiv.org/abs/2206.10128)\]\[[2401.10487](http://arxiv.org/abs/2401.10487)\]\[[2308.14968](http://arxiv.org/abs/2308.14968)\]\[[2006.15408](http://arxiv.org/abs/2006.15408)\].
