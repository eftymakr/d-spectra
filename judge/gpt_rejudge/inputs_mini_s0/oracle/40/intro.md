## Related Works

Zero-Knowledge Proofs (ZKPs) have become fundamental in privacy-preserving and verifiable computation, allowing a prover to convince a verifier of a statement's validity without revealing underlying data. Despite their broad applicability, including blockchain, verifiable machine learning, and electronic voting, the computational cost of proof generation remains a critical bottleneck \[[2408.05890](http://arxiv.org/abs/2408.05890)\]. To address this, recent research has concentrated on accelerating the core computational primitives of ZKP protocols, particularly Multi-Scalar Multiplications (MSMs) and Number Theoretic Transforms (NTTs).

SZKP presents a pioneering ASIC-based accelerator that fully integrates the proof generation process on-chip by leveraging structured dataflows tailored for NTT and MSM operations. This approach achieves speedups exceeding 400× compared to CPU implementations and significantly outperforms existing GPU and ASIC solutions \[[2408.05890](http://arxiv.org/abs/2408.05890)\]. The design addresses challenges related to irregular memory access patterns in butterfly computations for NTTs and data-dependent memory accesses in MSM calculations, particularly those using Pippenger's algorithm \[[2408.05890](http://arxiv.org/abs/2408.05890)\].

Complementing ASIC efforts, multiple GPU-accelerated MSM optimizations have been proposed. For example, cuZK and MSMAC focus on parallelizing MSM computations on GPUs, exploiting algorithmic refinements and parallel architectures to improve throughput \[[https://eprint.iacr.org/2022/1321](https://eprint.iacr.org/2022/1321)\]\[[https://doi.org/10.1145/3649329.3655672](https://doi.org/10.1145/3649329.3655672)\]. Elastic (MSM) introduces a modular preprocessing technique to adapt MSM computations elastically across GPU resources, enhancing scalability and efficiency \[[https://eprint.iacr.org/2024/057](https://eprint.iacr.org/2024/057)\]. BatchZK further explores GPU acceleration by implementing a fully pipelined system for batch generation of ZKPs, facilitating high-throughput proof generation in practical scenarios \[[https://eprint.iacr.org/2024/1862](https://eprint.iacr.org/2024/1862)\]. Multi-GPU systems have also been leveraged to distribute MSM workloads, achieving enhanced performance by balancing memory and computational demands \[[https://doi.org/10.1145/3620666.3651364](https://doi.org/10.1145/3620666.3651364)\]. These GPU-based approaches collectively emphasize the importance of parallelism and memory-aware algorithms in accelerating ZKP primitives.

Further architectural innovations include load-balanced, fully pipelined Montgomery multipliers for combined NTT/MSM acceleration, which improve throughput and resource utilization in hardware implementations \[[https://tches.iacr.org/index.php/TCHES/article/view/11930](https://tches.iacr.org/index.php/TCHES/article/view/11930)\]. Chiplet-based designs have also been explored to enable scalable and memory-efficient MSM acceleration by partitioning computation across interconnected chiplets, addressing scalability constraints in large-scale accelerators \[[https://eprint.iacr.org/2025/252](https://eprint.iacr.org/2025/252)\].

While zkSNARKs have been widely studied due to their succinct proofs and fast verification, their proof generation remains computationally intensive, motivating middleware solutions that integrate ZKP capabilities into broader systems, such as zkFi for cross-chain privacy-preserving transactions \[[https://eprint.iacr.org/2019/458](https://eprint.iacr.org/2019/458)\]. However, many zkSNARK variants rely on trusted setups that are application-specific, limiting flexibility.

The HyperPlonk protocol, which zkSpeed targets, addresses these limitations by supporting a one-time, universal trusted setup combined with small proof sizes suitable for publicly verifiable, consensus-based environments. Prior accelerations have mostly focused on individual primitives like SumCheck or MSMs but have not delivered full-protocol acceleration with comparable scalability and efficiency \[[2408.05890](http://arxiv.org/abs/2408.05890)\]. zkSpeed advances this by integrating hardware acceleration across the entire proof generation pipeline, including SumCheck and MSM, achieving geometric mean speedups of 801× over CPU baselines through a carefully engineered full-chip architecture with 366.46 mm² area and 2 TB/s bandwidth \[[2408.05890](http://arxiv.org/abs/2408.05890)\].

In addition to hardware acceleration, zero-knowledge proof systems have expanded into application domains like verifiable deep learning training, where zkDL demonstrates efficient ZKPs for non-linear neural network computations, highlighting the growing intersection of ZKPs with privacy-preserving AI \[[https://www.usenix.org/conference/usenixsecurity23/presentation/xiong](https://www.usenix.org/conference/usenixsecurity23/presentation/xiong)\].

Overall, the landscape of ZKP acceleration encompasses a spectrum of approaches from ASIC and GPU optimizations of core primitives to full-protocol accelerators like zkSpeed. These efforts collectively push the boundaries of practical ZKP deployment by mitigating computational overheads and addressing setup flexibility, proof size, and verification cost challenges \[[2408.05890](http://arxiv.org/abs/2408.05890)\]\[[https://eprint.iacr.org/2022/1321](https://eprint.iacr.org/2022/1321)\]\[[https://doi.org/10.1145/3649329.3655672](https://doi.org/10.1145/3649329.3655672)\]\[[https://eprint.iacr.org/2024/1862](https://eprint.iacr.org/2024/1862)\]\[[https://eprint.iacr.org/2024/057](https://eprint.iacr.org/2024/057)\]\[[https://doi.org/10.1145/3620666.3651364](https://doi.org/10.1145/3620666.3651364)\]\[[https://tches.iacr.org/index.php/TCHES/article/view/11930](https://tches.iacr.org/index.php/TCHES/article/view/11930)\]\[[https://eprint.iacr.org/2025/252](https://eprint.iacr.org/2025/252)\]\[[https://eprint.iacr.org/2019/458](https://eprint.iacr.org/2019/458)\]\[[https://www.usenix.org/conference/usenixsecurity23/presentation/xiong](https://www.usenix.org/conference/usenixsecurity23/presentation/xiong)\].
