## Related Works

Model calibration is essential for producing reliable probabilistic predictions that reflect true likelihoods, particularly in ranking systems where both relative order and absolute accuracy matter. Traditional calibration techniques often rely on predefined, order-preserving transformation functions, such as piece-wise linear mappings, to adjust predictions post hoc. While these methods maintain monotonicity and relative order, their fixed functional forms limit expressiveness and flexibility, constraining effectiveness in complex, real-world calibration scenarios \[[2202.04348](http://arxiv.org/abs/2202.04348)\]\[[1905.10713](http://arxiv.org/abs/1905.10713)\].

Binning-based calibration methods, such as Multiple Boosting Calibration Trees (MBCT), improve calibration by integrating feature-aware binning schemes and individualized adjustments within bins, addressing the shortcomings of standard binning approaches that treat all samples within a bin identically \[[2202.04348](http://arxiv.org/abs/2202.04348)\]. However, these methods often remain limited by the discrete nature of binning and may struggle with fine-grained calibration needs.

Field-aware calibration approaches have been developed to tackle calibration discrepancies that arise within specific feature subsets or "fields," such as user or item IDs in advertising, where data sparsity and distribution shifts pose challenges. Neural Calibration and Deep Ensemble Shape Calibration (DESC) represent advances in this direction, leveraging multi-field information and adaptive calibration functions to improve calibration accuracy across heterogeneous data segments \[[1905.10713](http://arxiv.org/abs/1905.10713)\]\[[2401.09507](http://arxiv.org/abs/2401.09507)\]\[[2402.17655](http://arxiv.org/abs/2402.17655)\]. These methods emphasize the importance of flexible calibration functions and adaptive mechanisms to handle varying data distributions.

Joint optimization strategies have been proposed to reconcile calibration and ranking objectives, which are often at odds in learning-to-rank models. Methods like Joint Ranking and Calibration (JRC) and Regression Compatible Ranking (RCR) integrate ranking and calibration losses to improve both aspects simultaneously, aligning output scores with calibrated probabilities while preserving ranking quality \[[2208.06164](http://arxiv.org/abs/2208.06164)\]\[[2211.01494](http://arxiv.org/abs/2211.01494)\]. Similarly, Calibration-compatible Listwise Distillation (CLID) extends this idea to privileged feature distillation, ensuring that student models retain both ranking ability and calibration quality \[[2312.08727](http://arxiv.org/abs/2312.08727)\]. Despite these advances, balancing the conflicting demands of ranking and calibration remains challenging, as naive multi-objective losses can yield suboptimal trade-offs \[[2406.08010](http://arxiv.org/abs/2406.08010)\].

Recent research has explored the application of neural networks for flexible and expressive calibration functions. Monotonic neural networks, such as Unconstrained Monotonic Neural Networks (UMNNs), enable learning arbitrary monotonic transformations without restricting the calibration function to fixed parametric forms. This approach increases flexibility while preserving order, preventing excessive distortion of original predictions \[[2112.07428](http://arxiv.org/abs/2112.07428)\]. Parametric calibration methods using distributions like Gaussian or Gamma have also been proposed to map ranking scores to well-calibrated probabilities, guided by unbiased empirical risk minimization frameworks to correct for dataset biases \[[2112.07428](http://arxiv.org/abs/2112.07428)\]. These methods demonstrate the growing trend toward leveraging differentiable, learnable calibration mappings to handle complex calibration needs in personalized ranking and recommendation systems.

Uncertainty estimation and posterior probability modeling have been integrated into calibration approaches to enhance probabilistic reliability. Doubly-adaptive calibration methods, such as AdaCalib, utilize isotonic regression guided by posterior statistics and field-adaptive mechanisms to refine calibration on a per-field basis, effectively managing data sparsity and distribution shifts in online advertising contexts \[[2205.07295](http://arxiv.org/abs/2205.07295)\]. Confidence-aware multi-field calibration further addresses challenges posed by limited samples in certain feature fields by adaptively modulating calibration intensity based on confidence derived from sample statistics \[[2402.17655](http://arxiv.org/abs/2402.17655)\].

In summary, existing calibration methods span a spectrum from simple, fixed-form transformations to complex, adaptive, and neural network-based approaches. While traditional methods focus on global calibration with limited flexibility, modern approaches emphasize field-aware, multi-objective, and neural calibration strategies to better capture the nuanced relationship between predicted scores and true probabilities. Our work builds upon these foundations by employing a UMNN-based calibrator to learn highly flexible monotonic functions, coupled with a novel Smooth Calibration Loss that enforces desirable smoothness properties. This combination addresses the expressiveness limitations of prior methods while maintaining order preservation, aligning with recent trends toward integrating powerful neural architectures for improved calibration in ranking systems \[[2202.04348](http://arxiv.org/abs/2202.04348)\]\[[1905.10713](http://arxiv.org/abs/1905.10713)\]\[[2208.06164](http://arxiv.org/abs/2208.06164)\]\[[2211.01494](http://arxiv.org/abs/2211.01494)\]\[[2312.08727](http://arxiv.org/abs/2312.08727)\]\[[2112.07428](http://arxiv.org/abs/2112.07428)\]\[[2205.07295](http://arxiv.org/abs/2205.07295)\]\[[2406.08010](http://arxiv.org/abs/2406.08010)\]\[[2402.17655](http://arxiv.org/abs/2402.17655)\].
