## Related Works

The study of protein dynamics is fundamental to understanding their biological functions, traditionally relying on molecular dynamics (MD) simulations to explore conformational ensembles. However, MD simulations are computationally expensive and often hindered by rare event sampling and slow equilibration, limiting their practical utility for large-scale or general systems \[[2306.03117](http://arxiv.org/abs/2306.03117)\]\[[2403.14088](http://arxiv.org/abs/2403.14088)\]. To address these challenges, recent research has focused on leveraging deep generative models, particularly diffusion-based approaches, to efficiently sample protein conformational landscapes.

Diffusion models have shown significant promise in protein structure prediction and conformation generation by learning distributions over crystallographic structures. For example, ConfDiff integrates force-guided SE(3) diffusion with data-driven score models to incorporate physical prior knowledge, improving the fidelity and diversity of generated conformations beyond traditional diffusion methods that lack explicit physical supervision \[[2403.14088](http://arxiv.org/abs/2403.14088)\]. Similarly, EigenFold employs a diffusion framework modeling protein structures as harmonic oscillators, enabling a cascading-resolution generative process that captures conformational heterogeneity and achieves high accuracy in structure prediction tasks \[[2304.02198](http://arxiv.org/abs/2304.02198)\]\[[2402.04845](http://arxiv.org/abs/2402.04845)\].

Complementing diffusion approaches, Str2Str offers a zero-shot conformation sampling framework based on score matching trained on general crystal structures, bypassing the need for costly simulation data and accelerating sampling by orders of magnitude compared to long MD runs \[[2306.03117](http://arxiv.org/abs/2306.03117)\]. AlphaFlow and ESMFlow repurpose highly accurate single-state predictors within a flow-matching generative framework, facilitating rapid generation of diverse conformational ensembles that better capture protein flexibility than traditional AlphaFold models or replicate MD trajectories \[[2402.04845](http://arxiv.org/abs/2402.04845)\]. Boltzmann Generators take a related approach using neural networks to learn coordinate transformations that enable unbiased one-shot equilibrium sampling of complex molecular systems, including proteins, effectively circumventing rare event bottlenecks inherent in MD \[[1812.01729](http://arxiv.org/abs/1812.01729)\].

While these generative models enhance sampling efficiency and diversity, integrating physical energy-based supervision remains a major challenge. Standard energy-based objectives are often intractable for direct optimization within deep generative frameworks. To address this, recent works have proposed methods that incorporate physical constraints or force information to guide generation. For instance, ConfDiff explicitly incorporates forces in its diffusion process, providing physical guidance that aligns generated conformations closer to equilibrium distributions \[[2403.14088](http://arxiv.org/abs/2403.14088)\]. Similarly, Distributional Graphormer (DiG) transforms simple distributions toward equilibrium distributions conditioned on molecular descriptors, inspired by thermodynamic annealing, enabling efficient sampling across diverse molecular tasks including protein conformations \[[2306.05445](http://arxiv.org/abs/2306.05445)\].

Another promising direction involves modeling protein conformations in latent or discrete spaces to improve generation efficiency. Structure Language Models (SLM) encode protein conformations into compact latent representations and apply conditional language modeling to capture sequence-specific conformational diversity, yielding up to 100-fold speedups compared to traditional methods while maintaining interpretability and diversity \[[2410.18403](http://arxiv.org/abs/2410.18403)\].

Generative modeling paradigms have also been extended to learn from MD trajectories directly. By conditioning on frames or parts of molecular systems, these models can perform forward simulation, transition path sampling, and dynamics-conditioned molecular design, broadening applications beyond static conformation generation \[[2409.17808](http://arxiv.org/abs/2409.17808)\].

Moreover, reinforcement learning techniques have begun to be explored for optimizing generative models toward downstream objectives that may involve physical plausibility or biological relevance, beyond likelihood maximization. Methods like denoising diffusion policy optimization (DDPO) optimize diffusion models using multi-step decision-making frameworks, enabling alignment with complex reward functions derived from domain-specific criteria \[[2305.13301](http://arxiv.org/abs/2305.13301)\].

In summary, recent advances in deep generative modeling for protein conformation sampling leverage diffusion and flow-based frameworks, often augmented with physical priors or force guidance, to overcome limitations of traditional MD simulations. Approaches such as Str2Str and Boltzmann Generators emphasize efficient zero-shot or one-shot sampling, while others like ConfDiff and DiG integrate physical energy or force information to improve physical realism. Latent space modeling and reinforcement learning further enhance efficiency and alignment with biological objectives. These efforts collectively form the foundation for methods like Energy-based Alignment (EBA), which aim to calibrate generative models with physical feedback to balance conformational states according to their energy differences, advancing the generation of physically plausible protein ensembles \[[2306.03117](http://arxiv.org/abs/2306.03117)\]\[[2403.14088](http://arxiv.org/abs/2403.14088)\]\[[1812.01729](http://arxiv.org/abs/1812.01729)\]\[[2304.02198](http://arxiv.org/abs/2304.02198)\]\[[2306.05445](http://arxiv.org/abs/2306.05445)\]\[[2305.13301](http://arxiv.org/abs/2305.13301)\]\[[2410.18403](http://arxiv.org/abs/2410.18403)\]\[[2402.04845](http://arxiv.org/abs/2402.04845)\]\[[2409.17808](http://arxiv.org/abs/2409.17808)\].
