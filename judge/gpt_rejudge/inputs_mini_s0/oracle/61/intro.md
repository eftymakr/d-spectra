## Related Works

Personalized outfit generation focuses on assembling compatible and user-tailored fashion items into cohesive outfits. Early approaches emphasized compatibility and personalization through data-driven recommendation frameworks. For instance, Alibaba's Personalized Outfit Generation (POG) model connects user preferences on individual items and outfits using Transformer architectures, leveraging large-scale datasets to improve both compatibility and personalization in outfit recommendations \[[1905.01866](http://arxiv.org/abs/1905.01866)\]. Similarly, Hierarchical Fashion Graph Networks (HFGN) model user-outfit-item relationships via graph neural networks to jointly optimize compatibility and personalization, achieving state-of-the-art performance on benchmark datasets \[[2005.12566](http://arxiv.org/abs/2005.12566)\].

The rise of generative AI has introduced novel paradigms for outfit recommendation and generation. Traditional outfit recommendation methods were limited to selecting from existing fashion products, constraining diversity and personalization. Diffusion-based generative models, such as DiFashion, have been proposed to enable Generative Outfit Recommendation (GOR), synthesizing multiple fashion items in parallel conditioned on user preferences to deliver high-fidelity, compatible, and personalized outfits \[[2402.17279](http://arxiv.org/abs/2402.17279)\]. Such models surpass classical retrieval-based methods by generating novel fashion images rather than only recommending from fixed pools.

Virtual try-on methods represent a related direction focusing on realistic garment transfer onto person images. Early works like VITON use coarse-to-fine image synthesis to overlay target clothing on human images without 3D information \[[1711.08447](http://arxiv.org/abs/1711.08447)\]. More recent approaches employ diffusion models combined with warping modules to preserve fine clothing details while generating high-quality try-on images, such as the Diffusion-based Conditional Inpainting for Virtual Try-ON (DCI-VTON) \[[2308.06101](http://arxiv.org/abs/2308.06101)\] and StableVITON, which learns semantic correspondence between clothing and body in latent diffusion spaces \[[2312.01725](http://arxiv.org/abs/2312.01725)\]. These advancements provide complementary tools for outfit generation systems that require realistic visualization.

Ensuring compatibility among fashion items remains a critical challenge. Compatibility Family Learning introduces latent space embeddings that model asymmetric and broad notions of compatibility, enabling generation of diverse and compatible fashion items through conditional GANs \[[1712.01262](http://arxiv.org/abs/1712.01262)\]. Complementary product recommendation frameworks like CRAFT utilize adversarial feature transformers to learn visual cues for complementary item generation, enhancing diversity and relevance \[[1804.10871](http://arxiv.org/abs/1804.10871)\]. Hybrid frameworks such as the Smart Fitting Room integrate retrieval and generative modules to balance personalized recommendations with high-quality try-on effects, emphasizing matching-aware virtual try-on beyond image synthesis quality \[[2401.16825](http://arxiv.org/abs/2401.16825)\].

Fine-tuning generative models to align with user preferences often employs reinforcement learning from human feedback (RLHF). However, RLHF can be complex and computationally intensive. Direct Preference Optimization (DPO) offers a stable, lightweight alternative for fine-tuning language models by directly optimizing on pairwise human preference data without explicit reward models \[[2305.1829](http://arxiv.org/abs/2305.18290)\]. This approach has been adapted for diffusion models (Diffusion-DPO), improving alignment with human preferences and enhancing visual appeal and prompt adherence in text-to-image generation \[[2311.12908](http://arxiv.org/abs/2311.12908)\]. Further refinements, such as D3PO, enable direct fine-tuning of diffusion models without reward model training, reducing computational overhead while maintaining quality and safety \[[2311.13231](http://arxiv.org/abs/2311.13231)\].

In personalized image generation, finetuning-free methods have gained interest for their efficiency, though often at the cost of image fidelity. PatchDPO extends DPO by estimating patch-level quality in generated images, enabling more granular optimization and improved consistency with reference images in personalized generation tasks \[[2412.03177](http://arxiv.org/abs/2412.03177)\]. Similarly, Joint-Image Diffusion models (JeDi) learn joint distributions of related image-text pairs to enable finetuning-free personalization with state-of-the-art quality \[[2407.06187](http://arxiv.org/abs/2407.06187)\].

Multi-expert feedback generation is crucial for providing comprehensive and objective evaluation signals during model fine-tuning. By considering multiple perspectives such as quality, compatibility, and personalization, feedback modules can generate richer supervision to guide model updates effectively. Such approaches have been shown to enhance the alignment of generative models with diverse user preferences without relying on handcrafted reward functions \[[2401.16825](http://arxiv.org/abs/2401.16825)\]\[[2306.03395](http://arxiv.org/abs/2306.03395)\].

Overall, the intersection of compatibility modeling, generative diffusion models, and preference-based fine-tuning represents a promising avenue for advancing personalized outfit generation. FashionDPO’s strategy of leveraging direct preference optimization with multi-expert feedback builds on these foundations, addressing limitations of traditional supervised learning and enabling more flexible, scalable fine-tuning of fashion generative models.
