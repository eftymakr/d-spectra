## Related Works

The integration of knowledge graphs (KGs) with large language models (LLMs) to enhance reasoning for claim verification has attracted growing attention. Traditional fact verification datasets predominantly focus on unstructured text sources such as Wikipedia articles. For example, FEVER introduced a large-scale dataset of claims verified against Wikipedia sentences, highlighting challenges in evidence retrieval and classification into Supported, Refuted, or NotEnoughInfo categories \[[1803.05355](http://arxiv.org/abs/1803.05355)\]. Similarly, HoVer extends this by requiring multi-hop fact extraction across multiple Wikipedia articles, emphasizing the complexity of long-range and multi-sentence reasoning dependencies that degrade standard semantic-matching models’ performance as hops increase \[[2011.03088](http://arxiv.org/abs/2011.03088)\]. These datasets underline the limitations of text-based verification and the need for multi-hop reasoning mechanisms.

More recently, FactKG has been proposed as a large dataset tailored for fact verification via reasoning on knowledge graphs, containing 108k claims requiring diverse reasoning types such as one-hop, conjunction, negation, and multi-hop over graph-structured data \[[2305.06590](http://arxiv.org/abs/2305.06590)\]. FactKG includes various linguistic styles to enhance practical applicability and presents a baseline that leverages KG reasoning to improve verification reliability. This dataset exemplifies the growing trend of utilizing structured knowledge sources to complement or replace unstructured text in verification tasks.

On the modeling side, recent approaches have sought to synergize LLMs with KGs to overcome LLMs’ limitations in multi-step, modular reasoning and hallucination tendencies. The Reasoning on Graphs (RoG) framework exemplifies this by generating relation paths grounded in KGs as faithful plans, enabling LLMs to conduct interpretable, faithful reasoning over retrieved KG subgraphs, improving both performance and explainability \[[2310.01061](http://arxiv.org/abs/2310.01061)\]. Likewise, Program-Guided Fact-Checking (ProgramFC) leverages LLMs’ in-context learning to decompose complex claims into modular sub-tasks executed by specialized handlers, yielding explicit reasoning programs that enhance interpretability and data efficiency \[[2305.12744](http://arxiv.org/abs/2305.12744)\]. These modular, programmatic approaches address the challenge of multi-step reasoning and provide transparent explanations critical for trustworthiness.

Explainability is further emphasized in First-Order-Logic-Guided Knowledge-Grounded (FOLK) reasoning, which translates claims into first-order logic clauses and performs reasoning over knowledge-grounded question-answer pairs using LLMs, generating human-readable explanations without requiring annotated evidence \[[2310.05253](http://arxiv.org/abs/2310.05253)\]. This line of work suggests that structured reasoning frameworks combined with LLMs can reduce dependence on costly annotated data while improving interpretability.

Robustness to subtle factual changes is another key challenge. The VitaminC benchmark introduces contrastive evidence pairs—nearly identical in language but differing in supportiveness—to train models that are sensitive to fine-grained factual revisions, thereby improving robustness in fact verification and natural language inference tasks \[[2103.08541](http://arxiv.org/abs/2103.08541)\]. This highlights the necessity of fine-grained evidence understanding beyond simple retrieval.

Graph Neural Networks (GNNs) provide a foundational approach for reasoning over graph-structured data, using message passing to encode relational dependencies. Variants such as graph convolutional networks and graph attention networks have demonstrated success across domains involving structured data and have potential synergy with LLMs for reasoning over KGs \[[1812.08434](http://arxiv.org/abs/1812.08434)\]. Additionally, advances in semantic parsing over structured data, such as TAPAS for table-based question answering, show how models can be trained end-to-end with weak supervision to reason over semi-structured inputs, a concept transferable to KG-based reasoning \[[2004.02349](http://arxiv.org/abs/2004.02349)\].

Collectively, these works illustrate the current landscape where datasets and methods are evolving from purely text-based fact verification to hybrid or KG-centric approaches that leverage structured knowledge and modular reasoning with LLMs. However, challenges remain in effectively integrating KGs with LLMs for multi-step reasoning, zero-shot generalization across datasets, and generating faithful, interpretable explanations. The proposed ClaimPKG framework aligns with these trends by employing a specialized LLM to represent claims as pseudo-subgraphs, guiding subgraph retrieval, and then using a general-purpose LLM for final verification and justification. This approach builds upon and extends prior work by explicitly combining KG structure and LLM reasoning to achieve strong performance and generalizability as demonstrated on FactKG, HoVer, and FEVEROUS datasets \[[2011.03088](http://arxiv.org/abs/2011.03088)\]\[[1803.05355](http://arxiv.org/abs/1803.05355)\]\[[2305.06590](http://arxiv.org/abs/2305.06590)\]\[[2103.08541](http://arxiv.org/abs/2103.08541)\]\[[2310.01061](http://arxiv.org/abs/2310.01061)\]\[[2305.12744](http://arxiv.org/abs/2305.12744)\]\[[2310.05253](http://arxiv.org/abs/2310.05253)\]\[[1812.08434](http://arxiv.org/abs/1812.08434)\]\[[2004.02349](http://arxiv.org/abs/2004.02349)\].
