## Related Works

Radar-camera extrinsic calibration is critical for sensor fusion in autonomous systems, yet it remains challenging due to the heterogeneous nature of radar and camera data, as well as radar’s inherent sparsity and measurement uncertainty. Early approaches predominantly relied on target-based calibration, requiring specially designed calibration objects or retroreflectors placed in the environment. While these methods can achieve accurate results, their dependency on controlled setups limits practical deployment in dynamic or large-scale scenarios \[[2103.07505](http://arxiv.org/abs/2103.07505)\]\[[2211.01871](http://arxiv.org/abs/2211.01871)\].

To overcome these limitations, recent research has focused on targetless and online calibration methods that do not require dedicated calibration targets or human intervention. For instance, data-driven frameworks using deep neural networks have demonstrated the ability to perform automatic extrinsic calibration by learning correspondences directly from raw sensor data. RegNet exemplifies this by integrating feature extraction, matching, and regression into a single CNN, achieving low calibration errors between LiDAR and camera sensors even under large decalibrations \[[1707.03167](http://arxiv.org/abs/1707.03167)\]. Similarly, CalibNet uses a self-supervised geometric consistency loss to estimate 6-DoF extrinsics between LiDAR and cameras, eliminating the need for calibration targets and extensive labeled data \[[1803.08181](http://arxiv.org/abs/1803.08181)\].

Extending these concepts to radar, which has sparser and noisier measurements than LiDAR, is non-trivial. The first data-driven radar-camera calibration method introduced a two-stage CNN framework to estimate rotational alignment without calibration targets, boosting accuracy through residual learning despite limited training data \[[1904.08743](http://arxiv.org/abs/1904.08743)\]. Continuous-time calibration methods have also been proposed for 3D radar-to-camera setups, leveraging radar velocity measurements to estimate extrinsics without requiring specialized radar reflectors, thus enabling operation in realistic environments \[[2103.07505](http://arxiv.org/abs/2103.07505)\].

Further advancements address the spatiotemporal calibration challenge, jointly estimating spatial transforms and temporal offsets between radar and camera sensors. A recent approach exploits radar ego-velocity and motion observability to perform infrastructure-free, targetless calibration with performance comparable to target-based methods, validated on real and handheld sensor arrays \[[2211.01871](http://arxiv.org/abs/2211.01871)\].

For LiDAR-camera calibration, methods like LCCNet improve robustness by employing cost volume networks for feature matching and iterative refinement, achieving sub-centimeter translation and minimal rotational errors even with significant decalibrations \[[2012.13901](http://arxiv.org/abs/2012.13901)\]. These techniques demonstrate how deep learning and geometric constraints can be combined for precise sensor alignment in multimodal settings.

Despite these advances, radar-camera calibration still faces challenges due to radar’s sparse height information and measurement uncertainty. Existing methods typically rely on either frontal view features, which are sensitive to height noise, or bird’s-eye views, which offer robustness but lack rich vertical detail. The proposed approach innovates by introducing a Dual-Perspective representation that simultaneously extracts features from both frontal and bird’s-eye views, thereby leveraging complementary information. This is combined with a Selective Fusion Mechanism to dynamically identify and fuse reliable features, mitigating the impact of height uncertainty. Additionally, a Multi-Modal Cross-Attention mechanism explicitly establishes cross-modal correspondences, enhancing feature matching between radar and camera data. A Noise-Resistant Matcher further strengthens training supervision to handle data sparsity and noise effectively.

By integrating these novel components, the method surpasses previous radar-camera auto-calibration techniques and even state-of-the-art LiDAR-camera calibration approaches, establishing a new performance benchmark on challenging datasets. This progression builds directly on prior work in deep learning-based sensor calibration \[[1707.03167](http://arxiv.org/abs/1707.03167)\]\[[1803.08181](http://arxiv.org/abs/1803.08181)\]\[[1904.08743](http://arxiv.org/abs/1904.08743)\]\[[2012.13901](http://arxiv.org/abs/2012.13901)\], continuous-time and ego-motion-informed radar-camera calibration \[[2103.07505](http://arxiv.org/abs/2103.07505)\]\[[2211.01871](http://arxiv.org/abs/2211.01871)\], and iterative refinement strategies for multimodal sensor alignment \[[2012.13901](http://arxiv.org/abs/2012.13901)\].
