## Related Works

Fine-grained emotion classification in app reviews is an emerging research area that seeks to move beyond traditional sentiment polarity (positive, negative, neutral) to capture nuanced emotional states such as joy, anger, fear, and surprise. This granularity is essential for understanding user feedback in greater depth and improving software development processes. Prior work has largely focused on sentiment analysis at a coarse level, with fewer studies addressing the challenges of fine-grained emotion annotation specifically in app reviews.

Tools like Appsent have been developed to analyze app reviews by extracting and visualizing user perceptions, demonstrating the importance of systematic approaches to understanding user feedback \[[1907.10191](http://arxiv.org/abs/1907.10191)\]. Although Appsent primarily focuses on identifying key opinions about software features, its approach underlines the utility of structured analysis in managing large volumes of app review data.

Manual annotation of complex pragmatic and discursive features, such as emotions, remains a bottleneck due to its time-consuming and error-prone nature. Recent studies have explored the use of large language models (LLMs) to assist or automate these annotation tasks. For example, research comparing GPT-3.5, GPT-4, and human coders in annotating apology components found that GPT-4 approaches human-level accuracy in pragmatic annotation tasks, suggesting that LLMs can effectively support complex linguistic annotation \[[2305.08339](http://arxiv.org/abs/2305.08339)\]. This finding is particularly relevant for emotion annotation in app reviews, where subtle contextual cues must be interpreted.

Similarly, LLMs have been evaluated for their effectiveness and efficiency in financial data annotation, revealing significant potential for reducing manual effort while maintaining annotation quality \[[https://aclanthology.org/2024.lrec-main.885/](https://aclanthology.org/2024.lrec-main.885/)\]. These results align with findings in the domain of app review emotion classification, where LLMs can substantially ease the annotation burden but still face challenges in fully capturing the complexity of emotional nuances.

The development of structured annotation frameworks tailored to app reviews is crucial for advancing fine-grained emotion classification. Adapting established taxonomies, such as Plutchik’s emotion model, provides a foundation for consistent labeling and facilitates the creation of high-quality datasets. These frameworks help address key challenges including ambiguity in emotional expression and overlap between emotion categories.

While full automation of emotion annotation remains difficult, integrating LLMs with human-in-the-loop processes offers a practical compromise, combining scalability with quality control. This hybrid approach supports the iterative refinement of annotation guidelines and datasets, which is essential for training robust emotion classification models.

In summary, prior work highlights three key themes: (1) the need for structured and scalable analysis tools for app reviews \[[1907.10191](http://arxiv.org/abs/1907.10191)\]; (2) the promising application of LLMs to automate complex pragmatic and emotional annotation tasks with performance nearing human coders \[[2305.08339](http://arxiv.org/abs/2305.08339)\]\[[https://aclanthology.org/2024.lrec-main.885/](https://aclanthology.org/2024.lrec-main.885/)\]; and (3) the importance of developing domain-specific annotation frameworks to effectively capture fine-grained emotions in user-generated text. This paper builds on these insights by providing a dedicated framework and dataset for fine-grained emotion classification in app reviews, alongside an evaluation of LLM-assisted annotation techniques.
