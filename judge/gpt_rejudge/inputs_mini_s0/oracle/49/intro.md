## Related Works

Neural retrieval models have become fundamental in information retrieval, particularly for Web search, due to their ability to learn dense semantic representations that capture complex query-document relationships \[[2004.04906](http://arxiv.org/abs/2004.04906)\]. However, such models typically require large-scale labeled query-document pairs, which are costly and labor-intensive to obtain. To mitigate this, synthetic query generation using large language models (LLMs) has emerged as a scalable alternative, leveraging the generative capabilities of LLMs to produce training queries from document collections \[[2202.05144](http://arxiv.org/abs/2202.05144)\]\[[1904.08375](http://arxiv.org/abs/1904.08375)\]. Despite their promise, synthetic queries often vary substantially in quality, which can degrade downstream retrieval performance.

Traditional approaches to improve synthetic query quality have relied on filtering noisy query-document pairs via external re-rankers or applying post-hoc query expansion techniques \[[1904.08375](http://arxiv.org/abs/1904.08375)\]. While effective, these methods introduce additional computational overhead and may not fully integrate the relevance signals into the query generation process. Recent advances suggest that directly incorporating ranking or preference signals during query generation can lead to more effective training data and retrieval models.

Reinforcement learning-based approaches, such as Token-level Proximal Policy Optimization (TPPO), have been proposed to fine-tune LLMs for query generation by optimizing token-level rewards derived from interaction feedback, addressing the sparse reward problem in traditional frameworks \[[2411.00722](http://arxiv.org/abs/2411.00722)\]. This paradigm aligns the generation process more closely with retrieval effectiveness but often requires careful reward design and complex training procedures.

Direct Preference Optimization (DPO), a method recently explored in alignment tasks for LLMs, offers an alternative by optimizing models directly based on pairwise preference data without explicit reward modeling or reinforcement learning \[[2411.00722](http://arxiv.org/abs/2411.00722)\]. Adapting such direct optimization techniques to query generation facilitates integrating ranking signals into the generation process, potentially leading to higher-quality synthetic queries that better match downstream retrieval objectives.

Dense retrieval models, such as those based on dual-encoder architectures, have shown significant improvements over traditional sparse retrieval methods like BM25 by learning dense embeddings for queries and passages \[[2004.04906](http://arxiv.org/abs/2004.04906)\]. However, training dense retrievers is often challenging due to sensitivity to noisy data and the need for large batch sizes or complex negative sampling strategies. Techniques like Approximate Nearest Neighbor Negative Contrastive Estimation (ANCE) address these issues by dynamically constructing realistic hard negatives from an approximate nearest neighbor index to improve training effectiveness \[[2007.00808](http://arxiv.org/abs/2007.00808)\]. Similarly, pre-training strategies such as RetroMAE leverage masked auto-encoding to produce robust dense representations that improve retrieval performance without extensive engineering \[[2205.12035](http://arxiv.org/abs/2205.12035)\].

The use of synthetic data for dense retrieval training has been further enhanced by approaches that generate task-specific queries through few-shot prompting of LLMs, as demonstrated by Promptagator, which creates effective retrievers from minimal annotated examples by generating high-quality synthetic queries \[[2209.11755](http://arxiv.org/abs/2209.11755)\]. Additionally, knowledge distillation from LLMs into compact embedding models, like Gecko, refines retrieval quality while maintaining efficiency \[[2403.20327](http://arxiv.org/abs/2403.20327)\].

Data augmentation with synthetic queries has also been shown to improve zero-shot transfer and robustness of retrieval models across domains, as seen in InPars, which generates synthetic training data via LLMs and improves retrieval without requiring manually labeled datasets \[[2202.05144](http://arxiv.org/abs/2202.05144)\]. This underscores the value of synthetic queries beyond mere training data augmentation, extending to domain adaptation and privacy-preserving scenarios.

Document expansion techniques by query prediction also complement synthetic query generation by enriching document representations with predicted queries, enhancing retrieval effectiveness in latency-sensitive settings \[[1904.08375](http://arxiv.org/abs/1904.08375)\].

In summary, prior works have explored multiple facets of integrating synthetic queries and ranking signals into retrieval systems, ranging from external re-ranking and query filtering to reinforcement learning and direct optimization methods. Our approach that leverages Direct Preference Optimization to integrate ranking signals directly into the query generation process aligns with these trends and offers a principled mechanism to produce higher-quality synthetic queries that improve downstream retrieval performance without the complexity of external re-rankers or reward engineering \[[2411.00722](http://arxiv.org/abs/2411.00722)\]\[[2007.00808](http://arxiv.org/abs/2007.00808)\]\[[2004.04906](http://arxiv.org/abs/2004.04906)\]\[[2205.12035](http://arxiv.org/abs/2205.12035)\]\[[2209.11755](http://arxiv.org/abs/2209.11755)\]\[[2202.05144](http://arxiv.org/abs/2202.05144)\]\[[2403.20327](http://arxiv.org/abs/2403.20327)\]\[[1904.08375](http://arxiv.org/abs/1904.08375)\].
