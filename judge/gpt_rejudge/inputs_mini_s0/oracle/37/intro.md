## Related Works

Vision Transformers (ViTs) have gained significant attention for their ability to capture global context through self-attention mechanisms, outperforming traditional convolutional neural networks in various vision tasks. However, their deployment on hardware, particularly resource-constrained platforms such as FPGAs, remains challenging due to the complex arithmetic operations involved, including Softmax and LayerNorm, which are sensitive to precision and difficult to efficiently implement in low-precision hardware \[[2005.03842](http://arxiv.org/abs/2005.03842)\].

Prior hardware acceleration efforts often focus on optimizing matrix multiplication components of ViTs while offloading complex nonlinear operations like Softmax and LayerNorm to CPUs. This hybrid approach incurs communication overhead, limiting overall performance gains \[[2005.03842](http://arxiv.org/abs/2005.03842)\]. To address computational efficiency, token pruning methods such as HeatViT have been proposed, which dynamically reduce input tokens to lower computational load with minimal accuracy loss. HeatViT implements attention-based token selectors and combines 8-bit fixed-point quantization with polynomial approximations for nonlinear functions, achieving significant speedup and accuracy improvements on FPGA platforms \[[2005.03842](http://arxiv.org/abs/2005.03842)\].

Quantization techniques play a crucial role in enabling efficient ViT inference by reducing bitwidth and thus hardware resource usage and memory bandwidth. Conventional quantization often applies uniform bitwidth across layers, which may not be optimal for balancing accuracy and resource constraints. Hardware-aware quantization frameworks like HAQ utilize reinforcement learning guided by hardware feedback (latency and energy) to assign mixed precision on a per-layer basis, thus optimizing the trade-offs for different hardware architectures and constraints \[[1811.08886](http://arxiv.org/abs/1811.08886)\]. This automated, hardware-informed approach contrasts with uniform quantization and leads to substantial latency and energy reductions with minimal accuracy loss.

More recent quantization formats have pushed beyond conventional fixed-point or block floating-point representations. The concept of shared microexponents (MX) introduces ultra-fine scaling factors that allow multiple levels of quantization scaling within the same block, enhancing the precision-efficiency trade-off. MX formats have demonstrated superior performance across a variety of large-scale models, including generative pretrained models and recommendation systems, outperforming other state-of-the-art narrow-precision floating-point and block floating-point methods \[[2302.08007](http://arxiv.org/abs/2302.08007)\]. This suggests that novel arithmetic formats like MX could better address the precision sensitivity of ViT operations such as Softmax and LayerNorm when mapped onto hardware.

In natural language processing, extreme quantization to very low bitwidths has been achieved without retraining, as exemplified by GOBO, which compresses BERT models to 3-bit parameters while maintaining accuracy. GOBO’s hardware design maintains weights in low-bitwidth format even during computation, enabling area-efficient processing elements, replacing many multiplications with additions, and reducing memory traffic—key insights for designing quantization-friendly hardware accelerators \[[2005.03842](http://arxiv.org/abs/2005.03842)\]. Such techniques inspire hardware-efficient designs for transformer-based architectures.

Few works tackle the direct hardware mapping of all ViT operations, particularly the complex nonlinear functions, on FPGA with custom arithmetic formats. The proposed MXInt format aligns with these advances by enabling full hardware mapping of ViTs, including sensitive operations, while balancing accuracy, area efficiency, and performance. This approach contrasts with prior works that rely on CPU for nonlinear operations or approximate them with polynomial functions, offering a more integrated and accelerated hardware solution.

In summary, the landscape of ViT hardware acceleration combines algorithmic optimizations such as token pruning, hardware-aware mixed-precision quantization, and novel data formats leveraging shared microexponents. These approaches collectively aim to reduce computation, memory bandwidth, and energy consumption while preserving accuracy on constrained hardware. The proposed MXInt-based ViT accelerator builds on these foundations by introducing a new arithmetic format and corresponding hardware optimizations that map all ViT operations onto FPGAs, thereby overcoming the limitations of prior partial hardware mappings and enabling significant speedups with minimal accuracy degradation.
