## Related Works

Sequential recommendation has seen significant advancements through the integration of attention mechanisms, recurrent architectures, and state-space models, each addressing distinct challenges in modeling user behavior over time. Traditional approaches such as Markov Chains and Recurrent Neural Networks (RNNs) have been widely used to capture short-term and long-term dependencies, respectively. Markov Chains are effective in sparse datasets due to their simplicity, while RNNs better capture richer sequential semantics in denser data \[[1808.09781](http://arxiv.org/abs/1808.09781)\]\[[1511.06939](http://arxiv.org/abs/1511.06939)\]. However, RNN-based methods often struggle with efficient modeling of very long sequences and may lack flexibility in handling diverse user behavior patterns.

Transformer-based models, particularly those leveraging self-attention mechanisms, have become dominant due to their ability to model long-range dependencies and dynamically weigh the importance of past interactions. Models like SASRec employ self-attention to adaptively focus on relevant items in the user's history, balancing the benefits of Markovian and RNN approaches and achieving strong empirical performance and efficiency improvements over CNN- and RNN-based baselines \[[1808.09781](http://arxiv.org/abs/1808.09781)\]. Nonetheless, the quadratic computational complexity of attention mechanisms poses scalability challenges, particularly for long sequences typical in real-world recommendation systems.

Addressing these efficiency issues, recent research has explored state-space models (SSMs), which offer linear-time complexity and robust modeling of temporal dynamics. The Mamba architecture, a selective state-space model, introduces input-dependent parameterization and hardware-aware parallel algorithms to efficiently capture sequence information with linear complexity while maintaining or exceeding Transformer-level accuracy across multiple domains \[[2312.00752](http://arxiv.org/abs/2312.00752)\]\[[2403.039](http://arxiv.org/abs/2403.03900)\]\[[2403.16371](http://arxiv.org/abs/2403.16371)\]. Mamba4Rec applies this paradigm specifically to sequential recommendation, demonstrating superior effectiveness and efficiency compared to RNN and attention baselines, particularly on long user behavior sequences \[[2403.039](http://arxiv.org/abs/2403.03900)\]. EchoMamba4Rec extends this by combining bidirectional SSMs with spectral filtering, leveraging frequency-domain processing via learnable FFT filters to capture complex periodic user interaction patterns and improve recommendation accuracy and stability \[[2406.02638](http://arxiv.org/abs/2406.02638)\]. These works highlight the potential of SSMs to unify efficient sequence modeling with expressive power in recommendation tasks.

Beyond efficiency, capturing diverse user behavior patterns remains a critical challenge. Mixture-of-experts (MoE) architectures provide a promising solution by routing different behavioral patterns to specialized expert models, enabling tailored handling of focused or exploratory user interactions. While MoE has been explored in broader recommendation contexts to address multi-domain and multi-task scenarios, its integration with sequential models remains an emerging area. STAR-Rec’s sequence-level MoE framework builds on this principle by adaptively selecting experts based on user behavior dynamics, allowing the model to handle varying preference scopes and sequence lengths effectively. This adaptive routing aligns with the growing recognition that a single monolithic model may not adequately capture the heterogeneity of user interactions \[[2405.16542](http://arxiv.org/abs/2405.16542)\].

Several models also address the challenge of long-range dependency and temporal dynamics through hybrid attention and memory mechanisms. The Frequency Enhanced Hybrid Attention Network (FEARec) improves upon self-attention by incorporating frequency-domain analysis to explicitly model both low- and high-frequency behavioral signals and periodic patterns, achieving superior performance on benchmark datasets \[[2304.09184](http://arxiv.org/abs/2304.09184)\]. Similarly, the Dynamic Memory-based Attention Network (DMAN) segments long user sequences into sub-sequences and maintains memory blocks to preserve long-term interests, combining short- and long-term preference modeling efficiently \[[2102.09269](http://arxiv.org/abs/2102.09269)\]. These approaches complement the state-space modeling perspective by explicitly focusing on capturing diverse temporal scales and patterns.

Linearized attention mechanisms offer another avenue for improving Transformer efficiency in long sequence modeling. LinRec proposes an L2-normalized linear attention that reduces computational complexity while preserving attention properties, enabling scalable Transformer-based sequential recommendation with comparable or better accuracy \[[2411.01537](http://arxiv.org/abs/2411.01537)\]. This line of work reflects ongoing efforts to reconcile the expressiveness of attention with the computational demands of large-scale recommendation.

Finally, personalization in session-based recommendations has been enhanced through hierarchical RNNs that transfer information across sessions to better capture evolving user states \[[1706.04148](http://arxiv.org/abs/1706.04148)\], and bidirectional Transformer models like BERT4Rec employ masked token prediction to leverage both past and future contexts, achieving strong performance in sequential recommendation \[[1904.0669](http://arxiv.org/abs/1904.06690)\]. However, these approaches often face efficiency bottlenecks or limitations in handling very long sequences, which SSM-based architectures like Mamba and its derivatives effectively address.

In summary, recent advances in sequential recommendation have converged on integrating preference-aware attention mechanisms, efficient state-space models, and adaptive mixture-of-experts frameworks to handle sequence length variability and diverse user behaviors. STAR-Rec’s combination of these elements is well grounded in this evolving landscape, leveraging the linear scalability and temporal compression of SSMs alongside the flexible, preference-sensitive modeling capacity of attention and MoE to deliver robust and efficient sequential recommendations.
