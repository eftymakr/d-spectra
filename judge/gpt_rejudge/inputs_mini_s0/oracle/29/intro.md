## Related Works

Maximum Inner Product Search (MIPS) is a critical problem in machine learning and information retrieval, particularly for high-dimensional data applications. Traditional approaches to MIPS mainly rely on inner product (IP) similarity measures but often suffer from challenges such as local optima and redundant computations \[[1606.00185](http://arxiv.org/abs/1606.00185)\]. To address these issues, some techniques reduce MIPS to nearest neighbor search (NNS) under the Euclidean metric via space projections. However, these reductions can distort the underlying data topology and cause information loss, which degrades search quality \[[1606.00185](http://arxiv.org/abs/1606.00185)\]\[[1405.5869](http://arxiv.org/abs/1405.5869)\].

The dichotomy between IP and Euclidean metrics has historically framed MIPS methods, but recent research suggests this division is not fundamental. Instead, integrating both metrics in indexing and search algorithms can enhance performance significantly \[[1410.541](http://arxiv.org/abs/1410.5410)\]. Graph-based search methods have emerged as an effective paradigm that leverages this insight. For example, the inner-product navigable small world graph (ip-NSW) exploits the norm bias inherent in MIPS, where large-norm items dominate search results. By focusing search efforts on these high-norm nodes, ip-NSW achieves substantial speedups compared to baseline methods \[[1909.13459](http://arxiv.org/abs/1909.13459)\]. Building on this, ip-NSW+ introduces an angular proximity graph to better capture directional similarity, enabling more robust and efficient search by combining inner product and angular information \[[1909.13459](http://arxiv.org/abs/1909.13459)\].

Hashing methods have also been extensively studied for MIPS. Asymmetric Locality Sensitive Hashing (ALSH) was the first to provide a provably sublinear time algorithm for approximate MIPS by transforming the problem into approximate near neighbor search through asymmetric transformations that align inner products with Euclidean distances \[[1405.5869](http://arxiv.org/abs/1405.5869)\]. Improvements to ALSH include converting MIPS into approximate cosine similarity search, allowing the use of efficient signed random projections and yielding better theoretical and empirical performance \[[1410.541](http://arxiv.org/abs/1410.5410)\]. Norm-ranging LSH further enhances hashing by partitioning datasets based on norm distributions to mitigate performance degradation caused by long-tail norm distributions, achieving orders of magnitude speedups over prior LSH approaches \[[1809.08782](http://arxiv.org/abs/1809.08782)\]. Complementary to hashing, vector quantization (VQ) methods have been adapted for MIPS by explicitly quantizing norm components separately from directional components, as in norm-explicit quantization (NEQ), which reduces quantization error impact on inner product computations and improves search accuracy \[[1911.04654](http://arxiv.org/abs/1911.04654)\]. Anisotropic vector quantization similarly adjusts loss functions to emphasize components aligned with queries, leading to state-of-the-art results on benchmark datasets \[[1908.10396](http://arxiv.org/abs/1908.10396)\].

Beyond hashing and quantization, advanced indexing techniques such as SOAR utilize orthogonality-amplified residuals to optimize multiple redundant data representations, which improves approximate nearest neighbor search quality with low memory overhead and fast indexing times. Although SOAR targets Euclidean ANN search, its principles suggest potential benefits in hybrid metric MIPS indexing \[[2404.00774](http://arxiv.org/abs/2404.00774)\].

The theoretical discourse on symmetric versus asymmetric LSH for inner product search reveals that while asymmetric transformations were initially believed necessary, certain symmetric LSH schemes exist that provide stronger guarantees and better empirical results in some settings, though asymmetry remains beneficial in others \[[1410.5518](http://arxiv.org/abs/1410.5518)\].

In summary, the landscape of MIPS research reveals a spectrum of methods that blend inner product and Euclidean metrics through hashing, quantization, and graph-based indexing. Graph-based methods, in particular, effectively exploit data topology by integrating norm and angular information, enabling efficient navigation and search \[[1909.13459](http://arxiv.org/abs/1909.13459)\]. Hashing and quantization approaches improve efficiency by addressing norm bias and reducing quantization errors critical for inner product accuracy \[[1405.5869](http://arxiv.org/abs/1405.5869)\]\[[1410.541](http://arxiv.org/abs/1410.5410)\]\[[1809.08782](http://arxiv.org/abs/1809.08782)\]\[[1911.04654](http://arxiv.org/abs/1911.04654)\]\[[1908.10396](http://arxiv.org/abs/1908.10396)\]. These insights collectively motivate hybrid indexing structures that adaptively leverage metric properties depending on data distribution and query characteristics, aligning with the motivation behind Metric-Amphibious Graph (MAG) and Adaptive Navigation with Metric Switch (ANMS).
