## Related Works

Diffusion models have become a dominant paradigm for high-quality image and video synthesis, with diffusion transformers (DiTs) emerging as state-of-the-art architectures due to their scalability and superior generative capabilities \[[2405.14854](http://arxiv.org/abs/2405.14854)\]\[[2405.16005](http://arxiv.org/abs/2405.16005)\]. Despite their success, DiTs pose significant challenges for deployment on resource-constrained devices because of their large parameter counts and intensive computational requirements during iterative denoising \[[2211.15736](http://arxiv.org/abs/2211.15736)\]\[[2405.16005](http://arxiv.org/abs/2405.16005)\]. Quantization, which reduces model bit-width to compress size and accelerate inference, offers a promising avenue to address these barriers. However, existing quantization approaches developed for image diffusion models often fail to generalize effectively to video diffusion transformers, primarily due to the added complexity of preserving temporal coherence and spatiotemporal correlations across frames.

Early diffusion models focused on image generation using U-Net architectures, which inspired several quantization strategies. Post-training quantization (PTQ) methods have been widely studied for diffusion models, aiming to compress noise estimation networks without retraining \[[2211.15736](http://arxiv.org/abs/2211.15736)\]\[[2302.04304](http://arxiv.org/abs/2302.04304)\]\[[2305.10657](http://arxiv.org/abs/2305.10657)\]. However, vanilla PTQ methods face difficulties due to the multi-timestep nature of diffusion sampling, where output distributions shift across denoising steps, and quantization noise accumulates, degrading sample quality \[[2211.15736](http://arxiv.org/abs/2211.15736)\]\[[2302.04304](http://arxiv.org/abs/2302.04304)\]\[[2305.10657](http://arxiv.org/abs/2305.10657)\]. Solutions such as timestep-aware calibration and split shortcut quantization have been proposed to mitigate these issues, enabling quantization down to 4-bit with minimal performance loss in image diffusion models \[[2302.04304](http://arxiv.org/abs/2302.04304)\]\[[2305.10657](http://arxiv.org/abs/2305.10657)\]. Complementary to PTQ, quantization-aware training (QAT) approaches, including efficient fine-tuning frameworks like EfficientDM, balance quantization errors and maintain generation quality while reducing computational overhead \[[2310.0327](http://arxiv.org/abs/2310.03270)\]. Selective finetuning strategies and mixed-precision quantization have also been effective in addressing imbalanced activation distributions and sensitivity in critical layers \[[2402.03666](http://arxiv.org/abs/2402.03666)\]\[[2405.17873](http://arxiv.org/abs/2405.17873)\].

Diffusion transformers, which replace convolutional U-Nets with transformer backbones, introduce unique quantization challenges. Salient channels with extreme magnitude distributions and temporal variability in activations complicate weight and activation quantization \[[2405.16005](http://arxiv.org/abs/2405.16005)\]. To overcome these, Channel-wise Salience Balancing and Spearman’s ρ-guided Salience Calibration have been proposed, successfully quantizing DiTs to 8-bit and even 4-bit weights while preserving generation fidelity \[[2405.16005](http://arxiv.org/abs/2405.16005)\]. Additionally, ternary quantization (3-bit) via quantization-aware training has been explored for large-scale DiTs, demonstrating the feasibility of extremely low-bit quantization without substantial quality degradation \[[2405.14854](http://arxiv.org/abs/2405.14854)\]. More aggressive compression methods like full binarization (1-bit) have been developed for latent diffusion models, using timestep-friendly binary structures and spatially aware distillation to maintain generation performance despite extreme quantization \[[2412.05926](http://arxiv.org/abs/2412.05926)\].

While these quantization efforts have primarily targeted image diffusion models, video generation models introduce further complexities. Maintaining temporal consistency and preserving spatiotemporal correlations across frames remain critical challenges unmet by direct applications of image quantization methods. Recent video diffusion models, such as Lumiere and W.A.L.T, achieve temporal coherence through architectural innovations like space-time U-Nets and window attention mechanisms but do not primarily focus on quantization \[[2405.14854](http://arxiv.org/abs/2405.14854)\]\[[2405.16005](http://arxiv.org/abs/2405.16005)\]. Emerging works on quantizing video diffusion transformers recognize the necessity of specialized approaches that address temporal dynamics alongside quantization noise. For example, ViDiT-Q designs enhanced quantization schemes accounting for token and feature-level quantization errors, highlighting the importance of temporal and spatial fidelity preservation \[[2302.04304](http://arxiv.org/abs/2302.04304)\]. Similarly, hybrid floating-point quantization methods for DiTs have been proposed to minimize quantization error in both weights and activations, supporting high-quality video generation \[[2402.03666](http://arxiv.org/abs/2402.03666)\].

In summary, prior research on diffusion model quantization establishes a foundation of PTQ and QAT techniques for image generation, addressing multi-timestep quantization noise, activation imbalance, and transformer-specific challenges. However, video diffusion transformers require dedicated solutions that jointly optimize for quantization efficiency and temporal consistency. Our Q-VDiT framework advances this direction by introducing Token-aware Quantization Estimator (TQE) and Temporal Maintenance Distillation (TMD), which explicitly compensate for quantization errors in token and feature spaces and preserve spatiotemporal correlations during optimization, respectively. This approach builds upon and extends the insights from image diffusion quantization and DiT quantization literature to the video domain, achieving superior scene consistency and quantization performance \[[2302.04304](http://arxiv.org/abs/2302.04304)\]\[[2405.14854](http://arxiv.org/abs/2405.14854)\]\[[2405.16005](http://arxiv.org/abs/2405.16005)\].
