## Related Works

Federated Learning (FL) has become a predominant paradigm for training machine learning models across decentralized data sources while preserving user privacy, as it enables local training on client devices without sharing raw data \[[2207.05521](http://arxiv.org/abs/2207.05521)\]\[[2201.09441](http://arxiv.org/abs/2201.09441)\]. Within this paradigm, Federated Online Learning to Rank (FOLTR) specifically addresses the training of ranking models based on implicit user feedback, such as clicks, to optimize search result ordering while safeguarding privacy by sharing only model updates, not data \[[2401.1341](http://arxiv.org/abs/2401.13410)\]\[[2307.01565](http://arxiv.org/abs/2307.01565)\]\[[2204.09272](http://arxiv.org/abs/2204.09272)\]. Early OLTR methods were limited to linear models, but recent advances such as Pairwise Differentiable Gradient Descent (PDGD) enable effective training of non-linear neural ranking models in online settings, improving ranking quality and convergence speed \[[2201.06658](http://arxiv.org/abs/2201.06658)\]\[[1809.08415](http://arxiv.org/abs/1809.08415)\].

The increasing emphasis on data privacy legislation, notably the "right to be forgotten" under regulations like the GDPR, has motivated the development of machine unlearning techniques—methods allowing the removal of specific user data contributions from trained models. While unlearning has been extensively studied in centralized settings, its application to FL poses unique challenges due to the decentralized nature of training and lack of access to raw data at the server \[[2203.0732](http://arxiv.org/abs/2203.07320)\]\[[2207.05521](http://arxiv.org/abs/2207.05521)\]\[[2201.09441](http://arxiv.org/abs/2201.09441)\]. Federated Unlearning (FU) thus emerges as a critical research area, focusing on efficiently erasing a client’s influence on the global model without requiring retraining from scratch or full dataset access \[[2207.05521](http://arxiv.org/abs/2207.05521)\]\[[2201.09441](http://arxiv.org/abs/2201.09441)\].

Several FU methods have been proposed. One approach involves local unlearning at the client to be removed, followed by a few rounds of federated retraining with the remaining clients to restore the global model, achieving comparable performance to full retraining but with greater efficiency and no need to store entire training histories or raw data \[[2207.05521](http://arxiv.org/abs/2207.05521)\]. Another line of work employs knowledge distillation to subtract accumulated client updates and restore model utility without client participation or data access, making it practical for diverse neural architectures \[[2201.09441](http://arxiv.org/abs/2201.09441)\]. In the recommendation domain, federated unlearning methods inspired by database rollback mechanisms selectively reverse and calibrate historical parameter updates to remove users’ contributions efficiently, addressing challenges related to resource constraints on personal devices \[[2210.10958](http://arxiv.org/abs/2210.10958)\].

Evaluation of unlearning effectiveness in federated settings remains an open challenge. Traditional approaches often rely on a single metric, which can be insufficient to capture nuanced issues such as under-unlearning (residual client influence) or over-unlearning (excessive removal damaging model utility). Recent work advocates for multi-metric assessments and rigorous experimental protocols, including adversarial poisoning attacks to verify unlearning success and balance privacy with model performance \[[2401.1341](http://arxiv.org/abs/2401.13410)\]\[[2210.10958](http://arxiv.org/abs/2210.10958)\]. This is particularly relevant for FOLTR systems, where the interplay between privacy preservation, ranking effectiveness, and robustness against malicious clients is complex \[[2401.1341](http://arxiv.org/abs/2401.13410)\]\[[2307.01565](http://arxiv.org/abs/2307.01565)\]\[[2210.10958](http://arxiv.org/abs/2210.10958)\].

Moreover, federated online learning to rank faces additional challenges from non-IID data distributions across clients, which can degrade model performance and complicate unlearning. Studies have begun to analyze the impact of data heterogeneity in FOLTR and explore mitigation strategies derived from federated learning literature, emphasizing the need for tailored approaches that consider the unique characteristics of ranking tasks and online learning dynamics \[[2204.09272](http://arxiv.org/abs/2204.09272)\].

Other specialized FL applications have also advanced unlearning methods accounting for domain-specific constraints, such as heterogeneous knowledge graph embedding learning, where unlearning combines cognitive-inspired mechanisms with mutual knowledge distillation to handle data drift and erase specific client knowledge effectively \[[2302.02069](http://arxiv.org/abs/2302.02069)\].

In summary, while federated unlearning has made significant progress in terms of methodology and efficiency, the specific context of FOLTR presents distinct challenges requiring comprehensive evaluation frameworks and adaptable unlearning strategies. This study contributes by systematically assessing multiple unlearning methods with novel metrics in FOLTR, addressing key gaps identified in prior works \[[2401.1341](http://arxiv.org/abs/2401.13410)\]\[[2203.0732](http://arxiv.org/abs/2203.07320)\]\[[2307.01565](http://arxiv.org/abs/2307.01565)\]\[[2207.05521](http://arxiv.org/abs/2207.05521)\]\[[2210.10958](http://arxiv.org/abs/2210.10958)\]\[[2302.02069](http://arxiv.org/abs/2302.02069)\]\[[2204.09272](http://arxiv.org/abs/2204.09272)\]\[[2201.09441](http://arxiv.org/abs/2201.09441)\].
