## Related Works

Collaborative filtering has long relied on learning latent user and item embeddings for recommendation tasks. Traditional methods such as matrix factorization (MF) and Bayesian Personalized Ranking (BPR) focused on optimizing personalized ranking from implicit feedback but were limited in capturing complex interaction patterns \[[1205.2618](http://arxiv.org/abs/1205.2618)\]. With the rise of deep learning, Neural Collaborative Filtering (NCF) introduced multi-layer perceptrons to model non-linear user-item interactions, significantly improving recommendation accuracy over MF by learning arbitrary interaction functions \[[1708.05031](http://arxiv.org/abs/1708.05031)\].

Graph Neural Networks (GNNs) have recently become a dominant paradigm for collaborative filtering by explicitly modeling the user-item interaction graph. Neural Graph Collaborative Filtering (NGCF) propagates embeddings over the bipartite graph to capture high-order connectivity, enhancing the expressiveness of learned representations \[[1905.08108](http://arxiv.org/abs/1905.08108)\]. LightGCN simplified GNN designs by removing feature transformation and nonlinear activation layers, showing that neighborhood aggregation alone suffices to boost collaborative filtering performance while improving training efficiency \[[2002.02126](http://arxiv.org/abs/2002.02126)\]. Disentangled Graph Collaborative Filtering (DGCF) further extended this by modeling diverse user intents within the user-item graph, learning disentangled representations that lead to better interpretability and accuracy \[[2007.01764](http://arxiv.org/abs/2007.01764)\].

Despite their effectiveness, GNN-based models often face computational and memory challenges, especially in large-scale settings. To address these, representation binarization techniques have been explored. Binarized embeddings reduce memory footprint and allow efficient bitwise operations during inference. Early deep hashing methods focused on continuous-to-binary quantization pipelines but suffered from information loss and degraded retrieval quality \[[1702.00758](http://arxiv.org/abs/1702.00758)\]\[[2112.08816](http://arxiv.org/abs/2112.08816)\]. Recent advancements have proposed end-to-end binarization frameworks that better preserve semantic information, such as HashNet, which employs a continuation method to directly learn exact binary hash codes from imbalanced data \[[1702.00758](http://arxiv.org/abs/1702.00758)\]. 

In recommender systems, binarization-based candidate generation frameworks like CIGAR adopt preference-preserving binary embeddings to efficiently retrieve candidates from large corpora, followed by re-ranking with real-valued models. This approach drastically reduces query latency while maintaining accuracy \[[1909.05475](http://arxiv.org/abs/1909.05475)\]. However, many existing binarization methods primarily focus on numerical quantization and often neglect the substantial information loss that occurs during the binarization process, leading to notable performance degradation.

To explicitly mitigate information loss, recent works have introduced multi-stage quantization reinforcement strategies. BiGeaR, for example, incorporates pre-, mid-, and post-stage quantization techniques to maintain embedding informativeness and accelerate online inference with bitwise operations, achieving significant performance improvements over prior quantization-based recommenders while reducing time and space complexity \[[2206.02115](http://arxiv.org/abs/2206.02115)\]. Building on this, BiGeaR++ further enhances performance by introducing supervisory signals from pseudo-positive samples that include both real items and synthesized latent embeddings, as well as a fine-grained inference distillation mechanism that guides the binarized representations during training.

Knowledge distillation (KD) techniques have also been adapted to graph-based models to compress large GNNs into smaller, efficient students without sacrificing performance. Distillation methods for GNNs preserve both local structural relationships and global graph topology, using contrastive learning or local structure preserving losses to transfer knowledge effectively \[[2003.10477](http://arxiv.org/abs/2003.10477)\]\[[2111.04964](http://arxiv.org/abs/2111.04964)\]. Recent frameworks such as FreeKD even enable mutual knowledge exchange between similarly sized GNNs, alleviating issues caused by over-parameterization and over-smoothing in teacher models \[[2206.06561](http://arxiv.org/abs/2206.06561)\]. Moreover, holistic knowledge distillation approaches integrate individual and relational knowledge via an attributed graph among instances, further improving student model learning \[[2108.05507](http://arxiv.org/abs/2108.05507)\].

In the broader context of graph-based learning, simplifications like LightGCN demonstrate that removing unnecessary transformations can improve both performance and efficiency \[[2002.02126](http://arxiv.org/abs/2002.02126)\], while disentangled approaches highlight the benefits of modeling nuanced user intents in recommendation \[[2007.01764](http://arxiv.org/abs/2007.01764)\]. Combining these advances with sophisticated binarization and distillation techniques addresses the dual challenges of scalability and accuracy in recommender systems.

In summary, the current literature spans foundational ranking optimization \[[1205.2618](http://arxiv.org/abs/1205.2618)\], neural and graph-based collaborative filtering \[[1708.05031](http://arxiv.org/abs/1708.05031)\]\[[2002.02126](http://arxiv.org/abs/2002.02126)\]\[[2007.01764](http://arxiv.org/abs/2007.01764)\]\[[1905.08108](http://arxiv.org/abs/1905.08108)\], binarization for efficiency \[[1702.00758](http://arxiv.org/abs/1702.00758)\]\[[1909.05475](http://arxiv.org/abs/1909.05475)\]\[[2206.02115](http://arxiv.org/abs/2206.02115)\], and knowledge distillation tailored for GNNs \[[2003.10477](http://arxiv.org/abs/2003.10477)\]\[[2111.04964](http://arxiv.org/abs/2111.04964)\]\[[2108.05507](http://arxiv.org/abs/2108.05507)\]\[[2206.06561](http://arxiv.org/abs/2206.06561)\]. Our work builds on these foundations by explicitly addressing information loss in graph embedding binarization through enhanced supervision and distillation, leading to improved efficiency and state-of-the-art recommendation performance.
