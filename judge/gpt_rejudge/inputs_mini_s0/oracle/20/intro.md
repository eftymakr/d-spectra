## Related Works

Single-pixel imaging (SPI) reconstructs images from measurements obtained via a single-pixel detector combined with structured illumination patterns, posing a challenging ill-posed inverse problem due to extreme undersampling \[[1601.06892](http://arxiv.org/abs/1601.06892)\]\[[2404.05001](http://arxiv.org/abs/2404.05001)\]. Traditional SPI methods initially relied on random or deterministic patterns such as Hadamard and Fourier masks, which improved sampling efficiency and reconstruction quality but still required high sampling rates or computationally intensive inversion \[[1601.06892](http://arxiv.org/abs/1601.06892)\]\[[2404.05001](http://arxiv.org/abs/2404.05001)\]. Recent advances leverage deep learning to enhance reconstruction, primarily through two dominant paradigms: deep-unrolling and plug-and-play (PnP) approaches.

Plug-and-play (PnP) methods integrate off-the-shelf denoisers as implicit regularizers within iterative optimization algorithms, allowing flexible adaptation to varying compression ratios (CRs) without retraining \[[1406.4175](http://arxiv.org/abs/1406.4175)\]\[[2110.0322](http://arxiv.org/abs/2110.03220)\]\[[1611.02862](http://arxiv.org/abs/1611.02862)\]. These methods treat denoisers as proximal operators or regularization functions, enabling the use of advanced denoising techniques to guide reconstruction. For instance, Regularization by Denoising (RED) formulates an explicit regularization functional based on a denoiser, ensuring convergence and flexibility across inverse problems \[[1611.02862](http://arxiv.org/abs/1611.02862)\]. More recent PnP frameworks improve on convergence and expressivity by learning proximal operators via deep networks, such as Learned Proximal Networks (LPN), which approximate nonconvex priors and guarantee convergence while offering interpretability \[[2310.14344](http://arxiv.org/abs/2310.14344)\]. Similarly, stochastic deep restoration priors employ ensembles of restoration networks to better capture complex image statistics, surpassing traditional Gaussian denoiser priors \[[2410.02057](http://arxiv.org/abs/2410.02057)\]. However, PnP methods often face limitations in reconstruction accuracy and computational speed compared to tailored unrolling networks.

Deep-unrolling methods unfold a truncated iterative optimization process into a multi-stage neural network, where each stage mimics an iteration of an algorithm such as half-quadratic splitting (HQS) or alternating direction method of multipliers (ADMM) \[[2110.09766](http://arxiv.org/abs/2110.09766)\]\[[2204.13348](http://arxiv.org/abs/2204.13348)\]\[[2304.13986](http://arxiv.org/abs/2304.13986)\]. This approach yields interpretable networks with faster inference and better accuracy by learning efficient proximal mappings or denoisers within the iterative framework. Despite their performance advantages, these unrolling networks typically require retraining or fine-tuning for each change in CR, limiting their flexibility \[[2110.09766](http://arxiv.org/abs/2110.09766)\]. Recent works address these drawbacks by incorporating memory mechanisms to preserve information across stages, as in Memory-Augmented Deep Unfolding Networks (MADUN), which combine short-term and long-term memory to reduce feature loss and improve representation \[[2110.09766](http://arxiv.org/abs/2110.09766)\]. Optimization-inspired transformers have also been introduced to enhance information flow and reduce network complexity, improving reconstruction quality with fewer parameters \[[2304.13986](http://arxiv.org/abs/2304.13986)\].

Hybrid architectures combining convolutional neural networks (CNNs) and transformers have emerged to leverage both local and global image context for compressive sensing reconstruction \[[2112.15299](http://arxiv.org/abs/2112.15299)\]. For example, CSformer fuses CNN-based local feature extraction with transformer-based global attention to capture complementary representations, achieving state-of-the-art results. Similarly, deep unfolding frameworks with hybrid attention transformers have been proposed for SPI, such as HATNet, which integrates efficient tensor gradient descent with multi-scale spatial and channel attention to handle large-scale SPI with improved computational efficiency and image quality \[[2404.05001](http://arxiv.org/abs/2404.05001)\].

Non-iterative deep learning approaches like ReconNet propose direct reconstruction from CS measurements via convolutional networks, followed by denoising steps to refine output \[[1601.06892](http://arxiv.org/abs/1601.06892)\]. While fast, these methods may not match the accuracy of iterative or unrolled methods but offer robustness to noise and extremely low sensing rates.

The challenge of combining the flexibility of PnP algorithms with the accuracy and speed of unrolling methods remains a central research focus. Recent contributions propose training unrolling networks with losses designed to approximate proximal operators of ideal explicit regularizers, thus enabling a single network to handle varying CRs without retraining while maintaining high reconstruction fidelity and efficiency. This approach unifies the strengths of both paradigms and advances the state of the art in SPI inverse problem solving.

In summary, the landscape of SPI reconstruction has evolved from classical sampling and inversion techniques to sophisticated deep learning frameworks. PnP methods provide flexible, theoretically grounded approaches using powerful denoisers as implicit priors \[[1406.4175](http://arxiv.org/abs/1406.4175)\]\[[2110.0322](http://arxiv.org/abs/2110.03220)\]\[[2310.14344](http://arxiv.org/abs/2310.14344)\]\[[2410.02057](http://arxiv.org/abs/2410.02057)\]\[[1611.02862](http://arxiv.org/abs/1611.02862)\], while deep-unrolling networks offer interpretable, high-performance solutions tailored to specific CRs \[[2110.09766](http://arxiv.org/abs/2110.09766)\]\[[2204.13348](http://arxiv.org/abs/2204.13348)\]\[[2112.15299](http://arxiv.org/abs/2112.15299)\]\[[2304.13986](http://arxiv.org/abs/2304.13986)\]. Hybrid models and memory-augmented architectures further enhance these methods. The integration of these strategies to develop efficient, accurate, and flexible SPI solvers remains an active and promising research direction.
