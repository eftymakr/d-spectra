## Related Works

Narrative summarization, which involves condensing long-form texts such as books, movies, and TV scripts into concise and coherent summaries, has been a challenging task for existing language models (LMs) due to the complexity of capturing intricate plotlines, character interactions, and thematic coherence \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/1409.0473)\]. Prior work has primarily focused on single-model approaches, often requiring extensive fine-tuning or domain-specific adaptations to achieve satisfactory results \[[17](http://arxiv.org/abs/1910.13461)\]. For instance, BERT, a bidirectional transformer-based model, has been used for various NLP tasks, including question answering and language inference, but its performance on narrative summarization remains suboptimal without additional training \[[18](http://arxiv.org/abs/1810.04805)\]. Similarly, the Transformer architecture, which relies solely on attention mechanisms, has shown promise in machine translation but has not been directly applied to the structured summarization of long-form narratives \[[16](http://arxiv.org/abs/1706.03762)\].

Recent advancements in multi-agent systems have demonstrated the potential of leveraging multiple LMs to improve task performance. For example, MetaGPT introduces a framework that incorporates human workflows into multi-agent collaboration, enabling structured coordination and reducing compounded errors in complex tasks \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/2308.00352)\]. Similarly, AutoGen provides a multi-agent conversation framework that enables next-generation LLM applications by facilitating structured interactions between agents \[[4](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/1a4c6856292b8c64d19a812a77f0aa6fd47cb96c)\]. These works highlight the importance of structured coordination in multi-agent systems, which is a key component of our approach.

The ability of LMs to perform few-shot learning has also been explored, with models like GPT-3 demonstrating strong performance on a variety of NLP tasks without fine-tuning \[[5](http://arxiv.org/abs/2005.14165)\]. However, these models often struggle with tasks requiring external knowledge or structured reasoning, such as arithmetic or factual lookup \[[7](http://arxiv.org/abs/2302.04761)\]. To address this, Toolformer has been proposed, allowing LMs to teach themselves to use external tools through simple APIs, thereby achieving improved performance on downstream tasks \[[7](http://arxiv.org/abs/2302.04761)\]. This aligns with our approach, which leverages multi-agent collaboration to enhance the structured summarization of long-form narratives.

In addition, the use of multi-agent systems for reasoning and acting has been explored in the ReAct framework, which interleaves reasoning traces with task-specific actions to improve interpretability and trustworthiness \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/2210.03629)\]. This synergy between reasoning and acting is also reflected in our hierarchical multi-LLM summarization pipeline, which optimizes chunk processing and output length to ensure accurate and high-quality summaries.

Finally, the development of narrative summarization frameworks has also been addressed in the context of event story generation, such as StoryGraph, which constructs coherent paths to tell evolving stories by tracking event evolution and identifying different themes \[[9](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/367134d092ac0a3a904682830b82118616866434)\]. These approaches provide a foundation for our work, which introduces a novel multi-agent framework specifically tailored for narrative summarization.

These advancements underscore the importance of attention-based models in modern NLP and machine translation.
