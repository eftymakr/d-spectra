## Related Works

Vision Transformers (ViTs) have emerged as a powerful alternative to convolutional neural networks (CNNs) in computer vision tasks, leveraging the transformer architecture to effectively capture global context \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/2010.11929)\]. However, the deployment of ViTs on hardware platforms, especially FPGAs, remains challenging due to the complex arithmetic operations such as Softmax and LayerNorm, which are sensitive to model accuracy and difficult to map onto efficient hardware with low precision \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2208.05163)\]. Prior work has primarily focused on optimizing matrix multiplication operations on hardware while keeping these complex operations on the CPU, resulting in suboptimal performance due to communication overhead between the CPU and accelerator \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2208.05163)\].

Several studies have explored hardware acceleration for ViTs. Auto-ViT-Acc proposes an FPGA-aware acceleration framework with mixed-scheme quantization, achieving higher accuracy than state-of-the-art quantization methods while improving frame rates \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2208.05163)\]. ViTCoD introduces a co-design framework that integrates algorithmic pruning and hardware acceleration, achieving significant speedups by reducing dominant data movement costs \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/2210.09573)\]. I-ViT enables integer-only inference for ViTs by approximating non-linear operations with integer arithmetic, achieving comparable accuracy to full-precision models \[[4](http://arxiv.org/abs/2207.01405)\]. FQ-ViT addresses the challenges of post-training quantization for ViTs, reducing inference complexity while maintaining high accuracy \[[7](http://arxiv.org/abs/2111.13824)\]. Patch Slimming reduces computational costs by discarding redundant patches in a top-down manner, achieving over 45% FLOPs reduction with minimal accuracy loss \[[8](http://arxiv.org/abs/2106.02852)\]. HeatViT introduces hardware-efficient token pruning, achieving higher accuracy and significant computation reduction on various ViT models \[[9](http://arxiv.org/abs/2211.08110)\]. VAQF proposes a fully automatic framework for low-bit ViT acceleration on FPGAs, achieving real-time execution with minimal compilation time \[[10](http://arxiv.org/abs/2201.06618)\].

Efforts to improve ViT efficiency have also focused on architectural innovations. T2T-ViT improves training efficiency by incorporating a Tokens-to-Token transformation, reducing parameter count and MACs while improving accuracy \[[5](http://arxiv.org/abs/2101.11986)\]. LeViT combines principles from CNNs and transformers to achieve faster inference, outperforming existing convnets and vision transformers in the speed/accuracy tradeoff \[[6](http://arxiv.org/abs/2104.01136)\]. CvT introduces convolutions into ViTs to improve performance and efficiency, achieving state-of-the-art results with fewer parameters and lower FLOPs \[[12](http://arxiv.org/abs/2103.15808)\]. DynamicViT proposes a dynamic token sparsification framework, reducing FLOPs and improving throughput with minimal accuracy loss \[[13](http://arxiv.org/abs/2106.02034)\]. Swin Transformer introduces a hierarchical architecture with shifted windows, enabling efficient and effective dense prediction tasks \[[15](http://arxiv.org/abs/2103.14030)\]. Unified Visual Transformer Compression proposes a unified framework combining pruning, layer skipping, and knowledge distillation for ViT compression \[[16](http://arxiv.org/abs/2203.08243)\]. Pyramid Vision Transformer (PVT) provides a convolution-free backbone for dense prediction tasks, achieving high output resolution and computational efficiency \[[17](http://arxiv.org/abs/2102.12122)\]. CrossViT introduces cross-attention multi-scale vision transformers, achieving better performance than existing methods with a moderate increase in FLOPs \[[18](http://arxiv.org/abs/2103.14899)\].

These works highlight the ongoing efforts to improve ViT efficiency and hardware compatibility, with a focus on reducing computational costs, improving inference speed, and maintaining model accuracy. The proposed MXInt format and hardware optimizations aim to address these challenges by mapping all ViT operations onto FPGAs, achieving significant speedups while maintaining high accuracy \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2208.05163)\].

### <a id='category-architectural-innovations-in-vits'></a>Architectural Innovations in ViTs: 7 papers found.
Architectural innovations in Vision Transformers (ViTs) have been a focal point in improving performance and efficiency for computer vision tasks. Early ViT models, such as the original Vision Transformer, faced challenges in capturing local structures and maintaining feature richness due to their tokenization and attention mechanisms \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2208.05163)\]. To address these issues, researchers proposed the Tokens-To-Token Vision Transformer (T2T-ViT), which introduces a layer-wise Tokens-to-Token transformation to better model local structures and reduce token length \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2208.05163)\]. Additionally, the LeViT architecture was introduced to optimize the speed-accuracy trade-off by integrating principles from convolutional neural networks and attention-based models, including the use of attention bias for positional information \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/2210.09573)\]. The Convolutional vision Transformer (CvT) further enhanced ViT performance by incorporating convolutions, leveraging their invariance properties while retaining the global context modeling of Transformers \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/2010.11929)\]. The Transformer iN Transformer (TNT) architecture improved upon ViT by introducing a hierarchical structure where local patches are further divided into smaller units, allowing for more efficient attention computation and better feature aggregation \[[4](http://arxiv.org/abs/2207.01405)\]. The Swin Transformer addressed scalability and efficiency by introducing a hierarchical design with shifted windows, enabling cross-window connections while maintaining linear computational complexity \[[5](http://arxiv.org/abs/2101.11986)\]. The Pyramid Vision Transformer (PVT) was developed to facilitate dense prediction tasks by enabling high-resolution outputs and efficient computation through a progressive shrinking pyramid \[[6](http://arxiv.org/abs/2104.01136)\]. These innovations collectively highlight the ongoing efforts to refine ViT architectures for better performance, efficiency, and adaptability to various vision tasks.
