## Related Works

Generative retrieval (GR) has gained significant attention in information retrieval (IR) as an alternative to traditional index-retrieve pipelines \[[14](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/5beaeee1e27007bd12967480bb9c379cebd15e6e)\]. Unlike conventional methods that rely on separate indexing and retrieval stages, GR models directly map queries to document identifiers (docids) using a sequence-to-sequence framework \[[14](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/5beaeee1e27007bd12967480bb9c379cebd15e6e)\]. This paradigm has been explored in several recent works, including Differentiable Search Index (DSI), which encodes all documents into the model parameters and allows direct query-to-docid mapping \[[4](http://arxiv.org/abs/2202.06991)\]. However, most existing GR models are evaluated on static document collections, and their performance on dynamic corpora, where new documents are continuously added, remains underexplored \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2206.02743)\].

Several studies have investigated the design of docids in GR models. For instance, the Neural Corpus Indexer (NCI) proposes a sequence-to-sequence network that generates relevant document identifiers directly for a query, with a focus on improving recall performance \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2206.02743)\]. Similarly, the Ultron model introduces a model-based indexer that unifies indexing and retrieval stages, aiming to directly retrieve relevant documents end-to-end \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/2208.09257)\]. These approaches emphasize the importance of semantic alignment and end-to-end optimization for retrieval quality.

The use of numeric-based docids, such as those generated by tokenized URLs, has also been explored for their efficiency in retrieval \[[16](http://arxiv.org/abs/2305.11161)\]. However, these docids often suffer from poor generalization over dynamic corpora due to their tendency to overfit to the initial document set \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2206.02743)\]. This issue has been observed in the TOME model, which highlights the discrepancy between training and inference in model-based retrieval \[[16](http://arxiv.org/abs/2305.11161)\]. Furthermore, the DSI model, which represents documents as sequences of docid tokens, has been shown to outperform dual encoder models in certain settings \[[4](http://arxiv.org/abs/2202.06991)\]. However, its performance on large-scale and dynamic corpora remains a challenge \[[12](http://arxiv.org/abs/2305.11841)\].

To address these limitations, several works have proposed methods to improve the generalization of GR models over dynamic corpora. For example, the CLEVER model introduces Incremental Product Quantization to encode new documents efficiently while maintaining retrieval performance \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/2308.14968)\]. Additionally, the DSI-QG framework generates potentially relevant queries during indexing, allowing the model to better align with query distributions during retrieval \[[5](http://arxiv.org/abs/2206.10128)\]. These approaches aim to mitigate the data distribution mismatch between indexing and retrieval phases.

The NOVO model further advances GR by proposing learnable and interpretable docids that can be optimized through training \[[6](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/6682a3679a17d090830635f1c420126d20eae54a)\]. This approach enables better semantic alignment and relationship learning between documents and queries. Similarly, the GenRet model introduces a discrete auto-encoding approach to tokenize documents into semantic docids, achieving significant improvements on unseen documents \[[8](http://arxiv.org/abs/2304.04171)\].

Overall, while GR models have shown promise in static settings, their performance on dynamic corpora remains a critical area for further research. The findings from this study highlight the importance of semantic alignment, fine-grained docid design, and lexical diversity in achieving robust performance over evolving document collections.

[Snippet]: «Recently, a new paradigm called Differentiable Search Index (DSI) has been proposed for document retrieval, wherein a sequence-to-sequence model is learned to directly map queries to relevant document identifiers. The key idea behind DSI is to fully parameterize traditional ''index-retrieve'' pipelines within a single neural model, by encoding all documents in the corpus into the model parameters. In essence, DSI needs to resolve two major questions: (1) how to assign an identifier to each document, and (2) how to learn the associations between a document and its identifier. In this work, we propose a Semantic-Enhanced DSI model (SE-DSI) motivated by Learning Strategies in the area of Cognitive Psychology. Our approach advances original DSI in two ways: (1) For the document identifier, we take inspiration from Elaboration Strategies in human learning. Specifically, we assign each document an Elaborative Description based on the query generation technique, which is more meaningful than a string of integers in the original DSI; and (2) For the associations between a document and its identifier, we take inspiration from Rehearsal Strategies in human learning. Specifically, we select fine-grained semantic features from a document as Rehearsal Contents to improve document memorization. Both the offline and online experiments show improved retrieval performance over prevailing baselines.»

Through extensive experiments, we reveal that existing GR models with \textit(text-based) docids show superior generalization to unseen documents.
