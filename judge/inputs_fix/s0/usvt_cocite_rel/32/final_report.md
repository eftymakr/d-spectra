## Related Works

Recent advancements in large language models (LLMs) have emphasized the importance of understanding how feature representations evolve across layers, as this knowledge is critical for improving interpretability and robustness \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2307.09288)\]. Several studies have explored the dynamics of representation learning in LLMs, focusing on how layers contribute to specific functions or behaviors. For example, \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/2109.01652)\] investigates the effectiveness of instruction tuning, showing that fine-tuning models on a collection of tasks described via natural language instructions significantly improves zero-shot performance on unseen tasks. This work highlights the potential of fine-tuning to enhance model capabilities without requiring extensive labeled data \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/2109.01652)\].

The concept of parameter-efficient fine-tuning has also gained traction, as full fine-tuning of large models is computationally expensive and impractical for many downstream tasks \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/2305.18290)\]. Techniques such as LoRA \[[7](http://arxiv.org/abs/2106.09685)\] and BitFit \[[6](http://arxiv.org/abs/2106.10199)\] have been introduced to address these challenges. LoRA, for instance, freezes the pre-trained model weights and injects trainable rank decomposition matrices into each layer of the Transformer architecture, drastically reducing the number of trainable parameters while maintaining model quality \[[7](http://arxiv.org/abs/2106.09685)\]. Similarly, BitFit modifies only the bias-terms of the model, demonstrating that small-to-medium training data can yield competitive results compared to full fine-tuning \[[6](http://arxiv.org/abs/2106.10199)\].

The importance of analyzing representation dynamics during fine-tuning has also been explored. \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2307.09288)\] introduces a data-oblivious approach to identify intrinsic critical layers in pre-fine-tuned LLMs by analyzing representation dynamics via Centered Kernel Alignment (CKA). This method reveals that layers with significant shifts in representation space are also those most affected during fine-tuning—a pattern that holds consistently across tasks for a given model \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2307.09288)\]. Such findings are crucial for understanding how models adapt to new tasks and can inform strategies for efficient domain adaptation and backdoor defense \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2307.09288)\].

Additionally, \[[4](http://arxiv.org/abs/2110.04366)\] presents a unified framework for parameter-efficient transfer learning, showing that various methods can be understood as modifications to specific hidden states in pre-trained models. This work provides a comprehensive analysis of design choices that influence the effectiveness of parameter-efficient fine-tuning methods \[[4](http://arxiv.org/abs/2110.04366)\]. Similarly, \[[5](http://arxiv.org/abs/2012.13255)\] explores the intrinsic dimensionality of pre-trained models, arguing that the low intrinsic dimensionality of these models explains their effectiveness in fine-tuning even with limited data \[[5](http://arxiv.org/abs/2012.13255)\].

In summary, the related works highlight the growing interest in understanding how LLMs evolve during fine-tuning and the development of parameter-efficient methods to achieve this. These studies provide a foundation for the current work, which introduces a novel data-oblivious approach to identify critical layers in pre-fine-tuned models \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2307.09288)\].

Parameter-efficient fine-tuning techniques have emerged as a critical area of research in NLP, particularly for adapting large pre-trained language models to downstream tasks without retraining all parameters \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2307.09288)\]. These methods aim to reduce computational costs and improve efficiency by modifying only a subset of parameters, such as bias terms \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/2109.01652)\], low-rank matrices \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/2305.18290)\], or adapter modules \[[4](http://arxiv.org/abs/2110.04366)\]. For instance, BitFit modifies only the bias-terms of the model, achieving competitive performance with full fine-tuning, especially on small-to-medium datasets \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/2109.01652)\]. Similarly, LoRA introduces trainable rank decomposition matrices into each Transformer layer, significantly reducing the number of trainable parameters while maintaining or improving model quality \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/2305.18290)\]. Adapter modules, on the other hand, add only a few trainable parameters per task, allowing for efficient model extension without revisiting previous tasks \[[4](http://arxiv.org/abs/2110.04366)\]. Prefix-tuning is another approach that keeps model parameters frozen and instead optimizes task-specific vectors, known as prefixes, which guide the generation process \[[5](http://arxiv.org/abs/2012.13255)\]. Compacter further refines these ideas by combining elements from adapters, low-rank optimization, and hypercomplex multiplication layers, achieving state-of-the-art performance with minimal parameter updates \[[6](http://arxiv.org/abs/2106.10199)\]. Child-Tuning strategically masks gradients during backpropagation to update only a subset of parameters, leading to improved performance on various downstream tasks \[[7](http://arxiv.org/abs/2106.09685)\]. These methods collectively highlight the importance of parameter efficiency in fine-tuning, offering practical solutions for deploying large models in resource-constrained environments \[[8](http://arxiv.org/abs/2407.21783)\].
