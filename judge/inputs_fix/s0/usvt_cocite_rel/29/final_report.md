## Related Works

Maximum Inner Product Search (MIPS) has been a focal point in machine learning and information retrieval, particularly for high-dimensional data. Existing approaches to MIPS can be broadly categorized into two paradigms: those that rely solely on inner product (IP) similarity and those that reduce the problem to nearest neighbor search under the Euclidean metric via space projection. The former often faces challenges such as local optima and redundant computations \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/1405.5869)\]. The latter, while effective, can lead to topology destruction and information loss due to the inherent differences between IP and Euclidean metrics \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/1909.13459)\]. However, recent studies argue that there is no inherent binary opposition between these two metrics \[[3](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/25454f456c514219c9d6eeb42200ebbf378db9ca)\]. Instead, integrating both in the design of indexing and search algorithms can significantly enhance MIPS performance \[[4](http://arxiv.org/abs/1610.03317)\].

Several works have explored hashing-based methods for approximate MIPS. Asymmetric LSH (ALSH) was introduced as the first provably sublinear time algorithm for approximate MIPS, leveraging asymmetric transformations to convert the problem into approximate near neighbor search \[[5](http://arxiv.org/abs/1202.6101)\]. This approach was later improved with the introduction of Norm-Ranging LSH, which addresses the issue of long-tailed 2-norm distributions by partitioning the dataset into sub-datasets and building hash indexes independently \[[9](http://arxiv.org/abs/1809.08782)\]. Similarly, the SIMPLE-LSH method was proposed, but it was found to suffer from performance degradation in the presence of long-tailed data distributions \[[9](http://arxiv.org/abs/1809.08782)\]. These findings highlight the importance of addressing data distribution characteristics in hashing-based approaches \[[9](http://arxiv.org/abs/1809.08782)\].

Graph-based methods have also gained traction for efficient MIPS. The ip-NSW graph, an inner-product navigable small world graph, has achieved an order of magnitude speedup over existing baselines by leveraging the norm bias of the MIPS problem \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/1909.13459)\]. This bias implies that large norm items are more likely to be the result of MIPS, and ip-NSW exploits this by assigning higher in-degrees to such items \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/1909.13459)\]. The ip-NSW+ algorithm further improves performance by introducing an angular proximity graph to refine the search process \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/1909.13459)\]. Another graph-based method, the norm adjusted proximity graph (NAPG), was proposed to improve the selection of meaningful data points during graph construction \[[3](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/25454f456c514219c9d6eeb42200ebbf378db9ca)\]. These methods demonstrate the potential of graph-based indexing for MIPS, especially when tailored to data-specific characteristics \[[3](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/25454f456c514219c9d6eeb42200ebbf378db9ca)\].

Efforts have also been made to develop lightweight and efficient indexing strategies for large-scale MIPS. The ProMIPS method, for instance, uses a lightweight iDistance index and 2-stable random projections to achieve c-approximate MIPS with probabilistic guarantees \[[7](http://arxiv.org/abs/2104.04406)\]. This approach reduces the index space and pre-processing cost while maintaining high search quality \[[7](http://arxiv.org/abs/2104.04406)\]. Similarly, the Quick-Probe method was introduced to accelerate the search process by determining the searching bound in advance \[[7](http://arxiv.org/abs/2104.04406)\]. These techniques highlight the ongoing efforts to balance efficiency and accuracy in large-scale MIPS applications \[[7](http://arxiv.org/abs/2104.04406)\].

In summary, the field of MIPS has seen significant advancements through hashing-based, graph-based, and hybrid approaches. While each method has its strengths and limitations, the integration of IP and Euclidean metrics in indexing and search algorithms offers a promising direction for future research \[[4](http://arxiv.org/abs/1610.03317)\].

Recent advancements in hashing-based approximate Maximum Inner Product Search (MIPS) have focused on overcoming the limitations of traditional Locality Sensitive Hashing (LSH) frameworks, which were deemed insufficient for MIPS due to their reliance on symmetric transformations \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/1405.5869)\]. Researchers have introduced asymmetric hashing schemes, which transform inner products into problems solvable by approximate near neighbor search, enabling sublinear time algorithms for MIPS \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/1405.5869)\]. This approach has led to the development of the extended asymmetric LSH (ALSH) framework, offering a provably efficient hashing scheme for MIPS \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/1405.5869)\]. Building on this, the SIMPLE-LSH algorithm was proposed as a state-of-the-art method, but it faced challenges due to long-tailed 2-norm distributions in real datasets, which affected its performance \[[9](http://arxiv.org/abs/1809.08782)\]. To address this, NORM-RANGING LSH was introduced, partitioning datasets into sub-datasets and building independent hash indexes, thereby improving query time complexity and reducing the number of probes required for the same recall \[[9](http://arxiv.org/abs/1809.08782)\]. Additionally, alternative transformations have been explored, converting MIPS into cosine similarity search using signed random projections, which theoretically and empirically outperforms previous methods \[[13](http://arxiv.org/abs/1410.5410)\]. These developments highlight the ongoing efforts to enhance MIPS through hashing techniques, emphasizing the importance of asymmetric transformations and dataset partitioning for improved efficiency and accuracy.

### <a id='category-graph-based-mips-indexing-techniques'></a>Graph-based MIPS Indexing Techniques: 5 papers found.
The category "Graph-based MIPS Indexing Techniques" encompasses several recent advancements in efficiently solving the Maximum Inner Product Search (MIPS) problem, which is critical for applications such as recommendation systems and information retrieval. These techniques leverage graph-based indexing to approximate MIPS, offering significant speedups over traditional methods. For instance, the inner-product navigable small world graph (ip-NSW) has been shown to achieve an order of magnitude speedup over existing baselines by exploiting the norm bias in MIPS, where large norm items are more likely to be relevant \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/1405.5869)\]. This performance is attributed to the high in-degrees of large norm items in the proximity graph, which allows the search process to focus on these items and avoid unnecessary computations on smaller norm items \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/1405.5869)\]. To further enhance performance, the ip-NSW+ algorithm introduces an angular proximity graph, enabling a two-step search process that first identifies angular neighbors and then refines the search on the inner-product graph \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/1405.5869)\]. Similarly, the norm adjusted proximity graph (NAPG) improves graph construction by incorporating adjusting factors estimated from sampled data, leading to more meaningful connections in the graph and better search efficiency \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/1909.13459)\]. The Inner Product Delaunay Graph (IPDG) also contributes to this field by transforming MIPS into a graph search problem, offering efficiency gains through its structure \[[4](http://arxiv.org/abs/1610.03317)\]. Additionally, the Hierarchical NSW (HNSW) approach introduces a multi-layer graph structure with controllable hierarchy, allowing for logarithmic complexity scaling and improved performance in high recall scenarios \[[5](http://arxiv.org/abs/1202.6101)\]. These techniques collectively highlight the growing importance of graph-based methods in addressing the challenges of MIPS, particularly in high-dimensional data applications.

These strategies highlight a growing trend toward lightweight indexing that reduces computational overhead while maintaining search quality, particularly in large-scale, high-dimensional datasets. By leveraging probabilistic guarantees and efficient filtering mechanisms, these approaches offer significant improvements in both speed and resource utilization \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/1405.5869)\]\[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/1909.13459)\].
