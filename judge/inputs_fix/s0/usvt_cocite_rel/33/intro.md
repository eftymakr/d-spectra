## Related Works

Rehearsal-based methods have emerged as a prominent approach to address catastrophic forgetting in continual learning (CL) by storing and training on a subset of past data alongside new data in current tasks \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/1612.00796)\]. These methods aim to mitigate the degradation of performance on previously learned tasks when new tasks are introduced. While concurrent rehearsal, where past and new data are trained together, is widely used, its optimality remains an open question \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/1612.00796)\]. 

Several studies have explored the effectiveness of rehearsal strategies in CL. For instance, \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/1811.11682)\] examined the use of experience replay buffers for all past events, combining on- and off-policy learning, and found that this approach could reduce catastrophic forgetting in both Atari and DMLab domains. Similarly, \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/1705.08690)\] proposed Deep Generative Replay, a framework inspired by the hippocampus's role in short-term memory, which enables the sampling and interleaving of past and new task data without requiring large memory storage. This approach has been shown to improve performance in sequential learning settings \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/1705.08690)\].

The idea of sequential rehearsal, where new data is trained first, followed by revisiting past data, has also been explored. \[[4](http://arxiv.org/abs/1801.01423)\] introduced a task-based hard attention mechanism that preserves previous tasks' information without affecting current task learning, reducing catastrophic forgetting by up to 80%. \[[5](http://arxiv.org/abs/1902.10486)\] further explored the use of tiny episodic memories for continual learning, demonstrating that a simple baseline that jointly trains on current and stored data outperforms state-of-the-art methods. These findings suggest that rehearsal strategies, whether concurrent or sequential, can significantly mitigate forgetting.

Theoretical analyses of rehearsal-based CL have also been conducted. \[[6](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/90e04f3ae23ca7df5f59b11453341e3db943b6f4)\] provided a theoretical analysis under overparameterized linear models, showing that sequential rehearsal performs better when tasks are less similar. This insight motivated the development of a hybrid rehearsal method, which trains similar tasks concurrently and revisits dissimilar tasks sequentially \[[6](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/90e04f3ae23ca7df5f59b11453341e3db943b6f4)\]. \[[7](http://arxiv.org/abs/1606.09282)\] further explored the theoretical implications of task similarity and the impact of overparameterization on forgetting and generalization error. These studies highlight the importance of task similarity and model capacity in determining the effectiveness of rehearsal strategies.

In addition to theoretical insights, practical implementations of rehearsal-based methods have been proposed. \[[8](http://arxiv.org/abs/2302.05836)\] introduced the Learning without Forgetting method, which uses only new task data to train the network while preserving original capabilities. \[[9](http://arxiv.org/abs/1904.07734)\] proposed a method that leverages generative replay combined with distillation to achieve superior performance in various scenarios. These methods demonstrate the potential of rehearsal-based approaches in real-world applications.

The effectiveness of rehearsal-based methods has also been evaluated in different learning scenarios. \[[10](http://arxiv.org/abs/1908.04742)\] compared three continual learning scenarios based on whether task identity is known and whether it must be inferred, finding that regularization-based approaches fail in scenarios where task identity must be inferred. In contrast, generative replay combined with distillation achieves superior performance. These findings underscore the importance of task identity and the need for adaptive rehearsal strategies.

In summary, the related works highlight the importance of rehearsal-based methods in addressing catastrophic forgetting in CL. While concurrent rehearsal remains a popular approach, sequential rehearsal and hybrid strategies have shown promise in improving performance, especially when tasks are less similar. Theoretical and empirical studies have provided valuable insights into the effectiveness of these methods, guiding the development of more robust and efficient continual learning systems.
