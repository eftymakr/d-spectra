## Related Works

Vectorized embeddings have become a cornerstone in modern recommender systems, enabling efficient user-item matching by capturing latent relationships in a compact form \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/1706.02216)\]. However, the computational and memory costs of maintaining dense embeddings have motivated research into representation binarization, which converts embeddings into compact binary sequences \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/1802.05814)\]. This approach has shown promise in optimizing both memory usage and computational overhead \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/1609.02907)\]. Despite these benefits, existing methods often focus on numerical quantization, which can lead to significant information loss and performance degradation \[[4](http://arxiv.org/abs/1806.01973)\]. 

Several studies have explored the use of graph-based models for recommendation tasks, leveraging the structural relationships between users and items \[[5](http://arxiv.org/abs/1905.08108)\]. For instance, GraphSAGE provides an inductive framework for generating embeddings of previously unseen nodes by aggregating features from a node's local neighborhood \[[6](http://arxiv.org/abs/1403.6652)\]. Similarly, GCN-based methods have been applied to large-scale recommender systems, where they combine efficient random walks and graph convolutions to generate embeddings that incorporate both graph structure and node features \[[7](http://arxiv.org/abs/1708.05031)\]. These models have demonstrated effectiveness in improving recommendation quality, especially in scenarios with massive graphs \[[8](http://arxiv.org/abs/1205.2618)\].

In the context of collaborative filtering, neural network-based approaches have been proposed to model user-item interactions more effectively. Neural Collaborative Filtering (NCF) introduces a multi-layer perceptron to learn the user-item interaction function, offering better performance than traditional matrix factorization methods \[[9](http://arxiv.org/abs/1708.04396)\]. Variational Autoencoders have also been extended to collaborative filtering, enabling the modeling of implicit feedback with a non-linear probabilistic framework \[[10](http://arxiv.org/abs/1905.07854)\]. These methods have shown significant improvements over state-of-the-art baselines, particularly in capturing complex interactions between users and items \[[11](http://arxiv.org/abs/1902.06236)\].

The integration of knowledge graphs into recommendation systems has also gained traction, as they provide rich side information that can enhance the modeling of user preferences \[[12](http://arxiv.org/abs/2001.00267)\]. Knowledge Graph Attention Networks (KGAT) explicitly model high-order relations in knowledge graphs, leading to more accurate and interpretable recommendations \[[13](http://arxiv.org/abs/1310.4546)\]. Additionally, methods like BiRank and TriRank have been developed to rank vertices in bipartite and n-partite graphs, respectively, by leveraging both graph structure and prior information \[[14](http://arxiv.org/abs/1708.05027)\]. These approaches demonstrate the potential of graph-based models in capturing collaborative signals that are often overlooked in traditional methods \[[15](http://arxiv.org/abs/1409.2944)\].

In summary, while existing approaches have made significant strides in improving recommendation accuracy and efficiency, there remains a need for more robust methods that minimize information loss during representation binarization. Our work builds on these prior contributions by introducing BiGeaR++, which explicitly mitigates information loss during the binarization process, leading to improved performance in collaborative filtering tasks \[[16](http://arxiv.org/abs/1810.00826)\].
