## Related Works

Fairness in recommendation systems has garnered significant attention in recent years, with a growing body of research addressing the trade-offs between recommendation accuracy and fairness. Prior work has identified that fairness constraints often lead to a decline in recommendation accuracy, particularly when fairness is enforced at the item level \[[16](http://arxiv.org/abs/2104.10671)\]. This trade-off is analogous to economic principles, where fairness considerations in re-ranking can be likened to a commodity tax on suppliers, ultimately translating into a cost passed on to consumers \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/1903.00780)\]. The extent to which such fairness constraints impact accuracy is formalized using the concept of elasticity, which measures how changes in fairness affect user-side performance \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/1903.00780)\]. 

Several studies have explored fairness-aware re-ranking algorithms. For instance, \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/1809.02921)\] introduces a framework for evaluating fairness in rankings through pairwise comparisons, offering a tractable means to reason about fairness in rankings. Similarly, \[[13](http://arxiv.org/abs/2104.09024)\] proposes a two-sided fairness-aware recommendation model that considers both customer and provider fairness, ensuring that neither side is disadvantaged. The work in \[[14](http://arxiv.org/abs/2110.15781)\] extends this idea by using Lorenz dominance to generate rankings that are fair towards both users and item producers, maximizing utility for the worse-off individuals. 

The concept of fairness through awareness, introduced in \[[5](http://arxiv.org/abs/1104.3913)\], provides a foundational framework for fair classification, which has been adapted to recommendation systems. This approach ensures that similar individuals are treated similarly, which is crucial for maintaining fairness in personalized recommendations. Additionally, \[[12](http://arxiv.org/abs/1705.08804)\] presents new fairness metrics for collaborative filtering, addressing different forms of unfairness that arise due to biased historical data. These metrics are optimized by adding fairness terms to the learning objective, effectively reducing unfairness in recommendations.

In the context of two-sided platforms, \[[7](http://arxiv.org/abs/2002.10764)\] investigates the problem of fair recommendation, considering both customer and producer fairness. The work proposes a novel mapping of the fair recommendation problem to a constrained version of the problem of fairly allocating indivisible goods, ensuring that both sides are treated fairly. Similarly, \[[13](http://arxiv.org/abs/2104.09024)\] introduces a two-sided fairness-aware recommendation model that balances recommendation quality, customer fairness, and provider fairness. The model is designed to maintain a high level of personalization while ensuring fairness for both sides.

The challenge of achieving fairness in rankings while maintaining ranking quality has been addressed in several works. \[[10](http://arxiv.org/abs/1704.06840)\] introduces a constrained ranking maximization problem, where fairness or diversity constraints are imposed alongside the traditional ranking objectives. The work provides fast exact and approximation algorithms, along with complementary hardness results, to solve this problem efficiently. \[[15](http://arxiv.org/abs/2303.06660)\] further explores the issue of provider fairness in recommender systems, proposing an online re-ranking model that balances user preferences and provider fairness in an online recommendation scenario.

In summary, the literature on fairness in recommendation systems highlights the importance of balancing accuracy and fairness, with various approaches proposed to address the trade-offs. These include fairness-aware re-ranking algorithms, two-sided fairness models, and elasticity-based frameworks for evaluating fairness-accuracy trade-offs.
