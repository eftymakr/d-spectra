## Related Works

Video super-resolution (VSR) has been a focal point in computer vision, with recent advances leveraging deep learning to address the challenges of temporal consistency and motion estimation \[[1](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/c6a8ff4d9dcdf9e5d82310b847af999ed6655f65)\]. Early methods often relied on explicit motion compensation, which is computationally expensive and prone to errors \[[1](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/c6a8ff4d9dcdf9e5d82310b847af999ed6655f65)\]. For instance, the Deep Video Super-Resolution Network Using Dynamic Upsampling Filters Without Explicit Motion Compensation introduced an end-to-end framework that generates dynamic upsampling filters and residual images, avoiding the need for explicit motion compensation \[[1](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/c6a8ff4d9dcdf9e5d82310b847af999ed6655f65)\]. This approach significantly improves the sharpness and temporal consistency of HR videos \[[1](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/c6a8ff4d9dcdf9e5d82310b847af999ed6655f65)\].

Several studies have explored spatio-temporal networks and motion compensation to enhance VSR performance. Real-Time Video Super-Resolution with Spatio-Temporal Networks and Motion Compensation proposed a novel joint motion compensation and video super-resolution algorithm that is more efficient than competing methods \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/1611.05250)\]. This method uses a fast multi-resolution spatial transformer module, enabling real-time processing while maintaining high accuracy \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/1611.05250)\]. Similarly, the Frame-Recurrent Video Super-Resolution framework introduced a recurrent structure that uses previously inferred HR estimates to super-resolve subsequent frames, naturally encouraging temporally consistent results \[[4](http://arxiv.org/abs/1801.04590)\]. This approach reduces computational cost by warping only one image per step and allows assimilation of a large number of previous frames without increased computational demands \[[4](http://arxiv.org/abs/1801.04590)\].

Temporal alignment remains a critical challenge in VSR, particularly when dealing with varying motion between frames. The Temporally-Deformable Alignment Network (TDAN) addresses this by aligning reference and supporting frames at the feature level without computing optical flow \[[5](http://arxiv.org/abs/1812.02898)\]. This method dynamically predicts offsets of sampling convolution kernels to transform supporting frames, improving alignment accuracy and reducing artifacts \[[5](http://arxiv.org/abs/1812.02898)\]. Similarly, the Enhanced Deformable Convolutional Networks (EDVR) tackle large motion alignment by using a Pyramid, Cascading, and Deformable (PCD) alignment module that operates at the feature level in a coarse-to-fine manner \[[6](http://arxiv.org/abs/1905.02716)\]. This module enables effective alignment of multiple frames with diverse motion and blur \[[6](http://arxiv.org/abs/1905.02716)\].

To further improve temporal consistency and reduce artifacts, the BasicVSR++ model introduced second-order grid propagation and flow-guided deformable alignment \[[11](http://arxiv.org/abs/2104.13371)\]. These enhancements allow the model to exploit spatiotemporal information across misaligned video frames more effectively, leading to improved performance \[[11](http://arxiv.org/abs/2104.13371)\]. Additionally, the Video Super-Resolution Transformer (VSR-Transformer) adapted the Transformer architecture for VSR by introducing a spatial-temporal convolutional self-attention layer and a bidirectional optical flow-based feed-forward layer \[[12](http://arxiv.org/abs/2106.06847)\]. These modifications enable better feature alignment and locality exploitation, leading to improved performance on benchmark datasets \[[12](http://arxiv.org/abs/2106.06847)\].

In summary, recent VSR methods have focused on improving temporal consistency, reducing computational cost, and enhancing alignment accuracy. These advancements have been driven by the integration of recurrent structures, attention mechanisms, and deformable convolutions \[[1](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/c6a8ff4d9dcdf9e5d82310b847af999ed6655f65)\]\[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/1611.05250)\]\[[4](http://arxiv.org/abs/1801.04590)\]\[[5](http://arxiv.org/abs/1812.02898)\]\[[6](http://arxiv.org/abs/1905.02716)\]\[[11](http://arxiv.org/abs/2104.13371)\]\[[12](http://arxiv.org/abs/2106.06847)\].

More recent approaches, such as spatio-temporal sub-pixel convolution networks, have been introduced to better exploit temporal redundancies while maintaining real-time performance, using techniques like early fusion, slow fusion, and 3D convolutions \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/1611.05250)\]. These methods have also incorporated novel joint motion compensation and video super-resolution algorithms that are more efficient and accurate \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/1611.05250)\]. Additionally, progressive fusion networks have been proposed to effectively utilize spatio-temporal information, reducing computational costs and improving efficiency compared to traditional methods \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/2201.12288)\]. Another key development involves the use of non-local operations to avoid complex motion estimation and compensation procedures, leading to faster and more effective video super-resolution \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/2201.12288)\]. Furthermore, the use of Transformer architectures has been explored, with modifications to address limitations in attention mechanisms and feature alignment, enabling better performance in VSR tasks \[[5](http://arxiv.org/abs/1812.02898)\]. These innovations collectively contribute to the current state-of-the-art in VSR, emphasizing the importance of temporal information, efficient computation, and high-quality output.

### <a id='category-spatio-temporal-networks'></a>Spatio-Temporal Networks: 3 papers found.
Video restoration and super-resolution techniques have seen significant advancements, particularly in leveraging spatio-temporal information for improved performance. Recent methods such as the Video Restoration Transformer (VRT) \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/2201.12288)\] introduce a parallel frame prediction approach using temporal reciprocal self attention (TRSA) and parallel warping to effectively utilize temporal information across misaligned video frames. This enables better motion estimation, feature alignment, and fusion, leading to superior results across multiple tasks including video super-resolution, deblurring, and denoising. Similarly, a deep convolutional neural network (CNN) model for video deblurring \[[19](http://arxiv.org/abs/2004.02501)\] employs optical flow estimation and a temporal sharpness prior to enhance latent frame restoration, demonstrating the importance of domain knowledge in designing efficient and compact models. In the context of space-time video super-resolution, a one-stage framework \[[26](http://arxiv.org/abs/2002.11616)\] directly synthesizes high-resolution slow-motion videos by integrating temporal interpolation and spatial super-resolution, avoiding the limitations of two-stage methods. These approaches highlight the ongoing efforts to improve temporal coherence and reduce artifacts through advanced architectures and training strategies, aligning with the goals of the proposed self-supervised VSR framework.

Recent advances in video super-resolution (VSR) have focused on improving temporal consistency and computational efficiency through recurrent frameworks \[[1](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/c6a8ff4d9dcdf9e5d82310b847af999ed6655f65)\]. To address these challenges, researchers have proposed recurrent models that leverage previously inferred high-resolution (HR) estimates to super-resolve subsequent frames, thereby reducing redundant computations and enhancing temporal consistency \[[1](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/c6a8ff4d9dcdf9e5d82310b847af999ed6655f65)\]. These models often incorporate hidden state adaptation modules to enhance robustness against appearance changes and error accumulation \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/1611.05250)\]. Furthermore, the integration of recurrent structures with transformers, such as the proposed RVRT, has enabled parallel processing of local frames within a globally recurrent framework, achieving a balance between model size, effectiveness, and efficiency \[[4](http://arxiv.org/abs/1801.04590)\]. These developments highlight the growing trend of using recurrent architectures to achieve superior performance in video super-resolution tasks.

[Snippet]: «Video super-resolution (VSR) aims to restore a photo-realistic high-resolution (HR) video frame from both its corresponding low-resolution (LR) frame (reference frame) and multiple neighboring frames (supporting frames). Due to varying motion of cameras or objects, the reference frame and each support frame are not aligned. Therefore, temporal alignment is a challenging yet important problem for VSR. Previous VSR methods usually utilize optical flow between the reference frame and each supporting frame to warp the supporting frame for temporal alignment. However, both inaccurate flow and the image-level warping strategy will lead to artifacts in the warped supporting frames. To overcome the limitation, we propose a temporally-deformable alignment network (TDAN) to adaptively align the reference frame and each supporting frame at the feature level without computing optical flow. The TDAN uses features from both the reference frame and each supporting frame to dynamically predict offsets of sampling convolution kernels. By using the corresponding kernels, TDAN transforms supporting frames to align with the reference frame. To predict the HR video frame, a reconstruction network taking aligned frames and the reference frame is utilized. Experimental results demonstrate that the TDAN is capable of alleviating occlusions and artifacts for temporal alignment and the TDAN-based VSR model outperforms several recent state-of-the-art VSR networks with a comparable or even much smaller model size.
