## Related Works

Modern databases often adopt weak isolation levels to achieve high availability and performance, sacrificing some correctness guarantees in favor of scalability \[[12](http://arxiv.org/abs/1302.0309)\]. This trade-off has led to the emergence of various weak isolation models, such as Read Committed (RC), Read Atomic (RA), and Causal Consistency (CC), which are less restrictive than serializability but still aim to prevent certain anomalies \[[8](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/38c443a37914e287c42a3238ccebd7372847d5f9)\]. However, these weaker models can introduce subtle correctness issues that are challenging to detect and debug \[[5](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/405ae0ebf74cfbf5f2edec62e2966827dcdc9bef)\].

Several studies have investigated the complexity of checking transactional consistency under different isolation levels. For example, it has been shown that consistency models like read committed, read atomic, and causal consistency are polynomial-time checkable, while prefix consistency and snapshot isolation are NP-complete in general \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/1908.04509)\]. This distinction is important because it influences the design of testing tools and the feasibility of automated verification. Moreover, for NP-complete models, polynomial-time algorithms can be devised under certain constraints, such as fixed session counts \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/1908.04509)\].

Black-box testing has emerged as a prominent technique for detecting isolation bugs by verifying whether transaction histories adhere to a prescribed isolation level \[[14](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/4f3081dbb68287f0d9d7bec3e7173e6c8ff035b9)\]. Tools like MonkeyDB \[[1](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/615ff2896b52bee2cbfd64ced324106a29b73397)\], PolySI \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/2301.07313)\], and Viper \[[6](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/9f96a48d2bc0e77004b32960495685be6c301c63)\] have been developed to simulate weak isolation behaviors and detect violations. MonkeyDB, for instance, uses a logical specification of isolation levels to compute possible return values for read operations, enabling the detection of anomalies that occur under weak isolation \[[1](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/615ff2896b52bee2cbfd64ced324106a29b73397)\]. Similarly, PolySI leverages SMT solvers and domain-specific optimizations to efficiently check snapshot isolation \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/2301.07313)\]. Viper, on the other hand, introduces BC-polygraphs to represent transaction dependencies and ensures sound and complete checking of SI \[[6](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/9f96a48d2bc0e77004b32960495685be6c301c63)\].

Recent work has also focused on improving the efficiency of isolation checkers. Plume \[[7](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/cbe191bda20907994d45bc835d20446968370add)\] presents a black-box checker that is both efficient and complete for weak isolation levels, using vectors and tree clocks to accelerate checking. It has been shown to detect new isolation bugs in production databases and reproduce known violations \[[7](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/cbe191bda20907994d45bc835d20446968370add)\]. IsoVista \[[14](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/4f3081dbb68287f0d9d7bec3e7173e6c8ff035b9)\] further advances this field by offering a comprehensive black-box checking system that ensures accuracy and efficiency, with visualizations to aid in debugging \[[14](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/4f3081dbb68287f0d9d7bec3e7173e6c8ff035b9)\].

The complexity of testing weak isolation levels has also been addressed in the context of large-scale workloads. AWDIT \[[16](http://arxiv.org/abs/1905.08406)\] is a novel tester that achieves significant improvements over existing tools, testing RC, RA, and CC in time complexities of $O(n^(3/2))$, $O(n^(3/2))$, and $O(n \cdot k)$, respectively. This represents a substantial improvement over the state of the art, particularly for large histories \[[16](http://arxiv.org/abs/1905.08406)\].
