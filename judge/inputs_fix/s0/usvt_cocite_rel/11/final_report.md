## Related Works

3D human pose estimation has attracted significant research interest due to its applications in robotics, virtual reality, and human-computer interaction. Early approaches primarily relied on 2D pose estimation followed by a mapping to 3D space, often using regression or optimization techniques \[[5](http://arxiv.org/abs/1705.03098)\]. However, these methods suffer from limited accuracy due to depth ambiguity and self-occlusions \[[1](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/50167a9e6bdc1f64a58fe8a3c990105fec0bd2bc)\]. Recent works have focused on leveraging spatial and temporal relationships to improve estimation accuracy \[[1](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/50167a9e6bdc1f64a58fe8a3c990105fec0bd2bc)\]\[[8](http://arxiv.org/abs/1711.08585)\].

Spatial dependencies are crucial for modeling the human body's structure. For instance, \[[1](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/50167a9e6bdc1f64a58fe8a3c990105fec0bd2bc)\] proposed a graph-based method that incorporates domain knowledge about human body configurations into graph convolutional operations. This approach explicitly models spatial relationships and improves 3D pose estimation by capturing local and global interactions. Similarly, \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/1704.00159)\] introduced a structure-aware regression approach that uses bones instead of joints to represent pose, enabling the modeling of long-range interactions through a compositional loss function. This method significantly advances the state-of-the-art on Human3.6M and is competitive with state-of-the-art results on MPII \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/1704.00159)\].

Temporal consistency is another critical factor, especially when estimating 3D poses from video sequences. \[[8](http://arxiv.org/abs/1711.08585)\] utilized a sequence-to-sequence network with LSTM units to enforce temporal smoothness, leading to more coherent 3D pose estimates. Additionally, \[[9](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/30de7fecb146356d7ae6cafa072997674601e0f5)\] proposed an attention-based framework that exploits temporal contexts by adaptively identifying significant frames and tensor outputs, resulting in a 8% error reduction compared to state-of-the-art methods \[[9](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/30de7fecb146356d7ae6cafa072997674601e0f5)\].

Graph-based models have also been widely explored for their ability to model complex human body structures. \[[1](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/50167a9e6bdc1f64a58fe8a3c990105fec0bd2bc)\] introduced a graph convolutional network architecture that processes features across multiple scales, enabling the learning of both local and global representations. \[[14](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/442928cd95d7bca15800b29ce2e2f8db5461bd58)\] further improved upon this by proposing a Locally Connected Network (LCN) that outperforms GCNs in terms of representation power and generalization. This approach is less data hungry and generalizes well to unseen actions and datasets \[[14](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/442928cd95d7bca15800b29ce2e2f8db5461bd58)\].

Transformers have recently gained traction in 3D human pose estimation due to their ability to model long-range dependencies. \[[6](http://arxiv.org/abs/2103.10455)\] introduced PoseFormer, a purely transformer-based approach that models spatial and temporal correlations within and across frames, achieving state-of-the-art results on Human3.6M and MPI-INF-3DHP \[[6](http://arxiv.org/abs/2103.10455)\]. Similarly, \[[22](http://arxiv.org/abs/2103.14304)\] proposed a Strided Transformer that reduces redundancy in 2D pose sequences and aggregates information from local contexts, leading to more accurate and smoother 3D pose estimation \[[22](http://arxiv.org/abs/2103.14304)\].

To address the limitations of existing methods, \[[1](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/50167a9e6bdc1f64a58fe8a3c990105fec0bd2bc)\] introduced a novel Pyramid Graph Attention (PGA) module that captures long-range cross-scale dependencies by concatenating information from various scales and computing correlations in parallel. This approach complements the context of human sub-structures and enables more accurate 3D pose estimation. \[[1](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/50167a9e6bdc1f64a58fe8a3c990105fec0bd2bc)\] further combined the PGA module with graph convolution modules to develop a lightweight multi-scale transformer architecture, PGFormer, which achieves lower error and smaller model size than state-of-the-art methods on Human3.6M and MPI-INF-3DHP datasets \[[1](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/50167a9e6bdc1f64a58fe8a3c990105fec0bd2bc)\].

To overcome these issues, recent works have introduced modulated GCNs that allow for learning different feature transformations per node and adjusting graph structures to include additional edges beyond the skeleton \[[15](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/0646857f867d9a624f2b53d1ffb4de99e225b2d3)\]. Additionally, semantic graph convolutional networks (SemGCN) have been proposed to capture both local and global semantic relationships in graph-structured data, leading to improved performance with fewer parameters \[[18](http://arxiv.org/abs/1904.03345)\]. Another approach, the high-order GCN, aggregates features from nodes at various distances to model a wide range of interactions among body joints \[[21](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/2170b81eaac4095f5948b7922f29200a25db9b2b)\]. Furthermore, methods like Pose2Mesh directly estimate 3D mesh coordinates from 2D pose inputs, leveraging graph CNNs to exploit mesh topology while avoiding representation issues of 3D rotations \[[26](http://arxiv.org/abs/2008.09047)\]. The integration of graph structures with multi-scale learning, as seen in the Pyramid Graph Attention (PGA) module, enables the capture of cross-scale dependencies, enhancing the modeling of human sub-structures \[[1](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/50167a9e6bdc1f64a58fe8a3c990105fec0bd2bc)\]. These advancements highlight the ongoing efforts to improve the accuracy and efficiency of 3D human pose estimation using graph-based methods.
