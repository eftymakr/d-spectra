## Related Works

Zero-Knowledge Proofs (ZKPs) have emerged as a critical cryptographic primitive for privacy-preserving computation, enabling a prover to demonstrate the validity of a statement without revealing additional information \[[5](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/9ccf7b6cb32cf89752a35bd910555adac54773e0)\]. Recent advancements have focused on accelerating the computational bottlenecks in ZKP generation, particularly for large-scale applications such as blockchain and machine learning \[[1](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/e149d93531d8c07cf43f6cae327a4795144d1304)\]. However, existing solutions face significant limitations, either requiring a trusted setup for each application \[[1](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/e149d93531d8c07cf43f6cae327a4795144d1304)\] or generating proofs with higher verification costs \[[1](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/e149d93531d8c07cf43f6cae327a4795144d1304)\]. This section reviews prior work on ZKP acceleration and related cryptographic protocols.

Several studies have explored hardware and software accelerations for ZKP protocols. GZKP leverages GPU acceleration to improve the performance of polynomial computations and multi-scalar multiplications (MSMs), achieving up to 48.1× speedup over state-of-the-art systems \[[1](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/e149d93531d8c07cf43f6cae327a4795144d1304)\]. Similarly, PipeZK introduces a pipelined architecture with two subsystems to handle large polynomial computations and elliptic curve operations, achieving 10× speedup on cryptographic benchmarks \[[2](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/6ee84674b7c426849c3da56cb278c08eb7a903cf)\]. cuZK further improves performance by optimizing the parallelization of MSM and matrix-vector multiplication (MUL) operations, achieving up to 4.86× speedup on Filecoin \[[3](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/e4a4608e8e35379e63b9df5bda1b7599ee8ab9ac)\]. These works highlight the importance of optimizing the key primitives in ZKP generation, particularly MSM and NTT, which are computationally intensive.

In the context of machine learning, zero-knowledge proofs have been applied to verify the correctness of decision tree and convolutional neural network (CNN) predictions \[[4](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/3becc2935d1f52c82ffd7216c28bc86f5e393208)\]\[[6](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/6336149196f02d0eb6c1a89bc6b662e4422bd794)\]. For example, zkCNN introduces a novel sumcheck protocol to efficiently prove CNN predictions, achieving 1264× speedup over existing schemes \[[6](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/6336149196f02d0eb6c1a89bc6b662e4422bd794)\]. These approaches demonstrate the potential of ZKPs in ensuring the integrity of machine learning models without exposing sensitive information about the model itself.

Recent hardware accelerators have also been proposed to address the computational overhead of ZKP protocols. CraterLake is the first FHE accelerator to support unbounded computation on encrypted data, achieving gmean 4,600× speedup over CPU implementations \[[8](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/b718a4b59ee6ee21c7c2ee1aa64085231853535e)\]. Similarly, F1 introduces a programmable accelerator for FHE, achieving up to 17,000× speedup over software implementations \[[10](http://arxiv.org/abs/2109.05371)\]. These works emphasize the need for specialized hardware to reduce the overhead of cryptographic computations, particularly for complex applications like deep learning.

In the context of ZKP protocols, SZKP presents a scalable accelerator framework that is the first ASIC to accelerate an entire proof on-chip, achieving over 400× speedup over CPU implementations \[[9](http://arxiv.org/abs/2408.05890)\]. This work highlights the potential of dedicated hardware for accelerating ZKP protocols, particularly for large-scale applications requiring high throughput and low latency.

In summary, prior work has focused on optimizing the computational bottlenecks in ZKP protocols through hardware and software accelerations, with a particular emphasis on improving the performance of key primitives such as MSM and NTT. These efforts have laid the foundation for more efficient and scalable ZKP systems, which are essential for widespread adoption in privacy-preserving applications.
