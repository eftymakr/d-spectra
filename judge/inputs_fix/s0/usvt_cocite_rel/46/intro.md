## Related Works

Cross-domain sequential recommendation (CDSR) has gained significant attention in recent years due to its ability to alleviate data sparsity and cold-start problems in recommender systems by leveraging user behavior across multiple domains \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2304.03891)\]. Early approaches primarily relied on Markov Chains (MCs) and Recurrent Neural Networks (RNNs) to model sequential patterns \[[4](http://arxiv.org/abs/1808.09781)\]. While MCs are effective in sparse datasets, they fail to capture long-term dependencies, whereas RNNs, though more expressive, suffer from computational inefficiency and difficulty in handling long sequences \[[4](http://arxiv.org/abs/1808.09781)\]. To address these limitations, self-attention based models like SASRec were introduced, which enable the capture of long-term semantics while maintaining computational efficiency by focusing on relevant items in the user's history \[[4](http://arxiv.org/abs/1808.09781)\].

Several works have explored the use of domain-aware modules to enhance cross-domain recommendations \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2304.03891)\]. For example, DA-GCN introduces a domain-aware graph convolution network to learn user-specific node representations and employs attention mechanisms to guide message passing \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/2105.03300)\]. Similarly, DASL proposes a dual learning mechanism that iteratively transfers information between domains to improve cross-domain CTR predictions \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/2106.02768)\]. These methods often focus on transferring knowledge from a source domain to a target domain but may overlook the inter-sequence relationships and the potential for bidirectional knowledge exchange \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/2106.02768)\].

Recent studies have also emphasized the importance of modeling inter-sequence and intra-sequence relationships in CDSR. C2DSR, for instance, leverages both intra- and inter-sequence item interactions to jointly learn single- and cross-domain user preferences \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2304.03891)\]. The model uses graph neural networks to mine collaborative relationships across sequences and sequential attentive encoders to capture sequential dependencies \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2304.03891)\]. Additionally, a contrastive cross-domain infomax objective is introduced to enhance the correlation between single- and cross-domain user representations \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2304.03891)\].

Another line of research focuses on the challenges of data sparsity and class imbalance in CDSR. DREAM introduces a decoupled representation framework that simultaneously learns single- and cross-domain user preferences while addressing data sparsity through data augmentation and class imbalance via Focal Loss \[[8](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/96bfe33a2503e960153a934e10be91da4d8b13a6)\]. Similarly, Tri-CDR jointly models source, target, and mixed behavior sequences to capture global and target preferences, using a triple cross-domain attention mechanism and a triple contrastive learning strategy \[[9](http://arxiv.org/abs/2304.05027)\].

Moreover, the open-world assumption has become an important consideration in CDSR, as real-world datasets often exhibit data distribution shifts and cold-start users \[[7](http://arxiv.org/abs/2311.04590)\]. AMID introduces an Adaptive Multi-Interest Debiasing framework that addresses these challenges by incorporating a multi-interest information module and a doubly robust estimator \[[7](http://arxiv.org/abs/2311.04590)\]. This framework is designed to be adaptable to open-world environments and improves the performance of existing single-domain sequential models \[[7](http://arxiv.org/abs/2311.04590)\].

In summary, while many approaches have focused on domain-specific components and knowledge transfer, recent works have emphasized the importance of leveraging self-attention mechanisms and modeling both intra- and inter-sequence relationships to improve cross-domain sequential recommendations \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2304.03891)\]\[[4](http://arxiv.org/abs/1808.09781)\]\[[7](http://arxiv.org/abs/2311.04590)\].
