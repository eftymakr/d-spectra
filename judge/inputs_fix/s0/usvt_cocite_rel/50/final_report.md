## Related Works

The rapid growth of open-source machine learning (ML) resources has significantly impacted information retrieval (IR) research, as noted in several recent studies \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2305.11164)\]. While platforms like Hugging Face have become central to the ML community, they lack structured representations that enable advanced querying and analysis \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2305.11164)\]. This limitation has motivated researchers to explore novel approaches to enhance the management and utilization of ML resources.

Several studies have examined the characteristics and usage patterns of ML models on Hugging Face. For instance, \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/2311.13380)\] highlights the dominance of NLP in carbon emissions reporting and the correlation between model size and emissions. Similarly, \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/2401.13822)\] discusses the evolution and maintenance of ML models, emphasizing the need for better documentation and versioning practices. These findings underscore the importance of structured metadata for effective resource management.

In the realm of dataset documentation, \[[4](http://arxiv.org/abs/2303.02552)\] provides a comprehensive analysis of dataset cards on Hugging Face, revealing that while certain sections like Dataset Description are well-documented, others such as Considerations for Using the Data are underdeveloped. This highlights a gap in the documentation practices that could hinder the usability and reproducibility of datasets. Additionally, \[[5](http://arxiv.org/abs/2406.08205)\] discusses the challenges of model reuse, including issues related to documentation quality and reproducibility, which are critical for the effective use of ML resources.

The importance of structured representations for advanced querying has also been emphasized in the context of model reuse. \[[6](http://arxiv.org/abs/2402.07323)\] discusses the challenges of reusing pre-trained models, including the lack of standardized documentation and the difficulty in assessing model performance. These challenges are closely related to the need for a knowledge graph like HuggingKG to provide a structured representation of ML resources.

Furthermore, \[[7](http://arxiv.org/abs/2405.13058)\] explores the evolution of the Hugging Face ecosystem, noting the rapid turnover of models and the strong correlation between documentation quality and model popularity. This finding supports the argument for a knowledge graph that captures both the technical and social aspects of ML resources. Similarly, \[[8](http://arxiv.org/abs/2401.13177)\] discusses the challenges of model reuse, including the lack of guidance for beginner users and the difficulty in understanding model outputs, which are addressed by the structured representation provided by HuggingKG.

In addition, \[[9](http://arxiv.org/abs/2307.14841)\] evaluates the suitability of Hugging Face Hub for empirical studies, highlighting its potential as a source for analyzing the development and evolution of AI-related projects. This aligns with the goal of HuggingKG to provide a comprehensive and structured view of ML resources for research and development.

Finally, \[[10](http://arxiv.org/abs/2405.16746)\] discusses the ecosystem of large language models for code, emphasizing the need for detailed documentation and licensing practices. These findings reinforce the importance of structured metadata in the management of ML resources.
