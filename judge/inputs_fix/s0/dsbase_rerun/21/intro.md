## Related Works

Video frame interpolation (VFI) has been extensively studied for its ability to synthesize intermediate frames in video sequences, which is crucial for applications such as video compression, slow-motion generation, and 3D reconstruction. Early methods relied on optical flow estimation and warping \[[8' 2019-04-01](http://arxiv.org/abs/1904.00830v1)\], but recent advances have introduced deep learning-based approaches that leverage neural networks to model motion and appearance \[[13' 2017-08-05](http://arxiv.org/abs/1708.01692v1)\]. For example, the Adaptive Separable Convolution method \[[13' 2017-08-05](http://arxiv.org/abs/1708.01692v1)\] merges motion estimation and pixel synthesis into a single convolutional process, improving efficiency and quality by using spatially adaptive kernels. Similarly, the Flow Transformer \[[11' 2023-07-30](http://arxiv.org/abs/2307.16144v1)\] incorporates motion dynamics from optical flows into the self-attention mechanism, enabling better handling of large motion scenarios. These methods have demonstrated significant improvements in visual quality and robustness to motion blur and occlusion.

Neural rendering methods have also gained attention for their ability to reconstruct 3D scenes from 2D images, often using implicit neural representations (INRs) to model geometry and appearance \[[1' 2022-06-22](http://arxiv.org/abs/2206.10886v1)\]. INRs have been used in video frame interpolation by explicitly constraining the derivatives of the INR to satisfy the optical flow constraint equation, leading to state-of-the-art results on limited motion ranges \[[1' 2022-06-22](http://arxiv.org/abs/2206.10886v1)\]. Additionally, the use of neural implicit representations for 3D scene reconstruction has been explored, with methods like the Online 3D Scene Reconstruction Using Neural Object Priors \[[3' 2025-03-24](http://arxiv.org/abs/2503.18897v1)\] introducing feature grid interpolation mechanisms to update object-centric representations as new views are acquired. These approaches address limitations in online reconstruction efficiency and shape completion.

The importance of temporal consistency in video processing has also been emphasized. For instance, the Video Frame Interpolation without Temporal Priors \[[2' 2021-12-02](http://arxiv.org/abs/2112.01161v1)\] derives a general curvilinear motion trajectory formula from four consecutive sharp frames or two consecutive blurry frames, allowing for interpolation without explicit temporal priors. This is particularly useful in real-world scenarios where exposure and frame rate settings may vary. Furthermore, the use of cycle consistency \[[6' 2019-06-13](http://arxiv.org/abs/1906.05928v3)\] has been proposed as an unsupervised technique to synthesize high frame rate videos from low frame rate inputs, improving performance without requiring additional training data. This approach has been shown to significantly enhance pre-trained models on new target domains.

The integration of perceptual quality assessment into VFI has also been explored. For example, the A Perceptual Quality Metric for Video Frame Interpolation \[[10' 2022-10-04](http://arxiv.org/abs/2210.01879v1)\] introduces a dedicated metric that learns perceptual features directly from videos, improving alignment with human perception. This is especially important as existing metrics often fail to capture the nuances of video interpolation artifacts. Additionally, the BVI-VFI dataset \[[14' 2022-10-03](http://arxiv.org/abs/2210.00823v3)\] provides a comprehensive collection of distorted sequences generated by applying various VFI algorithms, enabling more accurate evaluation of quality assessment methods.

In summary, the field of video frame interpolation and neural rendering has seen significant advancements, with methods ranging from traditional optical flow-based approaches to modern deep learning-based frameworks. These developments have laid the groundwork for more robust and efficient techniques, such as the proposed PS4PRO model, which leverages video frame interpolation as a data augmentation method for neural rendering.
