## Related Works

Differential privacy (DP) has emerged as a de facto standard for ensuring data privacy in statistical databases and machine learning systems \[[19' 2020-06-10](http://arxiv.org/abs/2006.05609v2)\]. However, its implementation in distributed settings is vulnerable to malicious behaviors by servers, such as sampling noise from incorrect distributions or generating correlated noise while appearing to follow established protocols \[[1' 2021-04-14](http://arxiv.org/abs/2104.06569v2)\]. This vulnerability has been extensively studied, with several works highlighting the risks of unfaithful execution of DP mechanisms \[[1' 2021-04-14](http://arxiv.org/abs/2104.06569v2)\]. For instance, \[[1' 2021-04-14](http://arxiv.org/abs/2104.06569v2)\] demonstrates that malicious data providers can manipulate their output before sending, leveraging the plausible deniability provided by randomization in LDP protocols. This issue has led to the development of verifiable randomization mechanisms to ensure that the data collector can verify the completeness of the randomization process \[[1' 2021-04-14](http://arxiv.org/abs/2104.06569v2)\].

Verifiable differential privacy (VDP) has been proposed as a solution to close the loophole introduced by the randomness in DP, where a malicious entity could manipulate results and use the randomness as a smokescreen \[[3' 2022-08-18](http://arxiv.org/abs/2208.09011v2)\]. The concept of VDP requires the publishing entity to output a zero-knowledge proof that convinces an efficient verifier that the output is both DP and reliable \[[3' 2022-08-18](http://arxiv.org/abs/2208.09011v2)\]. This idea has been further extended to distributed settings, where the introduction of verifiable distributed differential privacy (VDDP) aims to address the challenges of unfaithful execution by incorporating additional verification mechanisms \[[1' 2021-04-14](http://arxiv.org/abs/2104.06569v2)\]. The relationship between zero-knowledge proofs (ZKP) and DP has also been explored, with \[[1' 2021-04-14](http://arxiv.org/abs/2104.06569v2)\] showing that while ZKPs are sufficient for achieving DP under verifiability requirements, they are not necessary.

Several works have proposed verifiable mechanisms for differential privacy. For example, \[[20' 2025-04-24](http://arxiv.org/abs/2504.17523v1)\] introduces a verifiable randomized response (VRR) mechanism under local DP, which achieves significant reductions in communication costs and verifier overhead. Similarly, \[[1' 2021-04-14](http://arxiv.org/abs/2104.06569v2)\] describes a verifiable randomization mechanism that allows the data collector to verify the completeness of the randomization process, thereby preventing malicious output manipulations. In addition, \[[2' 2025-02-25](http://arxiv.org/abs/2502.18535v2)\] explores the use of zero-knowledge proofs in verifiable machine learning, highlighting the potential of ZKPs to enable secure and private computation while preserving model confidentiality.

The trade-offs between privacy, utility, and efficiency in differentially private systems have also been extensively studied. For instance, \[[7' 2016-01-24](http://arxiv.org/abs/1601.06426v3)\] discusses the privacy-utility tradeoff under differential privacy and Hamming distortion, providing theoretical guarantees for differentially private community detection. Furthermore, \[[13' 2012-12-05](http://arxiv.org/abs/1212.1186v3)\] derives the optimal $ \varepsilon $-differentially private mechanism for single real-valued query functions, showing that the Laplacian mechanism is asymptotically optimal in the high privacy regime.

In summary, the related works highlight the challenges of ensuring the correct execution of DP mechanisms in distributed settings, the role of verifiable mechanisms in enhancing privacy guarantees, and the trade-offs between privacy, utility, and efficiency in differentially private systems.
