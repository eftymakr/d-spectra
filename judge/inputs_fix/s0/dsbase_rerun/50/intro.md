## Related Works

Recent advancements in machine learning (ML) have led to the proliferation of open-source resources, including models and datasets, which have significantly accelerated research in information retrieval (IR) \[[1' 2021-12-18](http://arxiv.org/abs/2112.10006v6)\]. However, existing platforms such as Hugging Face, while rich in resources, lack structured representations that enable advanced queries and analyses such as tracing model evolution and recommending relevant datasets \[[1' 2021-12-18](http://arxiv.org/abs/2112.10006v6)\]. This limitation has prompted the development of knowledge graph-based approaches to better organize and leverage these resources.

Knowledge graphs (KGs) have been extensively studied for their ability to capture structured relationships and semantic information, which is crucial for tasks such as recommendation and classification \[[2' 2024-09-25](http://arxiv.org/abs/2409.16674v3)\]. For instance, zero-shot and few-shot learning with KGs have been explored to reduce reliance on labeled data by leveraging auxiliary information \[[2' 2024-09-25](http://arxiv.org/abs/2409.16674v3)\]. These methods often employ paradigms such as mapping-based, data augmentation, and propagation-based approaches to enhance model performance \[[2' 2024-09-25](http://arxiv.org/abs/2409.16674v3)\]. Similarly, the integration of KGs into recommendation systems has been investigated to improve the semantic representation of users and items \[[3' 2020-10-28](http://arxiv.org/abs/2011.01103v1)\]. A recent work introduced a prompting-based representation learning method for recommendation using large language models (LLMs), which generates personalized item profiles and aligns them with collaborative filtering representations \[[3' 2020-10-28](http://arxiv.org/abs/2011.01103v1)\].

The construction of knowledge graphs from textual data has also been a focus of research, particularly in the scholarly domain \[[4' 2020-06-29](http://arxiv.org/abs/2006.15772v2)\]. Techniques such as natural language processing (NLP) and machine learning have been employed to extract entities and relationships from research publications, leading to the creation of large-scale scientific knowledge graphs \[[4' 2020-06-29](http://arxiv.org/abs/2006.15772v2)\]. These graphs capture metadata such as authors, venues, and citations, providing a structured representation of scientific knowledge \[[4' 2020-06-29](http://arxiv.org/abs/2006.15772v2)\]. The HuggingKG project builds on these ideas by creating a large-scale KG from the Hugging Face community, capturing domain-specific relations and textual attributes \[[1' 2021-12-18](http://arxiv.org/abs/2112.10006v6)\].

In the context of recommendation systems, multi-stakeholder considerations have gained attention, as many real-world recommenders involve multiple parties with differing needs \[[5' 2022-10-04](http://arxiv.org/abs/2210.01942v1)\]. This has led to the development of frameworks that address the exposure bias problem, where popular items are over-recommended while others are underrepresented \[[5' 2022-10-04](http://arxiv.org/abs/2210.01942v1)\]. Additionally, the challenge of aligning user preferences with recommendation algorithms has been tackled through methods that incorporate social interactions and diffusion patterns \[[6' 2017-07-27](http://arxiv.org/abs/1707.08913v1)\]. For example, the IGNiteR framework uses a diffusion-aware approach to recommend news in microblogging environments, considering factors such as temporal attractiveness and user preferences \[[6' 2017-07-27](http://arxiv.org/abs/1707.08913v1)\].

The use of LLMs in recommendation systems has also been explored, with methods such as P4R leveraging LLMs to generate personalized item profiles and align them with collaborative filtering models \[[3' 2020-10-28](http://arxiv.org/abs/2011.01103v1)\]. Furthermore, the integration of uncertainty quantification in ML models has been emphasized, particularly in healthcare applications, where bias and generalization are critical \[[7' 2017-07-28](http://arxiv.org/abs/1707.09258v1)\]. These efforts highlight the growing interest in combining structured representations, such as KGs, with advanced ML techniques to improve the accuracy and fairness of recommendation systems.
