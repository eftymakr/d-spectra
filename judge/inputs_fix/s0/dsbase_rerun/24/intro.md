## Related Works

Diffusion transformers (DiT) have emerged as a powerful architecture for image and video generation tasks, offering state-of-the-art performance in generating high-quality, coherent content \[[1' 2024-06-04](http://arxiv.org/abs/2406.02540v3)\]. However, their large parameter count and computational complexity hinder deployment on resource-constrained devices \[[1' 2024-06-04](http://arxiv.org/abs/2406.02540v3)\]. Quantization has been proposed as a promising solution to reduce memory usage and improve inference speed by lowering the bit-width of model parameters \[[1' 2024-06-04](http://arxiv.org/abs/2406.02540v3)\]. Existing quantization methods, however, often fail to generalize to video generation tasks due to unique challenges such as temporal consistency and loss of information during quantization \[[1' 2024-06-04](http://arxiv.org/abs/2406.02540v3)\].

Several works have explored quantization for diffusion models. Q-Diffusion \[[4' 2023-02-08](http://arxiv.org/abs/2302.04304v3)\] introduced a novel PTQ method tailored for diffusion models, addressing the changing output distributions of noise estimation networks over multiple time steps and the bimodal activation distribution of shortcut layers. This work demonstrated that full-precision unconditional diffusion models can be quantized into 4-bit while maintaining comparable performance \[[4' 2023-02-08](http://arxiv.org/abs/2302.04304v3)\]. Similarly, ViDiT-Q \[[1' 2024-06-04](http://arxiv.org/abs/2406.02540v3)\] was designed specifically for DiT models, achieving W8A8 and W4A8 quantization with negligible degradation in visual quality and metrics. It also implemented efficient GPU kernels for practical memory saving and latency reduction \[[1' 2024-06-04](http://arxiv.org/abs/2406.02540v3)\].

DVD-Quant \[[2' 2025-05-24](http://arxiv.org/abs/2505.18663v4)\] further advanced the field by proposing a data-free quantization framework for Video DiTs. It introduced BGR and ARQ for calibration data-free quantization error reduction and $δ$-GBS for adaptive bit-width allocation. This approach achieved a 2× speedup over full-precision baselines while maintaining visual fidelity \[[2' 2025-05-24](http://arxiv.org/abs/2505.18663v4)\]. Additionally, the Hardware-Friendly Static Quantization Method \[[3' 2025-02-20](http://arxiv.org/abs/2502.15077v3)\] proposed a static quantization approach for video diffusion transformers, achieving video quality comparable to FP16 and dynamically quantized ViDiT-Q methods. It utilized per-step calibration data and channel-wise quantization for weights and tensor-wise quantization for activations \[[3' 2025-02-20](http://arxiv.org/abs/2502.15077v3)\].

TaQ-DiT \[[5' 2024-11-21](http://arxiv.org/abs/2411.14172v2)\] addressed the issue of varying quantization sensitivities across different layers and introduced a joint reconstruction method to resolve non-convergence during quantization. It also proposed time-variance-aware transformations to improve quantization of Post-GELU activations, which are particularly sensitive to quantization due to their variability across denoising steps \[[5' 2024-11-21](http://arxiv.org/abs/2411.14172v2)\]. These methods collectively highlight the challenges of quantizing DiT models and the need for specialized techniques to maintain performance and temporal consistency.

In summary, while existing quantization methods have made significant strides, they often overlook the unique requirements of video generation tasks, such as temporal consistency and the sensitivity of certain model components to quantization \[[1' 2024-06-04](http://arxiv.org/abs/2406.02540v3)\]. Our work, Q-VDiT, addresses these challenges by introducing Token-aware Quantization Estimator (TQE) and Temporal Maintenance Distillation (TMD), which together ensure both accurate quantization and preservation of spatiotemporal correlations \[[1' 2024-06-04](http://arxiv.org/abs/2406.02540v3)\].
