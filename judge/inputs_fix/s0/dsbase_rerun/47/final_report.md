## Related Works

Multi-Domain Recommendation (MDR) has emerged as a promising approach to improve recommendation performance by leveraging knowledge transfer across different domains \[[1' 2023-02-12](http://arxiv.org/abs/2302.05990v2)\]. However, existing methods often adopt a single structure to transfer complex domain-shared knowledge, which can lead to the Negative Transfer Problem (NTP) when there is knowledge conflict or poor-quality domains \[[2' 2023-09-12](http://arxiv.org/abs/2309.06217v2)\]. This issue has been recognized in various contexts, including cross-domain sequential recommendation \[[3' 2023-11-22](http://arxiv.org/abs/2311.13188v1)\], where heterogeneous knowledge between dissimilar domains can lead to performance degradation \[[4' 2019-05-26](http://arxiv.org/abs/1905.10756v4)\]. 

Several approaches have been proposed to address the NTP. For instance, the work by \[[5' 2024-05-21](http://arxiv.org/abs/2405.12892v2)\] introduces a cooperative game theory framework to assess the extent of negative transfer and adaptively assign low weights to corresponding prediction losses. This method estimates the marginal contribution of each domain to model performance, offering a principled way to handle domain interactions \[[6' 2023-06-09](http://arxiv.org/abs/2306.06302v2)\]. Similarly, \[[7' 2022-01-04](http://arxiv.org/abs/2201.01002v1)\] proposes a hierarchical contrastive learning approach that incorporates information from coarse-level categories to mitigate negative transfer at the fine-level item level \[[8' 2022-01-15](http://arxiv.org/abs/2201.05867v1)\].

In the realm of domain adaptation, \[[9' 2018-12-15](http://arxiv.org/abs/1812.06229v1)\] presents a model that extracts both homogeneous and divergent features across domains, enabling equal support for all domains. This approach is particularly useful when the source domain is not accessible, as is often the case in real-world applications \[[10' 2025-04-29](http://arxiv.org/abs/2504.20371v3)\]. Additionally, \[[11' 2023-11-03](http://arxiv.org/abs/2311.01831v1)\] introduces a domain-to-domain translation model based on GANs, VAEs, and Cycle-Consistency to capture both similarities and differences among domains \[[12' 2023-06-30](http://arxiv.org/abs/2306.17555v2)\]. This model has shown significant improvements over state-of-the-art methods in multi-domain recommendation tasks \[[13' 2015-09-08](http://arxiv.org/abs/1509.02409v1)\].

Another line of research focuses on improving the adaptability of models to different domains. \[[14' 2024-02-09](http://arxiv.org/abs/2402.06213v2)\] proposes a method that leverages both high-level and pixel-level information for partial domain adaptation, using a reinforced transfer network to filter out outlier source samples \[[15' 2023-03-02](http://arxiv.org/abs/2303.01003v1)\]. This approach enhances the robustness of models in the presence of domain shifts \[[16' 2021-02-24](http://arxiv.org/abs/2102.12225v3)\]. Furthermore, \[[17' 2025-02-12](http://arxiv.org/abs/2502.08139v1)\] explores the use of negative control outcomes to select valid instrumental variables, which is relevant to the broader context of transfer learning and domain adaptation \[[18' 2024-04-26](http://arxiv.org/abs/2404.17450v2)\].

In summary, while existing methods have made progress in modeling cross-domain interactions, they often fail to account for the dynamic and complex nature of domain relationships. The proposed Similar Domain Selection Principle (SDSP) aims to address these limitations by dynamically selecting appropriate domains based on both supervised and unsupervised signals \[[19' 2020-06-17](http://arxiv.org/abs/2006.09807v1)\].

The Negative Transfer Problem (NTP) in Multi-Domain Recommendation (MDR) refers to the degradation of model performance caused by the transfer of irrelevant or conflicting knowledge between domains \[[1' 2023-02-12](http://arxiv.org/abs/2302.05990v2)\]. This issue is particularly pronounced when domains differ significantly in user preferences or data quality, leading to performance degradation \[[5' 2024-05-21](http://arxiv.org/abs/2405.12892v2)\]. Several studies have explored methods to mitigate NTP, such as introducing domain-specific modeling or adaptive training regimes \[[1' 2023-02-12](http://arxiv.org/abs/2302.05990v2)\]. For example, one approach involves learning two graph-based sequential representations simultaneously: a domain-guided one for recent user interest and a general one for long-term interest, which helps mitigate negative knowledge transfer \[[1' 2023-02-12](http://arxiv.org/abs/2302.05990v2)\]. Another study proposes a framework that estimates the extent of negative transfer using cooperative game theory and adaptively assigns lower weights to prediction losses from domains contributing negatively \[[3' 2023-11-22](http://arxiv.org/abs/2311.13188v1)\]. Additionally, a hierarchical contrastive learning approach has been developed to incorporate information from coarse-level categories into fine-level ones, thereby reducing the impact of negative transfer \[[3' 2023-11-22](http://arxiv.org/abs/2311.13188v1)\]. In the context of acoustic modeling, data selection techniques based on submodular functions have been proposed to limit negative transfer by focusing on acoustically relevant training data \[[13' 2015-09-08](http://arxiv.org/abs/1509.02409v1)\]. Overall, these approaches highlight the importance of carefully modeling domain relationships and selectively transferring knowledge to avoid performance degradation \[[1' 2023-02-12](http://arxiv.org/abs/2302.05990v2)\]\[[3' 2023-11-22](http://arxiv.org/abs/2311.13188v1)\]\[[5' 2024-05-21](http://arxiv.org/abs/2405.12892v2)\]\[[13' 2015-09-08](http://arxiv.org/abs/1509.02409v1)\].

	Document 1: [Snippet]: «One crucial aspect of partial domain adaptation (PDA) is how to select the relevant source samples in the shared classes for knowledge transfer. Previous PDA methods tackle this problem by re-weighting the source samples based on their high-level information (deep features). However, since the domain shift between source and target domains, only using the deep features for sample selection is defective. We argue that it is more reasonable to additionally exploit the pixel-level information for PDA problem, as the appearance difference between outlier source classes and target classes is significantly large. In this paper, we propose a reinforced transfer network (RTNet), which utilizes both high-level and pixel-level information for PDA problem. Our RTNet is composed of a reinforced data selector (RDS) based on reinforcement learning (RL), which filters out the outlier source samples, and a domain adaptation model which minimizes the domain discrepancy in the shared label space. Specifically, in the RDS, we design a novel reward based on the reconstruct errors of selected source samples on the target generator, which introduces the pixel-level information to guide the learning of RDS. Besides, we develope a state containing high-level information, which used by the RDS for sample selection. The proposed RDS is a general module, which can be easily integrated into existing DA models to make them fit the PDA situation. Extensive experiments indicate that RTNet can achieve state-of-the-art performance for PDA tasks on several benchmark datasets.»
