## Related Works

Economic inequality remains a critical challenge, influencing disparities in education, healthcare, and social stability \[[1' 2025-01-15](http://arxiv.org/abs/2501.08573v1)\]. Traditional tax systems, such as the U.S. federal income tax, aim to reduce inequality but often lack adaptability to complex economic dynamics \[[1' 2025-01-15](http://arxiv.org/abs/2501.08573v1)\]. Models like the Saez Optimal Taxation framework attempt to dynamically adjust tax policies but struggle with addressing taxpayer heterogeneity and irrational behavior \[[1' 2025-01-15](http://arxiv.org/abs/2501.08573v1)\]. This paper introduces TaxAgent, an innovative integration of large language models (LLMs) with agent-based modeling (ABM) to design adaptive tax policies \[[1' 2025-01-15](http://arxiv.org/abs/2501.08573v1)\].

Agent-based models (ABMs) have been widely used to simulate economic systems and evaluate policy interventions \[[2' 2020-07-08](http://arxiv.org/abs/2007.04192v1)\]. These models allow for the exploration of emergent behaviors in multi-agent decision-making settings \[[2' 2020-07-08](http://arxiv.org/abs/2007.04192v1)\]. However, the validity of inferences from ABMs depends on the quality of the agents' behavioral models \[[3' 2020-06-09](http://arxiv.org/abs/2006.05048v3)\]. Standard specifications of agent behavioral models rely on heuristic decision-making rules or regressions trained on past data, which can be limiting \[[3' 2020-06-09](http://arxiv.org/abs/2006.05048v3)\]. Reinforcement learning (RL) models offer an alternative, providing adaptive, high-performing, and behaviorally-valid models for agent decision-making in ABMs \[[3' 2020-06-09](http://arxiv.org/abs/2006.05048v3)\]. This is particularly relevant for TaxAgent, where heterogeneous H-Agents simulate real-world taxpayer behaviors \[[1' 2025-01-15](http://arxiv.org/abs/2501.08573v1)\].

The use of LLMs in policy analysis is a growing area of research \[[3' 2020-06-09](http://arxiv.org/abs/2006.05048v3)\]. LLMs can be integrated into ABMs to optimize tax rates iteratively, balancing equity and productivity \[[1' 2025-01-15](http://arxiv.org/abs/2501.08573v1)\]. This approach aligns with the broader trend of using LLMs for policy analysis, where RL behavioral models have shown effectiveness in producing reward-seeking or reward-maximizing behaviors in ABM agents \[[3' 2020-06-09](http://arxiv.org/abs/2006.05048v3)\]. The TaxAgent framework leverages this capability, enabling the government (TaxAgent) to dynamically adjust tax policies based on real-time economic data \[[1' 2025-01-15](http://arxiv.org/abs/2501.08573v1)\].

Taxation and inequality have also been studied through computational economic analysis \[[6' 2019-01-13](http://arxiv.org/abs/1901.03951v1)\]. This work highlights the role of financial accumulation processes and institutional arrangements, such as taxation, in shaping economic inequality \[[6' 2019-01-13](http://arxiv.org/abs/1901.03951v1)\]. The findings support the importance of well-designed income redistribution policies and rigid control of tax planning possibilities to promote more egalitarian societies \[[1' 2025-01-15](http://arxiv.org/abs/2501.08573v1)\]. These insights are critical for the development of the TaxAgent framework, which aims to enhance equity-efficiency trade-offs in tax policy \[[1' 2025-01-15](http://arxiv.org/abs/2501.08573v1)\].

The effectiveness of taxation on consumer behavior is another area of interest \[[10' 2019-09-23](http://arxiv.org/abs/1910.03141v1)\]. Behavioral economics and neuroeconomics suggest that consumer behavior can deviate from traditional economic theory due to irrationality, limited willpower, and altruistic tendencies \[[10' 2019-09-23](http://arxiv.org/abs/1910.03141v1)\]. This implies that the impact of taxes such as personal income tax or value-added tax can be ambiguous \[[10' 2019-09-23](http://arxiv.org/abs/1910.03141v1)\]. The TaxAgent framework addresses this by incorporating heterogeneous agent behaviors, allowing for more realistic simulations of taxpayer responses to tax policies \[[1' 2025-01-15](http://arxiv.org/abs/2501.08573v1)\].

In summary, the related works highlight the limitations of traditional tax systems, the potential of ABMs for policy analysis, and the importance of behavioral modeling in economic simulations. These insights form the foundation for the TaxAgent framework, which integrates LLMs with ABMs to design adaptive tax policies \[[1' 2025-01-15](http://arxiv.org/abs/2501.08573v1)\].
