## Related Works

Video super-resolution (VSR) has been a focal point in computer vision, with recent advances leveraging deep learning to enhance low-resolution (LR) videos into high-resolution (HR) counterparts. Early methods primarily relied on supervised learning, where training data was generated using predefined degradation models such as bicubic kernels and decimation \[[2' 2022-01-19](http://arxiv.org/abs/2201.07422v1)\]. However, these approaches struggled with real-world scenarios where degradation processes are complex and not well approximated by idealized models \[[2' 2022-01-19](http://arxiv.org/abs/2201.07422v1)\]. This limitation has motivated the development of self-supervised learning strategies, which aim to learn robust representations without explicit ground-truth data \[[11' 2021-03-18](http://arxiv.org/abs/2103.10081v1)\].

Self-supervised learning has been applied to various VSR tasks, including blind video super-resolution, where both blur kernels and HR videos are estimated from LR inputs \[[2' 2022-01-19](http://arxiv.org/abs/2201.07422v1)\]. These methods often generate auxiliary paired data from original LR videos to better constrain the learning process \[[2' 2022-01-19](http://arxiv.org/abs/2201.07422v1)\]. Additionally, optical flow estimation modules have been introduced to exploit temporal information for HR video restoration \[[2' 2022-01-19](http://arxiv.org/abs/2201.07422v1)\]. Similarly, self-supervised adaptation techniques have been proposed to fine-tune pre-trained VSR networks without ground-truth datasets, using self-similar patches across space and time to improve temporal consistency \[[11' 2021-03-18](http://arxiv.org/abs/2103.10081v1)\].

Diffusion models have also gained traction in VSR due to their ability to generate high-quality outputs. However, their inherent randomness can introduce artifacts and degrade temporal consistency \[[1' 2023-05-24](http://arxiv.org/abs/2305.14669v3)\]. To address this, several works have proposed modifications to diffusion models, such as introducing temporal consistency mechanisms and leveraging pre-trained models for better performance \[[10' 2025-01-04](http://arxiv.org/abs/2501.02269v1)\]. For instance, the Upscale-A-Video framework introduces a flow-guided recurrent latent propagation module to enhance video stability across sequences \[[3' 2023-12-11](http://arxiv.org/abs/2312.06640v1)\]. Similarly, the TDM framework proposes an all-in-one approach using pre-trained Stable Diffusion and a fine-tuned ControlNet to restore various types of video degradation \[[10' 2025-01-04](http://arxiv.org/abs/2501.02269v1)\].

Another key challenge in VSR is handling real-world noise and blur, which are often not well simulated by classical degradation models \[[1' 2023-05-24](http://arxiv.org/abs/2305.14669v3)\]. To overcome this, the NegVSR method proposes a sequential noise generation strategy to extract practical noise sequences and uses negative augmentation to build diverse real-world noise sets \[[1' 2023-05-24](http://arxiv.org/abs/2305.14669v3)\]. Additionally, the proposed augmented negative guidance loss helps learn robust features from augmented negatives \[[1' 2023-05-24](http://arxiv.org/abs/2305.14669v3)\]. These approaches highlight the importance of realistic noise modeling and robust feature learning in VSR.

Recent works have also explored self-supervised learning for real-world VSR, particularly in scenarios where reference images are available \[[7' 2024-05-03](http://arxiv.org/abs/2405.02171v1)\]. For example, the SelfDZSR_PlusPlus method leverages dual and multiple zoomed observations to train RefSR models in a self-supervised manner \[[7' 2024-05-03](http://arxiv.org/abs/2405.02171v1)\]. This approach uses telephoto images as references to guide the super-resolution of ultra-wide images and employs patch-based optical flow alignment to mitigate misalignment issues during training \[[7' 2024-05-03](http://arxiv.org/abs/2405.02171v1)\]. These methods demonstrate the potential of self-supervised learning in real-world VSR tasks.

In summary, recent VSR methods have focused on improving robustness to real-world degradations, enhancing temporal consistency, and leveraging self-supervised learning to reduce dependency on labeled data. These advancements have led to significant improvements in perceptual quality and generalization capability across various VSR benchmarks.
