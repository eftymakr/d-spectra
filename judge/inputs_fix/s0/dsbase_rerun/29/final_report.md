## Related Works

Maximum Inner Product Search (MIPS) has been extensively studied in recent years due to its critical role in machine learning and information retrieval, especially in high-dimensional settings \[[1' 2025-03-10](http://arxiv.org/abs/2503.06882v2)\]. Early approaches to MIPS primarily relied on inner product (IP) similarity, which, while intuitive, suffers from issues such as local optima and redundant computations \[[1' 2025-03-10](http://arxiv.org/abs/2503.06882v2)\]. To address these limitations, some methods attempted to reduce MIPS to Nearest Neighbor Search (NNS) under the Euclidean metric by projecting data into a lower-dimensional space \[[1' 2025-03-10](http://arxiv.org/abs/2503.06882v2)\]. However, this approach often leads to topology destruction and information loss, as the Euclidean metric does not inherently align with the inner product structure \[[1' 2025-03-10](http://arxiv.org/abs/2503.06882v2)\].

The dichotomy between IP and Euclidean metrics has been a central theme in the literature. For instance, the paper by \[[2' 2019-09-30](http://arxiv.org/abs/1909.13459v2)\] highlights that the inner product navigable small world graph (ip-NSW) achieves an order of magnitude speedup over the fastest baseline by aligning with the norm bias inherent in MIPS. This suggests that the Euclidean metric, while often used as a proxy, may not be the optimal choice for all scenarios. Similarly, \[[3' 2020-12-21](http://arxiv.org/abs/2012.11098v2)\] introduces the concept of concomitants of extreme order statistics to reduce dimensionality, allowing for sublinear MIPS with exponential indexing space complexity. These works underscore the importance of understanding the intrinsic properties of the data when choosing between IP and Euclidean-based methods.

Recent advancements have sought to bridge the gap between these two paradigms. The paper \[[4' 2014-05-22](http://arxiv.org/abs/1405.5869v1)\] presents a novel hashing scheme for MIPS that leverages asymmetric transformations, enabling sublinear time search without requiring space augmentation. This work is significant as it formally shows that existing LSH frameworks are insufficient for MIPS and introduces an extended framework that allows asymmetric hashing. Another notable contribution is the work by \[[5' 2015-09-04](http://arxiv.org/abs/1509.01469v1)\], which proposes a quantization-based approach that approximates inner products using subspace quantizers, avoiding the need for high-dimensional feature space augmentation.

The paper \[[6' 2022-12-14](http://arxiv.org/abs/2212.07551v3)\] introduces BanditMIPS, a randomized algorithm that achieves sublinear time complexity independent of the dimensionality $d$, making it suitable for high-dimensional settings. This method is particularly effective in scenarios where the number of dimensions is large, as it adaptively samples coordinates based on multi-armed bandit strategies. The paper \[[7' 2015-07-21](http://arxiv.org/abs/1507.05910v3)\] further explores the use of clustering for approximate MIPS, showing that simple k-means-based approaches can outperform state-of-the-art hashing and tree-based methods in terms of speed and robustness to noise.

In addition, \[[8' 2021-04-09](http://arxiv.org/abs/2104.04406v1)\] proposes ProMIPS, which uses a lightweight index and 2-stable random projections to achieve c-approximate MIPS with guaranteed accuracy. This work addresses the high index space and preprocessing costs of LSH-based methods, making it suitable for large-scale datasets. The paper \[[9' 2020-10-04](http://arxiv.org/abs/2010.07027v5)\] introduces a light heterogeneous graph collaborative filtering model that incorporates textual information, demonstrating that inner product-based matching can be enhanced with semantic knowledge.

These works collectively highlight the ongoing efforts to improve MIPS performance by leveraging both IP and Euclidean metrics, as well as hybrid approaches that combine their strengths. The proposed method in this paper builds on these insights by introducing a novel graph-based index called Metric-Amphibious Graph (MAG) and a corresponding search algorithm, Adaptive Navigation with Metric Switch (ANMS), which dynamically switches between IP and Euclidean-based strategies to optimize performance.
