## Related Works

Generative retrieval (GR) has gained significant attention in information retrieval (IR) due to its ability to directly generate document identifiers (docids) from queries, enabling end-to-end optimization of retrieval tasks \[[2' 2023-04-09](http://arxiv.org/abs/2304.04171v1)\]. However, most existing GR models are primarily evaluated on static document collections, with limited exploration of their performance on dynamic corpora where documents are continuously added \[[1' 2023-08-29](http://arxiv.org/abs/2308.14968v2)\]. This section reviews related works that address the challenges of dynamic document retrieval, docid design, and the effectiveness of GR models in evolving environments.

Several studies have focused on the design of docids in GR systems. For instance, GenRet proposes a discrete auto-encoding approach to tokenize documents into short, semantic docids, enabling end-to-end modeling of retrieval tasks \[[2' 2023-04-09](http://arxiv.org/abs/2304.04171v1)\]. This method outperforms fixed rule-based docids, such as document titles or BERT embeddings, which often fail to capture full semantic information \[[2' 2023-04-09](http://arxiv.org/abs/2304.04171v1)\]. Similarly, the CLEVER model introduces Incremental Product Quantization to encode new documents efficiently while maintaining performance on dynamic corpora \[[1' 2023-08-29](http://arxiv.org/abs/2308.14968v2)\]. These approaches highlight the importance of semantic alignment between docids and language models' pre-trained knowledge, a key factor in the effectiveness of GR models on dynamic data \[[5' 2025-04-07](http://arxiv.org/abs/2504.05181v2)\].

The performance of GR models on dynamic corpora has also been investigated. The paper "Exploring the Practicality of Generative Retrieval on Dynamic Corpora" demonstrates that GR models are more adaptable to evolving knowledge and efficient in terms of inference FLOPs, indexing time, and storage footprint compared to Dual Encoders (DE) \[[3' 2023-05-27](http://arxiv.org/abs/2305.18952v5)\]. This aligns with findings from the current work, which shows that GR models with text-based docids outperform numeric-based docids in dynamic settings \[[1' 2023-08-29](http://arxiv.org/abs/2308.14968v2)\]. The underperformance of numeric-based docids is attributed to their tendency to overfit the initial document set, leading to poor generalization on unseen documents \[[1' 2023-08-29](http://arxiv.org/abs/2308.14968v2)\].

Moreover, the paper "Lightweight and Direct Document Relevance Optimization for Generative Information Retrieval" addresses token-level misalignment in GR models and proposes direct document relevance optimization (DDRO), which improves retrieval effectiveness by aligning token-level generation with document-level relevance \[[5' 2025-04-07](http://arxiv.org/abs/2504.05181v2)\]. This method outperforms reinforcement learning-based approaches, demonstrating the potential of simplified optimization pipelines in GR systems \[[5' 2025-04-07](http://arxiv.org/abs/2504.05181v2)\].

Finally, the "Few-Shot GR" framework proposes a few-shot indexing process that eliminates the need for extensive training, leveraging large language models (LLMs) to generate docids for all documents in a corpus \[[6' 2024-08-04](http://arxiv.org/abs/2408.02152v3)\]. This approach offers a promising alternative to training-based indexing, particularly for dynamic corpora where retraining is impractical.

In summary, recent works have explored various aspects of GR, including docid design, dynamic corpus adaptation, and optimization strategies. These studies provide a foundation for the current work, which systematically evaluates GR models on dynamic corpora and proposes a novel multi-docid design that combines the advantages of text-based and numeric-based docids \[[1' 2023-08-29](http://arxiv.org/abs/2308.14968v2)\].
