## Related Works

Generative retrieval has emerged as a promising paradigm that replaces traditional search index data structures with a single large-scale neural network, enabling improved efficiency and seamless integration with generative large language models \[[1' 2025-04-07](http://arxiv.org/abs/2504.05181v2)\]. This approach formulates retrieval as a document identifier (docid) generation task, allowing for end-to-end optimization toward a unified global retrieval objective \[[1' 2025-04-07](http://arxiv.org/abs/2504.05181v2)\]. However, existing methods face challenges such as token-level misalignment, where models trained to predict the next token often fail to capture document-level relevance effectively \[[1' 2025-04-07](http://arxiv.org/abs/2504.05181v2)\]. 

Several approaches have been proposed to address these limitations. Reinforcement learning-based methods, such as reinforcement learning from relevance feedback (RLRF), aim to resolve this misalignment through reward modeling \[[1' 2025-04-07](http://arxiv.org/abs/2504.05181v2)\]. However, these methods introduce significant complexity, requiring the optimization of an auxiliary reward function followed by reinforcement fine-tuning, which is computationally expensive and often unstable \[[1' 2025-04-07](http://arxiv.org/abs/2504.05181v2)\]. To simplify this process, direct document relevance optimization (DDRO) has been introduced, aligning token-level docid generation with document-level relevance estimation through direct optimization via pairwise ranking, eliminating the need for explicit reward modeling and reinforcement learning \[[1' 2025-04-07](http://arxiv.org/abs/2504.05181v2)\]. Experimental results show that DDRO outperforms reinforcement learning-based methods, achieving notable improvements in MRR@10 on benchmark datasets such as MS MARCO and Natural Questions \[[1' 2025-04-07](http://arxiv.org/abs/2504.05181v2)\].

Another approach, Nonparametric Decoding (Np Decoding), addresses the limited information capacity of generative retrieval models by utilizing nonparametric contextualized vocab embeddings as decoder vocab embeddings, allowing the model to leverage both parametric and nonparametric space \[[5' 2022-10-05](http://arxiv.org/abs/2210.02068v3)\]. This method has been shown to significantly improve performance across nine datasets, demonstrating data- and parameter-efficiency, as well as high performance in the zero-shot setting \[[5' 2022-10-05](http://arxiv.org/abs/2210.02068v3)\].

Additionally, the concept of planning ahead in generative retrieval has been explored through PAG, which guides autoregressive generation of document identifiers through simultaneous decoding. PAG constructs a set-based and sequential identifier for each document, leveraging the bag-of-words assumption in information retrieval and quantizing relevance-based representations \[[6' 2024-04-22](http://arxiv.org/abs/2404.14600v1)\]. Experiments on MSMARCO and TREC Deep Learning Track data reveal that PAG outperforms state-of-the-art generative retrieval models by a large margin, achieving significant improvements in MRR while reducing query latency \[[6' 2024-04-22](http://arxiv.org/abs/2404.14600v1)\].

The use of KL divergence minimization for constrained decoding has also been explored in (G)I-DLE, which preserves the intrinsic conditional probability distribution of autoregressive language models while excluding undesirable tokens \[[7' 2025-03-23](http://arxiv.org/abs/2503.18050v1)\]. This method re-normalizes allowed token probabilities to minimize distortion, improving output quality and reducing variance \[[7' 2025-03-23](http://arxiv.org/abs/2503.18050v1)\].

Finally, the generalization capabilities of generative retrieval on out-of-distribution corpora have been studied, with a focus on the inherent limitations of constrained auto-regressive generation from two perspectives: constraints and beam search \[[12' 2016-11-26](http://arxiv.org/abs/1611.08706v1)\]. This work derives a lower bound of the error in terms of the KL divergence between the ground-truth and the model-predicted step-wise marginal distributions and reveals that the usage of marginal distributions may not be an ideal approach for beam search \[[12' 2016-11-26](http://arxiv.org/abs/1611.08706v1)\].
