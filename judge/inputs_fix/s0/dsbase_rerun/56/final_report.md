## Related Works

Cross-domain Sequential Recommendation (CDSR) has gained significant attention in recent years due to its ability to leverage user behavior across multiple domains to improve recommendation performance. Early approaches primarily focused on capturing sequential patterns within a single domain, often neglecting the rich cross-domain interactions that could enhance recommendation accuracy \[[1' 2020-12-01](http://arxiv.org/abs/2012.00485v3)\]. However, the challenge of modeling cross-domain preferences remains a critical issue, especially when user interactions are sparse or non-overlapping across domains \[[2' 2024-06-17](http://arxiv.org/abs/2406.11156v4)\].

A key limitation of existing CDSR methods is the reliance on overlapping users, which restricts their applicability in real-world scenarios where user data is often sparse or entirely non-overlapping \[[3' 2024-12-31](http://arxiv.org/abs/2502.15694v2)\]. This issue, referred to as the "overlap dilemma," limits the ability of models to generalize across domains \[[4' 2023-09-15](http://arxiv.org/abs/2309.08420v7)\]. Additionally, modeling complex transition patterns from mixed behavior sequences remains a significant challenge, as existing methods often fail to capture the nuanced relationships between items and users across domains \[[5' 2025-03-17](http://arxiv.org/abs/2503.13254v1)\].

To address these challenges, several works have explored the integration of advanced models and techniques. For instance, \[[6' 2024-01-10](http://arxiv.org/abs/2401.04971v4)\] proposed a mixed information flow network that considers both behavioral and knowledge transfer to improve cross-domain recommendations. This approach enables the model to dynamically decide when and how to use cross-domain information based on user preferences. Similarly, \[[7' 2025-04-21](http://arxiv.org/abs/2504.15085v5)\] introduced a framework that leverages large language models (LLMs) to enhance sequential recommendation by capturing semantic relationships between items and users. This work demonstrated that LLMs can effectively model complex user preferences and improve recommendation performance, especially in scenarios with limited data.

Another line of research focuses on improving the representation of items and users across domains. For example, \[[8' 2023-11-08](http://arxiv.org/abs/2311.04760v3)\] proposed a method that integrates item image information to better capture visual preferences, while \[[9' 2022-09-21](http://arxiv.org/abs/2209.10163v1)\] introduced a framework that uses frozen CLIP models to generate image and text embeddings, enriching item representations with multimodal data. These approaches highlight the importance of incorporating auxiliary information to enhance the modeling of cross-domain preferences.

Furthermore, \[[10' 2024-10-27](http://arxiv.org/abs/2410.20580v2)\] explored the use of federated learning to address privacy concerns and enable cross-domain knowledge sharing without sharing user data. This work introduced a framework that disentangles domain-specific and domain-shared features, allowing for more effective cross-domain learning while preserving data privacy. Similarly, \[[11' 2023-11-14](http://arxiv.org/abs/2311.08272v1)\] proposed a model that leverages dual dynamic graphs and hybrid metric training to improve the performance of CDSR by capturing both intra- and inter-domain sequential patterns.

In addition to these approaches, \[[12' 2023-11-08](http://arxiv.org/abs/2311.04590v6)\] introduced a contrastive cross-domain infomax objective to enhance the correlation between single- and cross-domain user representations, while \[[13' 2023-04-08](http://arxiv.org/abs/2304.03891v1)\] proposed a framework that models domain and feedback transitions to better capture user preferences across different domains. These works demonstrate the ongoing efforts to address the challenges of CDSR by incorporating advanced modeling techniques and leveraging diverse data sources.

Finally, \[[14' 2024-10-17](http://arxiv.org/abs/2410.13588v1)\] explored the use of automated prompting to address the non-overlapping cross-domain scenario, where no shared entities exist between domains. This work introduced a framework that learns domain-invariant and domain-specific representations to enable effective cross-domain recommendations without relying on shared users or content.

### <a id='category-cross-domain-sequential-recommendation-(cdsr)-overview'></a>Cross-domain Sequential Recommendation (CDSR) Overview: 7 papers found.
Cross-domain Sequential Recommendation (CDSR) aims to predict future user interactions based on historical interactions across multiple domains, addressing challenges such as data sparsity and cold-start problems \[[5' 2025-03-17](http://arxiv.org/abs/2503.13254v1)\]. Existing methods often rely on overlapping users to propagate cross-domain information, but they typically assume a closed-world scenario where user sets and data distributions remain consistent \[[12' 2023-11-08](http://arxiv.org/abs/2311.04590v6)\]. This assumption limits their effectiveness in real-world scenarios where data distribution shifts are common \[[12' 2023-11-08](http://arxiv.org/abs/2311.04590v6)\]. To address these limitations, recent approaches have explored adaptive frameworks that can handle open-world assumptions, such as the Adaptive Multi-Interest Debiasing (AMID) framework, which considers both overlapping and non-overlapping users to better capture user intent \[[12' 2023-11-08](http://arxiv.org/abs/2311.04590v6)\]. Additionally, methods like the Image Fusion for Cross-Domain Sequential Recommendation (IFCDSR) integrate visual data to enhance the modeling of cross-domain preferences by leveraging frozen CLIP models to generate image embeddings \[[3' 2024-12-31](http://arxiv.org/abs/2502.15694v2)\]. Similarly, the Hierarchical Attention Fusion of Visual and Textual Representations (HAF-VT) integrates multimodal data to improve cognitive modeling, mimicking human information integration processes \[[7' 2025-04-21](http://arxiv.org/abs/2504.15085v5)\]. Another approach, C^2DSR, focuses on capturing both intra- and inter-sequence item relationships to jointly learn single- and cross-domain user preferences, using graph neural networks and sequential attentive encoders \[[13' 2023-04-08](http://arxiv.org/abs/2304.03891v1)\]. These methods highlight the ongoing efforts to improve cross-domain preference modeling by incorporating diverse data sources and advanced neural architectures. The challenge remains in effectively transferring knowledge across domains while accounting for the complexities of sequential behavior \[[1' 2020-12-01](http://arxiv.org/abs/2012.00485v3)\].

### <a id='category-llm-integration-in-cdsr'></a>LLM Integration in CDSR: 2 papers found.
The integration of large language models (LLMs) into cross-domain sequential recommendation (CDSR) has been a focal point of recent research, addressing the limitations of conventional models that often fail to capture semantic and contextual information effectively \[[2' 2024-06-17](http://arxiv.org/abs/2406.11156v4)\]. Traditional sequential recommendation models primarily rely on sequential patterns within training data, neglecting broader semantic cues from item descriptions or external sources, which limits their adaptability and predictive power \[[2' 2024-06-17](http://arxiv.org/abs/2406.11156v4)\]. LLMs, with their advanced understanding and generalization capabilities, have shown promise in overcoming these limitations by incorporating richer semantic representations \[[2' 2024-06-17](http://arxiv.org/abs/2406.11156v4)\]. However, early attempts to integrate LLMs into CDSR faced challenges, including insufficient textual information leading to suboptimal performance, incomplete utilization of conventional model insights by LLMs, and the complexity and low interpretability of LLM-based approaches \[[2' 2024-06-17](http://arxiv.org/abs/2406.11156v4)\]. To address these issues, researchers have proposed frameworks such as DELRec, which distills sequential patterns from conventional models to enhance LLMs' ability to comprehend and utilize this knowledge for more effective recommendations \[[2' 2024-06-17](http://arxiv.org/abs/2406.11156v4)\]. Additionally, studies have highlighted the importance of using cross-entropy loss for training LLMs in CDSR tasks, as it provides tighter bounds and better coverage compared to pointwise or pairwise loss functions, although its optimality remains a topic of ongoing investigation \[[20' 2024-08-26](http://arxiv.org/abs/2408.14238v1)\]. These developments underscore the growing interest in leveraging LLMs to improve cross-domain sequential recommendation by bridging semantic gaps and enhancing model interpretability.

This reliance limits the generalizability of CDSR models in real-world scenarios where long-tailed and cold-start users dominate \[[1' 2020-12-01](http://arxiv.org/abs/2012.00485v3)\]. Furthermore, traditional approaches fail to account for the semantic gap between target and auxiliary behaviors, as well as the deviation in user interests across domains, which hinders the performance of multi-behavior SR methods in CDSR settings \[[1' 2020-12-01](http://arxiv.org/abs/2012.00485v3)\]. To address these limitations, recent work has proposed models that incorporate mixed attention mechanisms to better capture domain-specific and cross-domain patterns, as well as dual learning frameworks that iteratively transfer information between domains with minimal overlap \[[2' 2024-06-17](http://arxiv.org/abs/2406.11156v4)\]. These approaches aim to reduce the dependency on overlapping users and improve the robustness of CDSR models in open-world scenarios \[[3' 2024-12-31](http://arxiv.org/abs/2502.15694v2)\].
