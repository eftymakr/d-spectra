## Related Works

Fine-grained emotion classification in app reviews has received increasing attention in recent years due to its critical role in understanding user sentiment and improving app development practices. While traditional sentiment analysis typically focuses on coarse-grained labels such as positive, negative, and neutral \[[16' 2020-06-17](http://arxiv.org/abs/2006.09739v1)\], fine-grained emotion classification aims to distinguish between more nuanced emotional states, such as joy, anger, fear, and surprise \[[6' 2021-08-17](http://arxiv.org/abs/2108.07638v2)\]. This distinction is particularly important in app reviews, where users often express complex emotional experiences that can inform product improvement and user engagement strategies \[[15' 2024-01-19](http://arxiv.org/abs/2401.11063v2)\].

Several studies have explored the use of large language models (LLMs) for sentiment analysis and emotion detection in app reviews. For instance, \[[16' 2020-06-17](http://arxiv.org/abs/2006.09739v1)\] applied machine learning algorithms using TF-IDF text representation and ensemble learning to classify app reviews, achieving an accuracy of 93.37% and an F-score of 0.88. Similarly, \[[12' 2022-10-12](http://arxiv.org/abs/2210.06235v1)\] proposed a framework called SOLAR to automatically summarize helpful app reviews, incorporating review helpfulness prediction, topic-sentiment modeling, and multi-factor ranking. These approaches highlight the potential of automated tools in managing the large volume of user feedback \[[10' 2021-03-26](http://arxiv.org/abs/2103.15774v2)\].

However, the accuracy and reliability of automated emotion classification remain challenging. \[[1' 2024-06-04](http://arxiv.org/abs/2406.02642v4)\] identified that in-context learning (ICL) struggles with fine-grained emotion recognition due to reliance on semantically similar but emotionally inaccurate prototypes and interference from irrelevant categories. To address these issues, they proposed E-ICL, an emotion context learning method that improves accuracy and robustness by using more emotionally accurate prototypes and exclusionary prediction strategies. This aligns with the findings of \[[15' 2024-01-19](http://arxiv.org/abs/2401.11063v2)\], which emphasize the importance of accurate emotion detection for ethical and responsible computing.

In addition, \[[17' 2019-04-11](http://arxiv.org/abs/1904.12607v1)\] studied fake reviews in app stores, highlighting the need for robust methods to detect misleading or incentivized feedback. While not directly related to emotion classification, this work underscores the importance of accurate and reliable sentiment analysis in app review mining. Similarly, \[[13' 2022-10-19](http://arxiv.org/abs/2210.10223v4)\] introduced RoseMatcher, a method to match user reviews with app release notes, demonstrating the value of integrating user feedback into app development processes.

The challenges of fine-grained emotion classification are further compounded by the complexity of emotional interpretation. \[[1' 2024-06-04](http://arxiv.org/abs/2406.02642v4)\] found that LLMs, while effective in reducing manual effort, still struggle with full automation due to the nuanced nature of emotional expression. This aligns with the findings of \[[15' 2024-01-19](http://arxiv.org/abs/2401.11063v2)\], which highlight the need for structured annotation frameworks and human-in-the-loop approaches to ensure the accuracy of emotion detection in app reviews.

In summary, while significant progress has been made in sentiment analysis and emotion detection in app reviews, the field remains challenging due to the complexity of emotional expression and the limitations of automated methods. Future work should focus on improving the accuracy and robustness of emotion classification models, as well as developing more effective tools for managing and analyzing user feedback \[[12' 2022-10-12](http://arxiv.org/abs/2210.06235v1)\]\[[15' 2024-01-19](http://arxiv.org/abs/2401.11063v2)\]\[[16' 2020-06-17](http://arxiv.org/abs/2006.09739v1)\].
