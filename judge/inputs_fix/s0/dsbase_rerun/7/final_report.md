## Related Works

Recent research has explored various methods for identifying and controlling behaviors in large language models (LLMs), particularly focusing on refusal mechanisms and steering techniques. Refusal tokens have been proposed as a way to calibrate refusal behavior in LLMs, allowing models to generate refusal messages for specific categories of queries \[[1' 2024-12-09](http://arxiv.org/abs/2412.06748v2)\]. This approach involves training models with varying proportions of refusal messages to achieve desired refusal rates, though it is computationally expensive \[[1' 2024-12-09](http://arxiv.org/abs/2412.06748v2)\]. In contrast, the proposed COSMIC framework uses cosine similarity to identify steering directions without relying on predefined refusal templates or model outputs \[[1' 2024-12-09](http://arxiv.org/abs/2412.06748v2)\].

The use of cosine similarity in LLMs has also been explored in the context of safety-aligned languages. Research has shown that refusal behavior can be mediated by a single direction in activation space, which can be transferred across languages with near-perfect effectiveness \[[2' 2025-05-22](http://arxiv.org/abs/2505.17306v2)\]. This cross-lingual universality suggests that a single refusal direction may be sufficient for multiple languages, reducing the need for language-specific fine-tuning.

In addition to refusal mechanisms, steering techniques have been extensively studied. Prompt-based steering methods have been found to outperform other methods in terms of steering accuracy and model-to-human alignment \[[4' 2025-05-25](http://arxiv.org/abs/2505.19333v2)\]. These methods leverage human similarity judgments to assess how well steered representations align with human cognition, offering a more grounded evaluation of model behavior \[[4' 2025-05-25](http://arxiv.org/abs/2505.19333v2)\]. However, LLMs have been observed to be biased towards `kind' similarity and struggle with `size' alignment, highlighting the need for more nuanced steering strategies \[[4' 2025-05-25](http://arxiv.org/abs/2505.19333v2)\].

One-shot optimized steering vectors have also been proposed as a way to mediate safety-relevant behaviors in LLMs. These vectors are optimized through gradient descent on a single training example, allowing for the induction of harmful or beneficial behaviors without the need for large contrastive datasets \[[6' 2025-02-26](http://arxiv.org/abs/2502.18862v2)\]. This approach has been successfully applied to refusal suppression, demonstrating high attack success rates and the ability to generalize across inputs \[[6' 2025-02-26](http://arxiv.org/abs/2502.18862v2)\].

Cosine similarity has also been used in knowledge distillation to transfer information from teacher to student models. By using cosine similarity instead of Kullback-Leibler divergence, student models can dynamically learn from the teacher's knowledge without being constrained by a fixed distribution \[[7' 2023-11-24](http://arxiv.org/abs/2311.14307v1)\]. This method has been shown to achieve results on par with or superior to teacher models, offering a promising alternative to existing knowledge distillation techniques \[[7' 2023-11-24](http://arxiv.org/abs/2311.14307v1)\].

The use of cosine similarity in measuring embedding similarity has also been critically examined. While cosine similarity is widely used to quantify semantic similarity, it has been shown to yield arbitrary and sometimes meaningless results, particularly for high-frequency words \[[10' 2022-05-10](http://arxiv.org/abs/2205.05092v1)\]. This underestimation of similarity for frequent words has been traced to differences in their representational geometry compared to low-frequency words \[[10' 2022-05-10](http://arxiv.org/abs/2205.05092v1)\].

Finally, the use of cosine similarity in adversarial detection has been explored. Traditional methods relying on cross-entropy training loss have been found to strengthen adversarial attacks, leading to poor detection accuracy. New training losses have been proposed to reduce unnecessary features and improve detection rates, achieving high true positive rates while maintaining low false positive rates \[[9' 2023-08-07](http://arxiv.org/abs/2308.03243v1)\].
