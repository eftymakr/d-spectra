## Related Works

Ranking models have traditionally focused on capturing the relative order of predictions, often at the expense of the accuracy of absolute values \[[2' 2024-06-12](http://arxiv.org/abs/2406.08010v1)\]. This limitation has significant implications for downstream tasks that require calibrated probability estimates, such as advertising bidding or recommendation systems \[[1' 2019-08-14](http://arxiv.org/abs/1908.05164v3)\]. To address this, existing calibration approaches typically employ predefined transformation functions with order-preserving properties to adjust the original predictions \[[1' 2019-08-14](http://arxiv.org/abs/1908.05164v3)\]. However, these functions often adhere to fixed forms, such as piece-wise linear functions, which exhibit limited expressiveness and flexibility, thereby constraining their effectiveness in complex calibration scenarios \[[1' 2019-08-14](http://arxiv.org/abs/1908.05164v3)\].

Several works have explored the calibration of probability estimates in ranking models. For instance, \[[3' 2021-12-09](http://arxiv.org/abs/2112.07428v2)\] investigates the calibration of personalized ranking models by proposing Gaussian and Gamma calibration methods, which map ranking scores to well-calibrated preference probabilities without affecting recommendation performance. Similarly, \[[4' 2024-11-05](http://arxiv.org/abs/2411.02988v2)\] addresses the calibration of classifiers with many classes by transforming the problem into calibrating a single surrogate binary classifier, which allows for more efficient use of standard calibration methods. These approaches, while effective in specific contexts, are limited by their reliance on fixed functional forms, which may not capture the complex relationships between predictions and true probabilities.

In the context of neural networks, \[[5' 2025-01-31](http://arxiv.org/abs/2501.19047v5)\] introduces the concept of monotonic neural networks, which enforce constraints on weights and activation functions to ensure invertibility. These architectures have been used in autoregressive flows to model continuous probability distributions \[[5' 2025-01-31](http://arxiv.org/abs/2501.19047v5)\]. However, such constraints can limit the expressiveness of the resulting transformations. In contrast, \[[6' 2014-08-12](http://arxiv.org/abs/1408.2764v2)\] proposes the Unconstrained Monotonic Neural Network (UMNN) architecture, which enforces monotonicity through the positiveness of the network's output, allowing for greater flexibility and modeling power. This approach is particularly relevant for calibration tasks, as it enables the learning of arbitrary monotonic functions without distorting the original predictions.

To further improve the calibration of neural networks, \[[7' 2019-03-08](http://arxiv.org/abs/1903.03238v8)\] introduces the Smooth Calibration Loss (SCLoss), a novel loss function designed to fulfill a necessary condition for achieving ideal calibration. This loss function is specifically tailored to the calibration task and has been shown to enhance the performance of calibration models. Additionally, \[[8' 2021-08-19](http://arxiv.org/abs/2108.08735v2)\] explores the use of graph neural networks (GNNs) in recommendation systems, proposing a sign-aware approach that incorporates both positive and negative user-item interactions. This work highlights the importance of incorporating diverse signal types in calibration tasks.

Finally, \[[9' 2016-09-15](http://arxiv.org/abs/1609.04846v1)\] discusses the use of contrastive loss in representation learning, particularly for pairwise sentence scoring tasks. The paper explores variations of the batch-softmax contrastive loss and demonstrates its effectiveness in improving the quality of sentence embeddings. These methods, while not directly addressing calibration, provide insights into the design of flexible and expressive calibration models.
