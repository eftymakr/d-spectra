## Related Works

Speech-driven 3D facial animation has been a focal point in computer vision and machine learning research, with numerous approaches aiming to synthesize realistic facial movements from audio input. Early methods often relied on handcrafted features and rule-based systems, which were limited in their ability to capture the complexity of human facial expressions \[[10' 2018-05-23](http://arxiv.org/abs/1805.09313v4)\]. More recent approaches have adopted deep learning techniques, particularly leveraging self-supervised audio models as encoders to extract meaningful features from speech signals \[[10' 2018-05-23](http://arxiv.org/abs/1805.09313v4)\]. These models have shown promise in generating lip movements that are synchronized with audio, but they often suffer from limitations in capturing the nuanced variations in facial expressions \[[10' 2018-05-23](http://arxiv.org/abs/1805.09313v4)\].

One significant challenge in this domain is the issue of phonetically similar syllables, which can lead to an averaging effect in self-supervised audio feature spaces. This phenomenon results in less expressive lip movements and reduced naturalness in facial animations \[[1' 2024-08-03](http://arxiv.org/abs/2408.01826v5)\]. To address this, several studies have explored the use of semantic information to enhance the discriminative power of audio encodings \[[1' 2024-08-03](http://arxiv.org/abs/2408.01826v5)\]. For instance, some works have integrated textual or emotional cues to better disentangle the relationship between speech content and facial expressions \[[1' 2024-08-03](http://arxiv.org/abs/2408.01826v5)\]. These methods aim to improve the precision of lip synchronization and the overall realism of generated animations \[[1' 2024-08-03](http://arxiv.org/abs/2408.01826v5)\].

The use of self-supervised learning has also been a key trend in recent research. Models like FaceXHuBERT have demonstrated the potential of self-supervised speech representation learning in generating expressive 3D facial animations without the need for extensive labeled data \[[3' 2023-03-09](http://arxiv.org/abs/2303.05416v1)\]. Similarly, AudioLDM 2 has introduced a framework that leverages self-supervised pretraining to enable the generation of diverse audio content, including speech, music, and sound effects \[[4' 2023-08-10](http://arxiv.org/abs/2308.05734v3)\]. These approaches highlight the importance of learning robust and generalizable representations from raw audio signals \[[4' 2023-08-10](http://arxiv.org/abs/2308.05734v3)\].

Another line of research has focused on improving the controllability and expressiveness of facial animations. For example, PMMTalk integrates complementary pseudo multi-modal features to enhance the accuracy of facial animation by incorporating visual and textual information \[[12' 2023-12-05](http://arxiv.org/abs/2312.02781v1)\]. Similarly, the work on AdaMesh proposes a method for personalized facial expressions and head poses by learning from a reference video \[[14' 2023-10-11](http://arxiv.org/abs/2310.07236v4)\]. These methods demonstrate the growing interest in incorporating identity-specific styles and dynamic facial expressions into speech-driven animation \[[14' 2023-10-11](http://arxiv.org/abs/2310.07236v4)\].

In addition to these efforts, the use of diffusion models has gained traction in generating high-quality facial animations. For example, SAiD introduces a diffusion model that enhances lip synchronization and enables diverse lip movements \[[18' 2023-12-25](http://arxiv.org/abs/2401.08655v2)\]. Similarly, EmoDiffusion disentangles emotional expressions from speech content to generate rich 3D emotional facial animations \[[25' 2025-03-14](http://arxiv.org/abs/2503.11028v1)\]. These approaches highlight the potential of diffusion-based models in capturing the intricate details of facial expressions and movements \[[25' 2025-03-14](http://arxiv.org/abs/2503.11028v1)\].

Overall, the field of speech-driven 3D facial animation continues to evolve, with a growing emphasis on semantic understanding, controllability, and the use of self-supervised learning to overcome data scarcity \[[1' 2024-08-03](http://arxiv.org/abs/2408.01826v5)\]. The proposed Wav2Sem module builds on these advancements by introducing a plug-and-play semantic decorrelation module to address the averaging effect of phonetically similar syllables \[[1' 2024-08-03](http://arxiv.org/abs/2408.01826v5)\].
