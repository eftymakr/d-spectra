## Related Works

Federated learning (FL) has gained significant attention for its ability to enable collaborative model training across distributed clients while preserving user privacy \[[27' 2020-07-28](http://arxiv.org/abs/2007.14390v5)\]. This paradigm is particularly relevant in scenarios where data is stored locally on edge devices, and only model parameters are shared \[[27' 2020-07-28](http://arxiv.org/abs/2007.14390v5)\]. However, the emergence of privacy regulations such as the General Data Protection Regulation (GDPR) has introduced new challenges, necessitating the development of federated unlearning (FU) techniques to allow users to remove their data's influence from the global model \[[1' 2024-01-19](http://arxiv.org/abs/2401.11018v1)\]. 

Federated unlearning has been explored in various contexts, including the removal of specific clients or data points from the global model \[[1' 2024-01-19](http://arxiv.org/abs/2401.11018v1)\]. A key challenge in this area is ensuring that the unlearned model is statistically indistinguishable from one trained without the deleted data \[[1' 2024-01-19](http://arxiv.org/abs/2401.11018v1)\]. This requirement is often referred to as "exact" federated unlearning \[[1' 2024-01-19](http://arxiv.org/abs/2401.11018v1)\]. To achieve this, researchers have introduced concepts such as total variation (TV) stability, which measures the sensitivity of the model parameters to slight changes in the dataset \[[1' 2024-01-19](http://arxiv.org/abs/2401.11018v1)\]. Based on this insight, the FATS algorithm was developed, which modifies the classical FedAvg algorithm to enhance TV stability and employs local SGD with periodic averaging to reduce communication rounds \[[1' 2024-01-19](http://arxiv.org/abs/2401.11018v1)\]. 

In addition to client-level unlearning, sample-level unlearning has also been investigated, allowing for the removal of specific data points from the model \[[1' 2024-01-19](http://arxiv.org/abs/2401.11018v1)\]. The effectiveness of these methods has been evaluated on benchmark datasets, demonstrating their superiority over state-of-the-art approaches in terms of accuracy, communication cost, and unlearning efficacy \[[1' 2024-01-19](http://arxiv.org/abs/2401.11018v1)\]. Furthermore, the concept of federated unlearning has been extended to other domains, such as federated domain unlearning, where the goal is to remove data from specific domains without affecting the performance of the model in other domains \[[7' 2024-06-05](http://arxiv.org/abs/2406.03078v1)\]. 

Recent work has also focused on the evaluation of unlearning methods, highlighting the need for comprehensive metrics to assess the success of unlearning \[[17' 2024-05-28](http://arxiv.org/abs/2405.17878v3)\]. The Information Difference Index (IDI) has been proposed as a novel metric to quantify the retained information in intermediate features, offering a more accurate assessment of unlearning efficacy \[[17' 2024-05-28](http://arxiv.org/abs/2405.17878v3)\]. Additionally, the problem of unlearning in the context of online learning to rank (OLTR) has been addressed, with the introduction of federated online learning to rank (FOLTR) as a framework to enhance privacy while maintaining search result quality \[[10' 2024-12-26](http://arxiv.org/abs/2412.19069v1)\]. 

The challenges of federated unlearning are further compounded by security concerns, such as poisoning attacks, where malicious clients can compromise the global model by sending harmful updates \[[26' 2025-01-29](http://arxiv.org/abs/2501.17396v1)\]. To mitigate these threats, robust frameworks such as UnlearnGuard have been proposed, which employ filtering strategies to verify the accuracy of local model updates during the unlearning process \[[26' 2025-01-29](http://arxiv.org/abs/2501.17396v1)\]. 

In summary, federated unlearning has evolved as a critical area of research, addressing the growing need for privacy-preserving machine learning in distributed settings. The development of efficient and provable unlearning methods remains a key focus, with ongoing efforts to balance data privacy, model performance, and system efficiency \[[1' 2024-01-19](http://arxiv.org/abs/2401.11018v1)\]\[[10' 2024-12-26](http://arxiv.org/abs/2412.19069v1)\]\[[17' 2024-05-28](http://arxiv.org/abs/2405.17878v3)\].
