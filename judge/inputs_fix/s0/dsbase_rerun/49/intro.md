## Related Works

Neural retrieval models have shown significant promise in Web search tasks, particularly due to their ability to learn complex patterns from large-scale data \[[5' 2021-02-23](http://arxiv.org/abs/2102.11903v2)\]. However, training these models typically requires substantial amounts of labeled query-document pairs, which are both expensive and time-consuming to obtain \[[1' 2024-04-15](http://arxiv.org/abs/2404.09473v1)\]. This challenge has led researchers to explore alternative methods for generating synthetic queries, especially leveraging large language models (LLMs) to create scalable and cost-effective training data \[[11' 2024-06-10](http://arxiv.org/abs/2406.06729v1)\]. While synthetic queries offer a promising solution, they often suffer from variability in quality, which can negatively impact downstream retrieval performance \[[1' 2024-04-15](http://arxiv.org/abs/2404.09473v1)\].

Existing approaches typically rely on external re-rankers to filter out noisy or irrelevant query-document pairs during training \[[1' 2024-04-15](http://arxiv.org/abs/2404.09473v1)\]. These methods often use signal-based filtering to remove low-quality pairs, but they may not fully capture the nuances of user intent or the contextual relevance of queries \[[1' 2024-04-15](http://arxiv.org/abs/2404.09473v1)\]. In contrast, our proposed framework integrates ranking signals directly into the query generation process using Direct Preference Optimization (DPO) \[[12' 2024-05-30](http://arxiv.org/abs/2405.19749v2)\]. This approach aims to optimize the generation of high-quality synthetic queries that maximize downstream retrieval effectiveness by aligning with human preferences \[[12' 2024-05-30](http://arxiv.org/abs/2405.19749v2)\].

DPO has been successfully applied in various domains, including RAG systems, where it improves the accuracy of retrievers by aligning them with human preferences \[[6' 2024-03-22](http://arxiv.org/abs/2404.07220v2)\]. By incorporating preference signals during query generation, our method ensures that the generated queries are more aligned with the actual needs of users, thereby improving the relevance of retrieved documents \[[6' 2024-03-22](http://arxiv.org/abs/2404.07220v2)\]. This is particularly important in scenarios where query logs are not available or are limited in scope \[[1' 2024-04-15](http://arxiv.org/abs/2404.09473v1)\].

Several studies have explored the use of LLMs to generate synthetic queries for specific applications, such as improving the performance of virtual assistants and enhancing the accuracy of legal document retrieval \[[11' 2024-06-10](http://arxiv.org/abs/2406.06729v1)\]\[[4' 2024-12-01](http://arxiv.org/abs/2412.00657v1)\]. These approaches have demonstrated that LLMs can generate queries that are both specific and representative of real-world usage patterns \[[11' 2024-06-10](http://arxiv.org/abs/2406.06729v1)\]. However, the quality of these synthetic queries can vary, and there is a need for more robust methods to ensure consistency and relevance \[[11' 2024-06-10](http://arxiv.org/abs/2406.06729v1)\].

In addition to query generation, the use of preference-based alignment techniques has gained traction in other areas, such as counter-speech generation and generative query recommendation \[[7' 2024-12-19](http://arxiv.org/abs/2412.15453v1)\]\[[12' 2024-05-30](http://arxiv.org/abs/2405.19749v2)\]. These methods leverage human preferences to guide the training of models, resulting in more accurate and contextually appropriate outputs \[[7' 2024-12-19](http://arxiv.org/abs/2412.15453v1)\]. The integration of preference signals into the query generation process, as proposed in this work, aligns with these broader trends in preference-based learning \[[12' 2024-05-30](http://arxiv.org/abs/2405.19749v2)\].

The effectiveness of DPO in improving retrieval performance has been validated through experiments on benchmark datasets such as MS MARCO \[[12' 2024-05-30](http://arxiv.org/abs/2405.19749v2)\]. These results demonstrate that integrating ranking signals during query generation leads to higher relevance scores and better downstream performance compared to traditional methods \[[12' 2024-05-30](http://arxiv.org/abs/2405.19749v2)\].

With the widespread availability of Web document collections like ClueWeb22, synthetic queries generated by large language models offer a scalable alternative. Still, synthetic training queries often vary in quality, which leads to suboptimal downstream retrieval performance. Existing methods typically filter out noisy query-document pairs based on signals from an external re-ranker. In contrast, we propose a framework that leverages Direct Preference Optimization (DPO) to integrate ranking signals into the query generation process, aiming to directly optimize the model towards generating high-quality queries that maximize downstream retrieval effectiveness. Experiments show higher ranker-assessed relevance between query-document pairs after DPO, leading to stronger downstream performance on the MS~MARCO benchmark when compared to baseline models trained with synthetic data.
