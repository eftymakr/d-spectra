## Related Works

Automatic calibration of radar and camera systems is a critical task in autonomous driving, as it ensures accurate sensor fusion and reliable perception. Prior work has explored various approaches to address the challenges of calibration, particularly in the context of target-based and targetless methods. Target-based methods, such as those proposed in \[[1' 2023-07-28](http://arxiv.org/abs/2307.15264v1)\], require specific calibration targets and controlled environments, which can be cumbersome and impractical for real-world deployment. In contrast, targetless methods aim to simplify the calibration process by leveraging inherent sensor properties or environmental cues. For instance, \[[5' 2023-09-02](http://arxiv.org/abs/2309.00787v2)\] introduced an online targetless calibration method that uses common features extracted from radar and camera data, enabling real-time calibration without the need for dedicated targets. This approach aligns with the goals of our method, which also eliminates the need for external calibration targets.

Several studies have focused on improving the robustness and accuracy of extrinsic calibration. \[[7' 2021-03-12](http://arxiv.org/abs/2103.07505v2)\] proposed a continuous-time 3D radar-to-camera extrinsic calibration algorithm that utilizes radar velocity measurements and does not require specialized radar retroreflectors. This method is particularly relevant for low-cost 3D radars, which are increasingly used in autonomous systems. Similarly, \[[9' 2019-04-18](http://arxiv.org/abs/1904.08743v2)\] introduced a data-driven method for automatic rotational radar-camera calibration without dedicated calibration targets, using a coarse and fine convolutional neural network to achieve precise sensor registration. These approaches demonstrate the feasibility of targetless calibration but often rely on specific assumptions about sensor behavior or environmental conditions.

In addition to extrinsic calibration, recent research has explored the integration of radar and camera data for object detection and semantic segmentation. \[[3' 2023-09-07](http://arxiv.org/abs/2309.03734v2)\] proposed ClusterFusion, an architecture that leverages radar spatial features for 3D object detection, demonstrating the complementary nature of radar and camera data. \[[6' 2023-05-25](http://arxiv.org/abs/2305.15883v2)\] introduced RC-BEVFusion, a modular radar-camera fusion network on the bird's eye view (BEV) plane, achieving significant performance gains in object detection. These methods highlight the potential of radar-camera fusion in autonomous driving, although they often rely on specific fusion strategies or data representations.

The challenge of sparsity and uncertainty in radar data has also been addressed in prior work. \[[5' 2023-09-02](http://arxiv.org/abs/2309.00787v2)\] proposed a method that uses deep learning to extract common features from radar and camera data, enabling robust feature matching and calibration. This approach is similar to our method, which also leverages feature-based matching to achieve automatic calibration. Additionally, \[[11' 2025-04-12](http://arxiv.org/abs/2504.09086v1)\] introduced RICCARDO, a method that explicitly models radar hit distributions to improve fusion accuracy, demonstrating the importance of understanding radar data characteristics for effective fusion.

In summary, the field of radar-camera calibration has seen significant advancements, with a growing focus on targetless methods and robust feature-based approaches. Our method builds on these contributions by introducing a novel Dual-Perspective representation and Selective Fusion Mechanism to address the challenges of height uncertainty and data sparsity. These innovations enable a more accurate and efficient calibration process, as demonstrated in our experimental results on the nuScenes dataset.
