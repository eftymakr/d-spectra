## Related Works

Collaborative filtering (CF) has been a cornerstone in recommendation systems, with recent advancements leveraging graph neural networks (GNNs) to model user-item interactions more effectively \[[1' 2024-01-23](http://arxiv.org/abs/2401.12590v2)\]. Traditional CF methods, such as matrix factorization, often fail to capture high-order connectivity and suffer from limited expressiveness \[[7' 2019-05-20](http://arxiv.org/abs/1905.08108v2)\]. This has led to the integration of graph-based models, which exploit the structure of user-item bipartite graphs to propagate information and enhance recommendation accuracy \[[11' 2018-04-24](http://arxiv.org/abs/1804.08891v1)\].

Graph convolutional networks (GCNs) have emerged as a powerful tool in this context, enabling the aggregation of neighborhood information through spectral filters \[[12' 2019-01-05](http://arxiv.org/abs/1901.01343v7)\]. However, existing GCN-based methods often suffer from issues such as over-smoothing and limited frequency response \[[17' 2020-04-30](http://arxiv.org/abs/2004.14734v1)\]. For instance, many GCNs are essentially low-pass filters, ignoring the middle and high-frequency components of graph signals \[[4' 2021-07-10](http://arxiv.org/abs/2107.04755v3)\]. This limitation hinders their ability to capture diverse and fine-grained preference patterns \[[1' 2024-01-23](http://arxiv.org/abs/2401.12590v2)\].

To address these limitations, several works have proposed alternative filtering mechanisms. For example, AutoGCN introduces an automatic bandwidth adjustment for graph convolutional filters, allowing for more flexible and accurate signal processing \[[4' 2021-07-10](http://arxiv.org/abs/2107.04755v3)\]. Similarly, the Graph Trend Filtering Networks (GTN) focus on capturing the adaptive reliability of interactions by introducing a principled graph trend collaborative filtering method \[[2' 2021-08-12](http://arxiv.org/abs/2108.05552v2)\]. These approaches highlight the importance of considering the reliability and dynamics of user-item interactions in recommendation systems.

Another line of research explores the use of polynomial and Chebyshev filters for graph signal processing. The PolyCF framework leverages polynomial graph filters to process interaction signals, capturing spectral features across multiple eigenspaces \[[1' 2024-01-23](http://arxiv.org/abs/2401.12590v2)\]. Additionally, the work on Chebyshev interpolation and its applications in graph filtering demonstrates the potential of using non-linear filters to approximate complex patterns in graph signals \[[20' 2020-08-01](http://arxiv.org/abs/2008.00240v1)\]. These methods provide a theoretical foundation for the development of more expressive and robust graph-based recommendation models.

Furthermore, the integration of contrastive learning with graph convolution has been explored to enhance the modeling of high-order connectivity \[[5' 2024-06-20](http://arxiv.org/abs/2406.13996v2)\]. The Simple Contrastive Collaborative Filtering (SCCF) model demonstrates that graph convolutional layers may not be essential for high-order connectivity modeling and could contribute to over-smoothing \[[5' 2024-06-20](http://arxiv.org/abs/2406.13996v2)\]. This highlights the need for careful design of graph-based models to avoid such pitfalls.

In summary, the related works emphasize the importance of addressing the limitations of existing graph-based recommendation models, particularly in terms of frequency response, over-smoothing, and the ability to capture diverse preference patterns. These insights form the foundation for the proposed ChebyCF framework, which leverages spectral graph theory and Chebyshev interpolation to overcome these challenges.

Graph convolutional networks (GCNs) have been extensively studied for learning from graph-structured data, with a focus on improving their ability to capture diverse frequency components of graph signals. Existing GCNs are often limited as low-pass filters, neglecting middle and high-frequency information \[[1' 2024-01-23](http://arxiv.org/abs/2401.12590v2)\]. Additionally, their fixed bandwidth restricts their adaptability to different signal characteristics without expert intervention \[[1' 2024-01-23](http://arxiv.org/abs/2401.12590v2)\].
