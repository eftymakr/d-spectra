## Related Works

Instruction-following text embedding has emerged as a critical area of research, aiming to generate dynamic text embeddings that adapt to user instructions by highlighting specific attributes of the text. This task is particularly challenging due to the computational overhead associated with re-encoding the entire corpus for each new instruction \[[1' 2024-02-15](http://arxiv.org/abs/2402.09642v1)\]. Several approaches have been proposed to address this issue, including InBedder \[[1' 2024-02-15](http://arxiv.org/abs/2402.09642v1)\], which treats instructions as questions about the input text and encodes the expected answers to obtain the representation. This method improves instruction-following capabilities by fine-tuning language models on abstractive question answering tasks \[[1' 2024-02-15](http://arxiv.org/abs/2402.09642v1)\]. Similarly, InF-Embed \[[2' 2025-05-27](http://arxiv.org/abs/2505.21439v1)\] introduces a large-scale, high-quality training corpus tailored for enhancing retrieval models in instruction-following information retrieval. This corpus expands traditional training pairs into expressive <instruction, query, passage> triplets, enabling efficient representation learning for smaller encoder-only models \[[2' 2025-05-27](http://arxiv.org/abs/2505.21439v1)\].

Another line of research focuses on the effectiveness of instruction tuning, with studies showing that small, high-quality datasets can suffice for general-purpose instruction following \[[4' 2023-11-22](http://arxiv.org/abs/2311.13133v1)\]. For instance, LIMIT \[[4' 2023-11-22](http://arxiv.org/abs/2311.13133v1)\] demonstrates that subsets of 1k-6k instruction fine-tuning samples are sufficient to achieve good performance on both traditional NLP benchmarks and model-based evaluation. Additionally, non-instructional fine-tuning has been explored as a viable alternative to instruction-following data, where pre-trained LLMs can gain instruction-following capabilities by being fine-tuned on non-instructional data \[[5' 2024-08-27](http://arxiv.org/abs/2409.00096v1)\]. This approach has shown promising results, with models like LLaMA-3-70B-Instruct achieving performance comparable to state-of-the-art instruction-following models \[[5' 2024-08-27](http://arxiv.org/abs/2409.00096v1)\].

Instruction-following is also closely related to the broader field of task execution and reinforcement learning. For example, Human Instruction-Following with Deep Reinforcement Learning via Transfer-Learning from Text \[[10' 2020-05-19](http://arxiv.org/abs/2005.09382v1)\] proposes a method for training instruction-following agents using deep RL, demonstrating substantially-above-chance zero-shot transfer from synthetic template commands to natural instructions \[[10' 2020-05-19](http://arxiv.org/abs/2005.09382v1)\]. Similarly, Learning to Follow Instructions in Text-Based Games \[[16' 2022-11-08](http://arxiv.org/abs/2211.04591v1)\] investigates the ability of RL agents to follow instructions in text-based environments, showing that equipping agents with an internal structured representation of instructions in the form of Linear Temporal Logic (LTL) improves their performance \[[16' 2022-11-08](http://arxiv.org/abs/2211.04591v1)\].

Moreover, recent work has explored the internal mechanisms of LLMs in relation to instruction-following, such as the study on whether LLMs "know" internally when they follow instructions \[[9' 2024-10-18](http://arxiv.org/abs/2410.14516v5)\]. This research identifies a direction in the input embedding space, termed the instruction-following dimension, that correlates with instruction-following success \[[9' 2024-10-18](http://arxiv.org/abs/2410.14516v5)\]. These findings provide insights into how LLMs process and respond to instructions, paving the way for more reliable instruction-following agents \[[9' 2024-10-18](http://arxiv.org/abs/2410.14516v5)\].

In summary, the field of instruction-following text embedding has seen significant advancements, with various approaches addressing the computational challenges and improving the performance of LLMs in adhering to user instructions.
