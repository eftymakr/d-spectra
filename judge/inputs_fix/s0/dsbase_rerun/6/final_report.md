## Related Works

Narrative summarization, particularly for long-form texts such as books, movies, and TV scripts, remains a challenging task for large language models (LLMs) due to the complexity of capturing intricate plotlines, character interactions, and thematic coherence \[[15' 2010-04-19](http://arxiv.org/abs/1004.3258v1)\]. Several approaches have been proposed to improve the summarization of such texts, often focusing on either preprocessing or post-processing techniques. For instance, plan-guided summarization has been explored to reduce hallucinations in small language models (SLMs) by grounding summaries to the source text, typically by targeting fine-grained details such as dates or named entities \[[4' 2025-04-12](http://arxiv.org/abs/2504.09071v2)\]. However, the results of this work show that neither plan-guided approaches nor higher-level narrative-based planning significantly improve summary quality or faithfulness \[[4' 2025-04-12](http://arxiv.org/abs/2504.09071v2)\]. Similarly, in the context of visual question answering (VQA), multi-agent systems have been proposed to overcome the limitations of foundation models in object detection and counting by using specialized agents as tools \[[3' 2024-03-21](http://arxiv.org/abs/2403.14783v1)\]. These systems operate without fine-tuning on specific datasets, making them more practical and robust in the open world.

In the realm of multi-agent reinforcement learning, the impact of environment and population diversity on agent performance has been investigated. Procedurally generating training levels significantly improves agent performance on held-out levels, but sometimes leads to a decline in performance on specific training levels \[[6' 2021-02-16](http://arxiv.org/abs/2102.08370v2)\]. Furthermore, the paper discusses how diversity in co-players can enhance agent performance in some cases, suggesting that population size and intrinsic motivation are effective methods of generating greater diversity \[[6' 2021-02-16](http://arxiv.org/abs/2102.08370v2)\]. These findings highlight the importance of diversity in multi-agent systems for improving generalization and robustness.

The paper also introduces a novel framework for generating causal graphs from narrative texts, which bridges high-level causality and detailed event-specific relationships \[[2' 2025-04-10](http://arxiv.org/abs/2504.07459v2)\]. This approach uses an "Expert Index" comprising seven linguistically informed features, integrated into a Situation-Task-Action-Consequence (STAC) classification model. The hybrid system, combining RoBERTa embeddings with the Expert Index, achieves superior precision in causal link identification compared to pure LLM-based approaches \[[2' 2025-04-10](http://arxiv.org/abs/2504.07459v2)\]. Additionally, a structured five-iteration prompting process is used to refine and construct connected causal graphs, demonstrating the potential of such methods for capturing nuanced causal chains in narratives.

Finally, the paper presents a multi-agent framework for narrative summarization, named NexusSum, which introduces two key innovations: (1) Dialogue-to-Description Transformation, a narrative-specific preprocessing method that standardizes character dialogue and descriptive text into a unified format, and (2) Hierarchical Multi-LLM Summarization, a structured summarization pipeline that optimizes chunk processing and controls output length for accurate, high-quality summaries \[[1' 2024-12-28](http://arxiv.org/abs/2412.20138v7)\]. These innovations contribute to the state-of-the-art in narrative summarization, achieving up to a 30.0% improvement in BERTScore (F1) across books, movies, and TV scripts \[[1' 2024-12-28](http://arxiv.org/abs/2412.20138v7)\].
