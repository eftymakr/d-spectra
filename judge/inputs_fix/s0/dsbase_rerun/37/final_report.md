## Related Works

Vision Transformers (ViTs) have emerged as a powerful alternative to convolutional neural networks (CNNs) in computer vision tasks due to their ability to capture global context through self-attention mechanisms \[[9' 2021-06-04](http://arxiv.org/abs/2106.02277v1)\]. However, the computational and memory demands of ViTs, particularly due to complex operations like Softmax and LayerNorm, have posed significant challenges for hardware acceleration \[[1' 2024-05-07](http://arxiv.org/abs/2405.04134v1)\]. These operations are sensitive to precision and are often executed on the CPU, leading to communication overhead and suboptimal performance \[[1' 2024-05-07](http://arxiv.org/abs/2405.04134v1)\]. 

Several works have focused on improving the efficiency of ViTs through algorithm-hardware co-design. ViTALiTy \[[2' 2022-11-09](http://arxiv.org/abs/2211.05109v1)\] introduces a framework that unifies low-rank and sparse approximations for attention mechanisms, aiming to reduce the quadratic cost of attention matrices. This approach leverages a linear Taylor attention with row-mean centering to approximate the dot-product softmax operation, improving both efficiency and accuracy. Similarly, HeatViT \[[12' 2022-11-15](http://arxiv.org/abs/2211.08110v2)\] proposes an adaptive token pruning framework that dynamically identifies and removes non-informative tokens, reducing computational load while maintaining accuracy. This method is implemented on FPGAs, demonstrating significant speedups and accuracy improvements over existing pruning techniques.

SoftmAP \[[3' 2024-11-26](http://arxiv.org/abs/2411.17847v1)\] presents a software-hardware co-design approach for implementing integer-only Softmax using In-Memory Compute (IMC) hardware, achieving substantial improvements in energy-delay product compared to high-end GPUs. This aligns with the goal of reducing the computational overhead of non-linear operations in ViTs. Softermax \[[7' 2021-03-16](http://arxiv.org/abs/2103.09301v1)\] further enhances the efficiency of Softmax by introducing a hardware-friendly design that includes base replacement, low-precision computation, and online normalization, achieving 2.35x energy efficiency with minimal accuracy loss.

Hardware accelerators for ViTs have also been explored. ViTA \[[10' 2023-02-17](http://arxiv.org/abs/2302.09108v1)\] is a configurable hardware accelerator targeting edge devices, employing a head-level pipeline and inter-layer MLP optimizations to reduce off-chip memory accesses. This design achieves high hardware utilization and low power consumption, making it suitable for resource-constrained environments. FLASH-D \[[6' 2025-05-20](http://arxiv.org/abs/2505.14201v1)\] re-evaluates the FlashAttention kernel, presenting a simplified formulation that reduces area and power consumption while maintaining numerical stability and computational efficiency. This work is particularly relevant for hardware implementations of attention mechanisms.

In addition, the use of mixed-precision arithmetic has been explored for improving the efficiency of ViTs. The work by \[[16' 2024-10-16](http://arxiv.org/abs/2410.12614v1)\] presents a fine-grained rounding error analysis of finite element kernels and assembly, guiding the development of hardware-accelerated mixed-precision implementations. This approach ensures accuracy while reducing computational costs, which is crucial for deploying ViTs on hardware.

These prior works highlight the need for a holistic approach to ViT acceleration, addressing both algorithmic and hardware-level optimizations. Our work extends this by introducing the Microscaling Integer (MXInt) format, enabling the mapping of all ViT operations onto FPGAs while maintaining high accuracy and performance.
