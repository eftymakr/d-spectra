## Related Works

Multi-token prediction (MTP) has emerged as a promising alternative to traditional next-token prediction (NTP) in language model pre-training. While NTP focuses on predicting one token at a time, MTP extends this by predicting multiple tokens simultaneously, using multiple prediction heads \[[6' 2024-04-30](http://arxiv.org/abs/2404.19737v1)\]. This approach has shown potential in improving downstream performance, inference speed, and training efficiency, particularly for large language models \[[6' 2024-04-30](http://arxiv.org/abs/2404.19737v1)\]. However, smaller language models (SLMs) have struggled with the MTP objective, prompting researchers to explore curriculum learning strategies to address this limitation \[[6' 2024-04-30](http://arxiv.org/abs/2404.19737v1)\].

Curriculum learning has been widely studied in various domains, including computer vision and machine translation, where it has been used to improve model training speed and performance \[[3' 2021-08-04](http://arxiv.org/abs/2108.02170v1)\]. In the context of language modeling, prior work has not found compelling evidence that curriculum learning methods improve language model training \[[3' 2021-08-04](http://arxiv.org/abs/2108.02170v1)\]. However, recent studies have demonstrated that curriculum learning can significantly enhance the performance of small decoder-only code language models on code execution tasks \[[2' 2024-07-14](http://arxiv.org/abs/2407.10194v1)\]. This suggests that curriculum learning may be particularly beneficial for SLMs when applied to specific tasks like code generation.

In the realm of multi-token prediction, several approaches have been proposed. For instance, Future Token Prediction (FTP) introduces a novel pretraining method where a large transformer encoder generates embedding vectors that are cross-attended by a small decoder to predict multiple future tokens \[[1' 2024-10-23](http://arxiv.org/abs/2410.18160v1)\]. This method has shown improvements in topic coherence and text classification performance compared to standard GPT models \[[1' 2024-10-23](http://arxiv.org/abs/2410.18160v1)\]. Similarly, DynaMo proposes a suite of multi-token prediction language models that dynamically predict multiple tokens based on their confidence in the joint probability distribution, achieving significant speedups in inference time \[[5' 2024-05-01](http://arxiv.org/abs/2405.00888v1)\].

Another approach, Leap Multi-Token Prediction (L-MTP), extends multi-token prediction by introducing a leap-based mechanism that skips intermediate tokens, enabling non-sequential token prediction in a single forward pass \[[7' 2025-05-23](http://arxiv.org/abs/2505.17505v2)\]. This method enhances the model's ability to capture long-range dependencies and improves inference efficiency \[[7' 2025-05-23](http://arxiv.org/abs/2505.17505v2)\]. Additionally, MuToR introduces a simple and effective approach to multi-token prediction by interleaving learnable register tokens into the input sequence, offering advantages such as minimal parameter overhead and compatibility with off-the-shelf pretrained models \[[8' 2025-05-15](http://arxiv.org/abs/2505.10518v1)\].

The integration of curriculum learning with multi-token prediction has also been explored. For example, a forward curriculum gradually increases the complexity of the pre-training objective from NTP to MTP, while a reverse curriculum does the opposite \[[6' 2024-04-30](http://arxiv.org/abs/2404.19737v1)\]. Experiments have shown that the forward curriculum enables SLMs to better leverage the MTP objective during pre-training, improving downstream NTP performance and generative output quality \[[6' 2024-04-30](http://arxiv.org/abs/2404.19737v1)\]. In contrast, the reverse curriculum achieves stronger NTP performance but fails to provide any self-speculative decoding benefits \[[6' 2024-04-30](http://arxiv.org/abs/2404.19737v1)\].

These findings highlight the potential of curriculum learning in addressing the challenges associated with multi-token prediction, particularly for smaller models. Further research is needed to explore the effectiveness of different curriculum designs and their impact on various downstream tasks.

### <a id='category-multi-token-prediction-(mtp)-frameworks'></a>Multi-Token Prediction (MTP) Frameworks: 4 papers found.
Multi-Token Prediction (MTP) frameworks have gained traction as an alternative to traditional next-token prediction (NTP) in pretraining large language models, with several studies demonstrating their potential to enhance downstream performance, inference speed, and training efficiency \[[1' 2024-10-23](http://arxiv.org/abs/2410.18160v1)\]\[[2' 2024-07-14](http://arxiv.org/abs/2407.10194v1)\]. Unlike NTP, which predicts only the immediate next token, MTP models are trained to predict multiple future tokens simultaneously, often using multiple output heads \[[2' 2024-07-14](http://arxiv.org/abs/2407.10194v1)\]. This approach has been shown to improve topic coherence in generated text and enhance algorithmic reasoning capabilities, particularly in coding tasks \[[1' 2024-10-23](http://arxiv.org/abs/2410.18160v1)\]. Additionally, MTP can lead to faster inference times, with models trained using multi-token prediction achieving up to 3 times speed improvements \[[2' 2024-07-14](http://arxiv.org/abs/2407.10194v1)\]. However, smaller language models (SLMs) face challenges in effectively leveraging MTP, prompting the development of curriculum learning strategies to gradually introduce the MTP objective \[[1' 2024-10-23](http://arxiv.org/abs/2410.18160v1)\]. These strategies include forward and reverse curricula, where the former increases complexity from NTP to MTP, while the latter does the opposite \[[1' 2024-10-23](http://arxiv.org/abs/2410.18160v1)\]. Research has shown that forward curricula enable SLMs to better utilize MTP during pretraining, improving downstream NTP performance and generative quality, while reverse curricula yield stronger NTP results but lack benefits from self-speculative decoding \[[1' 2024-10-23](http://arxiv.org/abs/2410.18160v1)\]. Furthermore, MTP has been adapted for speech processing, where prompting methods using quantized speech units have demonstrated competitive performance against fine-tuning approaches \[[10' 2024-08-23](http://arxiv.org/abs/2408.13040v1)\]. Overall, MTP represents a promising direction for improving language model capabilities, though its effectiveness varies across model sizes and task domains.

### <a id='category-curriculum-learning-strategies'></a>Curriculum Learning Strategies: 5 papers found.
Curriculum learning has been explored across various domains, including natural language processing, reinforcement learning, and quantum machine learning, with differing outcomes on model performance. In natural language processing, studies such as \[[2' 2024-07-14](http://arxiv.org/abs/2407.10194v1)\] and \[[3' 2021-08-04](http://arxiv.org/abs/2108.02170v1)\] have examined the impact of curriculum learning on language model pretraining, finding limited evidence of performance improvement despite structured training regimes. However, in code language models, research \[[1' 2024-10-23](http://arxiv.org/abs/2410.18160v1)\] suggests that curriculum learning can significantly enhance the accuracy of small decoder-only models on code execution tasks, although its effect on code completion remains less pronounced. In reinforcement learning, curriculum learning is used to shape exploration by introducing tasks in increasing order of complexity, aiming to improve initial performance and reduce suboptimal actions during training \[[3' 2021-08-04](http://arxiv.org/abs/2108.02170v1)\]. Similarly, in quantum machine learning, a framework called quantum curriculum learning (Q-CurL) has been proposed to address training complexity and generalization by introducing simpler tasks before progressing to more complex ones, showing robustness to noise and data limitations \[[5' 2024-05-01](http://arxiv.org/abs/2405.00888v1)\]. These findings highlight the potential of curriculum learning in different contexts, though its effectiveness varies depending on the model architecture and task complexity.

### <a id='category-inference-efficiency-and-speed-enhancements'></a>Inference Efficiency and Speed Enhancements: 3 papers found.
In the category of Inference Efficiency and Speed Enhancements, several approaches have been proposed to improve the performance and efficiency of language models during inference. Traditional models operate autoregressively, predicting one token at a time, which leads to high inference times as model sizes increase \[[1' 2024-10-23](http://arxiv.org/abs/2410.18160v1)\]. To address this, multi-token prediction (MTP) methods have been introduced, allowing models to predict multiple tokens simultaneously, thereby reducing inference time \[[2' 2024-07-14](http://arxiv.org/abs/2407.10194v1)\]. For instance, DynaMo dynamically predicts multiple tokens based on confidence in the joint probability distribution, achieving a 2.57× speed-up with minimal overhead \[[1' 2024-10-23](http://arxiv.org/abs/2410.18160v1)\]. Similarly, leap multi-token prediction (L-MTP) introduces a leap-based mechanism that skips intermediate tokens, enabling non-sequential prediction in a single forward pass and improving inference efficiency \[[2' 2024-07-14](http://arxiv.org/abs/2407.10194v1)\]. Additionally, multi-word tokenizers like MWT offer a more compact and efficient tokenization by representing frequent multi-word expressions as single tokens, leading to faster inference and better performance across shorter sequences \[[3' 2021-08-04](http://arxiv.org/abs/2108.02170v1)\]. These techniques collectively aim to enhance both the speed and quality of text generation while reducing computational costs.

### <a id='category-long-range-dependency-capture-methods'></a>Long-Range Dependency Capture Methods: 2 papers found.
In the context of Long-Range Dependency Capture Methods, recent research has focused on enhancing the predictive capabilities of models by integrating diverse data modalities and improving the handling of complex dependencies. For instance, in chemistry, the integration of Large Language Models (LLMs) with Graph Neural Networks (GNNs) has shown promise in overcoming limitations of existing graph deep learning methods, particularly in capturing long-range dependencies within molecular structures \[[9' 2024-08-27](http://arxiv.org/abs/2408.14964v1)\]. Similarly, in medical imaging, deep learning approaches have been enhanced by incorporating radiomics features to better leverage prognostic information from tumor regions, even when segmentation masks are not available \[[12' 2022-11-10](http://arxiv.org/abs/2211.05409v1)\]. These studies highlight the importance of multi-modal fusion and the need for models that can effectively capture long-range dependencies across different data types and structures. Such approaches not only improve the accuracy of predictions but also enhance the robustness of models in real-world applications where data distribution may shift \[[9' 2024-08-27](http://arxiv.org/abs/2408.14964v1)\].
