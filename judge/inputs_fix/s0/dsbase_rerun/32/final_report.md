## Related Works

Understanding the evolution of feature representations across layers in large language models (LLMs) is crucial for improving interpretability and robustness \[[1' 2012-03-02](http://arxiv.org/abs/1203.0550v3)\]. Recent studies have identified critical layers linked to specific functions or behaviors, but these efforts typically rely on data-dependent analyses of fine-tuned models, limiting their use to post-hoc settings \[[1' 2012-03-02](http://arxiv.org/abs/1203.0550v3)\]. In contrast, our approach introduces a data-oblivious method to identify intrinsic critical layers in pre-fine-tuned LLMs by analyzing representation dynamics via Centered Kernel Alignment (CKA) \[[1' 2012-03-02](http://arxiv.org/abs/1203.0550v3)\]. This method allows for the identification of critical layers without requiring fine-tuning, offering a more generalizable framework \[[1' 2012-03-02](http://arxiv.org/abs/1203.0550v3)\].

The use of CKA to analyze representation alignment has been explored in several works. For instance, \[[6' 2021-12-17](http://arxiv.org/abs/2112.09305v1)\] demonstrates that CKA based on a Gaussian RBF kernel converges to linear CKA in the large-bandwidth limit, highlighting the sensitivity of convergence to the geometry of feature representations. This aligns with our findings that significant shifts in representation space are driven by changes in the top principal components, which encode semantic transitions from rationales to conclusions \[[1' 2012-03-02](http://arxiv.org/abs/1203.0550v3)\]. Furthermore, \[[7' 2025-02-23](http://arxiv.org/abs/2502.16722v1)\] investigates the layer-wise evolution of representations in fine-tuned transformers, observing that early layers retain general representations while later layers specialize in task adaptation. This supports our observation that layers with significant representation shifts are most affected during fine-tuning \[[1' 2012-03-02](http://arxiv.org/abs/1203.0550v3)\].

Our work also relates to methods that aim to improve the efficiency of fine-tuning. For example, \[[2' 2024-08-16](http://arxiv.org/abs/2408.08670v1)\] introduces an efficient fine-tuning method for Vision Transformers (ViTs) called ALaST, which adaptively allocates computational resources across layers based on their importance. Similarly, \[[13' 2025-01-31](http://arxiv.org/abs/2501.19389v4)\] proposes FSLoRA, a federated sketching LoRA framework that enables clients to selectively update submatrices of global LoRA modules, adapting to varying client capabilities. These methods share the goal of optimizing resource allocation during fine-tuning, though they focus on different architectures and settings \[[1' 2012-03-02](http://arxiv.org/abs/1203.0550v3)\].

In the context of parameter-efficient fine-tuning, \[[16' 2021-02-08](http://arxiv.org/abs/2102.03983v1)\] proposes a method that transfers partial knowledge by freezing or fine-tuning specific layers, controlling the extent of preserved transferability through learning rates. This approach is similar to our focus on identifying and fine-tuning critical layers for domain adaptation and backdoor defense \[[1' 2012-03-02](http://arxiv.org/abs/1203.0550v3)\]. Additionally, \[[15' 2024-11-14](http://arxiv.org/abs/2411.09539v2)\] provides a comprehensive survey of fine-tuning strategies for LLMs with limited data, emphasizing the importance of efficient and effective adaptation techniques \[[1' 2012-03-02](http://arxiv.org/abs/1203.0550v3)\].

Finally, \[[21' 2021-06-09](http://arxiv.org/abs/2106.05057v2)\] explores multiple kernel representation learning on networks, proposing a weighted matrix factorization model that encodes random walk-based information. ### <a id='category-efficient-fine-tuning-strategies'></a>Efficient Fine-Tuning Strategies: 8 papers found.
Efficient fine-tuning strategies have emerged as critical approaches to reduce computational costs and improve performance in various domains. These methods aim to optimize resource allocation during the fine-tuning process by selectively adjusting model layers based on their importance. For instance, ALaST (Adaptive Layer Selection Fine-Tuning for Vision Transformers) dynamically assigns compute budgets to layers, allowing some to be frozen or trained with fewer tokens, thereby reducing training time and memory usage \[[2' 2024-08-16](http://arxiv.org/abs/2408.08670v1)\]. Similarly, Feature Discrimination Alignment (FD-Align) enhances generalizability in few-shot learning by preserving spurious feature consistency across fine-tuning steps \[[3' 2023-10-23](http://arxiv.org/abs/2310.15105v4)\]. In time-series forecasting, LLM4TS employs a two-stage fine-tuning strategy to align pre-trained large language models with time-series data, improving adaptability and performance \[[5' 2023-08-16](http://arxiv.org/abs/2308.08469v6)\]. The structured graph-to-text model also leverages a two-step fine-tuning mechanism, incorporating novel tree-level embeddings to capture graph dependencies, leading to improved text generation metrics \[[9' 2021-05-17](http://arxiv.org/abs/2105.08021v2)\]. Additionally, robotic learning systems benefit from efficient fine-tuning, where pre-trained policies can be adapted to new environments with minimal data, achieving substantial performance gains \[[14' 2020-04-21](http://arxiv.org/abs/2004.10190v2)\]. For few-shot learning, partial knowledge transfer methods, such as freezing or fine-tuning specific layers with adjusted learning rates, have been proposed to avoid overfitting and maintain model robustness \[[16' 2021-02-08](http://arxiv.org/abs/2102.03983v1)\]. These approaches collectively highlight the importance of adaptive and selective fine-tuning strategies in enhancing model efficiency and effectiveness across diverse applications.

### <a id='category-parameter-efficient-fine-tuning-techniques'></a>Parameter-Efficient Fine-Tuning Techniques: 5 papers found.
Parameter-Efficient Fine-Tuning (PEFT) techniques have emerged as critical approaches to adapt large language models (LLMs) to downstream tasks without requiring full parameter updates, which are computationally expensive \[[1' 2012-03-02](http://arxiv.org/abs/1203.0550v3)\]. These methods, such as LoRA, focus on modifying only a subset of parameters, thereby reducing resource consumption while maintaining model performance \[[2' 2024-08-16](http://arxiv.org/abs/2408.08670v1)\]. Recent work has explored the concept of task-specific directions (TSDs), which guide the fine-tuning process by identifying the most impactful parameter adjustments for specific tasks \[[3' 2023-10-23](http://arxiv.org/abs/2310.15105v4)\]. This idea has led to the development of LoRA-Dash, which enhances model performance by maximizing the influence of TSDs during training \[[4' 2024-09-02](http://arxiv.org/abs/2409.01035v5)\]. Additionally, the initialization of LoRA modules has been shown to significantly affect performance, prompting the introduction of LoRA-Init, which leverages TSDs to initialize LoRA matrices more effectively \[[5' 2023-08-16](http://arxiv.org/abs/2308.08469v6)\]. In distributed settings, methods like FSLoRA have been proposed to address resource heterogeneity by allowing clients to selectively update submatrices of global LoRA modules, ensuring adaptability to varying computational constraints \[[6' 2021-12-17](http://arxiv.org/abs/2112.09305v1)\]. Furthermore, the application of PEFT extends beyond traditional NLP tasks, with frameworks like Point-PEFT being designed for 3D pre-trained models, enabling efficient adaptation with minimal learnable parameters \[[7' 2025-02-23](http://arxiv.org/abs/2502.16722v1)\]. These advancements highlight the growing importance of PEFT in enabling practical and efficient model adaptation across diverse domains and deployment scenarios \[[8' 2022-10-11](http://arxiv.org/abs/2210.05643v4)\].
