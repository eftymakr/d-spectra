## Related Works

The rapid adoption of large language models (LLMs) has underscored the critical need for robust fairness benchmarks \[[1' 2024-09-22](http://arxiv.org/abs/2409.14583v4)\]. Traditional fairness metrics, such as Equalized Odds, focus on discrete accuracy-based evaluations, which fail to capture the implicit impact of model uncertainty \[[1' 2024-09-22](http://arxiv.org/abs/2409.14583v4)\]. This limitation has prompted researchers to explore alternative fairness evaluation frameworks that incorporate uncertainty-aware metrics \[[1' 2024-09-22](http://arxiv.org/abs/2409.14583v4)\]. For instance, \[[2' 2025-04-22](http://arxiv.org/abs/2504.15941v2)\] highlights the persistent challenges in achieving equitable outcomes in machine translation, emphasizing the need for more nuanced fairness measures. Similarly, \[[3' 2024-07-15](http://arxiv.org/abs/2407.10853v6)\] discusses the importance of evaluating fairness across different deployment contexts, suggesting that fairness risks cannot be reliably assessed from benchmark performance alone. This aligns with the need for a more comprehensive fairness metric that reflects model uncertainty.

Several studies have examined gender and occupational biases in LLMs. \[[4' 2025-05-05](http://arxiv.org/abs/2505.02456v2)\] reports significant gender and country biases in occupation recommendations, showing that even when models show parity for gender or country individually, intersectional occupational biases persist. This highlights the complexity of addressing fairness in multilingual and intersectional settings. \[[5' 2023-11-30](http://arxiv.org/abs/2311.18580v2)\] further explores the impact of gender and occupation stereotypes in sentiment analysis, demonstrating how these biases can influence model outputs. The findings from \[[5' 2023-11-30](http://arxiv.org/abs/2311.18580v2)\] support the need for datasets that capture diverse occupational and gender representations to evaluate LLM fairness effectively.

The development of new datasets for fairness evaluation has also been a focal point in recent research. \[[6' 2024-04-30](http://arxiv.org/abs/2404.19371v1)\] introduces FairTranslate, a dataset designed to evaluate non-binary gender biases in machine translation, emphasizing the importance of annotated metadata for accurate bias assessment. \[[7' 2017-11-14](http://arxiv.org/abs/1711.05144v5)\] presents a benchmark for evaluating fairness in chest radiography without demographic data, showcasing the potential of using backbone foundation models to create protected attribute groups. These studies underscore the importance of diverse and representative datasets in fairness evaluation.

Moreover, \[[8' 2020-01-21](http://arxiv.org/abs/2001.09784v1)\] discusses the challenges of bridging the gap between algorithmic fairness and legal frameworks, emphasizing the need for best practices in defining and implementing fairness in real-world systems. \[[9' 2023-10-02](http://arxiv.org/abs/2310.00905v2)\] explores the limitations of existing fairness benchmarks, particularly in capturing the nuances of model uncertainty and bias. These studies collectively highlight the need for a more comprehensive and context-aware approach to fairness evaluation in LLMs.

In addition, \[[10' 2025-03-09](http://arxiv.org/abs/2503.06792v1)\] introduces a new fairness metric, UCerF, which evaluates model fairness with uncertainty awareness, addressing the limitations of conventional metrics. This work is closely aligned with the proposed benchmark in the current paper, as it aims to provide a more reflective evaluation of model fairness by incorporating uncertainty. \[[11' 2019-06-24](http://arxiv.org/abs/1906.10256v2)\] further discusses the challenges of evaluating fairness in multilingual settings, emphasizing the need for datasets that span multiple languages and cultures.

Overall, the related works highlight the growing interest in developing more nuanced and context-aware fairness metrics and datasets for evaluating LLMs. These studies provide a foundation for the proposed benchmark, which aims to address the limitations of existing approaches by incorporating uncertainty-aware fairness evaluation.
