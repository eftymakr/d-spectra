## Related Works

Dense retrieval (DR) has gained significant attention in recent years due to its ability to generate high-quality text representations for efficient search. Early approaches relied on pre-trained language models (PLMs) such as BERT, which were fine-tuned using contrastive learning to align query and document embeddings in a shared embedding space \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2208.09846)\]. The success of these models was largely attributed to the ability of PLMs to capture semantic relationships between words and phrases \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/2108.05540)\]. However, the role of fine-tuning versus pre-training in DR has been a topic of debate. Some studies argue that retrieval knowledge is primarily acquired during pre-training, and fine-tuning mainly adjusts neuron activations rather than reorganizing knowledge \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/2306.03166)\]. This claim was initially tested in the context of BERT-based encoders using Dense Passage Retrieval (DPR) as a representative DR model \[[4](http://arxiv.org/abs/2104.08253)\]. 

Recent research has expanded on this idea by exploring different representation approaches, such as using CLS tokens versus mean pooling, and varying backbone architectures, such as encoder-only BERT versus decoder-only LLaMA \[[5](http://arxiv.org/abs/2208.00511)\]. The results indicate that while pre-trained knowledge underpins retrieval performance in DPR tuning, this pattern does not universally apply to all models. For example, in mean-pooled models like Contriever and decoder-based models like LLaMA, the role of fine-tuning appears to be more significant \[[6](http://arxiv.org/abs/2010.02616)\]. 

Contrastive pre-training has also been explored as a way to improve the discriminative ability of autoencoder-based language models for dense retrieval. Techniques such as contrastive span prediction and relevance-aware contrastive learning have been proposed to enhance the quality of text representations by focusing on key words and reducing the impact of common words \[[7](http://arxiv.org/abs/2002.08909)\]. These methods have shown promising results in improving retrieval performance on benchmarks such as BEIR and MS MARCO \[[8](http://arxiv.org/abs/2004.14218)\]. 

Additionally, the use of unsupervised pre-training strategies, such as masked auto-encoder (MAE) and contrastive learning, has been investigated to reduce the reliance on labeled data and improve generalization across domains \[[9](http://arxiv.org/abs/2208.00635)\]. For instance, RetroMAE introduces a novel MAE workflow that enables efficient pre-training of dense retrievers by leveraging asymmetric masking ratios and model structures \[[10](http://arxiv.org/abs/2312.05934)\]. Similarly, Llama2Vec proposes an unsupervised adaptation approach for large language models, where the model is fine-tuned using pretext tasks that encourage the generation of discriminative embeddings \[[11](http://arxiv.org/abs/2205.12035)\].

In summary, the related works highlight the importance of pre-training in dense retrieval, the role of fine-tuning in adjusting model behavior, and the potential of contrastive and unsupervised learning strategies to improve retrieval performance. These findings provide a foundation for further exploration into the interplay between pre-training and fine-tuning in dense retrieval systems.

### <a id='category-contrastive-learning-techniques'></a>Contrastive Learning Techniques: 9 papers found.
Contrastive learning has emerged as a powerful paradigm in dense retrieval, where models are trained to learn discriminative text representations by contrasting positive and negative examples \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2208.09846)\]. This approach has been widely applied to enhance the performance of dense retrievers, which rely on pre-trained language models (e.g., BERT, LLaMA) fine-tuned via contrastive learning to encode text into semantic representations \[[13](http://arxiv.org/abs/2204.10641)\]. Recent studies have questioned the extent to which fine-tuning contributes to retrieval performance, suggesting that much of the knowledge is acquired during pre-training rather than through fine-tuning \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2208.09846)\]. This idea was initially tested using BERT-based encoders with the DPR framework, but subsequent work has extended the analysis to other representation methods, such as CLS token vs. mean pooling, and different backbone architectures, including encoder-only BERT and decoder-only LLaMA \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2208.09846)\]. Additionally, the effectiveness of contrastive learning has been explored in the context of unsupervised training, where pseudo-positive examples are generated from unlabeled data to improve generalization and reduce the dependency on large training sets \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/2306.03166)\]. Furthermore, contrastive strategies have been combined with other techniques, such as variational auto-encoders and supervised contrastive learning, to address issues like representation anisotropy and the bypass effect of weak decoders \[[14](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/c7f8acb0bdae40eeb0505bf87744fa29270073ac)\]. These approaches aim to generate isotropic sentence embeddings and improve the discriminative power of learned representations \[[6](http://arxiv.org/abs/2010.02616)\]. Overall, contrastive learning remains a key component in advancing dense retrieval, with ongoing efforts to refine its application across different architectures and tasks \[[29](http://arxiv.org/abs/2107.07773)\].

### <a id='category-pre-trained-language-models-in-dense-retrieval'></a>Pre-trained Language Models in Dense Retrieval: 9 papers found.
Recent research has focused on improving dense retrievers by leveraging pre-trained language models (PLMs) and addressing challenges related to training data noise and embedding space learning \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/2108.05540)\]. The Condenser architecture was introduced to pre-train models that condense information into dense vectors, enhancing retrieval performance \[[4](http://arxiv.org/abs/2104.08253)\]. However, PLMs like BERT are not structurally ready for dense passage retrieval (DPR) due to a mismatch between pre-training and fine-tuning objectives, leading to the need for computationally expensive techniques such as hard negative mining and cross-encoder distillation \[[5](http://arxiv.org/abs/2208.00511)\]. To address this, the Aggretriever model aggregates contextualized token embeddings into a dense vector, improving retrieval effectiveness without significant training overhead \[[5](http://arxiv.org/abs/2208.00511)\]. Additionally, RetroMAE, a retrieval-oriented pre-training paradigm based on Masked Auto-Encoder (MAE), has shown competitive performance on dense retrieval benchmarks by employing asymmetric masking ratios and model structures \[[11](http://arxiv.org/abs/2205.12035)\]. Furthermore, pre-training large bi-encoder models on synthetic and real-world datasets has demonstrated substantial improvements over supervised baselines in information retrieval tasks \[[15](http://arxiv.org/abs/2107.13602)\]. These studies collectively highlight the importance of pre-training strategies and architectural innovations in enhancing dense retrieval systems.

### <a id='category-model-adaptation-and-fine-tuning-methods'></a>Model Adaptation and Fine-tuning Methods: 8 papers found.
The category "Model Adaptation and Fine-tuning Methods" encompasses a range of approaches aimed at improving the performance of pre-trained language models through fine-tuning and adaptation. Research highlights that fine-tuning can significantly alter the internal representations of pre-trained models, affecting their linguistic knowledge and performance on probing tasks \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2208.09846)\]. This is particularly evident in cross-lingual models, where fine-tuning can weaken cross-lingual abilities, necessitating techniques like continual learning to preserve original capabilities \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/2108.05540)\]. Additionally, the effectiveness of fine-tuning varies across models and tasks, with some models showing minimal improvement while others experience substantial changes in probing accuracy \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2208.09846)\]. For dense retrieval tasks, methods such as LoRA have been introduced to reduce training parameters and costs while maintaining or improving performance \[[4](http://arxiv.org/abs/2104.08253)\]. Similarly, approaches like Llama2Vec focus on adapting large language models for dense retrieval through unsupervised methods, achieving state-of-the-art results on benchmark datasets \[[5](http://arxiv.org/abs/2208.00511)\]. The pre-train-then-align paradigm, which involves fine-tuning models to align with human preferences or downstream tasks, has also been studied, revealing that continual pre-training can enhance model performance in ways that become evident after fine-tuning \[[6](http://arxiv.org/abs/2010.02616)\]. Furthermore, the relationship between pre-training and fine-tuning has been explored, showing that while fine-tuning can improve performance, it may also lead to forgetting previously learned knowledge \[[6](http://arxiv.org/abs/2010.02616)\]. These studies collectively emphasize the importance of understanding how fine-tuning affects model representations and the need for efficient and effective adaptation strategies.
