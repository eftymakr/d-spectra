## Related Works

Sequential recommendation systems aim to predict the next item a user may interact with, based on their historical behavior sequences. These systems have evolved significantly, incorporating advanced neural architectures to better model user preferences and interaction patterns. Early approaches, such as Markov Chains (MCs), assumed that the next action depends only on the last few actions, while Recurrent Neural Networks (RNNs) were introduced to capture longer-term dependencies \[[7](http://arxiv.org/abs/1808.09781)\]. However, RNNs often struggle with long-term dependencies and are computationally intensive \[[15](http://arxiv.org/abs/2008.08273)\]. More recently, self-attention mechanisms have gained popularity due to their ability to model complex sequential dependencies without explicit recurrence \[[7](http://arxiv.org/abs/1808.09781)\]. 

Several works have attempted to incorporate temporal information into sequential recommendation models. For instance, TiSASRec explicitly models time intervals between interactions, improving the accuracy of next-item prediction by considering both absolute positions and time gaps \[[6](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/9c7a455b1e48d01a99e58e884a6c1acb75074ad0)\]. Similarly, SASRec introduces a self-attention mechanism that captures long-term semantics while maintaining efficiency, making it suitable for both sparse and dense datasets \[[7](http://arxiv.org/abs/1808.09781)\]. However, these models often neglect the irregularity of user interaction times, which can affect the modeling of dynamic user preferences \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2502.08132)\]. 

To address the limitations of traditional models, researchers have proposed hybrid architectures that combine attention with state-space models (SSMs). SS4Rec, for example, integrates a time-aware SSM to handle irregular time intervals and a relation-aware SSM to model contextual dependencies, enabling it to infer user interest from both temporal and sequential perspectives \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2502.08132)\]. This approach allows for more accurate modeling of user behavior over time. Additionally, TLSRec introduces a time-aware Transformer model that combines long-term and short-term temporal information, adjusting personalized trade-offs using adaptive fusion layers \[[2](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/417f89b6992377e3e5d706672bfa4da837f011df)\]. These models demonstrate the importance of incorporating temporal information to improve recommendation accuracy. 

Another line of research focuses on capturing both long-term and short-term user preferences. TLSRec employs a hierarchical self-attention network to learn user preferences at both global and local time scales, while also using a neural time gate to adaptively regulate the contributions of long-term and short-term preferences \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/2208.04760)\]. Similarly, IRUP integrates item relationships and user preferences by using graph convolution networks (GCNs) and attention mechanisms to model long-term and short-term preferences, respectively \[[12](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/eb2ad6471c9e665a6393383a2deb2224f1ef07d7)\]. These models highlight the value of combining multiple modeling strategies to better represent user behavior. 

In addition to temporal modeling, some works have explored the integration of knowledge graphs to enhance recommendation quality. KATRec, for example, leverages a knowledge graph attention network to model both short-term and long-term user interests, improving item representation learning by incorporating higher-order connections \[[9](http://arxiv.org/abs/2012.03323)\]. This approach demonstrates the potential of integrating external knowledge into sequential recommendation systems. 

Overall, these works demonstrate the evolving landscape of sequential recommendation models, with increasing emphasis on temporal modeling, attention mechanisms, and hybrid architectures to better capture user preferences and interaction patterns. To address these limitations, several models have been proposed that integrate time-aware mechanisms into their architectures. Additionally, TiSASRec explicitly models time intervals between interactions, integrating both absolute item positions and time-aware positional encodings to improve next-item prediction accuracy \[[6](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/9c7a455b1e48d01a99e58e884a6c1acb75074ad0)\]. ### <a id='category-attention-based-sequential-recommendation'></a>Attention-Based Sequential Recommendation: 6 papers found.
Attention-based sequential recommendation has seen significant advancements, with researchers addressing challenges such as capturing long-term dependencies, modeling diverse user interests, and efficiently handling variable-length sequences \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2502.08132)\]. Traditional models like Markov Chains (MCs) and Recurrent Neural Networks (RNNs) have limitations in balancing model complexity and performance, prompting the development of self-attention based models that offer more flexible and efficient alternatives \[[2](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/417f89b6992377e3e5d706672bfa4da837f011df)\]. For instance, SASRec leverages self-attention to capture long-term semantics while making predictions based on fewer actions, improving efficiency and performance on both sparse and dense datasets \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/2208.04760)\]. Similarly, methods like LimaRec introduce lifelong incremental multi-interest self-attention mechanisms to better model evolving user preferences and diverse interests \[[4](http://arxiv.org/abs/2105.14060)\]. Other approaches, such as S3-Rec, focus on self-supervised learning to enhance data representations by capturing correlations between attributes, items, and subsequences \[[5](http://arxiv.org/abs/2008.07873)\]. Additionally, models like MEANTIME address limitations in positional embeddings by utilizing multi-temporal embeddings to better capture temporal dynamics and avoid information bottlenecks \[[6](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/9c7a455b1e48d01a99e58e884a6c1acb75074ad0)\]. Furthermore, recent work has emphasized the importance of incorporating global context and diversity in recommendations, leading to the development of probabilistic attention mechanisms that balance relevance and diversity \[[7](http://arxiv.org/abs/1808.09781)\]. These advances collectively highlight the growing trend toward more sophisticated attention-based models that better align with the complexities of real-world sequential recommendation tasks.
