## Related Works

Zero-Knowledge Proofs (ZKPs) have emerged as a critical cryptographic primitive for privacy-preserving computation, enabling a prover to demonstrate the validity of a statement without revealing additional information \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2408.05890)\]. Despite their potential, the computational complexity of ZKP generation has limited their adoption in real-world applications \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2408.05890)\]. Recent efforts have focused on accelerating the key operations in ZKP protocols, particularly on GPU and ASIC platforms \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2408.05890)\]. For instance, GZKP presents a GPU-accelerated ZKP system that improves performance by eliminating costly external shuffles and optimizing memory access patterns \[[2](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/e149d93531d8c07cf43f6cae327a4795144d1304)\]. Similarly, cuZK introduces a faster parallel multi-scalar multiplication (MSM) algorithm on GPUs, achieving nearly linear speedup over the Pippenger algorithm \[[6](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/e4a4608e8e35379e63b9df5bda1b7599ee8ab9ac)\]. 

The challenge of accelerating ZKP protocols often involves optimizing the Number Theoretic Transform (NTT) and MSM operations, which are computationally intensive \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2408.05890)\]. SZKP is a notable effort in this regard, presenting an ASIC-based accelerator that achieves over 400× speedup over CPU implementations for full-proof generation \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2408.05890)\]. ReZK, another hardware accelerator, focuses on NTT/INTT and MSM by employing a reconfigurable architecture that supports variable bit-widths and sizes \[[10](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/8b12309fd3c5a1851ff8b6d9cfff018811d08d93)\]. These accelerators highlight the importance of structured dataflows and parallelism in improving performance \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2408.05890)\]. 

However, existing accelerators often face limitations such as the need for trusted setup or larger proof sizes, which hinder their applicability in scenarios with numerous verifiers or strict verification time constraints \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2408.05890)\]. For example, some systems require a one-time setup for each application, which can be impractical for dynamic environments \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2408.05890)\]. In contrast, HyperPlonk supports a universal setup, allowing for more flexible deployment \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2408.05890)\]. 

The work presented here, zkSpeed, addresses these limitations by accelerating the entire HyperPlonk protocol, including the SumCheck and MSM primitives, on a full-chip architecture. This approach ensures that the entire proof generation process is optimized, achieving geometric mean speedups of 801× over CPU baselines \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2408.05890)\]. The design leverages high bandwidth and efficient dataflow to minimize latency, making it suitable for publicly verifiable systems \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2408.05890)\]. 

Several prior works have explored similar optimizations. For example, PipeZK introduces a pipelined architecture that handles large polynomial computations and MSMs efficiently \[[5](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/6ee84674b7c426849c3da56cb278c08eb7a903cf)\]. It achieves a 10× speedup on standard benchmarks and a 5× speedup on the Zcash application \[[5](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/6ee84674b7c426849c3da56cb278c08eb7a903cf)\]. Similarly, if-ZKP presents an FPGA-based accelerator for the BLS12-381 and BN128 elliptic curves, achieving 110×–150× speedup over software \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/2412.12481)\]. These works underscore the importance of hardware acceleration in overcoming the performance bottlenecks of ZKP systems \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2408.05890)\].
