## Related Works

Sensor fusion is a cornerstone of autonomous driving, enabling robust perception in diverse environmental conditions \[[20](http://arxiv.org/abs/2304.10410)\]. Among the most commonly used sensor pairs, radar and camera systems offer complementary advantages: radar provides reliable measurements in adverse weather, while cameras capture rich visual information \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2307.15264)\]. However, achieving accurate calibration between these sensors remains a critical challenge, particularly in dynamic and unstructured environments \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2307.15264)\]. This section reviews existing approaches to radar-camera calibration and fusion, highlighting their limitations and the innovations presented in this work.

Early methods for radar-camera calibration often relied on target-based approaches, which require carefully designed calibration environments and specialized hardware \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2307.15264)\]. For example, one method places a single corner reflector on the ground and uses iterative data collection to estimate the extrinsic calibration matrix \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2307.15264)\]. While these methods achieve reasonable accuracy, they are not suitable for real-world deployment due to their dependency on controlled environments \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2307.15264)\]. Another method leverages the 6-DoF pose estimation of LiDAR sensors to build a 3D map and then calibrates radar sensors against this map \[[2](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/0e88fc24ae7694d0b1a2fe78322936fc568bc7d2)\]. However, this approach assumes overlapping fields of view between LiDAR and radar, which is not always feasible \[[2](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/0e88fc24ae7694d0b1a2fe78322936fc568bc7d2)\].

More recent approaches have focused on targetless calibration, enabling real-time and automatic calibration without the need for dedicated calibration targets \[[12](http://arxiv.org/abs/1904.08743)\]. For instance, one method uses a coarse and fine convolutional neural network to estimate rotational calibration between radar and camera \[[12](http://arxiv.org/abs/1904.08743)\]. Another method employs a continuous-time formulation to calibrate 3D radar to camera, using radar velocity measurements without requiring external targets \[[8](http://arxiv.org/abs/2103.07505)\]. These methods demonstrate the feasibility of automatic calibration but often lack robustness in the presence of noise and sparse data \[[8](http://arxiv.org/abs/2103.07505)\].

In addition to calibration, several works have explored radar-camera fusion for object detection and semantic segmentation \[[10](http://arxiv.org/abs/2509.05785)\]. For example, CRAB proposes a novel fusion approach that leverages backward projection and radar to reduce depth ambiguity in BEV feature generation \[[10](http://arxiv.org/abs/2509.05785)\]. Similarly, CramNet introduces a ray-constrained cross-attention mechanism to align radar and camera features in 3D space \[[11](http://arxiv.org/abs/2210.09267)\]. These methods highlight the importance of accurate calibration for effective fusion but often rely on pre-calibrated sensors or assume ideal conditions \[[10](http://arxiv.org/abs/2509.05785)\]\[[11](http://arxiv.org/abs/2210.09267)\].

To address these limitations, this work introduces a novel online automatic geometric calibration method for radar and camera systems. Unlike previous methods that require specialized infrastructure or target-based calibration, our approach leverages dual-perspective representations and selective fusion mechanisms to reduce height uncertainty \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2307.15264)\]. Furthermore, we incorporate multi-modal cross-attention and a noise-resistant matcher to enhance robustness against sparsity and uncertainty \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2307.15264)\]. Experimental results on the nuScenes dataset demonstrate that our method significantly outperforms existing auto-calibration techniques and state-of-the-art LiDAR-camera calibration methods \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2307.15264)\].
