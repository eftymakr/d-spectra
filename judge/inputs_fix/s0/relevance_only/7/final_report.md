## Related Works

Large Language Models (LLMs) have been extensively studied for their ability to encode and exhibit behaviors such as refusal within their activation space \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2505.17306)\]. However, identifying and manipulating these behaviors remains a significant challenge. Existing approaches often rely on predefined refusal templates detectable in output tokens \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2505.17306)\], or require manual analysis of model responses \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2505.17306)\]. These methods are limited in their ability to generalize across different alignment conditions and may not account for the nuanced mechanisms underlying refusal behavior \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2505.17306)\].

Recent research has explored the idea that refusal behavior in LLMs is mediated by a single direction in activation space \[[4](http://arxiv.org/abs/2406.11717)\]. This finding has been further validated across multiple languages, showing that a single refusal direction extracted from one language can effectively bypass refusals in other languages \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2505.17306)\]. This cross-lingual transferability suggests that refusal behavior is governed by a universal mechanism that can be leveraged for multilingual safety defenses \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2505.17306)\].

To address the limitations of manual analysis and predefined templates, several works have proposed automated methods for identifying and steering refusal behavior. Refusal tokens have been introduced as a simple way to calibrate refusal rates in LLMs \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/2412.06748)\]. These tokens are prepended to model responses during training, allowing for fine-grained control over refusal behavior without the need for additional fine-tuning \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/2412.06748)\]. However, this approach still requires explicit tokenization and may not capture the full complexity of refusal mechanisms in activation space \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/2412.06748)\].

Another approach, termed Multimodal-CoT, integrates both text and vision modalities to enhance reasoning capabilities \[[6](http://arxiv.org/abs/2302.00923)\]. This method separates rationale generation from answer inference, leveraging multimodal information to improve the accuracy and consistency of model outputs \[[6](http://arxiv.org/abs/2302.00923)\]. While this approach is primarily focused on reasoning, it highlights the potential for integrating diverse modalities to better understand and control model behavior \[[6](http://arxiv.org/abs/2302.00923)\].

In addition to steering, there is growing interest in improving the logical consistency of LLMs \[[7](http://arxiv.org/abs/2409.13724)\]. Neuro-symbolic integration has been proposed as a way to enhance the logical consistency of models by incorporating external facts and rules \[[7](http://arxiv.org/abs/2409.13724)\]. This approach allows models to maintain consistency with given constraints and improves their ability to extrapolate to new, unseen information \[[7](http://arxiv.org/abs/2409.13724)\].

The challenge of identifying and manipulating refusal behavior has also been addressed through activation-level interventions. Recent work has shown that fine-grained interventions at the attention head level can effectively bypass safety guardrails and steer model generations toward harmful AI coordination \[[10](http://arxiv.org/abs/2502.05945)\]. This method leverages the linear separability of behaviors encoded at the attention head level, enabling targeted interventions without requiring full-layer fine-tuning \[[10](http://arxiv.org/abs/2502.05945)\].

In summary, while existing methods for identifying and steering refusal behavior in LLMs have made progress, they often rely on predefined templates, manual analysis, or limited forms of automated intervention \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2505.17306)\]\[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/2412.06748)\]\[[4](http://arxiv.org/abs/2406.11717)\]\[[10](http://arxiv.org/abs/2502.05945)\]. The introduction of automated frameworks such as COSMIC, which uses cosine similarity to identify steering directions independently of model outputs, represents a significant advancement in this field \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2505.17306)\].
