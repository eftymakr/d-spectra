## Related Works

Maximum Inner Product Search (MIPS) has been extensively studied in recent years due to its critical role in recommendation systems, information retrieval, and high-dimensional data applications \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2503.06882)\]. Traditional approaches to MIPS either rely solely on inner product similarity, which can lead to local optima and redundant computations \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2503.06882)\], or reduce the problem to nearest neighbor search (NNS) under the Euclidean metric through space projection, which often results in topology destruction and information loss \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2503.06882)\]. These two paradigms, while distinct, are not inherently opposed. In fact, recent work has demonstrated that integrating inner product and Euclidean metrics in indexing and search algorithms can significantly enhance MIPS performance \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2503.06882)\].

Several methods have attempted to address the limitations of existing approaches. For instance, \[[2](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/25454f456c514219c9d6eeb42200ebbf378db9ca)\] introduces the Norm Adjusted Proximity Graph (NAPG), which adjusts the graph construction process to select more meaningful data points, thereby improving the efficiency of inner product search. Similarly, \[[3](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/386854741f828d30e25a5e01d75e3363d49cad1e)\] proposes Indexable Probabilistic Matrix Factorization (IPMF), which integrates the indexing process into the training phase, achieving faster retrieval times while maintaining recommendation accuracy. These methods highlight the importance of coupling inference and indexing to improve performance.

Graph-based methods have also gained traction in MIPS due to their ability to model complex relationships between vectors \[[4](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/92fb60fde7aa7c94a22571dec416d4a795548f67)\]. The inner-product navigable small world graph (ip-NSW) is a notable example, achieving an order of magnitude speedup over traditional methods by leveraging the norm bias inherent in MIPS \[[5](http://arxiv.org/abs/2201.09290)\]. However, ip-NSW's performance is closely tied to the data distribution, and it may not generalize well across different datasets \[[5](http://arxiv.org/abs/2201.09290)\]. To address this, \[[6](http://arxiv.org/abs/1610.03317)\] proposes ip-NSW+, which introduces an additional angular proximity graph to improve robustness and performance across varying data distributions.

Efforts to improve search efficiency have also focused on reducing computational complexity. For example, \[[7](http://arxiv.org/abs/1909.13459)\] presents BanditMIPS, a randomized algorithm that estimates inner products by subsampling coordinates and adaptively evaluating more promising vectors. This approach achieves a complexity independent of the dimensionality, making it suitable for high-dimensional settings \[[7](http://arxiv.org/abs/1909.13459)\]. Similarly, \[[8](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/0a27db33fcdb1117c971263ada20718828a250d1)\] introduces a greedy approach for budgeted MIPS, allowing users to control the trade-off between search efficiency and quality \[[8](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/0a27db33fcdb1117c971263ada20718828a250d1)\].

In addition to efficiency, diversity in search results has become an important consideration. \[[9](http://arxiv.org/abs/2212.07551)\] introduces the categorical diversity-aware inner product search problem, where users can select preferred categories, and proposes an approximation algorithm with sub-linear time complexity \[[9](http://arxiv.org/abs/2212.07551)\]. This work highlights the growing need for diversified recommendations in real-world applications \[[9](http://arxiv.org/abs/2212.07551)\].

Finally, \[[10](http://arxiv.org/abs/2309.09013)\] explores the use of GPU acceleration for MIPS, leveraging hardware efficiency and pruning techniques to significantly reduce query processing time \[[10](http://arxiv.org/abs/2309.09013)\]. This work demonstrates the potential of parallel computing in improving the scalability of MIPS algorithms.

In summary, while existing methods have made significant strides in improving MIPS performance, there remains a need for more robust, efficient, and adaptable solutions that can handle the complexities of high-dimensional data and diverse application requirements.
