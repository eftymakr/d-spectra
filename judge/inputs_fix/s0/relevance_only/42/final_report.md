## Related Works

The growing adoption of cloud-native applications has led to increased interest in optimizing costs associated with containerized workloads. While prior research has explored system-level optimization strategies for cost and resource efficiency in containerized systems \[[1](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/8335555ae08140dc2fe0da4807040fc88b0cace1)\], the analysis of network costs in Kubernetes clusters remains underexplored \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/2501.14753)\]. This gap is addressed in this paper, which investigates the network usage and cost implications of containerized applications running on Kubernetes clusters. 

Several studies have examined cost optimization techniques for cloud environments. For instance, \[[3](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/2ce38cfb0bf3be6e875f5feb8c4b5e140587bb10)\] discusses cloud cost optimization strategies, emphasizing the importance of resource allocation, workload distribution, and the use of cloud management tools to monitor and predict cloud spending. Additionally, \[[4](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/e7d7f1366e96c3970cfdabb69372b774fe9c5e26)\] introduces ABACUS, a FinOps solution that enables automated budget analysis and cloud usage surveillance, helping organizations enforce budgets and alert teams when spending thresholds are exceeded. These approaches highlight the role of automation and visibility in managing cloud expenditures.

In the context of Kubernetes, research has focused on improving resource management and scheduling. \[[5](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/c60cd2fe9e05152ae366a070ba5d8798f7e64151)\] explores the challenges of over-provisioning and under-utilization in Kubernetes environments and proposes the use of AI and machine learning techniques to enhance resource allocation efficiency. These techniques include predictive scaling algorithms and reinforcement learning-based orchestration, which aim to improve resource utilization and reduce operational expenses \[[6](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/2a7396b670ac72d37afbf5da2925116d35a4030a)\]. Similarly, \[[7](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/8e1ccea4a7235a295ddf13ba43061889e39bcb13)\] investigates the performance of Kubernetes in managing containerized applications, highlighting the need for optimized scheduling strategies to improve resource utilization and reduce latency.

The security of Kubernetes clusters has also been a focus of recent research. \[[8](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/00f68d85141a6981a05bb57b9e84610b2ef61941)\] presents a comprehensive review of best practices for securing Kubernetes environments, covering areas such as cluster setup, authentication, network security, and logging. Additionally, \[[9](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/d11e5c5c08af88ee8abb9ebaa166b1d6fd198501)\] explores the security aspects of container orchestration in Kubernetes, identifying potential vulnerabilities and proposing solutions to enhance security. These studies underscore the importance of robust security practices in Kubernetes deployments.

Furthermore, \[[10](http://arxiv.org/abs/1901.04946)\] investigates the challenges of managing availability in microservice-based applications using Kubernetes, emphasizing the role of redundancy and healing capabilities in improving service availability. The paper also compares Kubernetes with the Availability Management Framework (AMF), demonstrating the potential of Kubernetes in achieving high availability.

In summary, the existing literature provides a foundation for understanding the challenges and opportunities in optimizing costs and improving the performance and security of containerized applications in Kubernetes environments. This paper builds on these insights to provide a detailed analysis of network costs in Kubernetes clusters and contribute to the emerging field of FinOps.
