## Related Works

Fine-grained emotion classification in app reviews has received increasing attention in recent years due to its potential to enhance user experience and improve software development practices. While sentiment analysis has been extensively studied, the ability to detect and classify more nuanced emotional states remains underexplored \[[1](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/7d0531aaaa4dbf4033da964289843e7812262702)\]. Early efforts in sentiment analysis focused on binary or ternary classifications (e.g., positive, negative, neutral), but recent work has shifted toward more detailed emotion categorization \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/2412.07906)\]. For instance, the GoEmotions dataset, which includes 58,000 Reddit comments labeled with 27 emotion categories, demonstrates the feasibility of fine-grained emotion analysis \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/2005.00547)\]. This dataset was created through manual annotation and validated using statistical methods to ensure reliability \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/2005.00547)\].

Several studies have explored the challenges of emotion annotation, particularly in the context of social media and user-generated content. One such study highlights the limitations of manual annotation, including high costs, subjectivity, and the difficulty of maintaining consistent labeling across annotators \[[4](http://arxiv.org/abs/1703.02256)\]. To address these issues, researchers have proposed using large language models (LLMs) to assist with emotion annotation tasks. For example, GPT-4 has been shown to achieve high agreement with human annotators in emotion perception tasks, suggesting its potential as a tool for semi-automated annotation \[[5](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/6fe8d604e8e19dc6ed738c8e58b7351aeeed515c)\]. However, differences between human and LLM annotations persist, underscoring the importance of human input in annotation studies \[[5](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/6fe8d604e8e19dc6ed738c8e58b7351aeeed515c)\].

In the context of app reviews, several studies have investigated the emotional sentiment expressed by users. For example, one study analyzed over seven million Apple AppStore reviews and found that emotional sentiment can provide valuable insights for software engineers \[[6](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/33c95f4d13958e65e676a9cd64bd4d8df0fdb44d)\]. Another study proposed a framework for extracting features from app reviews and analyzing the sentiment associated with each feature, enabling developers to better understand user feedback \[[7](http://arxiv.org/abs/2107.12135)\]. This approach leverages topic modeling and sentiment analysis to provide a more granular understanding of user experiences \[[7](http://arxiv.org/abs/2107.12135)\].

The use of emotion definitions to improve fine-grained emotion prediction has also been explored. A recent paper introduced a multi-task learning framework that models emotion definitions as an auxiliary task, leading to improved performance on the GoEmotions dataset \[[8](http://arxiv.org/abs/2003.07723)\]. This approach demonstrates the potential of incorporating semantic knowledge into emotion classification models \[[8](http://arxiv.org/abs/2003.07723)\].

Additionally, the use of appraisal theories in emotion analysis has been investigated, with researchers proposing that appraisals—cognitive evaluations of events—can help explain the link between events and emotional responses \[[9](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/bbdeae12a2a5e760cfacfa91b5a183396d749b24)\]. This work suggests that emotional responses are not only influenced by the content of the text but also by the context in which it is perceived \[[9](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/bbdeae12a2a5e760cfacfa91b5a183396d749b24)\].

In summary, while sentiment analysis has been widely studied, the field of fine-grained emotion classification in app reviews remains an active area of research. Recent advances in annotation frameworks, LLMs, and emotion modeling have contributed to the development of more accurate and nuanced emotion detection systems \[[10](http://arxiv.org/abs/2206.05238)\].

### <a id='category-annotation-frameworks-and-datasets'></a>Annotation Frameworks and Datasets: 8 papers found.
The category "Annotation Frameworks and Datasets" highlights the ongoing efforts to develop robust datasets and annotation frameworks for emotion analysis, particularly in social media and app reviews. Researchers have identified the limitations of existing emotion lexicons and datasets, emphasizing the need for fine-grained emotion classification \[[16](http://arxiv.org/abs/1308.6297)\]. For instance, the GoEmotions dataset, consisting of 58k Reddit comments labeled for 27 emotion categories, demonstrates the potential of large-scale, manually annotated datasets in improving emotion detection \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/2005.00547)\]. Similarly, the SemEval 2016 dataset was extended with emotion annotations to address the lack of resources for social media domains \[[1](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/7d0531aaaa4dbf4033da964289843e7812262702)\]. The complexity of emotion annotation is further illustrated by the poetry-emotion dataset, which captures mixed and subtle emotions elicited in readers, requiring multi-label annotations per line \[[8](http://arxiv.org/abs/2003.07723)\]. Additionally, the Emotion Quantization Network (EQN) framework addresses challenges in micro-emotion detection by mapping label values to energy intensity levels, enabling more nuanced emotion annotation \[[20](http://arxiv.org/abs/2411.06160)\]. In the context of app reviews, studies have explored the impact of annotation guidelines on the quality of feature extraction, showing that refined guidelines can lead to more informative and less noisy results \[[12](http://arxiv.org/abs/1810.05187)\]. These efforts collectively underscore the importance of structured annotation frameworks and high-quality datasets in advancing emotion analysis, particularly for complex and context-dependent emotional expressions.

### <a id='category-large-language-models-in-emotion-annotation'></a>Large Language Models in Emotion Annotation: 2 papers found.
The use of large language models (LLMs) in emotion annotation has gained significant attention due to their potential to reduce the cost and effort associated with human annotation while maintaining reasonable alignment with human judgments. Studies have shown that models like GPT-4 can achieve high performance in emotion annotation tasks, often outperforming previous work that relied solely on human labels as ground truth \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/2412.07906)\]. This is particularly relevant in contexts such as mobile app review analysis, where user-generated content is often noisy, subjective, and low-quality, making manual annotation challenging \[[17](http://arxiv.org/abs/2408.01063)\]. Encoder-only models based on the Transformer architecture have also demonstrated promise in improving feature extraction from such data by leveraging pre-training on large corpora and fine-tuning with instance selection techniques to optimize performance \[[17](http://arxiv.org/abs/2408.01063)\]. However, while LLMs can significantly reduce manual effort and maintain substantial agreement with human annotators, full automation remains challenging due to the complexity of emotional interpretation \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/2412.07906)\]. These findings highlight the potential of LLMs as a complementary tool in emotion annotation pipelines, offering opportunities for new labeling practices that integrate both human and machine perspectives \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/2412.07906)\].

### <a id='category-fine-grained-emotion-classification'></a>Fine-Grained Emotion Classification: 4 papers found.
Fine-grained emotion classification has gained significant attention in recent research due to its applications in understanding nuanced emotional expressions in text. Several studies have focused on improving the accuracy and generalization of emotion prediction models. For instance, a multi-task learning framework has been proposed to model emotion definitions as an auxiliary task, enhancing performance on the primary emotion prediction task, with notable improvements on the GoEmotions dataset \[[7](http://arxiv.org/abs/2107.12135)\]. Additionally, the integration of fine-grained emotion labels into paraphrase generation tasks has shown promise, as it allows for the creation of paraphrases that maintain the original meaning while altering emotional intensities along affective dimensions, leading to significant improvements in paraphrase quality metrics \[[18](http://arxiv.org/abs/2212.03297)\]. Furthermore, the development of large-scale annotated datasets has been crucial for advancing the field, with one study creating a dataset with 24 fine-grained emotion types and achieving an average accuracy of 87.58%, while another extended the task to model Plutchik’s 8 primary emotion dimensions with superior accuracy \[[21](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/6a6f1b8ef3eefaf8027a76134859b44886a5f795)\]. Multilingual approaches have also been explored, utilizing machine translation and attention mechanisms to improve emotion recognition across languages, demonstrating performance improvements on datasets with 28 emotion categories \[[23](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/5f8a43da7bc3193ad480e3eae1fe357354b59d04)\]. These efforts collectively highlight the growing interest in fine-grained emotion classification and the various techniques being developed to enhance its accuracy and applicability.

### <a id='category-appraisal-theory-in-emotion-analysis'></a>Appraisal Theory in Emotion Analysis: 1 papers found.
Appraisal theory provides a cognitive framework for understanding how emotions are elicited by events, emphasizing the role of evaluative judgments in emotional responses \[[10](http://arxiv.org/abs/2206.05238)\]. This approach is particularly relevant to emotion analysis in text, as it allows for the identification of implicit emotional expressions through the analysis of appraisals—cognitive evaluations of events that are considered relevant to the individual experiencing them \[[10](http://arxiv.org/abs/2206.05238)\]. These appraisals, such as the assessment of novelty, responsibility, and goal alignment, serve as predictors of emotional outcomes, such as surprise or fear \[[10](http://arxiv.org/abs/2206.05238)\]. The integration of appraisal theory into computational models of emotion analysis offers an alternative paradigm that enhances the categorization of emotions by capturing the nuanced relationship between events and emotional responses \[[10](http://arxiv.org/abs/2206.05238)\]. This aligns with the broader goal of improving fine-grained emotion classification in domains like app reviews, where the complexity of emotional expression requires both human insight and automated methods \[[10](http://arxiv.org/abs/2206.05238)\].
