## Related Works

Federated Learning (FL) has emerged as a promising paradigm for collaborative machine learning while preserving data privacy by keeping raw data on client devices \[[15](http://arxiv.org/abs/2210.04505)\]. However, FL faces significant challenges due to data heterogeneity, which includes both statistical and system-level variations across clients \[[15](http://arxiv.org/abs/2210.04505)\]. This heterogeneity can lead to slow convergence and poor generalization of the global model \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2504.01921)\]. To address these issues, various client selection strategies have been proposed to improve convergence and performance in FL settings \[[13](http://arxiv.org/abs/2010.01243)\]. For instance, Power-of-Choice selection strategies have been introduced to bias client selection toward those with higher local loss, leading to faster convergence \[[13](http://arxiv.org/abs/2010.01243)\]. Similarly, HiCS-FL leverages statistical heterogeneity to cluster clients and improve convergence efficiency \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/2310.00198)\]. 

Active learning (AL) has also been explored in the context of FL to reduce annotation costs by selecting the most informative unlabeled samples \[[18](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/6b0d47501ad9efd386837959ea31cd3f58199797)\]. However, existing methods often fail to account for the heterogeneity of data distributions and the associated fluctuations in model parameters, which can degrade model accuracy \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2504.01921)\]. This limitation is particularly pronounced in federated active learning (FAL), where collaborative data selection and model training are required while preserving data privacy \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2504.01921)\]. 

To address these challenges, recent works have focused on improving client selection and model personalization in FL. For example, FedDiverse introduces a client selection algorithm that promotes collaboration between clients with complementary data distributions, enhancing performance while maintaining low computational overhead \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/2504.11216)\]. Similarly, WAFFLE uses weighted averaging based on client updates to personalize models, achieving faster convergence and improved accuracy \[[12](http://arxiv.org/abs/2110.06978)\]. 

In addition, methods such as FLAME have been proposed to handle both statistical and system heterogeneity in multi-device environments, improving inference consistency across devices \[[4](http://arxiv.org/abs/2202.08922)\]. These approaches highlight the importance of addressing both data and system-level heterogeneity in FL systems. 

Furthermore, methods like HHHFL have been developed to handle heterogeneous EEG data in FL settings, ensuring privacy while improving model performance \[[14](http://arxiv.org/abs/1909.05784)\]. These works demonstrate the need for robust client selection and model personalization strategies in FL, especially when dealing with non-IID data and heterogeneous client resources \[[15](http://arxiv.org/abs/2210.04505)\].

In the context of unlabeled data, some works have explored clustering-based labeling methods to enable federated learning without requiring explicit labels \[[18](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/6b0d47501ad9efd386837959ea31cd3f58199797)\]. These methods are particularly useful in IoT environments where labeling is impractical. 

In summary, while existing FL methods have made progress in addressing data heterogeneity and improving convergence, there remains a need for more robust and adaptive strategies that can handle both statistical and system-level variations in real-world settings. The proposed CHASe method aims to address these limitations by focusing on identifying high-epistemic variation samples and improving data selection efficiency through novel techniques \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2504.01921)\].

Client selection strategies in federated learning (FL) have been extensively studied to address challenges posed by data and communication heterogeneity \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2504.01921)\]. Traditional approaches often assume uniform client participation or ignore the impact of resource constraints on training efficiency \[[7](http://arxiv.org/abs/1804.08333)\]. However, recent research has focused on developing more sophisticated selection methods that account for both data distribution differences and varying client capabilities \[[13](http://arxiv.org/abs/2010.01243)\]. For instance, some studies have introduced biased selection strategies, such as favoring clients with higher local loss, to accelerate convergence \[[13](http://arxiv.org/abs/2010.01243)\]. Others have proposed methods like FedCS, which actively manage client participation based on resource conditions, leading to faster training times \[[7](http://arxiv.org/abs/1804.08333)\]. Additionally, there is growing interest in methods that enhance model robustness by ensuring diverse label representation across clients, such as FedEntOpt, which maximizes the entropy of the aggregated label distribution \[[16](http://arxiv.org/abs/2411.01240)\]. These advancements highlight the evolving landscape of client selection in FL, aiming to improve both performance and practical deployment in real-world scenarios.
