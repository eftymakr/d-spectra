## Related Works

Open-vocabulary object detection (OVD) has gained significant attention in recent years due to its ability to detect objects from arbitrary text descriptions, enabling flexible and user-driven detection \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2306.05493)\]. Traditional object detection methods are limited to a fixed set of classes, requiring extensive manual annotation for new categories \[[5](http://arxiv.org/abs/2011.10678)\]. To overcome this limitation, several approaches have been proposed that leverage vision-language models (VLMs) for zero-shot or open-vocabulary detection \[[11](http://arxiv.org/abs/2203.11876)\]. These methods typically rely on pre-trained models such as CLIP to align visual and textual representations, enabling detection of novel objects without additional training \[[15](http://arxiv.org/abs/2303.13076)\].

One line of work focuses on improving the alignment between visual and textual features using contrastive learning and knowledge distillation \[[19](http://arxiv.org/abs/2104.13921)\]. For instance, ViLD distills knowledge from a pre-trained open-vocabulary image classification model into a two-stage detector, aligning region embeddings with text and image embeddings \[[19](http://arxiv.org/abs/2104.13921)\]. Similarly, CORA adapts CLIP for open-vocabulary detection by introducing region prompting and anchor pre-matching, achieving state-of-the-art results on COCO and LVIS benchmarks \[[15](http://arxiv.org/abs/2303.13076)\]. These methods demonstrate that pre-trained VLMs can significantly enhance the performance of open-vocabulary detectors, even in the absence of explicit annotations for novel categories \[[15](http://arxiv.org/abs/2303.13076)\].

Another approach to OVD involves using pseudo labels generated from image-text pairs to train detectors on a larger set of categories \[[6](http://arxiv.org/abs/2111.09452)\]. For example, PromptDet introduces a two-stage open-vocabulary detector that uses a text encoder from a pre-trained VLM to classify object proposals \[[10](http://arxiv.org/abs/2203.16513)\]. This method enables zero manual annotations and improves detection accuracy for novel classes by leveraging the rich semantic understanding of VLMs \[[10](http://arxiv.org/abs/2203.16513)\]. Similarly, DST-Det employs a self-training strategy to identify potential novel classes through zero-shot classification, enhancing recall and accuracy without requiring extra annotations \[[14](http://arxiv.org/abs/2310.01393)\].

The challenge of aligning object proposals with textual descriptions has also been addressed through region-word alignment techniques \[[23](http://arxiv.org/abs/2310.16667)\]. CoDet reformulates region-word alignment as a co-occurring object discovery problem, leveraging visual similarities to discover co-occurring objects and align them with shared concepts \[[23](http://arxiv.org/abs/2310.16667)\]. This approach improves the scalability and performance of open-vocabulary detectors, particularly in scenarios with limited training data \[[23](http://arxiv.org/abs/2310.16667)\].

In addition to improving detection accuracy, recent work has focused on addressing the issue of visual-textual misalignment and long-tailed category imbalance \[[20](http://arxiv.org/abs/2502.16486)\]. MQADet introduces a plug-and-play paradigm that enhances existing open-vocabulary detectors by leveraging the cross-modal reasoning capabilities of multimodal large language models (MLLMs) \[[20](http://arxiv.org/abs/2502.16486)\]. This method allows detectors to accurately localize objects described by complex textual queries while refining the focus of existing detectors toward semantically relevant regions \[[20](http://arxiv.org/abs/2502.16486)\].

Finally, the problem of detecting objects in 3D space has also been explored in the context of open-vocabulary detection \[[21](http://arxiv.org/abs/2403.13556)\]. Find n' Propagate introduces a universal approach for 3D open-vocabulary object detection, maximizing the recall of novel objects and propagating this detection capability to more distant areas \[[21](http://arxiv.org/abs/2403.13556)\]. This method addresses the limitations of current LiDAR-based systems by leveraging pre-trained vision-language models and multi-sensor data \[[21](http://arxiv.org/abs/2403.13556)\].
