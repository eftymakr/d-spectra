## Related Works

Instruction-following text embedding has emerged as a critical area of research, aiming to generate dynamic text embeddings that adapt to user instructions. This task is particularly important in scenarios where embeddings must reflect specific attributes of text, such as semantic relevance, contextual meaning, or task-specific features \[[19](http://arxiv.org/abs/2303.10475)\]. However, existing approaches often suffer from significant computational overhead, as they require re-encoding the entire corpus for each new instruction \[[19](http://arxiv.org/abs/2303.10475)\]. This limitation has motivated the development of more efficient methods that can adapt pre-computed embeddings in real time \[[19](http://arxiv.org/abs/2303.10475)\].

Several works have explored instruction-tuned text embeddings. INSTRUCTOR introduces a method where text is embedded together with instructions explaining the use case, enabling a single embedder to generate embeddings tailored to different downstream tasks without further training \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2212.09741)\]. This approach is evaluated on 70 diverse datasets, achieving state-of-the-art performance with an average improvement of 3.4% over previous methods \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2212.09741)\]. Similarly, InBedder treats instructions as questions about the input text and encodes the expected answers to obtain the representation \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/2402.09642)\]. This method demonstrates improved instruction-following capabilities, particularly when applied to both large language models (LLMs) and smaller encoder-based models \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/2402.09642)\].

Another line of research focuses on the alignment of text and speech embeddings, aiming to bridge the gap between modalities \[[4](http://arxiv.org/abs/1805.07467)\]. While this work is not directly related to instruction-following, it highlights the importance of cross-modal alignment in generating meaningful embeddings \[[4](http://arxiv.org/abs/1805.07467)\]. Similarly, T-MASS proposes a stochastic text modeling method to enrich text embeddings with a flexible semantic range, enabling more accurate text-video retrieval \[[5](http://arxiv.org/abs/2403.17998)\]. This approach introduces a similarity-aware radius module and support text regularization to improve embedding quality \[[5](http://arxiv.org/abs/2403.17998)\].

Recent advances in instruction-following have also focused on improving the interpretability and robustness of models. For example, the InF-IR corpus is designed to enhance retrieval models in instruction-following tasks by providing over 38,000 expressive triplets as positive samples \[[7](http://arxiv.org/abs/2505.21439)\]. This approach enables efficient representation learning for smaller encoder-only models, facilitating direct embedding-based retrieval \[[7](http://arxiv.org/abs/2505.21439)\]. Additionally, UVLN introduces a multilingual vision-language navigation framework that improves the accessibility and intractability of instruction-following agents \[[8](http://arxiv.org/abs/2305.06358)\].

The challenge of instruction-following has also been addressed through pseudo-code representations of natural language instructions, which can improve model reliability \[[9](http://arxiv.org/abs/2505.18011)\]. This method fine-tunes LLMs using instruction-tuning data augmented with pseudo-code, achieving relative gains of 8-21% on instruction-following benchmarks \[[9](http://arxiv.org/abs/2505.18011)\]. Furthermore, GRIT introduces a unified approach for handling both generative and embedding tasks by distinguishing between them through instructions \[[10](http://arxiv.org/abs/2402.09906)\]. This method achieves state-of-the-art results on the Massive Text Embedding Benchmark (MTEB) and outperforms other models on a range of generative tasks \[[10](http://arxiv.org/abs/2402.09906)\].

In summary, while existing methods have made significant progress in instruction-following text embedding, they often suffer from computational inefficiencies or limited generalization. Our proposed GSTransform addresses these limitations by introducing a lightweight transformation mechanism that adapts pre-computed embeddings in real time, guided by a small amount of instruction-focused label annotation \[[19](http://arxiv.org/abs/2303.10475)\].
