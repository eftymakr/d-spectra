## Related Works

The rapid growth of open-source machine learning (ML) resources has significantly impacted information retrieval (IR) research, as these resources are increasingly used for tasks such as model evolution tracing and dataset recommendation \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2502.04484)\]. However, existing platforms like Hugging Face lack structured representations, which limits advanced querying and analysis \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2502.04484)\]. This section reviews related works that address similar challenges in ML resource management and knowledge graph construction.

Several studies have explored the challenges of documenting and managing ML models and datasets on Hugging Face. For instance, \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/2401.13822)\] conducted a large-scale analysis of dataset cards on Hugging Face and found that while some sections like "Dataset Description" are well-documented, others such as "Considerations for Using the Data" are underdeveloped. This highlights a need for more comprehensive documentation, which aligns with the goals of HuggingKG in capturing rich textual attributes \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2502.04484)\]. Similarly, \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/2311.13380)\] analyzed the evolution and maintenance of ML models on Hugging Face and found that model maintenance involves various types of commits, including corrective, perfective, and adaptive changes. This work underscores the importance of tracking model changes over time, a feature that HuggingKG supports through its extensive node and edge structure \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2502.04484)\].

The importance of structured representations for ML resources has also been emphasized in other studies. \[[4](http://arxiv.org/abs/2402.07323)\] investigated the carbon footprint of Hugging Face models and found that carbon emissions are correlated with model size and dataset size, highlighting the need for energy-efficient model development. This aligns with the goals of HuggingKG to enable more efficient resource management and analysis. \[[5](http://arxiv.org/abs/2305.11164)\] further explored the suitability of Hugging Face Hub for empirical studies and found that it offers sufficient features and data for such research, supporting the use of Hugging Face as a source for constructing knowledge graphs like HuggingKG \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2502.04484)\].

In addition, \[[6](http://arxiv.org/abs/2307.14841)\] examined the challenges of reusing pre-trained models (PTMs) on Hugging Face and identified common issues such as limited guidance for beginners and difficulties in understanding model outputs. These challenges are addressed by HuggingKG through its structured representation of models and datasets, enabling better traceability and recommendation \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2502.04484)\]. \[[7](http://arxiv.org/abs/2303.17580)\] conducted a systematic literature review on Hugging Face and found that documentation quality is strongly correlated with model popularity, reinforcing the importance of comprehensive documentation in HuggingKG \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2502.04484)\].

Finally, \[[8](http://arxiv.org/abs/2405.13058)\] explored the role of women in Hugging Face organizations and found that they are underrepresented, highlighting the need for more inclusive practices in open-source communities. This aligns with the broader goal of promoting transparency and fairness in AI development, which is supported by HuggingKG through its structured and accessible representation of ML resources \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2502.04484)\].
