## Related Works

Fund allocation in financial domains has attracted significant research interest due to its critical role in investment decision-making. Traditional approaches often rely on prediction-only or predict-then-optimize (PnO) strategies, which can lead to goal misalignment and suboptimal resource allocation \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2503.06928)\]. Furthermore, the integration of state-of-the-art time series forecasting models introduces additional uncertainty into the prediction process, complicating the decision-making pipeline \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/1809.04206)\]. To address these challenges, this paper introduces the Risk-aware Time-Series Predict-and-Allocate (RTS-PnO) framework, which is designed to align forecasting with allocation objectives while managing forecasting uncertainty \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/2308.11200)\].

Several studies have explored the use of advanced time series forecasting models for financial applications. For instance, FinTSBridge \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2503.06928)\] highlights the need for a bridge between cutting-edge forecasting models and financial asset pricing, emphasizing the importance of evaluating models using domain-specific metrics such as msIC and msIR. Similarly, the work on Temporal pattern attention for multivariate time series forecasting \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/1809.04206)\] introduces a novel attention mechanism to capture long-term dependencies, achieving state-of-the-art performance on real-world tasks. These models are often evaluated on financial datasets, such as those used in the RTS-PnO framework \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/2308.11200)\].

The integration of large language models (LLMs) into financial forecasting has also gained traction. For example, \[[4](http://arxiv.org/abs/2306.11025)\] demonstrates that LLMs can provide explainable forecasts by reasoning over textual news and price time series, leveraging cross-sequence information. This aligns with the RTS-PnO framework's goal of incorporating uncertainty-aware forecasting. Additionally, \[[5](http://arxiv.org/abs/2503.12648)\] proposes a multi-source transfer learning approach for forecasting financial volatility, particularly useful for assets with limited historical data, such as new issues or spin-offs. This approach is relevant to the RTS-PnO framework, as it addresses the challenge of forecasting under data scarcity.

Recent advances in deep learning have also led to the development of hybrid models that combine the strengths of different architectures. For instance, \[[6](http://arxiv.org/abs/2304.04912)\] proposes a model that integrates CNNs and Transformers to capture both short-term and long-term dependencies in financial time series. This is a key aspect of the RTS-PnO framework, which aims to improve forecasting accuracy while maintaining alignment with allocation goals. Similarly, \[[7](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/e11d20dbd5103c75c58a5f5f241f7df663aabb2d)\] introduces SegRNN, a model that reduces the number of recurrent iterations in long-term forecasting tasks, improving both accuracy and efficiency.

In addition to forecasting, the integration of uncertainty quantification has become increasingly important in financial applications. \[[8](http://arxiv.org/abs/1912.09363)\] introduces a probabilistic forecasting framework using recurrent neural networks with variational residual boosting, which allows for the estimation of confidence intervals. This is a crucial component of the RTS-PnO framework, which aims to provide risk-aware forecasts. Furthermore, \[[9](http://arxiv.org/abs/2412.09394)\] explores the use of LLMs for financial time series forecasting, demonstrating their ability to handle noisy and complex data, which is essential for the RTS-PnO framework.

The evaluation of forecasting models in financial contexts often involves benchmarking against traditional models such as ARMA-GARCH and gradient-boosting trees \[[10](http://arxiv.org/abs/2410.22377)\]. The RTS-PnO framework's performance is validated against these baselines, demonstrating its superiority in both offline and online experiments.

### <a id='category-time-series-forecasting-models-in-finance'></a>Time Series Forecasting Models in Finance: 6 papers found.
Recent advancements in time series forecasting models have focused on improving accuracy and adaptability in financial applications, with several studies addressing the challenges of modeling complex temporal dependencies and uncertainty \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2503.06928)\]. For instance, researchers have explored the use of recurrent neural networks (RNNs) with attention mechanisms to capture long-term dependencies in multivariate time series, achieving state-of-the-art performance in tasks such as electricity consumption and solar power prediction \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/1809.04206)\]. Additionally, spatio-temporal graph neural networks (GNNs) have gained attention for their ability to simultaneously model dependencies across variables and time points, offering a promising approach for time series classification and forecasting \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/2308.11200)\]. To enhance forecasting performance, some studies have proposed novel attention mechanisms that incorporate frequency domain information, enabling more accurate multivariate forecasting \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/1809.04206)\]. Furthermore, time series diffusion models have been introduced, though their performance remains unstable due to insufficient datasets and lack of guidance, prompting the development of retrieval-augmented models that leverage historical data to improve denoising \[[5](http://arxiv.org/abs/2503.12648)\]. In parallel, efficient Transformer-based models have been designed for multivariate time series forecasting, utilizing patch-based segmentation and channel-independent architectures to reduce computational complexity while improving long-term forecasting accuracy \[[4](http://arxiv.org/abs/2306.11025)\]. These models have also been applied to self-supervised pre-training tasks, demonstrating strong fine-tuning performance across diverse datasets \[[4](http://arxiv.org/abs/2306.11025)\]. Moreover, time series foundation models such as Chronos and Time-MoE have shown promise in household electricity load forecasting, offering zero-shot learning capabilities that reduce the need for domain-specific training \[[6](http://arxiv.org/abs/2304.04912)\]. Overall, these developments highlight the growing interest in integrating advanced forecasting models with financial applications, while addressing key challenges such as uncertainty calibration and model adaptability \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2503.06928)\].

### <a id='category-hybrid-deep-learning-architectures-for-financial-time-series'></a>Hybrid Deep Learning Architectures for Financial Time Series: 7 papers found.
In the context of hybrid deep learning architectures for financial time series, recent research has focused on combining the strengths of different models to improve forecasting accuracy and adaptability. For instance, traditional RNN-based methods have struggled with long-term time-series forecasting (LTSF) due to their high computational demands, prompting the development of SegRNN, which reduces recurrent iterations through segment-wise iterations and parallel multistep forecasting \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/2308.11200)\]. Similarly, CNNs excel at capturing local patterns but lack the ability to model long-term dependencies, while Transformers are effective at learning global context and long-term dependencies \[[6](http://arxiv.org/abs/2304.04912)\]. To address these limitations, hybrid models like the Temporal Fusion Transformer (TFT) have been introduced, integrating recurrent layers for local processing with self-attention mechanisms for long-term dependency learning \[[8](http://arxiv.org/abs/1912.09363)\]. Additionally, models such as GridTST and FFTNet have been proposed to handle multi-dimensional time series by combining time and variate-aware attention mechanisms or integrating frequency and time domain representations \[[16](http://arxiv.org/abs/2405.13810)\]\[[17](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/143da25b6b3215acc9909d9b43796cb9d24bdc49)\]. These approaches highlight the growing trend of leveraging hybrid architectures to enhance forecasting performance in financial applications, particularly in scenarios involving non-stationary and multivariate time series \[[14](http://arxiv.org/abs/2406.03710)\].
