## Related Works

The integration of knowledge graphs (KGs) with large language models (LLMs) to enhance reasoning capabilities has gained significant attention in recent years. While LLMs have demonstrated strong reasoning abilities, they often struggle with hallucinations and lack up-to-date knowledge, particularly in complex reasoning tasks \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2305.13168)\]. KGs, on the other hand, provide structured, semantically rich representations that are well-suited for reasoning \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/2410.20724)\]. However, most existing claim verification methods rely on unstructured text corpora, limiting their ability to effectively leverage KGs \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/2310.01061)\]. 

Several studies have explored the use of KGs to improve LLM reasoning. For instance, \[[4](http://arxiv.org/abs/2406.06621)\] introduced RoG, a method that synergizes LLMs with KGs to enable faithful and interpretable reasoning. RoG employs a planning-retrieval-reasoning framework, where the LLM generates relation paths grounded in KGs, and these paths are used to retrieve valid reasoning paths from the KGs for the LLM to conduct faithful reasoning. This approach improves the reliability and interpretability of LLM outputs \[[4](http://arxiv.org/abs/2406.06621)\]. Similarly, \[[5](http://arxiv.org/abs/2402.09911)\] proposed a framework that combines pseudo-graph generation and atomic knowledge verification to enhance open-ended question-answering by leveraging KGs. This method improves the accuracy and generalizability of LLMs across different KG sources \[[5](http://arxiv.org/abs/2402.09911)\].

Another line of work focuses on the integration of LLMs with KGs for question-answering tasks. For example, \[[6](http://arxiv.org/abs/2504.07087)\] introduced SubgraphRAG, which extends the KG-based RAG framework by retrieving subgraphs and leveraging LLMs for reasoning and answer prediction. This approach integrates a lightweight multilayer perceptron with a parallel triple-scoring mechanism to improve retrieval effectiveness and efficiency \[[6](http://arxiv.org/abs/2504.07087)\]. Additionally, \[[7](http://arxiv.org/abs/2502.12767)\] proposed R2-KG, a dual-agent framework that separates reasoning into two roles: an Operator (a low-capacity LLM) that gathers evidence and a Supervisor (a high-capacity LLM) that makes final judgments. This design enhances reliability while maintaining strong reasoning accuracy \[[7](http://arxiv.org/abs/2502.12767)\].

Furthermore, \[[8](http://arxiv.org/abs/2505.20099)\] introduced KG-CQAM, a novel approach that utilizes the chain of thought and mind maps as key prompts to dynamically reason over knowledge graphs while preserving graph structure information. This method enables LLMs to handle multi-hop complex questions without requiring any model training \[[8](http://arxiv.org/abs/2505.20099)\]. In addition, \[[9](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/3ccaf0e38d0f80e46a2c9317ad3e98dfc507b1e0)\] proposed Paths-over-Graph (PoG), a method that enhances LLM reasoning by integrating knowledge reasoning paths from KGs, improving the interpretability and faithfulness of LLM outputs. PoG tackles multi-hop and multi-entity questions through a three-phase dynamic multi-hop path exploration, combining the inherent knowledge of LLMs with factual knowledge from KGs \[[9](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/3ccaf0e38d0f80e46a2c9317ad3e98dfc507b1e0)\].

These works highlight the potential of integrating KGs with LLMs to improve reasoning accuracy and reliability. However, they also reveal challenges such as hallucinations, limited generalizability, and the need for adaptation to different KG structures. The proposed framework, ClaimPKG, addresses these challenges by seamlessly integrating LLM reasoning with structured knowledge from KGs, achieving state-of-the-art performance on benchmark datasets \[[10](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/45cd74606c8b561230ce9f116e80eac3c2ed7e3c)\].

These works collectively highlight the growing interest in leveraging KGs to enhance LLMs' reasoning capabilities, reduce hallucinations, and improve the reliability of knowledge-based inference.

### <a id='category-interpretability-and-faithfulness'></a>Interpretability and Faithfulness: 2 papers found.

Existing approaches often treat KGs as mere knowledge bases, neglecting their structural information, which is crucial for effective reasoning \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/2310.01061)\]. To address this, methods like RoG have been proposed, which combine LLMs with KGs through a planning-retrieval-reasoning framework, enabling faithful and interpretable reasoning by utilizing KGs' relational paths \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/2310.01061)\]. These developments underscore the growing interest in synergizing LLMs with KGs to enhance reasoning accuracy and transparency, while also addressing the challenges of user trust and system reliability.

Notably, they can approach these problems in a zero or few-shot manner, thereby eliminating the need for training or fine-tuning task-specific models. However, LLMs face some challenges, including hallucination and the presence of outdated knowledge or missing information from specific domains in the training data. These problems cannot be easily solved by retraining the models with new data as it is a time-consuming and expensive process. To mitigate these issues, Knowledge Graphs (KGs) have been proposed as a structured external source of information to enrich LLMs. With this idea, in this work we use KGs to enhance LLMs for zero-shot Entity Disambiguation (ED). For that purpose, we leverage the hierarchical representation of the entities' classes in a KG to gradually prune the candidate space as well as the entities' descriptions to enrich the input prompt with additional factual knowledge. Our evaluation on popular ED datasets shows that the proposed method outperforms non-enhanced and description-only enhanced LLMs, and has a higher degree of adaptability than task-specific models. Furthermore, we conduct an error analysis and discuss the impact of the leveraged KG's semantic expressivity on the ED performance.»

large language models (LLMs) is an emerging research challenge in claim
verification. While KGs provide structured, semantically rich representations
well-suited for reasoning, most existing verification methods rely on
unstructured text corpora, limiting their ability to effectively leverage KGs.
Additionally, despite possessing strong reasoning abilities, modern LLMs
struggle with multi-step modular pipelines and reasoning over KGs without
adaptation. To address these challenges, we propose ClaimPKG, an end-to-end
framework that seamlessly integrates LLM reasoning with structured knowledge
from KGs. Specifically, the main idea of ClaimPKG is to employ a lightweight,
specialized LLM to represent the input claim as pseudo-subgraphs, guiding a
dedicated subgraph retrieval module to identify relevant KG subgraphs. These
retrieved subgraphs are then processed by a general-purpose LLM to produce the
final verdict and justification. Extensive experiments on the FactKG dataset
demonstrate that ClaimPKG achieves state-of-the-art performance, outperforming
strong baselines in this research field by 9%-12% accuracy points across
multiple categories. Furthermore, ClaimPKG exhibits zero-shot generalizability
to unstructured datasets such as HoVer and FEVEROUS, effectively combining
structured knowledge from KGs with LLM reasoning across various LLM backbones.
