## Related Works

Cross-Domain Sequential Recommendation (CDSR) has attracted significant attention due to its ability to alleviate data sparsity and cold-start issues by leveraging sequential interactions across multiple domains \[[1](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/e33f51c13728bedeeb54a76b398949a381d6152f)\]. Traditional approaches often rely on overlapping users to transfer knowledge between domains, but this limits their applicability to real-world scenarios where such users are rare \[[5](http://arxiv.org/abs/2410.13588)\]. Moreover, these methods struggle to capture complex transition patterns from mixed behavior sequences \[[5](http://arxiv.org/abs/2410.13588)\]. Recent studies have explored the integration of Large Language Models (LLMs) into CDSR to address these limitations by leveraging their semantic reasoning capabilities \[[15](http://arxiv.org/abs/2209.10163)\].

Several works have attempted to enhance CDSR through the use of LLMs. For instance, \[[1](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/e33f51c13728bedeeb54a76b398949a381d6152f)\] proposes LLMCDSR, which employs LLMs to predict pseudo items within single-domain interactions and uses a Collaborative-Textual Contrastive Pre-Training strategy to infuse collaborative information into textual features. This approach aims to improve the reliability of LLM-generated recommendations by reducing hallucination and enhancing semantic alignment. Similarly, \[[19](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/7768e63d08be5df8ad7295b271d6bb11bc801cc4)\] introduces MIBR, which addresses the issue of negative transfer by capturing diverse user interests through a Multi-Interest Extraction (MIE) module and using a cross-domain bridging (CDB) module to align interests across domains. These methods highlight the potential of LLMs to improve the semantic understanding of user preferences in cross-domain settings.

Another line of research focuses on the integration of LLMs with traditional sequential recommendation models. For example, \[[20](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/6dd0605f7de7581c69b4acf0ab78000748a09f3e)\] proposes CD-ASR, which combines general and current user preferences to improve recommendation accuracy. The model uses a general attention mechanism to aggregate user information from multiple domains and a self-attention mechanism to capture current preferences in the target domain. This dual approach enables more accurate knowledge transfer from source to target domains. Additionally, \[[22](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/c13603992b90529918bd170e2f7a10858a340d43)\] introduces MARL4CDSR, a multi-agent reinforcement learning framework that selects relevant items from source domains to enhance recommendations in sparse target domains. This method optimizes the transfer process by coordinating agents' strategies and aligning embeddings between source and target domains.

Despite these advances, challenges remain. For instance, \[[18](http://arxiv.org/abs/2304.04218)\] highlights the limitations of existing CDSR methods in handling non-overlapping domains, where there are no shared users or items. The work proposes PLCR, an automated prompting-based framework that learns domain-invariant and domain-specific representations without requiring explicit domain alignment constraints. This approach is particularly useful for scenarios with sparse or cold-start users. Similarly, \[[21](http://arxiv.org/abs/2311.04590)\] introduces AMID, an Adaptive Multi-Interest Debiasing framework that addresses biases in cross-domain settings by considering both overlapping and non-overlapping users. The framework includes a multi-interest information module (MIM) and a doubly robust estimator (DRE) to improve recommendation accuracy under open-world assumptions.

In summary, while existing methods have made progress in CDSR, the integration of LLMs offers a promising direction for improving semantic understanding and knowledge transfer across domains. Future work should focus on addressing the challenges of non-overlapping domains and improving the robustness of LLM-based CDSR models.
