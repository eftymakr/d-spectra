## Related Works

Differential privacy (DP) has been widely recognized as the de facto standard for privacy-preserving data analysis \[[16](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/770ad70607ce60d8c411916129a423b5aad45e93)\]. However, its implementation in distributed settings is vulnerable to malicious behaviors by servers, such as sampling noise from incorrect distributions or generating correlated noise while appearing to follow established protocols \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2406.18940)\]. This issue has been explored in several works that focus on enhancing the robustness of DP mechanisms against adversarial attacks. For instance, \[[4](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/375e25dfc061ec12c2c870a2177d7c2abaa5f36e)\] analyzes output poisoning attacks on LDP protocols, where malicious users can manipulate their output before sending it to the aggregator. To counteract such attacks, \[[6](http://arxiv.org/abs/2104.06569)\] proposes verifiable LDP protocols that allow the aggregator to verify the perturbation process and prevent output poisoning. These protocols typically rely on zero-knowledge proofs (ZKPs), which, while effective, introduce significant computational and communication overheads \[[4](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/375e25dfc061ec12c2c870a2177d7c2abaa5f36e)\]. 

In response to these challenges, \[[15](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/25b7fe2ac81189952c72c5b869a6be1c5fcf410c)\] introduces a framework for verifiable LDP that allows the data collector to verify the completeness of the randomization process performed by each user. This approach provides a stronger guarantee against output manipulation attacks without requiring additional assumptions about the users' behavior. Similarly, \[[17](http://arxiv.org/abs/1909.01917)\] presents a system called Cryptε that leverages cryptographic techniques to ensure DP guarantees even in the presence of untrusted servers. This system allows for the execution of DP programs on encrypted data, thereby mitigating the risk of malicious server behavior. However, such approaches often come with performance limitations due to the complexity of cryptographic operations \[[18](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/ac8be9c934d6ff9ae095ebde81c8d3a57f7b918d)\].

The concept of verifiable differential privacy has also been explored in the context of the shuffle model, where the link between clients and the aggregator is severed to enhance privacy \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2406.18940)\]. \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2406.18940)\] proposes a scheme for verifiable LDP in the shuffle model, demonstrating that it is possible to achieve both high efficiency and strong privacy guarantees. This work highlights the importance of incorporating verification mechanisms into LDP protocols to ensure that the perturbation process is executed correctly. 

In addition to addressing output poisoning attacks, several works have focused on improving the utility of DP mechanisms while maintaining strong privacy guarantees. For example, \[[11](http://arxiv.org/abs/1907.00782)\] presents a framework for collecting and analyzing multidimensional data under LDP, proposing novel mechanisms that improve the accuracy of statistical estimation while preserving privacy. Similarly, \[[19](http://arxiv.org/abs/1905.06361)\] introduces a specialized form of LDP called Condensed Local Differential Privacy (CLDP) that is tailored for small user populations, where traditional LDP protocols perform poorly. These works demonstrate that there is ongoing research into enhancing the practicality of DP in real-world scenarios.

Finally, \[[20](http://arxiv.org/abs/1112.2680)\] proposes a relaxed privacy definition called Random Differential Privacy (RDP), which allows for more accurate statistical releases compared to traditional DP mechanisms. This approach is particularly useful in scenarios where high accuracy is required while maintaining strong privacy guarantees. These contributions highlight the evolving landscape of DP research, with a growing emphasis on robustness, efficiency, and practical deployment.
