## Related Works

Generative retrieval (GR) has gained significant attention in information retrieval (IR) as an alternative to traditional retrieval paradigms \[[1](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/5beaeee1e27007bd12967480bb9c379cebd15e6e)\]. Unlike conventional methods that rely on index-retrieve-then-rank pipelines, GR models directly generate document identifiers (docids) based on a query, enabling end-to-end optimization \[[1](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/5beaeee1e27007bd12967480bb9c379cebd15e6e)\]. However, most existing GR models are primarily evaluated on static document collections, with limited exploration of their performance in dynamic environments \[[1](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/5beaeee1e27007bd12967480bb9c379cebd15e6e)\]. This section reviews related works that address dynamic corpora, docid design, and continual learning in GR.

Several studies have explored the adaptability of GR models to dynamic corpora. For instance, \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/2305.18952)\] evaluated GR on the StreamingQA benchmark and found that GR models are more adaptable to evolving knowledge compared to Dual Encoders (DE), with improvements in inference efficiency and robustness to temporal information. Similarly, \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/2308.14968)\] introduced the CLEVER model, which enables continual learning for GR by incrementally indexing new documents while preserving retrieval performance on previously indexed documents. This work emphasizes the importance of efficient indexing and memory-augmented learning mechanisms for dynamic scenarios \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/2308.14968)\].

Docid design plays a critical role in GR performance. While numeric-based docids are often favored for their efficiency \[[1](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/5beaeee1e27007bd12967480bb9c379cebd15e6e)\], they tend to underperform in dynamic settings due to their tendency to overfit to the initial document set \[[1](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/5beaeee1e27007bd12967480bb9c379cebd15e6e)\]. This issue is attributed to the lack of semantic alignment with language models' pre-trained knowledge \[[1](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/5beaeee1e27007bd12967480bb9c379cebd15e6e)\]. In contrast, text-based docids, such as those generated through n-grams or titles, offer better generalization to unseen documents \[[1](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/5beaeee1e27007bd12967480bb9c379cebd15e6e)\]. \[[9](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/6682a3679a17d090830635f1c420126d20eae54a)\] proposed NOVO docids, which are learnable and interpretable, allowing for semantic optimization during training. These docids are generated through query denoising modeling and retrieval tasks, improving both semantic and token representations \[[9](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/6682a3679a17d090830635f1c420126d20eae54a)\]. Similarly, \[[10](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/ced090617c0cd46d4c3880f9e9c7c012acefc451)\] introduced D2-DocID, which combines descriptive and discriminative properties to enhance retrieval performance by distinguishing similar documents \[[10](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/ced090617c0cd46d4c3880f9e9c7c012acefc451)\].

Recent works have also focused on improving the efficiency and scalability of GR models. \[[13](http://arxiv.org/abs/2311.09134)\] proposed RIPOR, an optimization framework that improves the performance of generative retrieval on large-scale benchmarks by introducing a prefix-oriented ranking optimization algorithm and constructing docids based on relevance associations \[[13](http://arxiv.org/abs/2311.09134)\]. Additionally, \[[15](http://arxiv.org/abs/2304.04171)\] introduced GenRet, a document tokenization learning method that enables end-to-end modeling of GR by learning semantic docids through a discrete auto-encoding approach \[[15](http://arxiv.org/abs/2304.04171)\].

In summary, while GR has shown promise in static settings, its application to dynamic corpora remains an active research area. The choice of docid design significantly impacts performance, with text-based docids offering better generalization and semantic alignment \[[1](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/5beaeee1e27007bd12967480bb9c379cebd15e6e)\]. Future work should focus on developing efficient yet effective docid designs that balance performance and scalability \[[1](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/5beaeee1e27007bd12967480bb9c379cebd15e6e)\].
