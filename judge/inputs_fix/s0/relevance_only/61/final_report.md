Personalized outfit generation has garnered significant attention in recent years, driven by the increasing demand for tailored fashion recommendations. Early approaches focused on pre-defined outfit recommendations and personalized outfit composition, both of which were constrained by existing fashion products, limiting their ability to address diverse user needs \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/2402.17279)\]. More recently, the advent of AI-generated content has enabled the development of generative outfit recommendation (GOR) systems, which aim to generate visually compatible and personalized outfits \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/2402.17279)\]. These systems typically rely on deep learning techniques, such as convolutional networks and transformers, to model compatibility and personalization \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/2402.17279)\].

Several studies have explored the use of generative adversarial networks (GANs) for outfit generation. For instance, OutfitGAN introduces a compatibility network to ensure that generated items are compatible with one another \[[11](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/61c15a1723d210ac75824bf185b547cee37c70af)\]. The importance of personalization in fashion recommendation has also been addressed in the context of outfit generation. Similarly, the work by \[[19](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/c16dbfb1303c5dce657e29a84a12ee8a43caedcd)\] introduces a graph-based approach to model outfit compatibility, using a dot-attention graph neural network to capture visual relationships between items \[[19](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/c16dbfb1303c5dce657e29a84a12ee8a43caedcd)\].

Recent advances in deep learning have also enabled the development of more sophisticated models for outfit generation.
