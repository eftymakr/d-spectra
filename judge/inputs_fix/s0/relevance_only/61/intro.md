## Related Works

Personalized outfit generation has garnered significant attention in recent years, driven by the increasing demand for tailored fashion recommendations. Early approaches focused on pre-defined outfit recommendations and personalized outfit composition, both of which were constrained by existing fashion products, limiting their ability to address diverse user needs \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/2402.17279)\]. More recently, the advent of AI-generated content has enabled the development of generative outfit recommendation (GOR) systems, which aim to generate visually compatible and personalized outfits \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/2402.17279)\]. These systems typically rely on deep learning techniques, such as convolutional networks and transformers, to model compatibility and personalization \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/2402.17279)\].

Several studies have explored the use of generative adversarial networks (GANs) for outfit generation. For instance, OutfitGAN introduces a compatibility network to ensure that generated items are compatible with one another \[[11](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/61c15a1723d210ac75824bf185b547cee37c70af)\]. Similarly, the work by \[[1](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/ca3ad34ff2652cd922c3476514a758674bc7e76b)\] proposes a personalized fashion design framework using generative adversarial training, where a convolutional network maps the query image into a latent vector, and a generator network produces the target item image. This approach emphasizes compatibility and personalization, using two discriminators to guide the generation process \[[1](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/ca3ad34ff2652cd922c3476514a758674bc7e76b)\].

The importance of personalization in fashion recommendation has also been addressed in the context of outfit generation. POG, a model developed for Alibaba iFashion, connects user preferences with a Transformer architecture to generate personalized outfits \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/1905.01866)\]. This model leverages large-scale user data and demonstrates superior performance in terms of compatibility and personalization \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/1905.01866)\]. Similarly, the work by \[[19](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/c16dbfb1303c5dce657e29a84a12ee8a43caedcd)\] introduces a graph-based approach to model outfit compatibility, using a dot-attention graph neural network to capture visual relationships between items \[[19](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/c16dbfb1303c5dce657e29a84a12ee8a43caedcd)\].

Recent advances in deep learning have also enabled the development of more sophisticated models for outfit generation. For example, the work by \[[17](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/4d431b88f7dfe1e75b0e23bb21fc9dd378b15cf2)\] presents OutfitTransformer, which models outfits as unordered sets of items and uses self-attention mechanisms to learn compatibility \[[17](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/4d431b88f7dfe1e75b0e23bb21fc9dd378b15cf2)\]. This approach improves compatibility prediction and complementary item retrieval, outperforming state-of-the-art methods \[[17](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/4d431b88f7dfe1e75b0e23bb21fc9dd378b15cf2)\]. Additionally, the use of diffusion models has been explored for generating high-fidelity outfits, as seen in the DiFashion model, which uses classifier-free guidance to enhance alignment between generated images and conditions \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/2402.17279)\].

The challenge of aligning generated content with human preferences has also been addressed in the context of 3D generation, where DreamDPO introduces a direct preference optimization framework that integrates human preferences into the generation process \[[5](http://arxiv.org/abs/2502.04370)\]. This approach reduces reliance on precise pointwise quality evaluations while enabling fine-grained controllability through preference-guided optimization \[[5](http://arxiv.org/abs/2502.04370)\].

In summary, the field of personalized outfit generation has evolved from pre-defined recommendations to more flexible and diverse generation methods, leveraging deep learning and generative models. These approaches emphasize compatibility, personalization, and the integration of user preferences, as demonstrated by various studies \[[1](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/ca3ad34ff2652cd922c3476514a758674bc7e76b)\]\[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/1905.01866)\]\[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/2402.17279)\]\[[5](http://arxiv.org/abs/2502.04370)\]\[[11](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/61c15a1723d210ac75824bf185b547cee37c70af)\]\[[17](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/4d431b88f7dfe1e75b0e23bb21fc9dd378b15cf2)\]\[[19](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/c16dbfb1303c5dce657e29a84a12ee8a43caedcd)\].
