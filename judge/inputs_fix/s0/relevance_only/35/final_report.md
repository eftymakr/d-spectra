## Related Works

Protein dynamics are essential for understanding biological functions and properties, yet traditional methods such as molecular dynamics (MD) simulations are computationally expensive and time-consuming \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2402.04845)\]. Recent advances in generative modeling have introduced more efficient approaches for protein structure prediction and conformation sampling \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/2004.13167)\]. Denoising diffusion models, in particular, have shown promise in learning distributions over crystallographic structures, enabling accurate and efficient conformational sampling \[[3](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/8fa4f6b5232868c2e400670716540f6877068439)\]. However, integrating physical supervision into these data-driven models remains a challenge, as standard energy-based objectives often lead to intractable optimization \[[4](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/5d2b69b7ba59017fbf7fed60ffe85165b3207e57)\].

Several studies have explored the use of energy-based models for protein conformation prediction. For instance, energy-based models (EBMs) trained on crystallized protein data have been shown to achieve comparable performance to the Rosetta energy function, a widely used method in protein structure prediction and design \[[5](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/21a4afa74caf78e28880b7fe4c30a09a5f9c14e3)\]. These models capture physicochemical properties relevant to protein energy, enabling accurate conformational recovery \[[6](http://arxiv.org/abs/2411.17196)\]. Additionally, the integration of physical principles into deep learning models has been explored through methods such as SE(3) flow matching, which incorporates physical laws governing the distribution of conformational ensembles \[[7](http://arxiv.org/abs/2411.08154)\]. This approach enhances the accuracy of generated structures by aligning them with known physical properties \[[8](http://arxiv.org/abs/2402.10433)\].

The use of diffusion models for generating protein conformations has also gained traction. For example, the Angular Deviation Diffuser, a transformer-based diffusion model, generates diverse conformational ensembles of protein backbones by utilizing angular deviations as data flow \[[9](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/43389c0e79b5db0b210bef998f867910db9d8d44)\]. This method improves the efficiency of conformational sampling while maintaining accuracy \[[10](http://arxiv.org/abs/2503.14569)\]. Similarly, the 4D Diffusion model incorporates molecular dynamics (MD) simulation data to learn dynamic protein structures, generating both backbone and side-chain conformations \[[11](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/bcf4f0cd55f0888b80c2bf7d47969aa34b222a99)\]. This approach enables the prediction of protein trajectories across multiple time steps, capturing both local flexibility and significant conformational changes \[[12](http://arxiv.org/abs/2205.15019)\].

Recent work has also focused on the integration of physical simulations with generative models to improve the accuracy of conformational sampling. For example, the P2DFlow model, based on SE(3) flow matching, generates high-quality protein ensembles by incorporating physical laws governing conformational distributions \[[13](http://arxiv.org/abs/2108.04227)\]. This method outperforms other baseline models in capturing observable dynamic fluctuations, as evidenced by crystal structure and MD simulations \[[14](http://arxiv.org/abs/2403.14088)\]. Additionally, the use of force-guided SE(3) diffusion models, such as ConfDiff, has been proposed to incorporate physical prior knowledge, resulting in more accurate and diverse conformational sampling \[[15](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/f5082a31aa5ccf39ab11a4515972d3493b757bbb)\].

In summary, while generative models have made significant strides in protein conformation prediction, integrating physical supervision remains a critical challenge. The proposed Energy-based Alignment (EBA) method addresses this by aligning generative models with feedback from physical models, improving the physical plausibility of generated structures \[[16](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/934892bbb74d9cac69061977f5937eba6fd8da52)\].
