## Related Works

Table discovery has emerged as a critical area of research in database systems, driven by the increasing complexity and diversity of tabular data in modern information systems. Early efforts in this domain focused on improving semantic connections between unrelated tables, often through concept-driven frameworks that aimed to enhance the semantic capabilities of database systems \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/1208.4016)\]. These approaches sought to bridge the gap between syntactically unrelated tables by leveraging semantic relationships, which was a significant step toward more meaningful data retrieval \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/1208.4016)\].

Recent advancements have shifted the focus toward deep learning methods for table detection, particularly in the context of document layout analysis. The introduction of large-scale, diverse datasets has enabled the development of more robust and scalable solutions. For instance, the STDW dataset, containing over 7,000 samples with varied table structures, has been instrumental in evaluating and improving convolutional neural network-based methods for table detection \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/2209.09207)\]. This dataset addresses previous limitations such as lack of diversity and poor sample quality, thereby enabling more accurate and generalizable models \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/2209.09207)\].

In parallel, the field of TableQA has also seen significant contributions, particularly in addressing the challenges posed by large language models (LLMs). The HiddenTables framework introduces a cooperative game between an LLM "Solver" and an "Oracle" to evaluate and improve the performance of LLMs in table question-answering tasks \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/2406.10803)\]. This approach not only enhances the ability of LLMs to handle complex queries but also ensures data privacy and security during the process \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/2406.10803)\]. The associated PyQTax dataset, containing 116,671 question-table-answer triplets, further supports this effort by providing a rich source of annotated data for training and evaluation \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/2406.10803)\].

The proposed nlcTD task represents a novel direction in table discovery, combining natural language conditions with query tables to refine search results . This task addresses the limitations of existing methods that rely solely on query tables or vague keywords, often leading to large result sets requiring manual filtering . The nlcTables dataset, with its diverse set of 627 queries and 22,200 candidate tables, serves as a comprehensive benchmark for evaluating the performance of new methods in this domain . The evaluation of six state-of-the-art methods on nlcTables highlights the substantial performance gaps that exist, underscoring the need for more advanced techniques .

These works collectively highlight the evolving landscape of table discovery and related tasks, emphasizing the importance of semantic understanding, data diversity, and model efficiency. Future research is likely to focus on integrating these advancements into more unified and scalable solutions.
