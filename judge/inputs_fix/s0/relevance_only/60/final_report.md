## Related Works

Ranking models traditionally focus on modeling the relative order of predictions, often at the expense of the accuracy of their absolute values \[[8](http://arxiv.org/abs/2406.08010)\]. This limitation has significant implications for downstream tasks that require calibrated predictions, such as advertising systems where the predicted click-through rate (CTR) must be accurately calibrated for effective bidding \[[3](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/7f6bb146b9c1504ec77100e640d624bdc204f0ac)\]. Existing calibration approaches typically employ predefined transformation functions with order-preserving properties to adjust the original predictions \[[8](http://arxiv.org/abs/2406.08010)\]. However, these functions often adhere to fixed forms, such as piece-wise linear functions, which exhibit limited expressiveness and flexibility \[[8](http://arxiv.org/abs/2406.08010)\]. This constraint limits their effectiveness in complex calibration scenarios \[[8](http://arxiv.org/abs/2406.08010)\].

To address these limitations, several studies have explored alternative calibration methods. For example, \[[6](http://arxiv.org/abs/1904.01685)\] discusses the use of focal loss to improve calibration in deep neural networks, demonstrating that it can yield state-of-the-art calibrated models while preserving accuracy. Similarly, \[[10](http://arxiv.org/abs/2012.07923)\] introduces an optimization method that leverages the relationship between accuracy and uncertainty to improve calibration. This approach introduces a differentiable accuracy versus uncertainty (AvUC) loss function, which allows models to learn well-calibrated uncertainties in addition to improved accuracy. These methods, however, rely on predefined transformation functions or fixed loss structures, which may not be flexible enough for complex calibration tasks \[[8](http://arxiv.org/abs/2406.08010)\].

The use of neural networks for calibration has also been explored. \[[14](http://arxiv.org/abs/2006.12800)\] introduces a spline-based recalibration method that approximates the empirical cumulative distribution using a differentiable function, resulting in a recalibration function that maps network outputs to calibrated class probabilities. This approach is evaluated on various image classification datasets and consistently outperforms existing methods on calibration measures such as the Kolmogorov-Smirnov (KS) error \[[14](http://arxiv.org/abs/2006.12800)\]. However, these methods still rely on fixed functional forms or limited architectural flexibility \[[8](http://arxiv.org/abs/2406.08010)\].

The proposed approach in this work introduces the use of an Unconstrained Monotonic Neural Network (UMNN) as a calibrator, which can learn arbitrary monotonic functions with great modeling power \[[8](http://arxiv.org/abs/2406.08010)\]. This approach significantly relaxes the constraints on the calibrator, improving its flexibility and expressiveness while avoiding excessive distortion of the original predictions by requiring monotonicity \[[8](http://arxiv.org/abs/2406.08010)\]. Additionally, a novel loss function termed Smooth Calibration Loss (SCLoss) is introduced to optimize the highly flexible network for calibration \[[8](http://arxiv.org/abs/2406.08010)\]. This method has been validated through extensive offline experiments and deployed in a large-scale online video ranking system, demonstrating its effectiveness in improving calibration and translating into enhanced business metrics \[[8](http://arxiv.org/abs/2406.08010)\].

In summary, while existing calibration methods have made significant strides, they often rely on predefined transformation functions or limited architectural flexibility, which may not be sufficient for complex calibration tasks. The proposed approach addresses these limitations by introducing a flexible and expressive calibrator based on UMNN, along with a novel loss function to optimize calibration performance \[[8](http://arxiv.org/abs/2406.08010)\].
