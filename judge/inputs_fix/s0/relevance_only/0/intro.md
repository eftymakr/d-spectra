## Related Works

Economic inequality has been a focal point for researchers and policymakers, with various models and frameworks proposed to address its complexities. Traditional economic systems, such as the U.S. federal income tax, aim to reduce inequality but often lack adaptability to heterogeneous taxpayer behavior and irrational decision-making \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2311.17252)\]. The Saez Optimal Taxation model provides a dynamic approach to tax policy but struggles to account for the diversity of taxpayer characteristics and behavioral inconsistencies \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2311.17252)\]. This study introduces TaxAgent, a novel integration of large language models (LLMs) with agent-based modeling (ABM) to design adaptive tax policies that better capture real-world complexities \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2311.17252)\].

Agent-based modeling (ABM) has gained traction as a powerful tool for simulating complex economic systems, offering insights into emergent behaviors and interactions among diverse agents \[[5](http://arxiv.org/abs/2312.11970)\]. Research has shown that ABMs can effectively model economic inequality, demonstrating how simple behavioral rules and local interactions can lead to complex macro-level patterns \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/1804.09284)\]. For instance, studies have explored how Islamic charity (sadaqah) can alleviate economic inequality by influencing resource allocation strategies \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/1804.09284)\]. Similarly, agent-based models have been used to study tax evasion dynamics, revealing how social norms and enforcement mechanisms shape taxpayer behavior \[[9](http://arxiv.org/abs/1112.0233)\].

Reinforcement learning (RL) has also been integrated into ABMs to enhance agent decision-making and policy evaluation. RL models have been shown to outperform traditional heuristic or regression-based behavioral models, particularly in policy-relevant scenarios such as influenza transmission and minority games \[[6](http://arxiv.org/abs/2006.05048)\]. Furthermore, the integration of LLMs into ABMs has been explored as a means to improve simulation capabilities, enabling more realistic and adaptive agent behaviors \[[5](http://arxiv.org/abs/2312.11970)\]. This aligns with the goals of TaxAgent, which leverages LLMs to iteratively optimize tax policies based on real-time data and agent interactions \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2311.17252)\].

The use of ABMs in economic policy analysis has also been extended to include heterogeneous agents, such as households, firms, and governments, to better reflect real-world economic systems \[[7](http://arxiv.org/abs/2402.09563)\]. These models have demonstrated the ability to generate simulated data that aligns with stylized economic facts, providing a more accurate representation of macroeconomic and microeconomic dynamics \[[7](http://arxiv.org/abs/2402.09563)\]. Additionally, the development of multi-level ABMs has enabled the simulation of complex socio-environmental systems, incorporating multiple levels of analysis and interaction \[[14](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/3377113a7cbe704e8bf99a0327462d1da378ff0c)\].

In the context of tax policy, recent studies have highlighted the importance of adaptive and data-driven approaches to address the limitations of traditional models. The integration of LLMs with ABMs represents a promising direction, as it allows for the simulation of heterogeneous taxpayer behaviors and the iterative optimization of tax policies \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2311.17252)\]. This approach is supported by research demonstrating the effectiveness of RL-based ABMs in capturing complex economic behaviors and generating policy insights \[[6](http://arxiv.org/abs/2006.05048)\].
