## Related Works

Long-tailed recognition remains a challenging problem in computer vision due to the significant imbalance in class distribution, where a few dominant classes (head classes) contain the majority of the data, while the remaining classes (tail classes) are severely underrepresented \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2403.06151)\]. This imbalance leads to biased feature learning and poor generalization performance on tail classes \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/2306.15925)\]. Several approaches have been proposed to address this issue, primarily focusing on data augmentation, loss function design, and contrastive learning strategies.

Contrastive learning has emerged as a powerful technique for representation learning, particularly in visual tasks. Supervised Contrastive Loss (SCL) is widely used, as it pulls positive samples (augmented versions of the anchor and other same-class images) together while pushing negative samples apart \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/2309.11177)\]. However, in long-tailed recognition, SCL suffers from imbalanced intra-category distances and ignores the semantic relationships among negative samples \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/2309.11177)\]. To address these issues, decoupled contrastive learning has been proposed, which separates the optimization of positive samples to alleviate the bias caused by class imbalance \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2403.06151)\]. Additionally, subclass-balancing contrastive learning (SBCL) has been introduced to cluster head classes into subclasses, enforcing a two-layer class hierarchy to better capture semantic structures \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/2306.15925)\].

To improve the generalization of models on tail classes, methods such as patch-based self-distillation have been proposed to transfer knowledge from head to tail classes \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2403.06151)\]. Similarly, saliency masked contrastive learning (SMCL) uses saliency detection to mask important parts of images and encourages contrastive learning to move masked images toward minor classes, reducing the correlation between background features and class predictions \[[6](http://arxiv.org/abs/2406.02223)\]. Targeted supervised contrastive learning (TSC) further improves feature uniformity by generating uniformly distributed targets on a hypersphere, ensuring that all classes, including minority classes, maintain a consistent distribution in the feature space \[[8](http://arxiv.org/abs/2111.13998)\].

Recent works have also explored the integration of contrastive learning with other techniques, such as graph-based methods. Long-tail augmented graph contrastive learning (LAGCL) addresses the data sparsity issue in recommendation systems by introducing a learnable long-tail augmentation approach and using auto-drop and knowledge transfer modules to improve representation learning \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/2309.11177)\]. In addition, probabilistic contrastive learning (ProCo) estimates the data distribution of each class in the feature space and samples contrastive pairs accordingly, allowing for efficient optimization even with small batches \[[17](http://arxiv.org/abs/2403.06726)\].

In summary, while contrastive learning has shown promise in addressing long-tailed recognition, several challenges remain, including the need for more effective handling of class imbalance, better utilization of semantic information, and improved generalization on tail classes. The proposed Aligned Contrastive Learning (ACL) algorithm aims to overcome these limitations by eliminating gradient conflicts and imbalanced attraction and repulsion gradients between positive and negative pairs \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2403.06151)\].

These limitations have prompted researchers to propose modifications to the training objective, such as decoupling the treatment of positive samples and optimizing their relationships toward distinct goals \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2403.06151)\]. ### <a id='category-long-tailed-recognition-challenges'></a>Long-Tailed Recognition Challenges: 10 papers found.
Long-tailed recognition remains a significant challenge in deep learning due to the imbalance in class distribution, where head classes dominate the dataset while tail classes have limited samples \[[13](http://arxiv.org/abs/2309.16135)\].
