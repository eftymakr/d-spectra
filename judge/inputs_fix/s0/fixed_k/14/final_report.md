## Related Works

Diffusion-based video super-resolution (VSR) has seen significant advancements, with recent works focusing on addressing the inherent randomness and artifacts that plague high-resolution video generation. Early approaches, such as those based on standard diffusion models, often struggle with temporal consistency and degradation modeling \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2410.18137)\]. For instance, the work by \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2410.18137)\] highlights the challenges of applying diffusion models directly to video restoration due to the introduction of artifacts and inconsistencies. This issue is further exacerbated by the lack of temporal coherence in the generated frames, which can lead to visually unappealing results.

To mitigate these issues, several studies have introduced mechanisms for improving temporal consistency. The TDM framework \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/2501.02269)\] proposes a diffusion-based all-in-one video restoration method that leverages pre-trained Stable Diffusion and a fine-tuned ControlNet. This approach enables restoration of various video degradations with a single model, enhancing both content preservation and temporal consistency. Similarly, the VRT framework \[[5](http://arxiv.org/abs/2201.12288)\] introduces a Video Restoration Transformer (VRT) that enables parallel frame prediction, addressing the limitations of frame-by-frame restoration strategies. The VRT model uses temporal reciprocal self attention (TRSA) and parallel warping to improve the quality of video restoration across multiple tasks.

Another key contribution in the field is the introduction of self-supervised learning strategies for video super-resolution. The Self-Supervised Adaptation for Video Super-Resolution \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/2103.10081)\] presents a learning algorithm that allows conventional video super-resolution (VSR) networks to adapt to test video frames without requiring ground-truth datasets. This approach improves the performance of fully pre-trained VSR networks and produces temporally consistent video frames. Additionally, the Self-Supervised Deep Blind Video Super-Resolution \[[6](http://arxiv.org/abs/2201.07422)\] proposes a self-supervised learning method that simultaneously estimates blur kernels and high-resolution videos from low-resolution inputs, overcoming the limitations of supervised learning in real-world scenarios.

To further enhance temporal consistency, the Upscale-A-Video framework \[[9](http://arxiv.org/abs/2312.06640)\] introduces a text-guided latent diffusion model that ensures temporal coherence through locally integrated temporal layers and a flow-guided recurrent latent propagation module. This approach allows for greater flexibility in balancing restoration and generation, enabling a trade-off between fidelity and quality. Similarly, the DiffIR2VR-Zero framework \[[13](http://arxiv.org/abs/2407.01519)\] enables zero-shot video restoration using pre-trained image diffusion models, maintaining high-quality restoration while addressing temporal inconsistencies through hierarchical latent warping and hybrid token merging.

In addition to these advancements, the Zooming SlowMo framework \[[7](http://arxiv.org/abs/2104.07473)\] presents an efficient one-stage space-time video super-resolution approach that directly reconstructs high-resolution slow-motion video from low-resolution and low-frame-rate inputs. This method avoids the limitations of two-stage approaches by integrating feature temporal interpolation and maintaining consistency across frames. Furthermore, the Self-Supervised Learning for Real-World Super-Resolution \[[11](http://arxiv.org/abs/2405.02171)\] explores self-supervised learning from dual and multiple zoomed observations, leveraging the natural alignment between telephoto and ultra-wide images to improve the quality of real-world super-resolution.

These works collectively highlight the ongoing efforts to improve the quality, consistency, and efficiency of diffusion-based video super-resolution methods, laying the groundwork for the proposed approach.
