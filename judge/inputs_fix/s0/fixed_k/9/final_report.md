## Related Works

Quantitative susceptibility mapping (QSM) has emerged as a powerful tool for characterizing pathologies in multiple sclerosis (MS), particularly for distinguishing between rim and non-rim lesions \[[20](http://arxiv.org/abs/2211.04764)\]. Rim lesions, surrounded by paramagnetic iron deposits, are an emerging biomarker for MS and are associated with increased disability \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/2312.16835)\]. However, manual identification and segmentation of rim lesions on QSM images are time-consuming and prone to inter-rater variability \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2412.10492)\]. Deep learning approaches have been proposed to automate this process, but existing methods often lack the ability to provide accurate rim segmentation for quantitative analysis \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2412.10492)\]. 

Several studies have focused on improving lesion segmentation in MS using deep learning techniques. For example, QSM-RimNet was introduced to detect PRLs but lacks rim segmentation capabilities \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2412.10492)\]. In contrast, QSM-RimDS was developed as a U-Net-based method that provides joint PRL detection and rim segmentation using T2-weighted FLAIR lesion masks \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2412.10492)\]. This method demonstrated a mean Dice similarity coefficient (DSC) of 0.57 with moderate to high agreement against expert segmentation \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2412.10492)\]. Additionally, QSM-RimDS showed improved detection performance with a mean area under the curve (AUC) of 0.754, surpassing QSM-RimNet's AUC of 0.514 \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2412.10492)\]. 

Beyond lesion detection, several methods have focused on lesion segmentation using convolutional neural networks (CNNs). For instance, a fully convolutional neural network (CNN) was proposed for segmenting white matter lesions from multi-contrast MR images, achieving a Dice score of 90.48 on the ISBI 2015 challenge dataset \[[5](http://arxiv.org/abs/1803.09172)\]. Similarly, a multi-branch CNN was introduced to handle multi-modal brain MRI data, showing high improvements in lesion segmentation compared to other publicly available tools \[[22](http://arxiv.org/abs/1811.02942)\]. 

Lesion activity segmentation, which involves detecting new and enlarging lesions between two time points, has also been addressed using deep learning. A two-path CNN architecture with attention-guided interactions was proposed to improve lesion activity segmentation, achieving a lesion-wise false positive rate of 26.4% at a true positive rate of 74.2% \[[4](http://arxiv.org/abs/2008.02001)\]. Additionally, a 4D deep learning approach was introduced to process a history of MRI volumes, demonstrating improved performance in lesion activity segmentation \[[18](http://arxiv.org/abs/2004.09216)\]. 

To address the challenge of class imbalance in rim lesion detection, synthetic QSM maps were generated using a generative adversarial network (GAN) to augment the minority class \[[29](http://arxiv.org/abs/1702.04869)\]. This approach improved classifier performance and enabled the generation of accompanying contrasts and probabilistic segmentation maps \[[29](http://arxiv.org/abs/1702.04869)\]. Furthermore, a denoising method was proposed to train on ambiguous rim cases, substantially increasing the minority class representation \[[29](http://arxiv.org/abs/1702.04869)\]. 

In summary, while deep learning methods have made significant strides in MS lesion segmentation, challenges remain in accurately detecting and segmenting rare rim lesions. The proposed method addresses these challenges by generating synthetic QSM maps and applying denoising techniques to improve classifier performance \[[29](http://arxiv.org/abs/1702.04869)\].

### <a id='category-multi-modal-and-multi-contrast-mri-analysis'></a>Multi-Modal and Multi-Contrast MRI Analysis: 4 papers found.
In the context of multi-modal and multi-contrast MRI analysis, recent studies have focused on improving the accuracy and robustness of lesion segmentation and susceptibility mapping in multiple sclerosis (MS). For instance, advanced techniques such as Quantitative Susceptibility Mapping (QSM) have been employed to detect pathologies by analyzing variations in magnetic susceptibility, particularly in identifying paramagnetic rim lesions (PRLs) that are critical for monitoring disease progression \[[8](http://arxiv.org/abs/2404.19309)\]. However, high-resolution QSM often faces challenges such as reduced signal-to-noise ratio (SNR), which can compromise diagnostic quality \[[8](http://arxiv.org/abs/2404.19309)\]. To address this, denoising techniques like Marchenko-Pastur Principal Component Analysis (MP-PCA) have been introduced to enhance the quality of QSM maps while maintaining clinical scan times \[[8](http://arxiv.org/abs/2404.19309)\]. Additionally, methods integrating generative models for white matter lesion segmentation have been developed, allowing for adaptation to different imaging protocols without retraining \[[19](http://arxiv.org/abs/2005.05135)\]. These models leverage separate representations of anatomical structures and their appearance in MRI, enhancing robustness across datasets \[[19](http://arxiv.org/abs/2005.05135)\]. Furthermore, Bayesian spatial models have been proposed to account for the binary nature of lesion maps and spatial dependencies, offering improved modeling and predictive capabilities over traditional methods \[[25](http://arxiv.org/abs/1407.8406)\]. In parallel, deep neural networks have been utilized for lesion segmentation, with innovations such as sequence dropout enabling robust performance even when specific MRI sequences are missing \[[26](http://arxiv.org/abs/1811.07491)\]. These approaches collectively highlight the ongoing efforts to enhance the accuracy and adaptability of MRI-based diagnostics in MS, leveraging multi-contrast data and advanced computational techniques.
