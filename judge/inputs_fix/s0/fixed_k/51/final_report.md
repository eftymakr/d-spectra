## Related Works

Federated unlearning has emerged as a critical research area in the context of privacy-preserving machine learning, particularly in response to the "right to be forgotten" regulation \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2412.19069)\]. This concept allows users to request the removal of their data from the global model trained across distributed clients, without requiring full retraining \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/2401.05146)\]. Federated Learning (FL) enables collaborative training of models across multiple parties while preserving data privacy by keeping raw data local \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/2310.20448)\]. However, the challenge lies in efficiently and securely unlearning the impact of specific clients or data points from the global model \[[4](http://arxiv.org/abs/2310.19218)\].

Several approaches have been proposed to address federated unlearning. For instance, FedEraser \[[5](http://arxiv.org/abs/2406.03078)\] was the first method to enable efficient data removal from FL models by reconstructing the unlearned model using historical parameter updates stored at the central server. This method significantly reduces the time required to construct the unlearned model compared to retraining from scratch \[[5](http://arxiv.org/abs/2406.03078)\]. Similarly, the work in \[[6](http://arxiv.org/abs/2403.02437)\] introduced a framework for verifiable federated unlearning, where the leaving participant can verify the unlearning effect through a combination of marking and checking steps \[[6](http://arxiv.org/abs/2403.02437)\]. This ensures that the unlearning process is transparent and verifiable, which is crucial for regulatory compliance \[[6](http://arxiv.org/abs/2403.02437)\].

Another significant contribution is the development of unlearning strategies that are both efficient and secure. For example, the paper \[[7](http://arxiv.org/abs/2308.12502)\] proposed a method that allows the server to unlearn a target client without requiring client communication or additional storage, thereby improving efficiency and security \[[7](http://arxiv.org/abs/2308.12502)\]. Additionally, the work in \[[8](http://arxiv.org/abs/2306.02216)\] introduced a novel training and unlearning strategy for deep neural networks, termed forgettable federated linear learning, which enables certified and efficient unlearning \[[8](http://arxiv.org/abs/2306.02216)\]. This approach leverages pretrained models to approximate deep neural networks linearly, allowing for efficient unlearning while maintaining model performance \[[8](http://arxiv.org/abs/2306.02216)\].

The importance of evaluation metrics in federated unlearning has also been emphasized. The paper \[[9](http://arxiv.org/abs/2012.13891)\] introduced a comprehensive framework for evaluating federated unlearning methods, highlighting the trade-offs between privacy, accuracy, and efficiency \[[9](http://arxiv.org/abs/2012.13891)\]. This work also proposed the OPENFEDERATEDUNLEARNING framework, which serves as a benchmark for evaluating federated unlearning methods \[[9](http://arxiv.org/abs/2012.13891)\]. Similarly, the paper \[[10](http://arxiv.org/abs/2205.12709)\] introduced a set of evaluation metrics to assess the effectiveness of unlearning in FL, emphasizing the need for multiple metrics to provide a more comprehensive assessment \[[10](http://arxiv.org/abs/2205.12709)\].

In summary, the field of federated unlearning has seen significant advancements, with various methods proposed to address the challenges of data removal, verification, and efficiency. These contributions have laid the groundwork for further research and development in this area.

Federated Online Learning to Rank (FOLTR) represents an advancement in privacy-preserving ranking models by integrating Online Learning to Rank (OLTR) with Federated Learning (FL) to avoid sharing raw user data \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2412.19069)\]. Unlike traditional OLTR methods that rely on static datasets and human annotations, FOLTR continuously updates models using implicit feedback such as clicks, while maintaining user privacy through decentralized training \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2412.19069)\]. However, FOLTR faces challenges in ranking effectiveness, robustness across client data distributions, and security, particularly in handling unlearning of user interactions \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2412.19069)\]. These issues are compounded by the need for unlearning mechanisms that align with regulatory requirements like the "right to be forgotten," which mandates that users can remove their data from training sets \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2412.19069)\]. Recent work has explored federated learning frameworks that enhance model accuracy through per-user domain adaptation and differential privacy constraints \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/2310.20448)\]. Additionally, methods have been proposed to aggregate both generic and client-specific model parameters, improving performance in scenarios with non-i.i.d data and disjoint classes \[[28](http://arxiv.org/abs/2011.06393)\]. These developments highlight ongoing efforts to address the limitations of FOLTR and improve its practical deployment in real-world applications.

### <a id='category-unlearning-in-federated-settings'></a>Unlearning in Federated Settings: 18 papers found.
The category "Unlearning in Federated Settings" explores the challenges and advancements in enabling federated unlearning (FU), which allows the removal of specific clients' contributions from a global model without full retraining. This is crucial for complying with privacy regulations like the GDPR and the right to be forgotten, as well as mitigating malicious influences such as backdoor attacks \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2412.19069)\]. Existing methods face significant hurdles, including the need for client cooperation, storage of historical models, and the lack of theoretical guarantees, especially for deep neural networks \[[6](http://arxiv.org/abs/2403.02437)\]. Researchers have proposed various approaches, such as FedEraser, which leverages historical parameter updates to reconstruct an unlearned model efficiently \[[7](http://arxiv.org/abs/2308.12502)\], and FATS, which ensures exact unlearning by leveraging total variation stability \[[15](http://arxiv.org/abs/2502.20709)\]. Additionally, frameworks like VeriFi introduce verifiable unlearning, allowing clients to independently verify the effectiveness of unlearning through marking and checking mechanisms \[[10](http://arxiv.org/abs/2205.12709)\]. Incentive mechanisms have also been developed to retain valuable users, as unlearning can reduce payoffs for both servers and users \[[5](http://arxiv.org/abs/2406.03078)\]. Despite these advances, challenges remain, such as ensuring privacy, maintaining model accuracy, and reducing computational overhead \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/2310.20448)\]. The field continues to evolve, with recent works addressing security concerns, such as poisoning attacks during unlearning, through robust frameworks like UnlearnGuard \[[29](http://arxiv.org/abs/2501.17396)\]. Overall, the category highlights the growing interest in efficient, secure, and verifiable federated unlearning techniques, driven by both regulatory demands and practical applications.
