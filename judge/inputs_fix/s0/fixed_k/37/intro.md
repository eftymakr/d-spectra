## Related Works

Vision Transformers (ViTs) have emerged as a powerful alternative to convolutional neural networks (CNNs) in computer vision tasks, leveraging self-attention mechanisms to capture global context \[[7](http://arxiv.org/abs/2503.02891)\]. However, their deployment on resource-constrained hardware remains challenging due to their high computational and memory demands \[[7](http://arxiv.org/abs/2503.02891)\]. Existing methods often rely on CPU for complex operations such as Softmax and LayerNorm, leading to suboptimal performance due to communication overhead between CPU and accelerator \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2211.08110)\]. This section reviews related works that address ViT hardware acceleration, model compression, and efficient inference strategies.

Several studies have focused on reducing the computational cost of ViTs through token pruning techniques. HeatViT proposes an image-adaptive token pruning framework that dynamically identifies and consolidates non-informative tokens, achieving up to 65.3% computation reduction while maintaining high accuracy \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2211.08110)\]. Similarly, Evo-ViT introduces a slow-fast token evolution approach, reducing computational cost by over 60% while maintaining comparable performance \[[9](http://arxiv.org/abs/2108.01390)\]. These methods highlight the importance of adaptive token selection in improving hardware efficiency.

Model quantization has also been explored to reduce the computational demands of ViTs. ViTCoD introduces a dedicated algorithm and accelerator co-design framework, achieving speedups of up to 235.3× over general computing platforms \[[5](http://arxiv.org/abs/2210.09573)\]. CHOSEN employs a software-hardware co-design framework with multi-kernel design and approximate non-linear functions, achieving 1.5x and 1.42x improvements in throughput on DeiT-S and DeiT-B models \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/2407.12736)\]. These approaches demonstrate the potential of quantization and hardware-aware design in accelerating ViT inference.

ViTA is a configurable hardware accelerator designed for resource-constrained edge devices, employing a head-level pipeline and inter-layer MLP optimizations to improve hardware utilization efficiency \[[4](http://arxiv.org/abs/2302.09108)\]. The accelerator achieves nearly 90% hardware utilization efficiency and reports a power consumption of 0.88W, making it suitable for edge applications \[[4](http://arxiv.org/abs/2302.09108)\]. This work underscores the importance of tailored hardware designs for efficient ViT deployment.

ViTALiTy unifies low-rank and sparse approximation for ViT acceleration, using a linear Taylor attention mechanism to reduce the quadratic cost of attention matrices \[[8](http://arxiv.org/abs/2211.05109)\]. The framework achieves 3× faster and 3× more energy-efficient inference compared to state-of-the-art solutions \[[8](http://arxiv.org/abs/2211.05109)\]. This approach demonstrates the effectiveness of combining sparsity and low-rank approximations in improving inference efficiency.

In summary, these works highlight the challenges and opportunities in accelerating ViTs on hardware, emphasizing the need for algorithm-hardware co-design, efficient quantization, and adaptive token pruning strategies. Our work builds on these contributions by introducing a novel arithmetic format, MXInt, for efficient ViT acceleration on FPGAs \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2211.08110)\].
