## Related Works

Recent advancements in Text-to-Speech (TTS) systems have significantly enhanced speech naturalness, driving the need for more precise prosody control and mispronunciation correction \[[25](http://arxiv.org/abs/1606.09222)\]. Traditional approaches for prosody manipulation often rely on specialized modules or additional training, which limits their ability to perform post-hoc adjustments \[[25](http://arxiv.org/abs/1606.09222)\]. Similarly, conventional mispronunciation correction methods depend on grapheme-to-phoneme dictionaries, which are less effective in low-resource settings \[[25](http://arxiv.org/abs/1606.09222)\]. To address these limitations, we introduce Counterfactual Activation Editing, a model-agnostic method that enables post-hoc control of prosody and pronunciation by manipulating internal representations in a pre-trained TTS model \[[25](http://arxiv.org/abs/1606.09222)\].

Several works have explored prosody modeling in TTS. For instance, \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2102.08328)\] proposed a context-aware method for text-based speech editing to address prosody mismatches around edited regions. This method uses neural networks to generate salient prosody features that are dependent on the surrounding speech, followed by a pitch-shift and time-stretch method and a denoising neural network to yield high-fidelity results. \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/2011.06465)\] introduced a hierarchical prosody modeling approach for non-autoregressive TTS, where phoneme-level prosody is conditioned on word-level prosody, leading to improved audio quality and prosody naturalness. \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/2211.13533)\] further advanced prosody control by adding utterance-level prosody control to a neural HMM-based TTS system, enabling stable and monotonic alignments for spontaneous speech. \[[4](http://arxiv.org/abs/2202.07816)\] proposed ProsoSpeech, which enhances prosody using quantized latent vectors pre-trained on large-scale unpaired and low-quality data, leading to more expressive speech.

In the realm of controllable TTS, \[[5](http://arxiv.org/abs/2110.02854)\] introduced Prosody-TTS, an end-to-end system that allows fine-grained control over fundamental frequency and phone duration, improving the naturalness of synthesized speech. \[[6](http://arxiv.org/abs/2206.14643)\] examined simple extensions to a Transformer-based FastSpeech-like system, finding that long context, powerful text features, and multi-speaker training improve prosody for multi-sentence TTS. \[[7](http://arxiv.org/abs/2110.02952)\] proposed a non-autoregressive parallel neural TTS model hierarchically conditioned on acoustic features, enabling control over each prosodic dimension. \[[8](http://arxiv.org/abs/2309.11487)\] presented a neural TTS system capable of transferring prosody from an unseen speaker, demonstrating the ability to maintain target speaker identity without quality degradation. \[[9](http://arxiv.org/abs/2412.20155)\] introduced Stable-TTS, a speaker-adaptive TTS framework that leverages prior samples for prosody consistency and timbre capture, even under limited target data.

For emotional and expressive prosody control, \[[10](http://arxiv.org/abs/2210.15364)\] proposed a method to control accent intensity in accented TTS using a speaker-adversarial loss and a FastSpeech2-based model. \[[11](http://arxiv.org/abs/2206.07956)\] developed an automatic prosody annotation framework using pre-trained text-speech models, achieving results comparable to human annotations. \[[12](http://arxiv.org/abs/2202.07200)\] introduced an unsupervised word-level prosody tagging approach, enabling controllable speech synthesis by clustering prosodies based on word types. \[[13](http://arxiv.org/abs/2206.12229)\] demonstrated exact prosody cloning in zero-shot multispeaker TTS, achieving high similarity to original prosody without quality degradation. \[[14](http://arxiv.org/abs/2309.01576)\] conducted a comparative analysis of pretrained language models for TTS, finding a logarithmic relationship between model size and quality. \[[15](http://arxiv.org/abs/2410.24019)\] explored the impact of prosody on speech-to-text translation, showing that E2E systems outperform cascades in prosody-aware translation. \[[16](http://arxiv.org/abs/2501.19258)\] proposed VisualSpeech, which integrates visual context to enhance prosody prediction in TTS. \[[17](http://arxiv.org/abs/2204.03238)\] introduced an unsupervised quantized prosody representation method, enabling fine-grained control over speaking styles.
