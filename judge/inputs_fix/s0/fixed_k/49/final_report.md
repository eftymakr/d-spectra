## Related Works

Neural retrieval models have shown significant promise in Web search, particularly in their ability to learn complex representations from raw text \[[15](http://arxiv.org/abs/2406.18960)\]. However, their training typically requires substantial amounts of labeled query-document pairs, which are costly to obtain \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2412.15453)\]. With the availability of large-scale document collections like ClueWeb22, synthetic queries generated by large language models (LLMs) have emerged as a scalable alternative to labeled data \[[12](http://arxiv.org/abs/2303.11648)\]. These synthetic queries, however, often vary in quality, leading to suboptimal downstream retrieval performance \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2412.15453)\].

Existing approaches typically filter out noisy query-document pairs using signals from an external re-ranker \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2412.15453)\]. In contrast, this work proposes a framework that integrates ranking signals directly into the query generation process using Direct Preference Optimization (DPO) \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/2410.04350)\]. DPO has been widely adopted for preference alignment of LLMs due to its simplicity and effectiveness \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/2410.04350)\]. By aligning token-level docid generation with document-level relevance estimation through direct optimization via pairwise ranking, DPO eliminates the need for explicit reward modeling and reinforcement learning \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/2504.05181)\]. This approach has shown improvements in retrieval effectiveness, achieving a 7.4% improvement in MRR@10 for MS MARCO and a 19.9% improvement for Natural Questions \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/2504.05181)\].

The proposed framework builds on the idea of using DPO to optimize query generation, ensuring that the generated queries maximize downstream retrieval effectiveness \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/2410.04350)\]. This is in line with the broader trend of leveraging preference-based alignment techniques to enhance retrieval performance \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2412.15453)\]. For instance, TIS-DPO, which assigns importance weights to each token based on its reward, has shown significant improvements in harmlessness and helpfulness alignment \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/2410.04350)\]. Similarly, Group Preference Optimization (GPO) enables few-shot alignment of LLMs to group-specific preferences, reducing the need for extensive group-specific preference data \[[11](http://arxiv.org/abs/2310.11523)\].

In addition to DPO, other approaches have explored ways to improve retrieval performance through query generation. For example, CtrlQGen generates diverse synthetic queries to improve retrievability in search systems \[[12](http://arxiv.org/abs/2303.11648)\]. This method addresses the issue of retrievability bias by generating queries that are more representative of user intent, thereby improving the discoverability of entities \[[12](http://arxiv.org/abs/2303.11648)\]. Similarly, the study of MatchPyramid models on ad-hoc retrieval has shown that deep neural networks can outperform traditional retrieval models like BM25 and language models \[[10](http://arxiv.org/abs/1606.04648)\].

The integration of DPO into query generation represents a novel approach that directly optimizes the model towards generating high-quality queries \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/2410.04350)\]. This aligns with the broader goal of improving the alignment of LLMs with human preferences, as demonstrated by the use of rationales in preference data \[[4](http://arxiv.org/abs/2407.14477)\]. By incorporating these rationales, models can learn more efficiently and achieve higher final performance \[[4](http://arxiv.org/abs/2407.14477)\].

In summary, the proposed framework builds on a range of existing techniques, including DPO, synthetic query generation, and preference-based alignment, to address the challenges of training neural retrieval models with limited labeled data \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/2410.04350)\]\[[12](http://arxiv.org/abs/2303.11648)\]\[[4](http://arxiv.org/abs/2407.14477)\].

However, these methods often lead to performance degradation due to the separation between encoding and compression, which has been addressed by Joint Product Quantization (JPQ), which jointly optimizes query encoding and PQ index in an end-to-end manner, achieving significant performance improvements while enabling efficient retrieval \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/2504.05181)\]. Furthermore, research has explored the potential of generating synthetic queries using large language models to overcome the limitations of labeled data, with recent work proposing Direct Preference Optimization (DPO) to integrate ranking signals directly into query generation, leading to higher relevance scores and improved downstream performance \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/2410.04350)\]. Query generation methods for enhanced retrieval have been extensively explored to address the limitations of traditional search engines, particularly in improving content discoverability and reducing retrievability bias. Existing approaches often rely on synthetic query generation to augment training data, especially when labeled query-document pairs are scarce \[[12](http://arxiv.org/abs/2303.11648)\]. However, synthetic queries can vary in quality, leading to suboptimal retrieval performance \[[12](http://arxiv.org/abs/2303.11648)\]. To mitigate this, researchers have proposed methods that integrate ranking signals directly into the query generation process, such as using Direct Preference Optimization (DPO) to optimize query generation for downstream retrieval effectiveness \[[12](http://arxiv.org/abs/2303.11648)\]. This aligns with broader efforts to improve the diversity and relevance of generated queries, as seen in methods like CtrlQGen, which generates synthetic queries to enhance retrievability for both narrow and broad intent searches \[[12](http://arxiv.org/abs/2303.11648)\]. Additionally, query generation techniques have been applied in conversational settings, where multiple query rewrites are used to improve passage retrieval by addressing challenges like coreference and ellipsis \[[15](http://arxiv.org/abs/2406.18960)\]. These approaches highlight the growing interest in leveraging advanced models to generate more effective and contextually relevant queries for retrieval systems.
