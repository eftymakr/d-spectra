## Related Works

Large Language Models (LLMs) have been increasingly studied for their ability to encode and exhibit behaviors such as refusal within their activation space \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2505.17306)\]. However, identifying and steering these behaviors remains a significant challenge. Existing approaches often rely on predefined refusal templates detectable in output tokens \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2505.17306)\], or require manual analysis \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2505.17306)\], which limits their scalability and adaptability. Recent research has explored the use of activation space vectors to bypass refusals in multilingual settings, revealing the cross-lingual universality of refusal directions \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2505.17306)\]. This work builds upon these findings by introducing a novel automated framework for direction selection, independent of model outputs, leveraging cosine similarity metrics.

The use of cosine similarity for measuring similarity between high-dimensional vectors has been widely explored, particularly in the context of document similarity and multidimensional comparisons \[[8](http://arxiv.org/abs/1505.03934)\]. However, cosine similarity has limitations in high-dimensional spaces, particularly due to its dependency on the dimensionality of vectors, leading to biased and poorly interpretable outcomes \[[5](http://arxiv.org/abs/2407.08623)\]. To address this, the Dimension Insensitive Euclidean Metric (DIEM) was introduced, offering a more robust and generalizable alternative \[[5](http://arxiv.org/abs/2407.08623)\]. While DIEM is not directly applicable to steering LLMs, it underscores the importance of using appropriate metrics for high-dimensional comparisons, a principle that informs the design of COSMIC.

Refusal mechanisms in LLMs are essential for ensuring safety, and recent work has shown that refusal behavior can be mediated by a single direction in activation space, enabling targeted interventions \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2505.17306)\]. This concept is closely related to the idea of steering LLMs through activation space, where specific directions can be identified and manipulated to alter model behavior \[[6](http://arxiv.org/abs/2502.05945)\]. The introduction of refusal tokens has also been proposed as a method to calibrate refusal behavior, allowing for fine-grained control over refusal rates without requiring additional fine-tuning \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/2412.06748)\]. These methods, while effective, often rely on predefined tokens or manual intervention, which limits their flexibility.

In contrast, COSMIC introduces an automated framework that identifies viable steering directions and target layers using cosine similarity, entirely independent of model outputs \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2505.17306)\]. This approach is particularly useful in adversarial settings and weakly aligned models, where traditional methods may fail \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2505.17306)\]. By leveraging cosine similarity, COSMIC achieves steering performance comparable to prior methods without requiring assumptions about a model's refusal behavior, such as the presence of specific refusal tokens \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2505.17306)\]. This makes COSMIC a promising approach for steering LLMs toward safer behavior with minimal increase in false refusals, demonstrating robustness across a wide range of alignment conditions \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2505.17306)\].

The ability to steer LLMs through activation space has also been explored in the context of behavioral biases in Vision-Language Models (LVLMs), where models may exhibit recency and authority biases \[[10](http://arxiv.org/abs/2409.15256)\]. While these biases are specific to vision-language models, they highlight the broader challenge of ensuring alignment and safety in LLMs. Similarly, the study of deceptive behaviors in LLMs has revealed the need for robust alignment guardrails, as models can be manipulated to bypass safety mechanisms \[[11](http://arxiv.org/abs/2403.09676)\]. These findings underscore the importance of developing methods like COSMIC that can reliably identify and steer LLM behaviors without relying on predefined tokens or manual intervention.
