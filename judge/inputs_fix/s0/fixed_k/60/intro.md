## Related Works

Calibration of predictive models has been a central topic in machine learning, particularly for ensuring that predicted probabilities reflect the true likelihood of events. Traditional approaches to calibration often rely on predefined transformation functions, such as Platt Scaling or isotonic regression, which are order-preserving but limited in expressiveness \[[13](http://arxiv.org/abs/1710.08901)\]. These methods typically assume a fixed functional form, such as piece-wise linear, which may not be sufficient for complex calibration tasks \[[13](http://arxiv.org/abs/1710.08901)\]. In contrast, recent work has explored the use of neural networks to learn more flexible and expressive calibration functions \[[11](http://arxiv.org/abs/2203.09278)\]. 

The use of monotonic neural networks for calibration has gained traction due to their ability to model arbitrary monotonic functions while preserving the order of predictions \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/1908.05164)\]. This property is crucial for calibration, as it ensures that the predicted probabilities maintain a consistent relationship with the true distribution. The Unconstrained Monotonic Neural Network (UMNN) architecture, introduced in \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/1908.05164)\], enables the learning of flexible monotonic functions without enforcing restrictive constraints on weights or activation functions. This approach has been further extended to distributional reinforcement learning, where UMNNs are used to model different representations of the random return distribution, such as PDF, CDF, and QF \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/2106.03228)\]. 

In the context of ranking models, the importance of both calibration and ranking has been highlighted. For example, in conversational search, the Probability Ranking Principle (PRP) requires that models are well-calibrated and provide certain probabilities of relevance \[[10](http://arxiv.org/abs/2101.04356)\]. However, deep neural networks often suffer from miscalibration, leading to overconfident or underconfident predictions. To address this, stochastic rankers have been proposed, which output predictive distributions rather than point estimates \[[10](http://arxiv.org/abs/2101.04356)\]. 

Furthermore, the calibration of neural networks has been extensively studied in the context of uncertainty estimation. Methods such as RECAST, which combines cosine annealing with Stochastic Gradient Langevin Dynamics, have been proposed to improve the calibration of uncertainty estimates \[[8](http://arxiv.org/abs/1911.00104)\]. Additionally, the concept of confidence calibration has been explored, where models are trained to balance accuracy and uncertainty \[[11](http://arxiv.org/abs/2203.09278)\]. 

In the field of conformal prediction, the Self-Calibrating Conformal Prediction method has been introduced to deliver both calibrated point predictions and prediction intervals with finite-sample validity \[[7](http://arxiv.org/abs/2402.07307)\]. This approach leverages Venn-Abers calibration to ensure that the predicted probabilities are well-calibrated, which is essential for reliable decision-making in safety-critical applications \[[7](http://arxiv.org/abs/2402.07307)\]. 

The proposed approach in this paper builds upon these ideas by introducing a calibrator based on the UMNN architecture, which can learn arbitrary monotonic functions with high modeling power. This approach significantly improves flexibility and expressiveness while maintaining the necessary monotonicity constraints \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/1908.05164)\]. Additionally, a novel loss function, termed Smooth Calibration Loss (SCLoss), is introduced to optimize the calibrator for calibration tasks \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/1908.05164)\].
