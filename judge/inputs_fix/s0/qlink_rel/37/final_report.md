## Related Works

Vision Transformers (ViTs) have emerged as a powerful alternative to convolutional neural networks (CNNs) in computer vision tasks, leveraging the transformer architecture to effectively capture global context \[[5](http://arxiv.org/abs/2010.11929)\]. However, the computational complexity and memory requirements of ViTs pose significant challenges for hardware acceleration, particularly due to the sensitivity of operations like Softmax and LayerNorm to model accuracy \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2208.05163)\]. Existing approaches often rely on CPU for these complex operations, resulting in suboptimal performance due to communication overhead between the CPU and accelerator \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2208.05163)\]. This section reviews related works that address these challenges through hardware acceleration, quantization, and architectural improvements.

Several works have explored hardware acceleration for ViTs. Auto-ViT-Acc introduces an FPGA-aware framework for ViT acceleration using mixed-scheme quantization, achieving a 5.6x improvement in frame rate with minimal accuracy loss \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2208.05163)\]. ViTCoD proposes a co-design framework that integrates algorithmic and hardware-level optimizations, achieving speedups of up to 235.3× over general computing platforms \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/2210.09573)\]. Similarly, DynamicViT employs dynamic token sparsification to reduce computational load while maintaining accuracy \[[12](http://arxiv.org/abs/2106.02034)\]. HeatViT further enhances hardware efficiency through adaptive token pruning and quantization, achieving significant computation reduction and accuracy improvements \[[13](http://arxiv.org/abs/2211.08110)\]. These works highlight the potential of hardware-specific optimizations for ViT acceleration.

Quantization has also been explored to reduce the computational and memory footprint of ViTs. VAQF introduces a fully automatic framework for low-bit ViT acceleration on FPGAs, achieving real-time execution with minimal accuracy loss \[[7](http://arxiv.org/abs/2201.06618)\]. FQ-ViT addresses the challenges of post-training quantization for ViTs, introducing techniques like Power-of-Two Factor and Log-Int-Softmax to maintain accuracy while reducing bit-width \[[14](http://arxiv.org/abs/2111.13824)\]. These methods demonstrate that quantization can be effectively applied to ViTs with careful design.

Architectural improvements have also been proposed to enhance the efficiency of ViTs. T2T-ViT introduces a Tokens-to-Token transformation to better capture local structures, reducing parameter count and computational cost \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/2101.11986)\]. CvT integrates convolutions into ViTs, combining the benefits of CNNs and Transformers for improved performance and efficiency \[[9](http://arxiv.org/abs/2103.15808)\]. LeViT further optimizes the speed-accuracy trade-off by incorporating principles from CNNs into the transformer architecture \[[10](http://arxiv.org/abs/2104.01136)\]. These architectural innovations contribute to the broader goal of making ViTs more efficient for deployment.

In summary, recent works have made significant strides in addressing the challenges of ViT hardware acceleration, quantization, and architectural efficiency. These advancements provide a foundation for the proposed approach, which aims to map all ViT operations onto FPGAs using a novel arithmetic format \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2208.05163)\].

Recent advances in low-precision ViT implementations have focused on addressing the challenges of quantizing transformer models without significant accuracy loss. Most existing quantization methods have primarily been developed for CNNs, leading to severe performance degradation when applied to vision transformers \[[14](http://arxiv.org/abs/2111.13824)\]. This issue is attributed to the inter-channel variation in LayerNorm inputs, which complicates the quantization process \[[14](http://arxiv.org/abs/2111.13824)\]. To mitigate this, researchers have proposed systematic methods such as the Power-of-Two Factor (PTF) to reduce performance degradation and inference complexity \[[14](http://arxiv.org/abs/2111.13824)\]. Additionally, the non-uniform distribution in attention maps has been observed, prompting the development of Log-Int-Softmax (LIS) to maintain accuracy while enabling 4-bit quantization and simplifying inference using the BitShift operator \[[14](http://arxiv.org/abs/2111.13824)\]. These techniques demonstrate that careful design of quantization strategies and hardware-specific optimizations can enable efficient and accurate deployment of fully quantized vision transformers. The success of these approaches is evidenced by results such as 84.89% top-1 accuracy with ViT-L on ImageNet and 50.8 mAP with Cascade Mask R-CNN (Swin-S) on COCO, achieving lossless accuracy degradation of approximately 1% \[[14](http://arxiv.org/abs/2111.13824)\].
