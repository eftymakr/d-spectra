## Related Works

De novo genome assembly has been a focal point of computational biology research, driven by the advent of next-generation sequencing technologies that generate massive volumes of short reads \[[1](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/7279acd9c61a8de019ea2d55d23124dfbb6b5269)\]. Early approaches, such as the overlap-layout-consensus (OLC) method, relied on identifying overlaps between reads to construct a consensus sequence \[[1](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/7279acd9c61a8de019ea2d55d23124dfbb6b5269)\]. However, these methods were limited by computational complexity and memory requirements, particularly for large genomes \[[1](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/7279acd9c61a8de019ea2d55d23124dfbb6b5269)\]. The de Bruijn graph approach, which uses k-mer frequencies to represent reads, became more prevalent due to its scalability and efficiency in handling high-throughput data \[[11](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/d206ac8572f7480e5d1e92620060a39a8c3fd3ce)\]. This method has been further optimized with parallel and distributed computing frameworks to address the computational challenges of assembling large genomes \[[23](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/7bf2f1c55f90e11a4d1df4dd608ac621a08f8b33)\].

Several studies have explored the use of long-read sequencing technologies, such as PacBio and Oxford Nanopore, which produce reads of tens of kilobases in length \[[3](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/2b59fd18ada769ef76fb7fcc7382f063791abfab)\]. These technologies have significantly improved the contiguity of genome assemblies, but they are accompanied by high error rates that complicate the assembly process \[[3](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/2b59fd18ada769ef76fb7fcc7382f063791abfab)\]. To address this, tools like Canu and Flye have been developed, which utilize repeat graphs and adaptive k-mer weighting to improve the accuracy and continuity of assemblies \[[3](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/2b59fd18ada769ef76fb7fcc7382f063791abfab)\]\[[21](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/e0f860a608ecfb4ac6d0010e16db5c13cca14da1)\]. These tools demonstrate that long-read assembly can achieve high-quality results, particularly for complex genomes \[[3](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/2b59fd18ada769ef76fb7fcc7382f063791abfab)\].

The computational challenges of de novo assembly have also spurred the development of parallel and distributed assembly frameworks. For example, PASHA leverages de Bruijn graphs and hybrid parallelism to efficiently assemble large genomes, achieving competitive performance with reduced computational resources \[[23](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/7bf2f1c55f90e11a4d1df4dd608ac621a08f8b33)\]. Similarly, SWAP-Assembler is designed for extreme-scale assembly using thousands of cores, demonstrating scalability and efficiency in handling massive datasets \[[18](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/fe8d86bc5520b5049104ff58ee7e980982816ebf)\]. These tools highlight the importance of parallel computing in addressing the growing data volumes and algorithmic complexity of de novo assembly \[[23](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/7bf2f1c55f90e11a4d1df4dd608ac621a08f8b33)\].

Despite these advancements, existing assemblers still face limitations in terms of memory usage, computational efficiency, and handling of complex genomic structures \[[1](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/7279acd9c61a8de019ea2d55d23124dfbb6b5269)\]. For instance, the memory footprint of many assemblers remains high, leading to memory-bound behavior and irregular data patterns that hinder performance \[[1](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/7279acd9c61a8de019ea2d55d23124dfbb6b5269)\]. The need for more efficient and scalable solutions has led to the exploration of hardware-software co-design approaches, such as NMP-PaK, which aims to reduce memory usage and improve performance through near-memory processing \[[1](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/7279acd9c61a8de019ea2d55d23124dfbb6b5269)\].

In summary, while significant progress has been made in de novo assembly algorithms and tools, the field continues to face challenges related to computational efficiency, memory usage, and handling of complex genomic data. The development of new methods, including hardware-software co-design, is essential to address these challenges and enable the assembly of large, complex genomes \[[1](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/7279acd9c61a8de019ea2d55d23124dfbb6b5269)\]\[[3](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/2b59fd18ada769ef76fb7fcc7382f063791abfab)\]\[[18](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/fe8d86bc5520b5049104ff58ee7e980982816ebf)\]\[[21](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/e0f860a608ecfb4ac6d0010e16db5c13cca14da1)\]\[[23](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/7bf2f1c55f90e11a4d1df4dd608ac621a08f8b33)\].

These frameworks aim to improve scalability and efficiency by leveraging parallel computing architectures, such as distributed-memory systems and hybrid CPU-NMP processing \[[23](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/7bf2f1c55f90e11a4d1df4dd608ac621a08f8b33)\].
