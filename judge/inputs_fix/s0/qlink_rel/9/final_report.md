## Related Works

The segmentation of multiple sclerosis (MS) lesions from magnetic resonance imaging (MRI) has been a focal point of research due to its importance in disease monitoring and treatment evaluation. Traditional methods often rely on manual segmentation, which is time-consuming and subject to inter- and intra-observer variability \[[1](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/71b5e8d9970f3b725127eb7da3d5183b921ad017)\]. To address these limitations, automated methods have been proposed, ranging from atlas-based techniques to deep learning approaches. Atlas-based methods, such as those described in \[[4](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/ecafc65d094c52300ffa2c9640303c584f4d569b)\], use statistical and topological atlases to constrain the segmentation of brain structures, ensuring anatomical consistency. These methods have demonstrated competitive accuracy with state-of-the-art techniques while segmenting the entire brain \[[4](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/ecafc65d094c52300ffa2c9640303c584f4d569b)\]. However, they may struggle with the high variability of MS lesions across different patients and imaging protocols \[[1](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/71b5e8d9970f3b725127eb7da3d5183b921ad017)\].

Deep learning has emerged as a powerful tool for MS lesion segmentation, particularly in handling the complexity of lesion appearance and anatomical variability. Early approaches, such as the cascaded 3D convolutional neural networks (CNNs) in \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/1702.04869)\], showed significant improvements in accuracy by using two CNNs in sequence to reduce misclassification. These methods were evaluated on public datasets like MICCAI2008 and demonstrated superior performance compared to existing tools \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/1702.04869)\]. Similarly, the fully convolutional neural network (FCN) approach in \[[5](http://arxiv.org/abs/1803.09172)\] introduced a dual-pathway architecture to capture multi-contrast MRI data, achieving a Dice score of 90.48 on the ISBI 2015 challenge data, comparable to human raters \[[5](http://arxiv.org/abs/1803.09172)\]. 

The challenge of class imbalance, particularly in distinguishing between rim and non-rim lesions, has also been addressed in recent work. For example, the multi-branch CNN in \[[6](http://arxiv.org/abs/1811.02942)\] was designed to handle multi-modal data and improve segmentation accuracy by fusing features from different modalities. This approach was evaluated on both public and private datasets, showing high improvements in segmentation performance \[[6](http://arxiv.org/abs/1811.02942)\]. Another notable work, \[[14](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/3fe6e8e47663a38c037813bc35524fe0931b31b2)\], proposed a 2.5D stacked slice method using Tiramisu networks, achieving a top rank in the ISBI challenge with a score of 93.1 \[[14](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/3fe6e8e47663a38c037813bc35524fe0931b31b2)\]. 

The issue of domain adaptation, where models trained on one dataset may not generalize to new imaging protocols, has also been explored. The one-shot domain adaptation method in \[[9](http://arxiv.org/abs/1805.12415)\] demonstrated that even with minimal annotated data from a new domain, the model could maintain performance comparable to fully trained models \[[9](http://arxiv.org/abs/1805.12415)\]. This is particularly relevant for clinical settings where labeled data is scarce \[[9](http://arxiv.org/abs/1805.12415)\]. 

In addition, the use of geometric loss functions has been proposed to address the challenges of lesion shape and size variability. The geometric loss in \[[20](http://arxiv.org/abs/2009.13755)\] was designed to exploit the spatial properties of lesions, leading to improved segmentation accuracy on datasets with varying scales and resolutions \[[20](http://arxiv.org/abs/2009.13755)\]. 

Finally, the importance of uncertainty estimation in deep learning models for MS lesion segmentation has been highlighted in \[[17](http://arxiv.org/abs/1808.01200)\], where Monte Carlo dropout was used to provide uncertainty measures for lesion detection and segmentation, improving clinical interpretability \[[17](http://arxiv.org/abs/1808.01200)\]. 

These studies collectively highlight the ongoing efforts to improve the accuracy, robustness, and generalization of MS lesion segmentation methods, particularly in the context of rare lesion types like paramagnetic rim lesions \[[1](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/71b5e8d9970f3b725127eb7da3d5183b921ad017)\].
