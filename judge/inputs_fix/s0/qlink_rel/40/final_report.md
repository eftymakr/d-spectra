## Related Works

Zero-Knowledge Proofs (ZKPs) have garnered significant attention for their ability to enable secure and private computation, allowing a prover to demonstrate the truth of a statement without revealing additional information \[[10](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/f056118f41849a3688634e1f2fc73108fc660435)\]. Despite their promise, the computational complexity of proof generation has hindered widespread adoption \[[1](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/6ee84674b7c426849c3da56cb278c08eb7a903cf)\]. Recent efforts have focused on accelerating the key primitives of ZKP protocols, particularly on GPU and ASIC platforms \[[7](http://arxiv.org/abs/2408.05890)\]. For instance, GZKP leverages GPU acceleration to improve performance for polynomial computations and multi-scalar multiplications (MSMs), achieving up to 48.1× speedup on cryptographic benchmarks \[[2](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/e149d93531d8c07cf43f6cae327a4795144d1304)\]. Similarly, cuZK introduces a faster parallel MSM algorithm on GPUs, achieving up to 4.86× speedup on a cryptocurrency application \[[9](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/e4a4608e8e35379e63b9df5bda1b7599ee8ab9ac)\]. 

However, many existing accelerators face limitations. Some require a trusted setup for each application \[[1](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/6ee84674b7c426849c3da56cb278c08eb7a903cf)\], which restricts their use in dynamic environments. Others generate larger proof sizes or incur higher verification costs, limiting their applicability in scenarios with numerous verifiers or strict time constraints \[[1](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/6ee84674b7c426849c3da56cb278c08eb7a903cf)\]. For example, PipeZK achieves 10× speedup on cryptographic benchmarks and 5× on Zcash, but does not address the issue of proof size \[[1](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/6ee84674b7c426849c3da56cb278c08eb7a903cf)\]. Similarly, SZKP, the first ASIC to accelerate an entire proof on-chip, achieves over 400× speedup over CPU implementations but does not address the trusted setup problem \[[7](http://arxiv.org/abs/2408.05890)\]. 

To overcome these limitations, this work presents zkSpeed, an accelerator for HyperPlonk, a ZKP protocol that supports one-time, universal setup and small proof sizes \[[1](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/6ee84674b7c426849c3da56cb278c08eb7a903cf)\]. By accelerating the entire protocol, including the SumCheck and MSM primitives, zkSpeed achieves geometric mean speedups of 801× over CPU baselines \[[1](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/6ee84674b7c426849c3da56cb278c08eb7a903cf)\]. The design leverages a full-chip architecture with 366.46 mm² and 2 TB/s of bandwidth to ensure efficient execution of the proof generation process \[[1](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/6ee84674b7c426849c3da56cb278c08eb7a903cf)\]. This approach addresses both the computational complexity and the scalability challenges faced by existing solutions \[[1](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/6ee84674b7c426849c3da56cb278c08eb7a903cf)\]. 

In addition to performance improvements, the work also emphasizes the importance of reducing proof sizes and verification costs, which are critical for real-world applications \[[1](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/6ee84674b7c426849c3da56cb278c08eb7a903cf)\]. For instance, zkCNN enables efficient zero-knowledge proofs for convolutional neural networks, achieving 1264× speedup over existing schemes with a proof size of 341 kilobytes \[[4](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/6336149196f02d0eb6c1a89bc6b662e4422bd794)\]. Similarly, the zero-knowledge proof system for decision tree predictions \[[5](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/3becc2935d1f52c82ffd7216c28bc86f5e393208)\] demonstrates the feasibility of applying ZKPs to machine learning models without compromising privacy or efficiency. 

These advancements highlight the growing interest in ZKPs for privacy-preserving applications, including blockchain, verifiable machine learning, and electronic voting \[[1](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/6ee84674b7c426849c3da56cb278c08eb7a903cf)\]. As such, the development of efficient and scalable ZKP accelerators remains a critical research direction \[[1](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/6ee84674b7c426849c3da56cb278c08eb7a903cf)\].
