## Related Works

Traditional approaches to modeling economic inequality and taxation have relied on static frameworks that fail to capture the complexity of heterogeneous taxpayer behavior and dynamic policy adjustments. For instance, the U.S. federal income tax system, while effective in reducing inequality, lacks adaptability to changing economic conditions and individual behaviors \[[15](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/0fba9c1bee4a5bda0d5dbf01f97ef5a8e212833e)\]. Similarly, models like the Saez Optimal Taxation framework, which dynamically adjusts tax rates, do not adequately address irrational taxpayer behavior or heterogeneity in income distribution \[[15](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/0fba9c1bee4a5bda0d5dbf01f97ef5a8e212833e)\]. These limitations highlight the need for more flexible and data-driven policy design.

Agent-based modeling (ABM) has been increasingly used to simulate economic systems, particularly in the context of tax evasion and social behavior. For example, the Ising model has been adapted to study tax evasion dynamics, where agents (taxpayers) interact with their environment and enforcement mechanisms to either evade or comply with tax laws \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/0805.0998)\]. This approach allows for the simulation of how different enforcement strategies, such as audits, can influence taxpayer behavior over time \[[4](http://arxiv.org/abs/0801.2980)\]. Additionally, the multi-agent-based approach to tax morale has shown that external factors, such as trust in governmental institutions, can significantly affect the prevalence of tax evasion \[[7](http://arxiv.org/abs/0806.0344)\]. These models provide a foundation for understanding how policy interventions can shape economic outcomes.

Recent advancements in large language models (LLMs) have also contributed to the field of economic modeling. LLMs, when fine-tuned on reasoning tasks, can generate structured thought processes that improve their ability to make complex decisions \[[6](http://arxiv.org/abs/2210.03629)\]. This capability is particularly relevant for policy design, where models must balance multiple objectives, such as equity and productivity. The ReAct framework, which synergizes reasoning and acting in LLMs, has demonstrated effectiveness in tasks requiring both logical deduction and external information retrieval \[[6](http://arxiv.org/abs/2210.03629)\]. These techniques can be integrated into ABMs to enhance the decision-making capabilities of policy agents.

Furthermore, reinforcement learning (RL) has been applied to agent-based models to improve the realism of agent behavior. RL-based models can learn adaptive strategies that maximize utility, making them suitable for simulating economic agents in policy environments \[[9](http://arxiv.org/abs/2006.05048)\]. This approach has been tested in models of influenza transmission and minority games, showing that RL agents can outperform traditional heuristic-based models \[[9](http://arxiv.org/abs/2006.05048)\]. Integrating RL with ABMs could lead to more accurate and responsive economic simulations.

In summary, while traditional taxation models and ABMs have provided valuable insights, the integration of LLMs and RL into ABMs offers a promising direction for designing adaptive and data-driven fiscal policies. These techniques can better capture the complexity of economic systems and improve the effectiveness of policy interventions.

### <a id='category-agent-based-modeling-in-economics'></a>Agent-Based Modeling in Economics: 8 papers found.
Agent-based modeling in economics has been increasingly utilized to simulate complex economic behaviors and policy impacts, offering a flexible framework to incorporate heterogeneous agent interactions and adaptive decision-making. For instance, models have been developed to embed tax evasion behaviors into economic simulations, where agents' trust in governmental institutions influences their compliance levels, and tax authorities can mitigate evasion through enforcement mechanisms \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/0805.0998)\]. Additionally, agent-based models have been applied to financial markets to explain dynamics beyond classical economic theories, emphasizing the importance of non-stationarity and self-organization in market behavior \[[5](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/ebc3c689a69f536d0b089d00cb19c8e8cce0f648)\]. These models often incorporate stochastic elements and network structures, such as Barabasi–Albert and Voronoi–Delaunay networks, to reflect real-world economic interactions and improve predictive accuracy \[[7](http://arxiv.org/abs/0806.0344)\]. Furthermore, advances in modeling opinion dynamics and policy responses have been explored using Markov chain approaches, allowing for rigorous analysis of macroeconomic outcomes from micro-level agent interactions \[[6](http://arxiv.org/abs/2210.03629)\]. Such methodologies highlight the potential of agent-based modeling to address economic challenges, including inequality and policy design, by integrating adaptive learning and behavioral realism into simulation frameworks.

### <a id='category-integration-of-ai-in-economic-modeling'></a>Integration of AI in Economic Modeling: 6 papers found.
The integration of large language models (LLMs) with agent-based modeling (ABM) represents a significant advancement in economic modeling, particularly in addressing complex policy challenges like tax design and economic inequality \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/0805.0998)\]. Traditional models, such as the Saez Optimal Taxation framework, provide dynamic adjustments but struggle with capturing taxpayer heterogeneity and irrational behavior \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/0805.0998)\]. Recent research has explored the use of LLMs to enhance reasoning and decision-making capabilities, such as through chain-of-thought (COT) prompting, which improves the quality of generated responses \[[1](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/12dba28ded03c013923430994d1aac347c647c8d)\]. Furthermore, the development of alignment fine-tuning (AFT) has addressed issues like assessment misalignment, where models may assign higher scores to subpar COTs, by introducing constraint alignment loss to ensure positive responses outperform negative ones \[[1](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/12dba28ded03c013923430994d1aac347c647c8d)\]. Additionally, methods like ReAct combine reasoning and action generation, enabling models to interact with external sources and generate interpretable task-solving trajectories \[[6](http://arxiv.org/abs/2210.03629)\]. These advancements highlight the potential of LLMs to improve economic modeling by incorporating adaptive, data-driven approaches that better reflect real-world complexities \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/0805.0998)\]. The use of reinforcement learning (RL) in ABMs has also shown promise, as RL-based agents can outperform traditional heuristic or regression-based models by learning optimal behaviors in multi-agent settings \[[9](http://arxiv.org/abs/2006.05048)\]. Overall, these studies underscore the growing role of LLMs in enhancing economic modeling through improved reasoning, adaptability, and interaction with external data sources \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/0805.0998)\]\[[6](http://arxiv.org/abs/2210.03629)\]\[[9](http://arxiv.org/abs/2006.05048)\].

### <a id='category-tax-evasion-and-compliance-behavior'></a>Tax Evasion and Compliance Behavior: 2 papers found.
The category "Tax Evasion and Compliance Behavior" explores how tax evasion can be modeled and mitigated through behavioral and policy interventions. Researchers have incorporated tax evasion dynamics into frameworks like the Ising model, which traditionally describes magnetic interactions in physics, to simulate how individuals might behave in a tax system \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/0805.0998)\]. These models consider factors such as social influence, personal incentives, and the likelihood of being audited \[[4](http://arxiv.org/abs/0801.2980)\]. Studies show that even minimal enforcement mechanisms, such as audits, can significantly reduce tax evasion by altering taxpayer behavior over time \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/0805.0998)\]. The effectiveness of such policies is often tested through simulations, which reveal that targeted enforcement can lead to substantial improvements in compliance \[[4](http://arxiv.org/abs/0801.2980)\]. These findings highlight the importance of integrating behavioral insights into tax policy design to address the complexities of taxpayer behavior and promote greater adherence to legal obligations.
