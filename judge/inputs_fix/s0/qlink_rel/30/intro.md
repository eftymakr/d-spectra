## Related Works

Modern databases often trade strong transactional isolation for weak isolation guarantees to achieve low latency and high throughput, especially in distributed systems that must tolerate network partitions \[[11](http://arxiv.org/abs/1302.0309)\]. This trade-off has led to the proliferation of weak isolation levels such as Read Committed (RC), Read Atomic (RA), and Causal Consistency (CC), which are designed to balance availability with correctness \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/1908.04509)\]. However, this approach has introduced new challenges in ensuring data consistency, as several production databases have been found to suffer from isolation bugs that violate their promised guarantees \[[16](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/04178561c9d8cd42904ed0a836e08e55b84703cf)\].

Black-box testing has emerged as a prominent technique for detecting these isolation bugs by verifying whether transaction histories adhere to a prescribed isolation level \[[16](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/04178561c9d8cd42904ed0a836e08e55b84703cf)\]. Early work on black-box checking focused on strong isolation levels, such as serializability, which was shown to be NP-complete \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/1908.04509)\]. More recently, weak isolation levels like RC, RA, and CC have been shown to be polynomial-time checkable, enabling the development of scalable testing tools \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/1908.04509)\]. However, existing testers often have large polynomial complexity, limiting their applicability to moderate-sized workloads \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/1908.04509)\].

Several tools have been proposed to address these limitations. Elle \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/2003.10554)\] is a checker that infers isolation anomalies by generating client workloads and injecting faults, but it lacks completeness and efficiency for real-world databases. Similarly, PolySI \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/2301.07313)\] and Viper \[[8](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/9f96a48d2bc0e77004b32960495685be6c301c63)\] have been developed to efficiently check Snapshot Isolation (SI), but they are limited to specific isolation levels. Plume \[[5](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/cbe191bda20907994d45bc835d20446968370add)\] introduces a more general approach, supporting multiple weak isolation levels with sound and complete characterizations. However, these tools still face challenges in scalability and efficiency for large workloads.

The complexity of checking weak isolation levels has also been studied in the context of formal verification and automated analysis. For instance, the work by \[[12](http://arxiv.org/abs/1806.08416)\] combines dependency graphs with abstract executions to detect serializability violations under weak consistency models, leveraging SMT solvers for automated reasoning. Similarly, \[[15](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/322ca3c1911230a84aab81b0f5ff8c2b543c56f7)\] proposes a static analysis technique for detecting non-serializable behaviors in causally consistent databases, using SMT solvers to check first-order logic formulas. These approaches, while effective, often struggle with performance and scalability for large-scale systems.

To address these challenges, recent work has focused on improving the efficiency of black-box checkers. For example, \[[16](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/04178561c9d8cd42904ed0a836e08e55b84703cf)\] introduces Emme, a white-box checker that uses version certificate recovery to efficiently validate isolation levels, particularly for histories involving predicate operations. This approach significantly outperforms existing checkers like Elle \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/2003.10554)\] and demonstrates improved speed and accuracy. However, it is still limited to specific database systems and may not be applicable to all weak isolation levels.

In this work, we introduce AWDIT, a novel black-box checker that improves upon existing tools by achieving a provably optimal time complexity for testing RC, RA, and CC. AWDIT runs in $O(n^(3/2))$, $O(n^(3/2))$, and $O(n \cdot k)$ time, respectively, for histories of size $n$ and $k$ sessions. This represents a significant improvement over the state of the art, with experimental results showing average speedups of up to $245\times$ for RC, $193\times$ for RA, and $62\times$ for CC \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/1908.04509)\].
