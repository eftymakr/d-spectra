## Related Works

Label noise is a pervasive issue in deep learning, particularly when training with large-scale datasets that may contain incorrect annotations. Several approaches have been proposed to address this challenge, each with distinct methodologies and theoretical foundations. Early works focused on modifying network architectures or estimating true labels from noisy ones \[[6](http://arxiv.org/abs/1712.09482)\]. For instance, \[[6](http://arxiv.org/abs/1712.09482)\] introduced robust loss functions that are inherently tolerant to label noise, showing that mean absolute error (MAE) is inherently robust to label noise, and that standard back propagation is sufficient to learn the true classifier even under label noise. This work laid the groundwork for later studies that explored loss correction and noise-tolerant training strategies.

More recently, researchers have explored loss correction approaches that are agnostic to both application domain and network architecture \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/1609.03683)\]. These methods rely on estimating the probability of each class being corrupted into another, which can be done using noise estimation techniques adapted to the multi-class setting. The proposed loss correction procedures involve at most a matrix inversion and multiplication, making them computationally efficient. This approach has been validated on a variety of datasets, including MNIST, CIFAR-10, and CIFAR-100, demonstrating its effectiveness in improving model robustness to label noise.

Another line of work focuses on co-teaching, where two neural networks are trained simultaneously, and they teach each other by selecting data with possibly clean labels \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/1804.06872)\]. This approach leverages the observation that deep models first memorize clean labels before noisy ones, and thus, the co-teaching paradigm can help mitigate the impact of noisy labels. The method has been evaluated on noisy versions of MNIST, CIFAR-10, and CIFAR-100, showing superior performance compared to state-of-the-art methods.

In addition to co-teaching, other approaches have been proposed that integrate distillation strategies to learn from noisy labels \[[4](http://arxiv.org/abs/1703.02391)\]. These methods use side information, such as a small clean dataset or label relations in a knowledge graph, to "hedge the risk" of learning from noisy labels. The proposed distillation framework has been evaluated on benchmark datasets in domains such as Sports, Species, and Artifacts, demonstrating its effectiveness in practical settings.

Furthermore, recent work has explored dimensionality-driven learning strategies that monitor the dimensionality of subspaces during training and adapt the loss function accordingly \[[8](http://arxiv.org/abs/1806.02612)\]. This approach is highly tolerant to significant proportions of noisy labels and can effectively learn low-dimensional local subspaces that capture the data distribution.

In summary, the related works on label noise mitigation span a wide range of approaches, from modifying loss functions and network architectures to leveraging semi-supervised learning and distillation strategies. These methods have been validated on various datasets and have shown promising results in improving the robustness of deep neural networks to label noise. The proposed dual debiasing framework in this paper builds upon these prior works, introducing a novel approach to address the biases inherent in perplexity-based methods for noisy annotations.
