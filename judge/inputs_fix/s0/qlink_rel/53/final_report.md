## Related Works

Sequential recommendation systems aim to predict the next item a user will interact with based on their historical behavior \[[9](http://arxiv.org/abs/2001.04830)\]. These systems have evolved significantly, incorporating various deep learning architectures to better capture the complex and dynamic nature of user interactions. Early approaches relied on Markov Chains (MCs), which assume that the next action depends only on the last few actions \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/1808.09781)\]. While effective in sparse datasets, MCs lack the ability to model longer-term dependencies. Recurrent Neural Networks (RNNs) were introduced to address this limitation, allowing for the modeling of longer-term semantics \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/1808.09781)\]. However, RNNs often suffer from vanishing gradient problems and are computationally expensive \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/1808.09781)\].

To overcome these limitations, attention-based models have emerged as a promising alternative. Self-Attention models, such as SASRec \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/1808.09781)\], enable the system to focus on the most relevant items in a user’s history, combining the efficiency of MCs with the expressiveness of RNNs. Similarly, TiSASRec \[[4](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/9c7a455b1e48d01a99e58e884a6c1acb75074ad0)\] extends this idea by incorporating time intervals between interactions, offering a more nuanced understanding of user behavior. These models demonstrate that temporal awareness can significantly improve recommendation accuracy, especially in scenarios where the timing of interactions is critical.

Convolutional Neural Networks (CNNs) have also been applied to sequential recommendation, with models like Caser \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/1809.07426)\] using convolutional filters to capture local features in item sequences. This approach provides a flexible and unified framework for modeling both general preferences and sequential patterns. However, CNNs may struggle with long-range dependencies, which are better handled by transformer-based models \[[13](http://arxiv.org/abs/2202.13556)\]. BERT4Rec \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/1904.06690)\], for instance, employs bidirectional self-attention to capture both left and right context, allowing for more comprehensive modeling of user behavior. This bidirectional approach is particularly beneficial in scenarios where user preferences evolve over time.

In addition to attention-based and CNN-based models, hierarchical attention networks have been proposed to better capture both long-term and short-term user preferences \[[10](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/0b42168f22713c48d8b0ce3bc8f680d347184d88)\]. These models use a two-layer structure to integrate user behavior at different levels, improving the accuracy of recommendations. Similarly, graph neural networks (GNNs) have been applied to session-based recommendations, where they effectively model complex item transitions by treating sessions as graph-structured data \[[12](http://arxiv.org/abs/1811.00855)\]. This approach has shown promising results in capturing the intricate relationships between items within a session.

Recent work has also focused on incorporating temporal information into sequential recommendation models. For example, the Déjà vu model \[[16](http://arxiv.org/abs/2002.00741)\] introduces a contextualized temporal attention mechanism that dynamically weighs the influence of past actions based on both time and context. This approach allows for more accurate predictions by considering how the timing of interactions affects user behavior.

In summary, while various models have been proposed to address the challenges of sequential recommendation, each has its strengths and limitations. Attention-based models offer flexibility and efficiency, CNNs provide local feature extraction, and GNNs excel at modeling complex item transitions. The proposed STAR-Rec model builds upon these advances by integrating preference-aware attention with state-space modeling, offering a novel approach to handle sequence length variations and diverse interaction patterns.

### <a id='category-sequential-recommendation-models'></a>Sequential Recommendation Models: 13 papers found.
Sequential recommendation models have evolved significantly, incorporating advanced techniques to better capture user behavior dynamics. Early approaches relied on Markov Chains (MCs) and Recurrent Neural Networks (RNNs), with MCs excelling in sparse datasets due to their simplicity, while RNNs handle denser data by capturing longer-term semantics \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/1808.09781)\]. More recently, attention-based models such as SASRec \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/1808.09781)\], Caser \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/1809.07426)\], and BERT4Rec \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/1904.06690)\] have emerged, leveraging self-attention mechanisms to effectively model sequential patterns and user preferences. These models address limitations of unidirectional architectures by allowing bidirectional context consideration, enhancing the ability to capture both general preferences and sequential dependencies. Additionally, methods like S3-Rec \[[5](http://arxiv.org/abs/2008.07873)\] introduce self-supervised learning to improve model robustness against data sparsity, while NARM \[[6](http://arxiv.org/abs/1711.04725)\] focuses on session-based recommendations by integrating attention mechanisms to identify user intent within sessions. Feature-level models like FDSA \[[7](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/63555b4f363ee72dd8d49d8deca68760542c0cd2)\] further enhance performance by considering item feature transitions alongside item sequences. Hierarchical attention networks \[[8](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/5574d6123deea94eba2cb35b2486851e826c16ce)\] and graph-based approaches \[[10](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/0b42168f22713c48d8b0ce3bc8f680d347184d88)\] also contribute by modeling long-term and short-term user preferences or capturing complex item transitions. Despite these advancements, challenges remain in handling noise and long-range dependencies, prompting solutions such as filtering techniques \[[11](http://arxiv.org/abs/1511.06939)\] and generative models with holed convolutions \[[14](http://arxiv.org/abs/1808.05163)\]. These developments collectively highlight the ongoing efforts to improve the accuracy and adaptability of sequential recommendation systems.
