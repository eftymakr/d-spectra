## Related Works

Differential privacy (DP) has become the de facto standard for achieving privacy in data analysis \[[20](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/e4ce10063cd25447dcde75c2d9ce327446ced952)\]. However, its implementation is vulnerable to malicious behaviors by servers, especially in distributed settings \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/1811.12469)\]. For instance, servers may sample noise from incorrect distributions or generate correlated noise while appearing to follow established protocols \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/1811.12469)\]. This issue has been explored in several works that examine the robustness of DP mechanisms against adversarial manipulation \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/1909.09630)\]. 

In the local differential privacy (LDP) model, each user perturbs their data locally before sending it to an untrusted data collector \[[14](http://arxiv.org/abs/1606.05053)\]. While LDP provides strong privacy guarantees, it is vulnerable to manipulation attacks where a small fraction of users can obscure the distribution of honest users' inputs \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/1909.09630)\]. This vulnerability has led to the development of countermeasures such as verifiable randomization mechanisms that allow the data collector to verify the completeness of the randomization process \[[4](http://arxiv.org/abs/2104.06569)\]. These mechanisms prevent malicious data providers from manipulating their output before sending it to the collector \[[4](http://arxiv.org/abs/2104.06569)\].

Several studies have focused on improving the utility of LDP protocols while ensuring robustness against adversarial attacks. For example, the paper \[[6](http://arxiv.org/abs/1905.08320)\] discusses the use of frequency oracle (FO) protocols to estimate frequencies of values, and shows that adding post-processing steps can significantly improve accuracy. Similarly, \[[7](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/25b7fe2ac81189952c72c5b869a6be1c5fcf410c)\] introduces PrivKV, a system for key-value data collection under LDP, which improves estimation accuracy through iterative refinement. These works highlight the ongoing efforts to enhance both the utility and robustness of LDP protocols.

The shuffled model of differential privacy, which lies between the local and central models, has also been explored as a way to achieve the accuracy of the central model without requiring a trusted server \[[17](http://arxiv.org/abs/1808.01394)\]. This model introduces an anonymous channel that randomly permutes user-supplied messages, enabling more accurate statistical analysis while preserving privacy. This approach has been shown to provide the power of the central model while avoiding the need to trust a central server \[[17](http://arxiv.org/abs/1808.01394)\].

In addition to these technical contributions, there have been studies that examine the broader implications of DP in real-world applications. For example, \[[18](http://arxiv.org/abs/2005.11753)\] discusses the challenges of publishing streaming data under both centralized and local DP, emphasizing the need for efficient noise calibration techniques to maintain utility. These studies underscore the importance of balancing privacy guarantees with the practicality of deploying DP mechanisms in real-world systems.

In summary, the related works highlight the challenges and opportunities in implementing DP in distributed settings, with a focus on improving robustness against adversarial manipulation and enhancing the utility of privacy-preserving mechanisms. These contributions form the foundation for the proposed Verifiable Distributed Differential Privacy (VDDP) framework \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/1811.12469)\].

### <a id='category-shuffled-model-of-differential-privacy'></a>Shuffled Model of Differential Privacy: 2 papers found.
The Shuffled Model of Differential Privacy offers a middle ground between the local and central models, addressing the trade-offs of accuracy and trust \[[17](http://arxiv.org/abs/1808.01394)\]. In this model, users randomize their data as in the local model, but their outputs are aggregated through an anonymous channel that randomly permutes messages, enhancing privacy without requiring a trusted server \[[17](http://arxiv.org/abs/1808.01394)\]. This approach allows for greater accuracy than the local model while maintaining the privacy guarantees of the local model \[[17](http://arxiv.org/abs/1808.01394)\]. Research has shown that the shuffled model can achieve the same level of accuracy as the central model for certain tasks, such as sum queries, by leveraging the anonymity of the shuffled messages \[[17](http://arxiv.org/abs/1808.01394)\]. Additionally, the shuffled model has been studied for its potential to provide stronger privacy guarantees under certain conditions, with recent work demonstrating that permutation-invariant algorithms satisfying local differential privacy can also satisfy central differential privacy through a new privacy amplification technique \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/1811.12469)\]. This suggests that the privacy cost of LDP protocols may be lower than previously thought when reports are anonymized, which is a key insight for understanding the practical implications of the shuffled model \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/1811.12469)\]. The shuffled model's ability to balance privacy and utility makes it a promising direction for future research in distributed differential privacy \[[17](http://arxiv.org/abs/1808.01394)\].

### <a id='category-local-differential-privacy-(ldp)-models'></a>Local Differential Privacy (LDP) Models: 7 papers found.
Local Differential Privacy (LDP) has been extensively studied for enabling privacy-preserving data analytics in distributed settings, where users perturb their data locally before sending it to an untrusted data collector \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/1811.12469)\]. Research has primarily focused on improving the utility of LDP protocols, such as frequency estimation and heavy hitter identification, while ensuring privacy \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/1911.02046)\]. However, the security of these protocols has received less attention, with studies showing that attackers can inject fake users to manipulate results through data poisoning attacks \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/1911.02046)\]. To address this, researchers have proposed countermeasures, though their effectiveness varies across scenarios \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/1911.02046)\]. Additionally, LDP has been extended to handle more complex data types, including key-value data and multidimensional data, with mechanisms like PrivKV, PrivKVM, and PrivKVM+ that improve estimation accuracy through iterative refinement \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/1909.09630)\]. For repeated data collection, new LDP mechanisms have been developed to maintain strong privacy guarantees over long periods, achieving comparable accuracy to single-round methods \[[4](http://arxiv.org/abs/2104.06569)\]. Furthermore, LDP has been applied to real-world scenarios, such as telemetry data collection, with mechanisms deployed at scale by companies like Microsoft \[[5](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/85983d70db7bef99103c3833793f503c18445546)\]. These advancements highlight the growing interest in LDP for both basic and complex statistical tasks, while also identifying challenges in security and scalability \[[6](http://arxiv.org/abs/1905.08320)\].
