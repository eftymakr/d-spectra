## Related Works

Opinion mining has garnered significant attention for its ability to extract insights from textual data, particularly in the context of user feedback \[[23](http://arxiv.org/abs/1909.05504)\]. While much of the research focuses on sentiment polarity—such as classifying text as positive, negative, or neutral—fine-grained emotion classification remains underexplored, especially in app reviews \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2005.00547)\]. This gap is addressed by several recent works that have attempted to classify emotions in textual data with varying degrees of granularity and accuracy.

The GoEmotions dataset, for instance, represents a significant step forward in emotion classification, offering 58,000 manually annotated Reddit comments labeled for 27 emotion categories or neutral \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2005.00547)\]. This dataset demonstrates the potential for large-scale emotion analysis, though its focus is on general text rather than app reviews. Similarly, the EmoNet paper introduces a large dataset for fine-grained emotion detection, achieving a new state-of-the-art with an average accuracy of 87.58% on 24 emotion types \[[9](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/6a6f1b8ef3eefaf8027a76134859b44886a5f795)\]. These datasets highlight the growing interest in emotion detection, but they are not tailored to the specific context of app reviews.

In the domain of app reviews, several studies have explored the classification of user feedback into categories such as bug reports, feature requests, or praise \[[14](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/af2bd19b042e60183644f94fc4fd122c471981cf)\]. The MARK framework, for example, proposes a keyword-based approach for semi-automated review analysis, allowing developers to identify relevant reviews based on predefined keywords \[[8](http://arxiv.org/abs/1505.04657)\]. This approach helps in filtering out noise and focusing on meaningful feedback, which is crucial for improving user satisfaction.

Another relevant work is the AR-Miner framework, which aims to mine informative reviews from mobile app marketplaces by filtering noisy data, grouping reviews using topic modeling, and prioritizing them based on relevance \[[3](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/ff921a209cc9e1c2120669d3d81e627ca5e53572)\]. This system provides a comprehensive approach to analyzing user feedback, which aligns with the goal of extracting meaningful emotional insights from app reviews.

The study on the automatic classification of app reviews also highlights the challenges of categorizing user feedback accurately \[[4](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/c32ed8d14de92c4a8d8cfbeac7d04e99431887cf)\]. This work emphasizes the need for more sophisticated models and better annotation practices to improve classification performance. Similarly, the analysis of annotated corpora for emotion classification in text underscores the importance of high-quality annotated datasets for training and evaluating emotion detection models \[[12](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/723f744455914bf1ead6ad267976a1500b7dee4a)\].

In summary, while there is a growing body of research on emotion classification in text and app reviews, the specific task of fine-grained emotion analysis in app reviews remains underexplored. The work described in this paper contributes to this area by adapting Plutchik's emotion taxonomy to app reviews, developing a structured annotation framework, and evaluating the feasibility of automating emotion annotation using large language models \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2005.00547)\].
