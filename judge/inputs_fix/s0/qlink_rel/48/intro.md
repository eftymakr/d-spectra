## Related Works

Neural retrieval methods using transformer-based pre-trained language models have significantly advanced multilingual and cross-lingual retrieval \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2004.12832)\]. However, their effectiveness for low-resource, morphologically rich languages such as Amharic remains underexplored due to data scarcity and suboptimal tokenization \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/1911.02116)\]. This challenge is not unique to Amharic; similar issues have been observed in other low-resource languages, where the lack of parallel data and the complexity of morphological structures hinder the performance of cross-lingual retrieval models \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/1907.11692)\]. For instance, the performance gap between high and low-resource languages in cross-lingual tasks has been attributed to unbalanced pre-training data and language bias in multilingual models \[[4](http://arxiv.org/abs/1810.04805)\]. 

To address these limitations, recent work has focused on improving cross-lingual retrieval through techniques such as optimal transport distillation, which transfers knowledge from high-resource to low-resource languages using bitext data \[[5](http://arxiv.org/abs/2201.08471)\]. This approach is particularly relevant for languages like Amharic, where training data is scarce. Additionally, the use of multilingual pre-trained models such as XLM-R has shown promise in cross-lingual settings, though they often require extensive training data and may not perform as well on low-resource languages \[[6](http://arxiv.org/abs/2004.04906)\]. 

The importance of language-specific adaptation has also been emphasized in prior work. For example, the development of language-specific embeddings, such as those derived from monolingual models trained on the target language, has been shown to improve retrieval performance in low-resource settings \[[7](http://arxiv.org/abs/2108.13897)\]. This is consistent with findings that monolingual models can generalize to other languages when trained on large-scale data, suggesting that language-specific adaptation is crucial for effective retrieval in under-resourced languages \[[8](http://arxiv.org/abs/1901.04085)\]. 

In the context of dense retrieval, models like ColBERT have demonstrated the potential of late interaction architectures, which allow for fine-grained modeling of query-document similarity \[[9](http://arxiv.org/abs/1908.10084)\]. ColBERT's ability to precompute document representations offline and leverage vector-similarity indexes has made it a powerful tool for efficient retrieval \[[10](http://arxiv.org/abs/2007.00808)\]. Similarly, the use of Siamese networks to generate sentence embeddings has enabled efficient semantic similarity search, reducing computational overhead while maintaining accuracy \[[11](http://arxiv.org/abs/2112.01488)\]. 

The work presented in this paper builds on these advancements by introducing Amharic-specific dense retrieval models based on pre-trained Amharic BERT and RoBERTa backbones. These models achieve significant improvements over existing multilingual baselines, highlighting the importance of language-specific adaptation in low-resource settings \[[12](http://arxiv.org/abs/1910.11856)\].
