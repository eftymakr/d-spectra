## Related Works

Sensor calibration is a critical step in multi-sensor fusion for autonomous driving systems, ensuring accurate alignment between different modalities such as radar, camera, and LiDAR. Prior work has focused on extrinsic calibration methods for radar-camera systems, often relying on calibration targets or specialized hardware \[[1](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/4d4dbbcf27d991aeab43dafb82a105bb5512b799)\]. However, these methods are not suitable for online automatic calibration, which is essential for long-term system reliability \[[6](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/c80265fa719eb85c8a56a6b36c74b99b793f39b2)\]. Recent efforts have explored data-driven approaches to overcome the limitations of traditional calibration techniques \[[7](http://arxiv.org/abs/1904.08743)\]. For example, \[[1](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/4d4dbbcf27d991aeab43dafb82a105bb5512b799)\] presents a novel open-source tool for extrinsic calibration of radar, camera, and LiDAR, emphasizing joint calibration of all three modalities. While this method improves calibration accuracy, it still requires manual intervention and is not suitable for real-time applications \[[1](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/4d4dbbcf27d991aeab43dafb82a105bb5512b799)\].

Online calibration methods have also been proposed to address the challenge of maintaining sensor alignment during system operation \[[6](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/c80265fa719eb85c8a56a6b36c74b99b793f39b2)\]. In \[[6](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/c80265fa719eb85c8a56a6b36c74b99b793f39b2)\], an online calibration method based on moving object tracking is introduced, leveraging detection and tracking information to estimate sensor parameters. This approach is resource-efficient and suitable for long-term reliability. However, it relies on ego-motion estimation and may not be robust under varying environmental conditions \[[6](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/c80265fa719eb85c8a56a6b36c74b99b793f39b2)\]. Another study \[[7](http://arxiv.org/abs/1904.08743)\] proposes a data-driven method for automatic rotational radar-camera calibration without dedicated calibration targets, using a coarse and fine convolutional neural network. This method demonstrates robustness and generalization capabilities, but it is limited to rotational calibration and does not address full 6DoF calibration \[[7](http://arxiv.org/abs/1904.08743)\].

The use of deep learning for sensor fusion has gained traction in recent years, enabling more accurate and robust perception systems \[[13](http://arxiv.org/abs/2209.06535)\]. For instance, \[[13](http://arxiv.org/abs/2209.06535)\] introduces a novel early fusion approach that exploits both spatial and contextual properties of camera and radar data for 3D object detection. This method outperforms late fusion strategies by effectively integrating information from both modalities. Similarly, \[[14](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/a94f8a34a9c4fce90727f62d652f2420fd1021a2)\] proposes a feature-level fusion detection framework that aligns radar and camera features in the bird's-eye view (BEV) domain, achieving significant improvements in object detection and positioning performance. However, these methods focus on object detection rather than calibration, which is a distinct challenge \[[14](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/a94f8a34a9c4fce90727f62d652f2420fd1021a2)\].

In addition to calibration, sensor fusion techniques have been extensively studied to improve perception accuracy under adverse conditions \[[16](http://arxiv.org/abs/2304.10410)\]. For example, \[[16](http://arxiv.org/abs/2304.10410)\] provides a comprehensive review of radar-camera fusion methods, highlighting the importance of complementary properties between radar and camera for robust perception. The review addresses key questions such as "why to fuse," "what to fuse," and "how to fuse," offering insights into the design of effective fusion strategies \[[16](http://arxiv.org/abs/2304.10410)\]. However, these studies do not directly address the challenge of online automatic calibration, which remains a critical open problem in autonomous driving systems \[[16](http://arxiv.org/abs/2304.10410)\].

In summary, while existing methods have made significant progress in sensor calibration and fusion, there remains a gap in online automatic calibration techniques that can operate without manual intervention or dedicated calibration targets. Our approach addresses this gap by introducing a novel Dual-Perspective representation and Selective Fusion Mechanism, enabling robust and automatic radar-camera calibration during system operation \[[1](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/4d4dbbcf27d991aeab43dafb82a105bb5512b799)\].
