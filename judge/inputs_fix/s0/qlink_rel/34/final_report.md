## Related Works

Time series forecasting has been a central challenge in various domains, including finance, energy, and logistics. Recent advances in deep learning have led to the development of numerous models tailored for time series analysis. Traditional approaches, such as autoregressive models and Gaussian processes, often struggle with capturing complex temporal dependencies and long-term patterns \[[4](http://arxiv.org/abs/1703.07015)\]. To address these limitations, several deep learning-based methods have been proposed. For instance, the LSTNet framework combines convolutional neural networks (CNNs) and recurrent neural networks (RNNs) to extract both short-term and long-term patterns \[[4](http://arxiv.org/abs/1703.07015)\]. Similarly, the Temporal Fusion Transformer (TFT) integrates recurrent layers with self-attention mechanisms to learn interpretable temporal relationships \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/1912.09363)\]. These models have demonstrated significant improvements over classical methods in various applications.

Transformers have also gained traction in time series forecasting due to their ability to capture long-range dependencies \[[9](http://arxiv.org/abs/2202.07125)\]. However, the use of Transformers in this domain has been challenged by issues such as quadratic time complexity and memory bottlenecks \[[5](http://arxiv.org/abs/2205.13504)\]. To mitigate these challenges, several variants have been proposed. For example, the Informer model introduces a ProbSparse self-attention mechanism that reduces computational complexity while maintaining performance \[[17](http://arxiv.org/abs/2012.07436)\]. Similarly, the Autoformer incorporates an Auto-Correlation mechanism to improve efficiency and accuracy in long-term forecasting \[[12](http://arxiv.org/abs/2106.13008)\]. The PatchTST model, on the other hand, employs a patch-based design to reduce attention map complexity and enable longer history attention \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2211.14730)\]. These innovations highlight the ongoing efforts to enhance Transformer-based models for time series tasks.

In addition to Transformers, other architectures have been developed for time series forecasting. The DeepAR model, based on autoregressive recurrent networks, provides probabilistic forecasts and has shown accuracy improvements over state-of-the-art methods \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/1704.04110)\]. The N-BEATS architecture, which uses deep residual blocks, has also demonstrated state-of-the-art performance on diverse datasets \[[13](http://arxiv.org/abs/1905.10437)\]. Furthermore, the Spatio-Temporal Graph Convolutional Network (STGCN) has been introduced to capture spatio-temporal dependencies in traffic forecasting \[[15](http://arxiv.org/abs/1709.04875)\]. These models illustrate the diversity of approaches in time series forecasting, ranging from purely neural network-based methods to hybrid models that combine classical statistical techniques with deep learning.

In the context of financial applications, the challenge of accurate forecasting is compounded by the need for uncertainty quantification and robust allocation strategies. The RTS-PnO framework addresses these challenges by integrating risk-aware forecasting and allocation mechanisms \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2211.14730)\]. This approach aligns with previous work that emphasizes the importance of uncertainty calibration in forecasting \[[18](http://arxiv.org/abs/1905.12417)\]. Additionally, the use of pre-trained models for time series analysis has been explored, with models like the Frozen Pretrained Transformer (FPT) showing promising results across multiple tasks \[[16](http://arxiv.org/abs/2302.11939)\]. These studies underscore the growing interest in leveraging advanced deep learning techniques for financial forecasting and decision-making.
