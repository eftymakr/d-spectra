## Related Works

Generative retrieval (GR) has gained significant attention in information retrieval (IR) due to its ability to directly generate document identifiers (docids) for a given query using autoregressive models \[[12](http://arxiv.org/abs/2204.10628)\]. This approach contrasts with traditional index-retrieve pipelines, which rely on pre-built indexes and separate retrieval and ranking stages \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/2203.00537)\]. Early work on GR focused on static document collections, where models are trained and evaluated on fixed corpora \[[12](http://arxiv.org/abs/2204.10628)\]. However, the practicality of GR in dynamic environments, where documents are continuously added or updated, remains underexplored \[[14](http://arxiv.org/abs/2305.18952)\].

Several studies have investigated the effectiveness of GR in static settings. For example, GENRE \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2010.00904)\] introduced an autoregressive system for entity retrieval, generating entity names token-by-token and achieving state-of-the-art results. Similarly, the Differentiable Search Index (DSI) \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/2202.06991)\] demonstrated that a single Transformer can encode all corpus information, enabling direct query-to-docid mapping. DynamicRetriever \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/2203.00537)\] further advanced this paradigm by eliminating the need for sparse or dense indexes, instead relying solely on a pre-trained model to generate docids during inference. This approach allows for dynamic updates and improved generalization to unseen documents \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/2203.00537)\].

The design of docids plays a crucial role in GR performance. Text-based docids, such as document titles or content-derived identifiers, offer semantic alignment with language models' pre-trained knowledge, enabling better generalization in dynamic settings \[[14](http://arxiv.org/abs/2305.18952)\]. In contrast, numeric-based docids, while efficient, often suffer from overfitting to the initial training set, leading to poor performance when new documents are added \[[14](http://arxiv.org/abs/2305.18952)\]. This issue was observed in experiments where GR models with numeric-based docids showed significant performance degradation over dynamic corpora \[[14](http://arxiv.org/abs/2305.18952)\]. The underperformance of numeric-based docids is attributed to their lack of semantic richness and inability to capture evolving document content \[[14](http://arxiv.org/abs/2305.18952)\].

Recent work has also explored the use of generative models for evidence retrieval in fact verification \[[8](http://arxiv.org/abs/2204.05511)\]. GERE \[[8](http://arxiv.org/abs/2204.05511)\] introduced a generative approach to retrieve both document titles and sentence identifiers, improving efficiency and reducing memory overhead compared to traditional index-retrieve pipelines. Similarly, CorpusBrain \[[9](http://arxiv.org/abs/2208.07652)\] proposed a pre-trained generative retrieval model that eliminates the need for separate indexes, enabling end-to-end optimization. These models demonstrated superior performance on knowledge-intensive tasks, particularly when fine-tuned for specific downstream applications \[[9](http://arxiv.org/abs/2208.07652)\].

In addition, the importance of docid design in dynamic environments has been further emphasized by studies such as Multiview Identifiers Enhanced Generative Retrieval \[[11](http://arxiv.org/abs/2305.16675)\], which proposed synthetic identifiers that integrate contextualized information from document content. This approach allows for more accurate and diverse docid generation, improving retrieval performance in evolving corpora \[[11](http://arxiv.org/abs/2305.16675)\].

Finally, the challenge of continual learning in GR has been addressed by models like CLEVER \[[13](http://arxiv.org/abs/2308.14968)\], which enables incremental indexing of new documents while preserving query performance. This work highlights the need for adaptive and efficient GR models that can handle dynamic document collections without requiring full retraining \[[13](http://arxiv.org/abs/2308.14968)\].
