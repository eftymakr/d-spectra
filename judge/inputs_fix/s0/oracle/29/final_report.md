## Related Works

Maximum Inner Product Search (MIPS) has been a focal point in machine learning and information retrieval, particularly for high-dimensional data. Early approaches to MIPS primarily relied on inner product (IP) similarity, which, while intuitive, suffers from issues such as local optima and redundant computations \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/1606.00185)\]. Alternatively, some methods reduced MIPS to nearest neighbor search under the Euclidean metric through space projection, which, although effective, often leads to topology destruction and information loss \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/1606.00185)\]. Despite these divergent paradigms, recent work has argued that there is no inherent binary opposition between IP and Euclidean metrics \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/1606.00185)\]. Instead, by integrating both metrics in the design of indexing and search algorithms, significant performance improvements can be achieved \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/1606.00185)\].

Several hashing-based methods have been proposed to address MIPS. Asymmetric LSH (ALSH) was introduced as the first provably sublinear time algorithm for approximate MIPS, leveraging asymmetric transformations to convert inner product search into near neighbor search \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/1405.5869)\]. This approach was later improved with a new transformation that converted MIPS into cosine similarity search, which could be efficiently solved using signed random projections \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/1410.5410)\]. These developments highlight the importance of asymmetric hashing in handling the challenges of MIPS \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/1405.5869)\]\[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/1410.5410)\].

Norm-Ranging LSH was introduced to address the limitations of Simple-LSH, which suffered from long tails in the 2-norm distribution of real datasets. By partitioning the dataset into sub-datasets and building hash indexes independently, Norm-Ranging LSH achieved lower query time complexity and better performance \[[4](http://arxiv.org/abs/1809.08782)\]. This approach demonstrates how dataset characteristics can influence the effectiveness of hashing-based methods \[[4](http://arxiv.org/abs/1809.08782)\].

Vector quantization (VQ) techniques have also been explored for MIPS. Traditional VQ methods, such as product quantization (PQ) and additive quantization (AQ), aim to minimize quantization error. However, recent work has shown that quantization errors in norm have a greater impact on inner products than those in direction \[[6](http://arxiv.org/abs/1911.04654)\]. Based on this insight, norm-explicit quantization (NEQ) was proposed, which explicitly quantizes norms to reduce norm-related errors, significantly improving performance on various VQ techniques \[[6](http://arxiv.org/abs/1911.04654)\].

Anisotropic vector quantization has also been introduced to enhance MIPS performance by penalizing the parallel component of a datapoint's residual more than its orthogonal component \[[7](http://arxiv.org/abs/1908.10396)\]. This approach leads to better performance on public benchmarks, demonstrating the effectiveness of tailored quantization strategies \[[7](http://arxiv.org/abs/1908.10396)\].

In addition to hashing and quantization, graph-based methods have been explored for MIPS. The inner-product navigable small world graph (ip-NSW) has achieved state-of-the-art performance by leveraging the norm bias in the MIPS problem \[[9](http://arxiv.org/abs/1909.13459)\]. Further improvements were made with ip-NSW+, which introduces an angular proximity graph to enhance search efficiency \[[9](http://arxiv.org/abs/1909.13459)\].

These developments highlight the diverse approaches to MIPS, ranging from hashing and quantization to graph-based indexing. Each method has its strengths and limitations, and the choice of method often depends on the data topology and application requirements \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/1606.00185)\]\[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/1405.5869)\]\[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/1410.5410)\]\[[4](http://arxiv.org/abs/1809.08782)\]\[[6](http://arxiv.org/abs/1911.04654)\]\[[7](http://arxiv.org/abs/1908.10396)\]\[[9](http://arxiv.org/abs/1909.13459)\].

### <a id='category-norm-explicit-quantization'></a>Norm-Explicit Quantization: 1 papers found.

However, recent research has introduced a new perspective on analyzing quantization error by decomposing it into norm error and direction error \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/1606.00185)\]. This decomposition reveals that quantization errors in the norm have a significantly greater impact on inner products than those in the direction \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/1606.00185)\]. Consequently, small quantization errors do not necessarily lead to good performance in maximum inner product search (MIPS) \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/1606.00185)\]. Based on this insight, norm-explicit quantization (NEQ) was proposed as a general paradigm to improve existing VQ techniques for MIPS by explicitly quantizing the norms of items to reduce norm-related errors, which are crucial for MIPS \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/1606.00185)\]. For direction vectors, NEQ can reuse existing VQ techniques without modification \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/1606.00185)\]. Experimental results on various datasets and parameter configurations demonstrate that NEQ enhances the performance of different VQ methods, including PQ, OPQ, RQ, and AQ, in the context of MIPS \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/1606.00185)\].
