## Related Works

3D human pose estimation has attracted significant research interest due to its applications in action recognition, human-computer interaction, and augmented reality. Early methods primarily relied on 2D pose estimation followed by 3D reconstruction using geometric constraints or prior knowledge \[[11](http://arxiv.org/abs/1705.03098)\]. However, these approaches often suffer from depth ambiguities and self-occlusions \[[1](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/50167a9e6bdc1f64a58fe8a3c990105fec0bd2bc)\]. Recent advances have focused on leveraging deep learning to directly estimate 3D poses from 2D inputs, with an emphasis on capturing spatial and temporal dependencies \[[16](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/0646857f867d9a624f2b53d1ffb4de99e225b2d3)\]. 

Several works have explored the use of graph-based models to exploit the structural relationships between body joints. For instance, \[[1](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/50167a9e6bdc1f64a58fe8a3c990105fec0bd2bc)\] proposed a graph convolutional network to model spatial-temporal relationships, while \[[12](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/2170b81eaac4095f5948b7922f29200a25db9b2b)\] introduced a high-order GCN to capture long-range dependencies. These approaches have shown promise in improving the accuracy of 3D pose estimation by incorporating anatomical knowledge and modeling complex interactions between joints \[[1](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/50167a9e6bdc1f64a58fe8a3c990105fec0bd2bc)\]. However, they often struggle with the computational complexity of modeling long-range dependencies \[[12](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/2170b81eaac4095f5948b7922f29200a25db9b2b)\].

To address the limitations of traditional GCNs, \[[16](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/0646857f867d9a624f2b53d1ffb4de99e225b2d3)\] introduced a modulated GCN that allows for dynamic modulation of graph structures, enabling the model to adapt to different motion patterns. This approach significantly improves the accuracy of 3D pose estimation while maintaining a compact model size \[[16](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/0646857f867d9a624f2b53d1ffb4de99e225b2d3)\]. Similarly, \[[24](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/92d61a05d76cc859c073e57eedbd82186aecdaff)\] proposed a novel Spatio-Temporal Criss-Cross attention (STC) block that decomposes correlation learning into space and time, leading to improved performance on benchmark datasets \[[24](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/92d61a05d76cc859c073e57eedbd82186aecdaff)\].

Another line of research focuses on temporal modeling to enhance the consistency of 3D pose estimation across frames. \[[6](http://arxiv.org/abs/1711.08585)\] introduced a sequence-to-sequence network with LSTM units to enforce temporal smoothness, while \[[8](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/30de7fecb146356d7ae6cafa072997674601e0f5)\] proposed an attention-based framework that adaptively identifies significant frames for more accurate estimation. These methods have demonstrated that leveraging temporal information can significantly reduce jitter and improve the overall quality of 3D pose sequences \[[6](http://arxiv.org/abs/1711.08585)\]\[[8](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/30de7fecb146356d7ae6cafa072997674601e0f5)\].

In addition to graph-based and attention-based approaches, some works have explored transformer architectures for 3D human pose estimation. \[[7](http://arxiv.org/abs/2103.10455)\] introduced PoseFormer, a purely transformer-based approach that models both spatial and temporal correlations effectively. This method has achieved state-of-the-art results on standard benchmarks \[[7](http://arxiv.org/abs/2103.10455)\]. More recently, \[[26](http://arxiv.org/abs/2310.16288)\] proposed MotionAGFormer, which combines transformer and GCN modules to better capture local and global dependencies, achieving superior performance with fewer parameters \[[26](http://arxiv.org/abs/2310.16288)\].

The challenge of modeling long-range dependencies remains a critical issue in 3D human pose estimation. To address this, \[[28](http://arxiv.org/abs/2108.07181)\] proposed a novel skeletal GNN learning solution that effectively captures relevant information from neighboring nodes while suppressing noise. This approach has shown significant improvements in accuracy, particularly for challenging poses \[[28](http://arxiv.org/abs/2108.07181)\].

In summary, the field of 3D human pose estimation has seen substantial progress, with various methods exploring graph-based, attention-based, and transformer architectures to capture spatial and temporal dependencies. While these approaches have achieved promising results, there is still room for improvement in terms of computational efficiency and handling of complex motion patterns.

### <a id='category-graph-convolutional-networks-(gcns)-for-pose-estimation'></a>Graph Convolutional Networks (GCNs) for Pose Estimation: 10 papers found.
Graph Convolutional Networks (GCNs) have been extensively explored for 3D human pose estimation, with various approaches addressing challenges such as depth ambiguity, self-occlusions, and long-range dependencies between body joints \[[1](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/50167a9e6bdc1f64a58fe8a3c990105fec0bd2bc)\]. Early methods focused on incorporating spatial and temporal consistency through graph-based architectures, leveraging domain knowledge about human body configurations to improve accuracy \[[1](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/50167a9e6bdc1f64a58fe8a3c990105fec0bd2bc)\]. Subsequent works introduced multi-scale architectures, such as the Graph Stacked Hourglass Networks, which process features across different skeletal scales to capture both local and global representations \[[5](http://arxiv.org/abs/2103.16385)\]. Higher-order GCNs were also proposed to model long-range interactions by aggregating features from nodes at various distances, improving the ability to capture complex joint relationships \[[12](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/2170b81eaac4095f5948b7922f29200a25db9b2b)\]. Additionally, residual connections were integrated to preserve important information from initial features as the network depth increases \[[9](http://arxiv.org/abs/2111.00950)\]. More recently, models like the Global-local Adaptive Graph Convolutional Network (GLA-GCN) have been developed to improve performance by leveraging ground truth 2D poses and incorporating global and local feature learning \[[14](http://arxiv.org/abs/2307.05853)\]. Furthermore, the Modulated GCN introduced weight and affinity modulation to adaptively adjust feature transformations and graph structures, enhancing performance while maintaining a compact model size \[[16](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/0646857f867d9a624f2b53d1ffb4de99e225b2d3)\]. The Semantic Graph Convolutional Networks (SemGCN) further advanced the field by learning semantic relationships between nodes through end-to-end training, achieving superior performance with fewer parameters \[[18](http://arxiv.org/abs/1904.03345)\]. The U-shaped GCN (UGCN) also contributed by introducing a motion loss and capturing both short-term and long-term motion information \[[20](http://arxiv.org/abs/2004.13985)\]. Finally, the Locally Connected Network (LCN) was proposed to overcome the limitations of GCNs by improving representation power and generalization ability \[[23](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/442928cd95d7bca15800b29ce2e2f8db5461bd58)\]. These advancements collectively highlight the evolving strategies for leveraging graph-based methods to enhance 3D human pose estimation.

### <a id='category-2d-to-3d-reconstruction-methods'></a>2D to 3D Reconstruction Methods: 6 papers found.
Recent advances in 2D-to-3D human pose reconstruction have focused on leveraging deep learning to bridge the gap between 2D joint detections and 3D spatial representations. Many approaches adopt a two-step strategy, first estimating 2D poses using state-of-the-art detectors and then mapping these to 3D space through regression or optimization \[[11](http://arxiv.org/abs/1705.03098)\].
