## Related Works

Video super-resolution (VSR) aims to generate high-resolution (HR) video from low-resolution (LR) inputs, leveraging both spatial and temporal information \[[1](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/c6a8ff4d9dcdf9e5d82310b847af999ed6655f65)\]. Early approaches relied on handcrafted features and optical flow estimation to align frames and enhance details \[[2](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/755227d7ea41b1d90df31f411f9ecbc998c20d26)\]. However, these methods often suffered from computational inefficiency and limited performance due to the complexity of motion estimation \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/2201.12288)\]. More recently, deep learning-based methods have emerged as a promising alternative, offering better accuracy and flexibility \[[4](http://arxiv.org/abs/1812.02898)\].

Several works have focused on improving temporal consistency in VSR by incorporating recurrent structures or attention mechanisms. For instance, the Frame-Recurrent Video Super-Resolution framework uses a recurrent architecture to model temporal dependencies and reduce computational costs by warping only one image per step \[[5](http://arxiv.org/abs/2112.10752)\]. Similarly, the Video Super-Resolution via Bidirectional Recurrent Convolutional Networks leverages bidirectional recurrent convolutional networks to efficiently model long-term temporal dependencies while maintaining low computational complexity \[[6](http://arxiv.org/abs/1801.04590)\]. These methods demonstrate the effectiveness of recurrent architectures in capturing motion patterns across frames \[[7](http://arxiv.org/abs/1707.02921)\].

Another line of research has explored the use of attention mechanisms to enhance feature alignment and fusion. The Progressive Fusion Video Super-Resolution Network introduces a progressive fusion strategy that effectively utilizes spatio-temporal information and avoids the need for explicit motion compensation \[[8](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/a2c50f93f6d0892918e0ff653750d198ccab48d0)\]. Similarly, the Video Restoration Transformer (VRT) employs a transformer-based architecture with parallel frame prediction ability, enabling efficient and accurate video restoration tasks such as super-resolution and deblurring \[[9](http://arxiv.org/abs/1611.05250)\]. These models highlight the potential of attention mechanisms in capturing complex spatio-temporal relationships \[[10](http://arxiv.org/abs/2007.10595)\].

Recent advances have also focused on improving the efficiency and quality of VSR by integrating advanced neural network designs. The Enhanced Deep Residual Networks for Single Image Super-Resolution introduce a more effective residual learning strategy, leading to improved performance in image super-resolution tasks \[[11](http://arxiv.org/abs/1905.02716)\]. The Video Super-Resolution with Recurrent Structure-Detail Network proposes a novel recurrent framework that divides the input into structure and detail components, allowing for more effective exploitation of previous frames \[[12](http://arxiv.org/abs/2206.02146)\]. These methods demonstrate the importance of designing efficient and effective network architectures for VSR \[[13](http://arxiv.org/abs/1511.04587)\].

In addition, the use of diffusion models has gained attention for their ability to generate high-quality images and videos. However, diffusion-based VSR methods often suffer from artifacts and degradations due to their inherent randomness \[[14](http://arxiv.org/abs/2008.00455)\]. To address this, recent works have explored the integration of self-supervised learning and advanced neural network architectures to improve stability and quality \[[15](http://arxiv.org/abs/1807.02758)\]. For example, the Real-Time Video Super-Resolution with Spatio-Temporal Networks and Motion Compensation introduces a spatio-temporal sub-pixel convolution network that effectively exploits temporal redundancies while maintaining real-time speed \[[16](http://arxiv.org/abs/2104.13371)\]. These approaches demonstrate the potential of combining diffusion models with advanced network designs to achieve better performance in VSR \[[17](http://arxiv.org/abs/2002.11616)\].
