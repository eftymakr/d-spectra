## Related Works

Multi-Domain Recommendation (MDR) has emerged as a promising approach to improve recommendation performance by leveraging knowledge transfer across different domains \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2103.01696)\]. Traditional MDR methods often assume a shared structure for transferring domain knowledge, but this approach can lead to the Negative Transfer Problem (NTP) when there is knowledge conflict or domain quality issues \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/1804.06769)\]. The NTP occurs when transferring information from one domain negatively affects the performance of another domain \[[3](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/2b031c7170c9dccbe506f3d3a1d6104f65e835ae)\]. To address this challenge, several approaches have been proposed to model domain relationships and selectively transfer knowledge.

One line of work focuses on modeling domain relationships using graph-based methods. For instance, BiTGCF introduces a bi-directional transfer learning method that uses Graph Collaborative Filtering networks to capture high-order interactions and enable two-way knowledge transfer between domains \[[4](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/d5630291a4ba3f6266c77e6d00920502f986afa6)\]. Similarly, MAGRec employs graph neural networks to learn sequential user interactions across domains, improving representation by capturing both intra- and inter-domain relationships \[[5](http://arxiv.org/abs/1910.05189)\]. These methods highlight the importance of modeling domain interactions to avoid NTP.

Another approach is to use multi-task learning (MTL) frameworks that allow for joint optimization across domains. For example, PLE proposes a progressive layered extraction model with a novel sharing structure to improve joint representation learning and reduce negative transfer \[[6](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/24a473700894dbfab0bfd9dc3d542acfeef66bca)\]. MAMDR, on the other hand, introduces a model-agnostic learning framework that enables scalable MDR by addressing domain conflicts and improving generalizability \[[7](http://arxiv.org/abs/1203.3535)\]. These methods emphasize the need for flexible and adaptive architectures to handle domain-specific characteristics.

In addition, some works focus on improving the quality of domain knowledge by leveraging side information. For example, RC-DFM deeply fuses review texts and item contents with ratings to enhance recommendation performance for cold-start users \[[8](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/7807d924fa46caec7b706b98156962caf8458d59)\]. Similarly, DDTCDR introduces a deep dual transfer model that explicitly models user and item features to improve cross-domain recommendations \[[9](http://arxiv.org/abs/2211.11964)\]. These approaches demonstrate the value of incorporating rich side information to enhance domain knowledge transfer.

Furthermore, the importance of domain selection in MDR has been explored. For instance, CoNet introduces collaborative cross networks that enable dual knowledge transfer by connecting hidden layers of base networks \[[10](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/fa990aee9a8f157b5d393f5f3eaa014e1e5c67aa)\]. DTCDR proposes a framework for dual-target cross-domain recommendation, where embeddings are shared across domains to improve performance on both richer and sparser domains \[[11](http://arxiv.org/abs/2203.16863)\]. These methods highlight the need for dynamic and selective domain selection to avoid NTP.

To address the limitations of existing methods, this paper proposes a lightweight and dynamic Similar Domain Selection Principle (SDSP) that explicitly measures domain-level gaps and dynamically selects appropriate domains for knowledge transfer \[[12](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/cb919954e93726aff490458bd61d6b3d8155fbd7)\]. SDSP introduces a novel prototype-based domain distance measure to model complex domain relationships and enables supervised and unsupervised domain selection \[[13](http://arxiv.org/abs/2202.12524)\]. This approach is designed to be compatible with existing MDR methods and avoids introducing significant overhead \[[14](http://arxiv.org/abs/2101.11427)\].
