## Related Works

Brain tumor segmentation remains a critical challenge in medical image analysis due to the high variability in tumor morphology, intensity, and spatial distribution across multimodal MRI scans \[[1](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/420f3f1078a6d8e0696572c032877079286051c6)\]. Early approaches relied on manual segmentation, which is time-consuming and prone to inter-observer variability \[[9](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/580062407427236ced45253a2ff7df2e147a81e2)\]. With the advent of deep learning, convolutional neural networks (CNNs) have become the dominant paradigm for automated segmentation, offering improved accuracy and consistency \[[15](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/f89fe7d194c63a62b2cb76fac08ef525b483b964)\]. 

Several studies have explored the use of U-Net architectures for brain tumor segmentation, leveraging their encoder-decoder structure to capture contextual information while maintaining precise localization \[[5](http://arxiv.org/abs/1505.04597)\]. The original U-Net was extended to 3D for volumetric MRI data, enabling dense segmentation of brain tumors \[[27](http://arxiv.org/abs/1606.06650)\]. However, training 3D CNNs remains computationally intensive, especially with limited annotated datasets \[[19](http://arxiv.org/abs/2011.11052)\]. To address this, researchers have proposed strategies such as data augmentation, transfer learning, and efficient network designs \[[16](http://arxiv.org/abs/1811.01328)\]. 

The nnU-Net framework represents a significant advancement by introducing a self-adapting architecture that optimizes model parameters based on the input data \[[25](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/f28e387d4229c5f690ce4570a391c0f47e7155c7)\]. This framework has achieved state-of-the-art performance in the BraTS challenges, demonstrating its robustness across different tumor sub-regions \[[18](http://arxiv.org/abs/2011.00848)\]. Despite its success, nnU-Net requires extensive training and may not fully utilize pre-trained weights from other domains \[[1](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/420f3f1078a6d8e0696572c032877079286051c6)\]. 

To enhance performance and reduce training requirements, recent work has integrated pre-trained models from ImageNet into medical segmentation tasks \[[1](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/420f3f1078a6d8e0696572c032877079286051c6)\]. For instance, the use of 2D pre-trained weights in 3D architectures has been explored, with strategies to preserve learned relationships and feature representations \[[1](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/420f3f1078a6d8e0696572c032877079286051c6)\]. This approach has led to reduced training epochs and improved efficiency \[[1](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/420f3f1078a6d8e0696572c032877079286051c6)\]. Additionally, some studies have combined segmentation with classification tasks, leveraging pre-trained encoders for glioma grade prediction to improve segmentation accuracy \[[28](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/281ede296764b95c329a7093e3b40b0b0c3ddee8)\]. 

Efforts to reduce computational overhead have also focused on lightweight architectures. For example, the LATUP-Net introduces parallel convolutions and attention mechanisms to achieve high segmentation performance with significantly fewer parameters \[[12](http://arxiv.org/abs/2404.05911)\]. Similarly, the HDC-Net replaces 3D convolutions with a novel hierarchical decoupled convolution module, reducing computational complexity while maintaining accuracy \[[20](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/9bf1c55d5441b29acf35c2193342723187dbe896)\]. These models demonstrate that efficiency and accuracy can be balanced through architectural innovations. 

In addition to architectural improvements, post-processing techniques such as fully connected CRFs have been employed to refine segmentation outputs and reduce false positives \[[6](http://arxiv.org/abs/1603.05959)\]. The use of multi-scale features and dense connections has also been shown to enhance the ability of networks to capture both local and global contextual information \[[14](http://arxiv.org/abs/2010.13082)\]. 

Overall, the field has seen a shift towards more efficient and adaptable models that can handle the complexity of brain tumor segmentation while reducing computational demands \[[24](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/8689d964b3d1acacfa698c9d81314a9571819757)\]. These advancements underscore the importance of integrating pre-trained models, optimizing network structures, and leveraging multi-task learning to improve performance in real-world clinical settings \[[28](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/281ede296764b95c329a7093e3b40b0b0c3ddee8)\].
