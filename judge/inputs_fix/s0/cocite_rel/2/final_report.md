## Related Works

Nonverbal communication plays a crucial role in human interaction, encompassing gestures, facial expressions, and body language that convey intent and emotion \[[7](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/4814337c5145dbea034b9c646163dc2e71e8ebff)\]. Existing large language models (LLMs) have primarily focused on text-based understanding and generation, often neglecting the integration of nonverbal cues \[[7](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/4814337c5145dbea034b9c646163dc2e71e8ebff)\]. This limitation restricts their ability to create fully immersive conversational experiences \[[7](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/4814337c5145dbea034b9c646163dc2e71e8ebff)\]. Recent efforts have sought to address this gap by exploring multimodal approaches that incorporate visual and acoustic modalities \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/1802.00923)\]. For instance, the Multi-attention Recurrent Network (MARN) was introduced to understand human communication by integrating language, vision, and acoustic modalities \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/1802.00923)\]. MARN utilizes a Multi-attention Block to discover interactions between modalities over time and stores these interactions in a hybrid memory using the Long-short Term Hybrid Memory (LSTHM) \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/1802.00923)\]. This model demonstrates state-of-the-art performance on multimodal sentiment analysis and speaker trait recognition \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/1802.00923)\].

Similarly, the CMU-MOSEI dataset and the Dynamic Fusion Graph (DFG) technique were developed to analyze human multimodal language, emphasizing the need for large-scale datasets and interpretable fusion methods \[[4](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/006fdeff6e1a81c404317ee4056d6cc72f9c0e50)\]. The DFG approach allows for the exploration of how modalities interact in human multimodal language, offering competitive performance compared to previous state-of-the-art methods \[[4](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/006fdeff6e1a81c404317ee4056d6cc72f9c0e50)\]. Another notable work is the introduction of the Qwen-VL series, which combines vision and language understanding, enabling tasks such as image description, question-answering, and visual grounding \[[8](http://arxiv.org/abs/2308.12966)\]. These models are trained on large-scale multimodal corpora and demonstrate strong performance across various benchmarks \[[8](http://arxiv.org/abs/2308.12966)\].

The Kosmos-1 model also highlights the importance of multimodal perception and language understanding, showcasing capabilities in vision tasks, perception-language tasks, and even OCR-free NLP \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2302.14045)\]. Kosmos-1 is trained on web-scale multimodal corpora and supports zero-shot and few-shot learning, demonstrating the potential of multimodal large language models (MLLMs) \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2302.14045)\]. Furthermore, the Kosmos-2 model extends these capabilities by incorporating grounding to the visual world, enabling tasks such as referring expression comprehension and phrase grounding \[[12](http://arxiv.org/abs/2306.14824)\].

In addition, the Macaw-LLM model integrates visual, audio, and textual information, demonstrating the effectiveness of multi-modal language modeling \[[9](http://arxiv.org/abs/2306.09093)\]. The model's alignment module bridges multi-modal features to textual features, simplifying the adaptation process from modality modules to the cognitive module \[[9](http://arxiv.org/abs/2306.09093)\]. Similarly, the Video-ChatGPT model merges a video-adapted visual encoder with a large language model (LLM), enabling detailed conversations about videos \[[6](http://arxiv.org/abs/2306.05424)\]. This model is trained on a large dataset of video-instruction pairs and includes a quantitative evaluation framework for video-based dialogue models \[[6](http://arxiv.org/abs/2306.05424)\].

Finally, the ChatBridge model leverages large language models (LLMs) to bridge modalities, demonstrating zero-shot capabilities for diverse multimodal inputs \[[10](http://arxiv.org/abs/2305.16103)\]. These models highlight the ongoing research in multimodal language modeling and the integration of nonverbal cues in conversational AI.
