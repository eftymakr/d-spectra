## Related Works

Vectorized embeddings play a crucial role in modern recommender systems, enabling efficient user-item matching by capturing latent relationships in a compact form \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/1706.02216)\]. However, the computational and memory costs of maintaining high-precision embeddings can be prohibitive, especially for large-scale applications \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/1806.01973)\]. To address this, representation binarization has emerged as a promising approach, where embeddings are converted into binary sequences to reduce storage and computation \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/1905.08108)\]. This technique has been widely studied in the context of collaborative filtering, where the goal is to learn compact yet informative representations that preserve the essential structure of the data \[[4](http://arxiv.org/abs/1609.02907)\].

Several works have explored the use of binary embeddings for recommendation tasks. For instance, BinaryConnect \[[10](http://arxiv.org/abs/1511.00363)\] introduced a method for training deep neural networks with binary weights during forward and backward propagation, which reduces computational overhead while maintaining model accuracy. This approach has been influential in the design of efficient neural architectures for recommendation systems \[[11](http://arxiv.org/abs/1403.6652)\]. Similarly, Variational Autoencoders (VAEs) have been extended to collaborative filtering, leveraging probabilistic models to generate binary representations that capture user-item interactions \[[6](http://arxiv.org/abs/1802.05814)\]. These models often rely on multinomial likelihoods and Bayesian inference to improve robustness and generalization \[[6](http://arxiv.org/abs/1802.05814)\].

In the context of graph-based recommendation systems, GraphSAGE \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/1706.02216)\] and Graph Convolutional Networks (GCNs) \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/1806.01973)\] have been widely used to generate node embeddings that capture both structural and feature information. These models are particularly effective in handling large-scale graphs, where traditional methods may struggle with scalability \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/1806.01973)\]. However, they typically require all nodes to be present during training, limiting their applicability to unseen data \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/1706.02216)\]. To overcome this limitation, inductive methods such as GraphSAGE have been developed, allowing for the generation of embeddings for previously unseen nodes \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/1706.02216)\].

Recent advances in collaborative filtering have also focused on integrating user-item interactions into the embedding process. Neural Graph Collaborative Filtering (NGCF) \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/1905.08108)\] explicitly incorporates the bipartite graph structure by propagating embeddings through the graph, thereby capturing high-order connectivity and collaborative signals \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/1905.08108)\]. This approach has shown significant improvements over traditional matrix factorization and other deep learning-based methods \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/1905.08108)\]. Similarly, Neural Collaborative Filtering (NCF) \[[8](http://arxiv.org/abs/1708.05031)\] uses deep neural networks to model user-item interactions, replacing the inner product with a more flexible architecture that can learn non-linear relationships \[[8](http://arxiv.org/abs/1708.05031)\].

In addition to these methods, ranking-based approaches such as BPR \[[7](http://arxiv.org/abs/1205.2618)\] and VBPR \[[15](http://arxiv.org/abs/1510.01784)\] have been developed to optimize personalized recommendations by directly modeling the ranking objective. These models leverage implicit feedback and side information to improve the quality of recommendations \[[7](http://arxiv.org/abs/1205.2618)\]. However, they often require extensive training data and may struggle with cold start problems \[[15](http://arxiv.org/abs/1510.01784)\].

Overall, the field of recommendation systems has seen significant advancements in the use of vectorized embeddings and graph-based models. These approaches have been tailored to address specific challenges, such as scalability, efficiency, and the incorporation of side information. The work presented in this paper builds upon these foundations, introducing a novel framework for graph representation binarization that explicitly mitigates information loss during the embedding process \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/1706.02216)\].
