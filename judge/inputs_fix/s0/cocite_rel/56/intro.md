## Related Works

Cross-domain Sequential Recommendation (CDSR) has gained significant attention due to its ability to alleviate data sparsity and cold-start problems by leveraging information across multiple domains \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2304.03891)\]. Early approaches primarily focused on transferring knowledge from a source domain to a target domain using latent space mappings \[[7](http://arxiv.org/abs/1910.05189)\]. However, these methods often relied on overlapping users, limiting their applicability in real-world scenarios where user interactions are sparse \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2304.03891)\]. To address this, several works have proposed graph-based models to capture both intra- and inter-sequence relationships \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/2106.02768)\]. For instance, C2DSR \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2304.03891)\] introduces a graph neural network to mine inter-sequence item collaborative relationships and a sequential attentive encoder to capture intra-sequence item relationships, enabling joint learning of single- and cross-domain user preferences. Similarly, DA-GCN \[[6](http://arxiv.org/abs/2105.03300)\] utilizes domain-aware graph convolution networks to learn user-specific node representations, enhancing cross-domain recommendations by considering explicit cross-domain graph structures.

Another line of research focuses on dual learning mechanisms to iteratively transfer information between domains \[[7](http://arxiv.org/abs/1910.05189)\]. The DASL model \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/2106.02768)\] introduces dual embedding and dual attention components to simultaneously learn user preferences in both domains. This approach improves cross-domain recommendations by matching latent embeddings with candidate items through a dual-attention learning mechanism. Similarly, DDTCDR \[[7](http://arxiv.org/abs/1910.05189)\] employs a latent orthogonal mapping to preserve relations between users across different latent spaces, enhancing cross-domain knowledge transfer. These methods highlight the importance of bidirectional latent relations and domain-specific preferences in improving cross-domain recommendation performance.

Recent studies have also explored the use of attention mechanisms to model complex sequential patterns \[[10](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/fa36b8d483b06e0bce366970fde747761151f411)\]. For example, the Mixed Information Flow Network \[[5](http://arxiv.org/abs/2012.00485)\] incorporates behavior transfer and knowledge transfer units to capture both behavioral and knowledge flows across domains. This allows the model to decide when and which cross-domain information to use based on users' current preferences. Similarly, the DREAM model \[[12](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/96bfe33a2503e960153a934e10be91da4d8b13a6)\] introduces a decoupled representation framework that simultaneously learns single- and cross-domain user preferences. It leverages supervised contrastive learning and focal loss to address data sparsity and class imbalance issues, demonstrating significant improvements over existing methods.

In addition to these, several works have focused on improving the generalization and adaptability of cross-domain recommendation models \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2304.03891)\]. For instance, the π-Net \[[16](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/fa990aee9a8f157b5d393f5f3eaa014e1e5c67aa)\] and PSJNet \[[18](http://arxiv.org/abs/1910.02448)\] models address the challenges of shared accounts and cross-domain settings by introducing parallel information-sharing and split-join architectures. These models effectively handle mixed user behaviors and enable simultaneous recommendations across domains. Furthermore, the IESRec model \[[19](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/44ecd5740798b8d90818fde07ea5bfceee22b83b)\] proposes joint internal multi-interest exploration and external domain alignment modules to better capture user preferences and reduce domain discrepancies.

In summary, existing approaches to CDSR have made significant strides in modeling cross-domain interactions and improving recommendation performance. However, challenges such as data sparsity, overlap limitations, and complex transition patterns remain. The proposed LLM4CDSR model  addresses these challenges by leveraging the powerful representation and reasoning capabilities of Large Language Models (LLMs) to capture semantic item relationships and user preferences.
