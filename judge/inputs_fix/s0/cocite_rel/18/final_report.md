## Related Works

Anomaly detection has gained significant attention in recent years due to its critical role in quality control and fault diagnosis in industrial settings. Several approaches have been proposed to address the challenges of anomaly detection, particularly in the context of unsupervised and one-class learning scenarios, where only normal data is available for training \[[11](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/3aa681914a7da79f7d7293f51a058eefe61c8bb7)\]. Early methods relied on handcrafted features and classical machine learning models, but recent advances have shifted toward deep learning-based techniques that leverage the power of convolutional neural networks (CNNs) for feature extraction and anomaly localization \[[11](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/3aa681914a7da79f7d7293f51a058eefe61c8bb7)\].

One of the most influential works in this area is the MVTec AD dataset, which provides a comprehensive benchmark for evaluating unsupervised anomaly detection methods. This dataset contains 5354 high-resolution images with over 70 different types of defects, including scratches, dents, and contaminations, and includes pixel-accurate ground truth annotations \[[11](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/3aa681914a7da79f7d7293f51a058eefe61c8bb7)\]. The dataset has been widely used to evaluate the performance of various deep learning-based approaches, such as convolutional autoencoders, generative adversarial networks (GANs), and feature descriptors using pre-trained CNNs \[[11](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/3aa681914a7da79f7d7293f51a058eefe61c8bb7)\].

PatchCore \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2106.08265)\] and PaDiM \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/2011.08785)\] are two notable methods that have achieved state-of-the-art results on the MVTec AD benchmark. PatchCore utilizes a maximally representative memory bank of nominal patch-features to improve detection and localization accuracy, achieving an AUROC score of up to 99.6% \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2106.08265)\]. PaDiM, on the other hand, employs a pretrained CNN for patch embedding and multivariate Gaussian distributions to model the normal class, while also exploiting correlations between different semantic levels of the CNN for better localization \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/2011.08785)\]. Both methods have demonstrated competitive performance in industrial settings, particularly for real-time applications \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2106.08265)\]\[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/2011.08785)\].

Another approach that has shown promise is the use of hybrid fusion schemes for multimodal anomaly detection. The M3DM method \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/2303.00601)\] combines 3D point cloud and RGB image data using a hybrid fusion strategy that includes unsupervised feature fusion with patch-wise contrastive learning and decision layer fusion with multiple memory banks. This approach has been evaluated on the MVTec-3D AD dataset, where it outperforms existing methods in both detection and segmentation precision \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/2303.00601)\].

In addition to industrial applications, anomaly detection has also been explored in the medical domain. The BMAD benchmark \[[4](http://arxiv.org/abs/2306.11876)\] provides a standardized evaluation framework for medical anomaly detection, covering six reorganized datasets from five medical domains, including brain MRI, liver CT, and retinal OCT. This benchmark includes fifteen state-of-the-art anomaly detection algorithms and three key evaluation metrics, enabling researchers to compare and evaluate different methods in a fair and comprehensive manner \[[4](http://arxiv.org/abs/2306.11876)\].

Recent work has also focused on improving the efficiency and scalability of anomaly detection models, particularly for deployment on resource-constrained embedded devices. The DRÆM method \[[6](http://arxiv.org/abs/2108.07610)\] proposes a discriminatively trained reconstruction anomaly embedding model that enables direct anomaly localization without the need for additional post-processing steps. This approach has shown significant improvements in both detection and localization accuracy on the MVTec dataset \[[6](http://arxiv.org/abs/2108.07610)\]. Similarly, the UniAD method \[[7](http://arxiv.org/abs/2206.03687)\] introduces a unified framework for multi-class anomaly detection, which addresses the challenge of training separate models for different object classes by leveraging query embedding and attention mechanisms \[[7](http://arxiv.org/abs/2206.03687)\].

### <a id='category-anomaly-detection-frameworks'></a>Anomaly Detection Frameworks: 9 papers found.
In the realm of anomaly detection frameworks, recent advancements have focused on improving efficiency and performance for industrial applications \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2106.08265)\]. Methods such as PatchCore leverage maximally representative memory banks of nominal patch-features to achieve state-of-the-art results on benchmarks like MVTec AD, with an AUROC score of up to 99.6% \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2106.08265)\]. Similarly, PaDiM employs pretrained CNNs and multivariate Gaussian distributions for probabilistic normal class modeling, offering competitive performance in both detection and localization \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/2011.08785)\]. Other approaches, like DRÆM, treat anomaly detection as a discriminative problem, learning a joint representation of an anomalous image and its reconstruction to enable direct localization without post-processing \[[6](http://arxiv.org/abs/2108.07610)\]. The challenge of training models with limited data has also been addressed, as seen in UniAD, which uses a unified framework for multi-class anomaly detection and surpasses existing methods by a significant margin \[[7](http://arxiv.org/abs/2206.03687)\]. Additionally, generative models such as AnoDDPM have been adapted for unsupervised anomaly detection, using denoising diffusion probabilistic models to improve scalability and performance on high-resolution imagery \[[9](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/20f6fce7726e7b3ab4ca45ef40d92b79f093f825)\]. Memory-augmented autoencoders, such as MemAE, further enhance detection by incorporating memory modules to strengthen reconstruction errors on anomalies \[[10](http://arxiv.org/abs/1904.02639)\]. These developments highlight the ongoing efforts to balance accuracy, efficiency, and adaptability in anomaly detection systems for industrial settings.
