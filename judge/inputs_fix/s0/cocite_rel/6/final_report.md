## Related Works

Narrative summarization, particularly for long-form texts such as books, movies, and TV scripts, remains a challenging task for large language models (LLMs) due to the complexity of plotlines, character interactions, and thematic coherence \[[17](http://arxiv.org/abs/2504.09071)\]. Several works have explored methods to improve summarization quality, often focusing on enhancing factual accuracy, coherence, and alignment with the source material \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/1908.08960)\]\[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/2305.14325)\]\[[17](http://arxiv.org/abs/2504.09071)\]. For instance, neural text summarization has been extensively studied, with researchers highlighting issues such as noisy datasets, weak evaluation metrics, and model overfitting to dataset biases \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/1908.08960)\]. These challenges are particularly pronounced in narrative summarization, where maintaining thematic coherence and fidelity to the original content is essential \[[17](http://arxiv.org/abs/2504.09071)\].

To address these limitations, recent approaches have leveraged multi-agent frameworks to enhance reasoning and collaborative processing. MedAgents, for example, proposes a zero-shot medical reasoning framework that uses multi-round discussions among LLMs to improve diagnostic accuracy and reasoning capabilities \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/2311.10537)\]. Similarly, Toolformer enables LLMs to use external tools via APIs, improving performance on tasks requiring factual lookup or arithmetic \[[10](http://arxiv.org/abs/2302.04761)\]. These methods demonstrate that multi-agent collaboration can enhance model performance by incorporating diverse perspectives and reducing hallucinations \[[10](http://arxiv.org/abs/2302.04761)\]. However, they are often tailored to specific domains, such as healthcare or finance, and may not generalize well to narrative summarization \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/2311.10537)\]\[[10](http://arxiv.org/abs/2302.04761)\].

The ReAct framework, which synergizes reasoning and acting in LLMs, has also been applied to improve task-solving by interleaving reasoning steps with action execution \[[5](http://arxiv.org/abs/2210.03629)\]. This approach allows models to interact with external knowledge sources, such as Wikipedia, to verify information and reduce error propagation \[[5](http://arxiv.org/abs/2210.03629)\]. While effective for question-answering and fact verification, ReAct is primarily designed for structured tasks rather than open-ended narrative summarization \[[5](http://arxiv.org/abs/2210.03629)\]. Similarly, BART, a denoising sequence-to-sequence model, has shown strong performance in abstractive summarization but is typically fine-tuned on specific datasets, limiting its applicability to long-form narratives without additional training \[[9](http://arxiv.org/abs/1910.13461)\].

In contrast, the proposed NexusSum framework introduces a structured, multi-agent pipeline that operates without fine-tuning, making it suitable for diverse narrative domains \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/1908.08960)\]. It incorporates a dialogue-to-description transformation to standardize input formats, improving coherence during summarization \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/1908.08960)\]. Additionally, the hierarchical multi-LLM summarization approach optimizes chunk processing and output length control, enabling accurate and high-quality summaries \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/1908.08960)\]. These innovations build on prior work in multi-agent collaboration, such as MetaGPT, which uses standardized operating procedures (SOPs) to enhance structured coordination among agents \[[4](http://arxiv.org/abs/2308.00352)\]. By integrating these principles, NexusSum achieves a 30.0% improvement in BERTScore (F1) across books, movies, and TV scripts, demonstrating the potential of multi-agent LLMs in structured summarization tasks \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/1908.08960)\].
