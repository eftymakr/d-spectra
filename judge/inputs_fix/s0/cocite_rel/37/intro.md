## Related Works

Vision Transformers (ViTs) have gained significant attention in computer vision due to their ability to capture global context through self-attention mechanisms, outperforming traditional convolutional neural networks (CNNs) in various tasks \[[5](http://arxiv.org/abs/2010.11929)\]. However, the deployment of ViTs on hardware platforms, especially FPGAs, remains challenging due to their complex arithmetic operations, such as Softmax and LayerNorm, which are sensitive to precision and difficult to map onto efficient hardware \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2208.05163)\]. Prior work has primarily focused on optimizing matrix multiplication operations while keeping complex operations on the CPU, resulting in suboptimal performance due to communication overhead between the CPU and accelerator \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2208.05163)\]. This section reviews related works that address ViT acceleration, quantization, and hardware design, highlighting their contributions and limitations.

Several studies have explored quantization techniques to reduce the computational and memory costs of ViTs. Auto-ViT-Acc introduces a mixed-scheme quantization approach for FPGA-based ViT acceleration, achieving higher accuracy than state-of-the-art quantization methods under the same bit-width \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2208.05163)\]. VAQF proposes a fully automatic software-hardware co-design framework for low-bit ViTs, enabling real-time execution on FPGAs with minimal compilation time \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/2201.06618)\]. Similarly, Q-ViT addresses the performance drop in low-bit ViTs by introducing an information rectification module and distribution-guided distillation to maintain accuracy \[[13](http://arxiv.org/abs/2210.06707)\]. These works demonstrate that quantization can significantly reduce inference latency without compromising accuracy, but they often focus on specific aspects of the problem.

Efforts to improve hardware efficiency for ViTs have also been explored. ME-ViT introduces a memory-efficient FPGA accelerator that minimizes off-chip memory access by implementing a single-load policy, storing intermediate results on-chip, and using a custom processing element to handle complex operations like Softmax and LayerNorm \[[18](http://arxiv.org/abs/2402.09709)\]. This approach improves memory bandwidth and throughput compared to existing accelerators. Similarly, Evo-ViT proposes a slow-fast token evolution mechanism to reduce computational cost while maintaining performance, achieving over 60% throughput improvement on DeiT-S with minimal accuracy loss \[[16](http://arxiv.org/abs/2108.01390)\]. These works highlight the importance of optimizing both the model and the hardware to achieve real-time inference.

Recent advancements in efficient ViT architectures have also contributed to the field. LeViT introduces a hybrid network that combines the speed of convnets with the accuracy of ViTs, achieving significant improvements in the speed-accuracy trade-off \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/2104.01136)\]. CvT integrates convolutions into ViT to improve performance and efficiency, achieving state-of-the-art results on ImageNet with fewer parameters and lower FLOPs \[[6](http://arxiv.org/abs/2103.15808)\]. CrossViT proposes a multi-scale attention mechanism to enhance feature representation, outperforming recent DeiT models on ImageNet \[[7](http://arxiv.org/abs/2103.14899)\]. These architectures demonstrate that combining the strengths of CNNs and ViTs can lead to more efficient and accurate models.

In summary, prior work has made significant strides in ViT acceleration, quantization, and architecture design. However, most approaches focus on specific aspects of the problem, such as quantization or hardware optimization, without addressing the full set of challenges. This paper presents a novel approach that maps all operations of ViT models onto FPGAs using a new arithmetic format, Microscaling Integer (MXInt), and hardware-specific optimizations, achieving significant speedup and accuracy \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2208.05163)\].
