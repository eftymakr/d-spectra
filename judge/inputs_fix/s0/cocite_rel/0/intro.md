## Related Works

Taxation and economic inequality have been extensively studied in both theoretical and applied economics. Traditional models of taxation, such as the U.S. federal income tax, aim to reduce inequality but often lack adaptability to heterogeneous taxpayer behavior and irrational decision-making \[[1](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/0fba9c1bee4a5bda0d5dbf01f97ef5a8e212833e)\]. The Saez Optimal Taxation model, while dynamic, fails to fully account for the complexity of taxpayer heterogeneity and behavioral biases \[[1](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/0fba9c1bee4a5bda0d5dbf01f97ef5a8e212833e)\]. This has led researchers to explore alternative frameworks that better capture the nuances of tax compliance and policy design.

Agent-based models (ABMs) have gained traction as a means to simulate heterogeneous economic agents and their interactions \[[10](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/444541ae4453cac61bd27d5e0a537d32ec0d322e)\]. These models allow for the representation of diverse behaviors and decision-making processes, which is critical for understanding tax evasion and compliance \[[1](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/0fba9c1bee4a5bda0d5dbf01f97ef5a8e212833e)\]. For instance, studies have shown that tax evasion can be modeled using the Ising model, where agents' behaviors are influenced by social interactions and enforcement mechanisms \[[14](http://arxiv.org/abs/0801.2980)\]. Similarly, the majority-vote model has been used to analyze and control tax evasion dynamics, demonstrating the effectiveness of punishment as an enforcement mechanism \[[16](http://arxiv.org/abs/0910.1964)\].

Reinforcement learning (RL) has also been applied to economic modeling, offering a flexible approach to solving dynamic stochastic general equilibrium (DSGE) models \[[5](http://arxiv.org/abs/2104.09368)\]. RL enables agents to learn optimal strategies through interaction with the environment, making it suitable for modeling bounded rationality in macroeconomic settings \[[5](http://arxiv.org/abs/2104.09368)\]. This approach has been used to develop optimal monetary policy rules that outperform traditional methods, reducing central bank losses by over 43% \[[7](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/faa50da104ab3b55075f39e1f416ab5b9fc2725e)\]. Such techniques are relevant to the design of adaptive tax policies that can respond to changing economic conditions.

The integration of large language models (LLMs) with ABMs, as proposed in the TaxAgent framework, represents a novel approach to tax policy design \[[1](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/0fba9c1bee4a5bda0d5dbf01f97ef5a8e212833e)\]. This combination allows for the simulation of heterogeneous taxpayer behaviors while enabling the government agent to iteratively optimize tax rates using advanced AI techniques. This aligns with broader efforts to develop high-fidelity market simulation environments that support AI research in economic applications \[[15](http://arxiv.org/abs/1904.12066)\].

Several studies have explored the role of social interactions in tax compliance, showing that fairness effects can influence individual decisions \[[6](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/a065b1629ff85e2a0db928bd8ce115b2f5e2a80f)\]. Additionally, research on tax morale has demonstrated that trust in governmental institutions plays a critical role in shaping tax evasion behaviors \[[9](http://arxiv.org/abs/0806.0344)\]. These findings underscore the importance of incorporating social and psychological factors into tax policy design.

In summary, the TaxAgent framework builds on a rich body of research in economic modeling, tax compliance, and AI-driven policy optimization. By integrating LLMs with ABMs, it offers a novel and scalable solution for designing adaptive tax policies that balance equity and productivity.
