## Related Works

The rapid growth of open-source machine learning (ML) resources has significantly impacted information retrieval (IR) research, particularly in the areas of resource management and recommendation systems. Several studies have explored the challenges and opportunities associated with the proliferation of ML models and datasets on platforms like Hugging Face. For instance, \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2305.11164)\] highlights the increasing importance of Hugging Face as a central hub for ML model development and sharing, emphasizing the need for better understanding of model evolution and maintenance. Similarly, \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/2311.13380)\] conducts a comprehensive analysis of over 380,000 models on Hugging Face, focusing on community engagement, evolution, and maintenance, which aligns with the need for structured representations to enable advanced queries and analyses.

The lack of structured representations in existing platforms has been a recurring issue. \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/2303.02552)\] points out that Hugging Face, while a popular platform, does not explicitly utilize structured representations, limiting advanced queries and analyses such as tracing model evolution and recommending relevant datasets. This limitation is also noted in \[[4](http://arxiv.org/abs/2401.13822)\], which discusses the challenges of dataset documentation on Hugging Face, highlighting the need for more systematic and comprehensive documentation practices to support reproducibility and transparency.

Several studies have focused on the documentation and metadata of ML models and datasets. \[[5](http://arxiv.org/abs/2402.07323)\] presents an empirical study of pre-trained model reuse in the Hugging Face ecosystem, identifying challenges such as missing attributes, discrepancies between claimed and actual performance, and model risks. These findings underscore the importance of structured metadata for effective resource management. Similarly, \[[6](http://arxiv.org/abs/2406.08205)\] discusses the need for improved documentation quality, showing a strong correlation between documentation quality and PTM popularity.

In addition, \[[7](http://arxiv.org/abs/2401.13177)\] explores the challenges and benefits of PTM reuse, identifying prevalent challenges such as limited guidance for beginner users and difficulties in model comprehension. These findings highlight the need for better documentation and structured representations to enhance usability and accessibility. \[[8](http://arxiv.org/abs/2307.14841)\] further emphasizes the importance of structured data in empirical studies, suggesting that Hugging Face Hub offers sufficient features and diverse data to support such studies.

The development of knowledge graphs for ML resources is a relatively new area, but several studies have explored similar concepts. \[[9](http://arxiv.org/abs/1810.03993)\] discusses the importance of model cards for transparent model reporting, advocating for structured documentation that includes performance characteristics and evaluation procedures. This aligns with the goal of HuggingKG to capture domain-specific relations and rich textual attributes. \[[10](http://arxiv.org/abs/1702.08734)\] also highlights the need for better documentation and metadata management in ML ecosystems, emphasizing the role of structured representations in enabling advanced queries and analyses.

In summary, the existing literature highlights the importance of structured representations and comprehensive documentation in ML resource management. These findings form the foundation for the development of HuggingKG and HuggingBench, which aim to address these gaps by providing a large-scale knowledge graph and a multi-task benchmark for IR tasks.
