## Related Works

Neural retrieval models have seen significant advancements in recent years, particularly in the domain of Web search. These models often rely on large-scale labeled query-document pairs for training, which are typically expensive to obtain \[[8](http://arxiv.org/abs/1611.09268)\]. However, the availability of Web document collections like ClueWeb22 has enabled the use of synthetic queries generated by large language models as a scalable alternative \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2305.18290)\]. Despite this, synthetic training queries can vary in quality, leading to suboptimal performance in downstream retrieval tasks \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2305.18290)\]. 

Existing methods typically filter out noisy query-document pairs using signals from an external re-ranker \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2305.18290)\]. In contrast, we propose a framework that integrates ranking signals directly into the query generation process, leveraging Direct Preference Optimization (DPO) to optimize the model towards generating high-quality queries that maximize downstream retrieval effectiveness \[[6](http://arxiv.org/abs/2402.13228)\]. This approach differs from prior work that relies on post-hoc filtering \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2305.18290)\].

DPO has been shown to improve the performance of large language models on various downstream tasks, including reasoning, summarization, and alignment \[[6](http://arxiv.org/abs/2402.13228)\]. It enables the extraction of the corresponding optimal policy in closed form, allowing for a simple classification loss to be used during fine-tuning \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2305.18290)\]. This results in a stable, performant, and computationally lightweight algorithm \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2305.18290)\]. 

In the context of dense retrieval, several approaches have been proposed to improve the quality of training data. For example, RocketQA introduces strategies such as cross-batch negatives, denoised hard negatives, and data augmentation to enhance the training of dense passage retrieval models \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/2010.08191)\]. Similarly, the Optimizing Dense Retrieval Model Training with Hard Negatives paper explores the effectiveness of hard negative sampling and proposes the Stable Training Algorithm for dense Retrieval (STAR) and the query-side training Algorithm for Directly Optimizing Ranking pErformance (ADORE) \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/2104.08051)\]. 

Additionally, the work on Semantic-Enhanced Differentiable Search Index (SE-DSI) introduces strategies inspired by human learning to improve the performance of differentiable search indexes \[[4](http://arxiv.org/abs/2305.15115)\]. These methods aim to address the challenges of training dual-encoder architectures for dense retrieval \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/2010.08191)\].

In the field of generative retrieval, the Learning to Rank in Generative Retrieval paper proposes a framework that bridges the gap between autoregressive models and the desired passage ranking target \[[18](http://arxiv.org/abs/2306.15222)\]. This approach enables generative retrieval to learn to rank passages directly, optimizing the autoregressive model toward the final passage ranking target via a rank loss.

In summary, while existing methods rely on external re-rankers or post-hoc filtering to improve the quality of synthetic queries, our approach integrates ranking signals directly into the query generation process using DPO, leading to higher ranker-assessed relevance and stronger downstream performance \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2305.18290)\].

### <a id='category-direct-preference-optimization-(dpo)-framework'></a>Direct Preference Optimization (DPO) Framework: 3 papers found.
The Direct Preference Optimization (DPO) framework represents a significant advancement in aligning large language models (LLMs) with human preferences by simplifying the reinforcement learning from human feedback (RLHF) process. Unlike traditional RLHF, which requires training a separate reward model and then fine-tuning the LLM using reinforcement learning, DPO introduces a parameterization that allows for the extraction of the optimal policy in closed form, eliminating the need for complex reward modeling and sampling during fine-tuning \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2305.18290)\]. This approach is not only computationally lightweight but also more stable and easier to implement, as it relies solely on a simple classification loss \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2305.18290)\]. Furthermore, DPO has been shown to outperform PPO-based RLHF in controlling the sentiment of generated responses and matches or improves performance in summarization and single-turn dialogue tasks \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2305.18290)\]. However, DPO still relies on the assumption that pairwise preferences can be substituted with pointwise rewards, a key approximation that limits its generalization to out-of-distribution data \[[7](http://arxiv.org/abs/2310.12036)\]. To address these limitations, recent work has proposed $\Psi$PO, a general objective that bypasses both approximations by directly learning from pairwise preferences, enabling a more robust alignment of LLMs with human expectations \[[7](http://arxiv.org/abs/2310.12036)\]. Additionally, methods like Direct Large Model Alignment (DLMA) have been developed to automatically generate preference data using contrastive prompt pairs, further reducing the reliance on human-annotated data while leveraging DPO for effective model alignment \[[16](http://arxiv.org/abs/2402.11907)\]. These developments highlight the ongoing efforts to refine and extend the DPO framework to achieve more accurate and efficient alignment of LLMs with human preferences.
