## Related Works

Multi-token prediction (MTP) has emerged as a promising pre-training objective for language models, offering potential improvements in downstream performance, inference speed, and training efficiency \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2404.19737)\]. Unlike traditional next-token prediction (NTP), which predicts one token at a time, MTP predicts multiple future tokens simultaneously using multiple prediction heads \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2404.19737)\]. This approach has been shown to enhance model capabilities, particularly in generative tasks such as coding, where models trained with MTP outperform comparable NTP models by several percentage points \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2404.19737)\]. For example, 13B parameter models trained with 4-token prediction solve 12% more problems on HumanEval and 17% more on MBPP than next-token models \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2404.19737)\].

Several studies have explored alternative training paradigms that aim to improve the efficiency and effectiveness of language models. For instance, process supervision, which provides feedback at each intermediate reasoning step, has been found to significantly outperform outcome supervision for solving complex problems, such as those in the MATH dataset \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/2305.20050)\]. Similarly, speculative decoding has been proposed as a method to accelerate inference by predicting multiple tokens in parallel and validating them in a single step \[[8](http://arxiv.org/abs/2401.07851)\]. This approach has demonstrated gains on tasks such as SQuAD, CommonSenseQA, and GSM8k \[[4](http://arxiv.org/abs/2310.02226)\]. Additionally, blockwise parallel decoding has been introduced to enable parallel prediction across multiple time steps, reducing generation time while maintaining quality \[[12](http://arxiv.org/abs/1811.03115)\].

The challenges of training smaller language models (SLMs) with MTP have also been addressed in prior work. Some studies suggest that SLMs struggle with the increased complexity of MTP, leading to suboptimal performance \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2404.19737)\]. To mitigate this, curriculum learning has been proposed as a strategy to gradually introduce MTP during pre-training, starting from simpler NTP tasks and progressively increasing complexity \[[13](http://arxiv.org/abs/2108.02170)\]. This approach has been shown to improve the performance of SLMs on downstream NTP tasks while retaining the benefits of self-speculative decoding \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2404.19737)\]. Conversely, a reverse curriculum, which starts with MTP and reduces complexity, has been found to yield stronger NTP performance but fails to provide the same inference speed benefits \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2404.19737)\].

In addition to training strategies, the effectiveness of transfer learning has been extensively studied. Transfer learning, which involves pre-training on a large corpus and fine-tuning on a specific task, has become a cornerstone of modern NLP \[[5](http://arxiv.org/abs/1910.10683)\]. This approach has led to significant improvements in various tasks, including translation, question-answering, and reasoning \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/2005.14165)\]. Furthermore, the use of pause tokens during inference has been explored as a way to delay output generation and allow the model to process more computation before committing to an answer \[[4](http://arxiv.org/abs/2310.02226)\].
