## Related Works

Video frame interpolation has been a focal point in computer vision, aiming to generate intermediate frames between existing ones to enhance temporal resolution. Early methods typically followed a two-step approach: motion estimation and pixel synthesis \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/1703.07514)\]. However, these methods were often limited by the accuracy of optical flow estimation and struggled with challenges like occlusion and blur \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/1712.00080)\]. More recent approaches have sought to merge these steps into a single process, leveraging deep learning for more robust and efficient results \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/1708.01692)\].

Several works have explored the use of convolutional neural networks (CNNs) for video frame interpolation. For instance, \[[4](http://arxiv.org/abs/1603.06041)\] proposed a method that combines motion estimation and synthesis through a single convolutional process, allowing for high-quality interpolation by adapting kernels spatially. Similarly, \[[5](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/a285a442317f24068f7cc262531976e82a5c41d6)\] introduced a method that uses separable convolutions to reduce the number of parameters while maintaining performance, which is particularly useful for handling large motions. These approaches have laid the groundwork for more advanced models that incorporate additional features such as depth and context \[[6](http://arxiv.org/abs/1506.06825)\].

The use of optical flow remains a cornerstone in many interpolation techniques, as it provides a way to estimate motion between frames \[[7](http://arxiv.org/abs/2011.01280)\]. However, traditional optical flow methods often fail in complex scenarios due to their reliance on hand-crafted features \[[8](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/bb4a9650ca3946c70a7e92007cc61dc0dfd75522)\]. To address this, \[[9](http://arxiv.org/abs/1911.00627)\] introduced a method that uses bi-directional optical flow and visibility maps to refine the interpolation process, effectively reducing artifacts caused by occlusions. This approach has been further refined in \[[10](http://arxiv.org/abs/1702.02463)\], where a U-Net architecture is used to compute and refine optical flow, leading to more accurate intermediate frame generation.

Another significant advancement comes from \[[11](http://arxiv.org/abs/2007.12622)\], which proposed a method based on adaptive separable convolutions. This approach not only reduces computational complexity but also improves the robustness of the model against challenging scenarios. The work in \[[12](http://arxiv.org/abs/2106.10689)\] further enhances this by introducing a channel attention mechanism, which allows the model to focus on relevant features during the interpolation process, leading to better performance in the presence of motion and occlusion.

In addition to optical flow-based methods, some works have explored alternative approaches such as phase-based motion representation \[[13](http://arxiv.org/abs/2003.05534)\]. These methods aim to capture motion in a more perceptually meaningful way, which can lead to more natural-looking interpolated frames. However, they are often limited in the amount of motion they can handle \[[14](http://arxiv.org/abs/1804.00884)\]. To overcome this, \[[15](http://arxiv.org/abs/1904.00830)\] proposed a method that explicitly detects occlusion using depth information, leading to more accurate synthesis of intermediate frames.

The integration of context-aware synthesis has also been explored, as seen in \[[16](http://arxiv.org/abs/2206.00665)\], where contextual information is used to improve the quality of the interpolated frames. This approach not only enhances the visual quality but also ensures temporal consistency, which is crucial for realistic video sequences.

To address these limitations, several studies have proposed end-to-end convolutional neural networks that directly learn the mapping from input frames to intermediate frames without explicit optical flow computation \[[8](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/bb4a9650ca3946c70a7e92007cc61dc0dfd75522)\]. Another method introduces a feature reshaping operation, such as PixelShuffle, combined with channel attention mechanisms to extract motion information at the pixel level, improving performance in complex motion and occlusion scenarios \[[8](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/bb4a9650ca3946c70a7e92007cc61dc0dfd75522)\]. Additionally, some works have focused on multi-frame interpolation, where the network generates multiple intermediate frames simultaneously by jointly modeling motion interpretation and occlusion reasoning \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/1712.00080)\]. These methods often employ U-Net architectures to compute bi-directional optical flow and refine it using visibility maps to exclude occluded regions during frame synthesis \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/1712.00080)\]. Furthermore, recent approaches have incorporated perceptual losses and cycle consistency losses to enhance the quality of synthesized frames and maintain consistency between input and output \[[28](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/380f46cd24a15271036ff49e9e0d91f39f89d8b4)\]. ### <a id='category-video-frame-interpolation-techniques'></a>Video Frame Interpolation Techniques: 9 papers found.
Video frame interpolation techniques have seen significant advancements, particularly through the integration of deep learning and optical flow estimation. Traditional methods often assume uniform motion between frames and rely on linear models, which struggle with complex, real-world motion patterns \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/1708.01692)\]. To address these limitations, researchers have proposed quadratic interpolation methods that incorporate acceleration information, enabling more accurate prediction of curvilinear trajectories and variable velocities \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/1708.01692)\]. Additionally, differentiable image sampling techniques, such as softmax splatting, have been introduced to handle forward warping challenges, allowing for seamless mapping of multiple source pixels to the same target location \[[13](http://arxiv.org/abs/2003.05534)\]. These methods are further enhanced by context-aware synthesis approaches that warp not only input frames but also their contextual information, leading to high-quality intermediate frame generation \[[18](http://arxiv.org/abs/1803.10967)\]. Recent works have also focused on improving robustness in challenging scenarios, such as lighting changes and motion blur, by leveraging phase-based motion representations and adaptive warping modules like AdaCoF, which generalize well to complex motions \[[14](http://arxiv.org/abs/1804.00884)\]\[[25](http://arxiv.org/abs/1907.10244)\]. Furthermore, extreme motion scenarios have been addressed with specialized networks like XVFI-Net, which utilize recursive multi-scale structures and bidirectional optical flow learning to handle high-speed and high-resolution video \[[30](http://arxiv.org/abs/2103.16206)\]. These developments highlight the ongoing efforts to enhance the accuracy and robustness of video frame interpolation, aligning with the goals of neural rendering methods that benefit from augmented data \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/1703.07514)\].
