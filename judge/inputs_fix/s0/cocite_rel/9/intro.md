## Related Works

Quantitative susceptibility mapping (QSM) has emerged as a powerful tool for characterizing multiple sclerosis (MS) lesions, particularly in distinguishing between "rim" and "non-rim" lesion types \[[1](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/71b5e8d9970f3b725127eb7da3d5183b921ad017)\]. Rim lesions, characterized by a paramagnetic rim due to iron deposition, are associated with more severe disease progression and greater tissue damage \[[11](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/bb5b80dc4165d0ac3e28d1882b898633e84779c2)\]. The ability to accurately detect and segment these lesions is critical for monitoring disease progression and evaluating therapeutic interventions \[[1](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/71b5e8d9970f3b725127eb7da3d5183b921ad017)\]. However, the rarity of rim lesions introduces a significant class imbalance challenge in machine learning models, which can hinder classifier performance \[[1](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/71b5e8d9970f3b725127eb7da3d5183b921ad017)\].

Several studies have explored automated methods for MS lesion segmentation, including both traditional image processing techniques and deep learning approaches. For instance, a cascaded 3D convolutional neural network (CNN) approach has been proposed to improve automated segmentation of white matter (WM) lesions by reducing misclassification \[[6](http://arxiv.org/abs/1702.04869)\]. This method demonstrated superior performance on clinical MS data compared to other state-of-the-art techniques \[[6](http://arxiv.org/abs/1702.04869)\]. Similarly, a rotation-invariant multi-contrast non-local means segmentation method was introduced to robustly identify lesions regardless of their orientation, shape, or size \[[7](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/1562d113593782a0ce01347b594e05abae02fbfa)\]. These methods highlight the ongoing efforts to enhance segmentation accuracy in the presence of lesion variability.

Deep learning has also been extensively applied to MS lesion segmentation. A study introduced a novel recurrent slice-wise attention network (RSANet) that models 3D MRI images as sequences of slices to capture long-range dependencies and contextual information \[[18](http://arxiv.org/abs/2002.12470)\]. This approach outperformed existing methods on a dataset of 43 patients \[[18](http://arxiv.org/abs/2002.12470)\]. Another study proposed a geometric loss function to address data imbalance and improve segmentation by incorporating geometric properties of lesions \[[22](http://arxiv.org/abs/2009.13755)\]. These innovations underscore the potential of deep learning in overcoming challenges associated with lesion segmentation.

The importance of addressing class imbalance in rim lesion detection has been highlighted in several works. For example, a study introduced a synthetic data generation method to create quantitative susceptibility maps of paramagnetic rim lesions, which improved classifier performance and allowed for multi-channel extension to generate accompanying contrasts and probabilistic segmentation maps \[[1](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/71b5e8d9970f3b725127eb7da3d5183b921ad017)\]. This approach enables the training of models on ambiguous rim cases, thereby increasing the minority class representation and improving detection accuracy \[[1](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/71b5e8d9970f3b725127eb7da3d5183b921ad017)\]. Additionally, a study proposed a denoising approach using a generative adversarial network (GAN) to train on ambiguous rim cases and increase the minority class representation \[[1](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/71b5e8d9970f3b725127eb7da3d5183b921ad017)\].

In summary, while significant progress has been made in MS lesion segmentation, challenges remain, particularly in addressing class imbalance and improving the accuracy of rim lesion detection. The proposed methods, including synthetic data generation and geometric loss functions, offer promising avenues for overcoming these challenges \[[1](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/71b5e8d9970f3b725127eb7da3d5183b921ad017)\]\[[6](http://arxiv.org/abs/1702.04869)\]\[[7](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/1562d113593782a0ce01347b594e05abae02fbfa)\]\[[18](http://arxiv.org/abs/2002.12470)\]\[[22](http://arxiv.org/abs/2009.13755)\].
