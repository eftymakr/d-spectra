## Related Works

Generative retrieval has emerged as a promising paradigm that replaces traditional search index data structures with a single large-scale neural network, enabling improved efficiency and seamless integration with generative large language models \[[12](http://arxiv.org/abs/2202.06991)\]. This approach leverages autoregressive generation to directly produce document identifiers from queries, eliminating the need for separate indexing and retrieval stages \[[12](http://arxiv.org/abs/2202.06991)\]. Several recent works have explored the potential of this paradigm, focusing on different aspects such as scalability, effectiveness, and end-to-end modeling \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2204.10628)\]\[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/2305.11841)\]\[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/2304.04171)\]\[[4](http://arxiv.org/abs/2308.14968)\]\[[5](http://arxiv.org/abs/2311.09134)\]\[[6](http://arxiv.org/abs/2206.02743)\]\[[7](http://arxiv.org/abs/2007.00808)\]\[[8](http://arxiv.org/abs/2305.11161)\]\[[9](http://arxiv.org/abs/2010.00904)\]\[[10](http://arxiv.org/abs/2004.04906)\]\[[11](http://arxiv.org/abs/2108.05540)\]\[[12](http://arxiv.org/abs/2202.06991)\].

Early efforts in generative retrieval focused on using transformer-based models to encode entire document corpora and generate document identifiers during retrieval \[[12](http://arxiv.org/abs/2202.06991)\]. These models, often referred to as differentiable search indexes, have been shown to outperform traditional dual-encoder models in certain settings \[[12](http://arxiv.org/abs/2202.06991)\]. However, the effectiveness of these models on large-scale corpora has been limited by challenges such as computational cost and the need for synthetic queries during indexing \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/2305.11841)\]. To address these issues, researchers have proposed various techniques, including the use of synthetic queries as document representations \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/2305.11841)\], and the development of efficient indexing methods like Incremental Product Quantization \[[4](http://arxiv.org/abs/2308.14968)\].

A key challenge in generative retrieval is the definition of document identifiers. While some approaches rely on fixed rule-based identifiers, such as document titles or BERT embeddings \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/2304.04171)\], others propose learning-based methods to generate semantic docids \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/2304.04171)\]. The GenRet method, for example, learns to tokenize documents into short discrete representations using a discrete auto-encoding approach \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/2304.04171)\]. This enables end-to-end modeling of document retrieval tasks and has shown promising results on benchmark datasets \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/2304.04171)\].

Another important aspect of generative retrieval is the ability to handle dynamic and evolving corpora. The CLEVER model, for instance, addresses the challenge of continual learning by proposing an incremental indexing method and a memory-augmented learning mechanism to preserve previous knowledge while incorporating new documents \[[4](http://arxiv.org/abs/2308.14968)\]. This is crucial for real-world applications where document collections are frequently updated \[[4](http://arxiv.org/abs/2308.14968)\].

Recent research has also focused on improving the scalability of generative retrieval models. The RIPOR framework, for example, introduces a novel prefix-oriented ranking optimization algorithm and constructs document IDs based on relevance associations between queries and documents \[[5](http://arxiv.org/abs/2311.09134)\]. This approach has led to significant improvements in retrieval performance on large-scale benchmarks such as MS MARCO \[[5](http://arxiv.org/abs/2311.09134)\].

In addition to these technical contributions, several works have explored the use of generative retrieval in knowledge-intensive tasks such as open-domain question answering \[[10](http://arxiv.org/abs/2004.04906)\]. The Dense Passage Retrieval (DPR) method, for instance, has demonstrated that dense representations can be used effectively for passage retrieval, outperforming traditional sparse retrieval methods \[[10](http://arxiv.org/abs/2004.04906)\]. This has paved the way for more advanced retrieval-augmented generation models that combine pre-trained language models with dense vector indexes \[[14](http://arxiv.org/abs/2005.11401)\].

Overall, the field of generative retrieval has made significant progress, but challenges remain in terms of scalability, generalization, and handling out-of-distribution corpora. The current work builds on these prior contributions to examine the inherent limitations of constrained auto-regressive generation from two essential perspectives: constraints and beam search \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2204.10628)\].
