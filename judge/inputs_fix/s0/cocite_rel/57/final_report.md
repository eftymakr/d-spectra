## Related Works

Generative retrieval (GR) has recently emerged as a promising paradigm in information retrieval (IR), shifting the focus from traditional index-retrieve pipelines to end-to-end document retrieval via sequence-to-sequence models \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/2202.06991)\]. However, most existing GR models are primarily developed and evaluated using static document collections, with limited exploration of their performance in dynamic corpora where document collections evolve over time \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2308.14968)\]. This gap motivates our work, which systematically evaluates various GR approaches on dynamic corpora and identifies key design factors that influence their effectiveness \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2308.14968)\].

Several recent works have explored the use of differentiable search indexes (DSIs) to encode document corpora into model parameters, enabling direct query-to-docid generation \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/2202.06991)\]. For example, the Differentiable Search Index (DSI) maps queries to relevant document identifiers using a single transformer model, eliminating the need for separate indexing and retrieval stages \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/2202.06991)\]. However, these models typically assume a static corpus, which limits their applicability in real-world scenarios where documents are continuously added \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2308.14968)\]. To address this, DSI++ was proposed, introducing a continual learning framework that allows for incremental indexing of new documents while maintaining retrieval performance on previously indexed documents \[[7](http://arxiv.org/abs/2212.09744)\]. This work highlights the importance of mitigating forgetting during continual learning, a challenge also observed in other GR models \[[7](http://arxiv.org/abs/2212.09744)\].

The design of document identifiers (docids) plays a crucial role in GR performance. While numeric-based docids are often favored for their efficiency, they suffer from poor generalization over dynamic corpora due to overfitting on the initial document set \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2308.14968)\]. In contrast, text-based docids, which are more semantically aligned with language models' pre-trained knowledge, demonstrate superior performance in dynamic settings \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2308.14968)\]. This aligns with findings from the Neural Corpus Indexer (NCI), which showed that end-to-end neural models can outperform traditional index-retrieve pipelines by directly generating docids for queries \[[4](http://arxiv.org/abs/2206.02743)\]. Similarly, the CorpusBrain model, pre-trained for generative retrieval, demonstrated strong zero-shot performance on knowledge-intensive language tasks by encoding all corpus information into its parameters \[[6](http://arxiv.org/abs/2208.07652)\].

To further improve GR models for dynamic corpora, the GenRet framework was introduced, which learns to tokenize documents into short discrete representations (docids) via a discrete auto-encoding approach \[[8](http://arxiv.org/abs/2304.04171)\]. This method enables fully end-to-end modeling of retrieval tasks, with empirical results showing significant improvements on unseen documents \[[8](http://arxiv.org/abs/2304.04171)\]. Additionally, the NOVO model proposed learnable and interpretable docids by optimizing them through query denoising and retrieval tasks, allowing for better semantic alignment and document relationships \[[14](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/6682a3679a17d090830635f1c420126d20eae54a)\].

In summary, while existing GR models have made significant strides in static settings, their performance in dynamic corpora remains underexplored. Our work builds on these advancements by systematically evaluating GR approaches on dynamic data and proposing a novel multi-docid design that leverages the strengths of both numeric- and text-based docids \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2308.14968)\].
