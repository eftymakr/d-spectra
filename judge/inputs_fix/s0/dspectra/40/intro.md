## Related Works

Zero-Knowledge Proofs (ZKPs) have emerged as a critical cryptographic primitive for privacy-preserving computation, enabling a prover to demonstrate the validity of a statement without revealing additional information \[[10](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/9ccf7b6cb32cf89752a35bd910555adac54773e0)\]. This has led to their adoption in blockchain technologies, verifiable machine learning, and secure voting systems \[[6](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/65f1e83a53c5ce9dee04042e0dd4191a451df2b8)\]. However, the computational complexity of the proving process has hindered widespread adoption \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2408.05890)\]. Recent efforts have focused on accelerating the key primitives of ZKP protocols using hardware accelerators, particularly GPUs and ASICs \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2408.05890)\]\[[3](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/e149d93531d8c07cf43f6cae327a4795144d1304)\]\[[5](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/e4a4608e8e35379e63b9df5bda1b7599ee8ab9ac)\]. 

Several works have explored the acceleration of critical operations such as the Number Theoretic Transform (NTT) and Multi-scalar Multiplication (MSM), which are essential for proof generation \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2408.05890)\]. For instance, SZKP introduces an ASIC-based accelerator that achieves speedups of over 400×, 3×, and 12× over CPU, ASIC, and GPU implementations, respectively \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2408.05890)\]. Similarly, PipeZK proposes a pipelined architecture with two subsystems to handle large polynomial computations and MSMs, achieving 10× and 5× speedups on cryptographic benchmarks and cryptocurrency applications like Zcash \[[2](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/6ee84674b7c426849c3da56cb278c08eb7a903cf)\]. GZKP, on the other hand, leverages GPU acceleration for ZKP systems, achieving up to 48.1× and 17.6× speedups over state-of-the-art systems \[[3](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/e149d93531d8c07cf43f6cae327a4795144d1304)\]. 

cuZK further improves GPU-based acceleration by introducing a new parallel MSM algorithm that achieves nearly perfect linear speedup over the Pippenger algorithm, along with parallelized matrix-vector multiplication (MUL) and optimized data transfer mechanisms \[[5](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/e4a4608e8e35379e63b9df5bda1b7599ee8ab9ac)\]. This results in significant performance gains, with cuZK achieving up to 4.86× speedup on standard benchmarks and 2.18× on a cryptocurrency application like Filecoin \[[5](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/e4a4608e8e35379e63b9df5bda1b7599ee8ab9ac)\]. 

In addition to hardware accelerators, there are also software-based approaches and libraries that aim to simplify the implementation of ZKPs. For example, zksk is a Python library that enables the definition and computation of sigma protocols, allowing for the composition of smaller proofs into larger ones \[[8](http://arxiv.org/abs/1911.02459)\]. This makes it easier for developers to construct complex ZKP systems without requiring deep cryptographic expertise \[[8](http://arxiv.org/abs/1911.02459)\]. 

The challenges of ZKP adoption include the need for trusted setups and the generation of larger proof sizes, which can increase verification costs \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2408.05890)\]. HyperPlonk, a state-of-the-art ZKP protocol, addresses these issues by supporting one-time, universal setup and small proof sizes, making it suitable for publicly verifiable, consensus-based systems \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2408.05890)\]. The proposed accelerator, zkSpeed, aims to further enhance the performance of HyperPlonk by accelerating both SumCheck and MSM operations, achieving geometric mean speedups of 801× over CPU baselines \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2408.05890)\].
