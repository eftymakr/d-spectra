## Related Works

Federated unlearning has emerged as a critical research area to address the "right to be forgotten" in federated learning (FL) systems \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2310.20448)\]. This concept allows users to remove their data contributions from the global model while preserving the model's utility \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/2401.05146)\]. Early approaches to federated unlearning primarily relied on retraining the model from scratch, which is computationally expensive \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/1910.01991)\]. To improve efficiency, several methods have been proposed that leverage historical parameter updates or local unlearning techniques \[[4](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/39da2d48f2132b919405e3bb6df6ece44ac6e15a)\]. For instance, FedEraser eliminates the influence of a client’s data by reconstructing the unlearned model using retained historical updates, achieving a speed-up of up to 4× compared to full retraining \[[5](http://arxiv.org/abs/2205.12709)\]. 

Recent work has also focused on improving the efficiency and effectiveness of unlearning in FL, particularly in the context of recommendation systems \[[6](http://arxiv.org/abs/2210.10958)\]. The FRU method, inspired by transaction rollback mechanisms, enables efficient unlearning by rolling back and recalibrating historical parameter updates \[[7](http://arxiv.org/abs/2207.05521)\]. This approach is especially relevant in scenarios where users request the removal of their contributions from federated training processes \[[8](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/f2f8f7a2ec1b2ede48cbcd189b376ab9fa0735ef)\]. Furthermore, the development of unlearning methods that can handle both class and sample-level unlearning has been a key focus \[[9](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/eadeffdec9fac8fd7f9aea732ca410eb082b7dcf)\]. For example, the SISA training framework reduces the computational overhead of unlearning by strategically limiting the influence of specific data points during training \[[10](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/a52c3ec7325e81bd87fdf8d74bf9acfd1e1ffe5f)\].

The evaluation of unlearning methods remains a challenge, as existing approaches often rely on a single metric, which may not fully capture the effectiveness of the unlearning process \[[11](http://arxiv.org/abs/2002.02730)\]. To address this, researchers have proposed multiple evaluation metrics, including reverse stochastic gradient ascent (SGA) and elastic weight consolidation (EWC), to assess unlearning efficacy and efficiency \[[12](http://arxiv.org/abs/2209.02299)\]. These metrics help quantify the impact of unlearning on model performance and ensure that the unlearning process does not introduce over-unlearning or under-unlearning issues \[[13](http://arxiv.org/abs/2203.07320)\]. Additionally, the need for verifiable federated unlearning has gained attention, as unlearning itself may not be sufficient to implement the right to be forgotten unless the unlearning effect can be independently verified \[[14](http://arxiv.org/abs/1912.03817)\]. Verifiable federated unlearning frameworks, such as VeriFi, have been introduced to enable systematic analysis and quantification of unlearning effects \[[15](http://arxiv.org/abs/2205.08096)\].

In the context of federated online learning to rank (FOLTR), the challenge of unlearning client interactions and data has been highlighted as a critical issue \[[16](http://arxiv.org/abs/2012.13891)\]. Current FOLTR methods face limitations in ranking effectiveness, robustness, and unlearning capabilities, which are essential for maintaining privacy and performance in decentralized settings \[[17](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/06edb1f6eadb6c141d720fcca4c854578dc5d670)\]. This paper addresses these limitations by proposing a comprehensive evaluation of unlearning strategies, adapting and introducing new metrics to assess both under-unlearning and over-unlearning scenarios \[[18](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/a08f1c5c9f0bdb24f0dc75c0d969eb353899156b)\]. The results of this study provide valuable insights into optimizing federated unlearning within FOLTR, balancing data privacy and system performance \[[19](http://arxiv.org/abs/1511.03575)\].
