## Related Works

Vision Transformers (ViTs) have gained significant traction in computer vision due to their ability to capture global context effectively \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2208.05163)\]. However, their deployment on hardware, particularly FPGAs, remains challenging due to the complexity of their arithmetic operations, such as Softmax and LayerNorm, which are sensitive to model accuracy and difficult to map onto efficient hardware with low precision \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2208.05163)\]. Existing methods often exploit parallelism in matrix multiplication operations while keeping these complex operations on the CPU, resulting in suboptimal performance due to communication overhead between the CPU and accelerator \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2208.05163)\]. This section reviews related works that address these challenges through hardware acceleration, quantization, and efficient design strategies.

Several studies have explored hardware acceleration for ViTs. Auto-ViT-Acc proposes an FPGA-aware automatic acceleration framework based on mixed-scheme quantization, achieving a 5.6x improvement in frame rate with minimal accuracy loss \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/2211.08110)\]. HeatViT introduces a hardware-efficient token pruning framework that reduces computation by up to 65.3% while maintaining high accuracy \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/2210.09573)\]. ViTCoD focuses on algorithm and accelerator co-design, achieving up to 235.3× speedup by reducing dominant data movement costs \[[4](http://arxiv.org/abs/2302.09108)\]. ViTA presents a configurable hardware accelerator for edge deployment, achieving nearly 90% hardware utilization efficiency and a power consumption of 0.88W \[[5](http://arxiv.org/abs/2205.11397)\]. SuperViT reduces computational costs while improving accuracy, achieving a 2× reduction in FLOPs with a 0.2% accuracy gain \[[6](http://arxiv.org/abs/2211.05109)\]. VITALITY unifies low-rank and sparse approximation for ViT acceleration, achieving 3× faster inference with comparable accuracy \[[7](http://arxiv.org/abs/2103.15358)\]. 

Efficient ViT architectures have also been explored. Multi-Scale Vision Longformer enhances ViT for high-resolution image encoding using linear complexity attention mechanisms \[[8](http://arxiv.org/abs/2101.11986)\]. Tokens-to-Token ViT improves training efficiency by structuring image tokens progressively, reducing parameter count and MACs by half \[[9](http://arxiv.org/abs/2103.14899)\]. CrossViT introduces cross-attention for multi-scale feature fusion, achieving a 2% accuracy improvement over DeiT \[[10](http://arxiv.org/abs/2201.06618)\]. VAQF proposes a fully automatic framework for low-bit ViT acceleration on FPGAs, achieving 24 FPS with 8-bit activation quantization \[[11](http://arxiv.org/abs/2403.20230)\]. An FPGA-based accelerator for EfficientViT improves throughput and energy efficiency by 780.2 GOPS and 105.1 GOPS/W \[[12](http://arxiv.org/abs/2106.04533)\]. Chasing Sparsity in ViTs explores end-to-end sparsity, achieving 49.32% FLOPs reduction with minimal accuracy loss \[[13](http://arxiv.org/abs/2104.01136)\]. LeViT improves inference speed by optimizing the trade-off between accuracy and efficiency \[[14](http://arxiv.org/abs/2203.03821)\]. CF-ViT reduces computational burden by implementing a coarse-to-fine inference strategy \[[15](http://arxiv.org/abs/2402.09709)\]. ME-ViT minimizes memory traffic by using a single-load policy, achieving up to 17.89x improvement in memory bandwidth \[[16](http://arxiv.org/abs/2202.07800)\]. EViT reorganizes image tokens during inference, improving inference speed with minimal accuracy loss \[[17](http://arxiv.org/abs/2104.11227)\]. Multiscale Vision Transformers (MViT) connect multiscale feature hierarchies with transformer models, achieving superior performance on video and image recognition tasks \[[18](http://arxiv.org/abs/2105.15075)\].
