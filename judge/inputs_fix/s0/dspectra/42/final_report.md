The category "Helm Chart Security Risks" highlights significant concerns regarding the security of Helm Charts used in Kubernetes environments. Recent studies have shown that Helm Charts, which are essential for deploying containerized applications, often contain vulnerabilities that can be exploited through complex attack paths \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2006.15275)\]. These vulnerabilities are exacerbated by the fact that many Helm Charts use outdated or insecure container images, with approximately 88.1% of images being exposed to vulnerabilities, thereby jeopardizing 93.7% of the charts \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/2501.14753)\]. Furthermore, the lack of proper maintenance and licensing for many Helm Charts contributes to the overall risk landscape, as most charts are not officially maintained or licensed \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/2501.14753)\]. The security risks are further compounded by the automation-heavy nature of container orchestration, which relies on tools like Kubernetes and Helm to manage deployments \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2006.15275)\]. As a result, the security of Helm Charts has become a critical area of focus, with researchers proposing methodologies to extract and evaluate security models, identify riskiest attack paths, and perform risk assessments based on MITRE ATT&CK frameworks \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2006.15275)\]. These findings underscore the need for improved practices in Helm Chart development and management to mitigate potential security threats in cloud-native environments.
