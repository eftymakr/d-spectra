## Related Works

Generative retrieval (GR) has recently gained significant attention as a novel paradigm in information retrieval (IR), offering a direct mapping from queries to relevant document identifiers (docids) without the need for traditional indexing or reranking \[[15](http://arxiv.org/abs/2406.01197)\]. This approach leverages sequence-to-sequence models to generate docids, enabling end-to-end learning of retrieval tasks \[[15](http://arxiv.org/abs/2406.01197)\]. However, most existing GR models are primarily evaluated on static document collections, with limited exploration of their performance in dynamic corpora where documents are continuously added \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2308.14968)\]. This section reviews related works that address the challenges of GR in dynamic settings, docid design, and model efficiency.

Several studies have explored the use of text-based docids in GR, which are often generated as natural language sequences such as titles or n-grams \[[3](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/6682a3679a17d090830635f1c420126d20eae54a)\]. These docids benefit from the pre-trained knowledge of language models, allowing for better semantic alignment and fine-grained representation of documents \[[16](http://arxiv.org/abs/2409.18409)\]. In contrast, numeric-based docids, which are typically fixed identifiers assigned during preprocessing, are more efficient but suffer from poor generalization to unseen documents \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2308.14968)\]. This is attributed to their tendency to overfit to the initial document set, leading to suboptimal performance in dynamic environments \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2308.14968)\]. 

The importance of docid design in GR has been highlighted in several works. For instance, \[[3](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/6682a3679a17d090830635f1c420126d20eae54a)\] proposes NOVO docids, which are learnable and interpretable n-gram sets that can be optimized during training to capture semantic relationships between documents. Similarly, \[[13](http://arxiv.org/abs/2305.13859)\] introduces Term-Set Generation (TSGen), which uses a set of terms as docids to improve retrieval accuracy by avoiding token pruning errors. These approaches emphasize the need for flexible and semantically rich docid representations that can adapt to evolving document collections.

Efforts to improve the scalability of GR to large-scale corpora have also been explored. \[[10](http://arxiv.org/abs/2305.11841)\] investigates how generative retrieval scales to millions of passages, finding that synthetic queries are crucial for indexing, while existing architectural modifications are often ineffective when considering computational costs. \[[14](http://arxiv.org/abs/2311.09134)\] proposes RIPOR, an optimization framework that enhances the accuracy of relevance estimation during sequential docid generation, achieving significant improvements on standard benchmarks. These studies underscore the challenges of deploying GR in real-world scenarios with large and dynamic document collections.

In addition, \[[7](http://arxiv.org/abs/2408.02152)\] introduces Few-Shot GR, which eliminates the need for extensive training by prompting large language models (LLMs) to generate docids during indexing. This approach reduces training costs and enables rapid adaptation to new documents. \[[17](http://arxiv.org/abs/2310.12455)\] further advances this idea by proposing an end-to-end retrieval paradigm that automatically learns optimal docids for both existing and new documents, demonstrating the potential of fully automated GR systems.

Overall, the literature highlights the trade-offs between efficiency and effectiveness in GR, with text-based docids offering better performance in dynamic settings but at the cost of computational overhead. Future work should focus on hybrid approaches that combine the strengths of both numeric and text-based docids \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2308.14968)\].

### <a id='category-generative-retrieval-(gr)-overview'></a>Generative Retrieval (GR) Overview: 4 papers found.
Generative retrieval (GR) has gained significant traction in information retrieval, with recent studies focusing on improving the efficiency and effectiveness of document retrieval through direct generation of relevant document identifiers (docids) \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2308.14968)\]. Unlike traditional retrieval pipelines that rely on separate indexing, retrieval, and ranking stages, GR employs sequence-to-sequence models to map queries directly to docids, enabling more flexible and integrated retrieval \[[2](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/5beaeee1e27007bd12967480bb9c379cebd15e6e)\]. However, existing GR models often face limitations, particularly in handling dynamic corpora where document collections evolve over time \[[3](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/6682a3679a17d090830635f1c420126d20eae54a)\]. For instance, models using numeric-based docids, while efficient, tend to overfit to the initial training data, leading to poor performance in dynamic settings \[[4](http://arxiv.org/abs/2304.04171)\]. In contrast, text-based docids demonstrate superior generalization, likely due to their fine-grained design and alignment with language models' pretrained knowledge \[[5](http://arxiv.org/abs/2305.18952)\]. This distinction highlights the trade-off between efficiency and adaptability in GR systems, prompting recent efforts to combine the strengths of both approaches \[[6](http://arxiv.org/abs/2208.09257)\]. Additionally, the challenge of generating distinct and relevant docids in multi-graded relevance scenarios has led to innovations such as GR$^2$, which incorporates constrained contrastive training to enhance relevance and diversity \[[7](http://arxiv.org/abs/2408.02152)\]. These developments underscore the ongoing evolution of GR, with a growing emphasis on scalability, adaptability, and performance in real-world, dynamic environments \[[8](http://arxiv.org/abs/2402.01176)\].

### <a id='category-end-to-end-retrieval-paradigms'></a>End-to-End Retrieval Paradigms: 5 papers found.
End-to-end retrieval paradigms have gained significant attention as alternatives to traditional index-retrieve frameworks, which are limited in their ability to jointly optimize retrieval quality \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2308.14968)\]. Generative retrieval, a key approach within this paradigm, represents documents as identifiers (docids) and retrieves them by generating these identifiers directly from queries, enabling fully end-to-end modeling \[[2](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/5beaeee1e27007bd12967480bb9c379cebd15e6e)\]. However, defining effective docids remains a challenge, as existing methods often rely on fixed rule-based approaches such as document titles or BERT clustering, which fail to capture comprehensive semantic information \[[3](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/6682a3679a17d090830635f1c420126d20eae54a)\]. To address this, GenRet introduces a discrete auto-encoding approach to learn semantic docids through a tokenization model, reconstruction model, and sequence-to-sequence retrieval model \[[4](http://arxiv.org/abs/2304.04171)\]. Similarly, Ultron proposes a model-based indexer that encodes document knowledge into the model, aiming to directly retrieve relevant documents end-to-end, while addressing issues like semantically deficient docids and limited supervised data \[[5](http://arxiv.org/abs/2305.18952)\]. The importance of docid design is further emphasized in CorpusLM, which integrates generative retrieval with RAG and closed-book generation, highlighting the need for richer, semantically meaningful docids to improve retrieval and downstream task performance \[[6](http://arxiv.org/abs/2208.09257)\]. DynamicRetriever also explores a model-based IR system that eliminates the need for static indexes by embedding document identifiers directly into the model, enabling dynamic and updatable retrieval \[[7](http://arxiv.org/abs/2408.02152)\]. Finally, Auto Search Indexer (ASI) proposes a fully end-to-end retrieval paradigm that automatically learns optimal docids for both existing and new documents, demonstrating the potential of joint optimization frameworks \[[8](http://arxiv.org/abs/2402.01176)\]. These works collectively highlight the evolving landscape of end-to-end retrieval, emphasizing the critical role of docid design and the benefits of fully integrated models.

The scalability of generative retrieval (GR) models remains a critical challenge, particularly when extending these models to large-scale document corpora. Existing GR approaches, which frame retrieval as a sequence-to-sequence task, have primarily been evaluated on smaller datasets, typically with corpora of up to 100k documents \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2308.14968)\]. However, recent studies have begun to explore the performance of GR models on larger-scale benchmarks, such as the MS MARCO passage ranking task, which includes 8.8M passages \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2308.14968)\]. These studies highlight key limitations, including the ineffectiveness of architectural modifications when considering computational costs and the challenges of naively scaling model parameters without sacrificing retrieval performance \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2308.14968)\]. Additionally, the use of synthetic queries during indexing has been identified as crucial for improving the effectiveness of GR models on large corpora \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2308.14968)\]. In parallel, researchers have proposed hybrid retrieval strategies that combine dense vector indexes with sparse encoder indexes to enhance retrieval accuracy, particularly in the context of Retrieval-Augmented Generation (RAG) systems \[[2](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/5beaeee1e27007bd12967480bb9c379cebd15e6e)\]. These blended approaches have demonstrated superior performance on benchmark datasets like NQ and TREC-COVID, and have been extended to improve Generative Q&A systems \[[2](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/5beaeee1e27007bd12967480bb9c379cebd15e6e)\]. Furthermore, the development of optimization frameworks like RIPOR has enabled GR models to achieve significant improvements in performance on large-scale benchmarks, such as the MS MARCO dataset, with MRR improvements of up to 30.5% \[[3](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/6682a3679a17d090830635f1c420126d20eae54a)\]. These advancements underscore the ongoing efforts to address the scalability challenges of GR models, particularly in dynamic environments where document collections evolve continuously \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2308.14968)\].

It assigns each document a unique identifier, known as the DocID, and employs a generative model to directly generate the relevant DocID for the input query. A common choice for the DocID is one or several natural language sequences, e.g. the title, synthetic queries, or n-grams, so that the pre-trained knowledge of the generative model can be effectively utilized. However, a sequence is generated token by token, where only the most likely candidates are kept and the rest are pruned at each decoding step, thus, retrieval fails if any token within the relevant DocID is falsely pruned. What's worse, during decoding, the model can only perceive preceding tokens in the DocID while being blind to subsequent ones, hence is prone to make such errors. To address this problem, we present a novel framework for generative retrieval, dubbed Term-Set Generation (TSGen). Instead of sequences, we use a set of terms as the DocID. The terms are selected based on learned weights from relevance signals, so that they concisely summarize the document's semantics and distinguish it from others. On top of the term-set DocID, we propose a permutation-invariant decoding algorithm, with which the term set can be generated in any permutation yet will always lead to the corresponding document. Remarkably, TSGen perceives all valid terms rather than only the preceding ones at each decoding step. Given the constant decoding space, it can make more reliable decisions due to the broader perspective. TSGen is also resilient to errors: the relevant DocID will not be falsely pruned as long as the decoded term belongs to it. Moreover, TSGen can explore the optimal decoding permutation of the term set on its own, which further improves the likelihood of generating the relevant DocID. Lastly, we design an iterative optimization procedure to incentivize the model to generate the relevant term set in its favorable permutation. We conduct extensive experiments on popular benchmarks of generative retrieval, which validate the effectiveness, the generalizability, the scalability, and the efficiency of TSGen.»

The design of text-based DocIDs plays a crucial role in generative retrieval, as they enable more precise and semantically rich document identification. Unlike numeric-based DocIDs, which are typically fixed identifiers, text-based DocIDs leverage natural language sequences such as titles, synthetic queries, or n-grams to align with the pre-trained knowledge of generative models \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2308.14968)\]. This alignment allows for better semantic representation and improved generalization, especially in dynamic corpora where document collections evolve over time \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2308.14968)\]. The use of text-based DocIDs also facilitates fine-grained document differentiation, as the terms included in the DocID are selected based on learned weights from relevance signals, ensuring they concisely capture the document’s meaning and distinguish it from others \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2308.14968)\]. Furthermore, text-based DocIDs offer high lexical diversity, which enhances their adaptability to new and unseen documents \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2308.14968)\]. These advantages make text-based DocIDs particularly effective in scenarios where models must perform well on evolving data, as demonstrated by recent studies showing their superiority over both numeric-based approaches and traditional retrieval methods like BM25 \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2308.14968)\].
