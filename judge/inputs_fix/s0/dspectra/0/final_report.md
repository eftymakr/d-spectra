### <a id='category-reasoning-and-action-integration-in-language-models'></a>Reasoning and Action Integration in Language Models: 11 papers found.
The category "Reasoning and Action Integration in Language Models" encompasses a range of studies focused on enhancing the reasoning and action capabilities of large language models (LLMs). Research has shown that fine-tuning LLMs on chain-of-thought (COT) reasoning data can significantly improve their reasoning abilities, but this approach often leads to an assessment misalignment problem where models assign higher scores to subpar COTs \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2309.02144)\]. To address this, Alignment Fine-Tuning (AFT) was introduced, involving three steps: COT training, generating multiple COT responses, and calibrating scores with a constraint alignment loss to ensure positive responses outperform negative ones \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2309.02144)\]. Additionally, the Beyond the Imitation Game benchmark (BIG-bench) was developed to evaluate LLMs on tasks believed to be beyond their current capabilities, highlighting the need for better understanding of their limitations and potential \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/2206.04615)\]. The ReAct approach integrates reasoning and action by allowing LLMs to generate reasoning traces and task-specific actions in an interleaved manner, improving performance on tasks like question answering and fact verification by interacting with external sources such as Wikipedia \[[4](http://arxiv.org/abs/2210.03629)\]. Furthermore, the emergence of new abilities in LLMs as they scale has been observed, indicating that additional scaling could further expand their capabilities \[[5](http://arxiv.org/abs/2206.07682)\]. Toolformer demonstrates how LLMs can teach themselves to use external tools via APIs, achieving improved zero-shot performance across various tasks \[[6](http://arxiv.org/abs/2302.04761)\]. The Gopher model, with up to 280 billion parameters, has been evaluated on a wide range of tasks, showing significant gains in areas like reading comprehension and fact-checking, though logical and mathematical reasoning benefits less \[[7](http://arxiv.org/abs/2006.05048)\]. These studies collectively highlight the ongoing efforts to enhance LLMs' reasoning and action capabilities through advanced training methods, benchmarking, and integration with external resources.
