## Related Works

Economic inequality remains a critical challenge with significant implications for education, healthcare, and social stability \[[10](http://arxiv.org/abs/2311.17252)\]. Traditional tax systems, such as the U.S. federal income tax, aim to reduce inequality but often lack the adaptability required to address dynamic economic conditions \[[10](http://arxiv.org/abs/2311.17252)\]. Models like the Saez Optimal Taxation framework attempt to provide a more flexible approach by adjusting tax rates based on economic conditions, yet they struggle to account for taxpayer heterogeneity and irrational behavior \[[10](http://arxiv.org/abs/2311.17252)\]. This limitation highlights the need for more sophisticated models that can simulate complex economic behaviors and adapt to changing conditions.

Agent-based modeling (ABM) has emerged as a powerful tool for simulating economic systems and evaluating policy interventions \[[9](http://arxiv.org/abs/1804.09284)\]. ABMs allow researchers to explore how individual agent behaviors and interactions can lead to macro-level economic outcomes \[[9](http://arxiv.org/abs/1804.09284)\]. However, traditional ABMs often rely on heuristic decision-making rules or regression models trained on historical data, which may not fully capture the complexity of human decision-making \[[7](http://arxiv.org/abs/2006.05048)\]. This limitation has led to the exploration of reinforcement learning (RL) models as a more adaptive and behaviorally valid approach to agent decision-making in ABMs \[[7](http://arxiv.org/abs/2006.05048)\]. RL models have been shown to produce reward-seeking behaviors and can outperform traditional behavioral models in certain economic simulations \[[7](http://arxiv.org/abs/2006.05048)\].

Large language models (LLMs) have also been increasingly integrated into economic modeling to enhance the realism and adaptability of simulations \[[10](http://arxiv.org/abs/2311.17252)\]. LLMs can generate more nuanced and context-aware agent behaviors, allowing for more accurate representations of economic decision-making \[[10](http://arxiv.org/abs/2311.17252)\]. The integration of LLMs with ABMs, as proposed in the TaxAgent framework, represents a novel approach to designing adaptive tax policies \[[10](http://arxiv.org/abs/2311.17252)\]. This integration enables the government agent (TaxAgent) to iteratively optimize tax rates based on real-time economic data and model outcomes \[[10](http://arxiv.org/abs/2311.17252)\]. This approach not only improves the adaptability of tax policies but also enhances the interpretability of the model by providing detailed reasoning traces \[[4](http://arxiv.org/abs/2210.03629)\].

Recent studies have also explored the use of LLMs in economic modeling, such as in the development of tax preparation software \[[13](http://arxiv.org/abs/2504.18693)\]. These studies highlight the potential of LLMs to automate the extraction and integration of tax law amendments into software systems, reducing the manual effort required for tax software maintenance \[[13](http://arxiv.org/abs/2504.18693)\]. The ability of LLMs to understand and apply complex legal and economic rules has been demonstrated in various domains, including tax law and economic policy \[[13](http://arxiv.org/abs/2504.18693)\].

In addition, the use of LLMs for reasoning and acting in language models has been explored through approaches like ReAct \[[4](http://arxiv.org/abs/2210.03629)\]. ReAct combines reasoning traces with task-specific actions, allowing models to interact with external sources to gather additional information and improve decision-making \[[4](http://arxiv.org/abs/2210.03629)\]. This approach has been successfully applied to tasks such as question answering and fact verification, demonstrating the potential of LLMs to enhance economic modeling by incorporating real-time data and external knowledge \[[4](http://arxiv.org/abs/2210.03629)\].

The integration of LLMs with ABMs, as proposed in the TaxAgent framework, represents a significant advancement in economic modeling by combining the strengths of both approaches \[[10](http://arxiv.org/abs/2311.17252)\]. This integration allows for more accurate and adaptive economic simulations, which can inform more effective tax policies \[[10](http://arxiv.org/abs/2311.17252)\].
