## Related Works

Neural retrieval methods using transformer-based pre-trained language models have significantly advanced multilingual and cross-lingual retrieval \[[22](http://arxiv.org/abs/1810.04805)\]. These models leverage the power of deep bidirectional transformers to capture rich semantic representations, enabling more effective query-document matching \[[22](http://arxiv.org/abs/1810.04805)\]. However, their effectiveness for low-resource, morphologically rich languages such as Amharic remains underexplored due to data scarcity and suboptimal tokenization \[[26](http://arxiv.org/abs/2503.18570)\]. This gap is addressed by introducing Amharic-specific dense retrieval models based on pre-trained Amharic BERT and RoBERTa backbones \[[26](http://arxiv.org/abs/2503.18570)\].

The RoBERTa-Base-Amharic-Embed model (110M parameters) achieves a 17.6% relative improvement in MRR@10 and a 9.86% gain in Recall@10 over the strongest multilingual baseline, Arctic Embed 2.0 (568M parameters) \[[26](http://arxiv.org/abs/2503.18570)\]. This performance is attributed to the model's ability to better capture the morphological richness of Amharic through fine-tuning on language-specific data. More compact variants, such as RoBERTa-Medium-Amharic-Embed (42M), remain competitive while being over 13x smaller, demonstrating the feasibility of deploying efficient models in resource-constrained settings \[[26](http://arxiv.org/abs/2503.18570)\].

Additionally, a ColBERT-based late interaction retrieval model is trained, achieving the highest MRR@10 score (0.843) among all evaluated models \[[26](http://arxiv.org/abs/2503.18570)\]. ColBERT introduces a late interaction architecture that independently encodes the query and the document using BERT and then employs a cheap yet powerful interaction step that models their fine-grained similarity \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/2004.12832)\]. This approach allows for more precise relevance modeling, which is crucial for low-resource languages where data is scarce \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/2004.12832)\].

The effectiveness of dense retrieval models is further supported by studies showing that pretraining multilingual language models at scale leads to significant performance gains for cross-lingual transfer tasks \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/1911.02116)\]. For instance, XLM-R, a multilingual language model trained on one hundred languages, significantly outperforms multilingual BERT on various cross-lingual benchmarks \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/1911.02116)\]. This highlights the importance of language-specific adaptation in improving retrieval performance for underrepresented languages \[[26](http://arxiv.org/abs/2503.18570)\].

To evaluate the proposed models, they are benchmarked against both sparse and dense retrieval baselines, providing a systematic assessment of retrieval effectiveness in Amharic \[[26](http://arxiv.org/abs/2503.18570)\]. This evaluation reveals key challenges in low-resource settings, such as the need for language-specific tokenization and the importance of domain adaptation \[[26](http://arxiv.org/abs/2503.18570)\].

In summary, the proposed models build upon existing research in neural retrieval and multilingual language modeling to address the specific challenges of low-resource languages like Amharic \[[26](http://arxiv.org/abs/2503.18570)\]. These efforts are crucial for advancing information retrieval in underrepresented languages and fostering future research in this area \[[26](http://arxiv.org/abs/2503.18570)\].

### <a id='category-efficient-model-variants'></a>Efficient Model Variants: 7 papers found.
Efficient Model Variants focus on improving the computational efficiency and performance of neural retrieval and language models. These models aim to reduce the high computational overhead associated with traditional transformer-based approaches, such as BERT and RoBERTa, which require significant resources for inference and training \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/2004.12832)\]. For example, Sentence-BERT (SBERT) introduces siamese and triplet network structures to generate semantically meaningful sentence embeddings, drastically reducing the time needed for semantic similarity search from hours to seconds \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/2004.12832)\]. Similarly, RepBERT uses fixed-length contextualized embeddings to represent queries and documents, achieving state-of-the-art results on tasks like MS MARCO Passage Ranking while maintaining efficiency comparable to bag-of-words methods \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/1907.11692)\]. In the context of cross-language retrieval, adapter-based models have been proposed to enhance transferability between languages without full fine-tuning, showing improved performance over traditional fine-tuning approaches \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/1911.02116)\]. Additionally, dense retrieval models, such as those used in open-domain question answering, have demonstrated superior performance over traditional sparse models like BM25, achieving significant accuracy improvements with minimal computational overhead \[[4](http://arxiv.org/abs/1901.04085)\]. Training strategies like Learning To Retrieve (LTRe) further enhance efficiency by avoiding negative sampling during training, leading to faster convergence and better retrieval performance \[[5](http://arxiv.org/abs/2201.08471)\]. The TAS-Balanced approach also contributes to efficiency by introducing topic-aware query sampling and balanced margin sampling, enabling training on a single GPU within a short time frame \[[6](http://arxiv.org/abs/1905.09217)\]. These advancements collectively highlight the growing emphasis on developing efficient and scalable neural models for information retrieval and natural language processing tasks.
