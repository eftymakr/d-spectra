## Related Works

KV cache management has emerged as a critical area of research for improving the efficiency of large language model (LLM) serving systems. Prior work has focused on reducing the memory footprint and computational overhead of KV caches, which are essential for enabling long-context inference. Several approaches have been proposed to address these challenges, including compression techniques, eviction policies, and adaptive reuse strategies.

Early efforts in KV cache compression focused on quantization and sparsity, aiming to reduce the memory usage of the cache while preserving generation quality. For instance, KIVI introduces a tuning-free 2bit quantization method that quantizes key caches per-channel and value caches per-token, achieving a 2.6× reduction in peak memory usage without significant performance degradation \[[7](http://arxiv.org/abs/2402.02750)\]. Similarly, MiniCache compresses the KV cache across layers by exploiting the high similarity between adjacent layers, achieving up to 5.02x compression ratios and improving inference throughput by approximately 5x \[[4](http://arxiv.org/abs/2405.14366)\]. These methods demonstrate the potential of compression to reduce memory demands, but they often require careful tuning or are limited in their applicability to specific model architectures.

In addition to compression, researchers have explored KV cache eviction strategies to manage memory constraints during inference. CAKE introduces a cascading and adaptive eviction policy that considers layer-specific attention patterns and temporal dynamics, enabling efficient resource allocation across layers \[[13](http://arxiv.org/abs/2503.12491)\]. This approach improves cache utilization by dynamically adjusting the cache size based on the importance of tokens over time. Similarly, KVMerger proposes an adaptive merging strategy that leverages the high similarity of key states within a sequence to reduce the cache size without significant performance loss \[[9](http://arxiv.org/abs/2407.08454)\]. These methods highlight the importance of adaptive and context-aware strategies in managing the KV cache effectively.

Another line of research focuses on the integration of KV cache compression with other optimization techniques, such as prompt compression and sparse attention. For example, VL-Cache extends KV cache compression to vision-language models (VLMs) by introducing sparsity-aware budget allocation and modality-aware token scoring, achieving a 90% reduction in memory footprint while maintaining high accuracy \[[5](http://arxiv.org/abs/2410.23317)\]. These methods demonstrate the versatility of KV cache management techniques across different model types and tasks.

Furthermore, recent studies have emphasized the need for comprehensive benchmarking of KV cache management strategies. SCBench provides a detailed analysis of long-context methods from a KV cache-centric perspective, evaluating techniques such as sparse attention, quantization, and retrieval \[[12](http://arxiv.org/abs/2412.10319)\]. This benchmarking framework helps identify the strengths and limitations of various approaches, guiding future research and development.

In summary, the field of KV cache management has seen significant advancements, with a growing focus on compression, eviction, and adaptive reuse strategies. These techniques aim to improve the efficiency and scalability of LLM serving systems, particularly in long-context scenarios. Future work should continue to explore the interplay between these methods and their impact on real-world deployment.

The category "Long-Context Inference Optimization" focuses on improving the efficiency and performance of large language models (LLMs) during inference, particularly in handling long-context scenarios. Key strategies include optimizing key-value (KV) cache management, which is critical for reducing redundant computations and improving memory utilization. Techniques such as KV cache compression, like CacheGen, reduce the size of KV caches by up to 3.5–4.3x while maintaining high generation quality \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2310.07240)\]. Similarly, KVMerger introduces adaptive compression methods that preserve performance under memory constraints by leveraging token-level similarities in key states \[[5](http://arxiv.org/abs/2410.23317)\]. KVLink enhances cache reuse by precomputing and concatenating KV caches for shared documents, reducing time-to-first-token by up to 96% \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/2412.19442)\]. InfiniGen further optimizes KV cache management by prefetching essential cache entries, improving performance by up to 3.00x compared to prior methods \[[4](http://arxiv.org/abs/2405.14366)\]. These approaches collectively address the challenges of long-context inference, including high memory usage and network delays, by introducing efficient compression, adaptive eviction policies, and improved cache reuse strategies.

### <a id='category-benchmarking-and-evaluation-frameworks'></a>Benchmarking and Evaluation Frameworks: 2 papers found.
The category "Benchmarking and Evaluation Frameworks" focuses on the development and evaluation of methods for improving the efficiency and performance of long-context large language models (LLMs). Existing research highlights the challenges of handling long context inputs, such as the exponential growth of the KV cache and the computational complexity of attending to extended sequences \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2310.07240)\]. To address these challenges, various efficiency-driven approaches have been proposed, including KV cache quantization, token dropping, prompt compression, and hybrid architectures that combine linear-time sequence models with attention mechanisms \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2310.07240)\]. However, these methods have not been comprehensively benchmarked in a unified framework that aligns with real-world usage scenarios \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2310.07240)\]. Recent work has introduced SCBench, a comprehensive benchmark that evaluates long-context methods from a KV cache-centric perspective, covering four key stages: KV cache generation, compression, retrieval, and loading \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/2407.01527)\]. SCBench uses shared context examples across 12 tasks, with two modes of shared context, to assess the performance of eight categories of long-context solutions, including Gated Linear RNNs, Mamba-Attention hybrids, and efficient methods like sparse attention and layer-level sparsity \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/2407.01527)\]. This benchmark provides critical insights into the trade-offs between memory usage and performance, revealing that sub-O(n) memory methods struggle in multi-turn scenarios, while O(n) memory approaches with sub-O(n²) pre-filling computation show robust performance \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/2407.01527)\]. These findings underscore the importance of developing benchmarking frameworks that reflect the complexities of real-world LLM serving environments.

### <a id='category-kv-cache-compression-techniques'></a>KV Cache Compression Techniques: 4 papers found.
Key-value (KV) caching is a critical technique for improving the inference speed and throughput of large language models (LLMs) by storing previously generated token states to avoid redundant computations \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2310.07240)\]. However, the memory footprint of the KV cache grows with sequence length and batch size, becoming a bottleneck in deployment \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2310.07240)\]. Several approaches have been proposed to address this challenge, including compression techniques that reduce memory usage while maintaining generation quality. For instance, MiniCache compresses the KV cache across layers by exploiting the high similarity between adjacent layers, achieving significant memory savings and improved throughput \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2310.07240)\]. Similarly, VL-Cache introduces a layer-adaptive sparsity-aware cache budget allocation method tailored for vision-language models (VLMs), enabling efficient KV cache compression without compromising accuracy \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/2407.01527)\]. Quantization has also been explored as a straightforward method to reduce the size of the KV cache, with KIVI proposing a 2-bit quantization algorithm that reduces memory usage by 2.6x while maintaining model quality \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/2412.19442)\]. Additionally, the Mixed-precision KV cache (MiKV) addresses the trade-off between compression and performance by retaining evicted KV pairs in low-precision and important KV pairs in high-precision, mitigating the risks of context loss and hallucinations \[[4](http://arxiv.org/abs/2405.14366)\]. These methods highlight the ongoing efforts to optimize KV caching for both LLMs and VLMs, emphasizing the need for workload-aware strategies that balance memory efficiency with generation accuracy.

### <a id='category-real-world-deployment-considerations'></a>Real-World Deployment Considerations: 1 papers found.
The "Real-World Deployment Considerations" category highlights the practical challenges and limitations of implementing KV cache compression in production environments. While various compression algorithms have been proposed to reduce memory consumption and improve computational efficiency, their adoption in real-world LLM serving systems remains limited. This is due to several factors, including suboptimal throughput performance caused by current implementations such as FlashAttention and PagedAttention, which are not optimized for production-level serving \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2310.07240)\]. Additionally, compression can lead to increased end-to-end latency as compressed KV caches may result in longer outputs \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2310.07240)\]. These issues underscore the need for more robust and efficient compression techniques tailored to the specific demands of real-world workloads. Furthermore, the accuracy of individual samples under compression reveals inherent limitations in handling specific LLM tasks, indicating that further research is necessary to address these challenges \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2310.07240)\]. The category also emphasizes the importance of empirical evaluation and the development of tools to facilitate the practical deployment of KV compression solutions, as demonstrated by the open-source repository provided \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2310.07240)\].

The field of adaptive cache eviction policies has seen significant advancements, particularly in the context of large language models (LLMs) where efficient memory management is critical for performance. Recent approaches have focused on improving the rational allocation of key-value (KV) cache across different layers of the model, considering both spatial and temporal dynamics of attention mechanisms \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2310.07240)\]. For instance, the CAKE framework introduces a novel method that frames KV cache eviction as a "cake-slicing problem," enabling a global view of cache allocation by assessing layer-specific preferences and managing memory constraints in a cascading manner \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2310.07240)\]. This approach not only adapts to varying attention patterns but also introduces an eviction indicator that accounts for the shifting importance of tokens over time, addressing limitations in existing methods that overlook temporal dynamics \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2310.07240)\]. Additionally, prior work has highlighted the importance of workload-aware strategies, as cache eviction policies must be tailored to the specific patterns of KV reuse observed in real-world scenarios \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/2407.01527)\]. These studies underscore the need for policies that balance performance and memory efficiency, especially under constrained resource conditions.
