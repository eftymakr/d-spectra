## Related Works

The rapid growth of open-source machine learning (ML) resources has significantly impacted information retrieval (IR) research, particularly in the areas of resource management and recommendation systems. Existing platforms such as Hugging Face have become central hubs for sharing and collaborating on ML models and datasets \[[10](http://arxiv.org/abs/2405.13058)\]. However, these platforms often lack structured representations of resources, which limits advanced querying and analysis capabilities such as tracing model evolution and recommending relevant datasets \[[10](http://arxiv.org/abs/2405.13058)\]. This limitation has been identified in multiple studies that examine the Hugging Face ecosystem \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2311.13380)\]\[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/2305.11164)\]\[[10](http://arxiv.org/abs/2405.13058)\].

Several studies have explored the characteristics and challenges of ML model and dataset documentation on Hugging Face. For example, \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2311.13380)\] highlights the importance of structured documentation for improving the reliability and reproducibility of ML models. Similarly, \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/2305.11164)\] discusses the need for better documentation practices to enhance the usability of datasets and models. These studies emphasize that poor documentation can hinder the effective use and evaluation of ML resources \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2311.13380)\]\[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/2305.11164)\].

In addition to documentation, the maintenance and evolution of ML models have been extensively studied. \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2311.13380)\] and \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/2305.11164)\] both examine how models are maintained and updated over time, noting that many models lack proper maintenance tracking and versioning. This issue is further compounded by the lack of standardized practices for reporting carbon emissions associated with model training and usage \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/2305.11164)\]. These studies highlight the need for more transparent and systematic approaches to model and dataset management \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2311.13380)\]\[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/2305.11164)\].

The Hugging Face Hub has also been evaluated for its suitability as a source for empirical studies on AI development. \[[9](http://arxiv.org/abs/2307.14841)\] compares Hugging Face with other code-hosting platforms like GitHub and GitLab, emphasizing its potential for supporting empirical research on AI-related projects. This study underscores the importance of a well-structured and accessible platform for facilitating large-scale analysis of ML resources \[[9](http://arxiv.org/abs/2307.14841)\].

Moreover, the use of pre-trained models (PTMs) in software systems has been a growing trend, with researchers exploring the challenges and benefits of model reuse. \[[12](http://arxiv.org/abs/2303.02552)\] and \[[17](http://arxiv.org/abs/2401.13177)\] both discuss the increasing reliance on PTMs and the challenges associated with their reuse, such as limited guidance for beginner users and difficulties in understanding model outputs. These studies highlight the need for better documentation and standardization in the PTM ecosystem \[[12](http://arxiv.org/abs/2303.02552)\]\[[17](http://arxiv.org/abs/2401.13177)\].

In summary, the existing body of work highlights the importance of structured representations, comprehensive documentation, and standardized practices in the management and reuse of ML resources. These insights provide a foundation for the development of HuggingKG, a knowledge graph designed to address these limitations by capturing domain-specific relations and textual attributes of ML resources \[[10](http://arxiv.org/abs/2405.13058)\].
