## Related Works

Diffusion models have become a cornerstone in generative AI, particularly for image and video synthesis due to their ability to iteratively refine outputs based on learned patterns \[[21](http://arxiv.org/abs/2105.05233)\]. However, the computational intensity of these models, especially diffusion transformers (DiT), poses challenges for deployment on resource-constrained devices \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2406.02540)\]. Prior work has focused on reducing the computational footprint of diffusion models through quantization, which involves reducing the bit-width of model parameters to lower memory usage and increase inference speed \[[25](http://arxiv.org/abs/2305.10657)\]. 

Several approaches have been proposed for quantizing diffusion models. For instance, Q-Diffusion introduces a novel PTQ method tailored to the unique multi-timestep pipeline of diffusion models, addressing the changing output distributions of noise estimation networks over time steps \[[5](http://arxiv.org/abs/2302.04304)\]. Similarly, PTQ4DiT identifies challenges in DiT quantization, such as salient channels with extreme magnitudes and temporal variability in activation distributions, and proposes solutions like Channel-wise Salience Balancing (CSB) and Spearmen's ρ-guided Salience Calibration (SSC) \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/2405.16005)\]. These methods aim to maintain high fidelity in image and video generation while enabling 4-bit quantization \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/2405.16005)\].

In the context of video generation, QVD introduces the first PTQ strategy specifically for video diffusion models, addressing the skewness of temporal features and inter-channel disparities in activation \[[20](http://arxiv.org/abs/2407.11585)\]. This work emphasizes the importance of preserving temporal consistency and spatial coherence in video generation, which is critical for maintaining visual quality \[[20](http://arxiv.org/abs/2407.11585)\]. Similarly, ViDiT-Q is designed for DiT models, achieving W8A8 and W4A8 quantization with minimal degradation in visual quality and metrics \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2406.02540)\]. It also implements efficient GPU kernels to enable practical memory savings and latency reduction \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2406.02540)\].

The challenges of quantizing diffusion models are further compounded by the need to maintain spatiotemporal consistency in video generation. For example, CogVideoX introduces a 3D VAE and expert transformer to improve text-video alignment and generate long, coherent videos \[[8](http://arxiv.org/abs/2408.06072)\]. Meanwhile, Q-DiT addresses the spatial and temporal variance in DiT models by integrating automatic quantization granularity allocation and sample-wise dynamic activation quantization \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/2406.17343)\]. These techniques help maintain high fidelity in image and video generation even at lower bit-widths \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/2406.17343)\].

Additionally, the importance of temporal consistency in video generation is highlighted by works such as FlowVid, which leverages optical flow to ensure temporal coherence during video-to-video synthesis \[[30](http://arxiv.org/abs/2312.17681)\]. Similarly, Adaptive Caching (AdaCache) accelerates video DiT models by caching computations and optimizing the quality-latency trade-off based on motion content \[[29](http://arxiv.org/abs/2411.02397)\]. These approaches demonstrate the growing interest in improving the efficiency and performance of diffusion models for video generation \[[29](http://arxiv.org/abs/2411.02397)\].

In summary, recent efforts in quantizing diffusion models have focused on addressing the unique challenges of video generation, including temporal consistency, spatial coherence, and the preservation of high-quality outputs at lower bit-widths. These works lay the foundation for more efficient and scalable diffusion models suitable for real-world applications \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/2405.16005)\]\[[5](http://arxiv.org/abs/2302.04304)\]\[[8](http://arxiv.org/abs/2408.06072)\]\[[20](http://arxiv.org/abs/2407.11585)\]\[[29](http://arxiv.org/abs/2411.02397)\].
