## Related Works

Diffusion models have become a cornerstone in generative AI, particularly for image and video synthesis due to their ability to iteratively refine outputs based on learned patterns \[[21](http://arxiv.org/abs/2105.05233)\]. However, the computational intensity of these models, especially diffusion transformers (DiT), poses challenges for deployment on resource-constrained devices \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2406.02540)\]. Prior work has focused on reducing the computational footprint of diffusion models through quantization, which involves reducing the bit-width of model parameters to lower memory usage and increase inference speed \[[25](http://arxiv.org/abs/2305.10657)\]. 

Several approaches have been proposed for quantizing diffusion models. For instance, Q-Diffusion introduces a novel PTQ method tailored to the unique multi-timestep pipeline of diffusion models, addressing the changing output distributions of noise estimation networks over time steps \[[5](http://arxiv.org/abs/2302.04304)\]. Similarly, PTQ4DiT identifies challenges in DiT quantization, such as salient channels with extreme magnitudes and temporal variability in activation distributions, and proposes solutions like Channel-wise Salience Balancing (CSB) and Spearmen's ρ-guided Salience Calibration (SSC) \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/2405.16005)\]. These methods aim to maintain high fidelity in image and video generation while enabling 4-bit quantization \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/2405.16005)\].

In the context of video generation, QVD introduces the first PTQ strategy specifically for video diffusion models, addressing the skewness of temporal features and inter-channel disparities in activation \[[20](http://arxiv.org/abs/2407.11585)\]. This work emphasizes the importance of preserving temporal consistency and spatial coherence in video generation, which is critical for maintaining visual quality \[[20](http://arxiv.org/abs/2407.11585)\]. Similarly, ViDiT-Q is designed for DiT models, achieving W8A8 and W4A8 quantization with minimal degradation in visual quality and metrics \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2406.02540)\]. It also implements efficient GPU kernels to enable practical memory savings and latency reduction \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2406.02540)\].

The challenges of quantizing diffusion models are further compounded by the need to maintain spatiotemporal consistency in video generation. For example, CogVideoX introduces a 3D VAE and expert transformer to improve text-video alignment and generate long, coherent videos \[[8](http://arxiv.org/abs/2408.06072)\]. Meanwhile, Q-DiT addresses the spatial and temporal variance in DiT models by integrating automatic quantization granularity allocation and sample-wise dynamic activation quantization \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/2406.17343)\]. These techniques help maintain high fidelity in image and video generation even at lower bit-widths \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/2406.17343)\].

Additionally, the importance of temporal consistency in video generation is highlighted by works such as FlowVid, which leverages optical flow to ensure temporal coherence during video-to-video synthesis \[[30](http://arxiv.org/abs/2312.17681)\]. Similarly, Adaptive Caching (AdaCache) accelerates video DiT models by caching computations and optimizing the quality-latency trade-off based on motion content \[[29](http://arxiv.org/abs/2411.02397)\]. These approaches demonstrate the growing interest in improving the efficiency and performance of diffusion models for video generation \[[29](http://arxiv.org/abs/2411.02397)\].

In summary, recent efforts in quantizing diffusion models have focused on addressing the unique challenges of video generation, including temporal consistency, spatial coherence, and the preservation of high-quality outputs at lower bit-widths. These works lay the foundation for more efficient and scalable diffusion models suitable for real-world applications \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/2405.16005)\]\[[5](http://arxiv.org/abs/2302.04304)\]\[[8](http://arxiv.org/abs/2408.06072)\]\[[20](http://arxiv.org/abs/2407.11585)\]\[[29](http://arxiv.org/abs/2411.02397)\].

Additionally, the Video LDM approach applies the latent diffusion model paradigm to high-resolution video generation, enabling efficient and expressive text-to-video modeling by introducing a temporal dimension to the latent space \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/2405.16005)\]. These methods highlight the ongoing efforts to improve temporal consistency, computational efficiency, and user control in video generation, paving the way for more practical and scalable solutions.

### <a id='category-efficiency-and-deployment-considerations'></a>Efficiency and Deployment Considerations: 4 papers found.
Efficiency and Deployment Considerations have been a focal point in improving the practicality of vision transformers and diffusion models. Researchers have focused on reducing memory storage and computational costs through quantization techniques, which are critical for deploying models on mobile devices and edge systems \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2406.02540)\]. For instance, post-training quantization algorithms have been developed to maintain the functionality of attention mechanisms by introducing ranking losses to preserve the relative order of self-attention results \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2406.02540)\]. Additionally, mixed-precision quantization schemes have been explored by leveraging the nuclear norm of attention maps and output features to balance performance and efficiency \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2406.02540)\]. Similarly, in diffusion models, the computational expense of classifier-free guided diffusion models has been addressed by distilling them into faster sampling models that require fewer steps while maintaining visual quality \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/2405.16005)\]. This approach has been demonstrated to significantly reduce inference time, achieving up to 256 times speedup on standard diffusion models and 10-fold acceleration on latent-space models like Stable Diffusion \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/2405.16005)\]. Furthermore, the challenges of quantization in vision transformers have been tackled by addressing inter-channel variation in LayerNorm inputs and proposing methods such as Power-of-Two Factor (PTF) and Log-Int-Softmax (LIS) to sustain performance with lower bit-widths \[[4](http://arxiv.org/abs/2312.00845)\]. Performance metrics and benchmarks in diffusion models have been extensively studied, with various approaches focusing on improving image and video generation quality. For instance, diffusion models have achieved superior image sample quality compared to existing state-of-the-art generative models, with FID scores as low as 2.97 on ImageNet 128×128 \[[21](http://arxiv.org/abs/2105.05233)\]. These models leverage classifier guidance to balance diversity and fidelity, demonstrating efficient computation while maintaining better coverage of the distribution \[[21](http://arxiv.org/abs/2105.05233)\]. Similarly, cascaded diffusion models have been shown to generate high-fidelity images on the class-conditional ImageNet benchmark without auxiliary classifiers, achieving FID scores of 1.48 at 64×64 resolution \[[24](http://arxiv.org/abs/2106.15282)\]. This approach relies on conditioning augmentation to prevent compounding errors during sampling. In the realm of 3D shape generation, DiT-3D has been proposed to directly operate on voxelized point clouds using plain Transformers, outperforming U-Net-based methods in terms of scalability and generation quality \[[17](http://arxiv.org/abs/2307.01831)\]. The model incorporates 3D positional and patch embeddings, along with 3D window attention to reduce computational costs, achieving significant improvements in metrics like 1-Nearest Neighbor Accuracy and Coverage \[[17](http://arxiv.org/abs/2307.01831)\]. Additionally, the integration of visual tokenizers with large language models has enabled LLMs to outperform diffusion models in image and video generation tasks, showcasing the potential for cross-modal generative models \[[27](http://arxiv.org/abs/2310.05737)\]. These advancements highlight the ongoing efforts to enhance performance metrics across different modalities and applications.
