## Related Works

Video super-resolution (VSR) has been a focal point in computer vision, with recent advances leveraging deep learning to address the challenges of temporal consistency and artifact reduction in high-resolution video generation. Early methods, such as those based on single-image super-resolution (SR), often failed to capture temporal dependencies, leading to inconsistent results across frames \[[7](http://arxiv.org/abs/1501.00092)\]. To address this, researchers introduced multi-frame SR approaches that incorporated motion estimation and compensation, such as optical flow-based methods \[[6](http://arxiv.org/abs/1611.05250)\]. However, these approaches were often computationally intensive and prone to errors due to inaccurate motion estimation \[[16](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/755227d7ea41b1d90df31f411f9ecbc998c20d26)\].

More recently, deep learning has enabled significant improvements in VSR. The introduction of deep convolutional neural networks (CNNs) for SR, such as the Enhanced Deep Residual Networks (EDSR) \[[9](http://arxiv.org/abs/1707.02921)\], demonstrated that residual learning could enhance performance by allowing deeper networks to train more effectively. These models were later extended to video restoration tasks, where temporal information from multiple frames was exploited using sliding window strategies or recurrent architectures \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/2201.12288)\]. However, these methods often suffered from high computational costs and limited temporal coherence \[[12](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/a2c50f93f6d0892918e0ff653750d198ccab48d0)\].

To further improve temporal consistency, researchers proposed recurrent architectures, such as the Bidirectional Recurrent Convolutional Network (BRCN) \[[12](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/a2c50f93f6d0892918e0ff653750d198ccab48d0)\], which modeled long-term dependencies using convolutional operations. This approach reduced parameter counts and improved efficiency compared to traditional RNNs. Similarly, the Progressive Fusion Video Super-Resolution Network \[[16](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/755227d7ea41b1d90df31f411f9ecbc998c20d26)\] introduced a novel fusion strategy that effectively integrated spatio-temporal information while reducing computational overhead.

Latent diffusion models (LDMs) have also gained traction in VSR, offering a promising approach to generate high-resolution videos while maintaining temporal consistency. The Stable Video Diffusion model \[[4](http://arxiv.org/abs/2311.15127)\] demonstrated the potential of latent space diffusion for text-to-video generation, but its application to real-world VSR remained limited due to the challenges of maintaining fidelity and consistency. Later work, such as the Video Diffusion Models \[[14](http://arxiv.org/abs/2204.03458)\], extended this idea by introducing temporal layers and finetuning on high-quality video datasets, enabling more realistic video generation. However, these models often required extensive training and were not optimized for real-world scenarios \[[13](http://arxiv.org/abs/2312.06640)\].

In response to these challenges, the Upscale-A-Video framework \[[13](http://arxiv.org/abs/2312.06640)\] introduced a text-guided latent diffusion model that ensured temporal coherence through locally integrated temporal layers and globally consistent latent propagation. This approach allowed for flexible control over texture generation and noise levels, enabling a trade-off between fidelity and quality. Additionally, the Lumiere model \[[23](http://arxiv.org/abs/2401.12945)\] introduced a space-time diffusion architecture that generated full-frame-rate videos directly, addressing the limitations of sequential synthesis methods.

In summary, while early VSR methods struggled with temporal consistency and computational efficiency, recent advances in deep learning and diffusion models have significantly improved performance. However, real-world applications still face challenges in maintaining fidelity and consistency, motivating the development of new frameworks such as the one proposed in this paper.
