## Related Works

Representation learning has become a cornerstone in modern recommender systems, enabling efficient and effective user-item matching \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/1905.08108)\]. Early methods relied on matrix factorization to learn low-dimensional embeddings, but recent advances have shifted toward deep learning-based approaches that better capture complex interactions \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/1905.08108)\]. Among these, graph neural networks (GNNs) have emerged as a powerful tool for modeling user-item interactions through graph structures \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/1905.08108)\]. However, the inherent complexity of GNNs, particularly in terms of computational overhead and over-smoothing, has motivated the development of simplified variants such as LightGCN \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/2002.02126)\], which focuses on linear propagation and avoids unnecessary nonlinear operations \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/2002.02126)\]. This approach has shown substantial improvements over more complex models like NGCF \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/2002.02126)\].

The use of binary representations for recommendation has also gained traction, as it enables efficient online inference by reducing memory usage and computational costs \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/1905.08108)\]. Prior work on binarized collaborative filtering, such as Binarized Collaborative Filtering with Distilling Graph Convolutional Networks \[[11](http://arxiv.org/abs/1906.01829)\], has explored the use of binary codes to represent users and items, leveraging distillation techniques to preserve ranking information. However, these methods often suffer from information loss due to the quantization process, leading to performance degradation \[[11](http://arxiv.org/abs/1906.01829)\]. To mitigate this, recent studies have proposed self-supervised learning approaches that enhance the robustness of graph-based recommendation models by incorporating auxiliary tasks that reinforce node representation learning \[[14](http://arxiv.org/abs/2010.10783)\]. These methods aim to minimize information loss during the binarization process, improving both accuracy and robustness.

Furthermore, the integration of contrastive learning into graph collaborative filtering has been explored to address data sparsity issues, with methods such as Neighborhood-enriched Contrastive Learning \[[13](http://arxiv.org/abs/2202.06200)\] explicitly incorporating structural and semantic neighbors into contrastive pairs. This approach enhances the model's ability to capture meaningful relationships by leveraging both local and global graph structures \[[13](http://arxiv.org/abs/2202.06200)\]. Similarly, the use of graph convolution networks (GCNs) for recommendation has been extensively studied, with models like DeepWalk \[[9](http://arxiv.org/abs/1403.6652)\] and LightGCN \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/2002.02126)\] demonstrating the effectiveness of learning latent representations from graph structures. However, these models often struggle with scalability and over-smoothing, prompting further research into simplification and efficiency improvements \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/2002.02126)\]\[[15](http://arxiv.org/abs/1902.07153)\].

In summary, while significant progress has been made in learning vectorized embeddings for recommendation, challenges remain in balancing efficiency, accuracy, and robustness. The proposed BiGeaR++ framework builds upon these prior works by introducing fine-grained inference distillation and embedding sample synthesis, further advancing the state of the art in graph-based collaborative filtering \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/1905.08108)\].
