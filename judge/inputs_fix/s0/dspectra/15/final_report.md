## Related Works

Brain tumor segmentation is a critical task in medical imaging, with significant implications for diagnosis, treatment planning, and prognosis. Several approaches have been proposed to address this challenge, leveraging deep learning models such as U-Net, 3D CNNs, and more recently, transformer-based architectures. Early works focused on 2D U-Net architectures, which were adapted for 3D MRI data by introducing 3D convolutions and modifications to the encoder-decoder structure \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/1812.01049)\]. These models demonstrated promising results but faced limitations in computational efficiency and handling of class imbalance \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/1812.01049)\].

The introduction of 3D CNNs allowed for better modeling of spatial relationships in MRI data, but they often required substantial computational resources due to the high dimensionality of 3D volumes \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/2001.02040)\]. To mitigate this, researchers explored efficient architectures, such as the 11-layer multi-scale 3D CNN combined with a 3D fully connected CRF for post-processing, which improved performance while maintaining computational efficiency \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/1603.05959)\]. This approach was evaluated on challenging tasks like brain tumor and stroke segmentation, achieving top rankings on public benchmarks \[[3](http://arxiv.org/abs/1603.05959)\].

Another significant advancement was the development of the nnU-Net framework, which is self-adapting and designed to handle diverse medical imaging tasks with minimal manual tuning \[[4](http://arxiv.org/abs/2210.13336)\]. This framework demonstrated high performance across multiple segmentation challenges, achieving the highest mean dice scores in several tasks \[[4](http://arxiv.org/abs/2210.13336)\]. However, it still required extensive training and underutilized pre-trained weights, which limited its applicability in resource-constrained settings \[[4](http://arxiv.org/abs/2210.13336)\].

To address these limitations, researchers proposed integrating pre-trained weights from ImageNet into the U-Net framework, which improved performance and reduced training time \[[5](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/420f3f1078a6d8e0696572c032877079286051c6)\]. This approach was further extended by incorporating axial-coronal-sagittal convolutions, which helped in capturing multi-orientation features critical for accurate segmentation \[[5](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/420f3f1078a6d8e0696572c032877079286051c6)\]. These strategies were validated on the BraTS dataset, showing improved efficiency and performance \[[5](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/420f3f1078a6d8e0696572c032877079286051c6)\].

Additionally, joint classification and segmentation models were explored, where pre-trained encoders from a glioma grade classification task were leveraged to enhance segmentation performance, especially for challenging tumor labels \[[6](http://arxiv.org/abs/2011.11052)\]. This approach demonstrated that transfer learning from related tasks could improve segmentation accuracy, particularly in cases with limited training data \[[6](http://arxiv.org/abs/2011.11052)\].

Recent works have also focused on efficient and lightweight architectures, such as the HDC-Net, which uses hierarchical decoupled convolutions to reduce computational overhead while maintaining accuracy \[[7](http://arxiv.org/abs/1705.03820)\]. Similarly, the E1D3 U-Net introduced a one-encoder, three-decoders architecture, enabling simultaneous segmentation of different tumor sub-regions \[[8](http://arxiv.org/abs/1709.00382)\]. These architectures demonstrated flexibility and efficiency, making them suitable for clinical applications \[[8](http://arxiv.org/abs/1709.00382)\].

In summary, the field of brain tumor segmentation has seen significant advancements, with a growing emphasis on efficiency, accuracy, and the utilization of pre-trained models. The proposed methods in this paper build upon these developments by integrating pre-trained weights and efficient convolutional strategies to enhance performance while reducing computational demands.
