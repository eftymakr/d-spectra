## Related Works

Maximum Inner Product Search (MIPS) has been a central challenge in machine learning and information retrieval, particularly in high-dimensional data applications \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/1405.5869)\]. Existing approaches to MIPS typically fall into two categories: those that rely solely on inner product (IP) similarity, which can suffer from local optima and redundant computations, and those that reduce MIPS to nearest neighbor search under the Euclidean metric via space projection, which can lead to topology destruction and information loss \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/1405.5869)\]. However, recent research has shown that these two paradigms are not inherently mutually exclusive \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/1405.5869)\]. Instead, by integrating IP and Euclidean metrics in the design of indexing and search algorithms, significant performance improvements can be achieved \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/1405.5869)\].

The use of graph-based methods for MIPS has gained traction due to their ability to capture complex relationships between vectors \[[7](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/25454f456c514219c9d6eeb42200ebbf378db9ca)\]. For instance, the inner-product navigable small world graph (ip-NSW) has been shown to achieve an order of magnitude speedup over existing baselines \[[5](http://arxiv.org/abs/1909.13459)\]. This graph-based approach leverages the norm bias of the MIPS problem, where large norm items are more likely to be the result of MIPS \[[5](http://arxiv.org/abs/1909.13459)\]. Similarly, the norm adjusted proximity graph (NAPG) improves upon graph-based indexing by adjusting factors based on sampled data, leading to more meaningful connections during graph construction \[[7](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/25454f456c514219c9d6eeb42200ebbf378db9ca)\]. These methods highlight the importance of data topology in the design of efficient MIPS algorithms.

Another line of research explores hashing-based approaches for approximate MIPS. Asymmetric LSH (ALSH) was introduced as a provably sublinear time algorithm for MIPS, leveraging asymmetric transformations to convert the problem into approximate near neighbor search \[[1](http://arxiv.org/abs/1405.5869)\]. This approach was later improved with norm-ranging LSH, which addresses the long-tail distribution of 2-norms in real datasets by partitioning the dataset into sub-datasets \[[12](http://arxiv.org/abs/1809.08782)\]. These hashing-based methods offer a balance between accuracy and efficiency, particularly for large-scale applications.

Quantization-based approaches have also been proposed for fast approximate MIPS. These methods quantize database vectors into multiple subspaces using codebooks learned to minimize inner product quantization error, allowing the inner product of a query to be approximated as the sum of inner products with the subspace quantizers \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/1509.01469)\]. This approach avoids the need for augmenting vectors in a higher-dimensional space, unlike some LSH-based methods \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/1509.01469)\]. Experimental results show that these quantization-based methods significantly outperform existing state-of-the-art techniques \[[2](http://arxiv.org/abs/1509.01469)\].

In addition to graph-based and hashing-based methods, clustering-based approaches have been explored for approximate MIPS. These methods partition vectors into clusters and only search a fraction of clusters during retrieval, offering an efficient solution for sparse MIPS \[[10](http://arxiv.org/abs/2309.09013)\]. This approach has been shown to be effective in scenarios where the data exhibits a clear structure that can be exploited through clustering \[[10](http://arxiv.org/abs/2309.09013)\].

In summary, the related works highlight the diversity of approaches to MIPS, ranging from graph-based and hashing-based methods to quantization and clustering-based techniques. Each approach offers unique advantages and trade-offs, and the choice of method often depends on the specific characteristics of the data and the application requirements.
