## Related Works

The field of radar-camera calibration has seen significant advancements in recent years, driven by the need for robust and accurate sensor fusion in autonomous systems. Early approaches relied on manual calibration procedures, which were labor-intensive and required controlled environments \[[Lei Cheng' 2023-09-02](http://arxiv.org/abs/2309.00787v2)\]. More recently, data-driven methods have emerged as a promising alternative, leveraging deep learning to automate the calibration process. These methods typically rely on either target-based or targetless calibration strategies, each with its own set of advantages and limitations.

Target-based calibration methods, such as those proposed in \[[Felix Fent' 2024-04-03](http://arxiv.org/abs/2404.03015v2)\], require the use of calibration targets to establish known correspondences between radar and camera data. While these methods can achieve high accuracy, they are limited by the need for specialized infrastructure and are not suitable for real-time or online calibration. In contrast, targetless methods, such as the one introduced in \[[Christoph Schöller' 2019-04-18](http://arxiv.org/abs/1904.08743v2)\], aim to calibrate the sensors without the need for external targets. These methods often rely on feature matching and geometric constraints to estimate the extrinsic parameters between the sensors. However, they can be sensitive to noise and require careful design to ensure robustness.

One notable approach in this area is the work by \[[Lei Cheng' 2023-07-28](http://arxiv.org/abs/2307.15264v1)\], which proposes an online targetless calibration method based on the common features of radar and camera data. This method leverages deep learning to extract common features from raw radar data and camera images, enabling the system to match identical objects from both data sources. The estimated extrinsic transformation matrix is then refined using RANSAC and Levenberg-Marquardt optimization. This approach demonstrates the feasibility of targetless calibration in real-world scenarios, although it may still face challenges in dynamic environments.

Another important contribution is the work by \[[Emmett Wise' 2022-11-03](http://arxiv.org/abs/2211.01871v4)\], which introduces a method for 3D radar-camera spatiotemporal calibration that does not require specialized infrastructure. This method leverages the ability of the radar unit to measure its own ego-velocity relative to a fixed external reference frame, enabling the system to calibrate in arbitrary environments. The approach is validated through both simulation and real-world experiments, showing promising results in terms of accuracy and robustness.

In addition to these methods, several works have focused on improving the accuracy and robustness of radar-camera calibration by incorporating advanced neural network architectures. For instance, \[[Taohua Zhou' 2022-08-25](http://arxiv.org/abs/2208.12079v2)\] proposes a method that uses a dual perspective representation to gather features from both frontal and bird's-eye views, reducing the impact of height uncertainty. This approach is complemented by a multi-modal cross-attention mechanism to find location correspondences through cross-modal matching, enhancing the overall calibration performance.

The work by \[[Ramin Nabati' 2021-07-11](http://arxiv.org/abs/2107.05150v1)\] further advances the field by introducing a noise-resistant matcher during the training phase, providing better supervision and improving the robustness of the matching mechanism against sparsity and height uncertainty. This approach is evaluated on the nuScenes dataset, demonstrating significant improvements over previous methods.
