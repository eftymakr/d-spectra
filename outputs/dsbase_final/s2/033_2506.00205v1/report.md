## Related Works

Rehearsal-based methods have emerged as a prominent approach in continual learning (CL) to mitigate catastrophic forgetting by storing and reusing past data during training \[[Lukas Balles' 2022-03-28](http://arxiv.org/abs/2203.14544v1)\]. These methods typically involve training on a subset of previously seen data alongside new data, a strategy known as concurrent rehearsal \[[Thang Doan' 2020-10-07](http://arxiv.org/abs/2010.04003v2)\]. However, recent studies have questioned whether this approach is always optimal, particularly in scenarios where tasks are dissimilar or where sequential revisiting of past tasks could offer greater benefits \[[Ronald Kemker' 2017-08-07](http://arxiv.org/abs/1708.02072v4)\].

The idea of sequential rehearsal, inspired by human learning strategies, has gained traction as a potential alternative to concurrent rehearsal. Human learning often involves revisiting previously learned tasks to reinforce memory and reduce forgetting \[[Vinay V. Ramasesh' 2020-07-14](http://arxiv.org/abs/2007.07400v1)\]. In the context of CL, sequential rehearsal refers to training new data first and then revisiting past data in a sequential manner \[[Rich Harang' 2023-06-16](http://arxiv.org/abs/2306.10181v1)\]. This approach has been explored in several works, with varying degrees of success. For example, \[[James Seale Smith' 2022-03-31](http://arxiv.org/abs/2203.17269v2)\] demonstrated that sequential rehearsal can be more effective in certain scenarios, particularly when tasks are less similar. Similarly, \[[Haruka Asanuma' 2021-05-16](http://arxiv.org/abs/2105.07385v1)\] found that sequential rehearsal outperformed concurrent rehearsal in tasks with high task similarity. However, these studies have not systematically compared the two strategies across a wide range of task similarity conditions or provided a theoretical justification for their effectiveness.

Theoretical analyses of rehearsal-based CL have been limited, with most studies focusing on empirical performance rather than underlying mechanisms \[[Prashant Bhat' 2025-05-28](http://arxiv.org/abs/2505.21942v1)\]. A recent work \[[Andrew Bai' 2024-02-12](http://arxiv.org/abs/2402.08096v3)\] introduced a theoretical framework for analyzing rehearsal-based CL in overparameterized linear models, comparing concurrent and sequential rehearsal strategies. This study showed that sequential rehearsal performs better when tasks are less similar, a finding that aligns with empirical observations \[[Wojciech Masarczyk' 2020-04-29](http://arxiv.org/abs/2004.14046v1)\]. Building on this, \[[Joan Serrà' 2018-01-04](http://arxiv.org/abs/1801.01423v3)\] proposed a hybrid rehearsal method that combines the strengths of both strategies: training similar tasks concurrently and revisiting dissimilar tasks sequentially. This approach was shown to outperform standard concurrent rehearsal in both theoretical analysis and empirical experiments \[[Daofeng Liu' 2023-03-06](http://arxiv.org/abs/2303.02954v1)\].

Several other works have explored variations of rehearsal-based methods. For instance, \[[Kemal Davaslioglu' 2024-10-14](http://arxiv.org/abs/2410.10521v1)\] introduced a method that leverages knowledge distillation to distill the cumulative knowledge of all previous tasks, while \[[Gido M. van de Ven' 2019-04-15](http://arxiv.org/abs/1904.07734v1)\] proposed a technique that uses a task pool to track the model's capabilities and distill knowledge from all tasks. These methods, while promising, have not been systematically compared against sequential and concurrent rehearsal strategies in the context of overparameterized models \[[Albin Soutif--Cormerais' 2023-08-20](http://arxiv.org/abs/2308.10328v3)\].

In summary, while concurrent rehearsal remains a widely used approach in CL, recent theoretical and empirical studies suggest that sequential rehearsal and hybrid strategies may offer superior performance in certain scenarios. These findings motivate further exploration into the theoretical foundations and practical implications of different rehearsal strategies in CL.
