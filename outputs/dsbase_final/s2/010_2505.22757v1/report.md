## Related Works

Multi-token prediction (MTP) has emerged as a promising pre-training objective for language models, offering potential improvements in downstream performance, inference speed, and training efficiency \[[Fabian Gloeckle' 2024-04-30](http://arxiv.org/abs/2404.19737v1)\]. Unlike traditional next-token prediction (NTP), which predicts one token at a time, MTP predicts multiple tokens simultaneously using multiple prediction heads \[[Fabian Gloeckle' 2024-04-30](http://arxiv.org/abs/2404.19737v1)\]. This approach has been shown to enhance the model's ability to capture long-range dependencies and improve the efficiency of inference \[[Xiaohao Liu' 2025-05-23](http://arxiv.org/abs/2505.17505v2)\]. However, prior work has demonstrated that smaller language models (SLMs) struggle with the MTP objective, often leading to suboptimal performance \[[Fabian Gloeckle' 2024-04-30](http://arxiv.org/abs/2404.19737v1)\]. To address this challenge, this paper proposes a curriculum learning strategy for MTP training, exploring two variants: a forward curriculum that gradually increases the complexity of the pre-training objective from NTP to MTP, and a reverse curriculum that does the opposite \[[Fabian Gloeckle' 2024-04-30](http://arxiv.org/abs/2404.19737v1)\].

Curriculum learning has been widely studied in the context of language models, with several works exploring its potential to improve training efficiency and model performance \[[Sanmit Narvekar' 2020-03-10](http://arxiv.org/abs/2003.04960v2)\]. For instance, \[[Kwangjun Ahn' 2025-03-24](http://arxiv.org/abs/2503.21801v1)\] investigated the use of curriculum learning in small code language models and found that a well-designed curriculum significantly improved the accuracy of small decoder-only code language models on the task of code execution. Similarly, \[[Somesh Mehra' 2025-02-13](http://arxiv.org/abs/2502.09419v1)\] explored curriculum learning for language modeling and evaluated its impact on the GLUE Benchmark, although they did not find compelling evidence that curriculum learning methods improved language model training. These findings highlight the importance of carefully designing curricula to align with the capabilities of the models being trained.

The proposed curriculum learning strategy for MTP training builds upon these insights, aiming to address the limitations of MTP for smaller models. The forward curriculum gradually introduces MTP as the pre-training objective, allowing SLMs to better leverage the MTP objective during pre-training and improve downstream NTP performance and generative output quality \[[Fabian Gloeckle' 2024-04-30](http://arxiv.org/abs/2404.19737v1)\]. In contrast, the reverse curriculum starts with MTP and gradually reduces the complexity, leading to stronger NTP performance and output quality but failing to provide any self-speculative decoding benefits \[[Fabian Gloeckle' 2024-04-30](http://arxiv.org/abs/2404.19737v1)\].

Several other works have explored curriculum learning in different contexts, such as reinforcement learning \[[Shikhar Srivastava' 2024-10-25](http://arxiv.org/abs/2410.19925v2)\], vision-language models \[[Marwa Naïr' 2024-07-14](http://arxiv.org/abs/2407.10194v1)\], and multimodal large language models \[[Francesco Foglino' 2019-01-31](http://arxiv.org/abs/1901.11478v3)\]. These studies have demonstrated the effectiveness of curriculum learning in improving model performance and training efficiency, further supporting the potential of curriculum learning for MTP training. Additionally, \[[Guangxin Su' 2024-10-15](http://arxiv.org/abs/2410.12096v1)\] proposed a framework for curriculum learning in reinforcement learning, showing that curriculum learning can be used to improve the initial performance, reduce the number of suboptimal actions during exploration, and discover better policies. These findings suggest that curriculum learning can be a valuable tool for improving the training of MTP models, particularly for smaller models that struggle with the MTP objective.

Additionally, \[[Suchir Salhan' 2024-10-30](http://arxiv.org/abs/2410.22886v2)\] explored the use of curriculum learning in the context of small-scale language models, finding that fine-grained acquisition-inspired curricula can outperform non-curriculum baselines and performance benefits of curricula strategies in SSLMs can be derived by specifying fine-grained language-specific curricula that precisely replicate language acquisition theories. This aligns with the forward curriculum approach, where gradual complexity increases are used to better align with the learning capabilities of smaller models.
