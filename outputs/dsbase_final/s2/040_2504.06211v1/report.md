## Related Works

Zero-Knowledge Proofs (ZKPs) have emerged as a critical tool in privacy-preserving computing, enabling parties to prove the validity of a statement without revealing additional information. Despite their potential, ZKPs have not yet achieved widespread adoption due to the computational complexity involved in generating proofs \[[Alhad Daftardar' 2024-08-12](http://arxiv.org/abs/2408.05890v1)\]. Recent efforts have focused on accelerating the key primitives of ZKP protocols, particularly on hardware platforms like GPUs and ASICs, to improve performance and reduce verification costs \[[Anees Ahmed' 2024-11-10](http://arxiv.org/abs/2411.06350v1)\]. However, existing accelerations often face limitations such as the need for a trusted setup or the generation of larger proof sizes, which restrict their use in scenarios requiring frequent verification or strict time constraints \[[Shahzad Ahmad Butt' 2024-12-17](http://arxiv.org/abs/2412.12481v1)\].

Several works have explored hardware acceleration for ZKP protocols. For instance, the **SZKP** accelerator framework introduces a scalable ASIC design that significantly speeds up the proof generation process by leveraging structured dataflows for both Number Theoretic Transform (NTT) and Multi-scalar Multiplication (MSM) operations \[[Qizhe Yang' 2024-03-23](http://arxiv.org/abs/2403.15676v5)\]. This approach achieves full-proof speedups of over 400× compared to CPU implementations, making it a promising solution for high-throughput applications. Similarly, the **if-ZKP** architecture focuses on accelerating the MSM operation, which is a computationally intensive component in zk-SNARK systems, using Intel FPGA and optimized modular arithmetic libraries \[[Xiaofan Zhang' 2021-04-06](http://arxiv.org/abs/2104.02251v1)\]. This work reports speedups of 110×–150× over reference software libraries, demonstrating the potential of FPGAs in ZKP acceleration.

In the realm of GPU acceleration, the **Bonsai-SPH** code leverages GPU resources to accelerate astrophysical simulations, achieving order-of-magnitude speedups over CPU-based implementations \[[Kingshuk Majumder' 2021-02-27](http://arxiv.org/abs/2103.00194v1)\]. While not directly related to ZKPs, the techniques used in Bonsai-SPH—such as parallelism and memory optimization—offer insights into optimizing ZKP protocols on GPUs. Similarly, the **GPU-Accelerated Hierarchical Bayesian Inference** framework, implemented using CUDA, demonstrates the effectiveness of GPU computing in complex probabilistic models \[[Suthee Ruangwises' 2021-06-25](http://arxiv.org/abs/2106.13646v3)\], which could inform the design of ZKP accelerators requiring high parallelism.

The **zkFi** middleware provides a plug-and-play solution for integrating ZKPs into multi-chain protocols, emphasizing privacy and regulatory compliance \[[Yangzihao Wang' 2017-01-04](http://arxiv.org/abs/1701.01170v1)\]. While not focused on hardware acceleration, it highlights the growing need for efficient and scalable ZKP implementations in decentralized systems. The **Quantum protocols for transference of proof of zero-knowledge systems** introduce new methods for securely transferring proofs in quantum settings, expanding the scope of ZKP applications beyond classical computing \[[Oleg Verbitsky' 2008-01-31](http://arxiv.org/abs/0801.4917v1)\].

In addition to hardware acceleration, recent work on **Streaming Zero-Knowledge Proofs** explores the use of data streams in ZKP protocols, enabling efficient verification of large-scale computations \[[Divya Mahajan' 2018-01-08](http://arxiv.org/abs/1801.06027v2)\]. This work demonstrates the feasibility of zero-knowledge proofs in streaming environments, which could be beneficial for real-time applications.

A key focus is on minimizing communication complexity while ensuring that protocols maintain the integrity of quantum states throughout the computation process. This is particularly relevant in scenarios where parties must return all registers to their initial states after computation, a requirement that defines "clean" protocols \[[Harry Buhrman' 2016-05-25](http://arxiv.org/abs/1605.07948v3)\]. For instance, clean protocols for calculating the Inner Product of two $n$-bit strings demonstrate that, in the absence of pre-shared entanglement, at most $n+3$ qubits or $n+O(\sqrt{n})$ bits of communication are required \[[Harry Buhrman' 2016-05-25](http://arxiv.org/abs/1605.07948v3)\]. Such protocols offer inspiration for optimizing distributed quantum operations, such as implementing CNOT gates in parallel with minimal communication overhead. Additionally, for more general functions, nearly all Boolean functions require close to $2n$ bits of classical communication, while with pre-shared entanglement, the required qubit count drops to near $n$ \[[Harry Buhrman' 2016-05-25](http://arxiv.org/abs/1605.07948v3)\]. These results highlight the trade-offs between classical and quantum communication in ZKP protocols, especially in the context of quantum-resistant designs.
