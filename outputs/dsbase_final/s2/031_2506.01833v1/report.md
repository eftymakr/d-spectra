## Related Works

Recent advances in pre-training paradigms have significantly influenced the development of models for DNA sequence analysis. Initially, unsupervised pre-training methods were widely adopted for DNA-related tasks, as they allow models to learn general representations from large volumes of raw sequence data \[[Daoan Zhang' 2023-07-11](http://arxiv.org/abs/2307.05628v3)\]. However, these approaches have limitations, as DNA sequences alone do not capture the full regulatory context of genomic functions, which are influenced by chromatin accessibility and other genomic profiles \[[Yikang Zhang' 2022-04-05](http://arxiv.org/abs/2204.02130v2)\]. This has led researchers to explore supervised pre-training strategies that incorporate additional biological signals, such as chromatin accessibility, to improve model performance \[[Renqi Chen' 2024-05-15](http://arxiv.org/abs/2405.09585v3)\].

Several studies have demonstrated the effectiveness of supervised pre-training for genomic tasks. For instance, \[[Michail Tarasiou' 2022-03-11](http://arxiv.org/abs/2203.06041v1)\] introduced a supervised approach for predicting chromatin accessibility, showing that incorporating such signals improves the accuracy of DNA sequence representation. Similarly, \[[Fatemah Alharthi' 2024-12-19](http://arxiv.org/abs/2412.14651v1)\] proposed a method that integrates chromatin state information during pre-training, leading to better performance in downstream tasks like gene expression prediction. These works highlight the importance of leveraging genomic profiles to enhance DNA representation learning.

The use of Mixture of Experts (MoE) architectures has also gained traction in the field of genomic modeling. MoE allows models to dynamically select the most relevant expert networks for specific tasks, improving efficiency and performance \[[Ray Coden Mercurius' 2024-02-13](http://arxiv.org/abs/2402.08698v2)\]. In the context of genomic data, \[[Jean-Marc Victor' 2002-11-09](http://arxiv.org/abs/cond-mat/0211177v1)\] applied MoE to predict gene expression levels, demonstrating that the model could adapt to different regulatory contexts. Another study \[[C. A. Brackley' 2016-07-22](http://arxiv.org/abs/1607.06640v1)\] extended this idea to multi-species genomic prediction, showing that MoE could effectively capture species-specific regulatory patterns. These findings align with our approach, where we leverage MoE to model the complex relationships between DNA sequences and genomic profiles across different species.

Additionally, the integration of multi-modal data has been explored to improve genomic prediction. For example, \[[Fatemah Alharthi' 2024-12-25](http://arxiv.org/abs/2412.18804v1)\] combined DNA sequences with epigenomic data to enhance the accuracy of gene regulation predictions. Similarly, \[[Nagur Shareef Shaik' 2024-07-28](http://arxiv.org/abs/2407.19385v1)\] proposed a framework that integrates chromatin accessibility and DNA methylation data, showing that such integration leads to more robust models. These studies support our hypothesis that incorporating genomic profiles during supervised pre-training can significantly enhance DNA representation learning.

Furthermore, \[[Alexandre Rosas' 2002-09-17](http://arxiv.org/abs/cond-mat/0209396v2)\] introduced a method for analyzing DNA sequences using multifractal properties, demonstrating that such analysis can reveal long-range order and structural characteristics of DNA. This work complements our approach by highlighting the importance of capturing structural and regulatory features of DNA sequences for improved representation learning.

In summary, while unsupervised pre-training has been widely used in DNA modeling, supervised approaches that incorporate genomic profiles have shown superior performance. Our work builds on these findings by introducing the SPACE framework, which leverages MoE to better capture the relationships between DNA sequences and genomic profiles across species.

A recent study introduced the Multi-modal Imaging Genomics Transformer (MIGTrans), which combines structural and functional MRI with genomic features to identify neuroanatomical and connectome abnormalities associated with SZ, achieving an accuracy of 86.05% \[[Nagur Shareef Shaik' 2024-07-28](http://arxiv.org/abs/2407.19385v1)\]. These examples underscore the growing trend of leveraging multi-modal data integration to uncover complex disease mechanisms and improve diagnostic outcomes.
