## Related Works

Recent advances in DNA pre-training have primarily focused on leveraging unsupervised learning paradigms to capture general patterns from DNA sequences \[[Lei Kang' 2025-04-12](http://arxiv.org/abs/2504.09265v2)\]. However, these approaches often fail to account for the regulatory context that governs DNA function, such as chromatin accessibility and other genomic profiles \[[Zewen Chi' 2022-04-20](http://arxiv.org/abs/2204.09179v3)\]. This limitation has motivated the exploration of supervised training strategies for genomic profile prediction, which can provide more informative and task-specific DNA representations \[[Yu-Chiao Chiu' 2018-05-20](http://arxiv.org/abs/1805.07702v1)\].

Several studies have investigated the use of mixture-of-experts (MoE) architectures to improve the modeling of complex biological data. For instance, ExpertFlow \[[Shentong Mo' 2021-10-11](http://arxiv.org/abs/2110.05231v2)\] introduces a lightweight MoE inference system that enhances expert utilization through predictive caching and token scheduling, reducing GPU memory usage by up to 93.72%. Similarly, Not All Experts are Equal \[[Pooja Kathail' 2024-11-17](http://arxiv.org/abs/2411.11158v2)\] proposes expert pruning and skipping techniques to improve deployment efficiency while maintaining model performance. These methods demonstrate the potential of MoE for reducing computational overhead and improving model efficiency.

In the context of genomic data, GraphMETRO \[[Jack Lanchantin' 2016-05-04](http://arxiv.org/abs/1605.01133v2)\] employs a MoE architecture to address complex distributional shifts in graph data, achieving state-of-the-art results on benchmark datasets. This work highlights the importance of modeling distributional shifts and aligning expert representations to ensure robust performance. Similarly, the Mixture of Virtual-Kernel Experts (MVKE) \[[Ananya Karthik' 2021-12-10](http://arxiv.org/abs/2112.05340v1)\] is designed for multi-objective user profile modeling, leveraging virtual-kernel experts to capture diverse user preferences and topics. These approaches underscore the versatility of MoE in handling heterogeneous data and capturing complex relationships.

The application of MoE to financial risk prediction has also been explored, with FinPT \[[Renqi Chen' 2024-05-15](http://arxiv.org/abs/2405.09585v3)\] introducing a novel approach that fine-tunes large foundation models using profile tuning. This method demonstrates the effectiveness of MoE in capturing nuanced patterns in financial data. Additionally, the MIGA framework \[[Byoungjip Kim' 2021-01-16](http://arxiv.org/abs/2101.06480v1)\] applies MoE with group aggregation to stock market prediction, dynamically switching between style-specific experts to improve prediction accuracy across different stock types.

In the field of genomic sequence analysis, the Normalized Conditional Compression Distance (NCCD) \[[Yang Liao' 2013-05-15](http://arxiv.org/abs/1305.3347v2)\] has been proposed to measure chromosomal distances between species, providing insights into evolutionary relationships. This work highlights the importance of compression-based methods for analyzing genomic data and understanding evolutionary dynamics.

The proposed SPACE model builds upon these advancements by integrating supervised training for genomic profile prediction with a MoE architecture that adapts to multi-species and multi-profile settings. This approach enables the model to better capture the relationships between DNA sequences across different species and genomic profiles, leading to more effective DNA representations.

Additionally, efforts have been made to mitigate representation collapse by estimating routing scores on a low-dimensional hypersphere, leading to more consistent and effective routing behavior \[[Zewen Chi' 2022-04-20](http://arxiv.org/abs/2204.09179v3)\]. These advancements highlight the ongoing evolution of MoE architectures, aiming to better capture complex relationships in data, such as those found in genomic profile prediction tasks \[[Lei Kang' 2025-04-12](http://arxiv.org/abs/2504.09265v2)\]\[[Zewen Chi' 2022-04-20](http://arxiv.org/abs/2204.09179v3)\].
