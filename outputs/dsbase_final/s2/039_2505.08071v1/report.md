Existing methods either apply de Bruijn graphs of single-sized k-mers to assemble the full set of transcripts, or conduct multiple runs of assembly, but still apply graphs of single-sized k-mers at each run. As such, Bermuda can deal with uneven expression levels and coverage not only among transcripts, but also within a single transcript. Extensive tests show that Bermuda outperforms popular de novo assemblers in reconstructing unevenly-expressed transcripts with longer length, better contiguity and lower redundancy.
