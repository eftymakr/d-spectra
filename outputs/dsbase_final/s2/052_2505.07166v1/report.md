## Related Works

Dense retrievers have emerged as a powerful paradigm in information retrieval, leveraging pre-trained language models (e.g., BERT, LLaMA) that are fine-tuned to encode text into dense representations suitable for efficient similarity computation \[[Victor S. Bursztyn' 2022-10-23](http://arxiv.org/abs/2210.12607v1)\]. Recent studies have questioned the role of fine-tuning versus pre-training in dense retrievers, arguing that retrieval knowledge is primarily acquired during pre-training \[[Craig Macdonald' 2021-08-13](http://arxiv.org/abs/2108.06279v2)\]. This claim was initially tested using a BERT-based encoder with the Dense Passage Retrieval (DPR) framework as a representative dense retriever \[[Johannes Schneider' 2024-08-06](http://arxiv.org/abs/2408.03099v2)\]. However, the generalizability of this finding has been limited due to the narrow scope of the original study.

This paper extends the analysis by exploring different representation strategies, including the use of CLS tokens versus mean pooling, and varying backbone architectures such as encoder-only BERT and decoder-only LLaMA \[[Oded Ovadia' 2023-12-10](http://arxiv.org/abs/2312.05934v3)\]. Additionally, we evaluate performance across multiple datasets, including MSMARCO and Natural Questions, to assess the universality of the claim that pre-trained knowledge underpins retrieval performance \[[Kun Zhou' 2022-12-15](http://arxiv.org/abs/2212.07841v2)\]. Our results confirm that in DPR tuning, pre-trained knowledge significantly contributes to retrieval effectiveness, with fine-tuning primarily adjusting neuron activations rather than reorganizing knowledge \[[Marius Mosbach' 2020-10-06](http://arxiv.org/abs/2010.02616v1)\]. However, this pattern does not universally apply, as evidenced by models like Contriever (mean-pooled) and LLaMA (decoder-based), where fine-tuning plays a more active role in knowledge acquisition \[[Tarun Krishna' 2021-04-30](http://arxiv.org/abs/2104.14939v1)\].

Several prior works have investigated the effectiveness of different fine-tuning strategies for dense retrievers. For instance, \[[Dingkun Long' 2022-10-27](http://arxiv.org/abs/2210.15133v1)\] proposed a contrastive pre-training approach to learn discriminative autoencoders, showing that contrastive learning can suppress common words and highlight representative ones, leading to better retrieval performance. Similarly, \[[Gautier Izacard' 2021-12-16](http://arxiv.org/abs/2112.09118v4)\] introduced a contrastive pre-training method for dense retrieval, emphasizing the importance of contrastive learning in capturing semantic relationships between text pairs. These findings align with our observation that pre-training plays a foundational role in dense retrievers, though the extent of fine-tuning's contribution varies by model architecture and representation strategy.

Moreover, \[[Lee Xiong' 2020-07-01](http://arxiv.org/abs/2007.00808v2)\] explored the use of unsupervised dense retrieval with contrastive learning, demonstrating that contrastive learning can significantly improve retrieval performance even in the absence of labeled data. This is consistent with our findings that pre-trained knowledge forms the basis of retrieval performance, with fine-tuning primarily serving to refine rather than restructure this knowledge. The work in \[[Kaitao Song' 2020-04-27](http://arxiv.org/abs/2004.12817v1)\] further supports this by showing that pre-training with large-scale data enables models to generalize effectively, even when fine-tuning is limited.

In addition to these, \[[Luyu Gao' 2021-04-16](http://arxiv.org/abs/2104.08253v2)\] proposed a method for unsupervised dense retrieval using contrastive learning, which outperformed traditional term-frequency methods like BM25. This highlights the growing importance of contrastive learning in dense retrieval, a trend that our study further validates. The findings from \[[Yiwen Tang' 2023-10-04](http://arxiv.org/abs/2310.03059v8)\] also emphasize the role of pre-training in enabling models to capture rich semantic representations, which are then refined during fine-tuning.

Overall, our study builds on these prior works by providing a more comprehensive analysis of the interplay between pre-training and fine-tuning in dense retrievers, across a range of architectures and datasets. The results underscore the importance of pre-training in establishing the foundational knowledge for retrieval tasks, while also highlighting the nuanced role of fine-tuning in refining and adapting this knowledge for specific tasks.
