## Related Works

The task of summarizing long-form narratives, such as books, movies, and TV scripts, has been extensively explored in recent years. Early approaches primarily relied on rule-based systems or statistical models, which struggled with capturing the intricate plotlines, character interactions, and thematic coherence inherent in such texts \[[Pinelopi Papalampidi' 2020-04-27](http://arxiv.org/abs/2004.12727v1)\]. More recently, deep learning models, particularly transformer-based architectures, have shown promise in handling long-form text summarization. However, these models often require extensive fine-tuning and struggle with maintaining coherence and fidelity in the generated summaries \[[Jerry Wei' 2024-03-27](http://arxiv.org/abs/2403.18802v4)\].

Several works have focused on improving the quality of long-form summaries by incorporating multi-agent frameworks. For instance, \[[Raphael Shu' 2024-12-06](http://arxiv.org/abs/2412.05449v1)\] introduces a multi-agent system that leverages the strengths of different LLMs to enhance the summarization process. This approach, however, is limited to specific domains and does not address the broader challenge of handling diverse narrative structures. Similarly, \[[Effi Levi' 2020-07-09](http://arxiv.org/abs/2007.04874v2)\] proposes a method for generating summaries of long-form texts by using a combination of reinforcement learning and human feedback, but it lacks a structured pipeline for processing complex narrative elements.

In the context of narrative summarization, \[[Jinhui Ye' 2025-04-03](http://arxiv.org/abs/2504.02259v3)\] presents a framework that uses a hierarchical approach to summarize long-form texts, focusing on the decomposition of the narrative into smaller, manageable segments. While this method improves the coherence of the generated summaries, it does not address the preprocessing challenges inherent in long-form narratives. The work in \[[F. Bredell' 2024-12-09](http://arxiv.org/abs/2412.06333v3)\] further explores the use of multi-agent systems for summarization, but it is primarily focused on short-form texts and does not extend to the complexities of long-form narratives.

The introduction of multi-agent frameworks for summarization has been a significant step forward. For example, \[[Haojie Pan' 2018-11-01](http://arxiv.org/abs/1811.00185v1)\] proposes a multi-agent system that uses a combination of LLMs to generate summaries, but it is not tailored for long-form narratives. The work in \[[Chairi Kiourt' 2018-07-07](http://arxiv.org/abs/1807.02648v1)\] introduces a structured summarization pipeline that optimizes chunk processing and controls output length, which is a key innovation in the field. However, it does not incorporate a preprocessing step to standardize narrative elements, which is crucial for maintaining coherence in the final summaries.

In addition to these efforts, \[[Woon Sang Cho' 2018-11-01](http://arxiv.org/abs/1811.00511v2)\] explores the use of multi-agent systems for summarizing long-form texts, but it focuses on the generation of summaries rather than the structured processing of narrative elements. The work in \[[Lifu Tu' 2024-11-24](http://arxiv.org/abs/2411.15993v2)\] introduces a method for summarizing long-form texts using a combination of reinforcement learning and human feedback, but it lacks the structured pipeline necessary for handling complex narrative structures.

The proposed NexusSum framework builds upon these prior works by introducing a structured, sequential pipeline that processes long-form text without requiring fine-tuning. This approach addresses the limitations of previous methods by incorporating a preprocessing step that standardizes narrative elements and a hierarchical summarization pipeline that optimizes chunk processing and controls output length. These innovations are critical for achieving high-quality summaries of long-form narratives.
