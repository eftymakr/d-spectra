## Related Works

Cross-domain Sequential Recommendation (CDSR) has emerged as a critical research direction in recommendation systems, aiming to leverage user behavior across multiple domains to improve recommendation accuracy in data-scarce scenarios \[[Jiangxia Cao' 2023-04-08](http://arxiv.org/abs/2304.03891v1)\]. Early approaches primarily focused on aligning user representations across domains, often relying on shared users or items to transfer knowledge \[[Lei Guo' 2022-06-16](http://arxiv.org/abs/2206.08050v1)\]. However, these methods face limitations in real-world applications where user and item overlaps are minimal \[[Wei Wang' 2025-03-26](http://arxiv.org/abs/2503.20232v1)\]. Recent studies have explored various strategies to address these challenges, including the use of graph neural networks \[[Wenchao Sun' 2019-10-06](http://arxiv.org/abs/1910.02448v4)\], contrastive learning \[[Lei Guo' 2021-05-07](http://arxiv.org/abs/2105.03300v1)\], and domain adaptation techniques \[[Jibang Wu' 2020-01-29](http://arxiv.org/abs/2002.00741v1)\]. These methods attempt to capture cross-domain relationships by either aligning representations or modeling domain-specific patterns.

One notable approach is the use of graph neural networks (GNNs) to model sequential interactions and cross-domain relationships \[[Wenchao Sun' 2019-10-06](http://arxiv.org/abs/1910.02448v4)\]. While GNNs have shown promise in capturing complex patterns, they often struggle with scalability and may not effectively handle the dynamic nature of sequential data \[[Yongjing Hao' 2023-10-21](http://arxiv.org/abs/2310.13925v1)\]. To address this, some works have introduced attention mechanisms to better capture the temporal dependencies and cross-domain interactions \[[Yixin Zhang' 2022-05-30](http://arxiv.org/abs/2205.14837v2)\]. However, these models typically require extensive training data and may not generalize well to unseen domains \[[Jung Hyun Ryu' 2023-07-05](http://arxiv.org/abs/2307.05476v1)\].

Another line of research focuses on contrastive learning to improve domain adaptation in sequential recommendation \[[Lei Guo' 2021-05-07](http://arxiv.org/abs/2105.03300v1)\]. Contrastive learning has been successfully applied in various domains, including image and text processing, to learn discriminative representations \[[Yifan Xu' 2021-04-21](http://arxiv.org/abs/2104.10376v1)\]. In the context of CDSR, contrastive learning helps align representations across domains by contrasting positive and negative samples \[[Junliang Yu' 2022-09-06](http://arxiv.org/abs/2209.02544v4)\]. However, these methods often rely on pre-defined data augmentation strategies and may not adapt well to diverse domain shifts \[[Ziqiang Cui' 2024-05-15](http://arxiv.org/abs/2405.09369v5)\].

The integration of large language models (LLMs) into recommendation systems has also gained attention \[[Dengcheng Yan' 2021-03-11](http://arxiv.org/abs/2103.06560v3)\]. LLMs offer powerful semantic understanding and reasoning capabilities, making them suitable for capturing complex user preferences \[[Jianxin Chang' 2021-06-27](http://arxiv.org/abs/2106.14226v2)\]. Some studies have explored using LLMs to generate item representations and improve recommendation accuracy \[[Jader Abreu' 2019-01-20](http://arxiv.org/abs/1901.06610v2)\]. However, the computational cost of LLMs and the need for extensive training data remain significant challenges \[[Gabriela Csurka' 2017-02-17](http://arxiv.org/abs/1702.05374v2)\].

To overcome these limitations, recent works have proposed lightweight models and efficient training strategies \[[Qi Cai' 2020-09-30](http://arxiv.org/abs/2009.14776v2)\]. For instance, knowledge distillation has been used to transfer knowledge from large models to smaller, more efficient ones \[[Shiqi Yang' 2022-06-07](http://arxiv.org/abs/2206.03600v2)\]. Additionally, prompt-based approaches have been introduced to enhance the interpretability and adaptability of LLMs in recommendation tasks \[[Indro Spinelli' 2019-05-06](http://arxiv.org/abs/1905.01907v2)\].

In summary, while existing methods have made significant progress in CDSR, there remains a need for more efficient and adaptable models that can effectively capture cross-domain relationships and handle the complexities of sequential data. The proposed LLM4CDSR model aims to address these challenges by leveraging the semantic and reasoning capabilities of LLMs \[[Hongbo Bo' 2021-04-23](http://arxiv.org/abs/2104.11641v1)\].
