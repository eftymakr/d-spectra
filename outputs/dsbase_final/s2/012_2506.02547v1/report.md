## Related Works

Event cameras, also known as neuromorphic sensors, have been widely studied for their ability to capture asynchronous brightness changes at the pixel level, enabling high temporal resolution . This unique property makes them particularly suitable for applications requiring fast motion tracking and high dynamic range, such as robotics and autonomous systems . However, the asynchronous nature of event data introduces challenges in processing, including high bandwidth and computational demands . Prior work has attempted to address these challenges through event downsampling techniques, which aim to reduce the volume of event data while preserving useful information for downstream tasks .

Most existing approaches to event downsampling rely on fixed heuristics or threshold-based strategies . For example, some methods use a fixed threshold to filter out events below a certain intensity or rate . While these methods are computationally efficient, they lack adaptability and may not perform well under varying scene conditions . Additionally, these approaches often assume a uniform distribution of event importance, which may not hold true in complex or dynamic environments .

A few studies have explored adaptive event downsampling techniques that adjust to scene characteristics . For instance, some methods use spatial and temporal filters to retain structurally important events . However, these approaches typically require prior knowledge of the scene or task-specific adaptation, limiting their applicability to new or unseen scenarios . Furthermore, many of these methods do not address the challenge of zero-shot event downsampling, where downsampled events must remain usable for models trained on the original event stream without task-specific adaptation .

In contrast, our proposed probabilistic framework, POLED, models event importance through an event-importance probability density function (ePDF), which can be arbitrarily defined and adapted to different applications . This approach operates in a purely online setting, estimating event importance on-the-fly from raw event streams, enabling scene-specific adaptation . Additionally, we introduce zero-shot event downsampling, where downsampled events must remain usable for models trained on the original event stream, without task-specific adaptation . Our contour-preserving ePDF prioritizes structurally important events, ensuring that critical information is retained even under event-budget constraints .

These contributions build upon and extend previous work in event-based vision, including methods for object detection , image interpolation , and surface normal estimation . By introducing a probabilistic framework for event importance modeling and zero-shot event downsampling, our work addresses key limitations of existing approaches and provides a more flexible and robust solution for event-based vision tasks.

Adaptive importance sampling techniques are also discussed, which aim to improve the efficiency of sampling by iteratively refining proposal distributions based on weighted moments, particularly useful in scenarios involving complex or non-closed-form distributions \[[Kaustav Chanda' 2025-04-16](http://arxiv.org/abs/2504.12515v2)\]. The quantum formalism for classical statistics is another area of interest, where probabilistic cellular automata are modeled as quantum systems with discrete time steps and real wave functions, enabling the computation of expectation values and the analysis of subsystem correlations \[[Alex Zihao Zhu' 2018-01-30](http://arxiv.org/abs/1801.10202v2)\]. These contributions collectively highlight the diverse applications and theoretical underpinnings of probabilistic modeling in event importance, spanning from computational science to machine learning and beyond.
