## Related Works

Long-tailed recognition remains a challenging problem in computer vision due to the skewed distribution of class frequencies, where a few dominant classes dominate the dataset while many minority classes are underrepresented \[[Shiyu Xuan' 2024-03-10](http://arxiv.org/abs/2403.06151v1)\]. Early approaches primarily relied on data reweighting and resampling techniques to address class imbalance \[[Zhisheng Zhong' 2022-03-22](http://arxiv.org/abs/2203.11506v2)\]. However, these methods often failed to capture the complex relationships between features and labels, leading to suboptimal performance \[[Min-Kook Suh' 2023-05-02](http://arxiv.org/abs/2305.01160v3)\]. More recently, contrastive learning has emerged as a promising approach to improve representation learning by leveraging the relationships between positive and negative samples \[[Jianggang Zhu' 2022-07-19](http://arxiv.org/abs/2207.09052v3)\].

Contrastive learning methods, such as Supervised Contrastive Learning (SCL), have shown effectiveness in learning discriminative features by pulling positive samples closer and pushing negative samples apart \[[Julien Denize' 2022-12-21](http://arxiv.org/abs/2212.11187v1)\]. However, these methods face challenges in long-tailed recognition due to the imbalanced distribution of data \[[Minki Jeong' 2022-11-22](http://arxiv.org/abs/2211.11938v1)\]. Specifically, gradient conflicts and imbalanced attraction and repulsion gradients between positive and negative pairs have been identified as key issues \[[Florian Graf' 2021-02-17](http://arxiv.org/abs/2102.08817v4)\]. These problems are exacerbated when the number of views increases, as the model struggles to maintain consistent learning signals \[[Sanglee Park' 2024-06-04](http://arxiv.org/abs/2406.02223v1)\].

To address these limitations, several works have proposed modifications to contrastive learning frameworks. For instance, Balanced Contrastive Learning (BCL) introduces class-averaging and class-complement strategies to balance the gradient contributions of negative classes and ensure uniform distribution of features \[[Qiong Chen' 2023-09-28](http://arxiv.org/abs/2309.16135v1)\]. Similarly, Targeted Supervised Contrastive Learning (TSC) aims to improve the uniformity of feature distributions by generating targets uniformly distributed on a hypersphere \[[Saurabh Sharma' 2020-04-07](http://arxiv.org/abs/2004.03706v2)\]. These approaches demonstrate that careful design of contrastive learning objectives can mitigate the adverse effects of class imbalance \[[Mahdiyar Molahasani' 2023-06-23](http://arxiv.org/abs/2306.13275v2)\].

Another line of research focuses on incorporating additional constraints to enhance the robustness of contrastive learning. For example, Decoupled Contrastive Learning (DSCL) decouples the training objective to separately optimize the relationships between positive and negative samples, thereby reducing the impact of imbalanced data \[[Qihao Zhao' 2024-07-17](http://arxiv.org/abs/2407.12568v2)\]. Additionally, Rebalanced Siamese Contrastive Mining (ResCom) introduces a class-balanced queue and hard positive/negative pair mining to improve the quality of contrastive pairs \[[Chongsheng Zhang' 2024-08-01](http://arxiv.org/abs/2408.00483v1)\]. These methods highlight the importance of carefully designing contrastive learning strategies to address the unique challenges of long-tailed recognition \[[Byoungjip Kim' 2021-01-16](http://arxiv.org/abs/2101.06480v1)\].

In summary, while contrastive learning has shown promise in improving representation learning, its application to long-tailed recognition requires careful consideration of the underlying challenges, such as gradient conflicts and imbalanced attraction/repulsion gradients \[[Zi-Hao Zhou' 2024-10-08](http://arxiv.org/abs/2410.06109v1)\]. Recent works have made significant strides in addressing these issues, paving the way for more robust and effective long-tailed recognition methods \[[Kaihua Tang' 2020-09-28](http://arxiv.org/abs/2009.12991v5)\].

Class-averaging and class-complement strategies have emerged as critical approaches to address the challenges of representation learning in imbalanced datasets. In long-tailed distributions, where a few majority classes dominate the data while minority classes are underrepresented, traditional classification models struggle to generalize effectively \[[Shiyu Xuan' 2024-03-10](http://arxiv.org/abs/2403.06151v1)\]. Recent studies highlight that supervised contrastive learning (SCL), although effective on balanced data, fails to form a regular simplex—a geometric configuration crucial for efficient representation learning—on long-tailed data \[[Shiyu Xuan' 2024-03-10](http://arxiv.org/abs/2403.06151v1)\]. This limitation leads to suboptimal performance in capturing the structure of minority classes. To mitigate this, researchers have proposed class-averaging, which balances the gradient contributions of negative classes, and class-complement, which ensures that all classes are represented in every mini-batch \[[Shiyu Xuan' 2024-03-10](http://arxiv.org/abs/2403.06151v1)\]. These strategies aim to improve the learning dynamics by reducing gradient conflicts and promoting more uniform attraction and repulsion between positive and negative pairs. By incorporating these techniques, the proposed balanced contrastive learning (BCL) framework achieves better performance on long-tailed benchmarks, demonstrating the importance of these strategies in enhancing model robustness and generalization \[[Shiyu Xuan' 2024-03-10](http://arxiv.org/abs/2403.06151v1)\].
