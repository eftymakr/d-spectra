## Related Works

Neural retrieval models have become a cornerstone of modern information retrieval systems, particularly in web search applications. These models typically rely on large amounts of labeled query-document pairs for training, which are often expensive to obtain \[[Fan Jiang' 2023-11-27](http://arxiv.org/abs/2311.15563v1)\]. Recent advances have focused on leveraging large language models (LLMs) to generate synthetic queries as a scalable alternative to labeled data \[[Kidist Amde Mekonnen' 2025-04-07](http://arxiv.org/abs/2504.05181v2)\]. However, synthetic queries often exhibit variability in quality, which can negatively impact downstream retrieval performance \[[Tetsuro Morimura' 2024-04-22](http://arxiv.org/abs/2404.13846v4)\].

Existing approaches typically filter out noisy query-document pairs using signals from an external re-ranker \[[Leonard Adolphs' 2022-10-21](http://arxiv.org/abs/2210.12084v1)\]. This method, while effective in some scenarios, introduces additional complexity and computational overhead. In contrast, our work proposes a framework that integrates ranking signals directly into the query generation process through Direct Preference Optimization (DPO) \[[Matthias Dorfer' 2017-05-19](http://arxiv.org/abs/1705.06979v2)\]. This approach aims to optimize the generation of high-quality synthetic queries that maximize downstream retrieval effectiveness.

DPO has been previously explored in the context of aligning large language models with human preferences \[[Gustavo Penha' 2021-11-25](http://arxiv.org/abs/2111.13057v3)\]. It leverages reinforcement learning to optimize model behavior based on preference signals, which can be derived from human feedback or other sources. Our framework extends this idea by embedding DPO within the query generation process, ensuring that the generated queries are directly aligned with the retrieval objectives \[[Andrea Bacciu' 2024-05-30](http://arxiv.org/abs/2405.19749v2)\]. This integration allows for a more coherent and efficient training process, reducing the reliance on external re-rankers \[[Dayu Yang' 2023-07-17](http://arxiv.org/abs/2307.08803v3)\].

Several studies have investigated the use of DPO in preference alignment tasks \[[Hoang Anh Just' 2024-07-19](http://arxiv.org/abs/2407.14477v4)\]. For example, \[[Xun Yang' 2020-07-06](http://arxiv.org/abs/2007.02503v1)\] demonstrated that DPO can significantly improve the alignment of LLMs with human preferences, achieving better performance than traditional reinforcement learning methods. Our work builds upon these findings by applying DPO to the domain of synthetic query generation, where the goal is to produce high-quality queries that enhance retrieval performance \[[Giorgio Morales' 2022-12-13](http://arxiv.org/abs/2212.06370v4)\].

In addition to DPO, other approaches have focused on improving the quality of synthetic queries through various means. For instance, \[[Sonal Sannigrahi' 2024-06-10](http://arxiv.org/abs/2406.06729v1)\] proposed a method that uses neural query rewriters to generate multiple query variations, which can be combined to improve retrieval performance. Similarly, \[[Gustavo Penha' 2023-01-13](http://arxiv.org/abs/2301.05508v1)\] explored the use of multi-task optimization to improve BERT-based query-by-document retrieval by leveraging additional document-level representation learning objectives. These approaches highlight the importance of query quality in retrieval tasks and the potential benefits of integrating ranking signals into the query generation process \[[Vijay Chandrasekhar' 2015-08-11](http://arxiv.org/abs/1508.02496v3)\].

In summary, while existing methods rely on external re-rankers to filter noisy synthetic queries, our approach integrates DPO directly into the query generation process, offering a more streamlined and effective solution \[[Thibault Formal' 2021-12-10](http://arxiv.org/abs/2112.05662v2)\]. This integration not only improves the quality of synthetic queries but also enhances downstream retrieval performance, as demonstrated by our experimental results on the MS MARCO benchmark \[[Siddhant Arora' 2019-04-11](http://arxiv.org/abs/1904.05737v1)\].
