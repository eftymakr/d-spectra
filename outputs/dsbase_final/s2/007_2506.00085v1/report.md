## Related Works

The field of aligning large language models (LLMs) with human values has seen significant advancements, particularly in the areas of steering model behavior and detecting refusal behaviors. Traditional methods often rely on predefined refusal templates detectable in output tokens or require manual analysis to identify refusal behaviors in LLMs, which can be both labor-intensive and limited in scope \[[Erich Schubert' 2021-07-08](http://arxiv.org/abs/2107.04071v1)\]. For instance, prior work has focused on detecting refusal tokens or specific patterns in output tokens that indicate a model's refusal to respond \[[Raz Lapid' 2023-09-04](http://arxiv.org/abs/2309.01446v4)\]. However, these methods are not robust in adversarial settings or when models are weakly aligned with human values \[[Yiqiao Chen' 2025-05-06](http://arxiv.org/abs/2505.03532v1)\].

Recent efforts have aimed to improve the alignment of LLMs by leveraging more sophisticated techniques. One such approach involves using reinforcement learning from human feedback (RLHF), which fine-tunes models based on human preferences \[[Zhifan Sun' 2024-03-14](http://arxiv.org/abs/2403.09832v1)\]. While RLHF has shown promise, it often requires extensive human annotations and is sensitive to the quality of the training data \[[Jannis Ahlert' 2024-07-10](http://arxiv.org/abs/2407.07530v1)\]. Additionally, methods that rely on direct preference optimization (DPO) have been proposed to reduce the dependency on human annotations by using pairwise comparisons of model outputs \[[Wenxuan Wang' 2023-10-02](http://arxiv.org/abs/2310.00905v2)\].

Another line of research has focused on improving the robustness of alignment techniques by incorporating more nuanced measures of model behavior. For example, some studies have explored the use of alignment loss functions that encourage models to align with human values while maintaining performance \[[Ian Smith' 2023-10-21](http://arxiv.org/abs/2310.13994v1)\]. These methods often involve training models to minimize a loss function that captures the difference between model outputs and human preferences \[[Harald Steck' 2024-03-08](http://arxiv.org/abs/2403.05440v1)\]. However, these approaches can be computationally intensive and may not generalize well across different alignment scenarios \[[Bradley Butcher' 2024-12-16](http://arxiv.org/abs/2412.11937v1)\].

In addition to these methods, there has been growing interest in developing automated frameworks for steering LLMs toward safer behavior. One such framework, called COSMIC, utilizes cosine similarity metrics to identify viable steering directions and target layers without relying on predefined templates or manual analysis \[[Zeping Yu' 2023-12-19](http://arxiv.org/abs/2312.12141v4)\]. This approach is particularly promising as it allows for more flexible and robust alignment of LLMs with human values \[[Satyajeet Sahoo' 2025-02-04](http://arxiv.org/abs/2502.02233v1)\]. The use of cosine similarity in alignment tasks has also been explored in the context of multi-modal data, where it enables the comparison of complex data types such as images and text \[[Avery Ma' 2023-11-30](http://arxiv.org/abs/2311.18495v2)\]. These findings highlight the potential of cosine similarity as a powerful tool for alignment tasks, particularly in scenarios where traditional methods fall short \[[Andrew Konya' 2023-12-06](http://arxiv.org/abs/2312.03893v1)\].

Furthermore, recent work has demonstrated that cosine similarity can be used to measure semantic alignment between models and human judgments, showing that even small language models can achieve human-level alignment \[[Peiyi Wang' 2023-09-05](http://arxiv.org/abs/2309.02144v1)\]. This has led to the development of novel methods such as Joint Generalized Cosine Similarity (JGCS), which extends cosine similarity to handle tasks involving an arbitrary number of vectors \[[Ahmed Abdelali' 2023-05-24](http://arxiv.org/abs/2305.14982v2)\]. These advancements underscore the importance of cosine similarity in the broader context of model alignment and evaluation.

Cosine similarity has been widely adopted in multi-modal alignment tasks due to its ability to measure the angular relationship between vectors, making it suitable for high-dimensional data such as neural embeddings and biological perturbational data \[[Erich Schubert' 2021-07-08](http://arxiv.org/abs/2107.04071v1)\]. However, its non-metric nature, which violates the triangle inequality, poses challenges for exact similarity search, prompting the development of approximation techniques like locality sensitive hashing \[[Erich Schubert' 2021-07-08](http://arxiv.org/abs/2107.04071v1)\]. In the context of neural network alignment with human perception, studies have shown that existing alignment metrics often exhibit low correlations with behavioral scores, suggesting that alignment is a multidimensional concept \[[Raz Lapid' 2023-09-04](http://arxiv.org/abs/2309.01446v4)\]. Furthermore, the statistical properties of cosine similarity, such as its variance, are influenced by the covariance structure of the data, with variance minimized when the covariance matrix has equal eigenvalues \[[Yiqiao Chen' 2025-05-06](http://arxiv.org/abs/2505.03532v1)\]. This has implications for improving the reliability of cosine similarity in biological data analysis. Additionally, while cosine similarity is commonly used in classification algorithms like K-Nearest Neighbors, its limitations in non-Euclidean spaces and the presence of variance and correlation in data have led to the proposal of modified cosine similarity metrics that adjust for these factors \[[Jannis Ahlert' 2024-07-10](http://arxiv.org/abs/2407.07530v1)\]. These findings highlight the need for careful consideration of cosine similarity's theoretical and practical constraints in various applications.
