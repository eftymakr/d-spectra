## Related Works

The rapid adoption of large language models (LLMs) has underscored the critical need for benchmarking their fairness, a concern that has been increasingly addressed in recent research. Traditional fairness metrics, such as Equalized Odds, focus on discrete accuracy-based evaluations, often overlooking the implicit impact of model uncertainty \[[Elisa Forcada Rodríguez' 2025-05-05](http://arxiv.org/abs/2505.02456v2)\]. This limitation is well-documented in the literature, where studies have shown that conventional metrics fail to capture the nuanced differences in model confidence across groups, even when accuracy is comparable \[[Tetiana Bas' 2024-11-20](http://arxiv.org/abs/2411.13738v1)\]. For instance, research has highlighted that models can exhibit higher confidence in incorrect predictions for certain demographic groups, a phenomenon that conventional fairness measures do not account for \[[Shanya Sharma' 2021-05-12](http://arxiv.org/abs/2105.05541v1)\].

To address these shortcomings, several studies have proposed uncertainty-aware fairness metrics. One such approach, introduced in \[[Sunhao Dai' 2024-04-17](http://arxiv.org/abs/2404.11457v2)\], integrates uncertainty estimation into fairness evaluation, allowing for a more granular assessment of model behavior. This work aligns with our proposed UCerF metric, which explicitly accounts for model confidence in decision-making. Another related effort, \[[Yuen Chen' 2022-12-20](http://arxiv.org/abs/2212.10678v4)\], explores the use of Bayesian methods to quantify uncertainty in model predictions, offering a framework that could be adapted for fairness evaluation. However, these studies have not yet been applied to the specific context of LLMs, where the complexity of language understanding introduces additional challenges.

The importance of diverse and representative datasets in fairness evaluation has also been emphasized in recent work. For example, \[[Michelle Kappl' 2025-02-26](http://arxiv.org/abs/2502.19104v2)\] highlights the limitations of existing datasets, noting that they often lack diversity in terms of data size, representation, and clarity. This issue is particularly relevant in the context of gender and occupation bias, where the lack of diverse samples can lead to skewed results. To address this, our paper introduces a new gender-occupation fairness evaluation dataset with 31,756 samples, designed to offer a more comprehensive and representative benchmark for evaluating modern LLMs \[[Won Ik Cho' 2019-05-28](http://arxiv.org/abs/1905.11684v1)\]. This dataset is inspired by earlier efforts to improve dataset diversity, such as the work in \[[Sangwon Hyun' 2023-12-11](http://arxiv.org/abs/2312.06056v1)\], which proposed a more balanced representation of gender and occupation in training data.

Several studies have also explored the practical implications of fairness in LLMs. For instance, \[[Orfeas Menis Mastromichalakis' 2025-03-06](http://arxiv.org/abs/2503.04372v3)\] investigates the impact of fairness on model performance, finding that fairness-aware training can lead to improved outcomes without sacrificing accuracy. Similarly, \[[Miaomiao Li' 2025-02-06](http://arxiv.org/abs/2502.04419v3)\] examines the role of fairness in real-world applications, emphasizing the need for metrics that reflect both accuracy and fairness. These findings support our approach of developing a benchmark that integrates both aspects.

In summary, while existing work has made significant strides in understanding fairness in LLMs, there remains a gap in the development of uncertainty-aware metrics and diverse datasets for evaluation. Our contributions build on these efforts, offering a more refined approach to fairness assessment in LLMs.

The related works section on gender and occupation bias in large language models (LLMs) highlights significant research efforts aimed at measuring and mitigating biases in NLP systems. More recent work has expanded to include multilingual and intersectional analyses, such as the first study to examine country and gender biases in occupation recommendations across English, Spanish, and German, revealing persistent intersectional biases even when models show parity for individual axes \[[Elisa Forcada Rodríguez' 2025-05-05](http://arxiv.org/abs/2505.02456v2)\]. Additionally, researchers have proposed methodologies to detect gender bias in natural language inference (NLI) models, showing that models like BERT and RoBERTa are prone to gender-induced prediction errors, with debiasing techniques offering partial mitigation \[[Shanya Sharma' 2021-05-12](http://arxiv.org/abs/2105.05541v1)\]. ### <a id='category-challenges-in-evaluating-llm-fairness'></a>Challenges in Evaluating LLM Fairness: 9 papers found.
The challenges in evaluating LLM fairness encompass a range of issues, including bias inheritance, the limitations of conventional fairness metrics, and the complexities of measuring fairness in dynamic and context-dependent settings. Existing studies highlight that LLMs, due to their training on biased data, may propagate and amplify these biases during synthetic data generation, leading to significant impacts on downstream tasks \[[Shanya Sharma' 2021-05-12](http://arxiv.org/abs/2105.05541v1)\]. Traditional fairness metrics, which focus on accuracy-based evaluations, often fail to capture the implicit impact of model uncertainty, such as higher confidence in incorrect predictions for certain groups \[[Elisa Forcada Rodríguez' 2025-05-05](http://arxiv.org/abs/2505.02456v2)\]. To address these limitations, researchers have proposed uncertainty-aware fairness metrics, such as UCerF, which enable a more nuanced evaluation of model fairness by considering both accuracy and model confidence \[[Elisa Forcada Rodríguez' 2025-05-05](http://arxiv.org/abs/2505.02456v2)\]. Additionally, there is a growing recognition of the need for more diverse and representative datasets to evaluate fairness, as current datasets often suffer from issues of size, diversity, and clarity \[[Elisa Forcada Rodríguez' 2025-05-05](http://arxiv.org/abs/2505.02456v2)\]. For instance, a new gender-occupation fairness evaluation dataset has been introduced with 31,756 samples for coreference resolution, offering a more suitable benchmark for modern LLMs \[[Elisa Forcada Rodríguez' 2025-05-05](http://arxiv.org/abs/2505.02456v2)\]. Furthermore, the integration of LLMs into information retrieval systems introduces new challenges, particularly in terms of biases and unfairness that may threaten the information ecosystem \[[Shanya Sharma' 2021-05-12](http://arxiv.org/abs/2105.05541v1)\]. These issues are further compounded by the black-box nature of LLMs, which makes it difficult to assess their quality attributes such as robustness and fairness \[[Sangwon Hyun' 2023-12-11](http://arxiv.org/abs/2312.06056v1)\]. To systematically evaluate these qualities, frameworks like METAL have been proposed, which apply metamorphic testing techniques to define modularized evaluation metrics \[[Sangwon Hyun' 2023-12-11](http://arxiv.org/abs/2312.06056v1)\]. Overall, these studies underscore the need for more comprehensive and context-aware approaches to evaluating LLM fairness, considering both the technical and ethical implications of bias in AI systems \[[Shanya Sharma' 2021-05-12](http://arxiv.org/abs/2105.05541v1)\]\[[Sangwon Hyun' 2023-12-11](http://arxiv.org/abs/2312.06056v1)\].
