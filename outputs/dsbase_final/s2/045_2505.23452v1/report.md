## Related Works

Fine-grained emotion classification in app reviews has become a critical area of research, particularly due to its applications in understanding user sentiment and improving product development. While sentiment analysis has been extensively studied, the ability to classify emotions at a finer level remains underexplored. This section reviews related works that address emotion classification in text, particularly in the context of app reviews, and highlights their contributions and limitations.

Several studies have focused on the challenges of fine-grained emotion classification in text. For instance, \[[Minxue Niu' 2024-12-10](http://arxiv.org/abs/2412.07906v1)\] presents a comprehensive study on the emotional sentiment of user reviews, analyzing over seven million reviews from the Apple AppStore. The study emphasizes the importance of emotional sentiment in software engineering and highlights the pitfalls of using sentiment analysis without considering the nuances of human emotions. This work sets the stage for more detailed emotion classification in app reviews.

In the context of app reviews, \[[Zhaochun Ren' 2024-06-04](http://arxiv.org/abs/2406.02642v4)\] explores the use of large language models (LLMs) for automating emotion annotation. The study evaluates the feasibility of using LLMs to reduce manual effort and assesses their agreement with human annotators. The findings indicate that while LLMs can significantly reduce manual effort, full automation remains challenging due to the complexity of emotional interpretation. This aligns with the findings of the current study, which also evaluates the effectiveness of LLMs in emotion classification.

The use of structured annotation frameworks for fine-grained emotion classification has also been explored. \[[Diogo Cortiz' 2021-08-17](http://arxiv.org/abs/2108.07638v2)\] introduces a structured annotation framework based on Plutchik's emotion taxonomy, which is adapted for app reviews. This framework provides clear guidelines for annotators and helps in defining the emotional content of reviews. The study emphasizes the importance of structured annotation in ensuring consistency and reliability in emotion classification.

Additionally, \[[Justin Xie' 2022-10-30](http://arxiv.org/abs/2212.03297v1)\] investigates the use of large language models for emotion classification in app reviews, highlighting the potential of LLMs in reducing manual effort. The study evaluates the performance of LLMs against human annotators and finds that while LLMs can maintain substantial agreement, they still face challenges in accurately capturing the nuances of emotional expressions.

The integration of multimodal data for emotion classification has also been explored. \[[Daniel Martens' 2017-03-07](http://arxiv.org/abs/1703.02256v1)\] presents a study on the use of multimodal data for emotion classification, highlighting the potential of combining textual and visual information to improve classification accuracy. This approach is particularly relevant in the context of app reviews, where both textual and visual elements can contribute to the emotional content.

Moreover, \[[Pankaj Gupta' 2019-09-13](http://arxiv.org/abs/1909.06162v1)\] discusses the use of large language models for emotion annotation in the context of affective computing. The study highlights the potential of LLMs to flag low-quality labels and reduce the workload of human annotators. This aligns with the current study's findings that LLMs can maintain substantial agreement with human annotators while significantly reducing manual effort.

The use of weakly supervised datasets for emotion classification has also been explored. \[[Alexandre Denis' 2013-09-11](http://arxiv.org/abs/1309.2853v1)\] presents a study on the creation of a lexical-based weakly supervised corpus for fine-grained emotion in Portuguese. The study evaluates the effectiveness of weakly supervised methods in low-resource environments, showing that lexical-based weak supervision can be a viable strategy for initial work in such settings.

In summary, the related works highlight the importance of fine-grained emotion classification in app reviews and the challenges associated with this task. The use of structured annotation frameworks, the evaluation of LLMs for automation, and the integration of multimodal data are key areas of research that contribute to the field of opinion mining and emotion analysis.
