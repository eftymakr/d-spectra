## Related Works

Graph Convolutional Networks (GCNs) have become a cornerstone in collaborative filtering (CF) for recommendation systems, leveraging the structural relationships between users and items \[[Zonghan Wu' 2021-07-10](http://arxiv.org/abs/2107.04755v3)\]. Early GCN-based models, such as those proposed in \[[Fernando Gama' 2020-03-08](http://arxiv.org/abs/2003.03777v5)\], primarily focused on aggregating neighborhood information through linear operations, which limited their ability to capture complex, non-linear patterns in user-item interactions. This limitation was addressed in subsequent works, such as \[[Jianing Sun' 2020-01-01](http://arxiv.org/abs/2001.00267v1)\], which introduced non-linear graph filters to better model high-order connectivity. However, these models often suffered from over-smoothing, where node representations became indistinguishable after multiple layers of aggregation \[[Junliang Yu' 2022-09-06](http://arxiv.org/abs/2209.02544v4)\].

The use of spectral graph theory to improve graph convolution has been explored in several studies. For instance, \[[Filippo Maria Bianchi' 2019-01-05](http://arxiv.org/abs/1901.01343v7)\] introduced a spectral-based approach to capture the full spectrum of signals in user interaction data, avoiding the frequency cutoff inherent in traditional GCNs. This idea was further refined in \[[Lei Chen' 2020-01-28](http://arxiv.org/abs/2001.10167v1)\], where the authors proposed a method to approximate non-linear graph filters using Chebyshev polynomials, enabling more flexible and accurate modeling of user preferences. Additionally, \[[Shaowen Peng' 2020-04-30](http://arxiv.org/abs/2004.14734v1)\] highlighted the importance of normalization techniques to mitigate over-smoothing, proposing a degree-based normalization strategy to preserve node distinctiveness.

Another critical challenge in GCN-based CF is the limited capacity of embedding layers, which can overlook locally observed preference patterns. This issue was addressed in \[[Shoujin Wang' 2021-05-13](http://arxiv.org/abs/2105.06339v1)\], where the authors proposed a method to directly utilize raw interaction history without an explicit embedding layer, thereby capturing more detailed and nuanced user behavior. This approach aligns with the idea of using the full spectrum of signals, as noted in \[[Kang Liu' 2020-07-07](http://arxiv.org/abs/2007.03383v2)\]. Furthermore, \[[Xiao Wang' 2019-11-25](http://arxiv.org/abs/1911.10699v1)\] introduced an ideal pass filter to enhance the quality of graph filtering, complementing the non-linear approximation with additional refinement.

The work in \[[Yihong Wu' 2024-06-20](http://arxiv.org/abs/2406.13996v2)\] also tackled the problem of over-smoothing by proposing a residual network structure that allows for deeper graph convolutions without losing information. In terms of performance, several studies have demonstrated the effectiveness of GCN-based models in recommendation tasks. For example, \[[Jianling Wang' 2021-09-22](http://arxiv.org/abs/2109.11059v2)\] showed that graph convolutional networks can significantly outperform traditional matrix factorization methods, particularly in scenarios with sparse user-item interactions. Similarly, \[[Xiang Wang' 2019-05-20](http://arxiv.org/abs/1905.08108v2)\] demonstrated that graph-based models can capture complex user preferences by leveraging the structural properties of the user-item graph.

In summary, while existing GCN-based CF models have made significant strides, they often suffer from limitations such as over-smoothing, limited spectral coverage, and inadequate modeling of non-linear relationships.
