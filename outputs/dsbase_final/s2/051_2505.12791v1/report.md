## Related Works

Federated Learning (FL) has emerged as a promising paradigm for training machine learning models while preserving data privacy across decentralized data sources \[[Yang Zhao' 2023-10-30](http://arxiv.org/abs/2310.19218v5)\]. Recent advancements in FL have led to the development of federated unlearning techniques, which aim to remove the influence of specific data points from the global model, aligning with the "right to be forgotten" principle \[[Hyejun Jeong' 2024-03-04](http://arxiv.org/abs/2403.02437v3)\]. This section reviews related works on federated unlearning and evaluation practices, focusing on methods and metrics that are relevant to the study of Federated Online Learning to Rank (FOLTR).

Several approaches have been proposed to address the challenge of unlearning in FL. One notable method is **Forgettable Federated Linear Learning**, which introduces a framework for unlearning by linearly approximating deep neural networks and enabling the removal of a target client's influence without retraining the entire system \[[Ziyao Liu' 2023-10-31](http://arxiv.org/abs/2310.20448v4)\]. This approach is particularly effective in scenarios where the server needs to maintain model performance while ensuring privacy and efficiency.

Another significant contribution is the introduction of **Efficient Federated Unlearning under Plausible Deniability**, which allows the FL server to plausibly deny a client's participation in the training process \[[Yann Fraboni' 2022-11-21](http://arxiv.org/abs/2211.11656v5)\]. This method enhances privacy by generating a "Proof-of-Deniability" that ensures the server can deny client involvement without compromising model accuracy. The approach also perturbs client updates to prevent inference by honest but curious servers, achieving differential privacy after a certain number of communication rounds.

The concept of **label unlearning in Vertical Federated Learning (VFL)** has also gained attention, with methods such as **Towards Privacy-Guaranteed Label Unlearning in VFL** proposing a technique that generates synthetic embeddings to facilitate label forgetting while maintaining model performance \[[Hyejun Jeong' 2024-03-04](http://arxiv.org/abs/2403.02437v3)\]. This method is particularly relevant in scenarios where labels are sensitive information and must be removed from the model without affecting the overall utility.

In addition to unlearning strategies, there has been growing interest in evaluating the effectiveness of unlearning methods. **FedUnlearn** \[[Nicolò Romandini' 2024-01-10](http://arxiv.org/abs/2401.05146v3)\] and **Federated Unlearning with Certified Data Unlearning** \[[Ziyao Liu' 2023-10-31](http://arxiv.org/abs/2310.20448v4)\] have proposed evaluation practices that measure the success of unlearning by comparing the performance of the model before and after the removal of specific data points. These evaluations often rely on multiple metrics to ensure a comprehensive assessment of unlearning effectiveness.

The **Federated Semi-Supervised Learning with Inter-Client Consistency & Disjoint Learning**  has also contributed to the field by addressing the challenge of unlearning in scenarios where data is partially labeled or completely unlabeled. This work introduces a method called **FedMatch**, which combines federated learning with semi-supervised learning techniques to improve the robustness of unlearning in heterogeneous data settings.

The **Federated Unlearning: How to Efficiently Erase a Client in FL?** paper proposes a method where local unlearning is performed at the client to be erased, and the locally unlearned model is used as the initialization for a few rounds of federated learning to obtain the unlearned global model \[[Anisa Halimi' 2022-07-12](http://arxiv.org/abs/2207.05521v3)\]. This approach avoids the need for global access to training data or storage of parameter updates, making it more efficient.

In summary, the field of federated unlearning is rapidly evolving, with various methods and evaluation practices being developed to address the challenges of data privacy and model performance. The study presented in this paper builds upon these contributions by systematically analyzing the effectiveness of unlearning strategies in the context of FOLTR, using adapted and newly proposed evaluation metrics to provide a more comprehensive assessment of unlearning success.
