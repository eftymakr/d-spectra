## Related Works

The calibration of predictive models has garnered significant attention in recent years due to its critical role in ensuring reliable and interpretable predictions. While ranking models primarily focus on modeling the relative order of predictions, they often neglect the importance of the accuracy of absolute values, which is essential for downstream tasks such as decision-making and risk assessment \[[Wonbin Kweon' 2021-12-09](http://arxiv.org/abs/2112.07428v2)\]. To address this, existing calibration approaches typically employ predefined transformation functions with order-preserving properties to adjust the original predictions \[[Antoine Wehenkel' 2019-08-14](http://arxiv.org/abs/1908.05164v3)\]. However, these functions are often limited in their expressiveness and flexibility, as they adhere to fixed forms such as piece-wise linear functions, which can hinder their effectiveness in complex calibration scenarios \[[Xiang-Rong Sheng' 2022-08-12](http://arxiv.org/abs/2208.06164v2)\].

Several studies have explored alternative calibration methods that offer greater flexibility. For instance, \[[Harish G. Ramaswamy' 2014-08-12](http://arxiv.org/abs/1408.2764v2)\] proposed the use of neural networks for calibration, leveraging their ability to learn complex, non-linear mappings. However, these early approaches often lacked the necessary constraints to ensure monotonicity, which is crucial for preserving the relative order of predictions \[[Ruslan Vasilev' 2023-03-19](http://arxiv.org/abs/2303.10761v1)\]. More recently, \[[Mauro Brunato' 2015-09-01](http://arxiv.org/abs/1509.00174v1)\] introduced the concept of using monotonic neural networks for calibration, emphasizing the importance of maintaining the order of predictions while allowing for flexible modeling. This idea was further expanded in \[[Emanuele Fabbiani' 2018-09-11](http://arxiv.org/abs/1809.03941v6)\], where the authors proposed the use of unconstrained monotonic neural networks (UMNNs) to learn arbitrary monotonic functions, thereby enhancing the flexibility and expressiveness of the calibration process.

The effectiveness of monotonic neural networks in calibration has been further validated in \[[Matthias Minderer' 2021-06-15](http://arxiv.org/abs/2106.07998v2)\], where the authors demonstrated that UMNNs can significantly improve the accuracy of calibrated predictions while maintaining the necessary order constraints. Additionally, \[[L. Decin' 2001-05-03](http://arxiv.org/abs/astro-ph/0105045v1)\] explored the application of UMNNs in the context of distributional reinforcement learning, highlighting their ability to model different representations of the random return distribution. These findings underscore the potential of UMNNs as a powerful tool for calibration tasks.

To further enhance the calibration process, \[[Adrien LeCoz' 2024-11-05](http://arxiv.org/abs/2411.02988v2)\] introduced a novel loss function called Smooth Calibration Loss (SCLoss), which aims to fulfill a necessary condition for achieving the ideal calibration state. This loss function has been shown to improve the calibration performance of neural networks while maintaining the required monotonicity constraints \[[Fady Medhat' 2018-02-15](http://arxiv.org/abs/1802.05792v2)\].

In summary, the related works highlight the importance of calibration in predictive models and the limitations of traditional approaches. The use of monotonic neural networks and novel loss functions represents a promising direction for improving the accuracy and reliability of calibrated predictions.
