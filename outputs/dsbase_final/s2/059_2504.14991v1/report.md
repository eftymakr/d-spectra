## Related Works

Fairness in re-ranking has been a focal point in recent research, especially in recommendation systems and other decision-making processes where algorithmic bias can lead to discriminatory outcomes \[[Chen Xu' 2024-04-27](http://arxiv.org/abs/2404.17826v1)\]. Prior work has primarily focused on balancing ranking accuracy with fairness, often using metrics such as statistical parity, equal opportunity, and equalized odds \[[Weiwen Liu' 2018-09-09](http://arxiv.org/abs/1809.02921v2)\]. However, these approaches typically treat fairness and accuracy as independent objectives, failing to capture the nuanced interplay between them \[[Nasim Sonboli' 2020-05-21](http://arxiv.org/abs/2005.12974v1)\].

A key challenge in fair re-ranking is the trade-off between accuracy and fairness, which has been likened to economic concepts such as elasticity and tax transfer \[[Chen Karako' 2018-09-10](http://arxiv.org/abs/1809.03577v1)\]. Specifically, the impact of fairness constraints on ranking accuracy can be understood through the lens of utility elasticity, where changes in fairness requirements affect the distribution of item scores and, consequently, user satisfaction \[[Hua Tang' 2023-10-19](http://arxiv.org/abs/2310.12785v1)\]. This insight highlights the limitations of current evaluation frameworks, which often rely on a single fairness metric and neglect the broader implications of fairness-accuracy trade-offs \[[Sanghamitra Dutta' 2019-10-17](http://arxiv.org/abs/1910.07870v2)\].

Several studies have proposed fairness-aware re-ranking algorithms that incorporate fairness constraints during the ranking process. For example, \[[Mohammadmehdi Naghiaei' 2022-04-17](http://arxiv.org/abs/2204.08085v1)\] introduces a re-ranking approach that balances accuracy and provider-side fairness by learning individual preferences across multiple fairness dimensions. Similarly, \[[Kit T. Rodolfa' 2020-12-05](http://arxiv.org/abs/2012.02972v3)\] presents a method that uses item-level tax policies to redistribute ranking slots while maintaining a balance between accuracy and fairness. These approaches, however, often lack a systematic framework for evaluating the trade-offs between fairness and accuracy across different elasticity levels \[[Joon Sik Kim' 2020-04-07](http://arxiv.org/abs/2004.03424v3)\].

To address these limitations, this work introduces the Elastic Fairness Curve (EF-Curve), an evaluation framework that enables a comparative analysis of algorithm performance under varying elasticity levels \[[Xuran Li' 2022-05-18](http://arxiv.org/abs/2205.08704v2)\]. This framework builds on the economic concept of elasticity, which has been used to model the impact of fairness constraints on ranking accuracy \[[Xiaopeng Ye' 2024-05-25](http://arxiv.org/abs/2405.16120v2)\]. Additionally, we propose ElasticRank, a fair re-ranking algorithm that leverages elasticity calculations to adjust inter-item distances within a curved space, thereby improving both fairness and accuracy \[[Lorenzo Porcaro' 2023-05-16](http://arxiv.org/abs/2305.09319v1)\].

Existing work on fairness-aware recommendation systems has primarily focused on either user-side or item-side fairness, often at the expense of the other \[[Suyun Liu' 2020-08-03](http://arxiv.org/abs/2008.01132v3)\]. For instance, \[[Sebastian Zezulka' 2023-10-12](http://arxiv.org/abs/2310.08349v3)\] emphasizes provider-side fairness by ensuring that all providers are treated equitably, while \[[Drago Plecko' 2024-05-24](http://arxiv.org/abs/2405.15443v2)\] focuses on user-side fairness by incorporating personalized preferences. These approaches, however, do not account for the dynamic interplay between fairness and accuracy in a unified framework \[[Rashida Hakim' 2024-08-19](http://arxiv.org/abs/2408.10002v1)\].

In summary, while prior work has made significant strides in addressing fairness in re-ranking, there remains a gap in systematically evaluating the trade-offs between fairness and accuracy using economic principles such as elasticity.
