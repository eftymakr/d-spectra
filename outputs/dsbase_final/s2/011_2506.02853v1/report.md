## Related Works

The task of 3D human pose estimation has been extensively studied, with a focus on capturing spatial and temporal dependencies among body joints. Early methods relied on 2D pose estimation followed by 3D lifting, often using kinematic constraints or multi-view geometry \[[Ti Wang' 2023-04-27](http://arxiv.org/abs/2304.14045v1)\]. More recently, deep learning approaches have gained prominence, leveraging convolutional neural networks (CNNs) and recurrent networks (RNNs) to model temporal dynamics \[[Wenbo Hu' 2021-07-16](http://arxiv.org/abs/2107.07797v2)\]. However, these methods often struggle with long-range dependencies between joints, especially in complex poses \[[Qiang Nie' 2020-07-14](http://arxiv.org/abs/2007.07053v2)\]. 

Several works have explored the use of graph-based models to capture spatial relationships between body parts. For instance, \[[Wen-Li Wei' 2022-03-16](http://arxiv.org/abs/2203.08534v1)\] introduced a graph convolutional network (GCN) to model the human skeleton as a graph, enabling the learning of spatial relationships. While effective, these models typically focus on local interactions and lack the ability to capture long-range dependencies across the entire body \[[Jianbin Jiao' 2024-01-30](http://arxiv.org/abs/2401.16700v2)\]. 

Transformers have emerged as a promising alternative due to their ability to model long-range dependencies through self-attention mechanisms \[[Junfa Liu' 2020-03-11](http://arxiv.org/abs/2003.14179v4)\]. However, applying transformers directly to 3D human pose estimation has been challenging due to computational complexity and the need for efficient attention mechanisms \[[Tianhan Xu' 2021-03-30](http://arxiv.org/abs/2103.16385v1)\]. Some works have attempted to integrate transformers with CNNs or GCNs, but they often suffer from high computational costs or limited performance on benchmark datasets \[[Ziniu Wan' 2021-09-06](http://arxiv.org/abs/2109.02303v1)\]. 

To address these limitations, \[[Zaedul Islam' 2023-07-29](http://arxiv.org/abs/2307.16074v2)\] proposed a multi-stage framework that combines spatial and temporal modules to capture both intra-image and inter-view relationships. This approach leverages the self-attention mechanism to reduce interference from non-human body parts and improve computational efficiency. Similarly, \[[Zhe Zhang' 2020-03-25](http://arxiv.org/abs/2003.11163v2)\] introduced a 3D CNN-based approach that models the 3D spatial relationships between joints, achieving real-time performance. 

Another line of research focuses on improving the robustness of 3D human pose estimation in challenging scenarios such as occlusions and viewpoint changes \[[Keze Wang' 2019-01-12](http://arxiv.org/abs/1901.03798v2)\]. For example, \[[Wenhao Li' 2022-06-13](http://arxiv.org/abs/2206.06420v5)\] proposed a method that uses a single depth map to estimate 3D hand and human pose by modeling the problem as a voxel-to-voxel prediction. This approach effectively handles perspective distortion and improves accuracy compared to traditional 2D-to-3D regression methods. 

Recent advances have also focused on integrating multiple modalities, such as RGB images and depth maps, to enhance the robustness of 3D human pose estimation \[[Haoyu Ma' 2021-10-18](http://arxiv.org/abs/2110.09554v3)\]. For instance, \[[Gyeongsik Moon' 2017-11-20](http://arxiv.org/abs/1711.07399v3)\] proposed a method that combines multi-view RGB videos with corresponding pointclouds to generate accurate ground truth annotations, improving the performance of 3D human pose estimation in outdoor scenes. 

In addition, some works have explored the use of graph attention mechanisms to capture both local and global dependencies in human pose estimation \[[Dushyant Mehta' 2016-11-29](http://arxiv.org/abs/1611.09813v5)\]. For example, \[[Luanxuan Hou' 2020-03-17](http://arxiv.org/abs/2003.07516v1)\] introduced a graph attention spatio-temporal convolutional network (GAST-Net) that models local and global spatial information through attention mechanisms, achieving competitive performance on benchmark datasets. 

Overall, while significant progress has been made in 3D human pose estimation, there remains a need for more efficient and robust methods that can effectively capture long-range dependencies across the entire body \[[Faisal Mehmood' 2024-11-10](http://arxiv.org/abs/2411.06553v1)\].
