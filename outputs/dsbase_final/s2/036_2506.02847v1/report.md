## Related Works

The deployment of large language models (LLMs) on edge devices has become a critical area of research due to the increasing demand for real-time, privacy-preserving AI services. However, the resource constraints of edge devices—such as limited storage, power, and computational capabilities—pose significant challenges to deploying LLMs, particularly in terms of maintaining both performance and energy efficiency \[[Erik Johannes Husom' 2025-04-04](http://arxiv.org/abs/2504.03360v1)\]. Several approaches have been proposed to address these challenges, focusing on algorithmic optimizations, hardware acceleration, and hybrid solutions that combine both \[[Yanbiao Liang' 2025-04-07](http://arxiv.org/abs/2505.03745v1)\]\[[Mozhgan Navardi' 2025-02-19](http://arxiv.org/abs/2502.15816v1)\].

Early efforts in deploying LLMs on edge devices primarily focused on reducing model size through pruning and quantization techniques. For instance, \[[Yu Zhang' 2024-11-25](http://arxiv.org/abs/2411.16158v1)\] introduced a method for task-agnostic and task-specific expert pruning in MoE LLMs, aiming to improve deployment efficiency while maintaining performance. Similarly, \[[Yuhao Ji' 2024-07-16](http://arxiv.org/abs/2407.12070v1)\] proposed a visual token pruning method, PruneVid, to enhance the efficiency of video LLMs by reducing redundancy in visual data. These methods, however, often require significant computational overhead and may not be suitable for real-time applications \[[Shengyuan Ye' 2025-04-11](http://arxiv.org/abs/2504.08242v1)\].

To further optimize LLM deployment, researchers have explored algorithm-hardware co-design strategies. For example, \[[Xiao Yan' 2025-03-10](http://arxiv.org/abs/2504.00002v1)\] introduced a framework for quantizing weights and key/value (KV) cache in LLMs, aiming to reduce memory consumption while maintaining performance. This approach, known as WKVQuant, leverages two-dimensional quantization strategies and cross-block reconstruction regularization to achieve a balance between accuracy and efficiency \[[Akshat Ramachandran' 2024-03-08](http://arxiv.org/abs/2403.05465v2)\]. Additionally, \[[Jianyu Wei' 2024-06-25](http://arxiv.org/abs/2407.00088v2)\] proposed a method for low-rank pruning and factorization of LLMs, demonstrating that such techniques can significantly reduce model size without compromising performance \[[Moshe Kimhi' 2022-05-30](http://arxiv.org/abs/2205.15437v2)\].

Another line of research focuses on the integration of LLMs with edge computing platforms to enable efficient inference. \[[Liangzhen Lai' 2018-06-20](http://arxiv.org/abs/1806.07846v1)\] developed a framework for deploying LLMs on resource-constrained devices by combining parameter-efficient fine-tuning (PEFT) with structured pruning, resulting in improved inference efficiency and accuracy. This work, FedSpine, demonstrates the potential of hybrid approaches that combine both algorithmic and hardware-level optimizations \[[Cong Hao' 2020-10-14](http://arxiv.org/abs/2010.07185v2)\].

Moreover, \[[Yao Lu' 2024-01-22](http://arxiv.org/abs/2401.12350v1)\] introduced a novel approach for efficient deployment of LLMs on edge devices by leveraging activation sparsity, a technique that reduces memory and computational demands while maintaining high accuracy. This method, known as Activation Sparsity Opportunities, has shown promising results in reducing the energy consumption and latency of LLM inference on edge devices \[[Cong Hao' 2021-03-25](http://arxiv.org/abs/2103.15750v2)\].

In addition to these algorithmic and hardware-based approaches, several studies have explored the use of lightweight models and specialized accelerators for LLM deployment. For example, \[[Ning Li' 2025-02-12](http://arxiv.org/abs/2502.08381v1)\] proposed a framework for deploying LLMs on edge devices by combining lightweight models with efficient inference techniques, achieving significant improvements in both speed and energy efficiency \[[Qingcheng Xiao' 2021-05-04](http://arxiv.org/abs/2105.01585v1)\].

### <a id='category-efficient-inference-optimization-strategies'></a>Efficient Inference Optimization Strategies: 2 papers found.
Efficient inference optimization strategies for large language models (LLMs) on edge devices have been a focal point of recent research, addressing the challenges of limited computational resources and energy constraints \[[Erik Johannes Husom' 2025-04-04](http://arxiv.org/abs/2504.03360v1)\]. T-MAC addresses this by introducing a lookup table (LUT)-based method that directly supports mpGEMM without dequantization, enhancing computational efficiency and reducing energy consumption \[[Shengyuan Ye' 2025-04-11](http://arxiv.org/abs/2504.08242v1)\].
