## Related Works

Fund allocation in financial domains has been extensively studied, with a growing emphasis on integrating forecasting with optimization to mitigate the inherent uncertainties in time series predictions. Early approaches often relied on naive prediction-only strategies or predict-then-optimize (PnO) frameworks, which suffer from goal mismatch between forecasting and allocation tasks \[[Parley Ruogu Yang' 2021-10-21](http://arxiv.org/abs/2110.11156v3)\]. These methods typically assume that accurate forecasts can be used directly in optimization, ignoring the potential for forecast errors to propagate into suboptimal allocations \[[Dionysios S. Kalogerias' 2019-12-06](http://arxiv.org/abs/1912.02933v1)\]. More recently, researchers have proposed frameworks that explicitly account for forecasting uncertainty during the allocation process, aiming to improve robustness and performance \[[Young-Chae Hong' 2025-02-25](http://arxiv.org/abs/2502.18410v2)\].

Several studies have explored the integration of time series forecasting with financial decision-making. For example, \[[Yanlong Wang' 2025-03-10](http://arxiv.org/abs/2503.06928v2)\] introduced a probabilistic forecasting framework that incorporates uncertainty quantification, which is crucial for risk-aware allocation. This work laid the foundation for later developments that explicitly model forecast uncertainty during the optimization phase. Similarly, \[[Adèle Gouttes' 2021-07-08](http://arxiv.org/abs/2107.03743v1)\] proposed a risk-aware framework for financial asset allocation, emphasizing the importance of aligning the forecasting and optimization objectives to avoid suboptimal outcomes.

The introduction of state-of-the-art (SOTA) time series forecasting models has further complicated the allocation process, as these models often introduce additional uncertainty into the predicted results \[[Md Kowsher' 2024-10-15](http://arxiv.org/abs/2410.11674v2)\]. To address this, \[[Christian Donner' 2023-11-22](http://arxiv.org/abs/2311.13247v2)\] proposed a framework that integrates forecasting and optimization in an end-to-end manner, ensuring that the allocation process is aware of the uncertainty in the forecasts. This approach aligns with the core principles of the Risk-aware Time-Series Predict-and-Allocate (RTS-PnO) framework introduced in this paper, which aims to eliminate the goal mismatch between forecasting and allocation \[[Xing Tang' 2025-03-05](http://arxiv.org/abs/2503.03165v1)\].

A key challenge in financial allocation is the need for models that are agnostic to the underlying forecasting method, as different models may yield varying levels of accuracy and uncertainty \[[Ali Lashgari' 2023-04-27](http://arxiv.org/abs/2304.14544v1)\]. \[[Arun Verma' 2021-04-12](http://arxiv.org/abs/2104.05781v1)\] addressed this by developing a framework that allows for the use of any forecasting model while maintaining the alignment between forecasting and optimization objectives. This aligns with the third feature of RTS-PnO, which emphasizes model agnosticism \[[Marco Bianchetti' 2025-03-06](http://arxiv.org/abs/2503.04662v1)\].

In addition to these works, \[[Peru Muniain' 2018-10-19](http://arxiv.org/abs/1810.08418v2)\] explored the use of Monte Carlo methods for uncertainty estimation in financial forecasting, which is a critical component of the adaptive forecasting uncertainty calibration in RTS-PnO. \[[Zhen Zeng' 2023-04-11](http://arxiv.org/abs/2304.04912v1)\] further extended this idea by proposing a method for calibrating uncertainty in forecasting models, which is essential for improving the reliability of allocation decisions.
