## Related Works

Neural rendering has emerged as a powerful technique for reconstructing 3D scenes from 2D images, leveraging implicit neural representations to capture both geometry and appearance \[[Wenbo Bao' 2019-04-01](http://arxiv.org/abs/1904.00830v1)\]. Early methods such as Neural Radiance Fields (NeRF) \[[Rohit Saha' 2020-05-14](http://arxiv.org/abs/2005.06684v1)\] demonstrated the potential of neural networks to learn continuous representations of 3D scenes from multi-view images. However, these methods typically require a large number of input views to achieve high-quality reconstructions \[[Youjian Zhang' 2021-12-02](http://arxiv.org/abs/2112.01161v1)\]. This limitation becomes more pronounced in dynamic or complex scenes where certain viewpoints are not available \[[Qiqi Hou' 2022-10-04](http://arxiv.org/abs/2210.01879v1)\]. 

To address this, several works have explored data augmentation techniques to improve the robustness and quality of neural rendering. For instance, \[[Weihao Zhuang' 2022-06-22](http://arxiv.org/abs/2206.10886v1)\] proposed using video frame interpolation to generate additional views, enhancing the training data for neural rendering models. Similarly, \[[Decai Chen' 2023-02-28](http://arxiv.org/abs/2303.00050v1)\] introduced a method that leverages video frame interpolation to simulate camera movement and improve the generalization of neural rendering models. These approaches aim to reduce the dependency on dense input views by generating synthetic data that captures realistic motion and geometry \[[Duolikun Danier' 2023-03-16](http://arxiv.org/abs/2303.09508v3)\].

In addition to data augmentation, recent research has focused on improving the efficiency and quality of video frame interpolation itself. Methods such as \[[Yue Wu' 2022-06-27](http://arxiv.org/abs/2206.13454v1)\] and \[[Wentao Shangguan' 2022-02-28](http://arxiv.org/abs/2203.00137v1)\] have introduced lightweight models that can handle large motion and produce high-quality interpolated frames. These models often incorporate adaptive motion estimation and warping techniques to ensure temporal consistency and reduce artifacts \[[Fitsum Reda' 2022-02-10](http://arxiv.org/abs/2202.04901v4)\]. Furthermore, \[[Fitsum A. Reda' 2019-06-13](http://arxiv.org/abs/1906.05928v3)\] proposed a method that combines motion estimation with frame interpolation to improve the quality of interpolated frames, particularly in dynamic scenes.

The integration of video frame interpolation with neural rendering has also been explored in several works. For example, \[[Ahmet Akman' 2023-03-03](http://arxiv.org/abs/2303.02025v1)\] introduced a method that uses video frame interpolation to enhance the training data for neural rendering models, leading to improved reconstruction performance. Similarly, \[[Hidekazu Takahashi' 2025-03-28](http://arxiv.org/abs/2503.22491v1)\] proposed a framework that combines video frame interpolation with neural rendering to generate more realistic and detailed 3D reconstructions. These approaches highlight the potential of video frame interpolation as a valuable tool for augmenting neural rendering datasets.

In addition to these methods, several works have focused on improving the efficiency and accuracy of video frame interpolation. For instance, \[[Yusen Wang' 2022-10-13](http://arxiv.org/abs/2210.06853v2)\] introduced a model that uses adaptive convolution to handle complex motion and produce high-quality interpolated frames. Similarly, \[[Dejia Xu' 2022-10-17](http://arxiv.org/abs/2210.08772v3)\] proposed a method that leverages optical flow and depth information to improve the accuracy of video frame interpolation. These methods demonstrate the importance of incorporating motion and depth information to achieve better results in video frame interpolation.

In summary, the related works in neural rendering and video frame interpolation highlight the potential of these techniques to improve the quality and robustness of 3D scene reconstruction. By leveraging video frame interpolation as a data augmentation method, researchers have been able to enhance the training data for neural rendering models, leading to improved performance on both static and dynamic scenes.
