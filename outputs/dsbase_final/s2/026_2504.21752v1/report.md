## Related Works

Differential privacy (DP) has emerged as the de facto standard for data privacy, offering rigorous guarantees against inference attacks \[[Ari Biswas' 2022-08-18](http://arxiv.org/abs/2208.09011v2)\]. However, its implementation in distributed settings remains vulnerable to adversarial behaviors, such as incorrect noise sampling or correlated noise generation by servers \[[Tariq Bontekoe' 2024-06-27](http://arxiv.org/abs/2406.18940v2)\]. These vulnerabilities highlight the need for additional verification mechanisms to ensure compliance with DP protocols \[[Aws Albarghouthi' 2017-09-15](http://arxiv.org/abs/1709.05361v2)\]. Prior work has explored various approaches to enhance DP's robustness, including the use of zero-knowledge proofs (ZKPs) and secure multi-party computation (MPC) \[[Yucheng Fu' 2024-09-16](http://arxiv.org/abs/2409.10667v2)\]. While ZKPs are sufficient for achieving DP under verifiability requirements, they are not necessary, as demonstrated by the development of verifiable differentially private mechanisms that rely on alternative verification strategies \[[Vitaly Feldman' 2020-12-23](http://arxiv.org/abs/2012.12803v3)\].

Several studies have investigated the limitations of traditional DP mechanisms in distributed environments. For instance, the Gaussian mechanism, commonly used for numerical data, suffers from unbounded support, potentially leading to invalid outputs \[[Ba Dung Le' 2021-02-25](http://arxiv.org/abs/2102.12621v1)\]. To address this, the bounded Gaussian mechanism was introduced, offering improved accuracy over bounded domains \[[Poushali Sengupta' 2020-06-10](http://arxiv.org/abs/2006.05609v2)\]. Similarly, the Laplacian mechanism, while effective for single real-valued queries, has been shown to degrade utility in low privacy regimes \[[Wentao Hu' 2022-12-01](http://arxiv.org/abs/2212.00306v2)\]. These findings underscore the importance of developing more efficient and accurate DP mechanisms, especially in scenarios where privacy and utility must be carefully balanced \[[Kousha Kalantari' 2016-01-24](http://arxiv.org/abs/1601.06426v3)\].

In the context of distributed systems, the challenge of ensuring DP while maintaining performance has been addressed through various techniques. The work on the shuffle model of privacy, for example, demonstrates that random shuffling can amplify privacy guarantees, leading to stronger protections in systems where data is contributed anonymously \[[Nirupam Gupta' 2018-09-06](http://arxiv.org/abs/1809.01794v3)\]. This aligns with the broader goal of improving DP's resilience against adversarial behaviors, such as those involving correlated noise or unfaithful execution of protocols \[[Carlos Efrain Quintero-Narvaez' 2023-10-20](http://arxiv.org/abs/2310.13618v1)\]. The proposed Verifiable Distributed Differential Privacy (VDDP) framework builds on these insights, incorporating additional verification mechanisms to ensure compliance with DP requirements in distributed client-server-verifier setups \[[Jiankai Jin' 2021-12-10](http://arxiv.org/abs/2112.05307v4)\].

Recent advancements in verifiable DP mechanisms have focused on reducing computational overhead and improving efficiency. For instance, the Verifiable Randomized Response (VRR) under local DP has been optimized to achieve significant reductions in communication costs and verifier overhead \[[Mohammad Alaggan' 2015-04-27](http://arxiv.org/abs/1504.06998v1)\]. Similarly, the Verifiable Distributed Discrete Laplacian Mechanism (VDDLM) demonstrates up to a $4 \times 10^5$x improvement in proof generation efficiency, with minimal impact on accuracy \[[Badih Ghazi' 2024-12-22](http://arxiv.org/abs/2412.16916v3)\]. These developments highlight the ongoing efforts to enhance the practicality of DP in real-world applications, particularly in environments where trust between parties is limited \[[Nikos Chondros' 2016-08-02](http://arxiv.org/abs/1608.00849v1)\].
