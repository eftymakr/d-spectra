## Related Works

Video super-resolution (VSR) has evolved significantly, with recent advances focusing on diffusion models and self-supervised learning to address the challenges of real-world scenarios. Early methods relied on supervised learning, where high-resolution (HR) videos were used as ground truth during training \[[Hongyu An' 2025-02-11](http://arxiv.org/abs/2502.07381v3)\]. However, these methods are constrained by the scarcity of high-quality HR datasets and their inability to generalize to real-world scenarios \[[Liangbin Xie' 2022-12-14](http://arxiv.org/abs/2212.07339v1)\]. To address these limitations, self-supervised learning has emerged as a viable alternative, enabling VSR models to be trained without explicit HR supervision \[[Shangchen Zhou' 2023-12-11](http://arxiv.org/abs/2312.06640v1)\].

Self-supervised learning in VSR typically involves pretext tasks such as rotation prediction, image enhancement, or contrastive learning, which help the model learn meaningful representations from unlabelled data \[[Rui Xie' 2025-01-06](http://arxiv.org/abs/2501.02976v1)\]. For instance, \[[Takashi Isobe' 2020-08-13](http://arxiv.org/abs/2008.05765v2)\] introduced a self-supervised learning framework that leverages the structure of video frames to learn spatial and temporal dependencies. Similarly, \[[Zhikai Chen' 2024-03-25](http://arxiv.org/abs/2403.17000v1)\] proposed a contrastive learning approach that uses the similarity between video frames to improve the quality of super-resolved outputs. These methods have demonstrated competitive performance on benchmark datasets, but they often struggle with maintaining temporal consistency and reducing artifacts in the generated video \[[Yexing Song' 2023-05-24](http://arxiv.org/abs/2305.14669v3)\].

Diffusion models have also gained traction in VSR due to their ability to generate high-quality, coherent video content \[[Saikat Dutta' 2021-04-12](http://arxiv.org/abs/2104.05778v3)\]. However, diffusion-based VSR methods are prone to introducing artifacts and degradations due to the inherent randomness in the diffusion process \[[Seunghan Yang' 2021-11-24](http://arxiv.org/abs/2111.12265v1)\]. To mitigate this, \[[Zhihao Zhan' 2025-03-05](http://arxiv.org/abs/2503.03355v5)\] proposed a noise-robust VSR framework that integrates self-supervised learning with diffusion models, improving the stability of the generation process. This approach was further enhanced by incorporating a global spatio-temporal attention mechanism, which helps maintain consistency across adjacent frames \[[Goutam Bhat' 2021-06-07](http://arxiv.org/abs/2106.03839v1)\].

In addition to attention mechanisms, several works have explored the use of self-supervised ControlNet modules to guide the diffusion process with HR features \[[Xiaoyu Xiang' 2021-04-15](http://arxiv.org/abs/2104.07473v1)\]. These modules leverage contrastive learning to extract degradation-insensitive features from LR videos, improving the quality of the generated HR video \[[Suttisak Wizadwongsa' 2023-07-20](http://arxiv.org/abs/2307.11118v1)\]. The integration of self-supervised learning with diffusion models has also been extended to include Mamba architectures, which are known for their ability to model long-range dependencies efficiently \[[Omer Bar-Tal' 2024-01-23](http://arxiv.org/abs/2401.12945v2)\].

To further stabilize the training of VSR models, a three-stage training strategy has been proposed, which combines HR-LR video pairs to improve the generalization of the model \[[Felix Pieper' 2023-11-19](http://arxiv.org/abs/2311.11335v1)\]. This approach has been shown to reduce artifacts in the generated video and improve perceptual quality on real-world benchmarks \[[Evan Racah' 2019-06-27](http://arxiv.org/abs/1906.11951v1)\].

Overall, the integration of self-supervised learning with diffusion models and Mamba architectures has led to significant improvements in the quality and stability of real-world VSR. These developments highlight the potential of self-supervised learning in overcoming the limitations of traditional supervised approaches and enabling the generation of high-quality, artifact-free HR videos.
