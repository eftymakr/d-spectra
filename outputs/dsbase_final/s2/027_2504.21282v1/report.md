## Related Works

Natural language (NL)-driven table discovery has become a critical area of research, especially with the increasing availability of large-scale table repositories and the rise of large language models (LLMs) that can understand and generate natural language. Traditional methods rely on dense vector search pipelines, which involve representation-index-search \[[Yi Tay' 2022-02-14](http://arxiv.org/abs/2202.06991v3)\]. These approaches, while effective, face significant limitations, such as error accumulation during the representation and indexing phases \[[Petru Neague' 2024-04-18](http://arxiv.org/abs/2404.12237v2)\], and insufficient query-table interaction that hinders semantic alignment \[[Sijia Yao' 2025-02-24](http://arxiv.org/abs/2502.17057v3)\]. 

To address these limitations, researchers have proposed alternative indexing and retrieval strategies. The Differentiable Search Index (DSI) framework \[[Xiaoyang Chen' 2023-05-03](http://arxiv.org/abs/2305.02073v2)\] unifies indexing and retrieval into a single transformer model, aiming to reduce error accumulation. However, DSI still faces challenges in handling data distribution mismatches between indexing and retrieval phases, especially in cross-lingual settings \[[Abhijeet Phatak' 2025-02-05](http://arxiv.org/abs/2502.02788v1)\]. 

In response to these challenges, the DSI-QG framework \[[Shahul Es' 2023-09-26](http://arxiv.org/abs/2309.15217v2)\] was introduced, which generates synthetic queries during indexing to improve query-table interaction. This approach allows the model to better align with the semantics of both queries and tables. Similarly, the work on synthetic data generation for table discovery \[[Li Deng' 2019-05-31](http://arxiv.org/abs/1906.00041v1)\] highlights the importance of generating relevant queries to enhance the accuracy of table retrieval. 

The use of LLMs for query generation has also been explored. For example, the GQR approach \[[Gowri Shankar Ramaswamy' 2012-08-20](http://arxiv.org/abs/1208.4016v1)\] frames query recommendation as a generative task, leveraging LLMs to produce relevant queries without requiring extensive training data. This is particularly useful in cold start scenarios where query logs are not available. 

Another line of research focuses on improving the efficiency and effectiveness of table discovery through better query representation and indexing. The work on the nlcTables dataset \[[Giacomo Pacini' 2024-12-18](http://arxiv.org/abs/2412.13834v1)\] demonstrates the need for more sophisticated query-table interaction, especially when dealing with complex natural language conditions. This dataset includes a variety of query types, including NL-only, union, join, and fuzzy conditions, highlighting the diversity of challenges in this domain. 

Additionally, the use of transformer-based models for table discovery has been explored, with some studies showing promising results. For example, the work on the nlcTables dataset \[[Giacomo Pacini' 2024-12-18](http://arxiv.org/abs/2412.13834v1)\] demonstrates the potential of using transformer models to handle complex query-table interactions. Similarly, the work on the Birdie framework \[[Dayu Yang' 2023-07-17](http://arxiv.org/abs/2307.08803v3)\] proposes a novel approach that unifies indexing and search into a single encoder-decoder model, reducing error accumulation and improving semantic alignment. 

These studies collectively highlight the importance of improving query-table interaction and reducing error accumulation in natural language-driven table discovery. While traditional methods have made significant progress, the integration of LLMs and more sophisticated indexing strategies offers promising avenues for further improvements.

Differentiable Search Index (DSI) frameworks represent a novel paradigm in information retrieval, where a single Transformer model encodes the entire corpus into its parameters, enabling direct query-to-docid mapping without explicit indexing \[[Yi Tay' 2022-02-14](http://arxiv.org/abs/2202.06991v3)\]. This approach simplifies the retrieval process by eliminating the need for separate indexing and search stages, thereby reducing error accumulation during representation and indexing \[[Sijia Yao' 2025-02-24](http://arxiv.org/abs/2502.17057v3)\]. Variations in document representation, training procedures, and model-corpora interactions have been explored to enhance performance, with DSI demonstrating strong generalization capabilities even in zero-shot scenarios \[[Yi Tay' 2022-02-14](http://arxiv.org/abs/2202.06991v3)\]. Furthermore, decentralized implementations like De-DSI extend this concept by using query-docid pairs and ensemble models to improve scalability and maintain retrieval accuracy across distributed systems \[[Petru Neague' 2024-04-18](http://arxiv.org/abs/2404.12237v2)\]. While DSI models excel at memorizing mappings from queries to document identifiers, they face challenges in distinguishing relevant documents from random ones, which can impact retrieval effectiveness \[[Sijia Yao' 2025-02-24](http://arxiv.org/abs/2502.17057v3)\]. To address this, multi-task distillation approaches have been proposed to enhance retrieval quality without altering the model structure \[[Sijia Yao' 2025-02-24](http://arxiv.org/abs/2502.17057v3)\]. Additionally, efforts have been made to balance relevance and diversity in DSI training, inspired by Maximal Marginal Relevance (MMR), allowing for diverse yet relevant recall sets without post-processing \[[Xiaoyang Chen' 2023-05-03](http://arxiv.org/abs/2305.02073v2)\]. These advancements highlight the evolving capabilities of DSI in addressing both traditional and complex retrieval tasks.
