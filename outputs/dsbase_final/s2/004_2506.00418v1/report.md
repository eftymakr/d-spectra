## Related Works

In-context learning (ICL) has emerged as a powerful paradigm for leveraging demonstrations within large annotated corpora to improve the performance of language models on new tasks \[[Max Wilcoxson' 2024-07-27](http://arxiv.org/abs/2407.19346v1)\]. Existing approaches often rely on perplexity-based methods to detect noisy annotations, assuming that noisy samples yield higher perplexities than their clean counterparts \[[Lianli Gao' 2023-08-10](http://arxiv.org/abs/2308.05286v2)\]. However, this assumption is challenged in scenarios with high noise ratios, where many demonstrations are flawed, leading to unreliable perplexity estimates \[[Akash Saravanan' 2023-02-05](http://arxiv.org/abs/2302.02453v1)\]. 

Several studies have explored the use of perplexity as a metric for evaluating text generation under noisy annotations. For instance, \[[Samuel Maddock' 2025-04-15](http://arxiv.org/abs/2504.10987v1)\] investigates the effectiveness of perplexity in identifying noisy samples, highlighting its limitations in high-noise environments. Similarly, \[[Deonna M. Owens' 2024-09-20](http://arxiv.org/abs/2409.13884v1)\] examines the impact of domain-specific knowledge in large language models (LLMs) on perplexity-based methods, noting that such knowledge can introduce biases that affect the accuracy of noise detection. These findings underscore the need for more robust approaches to address the limitations of perplexity-based methods in noisy settings \[[Jacob Beck' 2024-10-10](http://arxiv.org/abs/2410.08355v3)\].

To mitigate these issues, researchers have proposed alternative strategies. For example, \[[Rahul Kale' 2022-12-02](http://arxiv.org/abs/2212.00966v1)\] introduces a method that uses synthetic neighbors to correct perplexity estimates, aiming to improve the reliability of noise detection. This approach is similar to the dual debiasing framework proposed in this work, which explicitly corrects perplexity estimates using synthesized neighbors to yield a robust Sample Cleanliness Score \[[Massimiliano Ciranni' 2025-02-13](http://arxiv.org/abs/2502.09564v5)\]. The dual debiasing framework builds upon earlier work on debiasing techniques, such as those presented in \[[Zifeng Cheng' 2024-08-06](http://arxiv.org/abs/2408.03202v1)\], which focus on reducing biases in training data. 

The importance of robustness in ICL is further emphasized by \[[Shahriar Noroozizadeh' 2023-12-10](http://arxiv.org/abs/2312.05933v1)\], which highlights the challenges of maintaining performance in the presence of noisy annotations. This work aligns with the broader goal of improving the reliability of ICL under challenging conditions. Additionally, \[[Clovis Farley' 2023-11-29](http://arxiv.org/abs/2311.17794v1)\] explores the use of in-context learning for tasks such as sentiment analysis, demonstrating the potential of ICL in real-world applications. 

The proposed dual debiasing framework is also related to recent advances in debiasing techniques for other NLP tasks. For instance, \[[Amit Kumar' 2023-05-22](http://arxiv.org/abs/2305.12658v1)\] introduces a method for debiasing word embeddings by addressing social biases, while \[[Ilya Zisman' 2023-12-19](http://arxiv.org/abs/2312.12275v3)\] presents a framework for debiasing in click-through rate (CTR) prediction. These approaches share a common goal of improving the fairness and reliability of machine learning models in the presence of biased or noisy data.

In summary, the dual debiasing framework for ICL is grounded in a growing body of research that addresses the challenges of noise and bias in machine learning. By building upon these prior works, the proposed approach offers a novel and effective solution to the problem of noisy annotations in ICL.
