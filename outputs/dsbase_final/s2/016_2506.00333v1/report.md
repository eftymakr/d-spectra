## Related Works

Open-vocabulary object detection (OVD) has emerged as a critical research direction in computer vision, enabling the detection of objects from an arbitrary set of categories defined by users in natural language \[[Lewei Yao' 2024-04-14](http://arxiv.org/abs/2404.09216v1)\]. This capability extends beyond traditional closed-set detection, offering greater flexibility in real-world applications. However, the effectiveness of OVD is often limited by the quality and relevance of the user-defined vocabulary, which can be overly broad or mis-specified \[[Fangyi Chen' 2024-05-30](http://arxiv.org/abs/2405.19854v1)\]. Prior work has explored methods to improve the alignment between visual content and textual descriptions, often relying on pre-trained vision-language models (VLMs) for zero-shot detection \[[Chuang Lin' 2022-11-27](http://arxiv.org/abs/2211.14843v1)\]. These approaches typically assume a fixed set of categories or require extensive training on labeled data, which limits their adaptability to novel or unseen classes \[[Zizhao Li' 2024-11-27](http://arxiv.org/abs/2411.18207v4)\].

Several studies have attempted to enhance open-vocabulary detection by leveraging the rich semantic knowledge of VLMs. For instance, \[[Mingfei Gao' 2021-11-18](http://arxiv.org/abs/2111.09452v3)\] proposes a framework that uses CLIP to align region embeddings with categorical labels, while \[[Yongchao Feng' 2025-04-13](http://arxiv.org/abs/2504.09480v1)\] introduces a method that incorporates fine-grained textual descriptions to improve region-text alignment. These approaches, however, often struggle with the challenge of accurately distinguishing between relevant and irrelevant categories in the user-defined vocabulary, especially when the vocabulary is large or poorly specified \[[Jianghang Lin' 2023-12-19](http://arxiv.org/abs/2312.12437v1)\].

To address these limitations, recent work has focused on improving the alignment between the detected objects and the textual descriptions provided by users. For example, \[[Yan Li' 2023-11-20](http://arxiv.org/abs/2311.11646v4)\] introduces a method that uses a language model to generate pseudo-labels for training, while \[[Hanoona Rasheed' 2022-07-07](http://arxiv.org/abs/2207.03482v3)\] proposes a framework that integrates both 2D and 3D modalities to enhance semantic understanding. These methods, however, often require additional training or are limited in their ability to handle dynamic vocabularies \[[Jiaqi Wang' 2024-06-17](http://arxiv.org/abs/2406.11739v1)\].

The proposed Vocabulary Adapter (VocAda) builds on these prior efforts by introducing a plug-and-play approach that operates at inference time without requiring any training. This is a significant advancement, as it allows for real-time adaptation of the vocabulary to the specific content of an image \[[Ruizhe Zeng' 2024-10-11](http://arxiv.org/abs/2410.08645v1)\]. VocAda follows a three-step process: first, it generates a caption of the visible objects in the image, then it parses the nouns from the caption, and finally, it selects relevant classes from the user-defined vocabulary while discarding irrelevant ones \[[Peixian Chen' 2022-06-22](http://arxiv.org/abs/2206.11134v4)\]. This approach ensures that the vocabulary is tailored to the specific context of the image, thereby improving detection performance \[[Mingxuan Liu' 2024-05-16](http://arxiv.org/abs/2405.10053v1)\].

Several studies have explored similar ideas, such as using image captions to guide detection \[[Dicong Qiu' 2024-06-26](http://arxiv.org/abs/2406.18115v1)\], but these approaches often require additional training or are limited in their ability to handle dynamic vocabularies \[[Jianzong Wu' 2023-06-28](http://arxiv.org/abs/2306.15880v4)\]. The key contribution of VocAda lies in its ability to operate without training, making it highly versatile and applicable to a wide range of scenarios \[[Gokul Karthik Kumar' 2022-05-11](http://arxiv.org/abs/2205.05543v1)\].

Open-vocabulary object detection (OVD) has emerged as a critical research direction, enabling models to detect objects from an unbounded vocabulary, including novel categories not seen during training \[[Lewei Yao' 2024-04-14](http://arxiv.org/abs/2404.09216v1)\]. This capability is largely attributed to the integration of vision-language pre-trained models, which provide semantic understanding to bridge the gap between visual content and textual descriptions \[[Jianzong Wu' 2023-06-28](http://arxiv.org/abs/2306.15880v4)\]. However, existing methods face challenges such as limited generalization to unseen objects, reliance on accurate prompts, and difficulties in aligning visual and linguistic representations \[[Chuang Lin' 2022-11-27](http://arxiv.org/abs/2211.14843v1)\]. To address these issues, various approaches have been proposed. For instance, DetCLIPv3 introduces a caption head to enhance generative capabilities and employs an auto-annotation pipeline to improve training data quality \[[Lewei Yao' 2024-04-14](http://arxiv.org/abs/2404.09216v1)\]. Similarly, VLDet directly learns from image-text pairs by formulating object-language alignment as a set matching problem, achieving superior performance on novel categories \[[Chuang Lin' 2022-11-27](http://arxiv.org/abs/2211.14843v1)\]. Additionally, methods like BIRDet tackle the limitations of CLIP by introducing background information modeling to better handle background samples \[[Hanoona Rasheed' 2022-07-07](http://arxiv.org/abs/2207.03482v3)\], while MEDet addresses confidence bias toward base categories through proposal mining and prediction equalization \[[Jiaqi Wang' 2024-06-17](http://arxiv.org/abs/2406.11739v1)\]. Furthermore, frameworks such as SHiNe incorporate semantic hierarchies to improve robustness across varying vocabulary granularities \[[Ruizhe Zeng' 2024-10-11](http://arxiv.org/abs/2410.08645v1)\], and CastDet leverages a student-teacher self-learning mechanism to enhance detection performance in aerial images \[[Yongchao Feng' 2025-04-13](http://arxiv.org/abs/2504.09480v1)\]. These advancements collectively highlight the ongoing efforts to improve open-vocabulary detection by addressing challenges related to generalization, alignment, and robustness.
