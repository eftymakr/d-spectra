## Related Works

The integration of knowledge graphs (KGs) with large language models (LLMs) to enhance reasoning capabilities in claim verification has gained significant attention in recent years. While KGs provide structured, semantically rich representations that are ideal for reasoning tasks, most existing claim verification methods rely on unstructured text corpora, thereby limiting their ability to effectively leverage KGs \[[Qiyuan Li' 2025-02-28](http://arxiv.org/abs/2502.20988v2)\]. This limitation is particularly evident in the context of multi-step reasoning pipelines, where LLMs often struggle to navigate the complexity of structured knowledge without adaptation \[[Zhi Zheng' 2025-05-18](http://arxiv.org/abs/2505.12348v1)\].

Several works have explored the use of KGs in conjunction with LLMs for claim verification. For example, \[[Lei Sun' 2024-04-11](http://arxiv.org/abs/2404.07677v2)\] presents a method for evaluating class membership relations in knowledge graphs using LLMs, demonstrating that LLMs can be used to assess the quality of class relations in KGs. Similarly, \[[Haisong Gong' 2025-02-17](http://arxiv.org/abs/2502.11959v2)\] introduces a framework for knowledge graph curation that emphasizes the importance of quality metrics and validation strategies to improve the reliability of KGs. These works highlight the potential of LLMs in KG-related tasks but do not directly address the integration of LLMs with KGs for claim verification.

The use of LLMs for knowledge engineering tasks has also been explored in the context of knowledge graph completion and correction. For instance, \[[Jonathan Leung' 2025-05-24](http://arxiv.org/abs/2505.18607v2)\] presents a pipeline using LLMs for knowledge engineering (LLMKE), which combines knowledge probing and Wikidata entity mapping to enhance the accuracy of knowledge graph completion. The results show that LLMs can be effective in this context, although their performance varies significantly depending on the domain \[[Jonathan Leung' 2025-05-24](http://arxiv.org/abs/2505.18607v2)\]. This variability underscores the need for domain-specific adaptations when integrating LLMs with KGs for claim verification.

In addition to these works, \[[Haoran Wang' 2023-10-08](http://arxiv.org/abs/2310.05253v2)\] proposes a framework for knowledge-driven agentic scientific corpus distillation, which is tailored for biomedical LLM training. This framework leverages multi-agent architectures to extract and synthesize high-quality textual data from scientific literature, demonstrating the potential of combining LLMs with structured knowledge sources \[[Haoran Wang' 2023-10-08](http://arxiv.org/abs/2310.05253v2)\]. Similarly, \[[Shirui Pan' 2023-06-14](http://arxiv.org/abs/2306.08302v3)\] introduces KELM, a framework that enhances pre-trained language models with knowledge from KGs using message passing on hierarchical relational graphs. This work demonstrates that integrating KGs with LLMs can significantly improve performance on tasks such as machine reading comprehension \[[Shirui Pan' 2023-06-14](http://arxiv.org/abs/2306.08302v3)\].
