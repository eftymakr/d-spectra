## Related Works

Maximum Inner Product Search (MIPS) has been a focal point in machine learning and information retrieval, particularly for high-dimensional data applications \[[Tingyang Chen' 2025-03-10](http://arxiv.org/abs/2503.06882v2)\]. Traditional approaches to MIPS either rely solely on inner product (IP) similarity, which can lead to local optima and redundant computations \[[Tingyang Chen' 2025-03-10](http://arxiv.org/abs/2503.06882v2)\], or reduce the problem to nearest neighbor search under the Euclidean metric through space projection, which may distort the original data topology and cause information loss \[[Tingyang Chen' 2025-03-10](http://arxiv.org/abs/2503.06882v2)\]. This paper argues that the IP and Euclidean metrics are not inherently opposed but can be integrated in indexing and search algorithms to enhance performance \[[Tingyang Chen' 2025-03-10](http://arxiv.org/abs/2503.06882v2)\].

Several works have explored graph-based methods for approximate MIPS. The inner-product navigable small world graph (ip-NSW) has been shown to achieve an order of magnitude speedup over the fastest baseline \[[Ruiqi Guo' 2015-09-04](http://arxiv.org/abs/1509.01469v1)\]. However, its performance is attributed to the norm bias in the MIPS problem, where large norm items are more likely to be the result of MIPS \[[Ruiqi Guo' 2015-09-04](http://arxiv.org/abs/1509.01469v1)\]. To improve performance, the ip-NSW+ algorithm was introduced, which adds an angular proximity graph to enhance search accuracy \[[Ruiqi Guo' 2015-09-04](http://arxiv.org/abs/1509.01469v1)\]. Similarly, BanditMIPS, a randomized algorithm, estimates inner products by subsampling coordinates and adaptively evaluates more coordinates for promising atoms, achieving a complexity independent of dimensionality \[[Qiang Huang' 2022-11-23](http://arxiv.org/abs/2211.12751v1)\].

Hashing techniques have also been applied to MIPS. Asymmetric LSH (ALSH) was introduced to address the challenge of finding hashing schemes for MIPS by converting the problem into approximate near neighbor search \[[Anshumali Shrivastava' 2014-10-20](http://arxiv.org/abs/1410.5410v2)\]. This approach was later extended to improve approximate MIPS performance \[[Alexander B. Veretennikov' 2020-06-14](http://arxiv.org/abs/2006.07954v1)\]. Norm-ranging LSH, proposed in \[[Alex Auvolat' 2015-07-21](http://arxiv.org/abs/1507.05910v3)\], partitions the dataset into sub-datasets and builds a hash index for each independently, resulting in lower query time complexity than Simple-LSH \[[Alex Auvolat' 2015-07-21](http://arxiv.org/abs/1507.05910v3)\].

Clustering-based approaches have also been explored for approximate MIPS. The work in \[[Stephan S. Lorenzen' 2019-08-23](http://arxiv.org/abs/1908.08656v2)\] compares clustering-based methods to others and shows that a simple k-means clustering approach can achieve much higher speedups than current state-of-the-art hashing-based and tree-based methods \[[Stephan S. Lorenzen' 2019-08-23](http://arxiv.org/abs/1908.08656v2)\]. Additionally, the paper in \[[Xiao Yan' 2018-09-24](http://arxiv.org/abs/1809.08782v2)\] investigates the use of spherical k-means to reduce the MIPS problem to a Maximum Cosine Similarity Search (MCSS), which is then solved using clustering-based methods \[[Xiao Yan' 2018-09-24](http://arxiv.org/abs/1809.08782v2)\].

In summary, existing literature on MIPS has explored various approaches, including graph-based methods, hashing techniques, and clustering-based strategies. These methods have been shown to offer significant improvements in performance and efficiency. However, the integration of IP and Euclidean metrics in indexing and search algorithms remains a relatively unexplored area, which this paper addresses through the introduction of the Metric-Amphibious Graph (MAG) and the Adaptive Navigation with Metric Switch (ANMS) algorithm \[[Tingyang Chen' 2025-03-10](http://arxiv.org/abs/2503.06882v2)\].
