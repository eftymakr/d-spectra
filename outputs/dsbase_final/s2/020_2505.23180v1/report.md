## Related Works

Deep-unrolling and plug-and-play (PnP) approaches have emerged as dominant methodologies for solving inverse problems in single-pixel imaging (SPI) \[[Guanxiong Luo' 2022-11-30](http://arxiv.org/abs/2211.16881v2)\]. PnP methods are iterative algorithms that implicitly regularize the solution using pre-trained deep denoisers, offering flexibility across varying compression ratios (CRs) but often at the cost of reconstruction accuracy and speed \[[Kai Song' 2023-10-25](http://arxiv.org/abs/2310.16869v2)\]. On the other hand, unrolling approaches transform truncated iterative optimization processes into end-to-end trainable neural networks, typically achieving better accuracy and faster inference but requiring retraining or fine-tuning when CR changes \[[Vishal Monga' 2019-12-22](http://arxiv.org/abs/1912.10557v3)\]. This paper addresses the challenge of integrating the strengths of both methodologies.

PnP methods, such as those proposed in \[[Yair Ben Sahel' 2021-09-28](http://arxiv.org/abs/2109.14025v1)\], leverage off-the-shelf denoisers to implicitly define image priors, enabling flexibility in handling different CRs without retraining. However, they often suffer from suboptimal reconstruction quality and slower convergence compared to unrolling-based methods. Unrolling approaches, such as those based on the Alternating Direction Method of Multipliers (ADMM) \[[Yanan Zhao' 2024-02-20](http://arxiv.org/abs/2402.12872v2)\], have been widely adopted for their ability to achieve high accuracy by learning the iterative steps of optimization algorithms. However, these methods typically require retraining for different CRs, limiting their adaptability \[[Indra Deep Mastan' 2020-12-11](http://arxiv.org/abs/2012.06469v1)\].

To address these limitations, recent works have explored hybrid approaches that combine the flexibility of PnP with the accuracy of unrolling. For instance, \[[Asma Balamane' 2019-11-10](http://arxiv.org/abs/1911.03991v1)\] proposes a deep learning framework that integrates PnP and unrolling strategies to improve reconstruction performance while maintaining adaptability to varying CRs. Similarly, \[[Xingguo Li' 2016-05-25](http://arxiv.org/abs/1605.07950v6)\] introduces a method that unrolls the ADMM algorithm into a neural network, allowing for end-to-end training while preserving the interpretability of the original optimization process. These methods demonstrate that unrolling can be adapted to handle different CRs without requiring complete retraining, aligning with the goals of this work.

A key contribution of this work is the design of an efficient deep image restorer (DIR) that approximates the proximal operator of an ideal explicit restoration regularizer \[[Monika Grewal' 2021-09-06](http://arxiv.org/abs/2109.02722v2)\]. This DIR is trained using a general proximal trajectory (PT) loss function, enabling the unrolled HQS and ADMM networks to achieve high accuracy across varying CRs. This approach is inspired by earlier works that explored the use of proximal operators in deep learning, such as \[[Kyungmin Choi' 2025-04-03](http://arxiv.org/abs/2504.02480v2)\], which proposed a framework for incorporating proximal operators into neural networks to improve convergence and stability.

In addition, this work builds upon the concept of algorithm unrolling, which has been widely studied in the context of image restoration and computational imaging \[[Dongliang Chang' 2020-02-11](http://arxiv.org/abs/2002.04264v3)\]. For example, \[[Maria Teresa Alzugaray' 2002-10-28](http://arxiv.org/abs/math/0210429v1)\] demonstrates how unrolling the ADMM algorithm can lead to improved performance in image deblurring tasks. Similarly, \[[Khoi Khac Nguyen' 2021-06-06](http://arxiv.org/abs/2106.03129v1)\] explores the use of unrolling for hyperspectral image restoration, showing that unrolled networks can outperform traditional methods in terms of both accuracy and speed.

The proposed PT loss function is a novel contribution that enables the training of unrolled networks to approximate the proximal operator of an ideal regularizer, thereby improving both accuracy and adaptability \[[Bo Wen' 2016-12-19](http://arxiv.org/abs/1612.06265v2)\]. This is a significant advancement over previous methods that either required retraining for different CRs \[[Olivier Leblanc' 2023-06-22](http://arxiv.org/abs/2306.12698v3)\] or were limited in their ability to generalize across varying conditions \[[Figen S. Oktem' 2020-08-26](http://arxiv.org/abs/2008.11625v3)\].
