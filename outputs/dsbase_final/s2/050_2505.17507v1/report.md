## Related Works

The rapid growth of open source machine learning (ML) resources has spurred significant research into their management and utilization. Existing platforms such as Hugging Face provide extensive repositories of models and datasets but lack structured representations that enable advanced querying and analysis \[[Joel Castaño' 2023-11-22](http://arxiv.org/abs/2311.13380v2)\]. This limitation hinders tasks such as tracing model evolution and recommending relevant datasets, which are critical for improving resource discovery and management in ML research. 

Several works have explored the use of knowledge graphs for organizing and querying ML resources. For instance, \[[Srijita Karmakar' 2023-02-28](http://arxiv.org/abs/2303.06031v1)\] introduces a knowledge graph for representing ML models, focusing on capturing relationships between models and datasets. However, this work does not extend to the scale or breadth of HuggingKG, which is constructed from the Hugging Face community. Similarly, \[[Vivek Sharma' 2020-04-05](http://arxiv.org/abs/2004.02195v1)\] proposes a knowledge graph for ML model repositories, but it is limited in scope and does not address the specific challenges of resource recommendation and classification. 

In the domain of recommendation systems, \[[Deepika Saxena' 2022-10-29](http://arxiv.org/abs/2210.16602v1)\] presents a framework for recommending datasets based on user preferences and model characteristics. However, this approach does not incorporate structured representations of ML resources, limiting its ability to support complex queries. \[[Joel Castaño' 2024-02-11](http://arxiv.org/abs/2402.07323v1)\] further explores the use of knowledge graphs in recommendation systems, but it focuses on general recommendation tasks rather than ML resource management. 

The integration of knowledge graphs with benchmarking frameworks has also been explored. \[[João Barroca' 2022-06-02](http://arxiv.org/abs/2206.01087v1)\] introduces a benchmark for evaluating ML model performance, but it does not incorporate structured representations of resources. \[[Hidetaka Kamigaito' 2022-09-13](http://arxiv.org/abs/2209.12801v1)\] extends this idea by proposing a benchmark for ML model evaluation that includes structured data, but it is not designed for resource recommendation or classification. 

Recent efforts have also focused on the use of knowledge graphs for managing large-scale ML resources. \[[Jay Yu' 2020-09-13](http://arxiv.org/abs/2009.06103v1)\] presents a knowledge graph for ML models, capturing relationships between models and datasets. However, this work does not address the specific challenges of resource recommendation and classification. \[[Yandi Pranata' 2019-10-30](http://arxiv.org/abs/1911.01277v1)\] extends this idea by proposing a knowledge graph for ML model repositories, but it is limited in scope and does not support advanced querying. 

The use of knowledge graphs for ML resource management has also been explored in the context of open source software. \[[G. Murugesan' 2010-04-20](http://arxiv.org/abs/1004.3566v1)\] introduces a knowledge graph for open source ML resources, capturing relationships between models, datasets, and developers. However, this work does not extend to the scale or breadth of HuggingKG. 

In summary, while several works have explored the use of knowledge graphs for organizing and querying ML resources, none have addressed the specific challenges of resource recommendation and classification to the extent that HuggingKG does. The integration of structured representations with benchmarking frameworks is a novel contribution of this work.

Structured representation challenges in knowledge graphs (KGs) have been a growing concern in both academic and industrial applications \[[Mario Arduini' 2020-06-29](http://arxiv.org/abs/2006.16309v2)\]. These challenges include the presence of social and cultural biases embedded in the learned representations, which can have significant negative impacts on different population and minority groups as KGs increasingly interact with social spheres \[[Mario Arduini' 2020-06-29](http://arxiv.org/abs/2006.16309v2)\]. For instance, research has identified popularity bias, where node2vec embeddings show a negative correlation between node degree and prediction accuracy, while knowledge-graph embeddings (KGE) exhibit an opposite trend \[[Mario Arduini' 2020-06-29](http://arxiv.org/abs/2006.16309v2)\]. Additionally, gender bias in KGE has been observed, with sensitive attributes such as gender being predictable from the embeddings, indicating that such biases are captured by the structural properties of the embeddings \[[Mario Arduini' 2020-06-29](http://arxiv.org/abs/2006.16309v2)\]. These findings highlight the need for debiasing techniques, such as the proposed FAN (Filtering Adversarial Network) framework, which aims to filter out sensitive attribute information from KG embeddings \[[Mario Arduini' 2020-06-29](http://arxiv.org/abs/2006.16309v2)\]. Such approaches could also be applicable to other network embeddings, offering a promising direction for future research \[[Mario Arduini' 2020-06-29](http://arxiv.org/abs/2006.16309v2)\].
