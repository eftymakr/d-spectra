## Related Works

Memory bandwidth regulation and cache partitioning are widely used techniques for achieving predictable timing in real-time computing systems \[[Avinatan Hassidim' 2012-10-15](http://arxiv.org/abs/1210.4053v3)\]. These methods are often combined with partitioned scheduling to ensure that task execution times remain bounded, as they strongly depend on the available allocated resources \[[Ankit Agrawal' 2018-09-16](http://arxiv.org/abs/1809.05921v1)\]. Several studies have explored the co-allocation of tasks and resources to cores, aiming to balance performance and predictability \[[Binqi Sun' 2023-10-04](http://arxiv.org/abs/2310.02959v1)\]. For instance, \[[Ryoki Hamano' 2025-04-10](http://arxiv.org/abs/2504.07884v6)\] presents a framework for co-designing cache partitioning and multi-core task scheduling, emphasizing the importance of cache sensitivity in improving schedulability. Similarly, \[[Sparsh Mittal' 2013-10-16](http://arxiv.org/abs/1310.4231v1)\] proposes a per-bank bandwidth regulation approach for multi-banked shared LLC based real-time systems, demonstrating how this can improve performance while maintaining isolation benefits.

To address the challenge of task-resource co-allocation, various optimization techniques have been proposed. A notable approach is the use of 0-1 integer linear programming (ILP) to model the problem, which has been explored in \[[Oliver Horst' 2020-09-01](http://arxiv.org/abs/2009.00466v1)\]. This method continues the classic idea of solving ILP with its LP relaxation and introduces strong cuts to enhance solution quality. Additionally, \[[Feng Li' 2022-05-23](http://arxiv.org/abs/2205.11173v1)\] introduces a polynomial-time algorithm for 0-1 ILP, which is significant as it implies P=NP, although this may not be directly applicable to real-time systems due to its theoretical nature. 

In the context of real-time systems, \[[Muhammad Ali Awan' 2017-04-28](http://arxiv.org/abs/1704.08876v1)\] presents a multi-objective heuristic designed to minimize resource usage while guaranteeing schedulability under a preemptive EDF scheduling policy.
