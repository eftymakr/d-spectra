## Related Works

The study of economic inequality and its mitigation through taxation has seen significant advancements in both theoretical and applied research. Traditional approaches to tax policy, such as the U.S. federal income tax, have been criticized for their limited adaptability to changing economic conditions and taxpayer behavior \[[Peiyi Wang' 2023-09-05](http://arxiv.org/abs/2309.02144v1)\]. The Saez Optimal Taxation model, which dynamically adjusts tax rates to balance equity and productivity, represents a more sophisticated approach, yet it struggles to account for the heterogeneity of taxpayers and irrational decision-making \[[Sina Gogani-Khiabani' 2025-04-25](http://arxiv.org/abs/2504.18693v1)\]. This limitation is echoed in the broader challenge of designing tax systems that effectively address inequality while maintaining economic efficiency \[[Iago Nascimento Barros' 2025-01-15](http://arxiv.org/abs/2501.08573v1)\].

Recent advances in artificial intelligence, particularly in the integration of large language models (LLMs) with agent-based modeling (ABM), offer promising new avenues for tax policy design. The TaxAgent framework, which combines LLMs with ABM to simulate heterogeneous taxpayer behavior and iteratively optimize tax rates, builds upon prior work in multi-agent systems and economic simulation \[[Gido H. Schoenmacker' 2022-09-12](http://arxiv.org/abs/2209.05109v1)\]. This approach aligns with the growing interest in using AI to model complex socio-economic systems, as seen in studies that explore the use of ABMs for policy evaluation \[[Kasun Amarasinghe' 2020-10-27](http://arxiv.org/abs/2010.14374v3)\]. For instance, the ABIDES-Economist model incorporates heterogeneous agents and reinforcement learning to simulate economic scenarios, demonstrating the potential of such frameworks for policy analysis \[[Andrew Blair-Stanek' 2023-09-15](http://arxiv.org/abs/2309.09992v2)\].

The integration of LLMs into economic modeling is a relatively new but rapidly growing field. Prior research has explored the use of LLMs for tasks such as tax policy generation and economic forecasting, often focusing on their ability to process and generate natural language instructions \[[Hangyu Mao' 2018-11-13](http://arxiv.org/abs/1811.07029v1)\]. However, the application of LLMs to tax policy design, particularly in the context of agent-based simulations, remains underexplored. The TaxAgent framework addresses this gap by leveraging LLMs to iteratively refine tax policies based on real-time feedback from simulated taxpayer behavior \[[Yiming Ai' 2024-02-22](http://arxiv.org/abs/2402.14679v2)\].

The equity-efficiency trade-off in tax policy is a central concern in economic literature. Studies such as the one by \[[Linying Yang' 2024-07-10](http://arxiv.org/abs/2407.08029v1)\] highlight the challenges of balancing these two objectives, particularly in the context of heterogeneous populations and varying economic conditions. The TaxAgent model's ability to achieve superior equity-efficiency trade-offs compared to traditional models underscores the potential of AI-driven approaches in this domain \[[Amit Chandak' 2020-05-22](http://arxiv.org/abs/2005.11257v2)\].

### <a id='category-natural-language-processing-for-tax-policy'></a>Natural Language Processing for Tax Policy: 6 papers found.
The category "Natural Language Processing for Tax Policy" encompasses research that explores the application of large language models (LLMs) in the context of tax law and policy. Several studies highlight the challenges in maintaining up-to-date tax preparation software due to the dynamic nature of tax regulations, suggesting that LLMs could be leveraged to automatically extract and integrate code changes from IRS publications \[[Sina Gogani-Khiabani' 2025-04-25](http://arxiv.org/abs/2504.18693v1)\]. Additionally, there is a focus on evaluating the reasoning capabilities of LLMs, particularly in the context of tax law, where models like GPT-4 have been observed to produce incorrect results due to limitations in their ability to accurately calculate taxes \[[Iago Nascimento Barros' 2025-01-15](http://arxiv.org/abs/2501.08573v1)\]. This has led to the development of methodologies aimed at improving the reasoning performance of LLMs through techniques such as chain-of-thought (COT) training and alignment fine-tuning \[[Peiyi Wang' 2023-09-05](http://arxiv.org/abs/2309.02144v1)\]. Furthermore, the use of LLMs in simulating taxpayer behavior and optimizing tax policies is being explored, with the introduction of frameworks like TaxAgent that integrate LLMs with agent-based modeling to create adaptive tax policies \[[Amit Chandak' 2020-05-22](http://arxiv.org/abs/2005.11257v2)\]. These studies collectively underscore the potential and challenges of applying NLP techniques to tax policy, emphasizing the need for robust and adaptable systems that can handle the complexities of real-world tax scenarios.

### <a id='category-agent-based-economic-modeling'></a>Agent-Based Economic Modeling: 2 papers found.
Agent-based economic modeling has been widely used to study the dynamics of wealth distribution and consumer behavior, offering insights into how policy interventions can influence economic outcomes. In capitalist societies, the unrestricted accumulation of wealth is a key driver of increasing inequality, highlighting the need for effective income redistribution policies \[[Peiyi Wang' 2023-09-05](http://arxiv.org/abs/2309.02144v1)\]. Research has shown that non-uniform income redistribution and strict control over tax evasion are essential for achieving more egalitarian societies \[[Peiyi Wang' 2023-09-05](http://arxiv.org/abs/2309.02144v1)\]. Similarly, in the context of consumer behavior, agent-based models have been employed to simulate how individuals make decisions in markets, such as the transition from incandescent to energy-efficient lighting \[[Sina Gogani-Khiabani' 2025-04-25](http://arxiv.org/abs/2504.18693v1)\]. These models incorporate individual preferences, learning from past actions, and social interactions, revealing that consumer resistance to change can be mitigated through targeted information campaigns \[[Sina Gogani-Khiabani' 2025-04-25](http://arxiv.org/abs/2504.18693v1)\]. By integrating large language models with agent-based modeling, recent studies have introduced innovative approaches to tax policy design, enabling more adaptive and equitable fiscal frameworks \[[Iago Nascimento Barros' 2025-01-15](http://arxiv.org/abs/2501.08573v1)\]. Such models allow for the simulation of heterogeneous taxpayer behaviors and the iterative optimization of tax rates, balancing equity and productivity in ways that traditional models fail to achieve \[[Iago Nascimento Barros' 2025-01-15](http://arxiv.org/abs/2501.08573v1)\]. These advancements underscore the growing importance of agent-based modeling in addressing complex economic challenges through data-driven and scalable solutions.

### <a id='category-ai-driven-policy-evaluation'></a>AI-Driven Policy Evaluation: 2 papers found.
The category "AI-Driven Policy Evaluation" encompasses research that leverages artificial intelligence to assess and optimize policy outcomes, particularly in complex domains like public health and economic policy. A key focus is on developing explainable machine learning (ML) systems that can support high-stakes decision-making, such as in criminal justice, education, and healthcare, where transparency and accountability are critical \[[Peiyi Wang' 2023-09-05](http://arxiv.org/abs/2309.02144v1)\]. However, much of the existing work in explainable ML lacks real-world applicability, often being evaluated on simplified benchmarks or with proxy users rather than engaging directly with end-users and their specific needs \[[Peiyi Wang' 2023-09-05](http://arxiv.org/abs/2309.02144v1)\]. This gap is addressed by identifying use cases within public policy and aligning ML methods with these contexts to ensure practical societal impact \[[Peiyi Wang' 2023-09-05](http://arxiv.org/abs/2309.02144v1)\]. Additionally, novel applications of active machine learning, such as ESOP, demonstrate how AI can be used to optimize non-pharmaceutical interventions like lockdown schedules by balancing public health and socio-economic factors through Bayesian optimization and agent-based modeling \[[Amit Chandak' 2020-05-22](http://arxiv.org/abs/2005.11257v2)\]. These approaches highlight the potential of AI to enhance policy evaluation by providing data-driven, adaptive solutions that consider both efficiency and equity.

### <a id='category-reinforcement-learning-in-economic-models'></a>Reinforcement Learning in Economic Models: 2 papers found.

Recent research in reinforcement learning (RL) has focused on improving cooperation in multi-agent systems, particularly through the development of attention mechanisms to model dynamic teammate policies \[[Hangyu Mao' 2018-11-13](http://arxiv.org/abs/1811.07029v1)\]. This is crucial for achieving efficient and scalable cooperative strategies in complex environments. For instance, the ATT-MADDPG framework extends the DDPG algorithm by incorporating a centralized critic and an attention mechanism, allowing agents to process and adapt to changing teammate behaviors more effectively \[[Hangyu Mao' 2018-11-13](http://arxiv.org/abs/1811.07029v1)\]. Such advancements are relevant to the broader goal of creating adaptive systems that can handle real-world economic challenges, such as designing tax policies that balance equity and productivity. Additionally, optimization techniques like the first-order primal-dual framework have been developed to enhance convergence rates in convex optimization problems, which can be applied to improve the efficiency of policy learning in multi-agent settings \[[Quoc Tran-Dinh' 2015-07-22](http://arxiv.org/abs/1507.06243v7)\]. These contributions highlight the growing intersection between RL, optimization, and economic modeling, offering new tools for addressing dynamic and heterogeneous systems.
