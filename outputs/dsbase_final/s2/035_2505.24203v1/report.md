## Related Works

Protein dynamics are fundamental to understanding biological functions and properties, traditionally studied through time-consuming molecular dynamics (MD) simulations . Recent advances in generative modeling, particularly denoising diffusion models, have enabled efficient and accurate protein structure prediction and conformation sampling by learning distributions over crystallographic structures . However, integrating physical supervision into these data-driven approaches remains challenging, as standard energy-based objectives often lead to intractable optimization .

Several studies have explored the use of diffusion models for protein structure prediction and conformation generation. For instance, ConfDiff proposes a force-guided SE(3) diffusion model to generate protein conformations with rich diversity while preserving high fidelity . Similarly, DiAMoNDBack introduces an autoregressive denoising diffusion probability model to restore all-atom details to coarse-grained protein representations . These models demonstrate the potential of diffusion-based approaches in capturing complex conformational landscapes. However, they often lack explicit physical guidance during the generation process, which can lead to deviations from the equilibrium distribution .

To address this, some works have attempted to incorporate physical constraints into diffusion models. For example, the work by  uses denoising diffusion probabilistic models (DDPMs) to train an uncertainty-aware surrogate model for turbulence simulations, capturing the whole distribution of solutions and estimating simulation uncertainties. This approach is adapted to airfoil flow simulations, showing that DDPMs can accurately estimate uncertainties and outperform Bayesian neural networks and heteroscedastic models . Similarly, the study by  introduces Structure Language Modeling (SLM) as a novel framework for efficient protein conformation generation, combining discrete variational auto-encoders with conditional language modeling to capture sequence-specific conformation distributions . These methods highlight the importance of integrating physical knowledge into generative models for more accurate and physically plausible results.

In addition, the work by  introduces Gibbs Diffusion (GDiff), a general methodology addressing posterior sampling of both the signal and the noise parameters. This approach allows for blind denoising of natural images and is applied to cosmology problems, demonstrating its versatility in handling complex data . Similarly, the study by  proposes Diffusion Sampling with Momentum to mitigate divergence artifacts, improving image quality and reducing artifacts in low-step sampling . These methods underscore the ongoing efforts to enhance the robustness and efficiency of diffusion models in various domains.

The integration of physical models with generative approaches is also explored in the work by , which introduces Fast Sampling of Protein Conformational Dynamics. This study modifies an analysis of molecular vibrations to efficiently isolate low-frequency vibrations in proteins, resulting in collective variables that enhance sampling of protein conformational transitions and associated free energy surfaces . These findings highlight the importance of incorporating physical insights into generative models to improve their performance and accuracy.

In summary, while diffusion models have shown great promise in protein structure prediction and conformation generation, integrating physical supervision remains a critical challenge. ### <a id='category-protein-conformational-dynamics-sampling'></a>Protein Conformational Dynamics Sampling: 8 papers found.
Protein conformational dynamics sampling has seen significant advancements with the integration of deep learning and molecular dynamics (MD) data. Recent approaches like ConfRover \[[Yuning Shen' 2025-05-23](http://arxiv.org/abs/2505.17478v2)\] and ProtScape \[[Kevin E. Wu' 2022-09-30](http://arxiv.org/abs/2209.15611v2)\] aim to address the limitations of traditional MD simulations by leveraging generative models to efficiently explore conformational spaces. ConfRover introduces an autoregressive model that simultaneously learns protein conformation and dynamics, supporting both time-dependent and time-independent sampling through a modular architecture involving an encoding layer, a temporal module, and an SE(3) diffusion model \[[Yuning Shen' 2025-05-23](http://arxiv.org/abs/2505.17478v2)\]. ProtScape, on the other hand, utilizes a generative geometric deep learning framework that learns structure and energy-aware representations from MD simulations, enabling ensemble generation, minimum-energy path finding, and energy-descent trajectories \[[Kevin E. Wu' 2022-09-30](http://arxiv.org/abs/2209.15611v2)\]. Additionally, methods such as stepwise assembly (SWA) \[[Rhiju Das' 2012-08-02](http://arxiv.org/abs/1208.2680v2)\] focus on enumerative sampling of protein loops, achieving high accuracy in predicting conformations with sub-Angstrom precision. These approaches highlight the growing trend of combining physical insights with data-driven models to enhance the accuracy and efficiency of protein conformation prediction and sampling.
