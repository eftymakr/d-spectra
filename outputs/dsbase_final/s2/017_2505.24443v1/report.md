## Related Works

Semi-supervised learning (SSL) has been a focal point in machine learning research due to its ability to leverage unlabeled data to improve model performance with minimal labeled samples \[[Kuniaki Saito' 2021-05-28](http://arxiv.org/abs/2105.14148v2)\]. However, conventional SSL methods typically assume that the labeled and unlabeled data share the same class distribution, which is often violated in practice due to the presence of outliers in the unlabeled data \[[Yue Fan' 2023-11-17](http://arxiv.org/abs/2311.10572v1)\]. These outliers, which belong to unseen classes, can significantly degrade the performance of SSL models \[[Jongjin Park' 2021-06-29](http://arxiv.org/abs/2107.08943v3)\].

Several studies have attempted to address this issue by introducing open-set semi-supervised learning (OSSL) frameworks that explicitly account for the presence of outliers \[[Zekun Li' 2023-08-25](http://arxiv.org/abs/2308.13168v1)\]. For instance, OpenMatch \[[Yang Yu' 2023-03-21](http://arxiv.org/abs/2303.12091v4)\] integrates novelty detection with consistency regularization to distinguish between inliers and outliers. Similarly, IOMatch \[[Zerun Wang' 2023-05-23](http://arxiv.org/abs/2305.13802v3)\] proposes a framework that jointly utilizes inliers and outliers during training, aiming to improve robustness in the presence of out-of-distribution samples. These methods typically rely on a single model trained on labeled data to detect outliers, which can be ineffective when labeled data is insufficient \[[Yang Yang' 2024-05-11](http://arxiv.org/abs/2405.06979v2)\].

Another line of research focuses on improving the robustness of SSL by incorporating multiple models with different biases toward the unlabeled data distribution \[[Erik Wallin' 2023-01-24](http://arxiv.org/abs/2301.10127v3)\]. For example, FreeMatch \[[Yen-Cheng Liu' 2022-08-29](http://arxiv.org/abs/2208.13722v1)\] introduces a self-adaptive thresholding mechanism to improve the utilization of unlabeled data, while SelfMatch \[[Zhe Huang' 2024-03-15](http://arxiv.org/abs/2403.10658v1)\] combines contrastive self-supervision with consistency regularization to enhance performance. These approaches, however, often fail to effectively leverage the diversity of predictions from multiple models when dealing with outliers \[[Dan Hendrycks' 2018-12-11](http://arxiv.org/abs/1812.04606v3)\].

In contrast, our proposed framework, Diversify and Conquer (DAC), constructs a collection of differently biased models through a single training process. This approach exploits prediction disagreements among these models to identify unknown concepts, offering a novel solution to the challenges posed by open-set SSL \[[Yan Luo' 2022-01-23](http://arxiv.org/abs/2201.09196v2)\]. The method encourages divergent heads to be biased towards outliers while maintaining consistent predictions for inliers, thereby improving robustness against outliers \[[Riccardo Presotto' 2021-04-15](http://arxiv.org/abs/2104.08094v3)\].

Existing methods often struggle with the degradation of performance when labeled data is insufficient, leading to worse results than naive SSL approaches that ignore outliers \[[Jianfei Yang' 2022-05-28](http://arxiv.org/abs/2205.14467v1)\]. By leveraging the discrepancies arising from training on unlabeled data, DAC enables robust outlier detection even with underspecified labeled data \[[Phi Vu Tran' 2019-06-25](http://arxiv.org/abs/1906.10343v2)\]. This approach is particularly relevant in scenarios where labeled data is scarce or unreliable \[[Zhanyun Lu' 2024-08-22](http://arxiv.org/abs/2408.12355v1)\].

### <a id='category-open-set-ssl-frameworks'></a>Open-Set SSL Frameworks: 15 papers found.
Open-set semi-supervised learning (OSSL) addresses the challenge where unlabeled data may contain outliers from unseen classes, which traditional SSL methods fail to handle effectively \[[Kuniaki Saito' 2021-05-28](http://arxiv.org/abs/2105.14148v2)\]. Existing approaches often rely on outlier detection mechanisms or assume that outliers are harmful and should be excluded \[[Yue Fan' 2023-11-17](http://arxiv.org/abs/2311.10572v1)\]. For instance, methods like OpenMatch integrate novelty detection with FixMatch to identify inliers and reject outliers \[[Kuniaki Saito' 2021-05-28](http://arxiv.org/abs/2105.14148v2)\], while SSB employs pseudo-negative mining and non-linear transformations to improve both inlier classification and outlier detection \[[Yue Fan' 2023-11-17](http://arxiv.org/abs/2311.10572v1)\]. Similarly, OpenCoS leverages self-supervised contrastive learning to identify out-of-class samples and uses one-hot and soft-labels for inlier and outlier data respectively \[[Jongjin Park' 2021-06-29](http://arxiv.org/abs/2107.08943v3)\]. IOMatch, on the other hand, jointly utilizes inliers and outliers by introducing a multi-binary classifier that treats all outliers as a single class, enhancing performance even with scarce labels \[[Zekun Li' 2023-08-25](http://arxiv.org/abs/2308.13168v1)\]. ANEDL improves scalability and uncertainty distinction by employing evidential deep learning to quantify different types of uncertainty \[[Yang Yu' 2023-03-21](http://arxiv.org/abs/2303.12091v4)\]. In the context of object detection, OSSOD methods like the one proposed in \[[Zerun Wang' 2023-05-23](http://arxiv.org/abs/2305.13802v3)\] introduce online outlier filtering and a threshold-free dual competing OOD head to enhance performance and efficiency. Additionally, WiseOpen proposes a data selection strategy based on gradient variance to improve ID classification by leveraging a subset of open-set data \[[Yang Yang' 2024-05-11](http://arxiv.org/abs/2405.06979v2)\]. These methods collectively highlight the importance of robust outlier detection, adaptive learning strategies, and efficient utilization of open-set data to improve SSL performance in realistic scenarios.
