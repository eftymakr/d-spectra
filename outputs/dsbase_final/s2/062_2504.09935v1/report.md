## Related Works

Generative retrieval has emerged as a promising paradigm that replaces traditional search index data structures with a single large-scale neural network, enabling end-to-end retrieval by directly generating document identifiers through constrained decoding \[[Yu-An Liu' 2023-06-22](http://arxiv.org/abs/2306.12756v1)\]. This approach leverages the capabilities of large language models (LLMs) to perform retrieval tasks without relying on pre-built indexes, thereby offering potential improvements in efficiency and seamless integration with generative models \[[Yu-An Liu' 2023-06-22](http://arxiv.org/abs/2306.12756v1)\]. Several recent works have explored various aspects of generative retrieval, including decoding strategies, constraint handling, and generalization capabilities.

One line of research focuses on improving the efficiency of auto-regressive decoding in generative retrieval. For example, **Speculative Decoding** has been introduced to accelerate the generation process by predicting multiple tokens at each step and accepting them based on a probabilistic criterion . This approach reduces the number of generation steps and maintains the diversity of outputs. Similarly, **Nonparametric Decoding (Np Decoding)** has been proposed to enhance the performance of generative retrieval models by leveraging contextualized vocab embeddings as decoder vocab embeddings \[[Marwah Alaofi' 2024-04-11](http://arxiv.org/abs/2404.08137v3)\]. This method allows the model to utilize both parametric and nonparametric spaces, leading to improved performance across various datasets.

Another important aspect of generative retrieval is the handling of constraints during decoding. **Constrained decoding** has been studied in the context of language models, where the goal is to enforce user-defined constraints on the generated output . Techniques such as **Discrete Auto-regressive Biasing** have been proposed to address this challenge by operating entirely in the discrete text domain and leveraging gradients for controlled text generation . These methods aim to balance fluency with constraint satisfaction, ensuring that the generated output adheres to the specified constraints without compromising quality.

The role of beam search in generative retrieval has also been extensively studied. **Beam search** is commonly used in decoding auto-regressive models, but its effectiveness in generative retrieval has been questioned. For instance, **LANTERN++** has been proposed to enhance relaxed speculative decoding by integrating static tree drafting with a tailored relaxed acceptance condition, allowing for deeper sequences and improved decoding efficiency . Additionally, **Incremental Blockwise Beam Search** has been introduced to enable simultaneous speech translation with controllable quality-latency tradeoffs, demonstrating the flexibility of beam search in different applications .

In terms of generalization capabilities, **Generative Retrieval with Few-shot Indexing** has been proposed to overcome the limitations of training-based indexing by using a few-shot indexing process without any training \[[Yedan Shen' 2025-03-27](http://arxiv.org/abs/2503.21098v3)\]. This approach prompts an LLM to generate docids for all documents in a corpus, creating a docid bank for efficient retrieval. Furthermore, **CARROT** has been introduced as a cost-constrained retrieval optimization framework for Retrieval-Augmented Generation (RAG), addressing challenges such as chunk retrieval, utility non-monotonicity, and query-specific adaptation \[[Hyunji Lee' 2022-10-05](http://arxiv.org/abs/2210.02068v3)\].

The robustness of generative retrieval models has also been a topic of recent research. **On the Robustness of Generative Retrieval Models: An Out-of-Distribution Perspective** highlights the importance of measuring the out-of-distribution (OOD) generalization of generative retrieval models, as they are deployed in real-world applications . The study defines OOD robustness from three perspectives: query variations, unforeseen query types, and unforeseen tasks. It further suggests that generative retrieval models require improvement in their OOD robustness to ensure reliable performance in diverse settings.

In addition, **Lightweight and Direct Document Relevance Optimization for Generative Information Retrieval** proposes a method called **Direct Document Relevance Optimization (DDRO)**, which aligns token-level docid generation with document-level relevance estimation through direct optimization via pairwise ranking \[[Arian Askari' 2024-08-04](http://arxiv.org/abs/2408.02152v3)\]. This approach eliminates the need for explicit reward modeling and reinforcement learning, simplifying the optimization pipeline and improving performance on benchmark datasets.

These works collectively highlight the ongoing efforts to improve the efficiency, effectiveness, and generalization capabilities of generative retrieval systems. They also emphasize the importance of addressing constraints and decoding strategies to ensure that generative retrieval models can perform well across diverse tasks and datasets.
