## Related Works

The task of discovering relevant tables from large repositories of tabular data has gained significant attention in recent years, driven by the increasing availability of structured data in various domains. Traditional approaches to table discovery primarily rely on query tables or vague keywords to retrieve candidate tables, often leading to large result sets that require manual filtering by users \[[Hugo Manuel Proença' 2021-03-25](http://arxiv.org/abs/2103.13686v4)\]. This manual filtering process is inefficient and limits the scalability of existing methods, especially when dealing with complex and diverse data sources. For instance, the work of Zhang et al. \[[Paul Brillant Feuto Njonko' 2014-06-09](http://arxiv.org/abs/1406.2096v1)\] highlights the limitations of keyword-based approaches in IoT data discovery, where the lack of semantic understanding leads to suboptimal results. Similarly, the study by Zhang et al. \[[Ondřej Dušek' 2017-08-05](http://arxiv.org/abs/1708.01759v1)\] on deformed relativistic Hartree-Bogoliubov theory in continuum demonstrates the importance of accurate data representation in scientific contexts, which is critical for effective table discovery.

Recent efforts have aimed to improve the precision and efficiency of table discovery by incorporating natural language (NL) requirements into the search process. This approach, known as NL-conditional table discovery (nlcTD), allows users to combine a query table with NL conditions to refine search results \[[Yiwu Zhong' 2024-03-27](http://arxiv.org/abs/2403.18252v2)\]. This paradigm shift aligns with broader trends in natural language processing (NLP), where the integration of language understanding into data retrieval tasks has shown promising results \[[Dayu Yang' 2024-11-04](http://arxiv.org/abs/2411.03356v1)\]. For instance, the use of NLP techniques in table structure recognition has enabled more accurate and context-aware retrieval of tabular data \[[William Watson' 2024-06-16](http://arxiv.org/abs/2406.10803v1)\]. The work by Zhang et al.  on the discovery of the $D$-basis in binary tables based on hypergraph dualization provides a foundational approach to integrating semantic understanding into table discovery tasks.

Several datasets have been introduced to benchmark and evaluate table discovery methods. The TabLeX dataset, for example, provides a large-scale benchmark for extracting both structural and content information from scientific tables . This dataset includes table images generated from scientific articles, along with their corresponding LaTeX source code, and is designed to facilitate the development of robust table information extraction tools. Similarly, the Open Graph Benchmark (OGB) offers a diverse set of challenging datasets for graph machine learning, highlighting the importance of benchmarking in advancing research . The nlcTables dataset, introduced by Zhang et al. , represents a significant contribution by introducing a comprehensive benchmark that includes 627 diverse queries spanning NL-only, union, join, and fuzzy conditions, along with 22,200 candidate tables and 21,200 relevance annotations. This dataset is designed to evaluate the performance of existing methods and highlight the need for more advanced techniques to address the challenges of this task. The evaluation of six state-of-the-art table discovery methods on nlcTables revealed substantial performance gaps, underscoring the need for further research in this area .

[Snippet]: «We introduce the problem of robust subgroup discovery, i.e., finding a set of interpretable descriptions of subsets that 1) stand out with respect to one or more target attributes, 2) are statistically robust, and 3) non-redundant. Many attempts have been made to mine either locally robust subgroups or to tackle the pattern explosion, but we are the first to address both challenges at the same time from a global modelling perspective. First, we formulate the broad model class of subgroup lists, i.e., ordered sets of subgroups, for univariate and multivariate targets that can consist of nominal or numeric variables, including traditional top-1 subgroup discovery in its definition. This novel model class allows us to formalise the problem of optimal robust subgroup discovery using the Minimum Description Length (MDL) principle, where we resort to optimal Normalised Maximum Likelihood and Bayesian encodings for nominal and numeric targets, respectively. Second, finding optimal subgroup lists is NP-hard. Therefore, we propose SSD++, a greedy heuristic that finds good subgroup lists and guarantees that the most significant subgroup found according to the MDL criterion is added in each iteration. In fact, the greedy gain is shown to be equivalent to a Bayesian one-sample proportion, multinomial, or t-test between the subgroup and dataset marginal target distributions plus a multiple hypothesis testing penalty. Furthermore, we empirically show on 54 datasets that SSD++ outperforms previous subgroup discovery methods in terms of quality, generalisation on unseen data, and subgroup list size.»
