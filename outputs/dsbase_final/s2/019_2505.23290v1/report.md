## Related Works

Speech-driven 3D facial animation has attracted significant attention in recent years, with researchers focusing on improving the realism and expressiveness of generated facial movements. Early approaches primarily relied on deterministic deep learning models, which often failed to capture the inherent stochasticity of facial expressions \[[Hui Fu' 2023-12-18](http://arxiv.org/abs/2312.10877v1)\]. These models typically produced consistent outputs for the same input, limiting their ability to generate diverse and natural facial animations. More recent methods have explored the use of pre-trained self-supervised audio models as encoders to extract meaningful audio features \[[Zhaojie Chu' 2023-10-17](http://arxiv.org/abs/2310.11295v1)\]. These models have demonstrated the ability to capture the temporal dynamics of speech, but they often suffer from the averaging effect when dealing with phonetically similar syllables that produce distinct lip shapes \[[Tianshun Han' 2023-12-05](http://arxiv.org/abs/2312.02781v1)\]. This issue is exacerbated by the coupling of near-homophone syllables in self-supervised audio feature spaces, leading to less expressive and less accurate lip motion generation \[[Jinbo Xing' 2023-01-06](http://arxiv.org/abs/2301.02379v2)\].

To address these limitations, several studies have proposed methods to enhance the semantic richness of audio encodings. For instance, \[[Yingruo Fan' 2021-12-04](http://arxiv.org/abs/2112.02214v2)\] introduced a method that leverages linguistic information to improve the alignment between audio and visual features. Similarly, \[[Chunzhi Gu' 2024-09-27](http://arxiv.org/abs/2409.19143v1)\] proposed a framework that integrates both audio and visual modalities to better capture the nuances of speech-driven facial animation. These approaches aim to disentangle the semantic content from the acoustic signal, enabling more accurate and expressive facial motion synthesis. However, they often require extensive training data and are not easily adaptable to new or unseen speech patterns \[[Guinan Su' 2023-11-08](http://arxiv.org/abs/2311.04766v2)\].

Another line of research has focused on the use of diffusion models for speech-driven facial animation \[[Konstantinos Vougioukas' 2018-05-23](http://arxiv.org/abs/1805.09313v4)\]. These models have demonstrated the ability to generate diverse and realistic facial expressions by learning from large-scale datasets. However, they often face challenges in maintaining temporal coherence and lip synchronization, particularly when dealing with complex speech patterns \[[Ingmar Steiner' 2012-09-22](http://arxiv.org/abs/1209.4982v1)\]. To overcome these challenges, \[[Sichun Wu' 2024-09-12](http://arxiv.org/abs/2409.07966v4)\] proposed a lightweight audio-conditioned diffusion model that can be trained on small datasets, making it more accessible for practical applications.

In addition to these approaches, \[[Xiangyu Liang' 2024-04-29](http://arxiv.org/abs/2404.18604v1)\] introduced a method that uses a joint audio-text model to capture contextual information for expressive speech-driven facial animation. This approach leverages pre-trained language models to disambiguate upper face expressions, which are not strongly correlated with the audio signal \[[Yingruo Fan' 2021-12-10](http://arxiv.org/abs/2112.05329v4)\]. This method has shown promising results in improving the realism and accuracy of facial animations, particularly in scenarios where the audio signal is ambiguous or contains multiple speakers \[[Haozhe Wu' 2023-08-10](http://arxiv.org/abs/2308.05428v1)\].

The proposed Wav2Sem module builds upon these advancements by introducing a plug-and-play semantic decorrelation module that enhances the expressiveness of audio features. This module leverages semantic information to decorrelate audio encodings, thereby mitigating the averaging effect caused by phonetically similar syllables \[[Stefan Stan' 2023-09-20](http://arxiv.org/abs/2309.11306v1)\]. This approach is inspired by earlier work on semantic audio-visual alignment \[[Liyang Chen' 2023-10-11](http://arxiv.org/abs/2310.07236v4)\], which demonstrated the importance of incorporating linguistic information to improve the accuracy of audio-visual synchronization.

[Snippet]: «Audio-aware large language models (ALLMs) have recently made great strides in understanding and processing audio inputs. These models are typically adapted from text-based large language models (LLMs) through additional training on audio-related tasks. This adaptation process presents two major limitations. First, ALLMs often suffer from catastrophic forgetting, where crucial textual capabilities like instruction-following are lost after training on audio data. In some cases, models may even hallucinate sounds that are not present in the input audio, raising concerns about reliability. Second, achieving cross-modal alignment between audio and language typically relies on large collections of task-specific question-answer pairs for instruction tuning, making it resource-intensive. To address these issues, previous works have leveraged the backbone LLMs to synthesize general-purpose, caption-style alignment data. In this paper, we propose a data generation framework that produces contrastive-like training data, designed to enhance ALLMs' ability to differentiate between present and absent sounds. We further extend our approach to multi-audio scenarios, enabling the model to either explain differences between audio inputs or produce unified captions that describe all inputs, thereby enhancing audio-language alignment. We refer to the entire ALLM training framework as bootstrapping audio-language alignment via synthetic data generation from backbone LLMs (BALSa). Experimental results indicate that our method effectively mitigates audio hallucinations while reliably maintaining strong performance on audio understanding and reasoning benchmarks, as well as instruction-following skills. Moreover, incorporating multi-audio training further enhances the model's comprehension and reasoning capabilities. Overall, BALSa offers an efficient and scalable approach to developing ALLMs.»
