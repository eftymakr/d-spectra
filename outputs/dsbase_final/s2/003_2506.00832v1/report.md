## Related Works

Recent advancements in Text-to-Speech (TTS) systems have significantly enhanced speech naturalness, leading to increased demands for precise prosody control and mispronunciation correction . Traditional approaches for prosody manipulation often rely on specialized modules or additional training, which limits their capacity for post-hoc adjustments . Similarly, conventional mispronunciation correction methods depend on grapheme-to-phoneme dictionaries, making them less practical in low-resource settings . This section reviews related works that address these challenges, focusing on methods for prosody control, mispronunciation correction, and post-hoc TTS adjustments.

Several studies have explored prosody control in TTS systems. For instance, \[[Chunfeng Wang' 2023-03-02](http://arxiv.org/abs/2303.01086v1)\] proposes a TTS architecture that adds utterance-level prosody control to an existing neural HMM-based TTS system, enabling stable, monotonic alignments for spontaneous speech. This work demonstrates that prosody control does not degrade synthesis quality, as evaluated through perceptual tests. Similarly, \[[Manuel Sam Ribeiro' 2023-07-31](http://arxiv.org/abs/2307.16643v1)\] introduces a non-autoregressive parallel neural TTS front-end model hierarchically conditioned on acoustic speech features to learn a latent prosody space with intuitive dimensions. This model enables fine-grained control over prosody dimensions and generates a wide variety of speaking styles while maintaining quality comparable to baseline models.

Mispronunciation correction in TTS has also been a focus of recent research.  highlights the limitations of traditional methods that rely on grapheme-to-phoneme dictionaries, particularly in low-resource scenarios. In contrast,  explores the manipulation of prosodic parameters in TTS systems to achieve controlled speech generation, comparing TTS-generated audio with human-recorded speech to analyze differences in pitch, duration, and energy. This work demonstrates that by adjusting key features using tools like PyWorld and Librosa, TTS outputs can more closely mirror natural human speech prosody.

Post-hoc adjustments in TTS have been addressed in several studies. \[[Seungtaek Kim' 2025-03-25](http://arxiv.org/abs/2503.19228v4)\] presents CopyCat2, a model capable of synthesizing speech with different speaker identities and transferring prosody at a fine-grained level between any pair of seen speakers. In addition, \[[Lothar Lemnitzer' 2009-12-15](http://arxiv.org/abs/0912.2881v2)\] discusses the challenges of individual risk in mean-field control models for decentralized control, highlighting the importance of robustness and reliability in control systems. While not directly related to TTS, this work underscores the broader importance of controllability and robustness in systems that require post-hoc adjustments.

Finally,  presents Nix-TTS, a lightweight TTS model achieved via knowledge distillation, which enables efficient inference while retaining voice naturalness and intelligibility. This work highlights the potential for lightweight models to support post-hoc adjustments in TTS systems.
