Nonverbal communication plays a crucial role in human interaction, encompassing gestures, facial expressions, and body language that convey intent and emotion \[[Leena Mathur' 2020-08-31](http://arxiv.org/abs/2008.13369v1)\]. Despite its significance, existing large language models (LLMs) have limited capacity to incorporate nonverbal elements, thereby restricting their ability to generate fully immersive conversational experiences \[[Leena Mathur' 2020-08-31](http://arxiv.org/abs/2008.13369v1)\]. This section reviews related works that have addressed nonverbal communication in the context of multimodal language models, datasets, and their integration into conversational AI.

Several studies have explored the integration of nonverbal cues into language models. For instance, \[[Leena Mathur' 2020-08-31](http://arxiv.org/abs/2008.13369v1)\] discusses the challenges of identifying cause-effect relationships in dyadic communication, emphasizing the transient and variant nature of nonverbal signals. This work highlights the importance of facial expressions and the use of Granger causality for causal inference, which aligns with the need for models to understand and generate nonverbal cues \[[Leena Mathur' 2020-08-31](http://arxiv.org/abs/2008.13369v1)\]. Additionally, \[[Laura Hanu' 2023-09-19](http://arxiv.org/abs/2309.10783v1)\] introduces the concept of Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs), which leverage powerful LLMs to perform multimodal tasks, such as writing stories based on images and OCR-free math reasoning.
