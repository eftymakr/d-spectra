## Related Works

Collaborative filtering has evolved significantly, with a growing emphasis on efficient representation learning and binarization techniques to optimize memory and computational efficiency. Traditional methods rely on dense vector representations for users and items, which, while effective, can be computationally intensive and memory-heavy \[[Hao-Ming Fu' 2022-03-29](http://arxiv.org/abs/2203.15789v1)\]. To address these limitations, recent research has explored binarization techniques that convert real-valued embeddings into compact binary representations, offering substantial gains in efficiency \[[Haoyu Wang' 2019-06-05](http://arxiv.org/abs/1906.01829v1)\]. However, these approaches often neglect the associated information loss, which can lead to performance degradation \[[Zihan Lin' 2022-02-13](http://arxiv.org/abs/2202.06200v2)\].

Several studies have investigated the use of graph-based collaborative filtering, where user-item interactions are modeled as graphs to capture complex relationships \[[Weijun Chen' 2024-08-05](http://arxiv.org/abs/2408.02404v1)\]. These methods leverage graph neural networks (GNNs) to propagate information across the graph, enabling more accurate and efficient recommendations \[[Osama Alshareet' 2023-12-05](http://arxiv.org/abs/2312.03167v1)\]. However, the challenge of preserving information during binarization remains a critical issue, as the loss of precision can negatively impact model performance \[[Jianing Sun' 2020-01-01](http://arxiv.org/abs/2001.00267v1)\].

In the context of binarization, some works have focused on minimizing information loss by incorporating additional constraints or using more sophisticated quantization strategies \[[Jiahao Liu' 2023-02-04](http://arxiv.org/abs/2302.02113v1)\]. For instance, one study proposed a method that integrates both real item data and latent embedding samples to generate pseudo-positive samples, which are then used to refine the binarization process \[[Xiang Wang' 2020-07-03](http://arxiv.org/abs/2007.01764v1)\]. This approach has shown promising results in mitigating the information loss inherent in binary representations \[[Minzhe Niu' 2018-03-09](http://arxiv.org/abs/1803.03502v1)\].

Another line of research has explored the use of variational inference and stochastic gradient descent to optimize the binarization process, ensuring that the resulting binary embeddings retain as much information as possible \[[Yijie Zhang' 2023-11-12](http://arxiv.org/abs/2311.06777v1)\]. These methods aim to balance the trade-off between computational efficiency and model accuracy, which is crucial for real-time recommendation systems \[[Xiang Wang' 2019-05-20](http://arxiv.org/abs/1905.08108v2)\].

The work presented in this paper builds upon these advancements by proposing BiGeaR++, an enhanced framework that specifically addresses the issue of information loss during binarization. By incorporating supervisory signals from pseudo-positive samples and introducing a fine-grained inference distillation mechanism, BiGeaR++ achieves significant improvements over its predecessor, BiGeaR \[[Nikolaos Polatidis' 2018-04-24](http://arxiv.org/abs/1804.08891v1)\]. The proposed approach not only mitigates information loss but also enhances the overall performance of collaborative filtering systems \[[Zhang Junlin' 2015-02-14](http://arxiv.org/abs/1502.04163v1)\].

### <a id='category-graph-based-collaborative-filtering'></a>Graph-Based Collaborative Filtering: 13 papers found.
Graph-based collaborative filtering has emerged as a powerful approach in recommendation systems, leveraging user-item interaction graphs to capture complex relationships and improve recommendation accuracy \[[Hao-Ming Fu' 2022-03-29](http://arxiv.org/abs/2203.15789v1)\]. Recent works have focused on enhancing model performance by incorporating advanced graph neural network (GNN) techniques, such as LightGCN \[[Yijie Zhang' 2023-11-12](http://arxiv.org/abs/2311.06777v1)\] and NGCF , which effectively model user and item representations through message passing on bipartite graphs. However, these methods often neglect the link prediction aspect, which is fundamental to item recommendation \[[Hao-Ming Fu' 2022-03-29](http://arxiv.org/abs/2203.15789v1)\]. To address this, researchers have proposed novel approaches that explicitly model link prediction, such as the introduction of a new connectivity score for bipartite graphs \[[Hao-Ming Fu' 2022-03-29](http://arxiv.org/abs/2203.15789v1)\], which outperforms existing GNN-based methods by exploiting local graph structures without deep learning. Additionally, contrastive learning has been integrated into graph collaborative filtering to enhance performance by considering both structural and semantic neighbors \[[Zihan Lin' 2022-02-13](http://arxiv.org/abs/2202.06200v2)\]. This is further extended by methods like Neighborhood-enriched Contrastive Learning (NCL), which explicitly incorporates potential neighbors into contrastive pairs, leading to significant performance gains on benchmark datasets \[[Zihan Lin' 2022-02-13](http://arxiv.org/abs/2202.06200v2)\]. Another challenge in collaborative filtering is the cold-start problem, which is mitigated by spectral graph wavelet collaborative filtering, which uses adaptive transfer functions to stabilize graph frequencies and improve recommendation quality \[[Weijun Chen' 2024-08-05](http://arxiv.org/abs/2408.02404v1)\]. Furthermore, models like Multi-Graph Convolution Collaborative Filtering (Multi-GCCF) integrate multiple graphs—user-user, item-item, and user-item—to capture high-order interactions and proximal information, leading to more accurate and discriminative embeddings \[[Osama Alshareet' 2023-12-05](http://arxiv.org/abs/2312.03167v1)\]. The issue of recommending unfascinating items is also addressed by Feedback Reciprocal Graph Collaborative Filtering (FRGCF), which partitions the interaction graph into fascinated and unfascinated subgraphs to enhance recommendation quality \[[Zihan Lin' 2022-02-13](http://arxiv.org/abs/2202.06200v2)\]. Overall, these works highlight the importance of graph-based methods in collaborative filtering, emphasizing the need for models that effectively capture both structural and semantic information while addressing key challenges such as data sparsity and cold-start.
