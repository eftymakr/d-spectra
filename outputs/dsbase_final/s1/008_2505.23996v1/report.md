## Related Works

The rapid adoption of large language models (LLMs) has underscored the critical need for fairness benchmarking, as these models increasingly influence high-stakes decisions. Traditional fairness metrics, such as Equalized Odds, focus on discrete accuracy-based evaluations and fail to capture the implicit impact of model uncertainty, such as higher confidence in predictions for certain groups despite similar accuracy \[[Haozhe An' 2025-03-09](http://arxiv.org/abs/2503.06792v1)\]. This limitation highlights a gap in current fairness assessment methodologies, which do not account for the internal biases that may influence model decisions \[[Haozhe An' 2025-03-09](http://arxiv.org/abs/2503.06792v1)\].

Several studies have explored fairness in LLMs, but most focus on group-level fairness without considering the nuanced impact of model confidence. For instance, \[[Haozhe An' 2025-03-09](http://arxiv.org/abs/2503.06792v1)\] discusses the limitations of conventional fairness metrics in capturing model uncertainty, emphasizing the need for more refined approaches. Similarly, \[[Elisa Forcada Rodríguez' 2025-05-05](http://arxiv.org/abs/2505.02456v2)\] evaluates gender, racial, and age disparities in LLMs, revealing that debiasing efforts often create new fairness trade-offs. These findings align with the need for uncertainty-aware fairness metrics that better reflect model behavior \[[Haozhe An' 2025-03-09](http://arxiv.org/abs/2503.06792v1)\].

The evaluation of fairness in LLMs has also been addressed through the development of specialized datasets. \[[Elisa Forcada Rodríguez' 2025-05-05](http://arxiv.org/abs/2505.02456v2)\] introduces a dataset with 31,756 samples for co-reference resolution, offering a more diverse and suitable dataset for evaluating modern LLMs. This dataset is designed to address issues of data size, diversity, and clarity in current benchmarks. These studies underscore the need for comprehensive datasets that capture the complexity of fairness in LLMs.
