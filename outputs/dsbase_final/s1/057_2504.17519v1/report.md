## Related Works

Generative retrieval (GR) has gained significant attention in the field of information retrieval (IR) due to its ability to directly generate document identifiers (docids) from queries, enabling end-to-end retrieval without reliance on traditional index-based methods \[[Arian Askari' 2024-08-04](http://arxiv.org/abs/2408.02152v3)\]. However, most existing GR models are primarily evaluated on static document collections, with limited exploration of their performance in dynamic environments where document sets evolve over time \[[Chaeeun Kim' 2023-05-27](http://arxiv.org/abs/2305.18952v5)\]. This section reviews relevant works that address dynamic retrieval, docid design, and the challenges of generative retrieval in evolving corpora.

Several studies have investigated the limitations of traditional retrieval methods in dynamic settings. For instance, \[[Marwah Alaofi' 2024-04-11](http://arxiv.org/abs/2404.08137v3)\] highlights the challenges of maintaining retrieval effectiveness when document collections are constantly updated, emphasizing the need for models that can adapt to evolving knowledge. Similarly, \[[Ghulam Ahmed Ansari' 2018-05-17](http://arxiv.org/abs/1805.07274v1)\] introduces a continual learning framework for GR, demonstrating the importance of incremental indexing and memory-augmented learning to preserve retrieval performance as new documents are added. These works align with the findings of our study, which reveals that GR models with text-based docids outperform numeric-based ones in dynamic settings \[[Chaeeun Kim' 2023-05-27](http://arxiv.org/abs/2305.18952v5)\].

The design of docids plays a crucial role in the effectiveness of GR systems. \[[Pirouz Nourian' 2023-10-13](http://arxiv.org/abs/2310.09243v1)\] proposes a novel framework, Term-Set Generation (TSGen), where docids are represented as sets of terms rather than sequences, improving the robustness of retrieval by avoiding token-level pruning errors. This approach supports the observation that fine-grained docid design enhances performance in dynamic corpora \[[Chaeeun Kim' 2023-05-27](http://arxiv.org/abs/2305.18952v5)\]. Additionally,  introduces GenRet, a method for learning to tokenize documents into semantic docids, which aligns with the semantic alignment advantage of text-based docids in dynamic environments \[[Chaeeun Kim' 2023-05-27](http://arxiv.org/abs/2305.18952v5)\].

Efforts to improve retrieval effectiveness in dynamic settings have also focused on hybrid approaches. \[[Nick Brown' 2020-10-04](http://arxiv.org/abs/2010.01547v1)\] proposes Blended RAG, which combines semantic search and hybrid query strategies to enhance retrieval accuracy. While not directly addressing dynamic corpora, this work highlights the importance of diverse retrieval strategies in complex IR tasks. Similarly,  introduces CARROT, a cost-constrained retrieval optimization framework for RAG, demonstrating the value of adaptive retrieval strategies in handling evolving document sets.

The performance of GR models in dynamic environments has also been studied in the context of real-time applications.  evaluates the performance portability of a SYCL implementation of a cosmology application, showing that portable programming models like SYCL can maintain high performance across different hardware architectures. While not directly related to GR, this work underscores the importance of adaptability in retrieval systems.

In summary, prior works have explored the challenges of dynamic retrieval, the impact of docid design on performance, and the need for adaptive retrieval strategies. Our study builds on these findings, demonstrating that text-based docids offer significant advantages in dynamic corpora while proposing a novel multi-docid design that balances efficiency and effectiveness.

It assigns each document a unique identifier, known as DocID, and employs a generative model to directly generate the relevant DocID for the input query. To address this problem, we present a novel framework for generative retrieval, dubbed Term-Set Generation (TSGen). Instead of sequences, we use a set of terms as DocID, which are automatically selected to concisely summarize the document's semantics and distinguish it from others. On top of the term-set DocID, we propose a permutation-invariant decoding algorithm, with which the term set can be generated in any permutation yet will always lead to the corresponding document. Remarkably, TSGen perceives all valid terms rather than only the preceding ones at each decoding step. Given the constant decoding space, it can make more reliable decisions due to the broader perspective. TSGen is also resilient to errors: the relevant DocID will not be pruned as long as the decoded term belongs to it. Lastly, we design an iterative optimization procedure to incentivize the model to generate the relevant term set in its favorable permutation. We conduct extensive experiments on popular benchmarks, which validate the effectiveness, the generalizability, the scalability, and the efficiency of TSGen.»
