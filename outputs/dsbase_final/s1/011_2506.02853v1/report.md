## Related Works

The task of 3D human pose estimation has been extensively studied, with a growing focus on modeling long-range dependencies between body joints to improve accuracy and robustness. Traditional approaches often rely on 2D-to-3D mapping, where 2D joint locations are lifted to 3D space using regression or optimization techniques \[[Ce Zheng' 2021-03-18](http://arxiv.org/abs/2103.10455v3)\]. However, these methods typically struggle with capturing the complex spatial relationships inherent in human motion, particularly when dealing with occlusions or ambiguous configurations \[[Yang Liu' 2024-07-14](http://arxiv.org/abs/2407.10099v2)\].

Recent advances have introduced graph-based models that explicitly model the human skeleton as a graph, enabling more accurate representation of spatial relationships \[[Ti Wang' 2023-04-27](http://arxiv.org/abs/2304.14045v1)\]. For example, the work by \[[Jianning Quan' 2021-11-01](http://arxiv.org/abs/2111.00950v1)\] proposed a graph convolutional network (GCN) to model the human skeleton, allowing for the capture of both local and global joint relationships. However, these models often require deep architectures to learn long-range dependencies, leading to increased computational complexity and the introduction of uncorrelated noise \[[Kamel Aouaidjia' 2025-05-02](http://arxiv.org/abs/2505.01003v1)\]. This issue is further exacerbated by the need to model both temporal and spatial dependencies in video-based 3D pose estimation \[[Guangsheng Xu' 2024-12-27](http://arxiv.org/abs/2412.19676v1)\].

To address these limitations, several works have explored the use of transformers for 3D human pose estimation. The Transformer-based approach in \[[Helge Rhodin' 2018-03-13](http://arxiv.org/abs/1803.04775v2)\] introduced a multi-head self-attention mechanism to capture long-range dependencies across joints, but it lacked explicit modeling of the hierarchical structure of the human body. Similarly, \[[Dushyant Mehta' 2017-12-09](http://arxiv.org/abs/1712.03453v3)\] proposed a graph transformer network that incorporated spatial and temporal attention, but it did not fully exploit the multi-scale nature of human motion. These models often require large amounts of training data and are sensitive to occlusions and viewpoint changes \[[Qiang Nie' 2020-07-14](http://arxiv.org/abs/2007.07053v2)\].

In contrast, the proposed Pyramid Graph Transformer (PGFormer) leverages a pyramid structure to better capture long-range dependencies across different scales. This approach is inspired by the success of pyramid networks in image processing, which enable the modeling of multi-scale features \[[Gyeongsik Moon' 2017-11-20](http://arxiv.org/abs/1711.07399v3)\]. By combining a pyramid structure with a graph attention module, PGFormer effectively captures cross-scale dependencies and reduces the need for deep networks, leading to a more compact and efficient architecture \[[Dushyant Mehta' 2016-11-29](http://arxiv.org/abs/1611.09813v5)\].

The use of graph attention modules has also been explored in other works. For instance, \[[Mona Fathollahi Ghezelghieh' 2016-09-18](http://arxiv.org/abs/1609.05522v1)\] introduced a graph attention spatio-temporal convolutional network (GAST-Net) that integrates spatial and temporal information to improve pose estimation accuracy. However, this approach primarily focuses on local joint relationships and does not explicitly model the hierarchical structure of the human body \[[Laura Bragagnolo' 2024-08-28](http://arxiv.org/abs/2408.15810v1)\]. Similarly, \[[Keze Wang' 2019-01-12](http://arxiv.org/abs/1901.03798v2)\] proposed a multi-hop graph transformer network that captures long-range dependencies, but it does not incorporate a pyramid structure for multi-scale modeling \[[Dongqiangzi Ye' 2023-06-21](http://arxiv.org/abs/2306.12525v2)\].

In summary, while existing methods have made significant progress in modeling long-range dependencies for 3D human pose estimation, they often suffer from high computational costs, sensitivity to occlusions, and limited modeling of hierarchical body structures. The proposed PGFormer addresses these challenges by integrating a pyramid structure with a graph attention module, enabling more efficient and accurate modeling of long-range dependencies across different scales.
