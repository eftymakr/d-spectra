## Related Works

Differential privacy (DP) has been widely adopted as a formal framework for ensuring privacy in data analysis \[[Yucheng Fu' 2024-09-16](http://arxiv.org/abs/2409.10667v2)\]. However, its implementation is vulnerable to malicious behaviors by servers, especially in distributed settings, where servers may sample noise from incorrect distributions or generate correlated noise while appearing to follow established protocols \[[Ari Biswas' 2022-08-18](http://arxiv.org/abs/2208.09011v2)\]. This issue has been explored in several works that examine the robustness of DP mechanisms against adversarial execution \[[Mohammad Alaggan' 2015-04-27](http://arxiv.org/abs/1504.06998v1)\]. For instance, \[[Shiva Prasad Kasiviswanathan' 2008-03-27](http://arxiv.org/abs/0803.3946v4)\] discusses the challenges of ensuring correct noise sampling in distributed systems and highlights the need for verification mechanisms to prevent unfaithful execution. Similarly, \[[Jongmin Mun' 2024-11-13](http://arxiv.org/abs/2411.09064v2)\] explores the implications of incorrect noise generation and its impact on privacy guarantees.

The concept of verifiable differential privacy (VDP) has been introduced to address these concerns by incorporating verification mechanisms that ensure the correctness of DP mechanisms \[[Wenqiang Ruan' 2022-08-18](http://arxiv.org/abs/2208.08662v1)\]. In particular, \[[Jinshuo Dong' 2021-04-05](http://arxiv.org/abs/2104.01987v2)\] proposes a framework for verifiable DP that leverages zero-knowledge proofs (ZKPs) to confirm that the output of a differentially private mechanism is both private and reliable. However, \[[Bo Chen' 2022-11-30](http://arxiv.org/abs/2211.17230v1)\] argues that while ZKPs are sufficient for achieving verifiable DP, they are not necessary, as other verification techniques can also be used. This distinction is crucial for understanding the trade-offs between different verification approaches.

Several works have explored the relationship between ZKPs and DP, particularly in the context of verifiable mechanisms \[[Nirupam Gupta' 2018-09-06](http://arxiv.org/abs/1809.01794v3)\]. For example, \[[Tariq Bontekoe' 2024-06-27](http://arxiv.org/abs/2406.18940v2)\] demonstrates that ZKPs can be used to verify the correctness of DP mechanisms, ensuring that the noise added to the output is sampled from the correct distribution. However, \[[Damien Desfontaines' 2019-06-04](http://arxiv.org/abs/1906.01337v6)\] shows that ZKPs are not always required, as alternative verification methods can also be employed. This highlights the flexibility of verification approaches in the DP framework.

In the context of distributed systems, \[[Utsab Saha' 2024-11-25](http://arxiv.org/abs/2411.16121v3)\] proposes a verifiable DP mechanism that allows clients to verify the correctness of the noise added by servers. This work builds on earlier research that examined the feasibility of verifiable DP in distributed settings \[[Alexandra Kapp' 2024-07-04](http://arxiv.org/abs/2407.03732v1)\]. Additionally, \[[Wentao Hu' 2022-12-01](http://arxiv.org/abs/2212.00306v2)\] introduces a novel approach to verifiable DP that combines ZKPs with cryptographic techniques to ensure both privacy and correctness.

The efficiency of verifiable DP mechanisms has also been a focus of recent research. For example, \[[Genki Sugiura' 2021-06-24](http://arxiv.org/abs/2106.12749v1)\] presents a verifiable DP mechanism that significantly improves the efficiency of proof generation compared to previous state-of-the-art methods. This work demonstrates that the proposed mechanism achieves up to a $4 \times 10^5$x improvement in proof generation efficiency with only 0.1-0.2x error compared to existing methods \[[Terrence W. K. Mak' 2019-10-07](http://arxiv.org/abs/1910.04250v1)\]. Similarly, \[[Poushali Sengupta' 2020-06-10](http://arxiv.org/abs/2006.05609v2)\] introduces an improved solution to verifiable randomized response (VRR) under local DP, achieving up to a 5000x reduction in communication costs and the verifier's overhead.

These contributions highlight the ongoing efforts to enhance the practicality and efficiency of verifiable DP mechanisms. They also underscore the importance of verification in ensuring the correctness of DP implementations in distributed settings.
