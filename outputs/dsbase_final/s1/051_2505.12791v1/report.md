## Related Works

Federated Learning (FL) has emerged as a promising paradigm for training machine learning models on decentralized data while preserving user privacy \[[Nicolò Romandini' 2024-01-10](http://arxiv.org/abs/2401.05146v3)\]. However, the need for users to remove their data from training sets, often referred to as the "right to be forgotten," has spurred the development of unlearning methods within FL \[[Shuyi Wang' 2024-12-26](http://arxiv.org/abs/2412.19069v1)\]. This paper focuses on the effectiveness and efficiency of federated unlearning strategies within Federated Online Learning to Rank (FOLTR), a framework that optimizes search results using implicit feedback while safeguarding user privacy \[[Xiangshan Gao' 2022-05-25](http://arxiv.org/abs/2205.12709v1)\].

Unlearning in FL is a relatively new area of research, with most prior work focusing on centralized settings or specific types of models \[[Youming Tao' 2024-01-19](http://arxiv.org/abs/2401.11018v1)\]. Recent efforts have explored the use of influence functions to identify and remove the impact of specific data points \[[Hyejun Jeong' 2024-03-04](http://arxiv.org/abs/2403.02437v3)\]. However, these methods often require coordination with other clients, which is not feasible in distributed settings \[[Dongjae Jeon' 2024-05-28](http://arxiv.org/abs/2405.17878v3)\]. In contrast, this study evaluates unlearning strategies in a federated online learning to rank context, where models are continuously updated with new data \[[Yang Zhao' 2023-10-30](http://arxiv.org/abs/2310.19218v5)\].

Several approaches have been proposed to address the unlearning problem in FL. One notable method is the use of perturbed local embeddings to ensure data privacy and improve communication efficiency \[[Ruinan Jin' 2023-06-03](http://arxiv.org/abs/2306.02216v3)\]. Another approach involves the use of gradient-based label forgetting to remove the influence of specific data points \[[Yann Fraboni' 2022-11-21](http://arxiv.org/abs/2211.11656v5)\]. These methods have shown promise in reducing the impact of poisoned or target clients without retraining the entire FL system \[[David Marco Sommer' 2020-03-09](http://arxiv.org/abs/2003.04247v2)\]. However, they often lack theoretical guarantees and may not be suitable for real-world applications \[[Hanlin Gu' 2024-10-14](http://arxiv.org/abs/2410.10922v4)\].

The evaluation of unlearning methods in FL has also been a topic of interest. Most studies have focused on using single evaluation metrics to assess the success of unlearning \[[Hanlin Gu' 2024-05-23](http://arxiv.org/abs/2405.17462v4)\]. However, this paper argues that a more comprehensive assessment is needed, as unlearning can lead to under-unlearning or over-unlearning scenarios \[[Anisa Halimi' 2022-07-12](http://arxiv.org/abs/2207.05521v3)\]. To address this, we propose adapted and newly proposed evaluation metrics that allow for a more accurate assessment of unlearning effectiveness \[[Zhengyi Zhong' 2025-02-28](http://arxiv.org/abs/2502.20709v1)\].

In addition to the above, several other works have explored the challenges of unlearning in FL. For example, \[[Hanlin Gu' 2024-05-24](http://arxiv.org/abs/2405.15474v2)\] introduced a method for certified data unlearning in FL, which allows the server to remove a target client's influence without additional client communication or storage. \[[Ayush K. Varshney' 2024-10-13](http://arxiv.org/abs/2410.09947v1)\] proposed an efficient way to achieve federated unlearning by employing a privacy model that allows the FL server to plausibly deny the client's participation in the training up to a certain extent. These methods have shown promise in reducing the risk of privacy leakage while maintaining model accuracy \[[Alex Oesterling' 2023-07-27](http://arxiv.org/abs/2307.14754v2)\].

In summary, the related works on federated unlearning highlight the need for more rigorous evaluation practices and the development of methods that can effectively manage both under-unlearning and over-unlearning scenarios. The proposed evaluation metrics in this study aim to address these challenges and provide a more comprehensive assessment of unlearning effectiveness in FOLTR.

### <a id='category-unlearning-in-federated-learning'></a>Unlearning in Federated Learning: 14 papers found.
Federated unlearning has emerged as a critical area of research to address the "right to be forgotten" in privacy-preserving machine learning systems like federated learning (FL), where data remains decentralized \[[Nicolò Romandini' 2024-01-10](http://arxiv.org/abs/2401.05146v3)\]. Existing approaches aim to remove the influence of specific clients or data points from the global model without retraining the entire system, balancing privacy, accuracy, and efficiency \[[Hyejun Jeong' 2024-03-04](http://arxiv.org/abs/2403.02437v3)\]. Techniques such as VeriFi introduce verifiable unlearning frameworks that allow clients to verify the effectiveness of unlearning through local checks and server-side adjustments \[[Shuyi Wang' 2024-12-26](http://arxiv.org/abs/2412.19069v1)\]. Similarly, FATS (Federated Training with Total Variation Stability) ensures exact unlearning by guaranteeing that the unlearned model is statistically indistinguishable from one trained without the deleted data, leveraging TV stability for efficient communication \[[Xiangshan Gao' 2022-05-25](http://arxiv.org/abs/2205.12709v1)\]. Other methods, like FedAU, focus on lightweight and efficient unlearning by incorporating auxiliary modules and linear operations, enabling concurrent unlearning across multiple clients and granular levels of data removal \[[David Marco Sommer' 2020-03-09](http://arxiv.org/abs/2003.04247v2)\]. Additionally, the concept of federated domain unlearning has been explored to address multi-domain scenarios, where traditional unlearning methods often fail due to domain-specific data influences \[[Hanlin Gu' 2024-05-23](http://arxiv.org/abs/2405.17462v4)\]. Despite these advancements, challenges remain, including the need for verifiable unlearning effects, the impact of unlearning on model performance, and the practicality of deploying these methods in real-world FL systems \[[Ruinan Jin' 2023-06-03](http://arxiv.org/abs/2306.02216v3)\]. Recent work has also highlighted the importance of incentive mechanisms to retain valuable users and prevent data unlearning, ensuring model performance is maintained while respecting user privacy \[[Anisa Halimi' 2022-07-12](http://arxiv.org/abs/2207.05521v3)\]. Overall, the field is advancing through diverse strategies, but further research is needed to address the complexities of unlearning in distributed, privacy-sensitive environments \[[Hyejun Jeong' 2024-03-04](http://arxiv.org/abs/2403.02437v3)\].

### <a id='category-gradient-based-label-forgetting'></a>Gradient-Based Label Forgetting: 2 papers found.
The category "Gradient-Based Label Forgetting" explores methods for unlearning specific data from machine learning models, particularly in federated learning settings. This approach is critical for compliance with privacy regulations such as the "right to be forgotten," which requires systems like Federated Online Learning to Rank (FOLTR) to allow users to remove their data from training sets \[[Nicolò Romandini' 2024-01-10](http://arxiv.org/abs/2401.05146v3)\]. One key contribution is the introduction of a representation-level manifold mixup mechanism, which generates synthetic embeddings to facilitate gradient-based label forgetting and recovery, ensuring model performance is maintained after unlearning \[[Nicolò Romandini' 2024-01-10](http://arxiv.org/abs/2401.05146v3)\]. Another method focuses on zero-shot unlearning, where an algorithm must remove data from a trained model without access to the original training data, leveraging information-theoretic principles and geometric properties of the model to achieve this \[[Shuyi Wang' 2024-12-26](http://arxiv.org/abs/2412.19069v1)\]. These techniques aim to balance the removal of sensitive information with the preservation of model utility, addressing challenges such as under-unlearning and over-unlearning through rigorous evaluation metrics \[[Nicolò Romandini' 2024-01-10](http://arxiv.org/abs/2401.05146v3)\]. The effectiveness of these strategies is validated across diverse datasets, demonstrating their scalability and practicality in real-world applications \[[Nicolò Romandini' 2024-01-10](http://arxiv.org/abs/2401.05146v3)\]. The integration of gradient-based forgetting into federated learning frameworks highlights the importance of developing efficient and privacy-preserving methods for unlearning in decentralized systems \[[Shuyi Wang' 2024-12-26](http://arxiv.org/abs/2412.19069v1)\].
