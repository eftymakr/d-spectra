## Related Works

Recent advances in Text-to-Speech (TTS) have significantly improved speech naturalness, increasing the demand for precise prosody control and mispronunciation correction \[[Max Morrison' 2020-08-07](http://arxiv.org/abs/2008.03388v2)\]. Existing approaches for prosody manipulation often depend on specialized modules or additional training, limiting their capacity for post-hoc adjustments \[[Perry Lam' 2024-08-13](http://arxiv.org/abs/2408.06827v1)\]. Similarly, traditional mispronunciation correction relies on grapheme-to-phoneme dictionaries, making it less practical in low-resource settings \[[Giridhar Pamisetty' 2021-10-06](http://arxiv.org/abs/2110.02854v1)\]. This section reviews related works in the areas of prosody control, mispronunciation correction, and post-hoc TTS modifications.

Prosody control in TTS has been extensively studied, with several approaches focusing on modeling and manipulating prosodic features. For instance, \[[Zhan Zhang' 2021-08-12](http://arxiv.org/abs/2108.05517v2)\] proposes a TTS architecture that adds utterance-level prosody control to an existing neural HMM-based TTS system, enabling stable and monotonic alignments for spontaneous speech. The work also evaluates control accuracy and performs perceptual tests to demonstrate that prosoding control does not degrade synthesis quality. Similarly, \[[Tuomo Raitio' 2021-10-06](http://arxiv.org/abs/2110.02952v2)\] introduces a non-autoregressive parallel neural TTS front-end model hierarchically conditioned on acoustic speech features to learn a latent prosody space with intuitive dimensions, allowing for fine-grained control of prosodic attributes such as pitch, duration, and energy.

Mispronunciation correction in TTS is often based on grapheme-to-phoneme (G2P) dictionaries, which are effective but limited in low-resource scenarios \[[Giridhar Pamisetty' 2021-10-06](http://arxiv.org/abs/2110.02854v1)\]. Recent works have explored data-driven approaches to improve pronunciation accuracy. For example, \[[Chung-Ming Chien' 2020-11-12](http://arxiv.org/abs/2011.06465v3)\] presents a speech BERT model to extract embedded prosody information in speech segments for improving the prosody of synthesized speech in neural TTS. This model can learn prosody attributes from large speech data and utilize them to enhance the prosody of the TTS output. Additionally, \[[Florian Lux' 2022-06-24](http://arxiv.org/abs/2206.12229v2)\] investigates the use of visual context to enhance prosody prediction in TTS, proposing a model that incorporates both textual and visual information to improve the expressiveness of synthesized speech.

Post-hoc TTS modifications, such as adjusting prosody and correcting mispronunciations, have also been explored. \[[Yutian Wang' 2022-04-07](http://arxiv.org/abs/2204.03238v1)\] introduces a novel model called CopyCat2, which enables multi-speaker TTS and fine-grained prosody transfer between speakers. This model uses a two-stage training approach to learn speaker-independent prosody representations and then uses contextual information from text to generate speech with contextually appropriate prosody. \[[Max Morrison' 2021-02-16](http://arxiv.org/abs/2102.08328v1)\] further explores the use of prosody embeddings for improving TTS, demonstrating that incorporating prosody information can enhance the naturalness and expressiveness of synthesized speech.

In addition, \[[Zhen Zeng' 2020-08-13](http://arxiv.org/abs/2008.05656v1)\] presents a system called Daisy-TTS, which simulates a wider spectrum of emotions by decomposing prosody embeddings into primary and secondary emotions, intensity levels, and emotion polarity. This approach allows for more nuanced emotional expression in TTS, enhancing the naturalness of synthesized speech.

These works highlight the ongoing efforts to improve prosody control, mispronunciation correction, and post-hoc TTS modifications. While existing methods have made significant progress, they often rely on specialized modules or additional training, limiting their flexibility and adaptability. The proposed method, Counterfactual Activation Editing, addresses these limitations by enabling post-hoc control of prosody and pronunciation without retraining, bridging the gap between pre-trained TTS models and editable speech synthesis.
