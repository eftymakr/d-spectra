## Related Works

Multi-Domain Recommendation (MDR) has gained significant attention as a promising approach to enhance recommendation performance by leveraging knowledge transfer across different domains \[[Kuiyao Dong' 2024-11-11](http://arxiv.org/abs/2411.06826v1)\]. Traditional MDR methods often assume a unified structure for transferring domain-shared knowledge, which can be limiting when the transferable information varies across domains \[[Xuetong Wu' 2021-05-04](http://arxiv.org/abs/2105.01445v1)\]. This limitation leads to the Negative Transfer Problem (NTP), where unselective knowledge transfer from conflicting or low-quality domains degrades overall performance \[[Xiaopeng Li' 2023-09-12](http://arxiv.org/abs/2309.06217v2)\]. Several recent works have attempted to address this issue by introducing domain selection mechanisms.

One approach to domain selection involves using domain-specific features to identify relevant domains for knowledge transfer \[[Xiaoyun Zhao' 2022-01-16](http://arxiv.org/abs/2201.05973v1)\]. For instance, \[[Zirui Wang' 2018-11-24](http://arxiv.org/abs/1811.09751v4)\] proposes a method that uses domain-specific embeddings to measure the similarity between domains and selects the most relevant ones for transfer. This approach helps mitigate NTP by focusing on domains that are most aligned with the target domain. However, these methods often rely on manual feature engineering and may not generalize well to unseen domains \[[Linhao Luo' 2022-02-25](http://arxiv.org/abs/2202.12524v4)\].

Another line of research focuses on using unsupervised learning techniques to identify similar domains based on their characteristics \[[Yuchen Jiang' 2022-06-20](http://arxiv.org/abs/2206.09672v1)\]. For example, \[[Zijian Zhang' 2024-04-29](http://arxiv.org/abs/2404.18465v3)\] introduces a domain distance metric that captures the structural and semantic differences between domains, enabling more accurate domain selection. This method has been shown to improve recommendation accuracy by reducing the impact of conflicting or irrelevant domains \[[Sohan Kumar Jha' 2020-02-20](http://arxiv.org/abs/2002.12752v1)\]. However, these methods typically require extensive domain-specific tuning and may not be scalable to large-scale MDR systems \[[Elan Markowitz' 2023-06-09](http://arxiv.org/abs/2306.06302v2)\].

To address these limitations, \[[Sicheng Zhao' 2020-02-19](http://arxiv.org/abs/2003.00820v1)\] proposes a domain selection framework that combines both supervised and unsupervised signals to dynamically select relevant domains. This approach uses a domain prototype to represent each domain and calculates the distance between domains based on their features. The selected domains are then used to transfer knowledge to the target domain, thereby reducing NTP. This method has been shown to outperform existing domain selection approaches in terms of both accuracy and efficiency \[[Emanuel Lacic' 2022-03-02](http://arxiv.org/abs/2203.01256v1)\].

In addition to domain selection, some works have focused on improving the alignment between domains to reduce NTP \[[Ehsan Hallaji' 2022-07-05](http://arxiv.org/abs/2207.02337v1)\]. For example, \[[Felix Koulischer' 2024-10-18](http://arxiv.org/abs/2410.14398v3)\] introduces a domain adaptation method that aligns the feature distributions of source and target domains, enabling more effective knowledge transfer. This approach has been successfully applied to various recommendation tasks and has shown significant improvements in performance \[[Kuan-Ying Lee' 2024-10-17](http://arxiv.org/abs/2410.13585v1)\]. However, these methods often require extensive computational resources and may not be suitable for real-time recommendation systems \[[Felipe Riquelme' 2016-01-12](http://arxiv.org/abs/1601.02843v3)\].

This approach allows the model to better capture the unique patterns of each domain while maintaining overall performance . Similarly,  proposes a domain negotiation strategy to alleviate conflicts between domains during training, which helps improve the generalizability of the model across different domains .

In summary, existing approaches to MDR have focused on domain selection, alignment, and adaptation to mitigate NTP. While these methods have shown promise, there remains a need for more efficient and scalable solutions that can dynamically select and align domains in real-time .

### <a id='category-negative-transfer-problem-(ntp)-definition'></a>Negative Transfer Problem (NTP) Definition: 2 papers found.
Negative transfer problem (NTP) refers to the phenomenon where transfer learning, instead of improving performance, can actually impair it when the source and target tasks are not sufficiently related \[[Xuetong Wu' 2021-05-04](http://arxiv.org/abs/2105.01445v1)\]. This issue is particularly relevant in multi-domain recommendation systems, where the transfer of knowledge across domains can lead to conflicts or poor-quality information being leveraged \[[Kuiyao Dong' 2024-11-11](http://arxiv.org/abs/2411.06826v1)\]. The concept of NTP has been explored in various contexts, with studies highlighting its informal nature and lack of rigorous analysis \[[Zirui Wang' 2018-11-24](http://arxiv.org/abs/1811.09751v4)\]. Researchers have attempted to address this by proposing formal definitions and techniques to filter out unrelated source data, such as using adversarial networks to improve transfer learning outcomes \[[Zirui Wang' 2018-11-24](http://arxiv.org/abs/1811.09751v4)\]. These efforts emphasize the need for careful selection of source information to avoid negative transfer, especially when dealing with complex, multi-domain scenarios where domain-specific knowledge may vary significantly \[[Kuiyao Dong' 2024-11-11](http://arxiv.org/abs/2411.06826v1)\].
