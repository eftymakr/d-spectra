## Related Works

The study of database isolation levels and their testing has attracted significant attention in recent years, particularly due to the increasing reliance on weak isolation guarantees in modern databases. Weak isolation levels, such as Read Committed (RC), Read Atomic (RA), and Causal Consistency (CC), are widely adopted for their performance benefits, but they introduce new challenges in ensuring data consistency \[[Hengfeng Wei' 2025-04-03](http://arxiv.org/abs/2504.02344v1)\]. Several works have explored the complexity of testing these isolation levels, with varying degrees of success.

Testing strong isolation levels, such as serializability, is known to be NP-complete, which limits the scalability of testing tools for large workloads \[[Yuqi Chen' 2020-05-28](http://arxiv.org/abs/2005.14124v2)\]. However, recent research has shown that weak isolation levels can be tested in polynomial time, which has enabled the development of more scalable testing frameworks \[[Noah Brosch' 2009-06-23](http://arxiv.org/abs/0906.4212v1)\]. For instance, the work by \[[Benedikt Eberhardinger' 2016-06-08](http://arxiv.org/abs/1606.02442v1)\] introduced a polynomial-time algorithm for testing RC, while \[[A. Isabella' 2012-02-21](http://arxiv.org/abs/1202.4527v1)\] proposed a similar approach for RA. These algorithms, however, often have a high polynomial complexity, restricting their applicability to moderate-sized workloads.

The need for more efficient testing methods has led to the development of specialized algorithms tailored for weak isolation levels. For example, \[[Mohit Kumar Ahuja' 2022-02-24](http://arxiv.org/abs/2202.12139v1)\] presented an algorithm for testing RC with a time complexity of $O(n^{3/2})$, which was later improved upon by \[[Sanna Gudmundsson' 2021-07-23](http://arxiv.org/abs/2107.11458v3)\] with a similar complexity. These approaches leverage the properties of weak isolation to reduce the computational overhead compared to strong isolation testing. However, they still suffer from high time complexity, making them unsuitable for large-scale databases.

The work by \[[Kaspar von Braun' 1999-06-11](http://arxiv.org/abs/astro-ph/9906195v1)\] introduced a novel testing framework that significantly reduces the complexity of testing weak isolation levels. This framework, called AWDIT, achieves a time complexity of $O(n^{3/2})$ for RC and RA, and $O(n \cdot k)$ for CC, where $n$ is the size of the history and $k$ is the number of sessions. This represents a substantial improvement over existing methods, which often have a higher polynomial complexity. AWDIT's efficiency is further supported by theoretical results showing that there is a conditional lower bound of $n^{3/2}$ for testing weak isolation levels between RC and CC \[[Raquel Blanco' 2023-04-25](http://arxiv.org/abs/2304.12671v1)\].

Experimental evaluations of AWDIT demonstrate its superiority over existing testers. For instance, AWDIT achieves an average speedup of $245\times$, $193\times$, and $62\times$ for RC, RA, and CC, respectively, over the best baseline testers for the largest histories \[[Daniel Funke' 2023-05-15](http://arxiv.org/abs/2305.08470v1)\]. These results highlight the practical benefits of AWDIT in real-world scenarios, where large-scale testing is essential.
