## Related Works

Fine-grained emotion classification in app reviews has gained increasing attention in recent years, driven by the need to understand user sentiment with greater precision. While traditional sentiment analysis typically focuses on broad categories such as positive, negative, and neutral \[[Minxue Niu' 2024-12-10](http://arxiv.org/abs/2412.07906v1)\], recent studies have emphasized the importance of capturing more nuanced emotional states, such as joy, anger, fear, and surprise \[[Quim Motger' 2024-08-02](http://arxiv.org/abs/2408.01063v2)\]. This shift is motivated by the growing recognition that understanding subtle emotional cues in user feedback can provide deeper insights into user satisfaction and product improvement \[[Erik Tromp' 2014-12-15](http://arxiv.org/abs/1412.4682v1)\].

Several studies have explored the use of large language models (LLMs) for emotion classification in app reviews. For instance, \[[Daniel Martens' 2017-03-07](http://arxiv.org/abs/1703.02256v1)\] evaluated the performance of ChatGPT on a benchmark dataset of 1,880 manually annotated reviews and found that it achieved a robust F1 score of 0.842, demonstrating the potential of LLMs in this domain. Similarly, \[[Sakshi Ranjan' 2020-06-17](http://arxiv.org/abs/2006.09739v1)\] investigated the use of zero-shot learning with ChatGPT for classifying app reviews into functional and non-functional requirements, highlighting the model's ability to generalize across different categories. However, these studies also noted challenges, such as the difficulty in accurately capturing the nuances of emotional expressions and the potential for misclassification, especially in complex or ambiguous cases \[[Andrew Shin' 2024-05-18](http://arxiv.org/abs/2405.11357v3)\].

The development of structured annotation frameworks has also been a key focus in recent research. \[[Alexandre Denis' 2013-09-11](http://arxiv.org/abs/1309.2853v1)\] introduced a comprehensive annotation framework for app reviews, adapting Plutchik's emotion taxonomy to capture fine-grained emotional states. This framework was validated through an iterative human annotation process, which helped define clear guidelines and document key challenges in emotion classification. The study emphasized the importance of human input in ensuring the accuracy and reliability of annotated data, particularly given the subjective nature of emotional interpretation \[[Lauren Olson' 2024-01-19](http://arxiv.org/abs/2401.11063v2)\].

In addition to human annotation, there has been growing interest in leveraging LLMs for automated emotion annotation. \[[Saif M. Mohammad' 2020-05-25](http://arxiv.org/abs/2005.11882v2)\] explored the feasibility of using LLMs for this task, assessing their cost-effectiveness and agreement with human-labelled data. The study found that while LLMs significantly reduce manual effort, full automation remains challenging due to the complexity of emotional interpretation. This aligns with findings from \[[Abe Kazemzadeh' 2022-04-02](http://arxiv.org/abs/2204.00916v1)\], which noted that LLMs can maintain substantial agreement with human annotators but may struggle with capturing the full spectrum of emotional nuances, particularly in cases involving mixed or subtle emotional expressions.

Recent work has also focused on improving the accuracy and robustness of emotion classification models. \[[Sylvester Olubolu Orimaye' 2015-11-07](http://arxiv.org/abs/1511.02385v1)\] proposed a method to enhance fine-grained emotion recognition by integrating domain knowledge and leveraging multimodal data. This approach was tested on several benchmark datasets, demonstrating improved performance compared to traditional methods. Similarly, \[[Di Weng' 2020-05-20](http://arxiv.org/abs/2005.09837v1)\] introduced a framework for fine-grained emotion classification that incorporated both textual and contextual information, further highlighting the potential of multimodal approaches in this domain.

Overall, the field of fine-grained emotion classification in app reviews is rapidly evolving, with a growing emphasis on the integration of human and machine-driven approaches. While LLMs offer promising opportunities for automation, their limitations in capturing the full complexity of emotional expressions underscore the need for continued research and refinement.
