## Related Works

Fairness in re-ranking has attracted significant attention in recent years, driven by the increasing awareness of algorithmic bias and its societal implications. Prior work has primarily focused on balancing ranking accuracy with fairness, often using metrics such as statistical parity or equal opportunity \[[Mohammadmehdi Naghiaei' 2022-04-17](http://arxiv.org/abs/2204.08085v1)\]. However, these approaches typically treat fairness and accuracy as independent objectives, neglecting the inherent trade-offs between them \[[Chen Xu' 2024-04-27](http://arxiv.org/abs/2404.17826v1)\]. 

The concept of fairness in re-ranking is closely related to the idea of item-side fairness, where the distribution of items across rankings is adjusted to ensure equitable representation \[[Charlotte Leininger' 2025-01-24](http://arxiv.org/abs/2501.14710v1)\]. This is particularly relevant in recommendation systems, where fairness considerations may involve ensuring that items from different providers or demographic groups are adequately represented \[[Xiaotian Han' 2025-03-10](http://arxiv.org/abs/2503.07066v1)\]. Several studies have proposed fairness-aware re-ranking algorithms that incorporate fairness constraints into the ranking process, often through regularization or post-processing steps \[[Hua Tang' 2023-10-19](http://arxiv.org/abs/2310.12785v1)\]. For example, the Fairness-Aware Re-ranking (FAR) algorithm balances accuracy and provider-side fairness by iteratively generating rankings that reflect both performance and diversity \[[Nasim Sonboli' 2020-05-21](http://arxiv.org/abs/2005.12974v1)\]. 

Another line of research has explored the use of elasticity in understanding the trade-offs between fairness and accuracy. In economics, elasticity measures how sensitive a quantity is to changes in another, and this concept has been analogously applied to the fairness-accuracy trade-off in re-ranking \[[Saeedeh Karimi' 2023-09-08](http://arxiv.org/abs/2309.04250v1)\]. The idea is that fairness constraints, akin to a tax on item providers, can lead to a loss in user-side accuracy, and the extent of this loss is governed by the elasticity of utility between item groups \[[Andrea Giuseppe Di Francesco' 2024-06-17](http://arxiv.org/abs/2406.11720v2)\]. This insight has led to the development of new evaluation frameworks that go beyond single fairness metrics, aiming to provide a more comprehensive assessment of fair re-ranking algorithms \[[Xiaopeng Ye' 2024-05-25](http://arxiv.org/abs/2405.16120v2)\]. 

Recent work has also introduced novel methods for fair re-ranking that leverage elasticity calculations to adjust inter-item distances within a curved space \[[Yunhao Zhou' 2021-05-04](http://arxiv.org/abs/2105.01447v2)\]. These methods aim to improve both fairness and accuracy by dynamically adapting to the characteristics of the item distribution. For instance, the Tax-rank method reformulates fair re-ranking as a taxation problem, where item-level tax policies are used to redistribute ranking slots \[[Baharan Nouriinanloo' 2024-06-26](http://arxiv.org/abs/2406.18740v1)\]. Similarly, the P-MMF algorithm addresses provider fairness by formulating the problem as a resource allocation task, where exposure slots are allocated to providers in a way that maximizes min-max fairness \[[Zhenzhong Lan' 2015-12-11](http://arxiv.org/abs/1512.03740v1)\]. 

In addition to these methods, there has been growing interest in the use of elasticity-based evaluation frameworks to assess the performance of fair re-ranking algorithms \[[A. Feder Cooper' 2021-02-01](http://arxiv.org/abs/2102.01203v3)\]. The Elastic Fairness Curve (EF-Curve) is one such framework that enables a comparative analysis of algorithm performance across different elasticity levels, facilitating the selection of the most suitable approach \[[Robert Lee Poe' 2023-06-02](http://arxiv.org/abs/2306.01417v1)\]. This approach has been validated through experiments on widely used ranking datasets, demonstrating its effectiveness and efficiency \[[Zhenlan Ji' 2023-05-22](http://arxiv.org/abs/2305.13057v3)\].
