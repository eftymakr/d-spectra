## Related Works

Semi-supervised learning (SSL) has gained significant attention for its ability to leverage unlabeled data to improve model performance with limited labeled examples. However, traditional SSL methods assume that labeled and unlabeled data share the same class distribution, which is often violated in practice due to the presence of outliers in unlabeled data \[[Kuniaki Saito' 2021-05-28](http://arxiv.org/abs/2105.14148v2)\]. These outliers, which belong to unknown classes, are typically treated as noise, leading to performance degradation \[[Yang Yang' 2024-05-11](http://arxiv.org/abs/2405.06979v2)\]. To address this limitation, several open-set SSL methods have been proposed, aiming to handle the presence of outliers during training \[[Yang Yu' 2023-03-21](http://arxiv.org/abs/2303.12091v4)\].

One line of research focuses on improving outlier detection by leveraging prediction discrepancies between inliers and outliers. For instance, OpenMatch integrates novelty detection with consistency regularization to distinguish between inliers and outliers \[[Jongjin Park' 2021-06-29](http://arxiv.org/abs/2107.08943v3)\]. Similarly, IOMatch proposes a framework that jointly utilizes inliers and outliers by introducing a multi-binary classifier to treat outliers as a single class \[[Dan Hendrycks' 2018-12-11](http://arxiv.org/abs/1812.04606v3)\]. These methods, however, often rely on a single model trained on labeled data, which can lead to performance degradation when labeled data is insufficient \[[Doyup Lee' 2022-01-17](http://arxiv.org/abs/2201.06247v2)\].

Another approach, such as FreeMatch, adapts the confidence threshold dynamically based on the model's learning status, aiming to improve pseudo-label quality \[[Dayananda Herurkar' 2025-04-29](http://arxiv.org/abs/2504.20862v1)\]. This self-adaptive thresholding mechanism helps in better utilizing unlabeled data while reducing the impact of outliers. Similarly, SelfMatch combines contrastive self-supervision with consistency regularization, achieving state-of-the-art results on benchmark datasets \[[Hongbin Xu' 2022-07-24](http://arxiv.org/abs/2207.11699v4)\]. These methods demonstrate the potential of combining self-supervised learning with SSL techniques to improve robustness.

In contrast, the proposed DAC framework introduces a novel approach by exploiting prediction disagreements among multiple models that are differently biased towards the unlabeled distribution \[[Malik Boudiaf' 2022-06-18](http://arxiv.org/abs/2206.09236v1)\]. This method constructs a collection of differently biased models through a single training process, encouraging divergent heads to be biased towards outliers while maintaining consistent predictions for inliers \[[Zhe Huang' 2024-03-09](http://arxiv.org/abs/2403.06024v1)\]. This approach leverages the disagreement among these models as a measure to identify unknown concepts, thereby improving robustness against outliers.

Additionally, methods like Poisson learning and InterLUDE have explored the use of graph-based and interaction-based approaches to enhance SSL performance \[[Huayi Zhou' 2024-04-03](http://arxiv.org/abs/2404.02544v4)\]. Poisson learning addresses the degeneracy of Laplacian SSL by solving the Poisson equation on the graph, while InterLUDE improves representation learning by incorporating interactions between labeled and unlabeled data \[[Alexander Genkin' 2019-08-21](http://arxiv.org/abs/1908.08145v1)\]. These methods highlight the importance of leveraging both labeled and unlabeled data effectively to enhance model performance.

In summary, while existing methods have made strides in handling open-set SSL, they often rely on single-model approaches or fixed thresholding mechanisms that may not perform well under data scarcity. The DAC framework offers a novel solution by leveraging prediction disagreements among multiple models, providing a more robust approach to open-set SSL \[[Wenyuan Li' 2019-05-16](http://arxiv.org/abs/1905.06484v2)\].
