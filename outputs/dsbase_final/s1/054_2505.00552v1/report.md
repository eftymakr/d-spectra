## Related Works

Graph Convolutional Networks (GCNs) have become a cornerstone in collaborative filtering (CF) for recommendation systems, leveraging the structural relationships between users and items to enhance recommendation accuracy \[[Kang Liu' 2020-07-07](http://arxiv.org/abs/2007.03383v2)\]. However, several limitations have been identified in existing GCN-based approaches. One major issue is the embedding layer, which often results in a latent space with limited capacity, thereby neglecting locally observed but potentially valuable preference patterns \[[Zonghan Wu' 2021-07-10](http://arxiv.org/abs/2107.04755v3)\]. Additionally, the widely used neighborhood aggregation mechanism is constrained in its ability to capture diverse and fine-grained preference patterns \[[Yifang Qin' 2024-01-23](http://arxiv.org/abs/2401.12590v2)\]. These limitations are rooted in the use of graph filtering with a cut-off in the frequency spectrum and a restricted linear form \[[Zirui Ge' 2022-03-28](http://arxiv.org/abs/2203.14748v1)\]. 

To address these challenges, several works have explored alternative graph filtering methods. For instance, \[[Dinh Van Tran' 2018-11-23](http://arxiv.org/abs/1811.10435v1)\] introduced a method that replaces the linear graph filter with a non-linear one, enabling more flexible modeling of user-item interactions. This approach, however, still relies on learned embeddings and does not fully exploit the raw interaction history. Similarly, \[[Pang Li' 2024-12-02](http://arxiv.org/abs/2412.01378v1)\] proposed a graph neural network that incorporates attention mechanisms to better capture local patterns, but it does not address the issue of limited spectral representation. 

Another line of research focuses on improving the efficiency and expressiveness of graph filters. \[[Haoxuan Li' 2024-04-09](http://arxiv.org/abs/2404.05962v2)\] proposed a method that uses Chebyshev polynomials to approximate non-linear graph filters, which can better capture the complex interactions in the graph. This approach is similar to our ChebyCF framework, which also employs Chebyshev interpolation to approximate flexible non-linear graph filters \[[Yihong Wu' 2024-06-20](http://arxiv.org/abs/2406.13996v2)\]. However, \[[Haoxuan Li' 2024-04-09](http://arxiv.org/abs/2404.05962v2)\] does not incorporate additional ideal pass filters or degree-based normalization to enhance the filtering process, which are key components of our approach. 

Moreover, \[[R. J. Cintra' 2014-11-10](http://arxiv.org/abs/1411.2389v1)\] introduced a method that uses a combination of spectral and spatial graph convolutions to improve the representation of node features. While this approach enhances the model's ability to capture both local and global patterns, it still relies on learned embeddings and does not fully utilize the raw interaction data. In contrast, ChebyCF directly utilizes the raw interaction history, enabling the model to capture the full spectrum of signals contained in it \[[Nikolaos Polatidis' 2018-04-24](http://arxiv.org/abs/1804.08891v1)\]. 

In addition to these works, \[[Shaowen Peng' 2020-04-30](http://arxiv.org/abs/2004.14734v1)\] explored the use of graph convolutional networks for recommendation systems, emphasizing the importance of capturing both structural and semantic information. However, their approach does not address the limitations of embedding layers and neighborhood aggregation as comprehensively as our framework. 

These related works highlight the ongoing efforts to improve the performance and expressiveness of GCN-based recommendation systems. Our ChebyCF framework builds upon these contributions by introducing a novel approach that directly utilizes raw interaction history and employs Chebyshev interpolation to approximate flexible non-linear graph filters, thereby overcoming the limitations of existing methods.
