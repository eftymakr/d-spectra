## Related Works

Recent advancements in pre-training methodologies have significantly influenced the development of DNA sequence models. Initially, researchers focused on unsupervised pre-training paradigms, which have shown promise in various domains \[[Xin He' 2024-10-23](http://arxiv.org/abs/2410.17954v2)\]. However, these approaches often fall short when applied to DNA pre-training due to the limited information content of pure DNA sequences \[[Pooja Kathail' 2024-11-17](http://arxiv.org/abs/2411.11158v2)\]. The functional regulation of DNA is heavily influenced by genomic profiles such as chromatin accessibility, which are not captured by pure sequence models \[[IGVF Consortium' 2023-07-24](http://arxiv.org/abs/2307.13708v1)\]. 

To address this limitation, supervised training for genomic profile prediction has emerged as a more effective alternative. This approach leverages the rich information contained in genomic profiles to enhance DNA representation learning \[[Theodore Roman' 2016-04-08](http://arxiv.org/abs/1604.02487v1)\]. Several studies have explored the use of supervised learning in genomic data analysis, including the prediction of chromatin accessibility and other genomic features \[[Gad Abraham' 2013-01-25](http://arxiv.org/abs/1301.5948v3)\]. These methods typically involve training models on labeled data to capture the complex relationships between DNA sequences and their associated genomic profiles \[[Renqi Chen' 2024-05-15](http://arxiv.org/abs/2405.09585v3)\].

The integration of Mixture of Experts (MoE) architectures has further advanced the field of genomic profile prediction. MoE models allow for the dynamic activation of specialized sub-models, enabling more efficient and accurate representation learning \[[Deepesh Data' 2020-05-16](http://arxiv.org/abs/2005.07866v1)\]. This concept has been applied in various domains, including natural language processing and computer vision \[[Yuan Xie' 2022-07-19](http://arxiv.org/abs/2207.09094v1)\]. In the context of genomic data, MoE models can be designed to handle the multi-species and multi-profile nature of genomic profile prediction by incorporating species- and profile-specific experts \[[Chuanyang Jin' 2023-06-26](http://arxiv.org/abs/2306.15111v2)\]. 

Several studies have demonstrated the effectiveness of MoE models in genomic data analysis. In addition to MoE, other pre-training strategies have been explored for genomic data. The work by \[[Samuel Cahyawijaya' 2022-04-14](http://arxiv.org/abs/2204.06699v1)\] focused on the use of self-supervised learning for predicting chromatin accessibility, demonstrating the effectiveness of this approach in capturing the dynamic nature of genomic profiles \[[Weiwei Zhang' 2013-08-09](http://arxiv.org/abs/1308.2134v1)\]. 

The application of MoE in genomic data analysis has also been extended to other domains, such as the prediction of DNA methylation and histone modifications \[[Alexander Koebler' 2025-03-26](http://arxiv.org/abs/2503.22731v1)\]. These studies highlight the versatility of MoE in capturing the complex relationships between DNA sequences and their associated genomic profiles. 

In summary, while unsupervised pre-training paradigms have laid the groundwork for DNA sequence models, supervised training for genomic profile prediction offers a more effective alternative.
