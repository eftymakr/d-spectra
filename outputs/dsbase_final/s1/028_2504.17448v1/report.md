## Related Works

Active learning (AL) has been widely studied for reducing annotation costs by selecting the most informative unlabeled data for labeling \[[Hussain Ahmad Madni' 2023-07-31](http://arxiv.org/abs/2308.00155v1)\]. However, individual AL approaches often suffer from limited data diversity and annotation budgets, which can hinder model performance \[[Zijian Li' 2022-06-11](http://arxiv.org/abs/2206.05507v1)\]. To address these limitations, Federated Active Learning (FAL) has emerged as a promising approach that enables collaborative data selection and model training while preserving data privacy \[[Hossein Zakerinia' 2022-06-20](http://arxiv.org/abs/2206.10032v4)\]. Despite these advancements, existing FAL methods have not adequately addressed the heterogeneity of data distribution across clients and the associated fluctuations in global and local model parameters, which can significantly degrade model accuracy \[[Seongyoon Kim' 2023-11-22](http://arxiv.org/abs/2311.13267v1)\].

Several studies have explored federated learning (FL) in the context of data heterogeneity. For example, FedDiverse tackles data heterogeneity by proposing a client selection algorithm that promotes collaboration between clients with complementary data distributions \[[Gergely D. Németh' 2025-04-15](http://arxiv.org/abs/2504.11216v2)\]. Similarly, FedFN introduces feature normalization to alleviate data heterogeneity issues in FL \[[Harsh Vardhan' 2025-04-02](http://arxiv.org/abs/2504.01921v1)\]. These approaches focus on improving model performance by addressing data distribution differences across clients. However, they do not directly address the issue of epistemic uncertainty in the context of active learning within a federated setting \[[Daniel Peterson' 2019-12-13](http://arxiv.org/abs/1912.06733v1)\].

The concept of epistemic uncertainty, which refers to the uncertainty about the model's knowledge, has been explored in various learning frameworks. For instance, in federated learning, the work by \[[Sannara Ek' 2022-07-17](http://arxiv.org/abs/2207.08187v1)\] highlights the importance of epistemic uncertainty estimation in improving model robustness. Additionally, the paper by \[[Wenqi Wei' 2024-09-19](http://arxiv.org/abs/2409.13004v1)\] introduces a framework for estimating epistemic uncertainty in distributed learning settings, which could be relevant to FAL. However, these studies do not explicitly incorporate active learning strategies or address the challenge of identifying high-epistemic variation samples in a federated environment \[[Motasem Alfarra' 2022-06-06](http://arxiv.org/abs/2206.02535v2)\].

To enhance the efficiency and effectiveness of FAL, several techniques have been proposed. For example, \[[Wenlin Chen' 2020-10-26](http://arxiv.org/abs/2010.13723v3)\] introduces a method for client adaptation in FL, which improves model performance by incorporating client-specific conditioning. This approach is particularly beneficial when there are large differences in data distribution across clients \[[Disha Makhija' 2022-05-25](http://arxiv.org/abs/2205.12493v3)\]. Similarly, \[[Paul Pu Liang' 2020-01-06](http://arxiv.org/abs/2001.01523v3)\] proposes a method for optimizing FL by entropy-based client selection, which ensures that the global model is exposed to data from all available classes \[[Ramya Muthukrishnan' 2022-06-11](http://arxiv.org/abs/2206.05575v5)\]. These methods aim to improve the efficiency of FL by selecting clients based on their contribution to the global model \[[Asfia Kawnine' 2023-11-26](http://arxiv.org/abs/2311.15382v1)\].

In addition to client selection, techniques for improving model generalization in FL have been explored. For example, \[[Feng Wang' 2022-09-12](http://arxiv.org/abs/2209.05395v1)\] proposes a method for mitigating the effects of non-IID data by using synthetic data generated with differentially private means. Client Adaptation Techniques in Federated Learning focus on improving model performance while maintaining data privacy and reducing communication overhead. These techniques aim to address the challenges posed by heterogeneous data distributions across clients and the limitations of traditional federated learning frameworks. One approach involves integrating domain adaptation strategies to enhance model accuracy, particularly under privacy constraints such as differential privacy \[[Daniel Peterson' 2019-12-13](http://arxiv.org/abs/1912.06733v1)\]. This is achieved by allowing each user to adapt the shared model to their specific domain, thereby improving generalization and accuracy across diverse data sources. Additionally, client subsampling schemes have been proposed to mitigate the communication bottleneck in federated learning, where only the most informative clients contribute updates to the master node \[[Wenlin Chen' 2020-10-26](http://arxiv.org/abs/2010.13723v3)\]. This method leverages the norm of the update to determine importance, ensuring that the limited participation of clients still closely approximates the performance of full participation. These techniques are orthogonal to existing methods for communication compression and can be combined with local training strategies to further reduce overhead. By focusing on both model adaptation and efficient communication, these approaches enhance the scalability and effectiveness of federated learning systems.
