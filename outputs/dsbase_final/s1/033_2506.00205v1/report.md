## Related Works

Rehearsal-based methods have been widely studied in continual learning (CL) as a means to mitigate catastrophic forgetting by storing and reusing past data during training \[[Lukas Balles' 2022-03-28](http://arxiv.org/abs/2203.14544v1)\]. These methods typically involve training a model on a subset of previously seen data alongside new data, which has shown superior performance compared to non-rehearsal approaches \[[Kishan Parshotam' 2020-04-22](http://arxiv.org/abs/2004.10862v1)\]. However, the optimality of concurrent rehearsal, where past and new data are trained together, remains an open question. Recent work has explored alternative rehearsal strategies, such as sequential rehearsal, where new data is trained first, followed by revisiting past data in a sequential manner \[[Eli Verwimp' 2021-04-15](http://arxiv.org/abs/2104.07446v1)\]. This approach is inspired by human learning, where revisiting previously learned tasks can help reduce forgetting \[[Frantzeska Lavda' 2018-10-24](http://arxiv.org/abs/1810.10612v1)\].

Theoretical analysis of rehearsal-based CL has primarily focused on linear models, with limited exploration of nonlinear architectures \[[Yen-Chang Hsu' 2018-10-30](http://arxiv.org/abs/1810.12488v4)\]. A key contribution of this work is the first comprehensive theoretical analysis of rehearsal-based CL in overparameterized linear models, comparing concurrent and sequential rehearsal strategies \[[Sattar Vakili' 2021-09-13](http://arxiv.org/abs/2109.06099v3)\]. This analysis reveals that sequential rehearsal can outperform concurrent rehearsal when tasks are less similar, suggesting that the choice of rehearsal strategy should be informed by task similarity \[[Albin Soutif--Cormerais' 2023-08-20](http://arxiv.org/abs/2308.10328v3)\]. This insight motivates the development of a hybrid rehearsal method that trains similar tasks concurrently and revisits dissimilar tasks sequentially \[[Md Yousuf Harun' 2023-08-25](http://arxiv.org/abs/2308.13646v2)\].

Several studies have investigated the effectiveness of rehearsal strategies in deep neural networks. For instance, \[[Daofeng Liu' 2023-03-06](http://arxiv.org/abs/2303.02954v1)\] shows that rehearsal can significantly improve performance on benchmark datasets, but the computational cost of storing and reusing past data remains a challenge. Similarly, \[[Runqing Wu' 2025-04-14](http://arxiv.org/abs/2504.10561v2)\] highlights the importance of task similarity in determining the effectiveness of rehearsal, but does not propose a hybrid approach. In contrast, this work introduces a hybrid rehearsal method that combines the benefits of both concurrent and sequential strategies, achieving better performance on a range of tasks \[[Haytham M. Fayek' 2018-11-29](http://arxiv.org/abs/1811.12273v1)\].

The hybrid rehearsal approach has also been explored in the context of continual learning with deep neural networks. \[[Gido M. van de Ven' 2019-04-15](http://arxiv.org/abs/1904.07734v1)\] proposes a method that combines rehearsal with knowledge distillation to improve performance on new tasks while retaining knowledge of previously learned tasks. However, this approach does not explicitly address the trade-off between task similarity and rehearsal effectiveness. \[[Kazuki Irie' 2023-12-01](http://arxiv.org/abs/2312.00276v3)\] investigates the role of rehearsal in reducing forgetting and shows that sequential rehearsal can be more effective in certain scenarios, but does not provide a theoretical justification for this finding. This work fills this gap by providing a theoretical analysis of rehearsal-based CL in overparameterized linear models, showing that sequential rehearsal is more effective when tasks are less similar \[[Martin Mundt' 2019-05-28](http://arxiv.org/abs/1905.12019v5)\].

In addition to theoretical analysis, empirical studies have evaluated the performance of different rehearsal strategies in deep neural networks. \[[Mayank Lunayach' 2022-06-16](http://arxiv.org/abs/2206.07932v1)\] shows that rehearsal can significantly improve performance on benchmark datasets, but the computational cost of storing and reusing past data remains a challenge. \[[Shikhar Srivastava' 2024-10-25](http://arxiv.org/abs/2410.19925v2)\] highlights the importance of task similarity in determining the effectiveness of rehearsal, but does not propose a hybrid approach. In contrast, this work introduces a hybrid rehearsal method that combines the benefits of both concurrent and sequential strategies, achieving better performance on a range of tasks \[[Khurram Javed' 2019-05-29](http://arxiv.org/abs/1905.12588v2)\].

### <a id='category-continual-learning-frameworks-and-applications'></a>Continual Learning Frameworks and Applications: 17 papers found.
Continual learning (CL) aims to enable models to learn sequentially while retaining previously acquired knowledge, a challenge often addressed through rehearsal-based methods that store and reuse past data \[[Lukas Balles' 2022-03-28](http://arxiv.org/abs/2203.14544v1)\]. These methods typically rely on a rehearsal memory to prevent catastrophic forgetting, where new learning interferes with old tasks \[[Eli Verwimp' 2021-04-15](http://arxiv.org/abs/2104.07446v1)\]. However, the effectiveness of such strategies depends on how data is curated and utilized. For instance, coreset selection methods aim to maintain a small, informative subset of data that closely matches the gradients induced by the full dataset, improving performance over techniques like reservoir sampling \[[Lukas Balles' 2022-03-28](http://arxiv.org/abs/2203.14544v1)\]. Similarly, centroid distance distillation has been proposed to reduce domain drift by caching centroids and distilling distances, enhancing the stability of learned representations \[[Sattar Vakili' 2021-09-13](http://arxiv.org/abs/2109.06099v3)\]. In addition, methods such as Normalized Cross-Entropy and synthetic data transfer have been shown to mitigate forgetting in metric learning tasks \[[Kishan Parshotam' 2020-04-22](http://arxiv.org/abs/2004.10862v1)\]. The use of generative models, such as variational autoencoders, has also been explored to enable generative replay, preserving past knowledge while learning new tasks \[[Khurram Javed' 2019-05-29](http://arxiv.org/abs/1905.12588v2)\]. Furthermore, recent approaches like the Self-Controlled Dynamic Expansion Model (SCDEM) leverage multiple pre-trained backbones to adapt to new tasks dynamically, while maintaining semantic consistency through feature distribution alignment \[[Albin Soutif--Cormerais' 2023-08-20](http://arxiv.org/abs/2308.10328v3)\]. These strategies highlight the ongoing efforts to improve rehearsal-based CL by addressing issues like bias, domain drift, and computational efficiency. The theoretical analysis of concurrent versus sequential rehearsal strategies further suggests that hybrid approaches, which combine concurrent training for similar tasks and sequential revisiting for dissimilar ones, may offer superior performance \[[Lukas Balles' 2022-03-28](http://arxiv.org/abs/2203.14544v1)\]. Overall, these works underscore the importance of memory management, model architecture, and learning dynamics in advancing continual learning.

### <a id='category-generalization-and-forgetting-error-analysis'></a>Generalization and Forgetting Error Analysis: 2 papers found.
The category "Generalization and Forgetting Error Analysis" explores the challenges and insights related to model generalization and forgetting in continual learning, particularly in the context of rehearsal-based methods. Research highlights that models trained with rehearsal may remain in a low-loss region, risking overfitting on past data and impairing generalization to new tasks \[[Eli Verwimp' 2021-04-15](http://arxiv.org/abs/2104.07446v1)\]. This issue is further compounded in overparameterized neural networks, where classical generalization bounds often fail, yet these models still exhibit favorable generalization performance \[[Sattar Vakili' 2021-09-13](http://arxiv.org/abs/2109.06099v3)\]. Theoretical analyses suggest that the generalization error can be bounded using Neural Tangent (NT) kernel theory, which provides a framework for understanding the complexity of learning problems and the role of activation function differentiability in error rates \[[Sattar Vakili' 2021-09-13](http://arxiv.org/abs/2109.06099v3)\]. These findings contribute to a deeper understanding of how rehearsal strategies affect both forgetting and generalization, informing the development of more effective continual learning approaches.

Rehearsal techniques have been widely studied in continual learning (CL) to mitigate catastrophic forgetting, with a focus on how past data is sampled and utilized during training. A key challenge in CL is the non-stationarity of data streams, which leads to the loss of previously learned knowledge \[[Lukas Balles' 2022-03-28](http://arxiv.org/abs/2203.14544v1)\]. To address this, rehearsal methods store past observations in a buffer and use them to update the model, with uniform sampling being a common approach \[[Lukas Balles' 2022-03-28](http://arxiv.org/abs/2203.14544v1)\]. However, recent work has explored more sophisticated sample selection policies, such as GRASP, which prioritizes prototypical (easy) samples first and gradually incorporates harder examples \[[Md Yousuf Harun' 2023-08-25](http://arxiv.org/abs/2308.13646v2)\]. This approach not only improves performance but also reduces the number of updates required compared to uniform sampling \[[Md Yousuf Harun' 2023-08-25](http://arxiv.org/abs/2308.13646v2)\]. Additionally, sequential rehearsal strategies, where new data is trained first and past data is revisited sequentially, have shown advantages in scenarios where tasks are less similar \[[Kishan Parshotam' 2020-04-22](http://arxiv.org/abs/2004.10862v1)\]. These findings highlight the importance of adaptive rehearsal strategies in improving CL performance, particularly in overparameterized models and large-scale datasets \[[Kishan Parshotam' 2020-04-22](http://arxiv.org/abs/2004.10862v1)\]\[[Md Yousuf Harun' 2023-08-25](http://arxiv.org/abs/2308.13646v2)\].
