## Related Works

Speech-driven facial animation has been a focal point of research, with recent advancements emphasizing lip synchronization, facial expression realism, and the handling of complex speech patterns. Early methods relied on handcrafted features and rule-based systems, which were limited in capturing nuanced facial movements \[[Se Jin Park' 2023-05-31](http://arxiv.org/abs/2305.19556v3)\]. The advent of deep learning has enabled end-to-end approaches, leveraging pre-trained audio models to encode speech signals and generate facial animations \[[Arturo Oncevay' 2022-10-05](http://arxiv.org/abs/2210.02509v1)\]. These models often use self-supervised learning to incorporate both lexical and non-lexical information, enhancing the realism of facial expressions \[[Inkyu Park' 2023-12-25](http://arxiv.org/abs/2401.08655v2)\].

Several studies have investigated the use of self-supervised audio models for speech-driven facial animation. For instance, FaceXHuBERT utilizes a self-supervised pre-trained HuBERT model to incorporate both lexical and non-lexical information in the audio without requiring a large lexicon \[[Inkyu Park' 2023-12-25](http://arxiv.org/abs/2401.08655v2)\]. This allows for the modeling of subtle facial cues and emotional expressions, which are critical for realistic animation. Similarly, the work by FaceDiffuser employs a diffusion model with a pre-trained large speech representation model to generate speech-driven 3D facial animations, emphasizing non-deterministic generation to capture the variability in facial expressions \[[Se Jin Park' 2023-05-31](http://arxiv.org/abs/2305.19556v3)\]. These models, however, often struggle with the averaging effect caused by phonetically similar syllables, which can lead to inaccurate lip shape generation \[[Se Jin Park' 2023-05-31](http://arxiv.org/abs/2305.19556v3)\].

To address this issue, the proposed Wav2Sem module introduces a semantic decorrelation mechanism that extracts semantic features from the entire audio sequence. This allows for the disentanglement of phonetically similar syllables, reducing the coupling in the audio feature space and improving the precision of lip motion generation \[[Se Jin Park' 2023-05-31](http://arxiv.org/abs/2305.19556v3)\]. This approach is similar to the work in \[[Liyang Chen' 2023-10-11](http://arxiv.org/abs/2310.07236v4)\], where the authors introduced a method to disentangle speech content and style, enhancing the controllability of facial expressions. Additionally, the use of a semantic-aware approach is also explored in \[[Guinan Su' 2023-11-08](http://arxiv.org/abs/2311.04766v2)\], where the authors proposed a method to generate facial expressions with multiple emotions, emphasizing the importance of semantic information in facial animation.

The challenge of handling phonetically similar syllables has also been addressed in other works. For example, the study in \[[Kazi Injamamul Haque' 2023-03-09](http://arxiv.org/abs/2303.05416v1)\] introduced a method to generate facial expressions with controllable emotions, leveraging the relationship between emotional content and facial expressions. This is further extended in \[[Jinbo Xing' 2023-01-06](http://arxiv.org/abs/2301.02379v2)\], where the authors proposed a framework for emotion disentanglement in speech-driven facial animation, enabling the generation of emotionally expressive facial movements. These methods, while effective, often rely on extensive training data and may not generalize well to unseen speakers or emotional contexts \[[Jinbo Xing' 2023-01-06](http://arxiv.org/abs/2301.02379v2)\].

In addition, the use of diffusion models for speech-driven facial animation has gained traction, as demonstrated in \[[Konstantinos Vougioukas' 2019-06-14](http://arxiv.org/abs/1906.06337v1)\], where the authors proposed a diffusion-based approach to generate realistic facial animations with high temporal coherence. This approach is further refined in \[[Jingying Liu' 2020-08-23](http://arxiv.org/abs/2008.10004v1)\], where the authors introduced a framework that combines diffusion with audio-visual information to enhance the realism of facial expressions. These methods, while promising, often require large-scale datasets and may not address the specific issue of phonetically similar syllables affecting lip shape generation \[[Jingying Liu' 2020-08-23](http://arxiv.org/abs/2008.10004v1)\].

In summary, while existing methods have made significant strides in improving the realism and controllability of speech-driven facial animations, the challenge of handling phonetically similar syllables remains a critical area for further research. ### <a id='category-speech-driven-facial-animation-overview'></a>Speech-Driven Facial Animation Overview: 14 papers found.
Speech-driven facial animation has been extensively explored in recent research, focusing on aligning audio and visual modalities to generate realistic talking faces. Early approaches relied on handcrafted features and post-processing techniques to achieve lip synchronization and facial expressions \[[Arturo Oncevay' 2022-10-05](http://arxiv.org/abs/2210.02509v1)\]. However, these methods often resulted in subject-dependent outputs and lacked generalization across different identities \[[Konstantinos Vougioukas' 2019-06-14](http://arxiv.org/abs/1906.06337v1)\]. More recent works have shifted toward end-to-end deep learning frameworks, such as temporal GANs, which directly map audio inputs to visual outputs without intermediate features \[[Guinan Su' 2023-11-08](http://arxiv.org/abs/2311.04766v2)\]. These models incorporate multiple discriminators to ensure detailed frame generation, audio-visual synchronization, and natural facial expressions like blinks and eyebrow movements \[[Konstantinos Vougioukas' 2019-06-14](http://arxiv.org/abs/1906.06337v1)\]. Additionally, some studies have emphasized the importance of modeling phonetic context to improve spatio-temporal alignment of lip movements \[[Se Jin Park' 2023-05-31](http://arxiv.org/abs/2305.19556v3)\]. For instance, the Context-Aware Lip-Sync (CALS) framework explicitly leverages phonetic context to generate more accurate lip motion, with an effective window size of approximately 1.2 seconds \[[Se Jin Park' 2023-05-31](http://arxiv.org/abs/2305.19556v3)\]. Other approaches, such as the Geometry-guided Dense Perspective Network (GDPnet), focus on speaker-independent 3D facial animation by integrating dense connections and attention mechanisms to capture complex interdependencies between audio and visual features \[[Liyang Chen' 2023-10-11](http://arxiv.org/abs/2310.07236v4)\]. Furthermore, methods like Audio-Lip Memory have been introduced to store and retrieve lip motion features aligned with audio inputs, enhancing fine-grained audio-visual coherence \[[Jinbo Xing' 2023-01-06](http://arxiv.org/abs/2301.02379v2)\]. These advancements highlight the growing emphasis on both temporal and spatial modeling to achieve high-quality, realistic speech-driven facial animations.
