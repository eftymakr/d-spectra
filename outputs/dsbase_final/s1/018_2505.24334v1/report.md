## Related Works

Anomaly detection has become a critical task in industrial and embedded systems, particularly in the context of intelligent manufacturing, where identifying defects or irregularities in real-time is essential for maintaining quality control and system reliability \[[Chaoning Zhang' 2023-06-25](http://arxiv.org/abs/2306.14289v2)\]. Traditional approaches have relied on statistical models such as Gaussian mixture models and kernel density estimation \[[Xiangzhong Luo' 2024-11-03](http://arxiv.org/abs/2411.01431v2)\], which are computationally efficient but struggle with complex, high-dimensional data and lack generalization to unseen anomalies. More recently, deep learning techniques have shown significant improvements in detection accuracy, especially for image-based anomaly detection \[[Mykhailo Koshil' 2024-08-27](http://arxiv.org/abs/2408.15113v2)\]. However, most of these models are too large and require substantial computational resources, limiting their applicability in embedded systems \[[Blaž Rolih' 2024-03-07](http://arxiv.org/abs/2403.04932v2)\].

To address these limitations, several lightweight models have been proposed. For instance, \[[Lucian Chauvin' 2025-04-10](http://arxiv.org/abs/2504.08115v1)\] introduced a memory-efficient framework for anomaly detection in time series data, reducing peak memory consumption by 2–5x with minimal computational overhead. Similarly, \[[Mathis Kruse' 2024-04-10](http://arxiv.org/abs/2404.06832v1)\] proposed a tiled ensemble approach that enables high-resolution anomaly detection while keeping memory usage low. This method divides images into tiles and trains a dedicated model for each tile, allowing for efficient processing on embedded devices. However, these methods primarily focus on time series or single-image scenarios and do not directly address the challenges of deploying deep learning models on embedded systems for industrial anomaly detection.

Another line of research focuses on leveraging pre-trained models for anomaly detection. For example, \[[Yuting Sun' 2023-03-07](http://arxiv.org/abs/2303.03611v1)\] proposed using large language models (LLMs) to detect tabular anomalies, demonstrating their potential in identifying low-density data regions without additional model fitting. However, LLMs are not typically used for image-based anomaly detection, and their deployment on embedded devices remains challenging due to their high computational requirements \[[Haiming Yao' 2024-06-17](http://arxiv.org/abs/2406.11507v1)\].

In the context of industrial anomaly detection, several studies have explored the use of vision models. For instance, \[[Qiyu Chen' 2025-05-23](http://arxiv.org/abs/2505.17551v1)\] introduced a method that combines neural radiance fields (NeRFs) with multi-view images to detect anomalies in 3D objects. While this approach achieves state-of-the-art results, it suffers from high computational costs, making it unsuitable for real-time deployment on embedded devices. In contrast, \[[Jiaxin Liu' 2019-12-05](http://arxiv.org/abs/1912.02549v1)\] proposed a lightweight anomaly detection system based on the MobileSAM model, which is specifically designed for mobile and embedded platforms. This work aligns closely with the goals of KairosAD, as it emphasizes the need for efficient and deployable models for industrial anomaly detection.

Additionally, \[[Andrea Castellani' 2020-11-12](http://arxiv.org/abs/2011.06296v1)\] introduced a weakly-supervised approach that utilizes digital twin systems to generate synthetic datasets for anomaly detection, which is particularly useful when labeled data is scarce. This method leverages the Digital Twin to simulate normal operation and combine it with a small set of labeled anomalies, enabling effective training with limited data \[[Martin Serror' 2021-11-23](http://arxiv.org/abs/2111.11714v1)\].

Furthermore, \[[Yedid Hoshen' 2023-07-20](http://arxiv.org/abs/2307.11085v1)\] proposed a method based on the MobileSAM model, which is designed for mobile and embedded platforms, demonstrating that lightweight models can achieve comparable performance to larger models while being more suitable for deployment on resource-constrained devices \[[Jonguk Kim' 2019-11-12](http://arxiv.org/abs/1911.04831v1)\].

In summary, while numerous approaches have been proposed for anomaly detection, the challenge of deploying these models on embedded devices remains a critical issue, particularly in industrial settings. The work presented in this paper addresses this gap by introducing KairosAD, a novel supervised approach that leverages the MobileSAM model to achieve efficient and accurate anomaly detection on embedded devices.
