## Related Works

Open-vocabulary object detection (OVD) has gained significant attention in recent years, enabling models to detect objects from a vocabulary of categories defined by users in natural language at inference time \[[Mingxuan Liu' 2024-05-16](http://arxiv.org/abs/2405.10053v1)\]. This capability extends the applicability of object detection models beyond the limitations of traditional closed-set approaches \[[Han-Cheol Cho' 2023-03-23](http://arxiv.org/abs/2303.13040v1)\]. However, the effectiveness of OVD is often constrained by the quality and specificity of the user-defined vocabulary \[[Sadia Ilyas' 2024-08-20](http://arxiv.org/abs/2408.11221v1)\]. Mis-specified or overly broad vocabularies can lead to suboptimal detection performance, as the model may struggle to focus on relevant categories \[[Yan Li' 2023-11-20](http://arxiv.org/abs/2311.11646v4)\].

To address these challenges, several approaches have been proposed. One line of work focuses on improving the alignment between visual features and textual descriptions, leveraging pre-trained vision-language models (VLMs) such as CLIP \[[María A. Bravo' 2022-11-23](http://arxiv.org/abs/2211.12914v2)\]. These models enable zero-shot detection by mapping visual inputs to semantic spaces \[[Ying Hua Tan' 2017-11-11](http://arxiv.org/abs/1711.05557v1)\]. However, they often rely on accurate and well-structured prompts, which can be difficult to provide for complex or novel object categories \[[Lewei Yao' 2024-04-14](http://arxiv.org/abs/2404.09216v1)\]. To enhance this process, some methods incorporate additional supervision, such as pseudo labels generated from image captioning models \[[Chuang Lin' 2022-11-27](http://arxiv.org/abs/2211.14843v1)\]. These pseudo labels help refine the detection process by providing more detailed and contextually relevant information about the scene \[[Hanoona Rasheed' 2022-07-07](http://arxiv.org/abs/2207.03482v3)\].

Another approach involves refining the vocabulary itself to better match the content of the image. For instance, methods like Pseudo Caption Labeling (PCL) generate dense pseudo captions that provide more detailed and contextually relevant information about the scene \[[Mingfei Gao' 2021-11-18](http://arxiv.org/abs/2111.09452v3)\]. These captions are then used to guide the detection process, improving the model's ability to focus on relevant objects \[[Bang Yang' 2019-11-27](http://arxiv.org/abs/1911.12018v6)\]. Similarly, the VIVO framework leverages large-scale image-tag data to pre-train a visual vocabulary, enabling the generation of more accurate and contextually relevant captions for novel objects \[[Tao Wang' 2022-12-23](http://arxiv.org/abs/2212.12130v7)\].

In addition to improving the alignment between visual and textual information, some works focus on refining the detection process itself. For example, the OpenSeeD framework jointly learns from segmentation and detection tasks, using a pre-trained text encoder to bridge the gap between visual and semantic representations \[[Jiancheng Pan' 2024-08-17](http://arxiv.org/abs/2408.09110v3)\]. This approach allows the model to better handle the complexities of both tasks, leading to improved performance on a variety of benchmarks \[[Shizhe Chen' 2019-07-11](http://arxiv.org/abs/1907.05092v1)\].

The proposed VocAda framework builds upon these ideas by introducing a plug-and-play method to refine the user-defined vocabulary. Unlike previous approaches that require extensive training or complex architectures, VocAda operates at inference time with minimal computational overhead \[[Zheng-Jun Zha' 2019-06-06](http://arxiv.org/abs/1906.02365v1)\]. It uses an image captioner to describe visible objects, parses nouns from those captions, and selects relevant classes from the user-defined vocabulary \[[Yuan Xue' 2021-11-10](http://arxiv.org/abs/2111.06399v1)\]. This approach ensures that the model focuses on the most relevant categories for the given image, improving detection accuracy without requiring additional training \[[Rui Hu' 2022-05-09](http://arxiv.org/abs/2205.04204v2)\].

Open-vocabulary object detection (OvOD) has emerged as a critical area in computer vision, enabling models to detect objects beyond their training set using natural language-defined vocabularies \[[Mingxuan Liu' 2024-05-16](http://arxiv.org/abs/2405.10053v1)\]. This approach allows users to dynamically specify the classes of interest during inference, enhancing flexibility and applicability in real-world scenarios \[[Mingxuan Liu' 2024-05-16](http://arxiv.org/abs/2405.10053v1)\]. However, existing frameworks face challenges in handling vocabularies of varying semantic granularities, leading to performance variability \[[Mingxuan Liu' 2024-05-16](http://arxiv.org/abs/2405.10053v1)\]. To address this, researchers have proposed methods that leverage semantic hierarchies to improve robustness, such as the Semantic Hierarchy Nexus (SHiNe), which integrates class hierarchies to refine detection accuracy \[[Mingxuan Liu' 2024-05-16](http://arxiv.org/abs/2405.10053v1)\]. Additionally, frameworks like CastDet utilize CLIP-activated student-teacher mechanisms to generate high-quality region proposals and pseudo-labels for novel object categories, particularly in aerial imagery \[[Han-Cheol Cho' 2023-03-23](http://arxiv.org/abs/2303.13040v1)\]. Another approach, VLDet, directly learns from image-text pairs to align object features with language embeddings, achieving competitive performance on benchmarks like COCO and LVIS \[[Sadia Ilyas' 2024-08-20](http://arxiv.org/abs/2408.11221v1)\]. Furthermore, object-centric alignment strategies, such as those in the object-centric OVD framework, aim to bridge the gap between object and image-centric representations by leveraging CLIP embeddings and pseudo-labeling \[[Yan Li' 2023-11-20](http://arxiv.org/abs/2311.11646v4)\]. These advancements highlight the ongoing efforts to enhance open-vocabulary detection through improved semantic understanding, efficient pseudo-labeling, and robust vocabulary adaptation \[[Mingxuan Liu' 2024-05-16](http://arxiv.org/abs/2405.10053v1)\]\[[Han-Cheol Cho' 2023-03-23](http://arxiv.org/abs/2303.13040v1)\]\[[Sadia Ilyas' 2024-08-20](http://arxiv.org/abs/2408.11221v1)\]\[[Yan Li' 2023-11-20](http://arxiv.org/abs/2311.11646v4)\].
