## Related Works

Neural rendering methods have gained significant attention for their ability to reconstruct 3D scenes from 2D images, leveraging implicit neural representations to capture geometric and appearance details \[[Youjian Zhang' 2021-12-02](http://arxiv.org/abs/2112.01161v1)\]. These methods typically require multiple input views to optimize the reconstructed scene by minimizing uncertainty across different viewpoints \[[Yue Wu' 2022-06-27](http://arxiv.org/abs/2206.13454v1)\]. However, the reconstruction quality is often limited by the number of input views, especially in complex or dynamic scenes where certain angles of objects are never observed \[[Fitsum Reda' 2022-02-10](http://arxiv.org/abs/2202.04901v4)\]. This limitation has motivated the exploration of data augmentation techniques to improve the robustness and generalization of neural rendering models.

Several studies have investigated the use of video frame interpolation as a data augmentation strategy for neural rendering. For instance, \[[Weihao Zhuang' 2022-06-22](http://arxiv.org/abs/2206.10886v1)\] proposed a motion-compensated frame rate up-conversion algorithm that creates interpolated frames by estimating motion vectors and combining them based on an adaptive threshold. This approach reduces block artifacts and improves the quality of interpolated frames. Similarly, \[[Duolikun Danier' 2023-03-16](http://arxiv.org/abs/2303.09508v3)\] introduced a novel approach for motion-free video coding that eliminates the need for motion estimation and compensation, thereby improving coding efficiency and reducing computational complexity. These methods highlight the importance of motion estimation in frame interpolation, which is crucial for generating high-quality intermediate frames.

Recent advances in video frame interpolation have also focused on improving the quality and efficiency of the interpolation process. For example, \[[Simon Niklaus' 2017-08-05](http://arxiv.org/abs/1708.01692v1)\] proposed a single unified network for frame interpolation that uses a multi-scale feature extractor and a Gram matrix loss to optimize the network for generating crisp and pleasing frames. This approach outperforms state-of-the-art methods on several benchmarks. Additionally, \[[Wentao Shangguan' 2022-02-28](http://arxiv.org/abs/2203.00137v1)\] introduced a framework that leverages optical flow and depth information to synthesize intermediate frames, which is particularly useful for videos with large motions. These methods demonstrate the potential of combining motion estimation with deep learning to achieve high-quality frame interpolation.

The use of neural rendering with augmented data has also been explored in the context of 3D reconstruction. \[[Zhixiang Chi' 2022-07-25](http://arxiv.org/abs/2207.12305v1)\] proposed a video restoration transformer (VRT) that utilizes parallel frame prediction and long-range temporal dependency modeling to improve the quality of video restoration tasks. This approach is particularly effective for tasks such as video super-resolution and frame interpolation. Furthermore, \[[Fitsum A. Reda' 2019-06-13](http://arxiv.org/abs/1906.05928v3)\] introduced a neural video rendering framework that preserves dynamic details even when trained on short monocular videos, which is essential for applications where training data is limited.

In addition to frame interpolation, other techniques have been proposed to enhance the quality of neural rendering. For example, \[[Fukun Yin' 2022-10-20](http://arxiv.org/abs/2210.11170v2)\] introduced a perceptual quality metric for video frame interpolation that learns perceptual features directly from videos, improving the consistency with human perception. This approach is particularly useful for evaluating the quality of interpolated frames in a more realistic manner.
