## Related Works

Neural retrieval models have become a cornerstone of modern information retrieval systems, particularly in Web search applications, due to their ability to learn complex, non-linear relationships between queries and documents \[[Pranav Kasela' 2024-03-20](http://arxiv.org/abs/2403.13468v1)\]. However, the training of these models typically requires a large amount of labeled query-document pairs, which are expensive to obtain \[[Pranav Kasela' 2024-03-20](http://arxiv.org/abs/2403.13468v1)\]. Recent advances have sought to alleviate this issue by leveraging synthetic queries generated by large language models (LLMs), which can be produced at scale using publicly available document collections like ClueWeb22 \[[Pranav Kasela' 2024-03-20](http://arxiv.org/abs/2403.13468v1)\]. While this approach offers scalability, the quality of synthetic queries can vary, leading to suboptimal retrieval performance \[[Pranav Kasela' 2024-03-20](http://arxiv.org/abs/2403.13468v1)\].

Existing methods to address this issue often rely on external re-rankers to filter out noisy query-document pairs \[[Pranav Kasela' 2024-03-20](http://arxiv.org/abs/2403.13468v1)\]. These re-rankers typically operate after the synthetic queries have been generated, which can introduce delays and additional complexity to the training pipeline \[[Pranav Kasela' 2024-03-20](http://arxiv.org/abs/2403.13468v1)\]. In contrast, our work proposes a framework that integrates ranking signals directly into the query generation process using Direct Preference Optimization (DPO) \[[Pranav Kasela' 2024-03-20](http://arxiv.org/abs/2403.13468v1)\]. This approach aims to optimize the generation of synthetic queries to maximize downstream retrieval effectiveness by aligning them with human preferences \[[Pranav Kasela' 2024-03-20](http://arxiv.org/abs/2403.13468v1)\].

DPO has been previously explored for aligning LLMs with human preferences in tasks such as counter-speech generation \[[Hongjin Su' 2024-02-19](http://arxiv.org/abs/2402.12317v2)\]. In that work, DPO was used to align LLM outputs with human preferences, ensuring contextually appropriate and linguistically adaptable responses \[[Hongjin Su' 2024-02-19](http://arxiv.org/abs/2402.12317v2)\]. Similarly, DPO has been applied in the context of generative retrieval for e-commerce search, where it was used to optimize query generation to improve document retrieval accuracy . These studies demonstrate the potential of DPO to improve the quality of synthetic queries by aligning them with human preferences.

In addition to DPO, other approaches have focused on improving the quality of synthetic queries by incorporating additional constraints or using alternative methods for query generation. For instance, some studies have explored the use of multi-task optimization to improve the effectiveness of BERT-based query-by-document retrieval \[[Victor De Lima' 2025-02-28](http://arxiv.org/abs/2503.00238v1)\]. This approach involves using an additional document-level representation learning objective alongside the ranking objective during fine-tuning, which has been shown to improve retrieval performance without requiring additional training samples \[[Victor De Lima' 2025-02-28](http://arxiv.org/abs/2503.00238v1)\].

Another line of research has focused on improving the robustness of dense retrievers against typos by using multi-positive contrastive learning . This approach leverages multiple positive samples (typoed queries) during training, which has been shown to improve the robustness of retrievers against typos . Similarly, some studies have explored the use of query expansion techniques to improve the effectiveness of retrieval models \[[Gustavo Penha' 2023-01-13](http://arxiv.org/abs/2301.05508v1)\]. These techniques involve expanding the original query with additional terms or phrases to increase the chances of retrieving relevant documents \[[Gustavo Penha' 2023-01-13](http://arxiv.org/abs/2301.05508v1)\].

In summary, while the use of synthetic queries generated by LLMs offers a scalable alternative to traditional labeled data, the quality of these queries can vary, leading to suboptimal retrieval performance. Existing methods often rely on external re-rankers to filter out noisy query-document pairs, but these approaches can introduce delays and additional complexity. Our work proposes a framework that integrates ranking signals directly into the query generation process using DPO, aiming to optimize the generation of synthetic queries to maximize downstream retrieval effectiveness.
