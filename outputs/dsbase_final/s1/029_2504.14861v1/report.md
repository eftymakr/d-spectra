## Related Works

Maximum Inner Product Search (MIPS) has been a central challenge in machine learning and information retrieval, particularly for high-dimensional data applications \[[Xinyan Dai' 2019-11-12](http://arxiv.org/abs/1911.04654v2)\]. Traditional approaches to MIPS either rely solely on inner product (IP) similarity, which can suffer from local optima and redundant computations, or reduce the problem to nearest neighbor search (NNS) under the Euclidean metric through space projection, which may lead to topology distortion and information loss \[[Rui Liu' 2018-12-15](http://arxiv.org/abs/1812.06360v1)\]. However, recent research has highlighted the potential of combining IP and Euclidean metrics in indexing and search algorithms to enhance MIPS performance \[[Anshumali Shrivastava' 2014-05-22](http://arxiv.org/abs/1405.5869v1)\].

Several studies have explored the use of graph-based methods for MIPS. For instance, the inner-product navigable small world graph (ip-NSW) has been proposed as a state-of-the-art method for approximate MIPS, achieving an order of magnitude speedup over baseline methods \[[Sebastian Bruch' 2023-09-16](http://arxiv.org/abs/2309.09013v1)\]. Similarly, the paper by Huang et al. introduces the ip-NSW+ algorithm, which improves ip-NSW by introducing an additional angular proximity graph to enhance performance \[[Chao Du' 2014-06-19](http://arxiv.org/abs/1406.4966v2)\]. These works demonstrate the effectiveness of graph-based approaches in MIPS, though they often focus on specific aspects like norm bias or angular proximity.

The use of hashing techniques for MIPS has also been extensively studied. Asymmetric LSH (ALSH) was introduced to address the limitations of traditional LSH for MIPS, converting the problem into approximate near neighbor search using asymmetric transformations \[[Sebastian Bruch' 2023-01-25](http://arxiv.org/abs/2301.10622v1)\]. This approach has been further refined in subsequent works, such as the improved asymmetric LSH for MIPS, which provides better performance guarantees and practical efficiency \[[Stephan S. Lorenzen' 2019-08-23](http://arxiv.org/abs/1908.08656v2)\]. These hashing-based methods have shown promise in reducing computational overhead, especially for large-scale datasets.

Another line of research focuses on dimensionality reduction and efficient indexing for MIPS. The CEOs method, based on concomitants of extreme order statistics, proposes a novel dimensionality reduction technique that allows for sublinear MIPS with exponential indexing space complexity \[[Daichi Amagata' 2021-10-14](http://arxiv.org/abs/2110.07131v1)\]. Variants like sCEOs-TA and coCEOs have been developed to address the space complexity issue, offering linear space solutions while maintaining high search recall \[[Jie Liu' 2019-09-30](http://arxiv.org/abs/1909.13459v2)\]. These methods highlight the importance of leveraging statistical properties of data for efficient MIPS.

In addition, the paper by Zhang et al. presents a lightweight index for c-Approximate Maximum Inner Product Search (c-AMIP), using 2-stable random projections and probability-guaranteed searching conditions to ensure accuracy \[[Xiao Yan' 2018-09-24](http://arxiv.org/abs/1809.08782v2)\]. This approach balances efficiency and accuracy, making it suitable for large-scale applications. The integration of quick-probe techniques further improves the efficiency of these methods by avoiding incremental search processes \[[Xiao Yan' 2018-09-24](http://arxiv.org/abs/1809.08782v2)\].

The work by Li et al. introduces a greedy approach for budgeted MIPS, which allows for flexible trade-offs between search efficiency and quality \[[Anshumali Shrivastava' 2014-10-20](http://arxiv.org/abs/1410.5410v2)\]. This method is particularly useful in scenarios where computational resources are constrained. Similarly, the paper by Wang et al. proposes a bandit-based approach for MIPS that does not require preprocessing and allows users to control the suboptimality of results \[[Ruiqi Guo' 2015-09-04](http://arxiv.org/abs/1509.01469v1)\].

These studies collectively demonstrate the diverse approaches to MIPS, ranging from graph-based methods and hashing techniques to dimensionality reduction and greedy algorithms. Each contributes to the understanding and improvement of MIPS, highlighting the need for adaptable and efficient solutions that can handle the complexities of high-dimensional data.
