## Related Works

Diffusion transformers (DiT) have emerged as a powerful architecture for video generation, offering high-quality results by iteratively denoising video frames \[[Younghye Hwang' 2025-02-06](http://arxiv.org/abs/2502.04056v1)\]. However, their large parameter count and computational demands hinder deployment on edge devices \[[Sanghyun Yi' 2025-02-20](http://arxiv.org/abs/2502.15077v3)\]. Quantization has been explored as a means to reduce storage and computational costs, but existing methods primarily target image generation tasks and struggle with the unique challenges of video generation \[[Zhiteng Li' 2025-05-24](http://arxiv.org/abs/2505.18663v4)\]. This section reviews related works that address these challenges and contribute to the development of efficient video diffusion models.

Several studies have investigated quantization techniques for diffusion models. For instance, \[[Junha Hyung' 2024-11-27](http://arxiv.org/abs/2411.18664v1)\] proposed a post-training quantization (PTQ) method tailored for diffusion models, addressing the issue of changing output distributions over time steps. Similarly, \[[Yifei Gao' 2024-07-22](http://arxiv.org/abs/2407.15508v3)\] introduced a PTQ strategy for video diffusion models, focusing on reducing quantization errors in both token and feature dimensions. However, these methods often fail to account for the spatiotemporal dependencies inherent in video data \[[Aniruddha Mahapatra' 2025-01-09](http://arxiv.org/abs/2501.05442v2)\].

Temporal consistency is another critical challenge in video generation. \[[Yen-Fu Ou' 2014-06-08](http://arxiv.org/abs/1406.2018v1)\] proposed a method to preserve temporal correlations during quantization, while \[[Shangchen Zhou' 2023-12-11](http://arxiv.org/abs/2312.06640v1)\] introduced a framework that aligns frame-wise optimization with the overall video context. These approaches highlight the need for methods that maintain both spatial and temporal coherence during model compression \[[Xiang Wang' 2025-04-15](http://arxiv.org/abs/2504.11289v1)\].

In the context of video diffusion models, \[[Tian Xia' 2024-03-04](http://arxiv.org/abs/2403.02332v4)\] presented a quantization strategy that leverages the unique properties of video data, such as motion and temporal dynamics. This work demonstrated that quantization can be effectively applied to video diffusion models without significant performance degradation. However, the methods often rely on calibration data and are not fully data-free \[[Weiyao Lin' 2015-02-28](http://arxiv.org/abs/1503.00085v1)\].

Recent advances in quantization for video diffusion models have also focused on reducing computational overhead while maintaining visual quality. \[[Yao-Chih Lee' 2023-01-30](http://arxiv.org/abs/2301.13173v1)\] introduced a data-free quantization framework for video DiTs, addressing the limitations of calibration-dependent methods. This work proposed a novel approach to reduce quantization errors without requiring extensive calibration data \[[Sangwoong Yoon' 2023-12-06](http://arxiv.org/abs/2312.03397v1)\].

Furthermore, \[[Desai Xie' 2024-10-10](http://arxiv.org/abs/2410.08151v2)\] explored the use of dynamic quantization for video diffusion models, demonstrating that it can significantly reduce inference time while maintaining high-quality outputs. However, dynamic quantization is not always feasible on resource-constrained devices \[[Andrew Melnik' 2024-05-06](http://arxiv.org/abs/2405.03150v2)\].

In addition to quantization, several studies have focused on improving the temporal consistency of video generation models. \[[Weiyao Lin' 2015-02-28](http://arxiv.org/abs/1503.00082v1)\] proposed a method to enhance temporal coherence by incorporating motion information during the diffusion process. This approach leverages optical flow to guide the diffusion process and improve the temporal alignment of generated frames \[[Kun Su' 2023-03-29](http://arxiv.org/abs/2303.16897v3)\].

Overall, these related works highlight the challenges and opportunities in developing efficient video diffusion models. While existing methods have made progress in reducing computational costs and maintaining visual quality, there remains a need for more robust and data-efficient quantization strategies that can effectively preserve the spatiotemporal coherence of video data.
