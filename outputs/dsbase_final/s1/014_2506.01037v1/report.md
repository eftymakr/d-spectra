## Related Works

Video super-resolution (VSR) has been a focal point in computer vision, with recent advancements leveraging diffusion models to generate high-resolution videos from low-resolution inputs. However, these methods often introduce artifacts and degradations due to the inherent randomness in the diffusion process \[[Janghyeok Han' 2025-02-05](http://arxiv.org/abs/2502.03502v2)\]. Early approaches focused on two-stage pipelines, where video frame interpolation (VFI) and video super-resolution (VSR) were treated as separate tasks, leading to suboptimal performance due to the lack of joint optimization \[[Hongyu An' 2025-02-11](http://arxiv.org/abs/2502.07381v3)\]. More recent methods have attempted to address this by integrating VFI and VSR into a unified framework, such as Zooming SlowMo, which directly reconstructs high-resolution slow-motion videos from low-resolution and low-frame-rate inputs \[[Rui Xie' 2025-01-06](http://arxiv.org/abs/2501.02976v1)\]. These methods, however, still face challenges in temporal consistency and computational efficiency \[[Claudio Rota' 2023-11-27](http://arxiv.org/abs/2311.15908v2)\].

Diffusion-based VSR methods have gained traction due to their ability to generate high-quality results, but they are prone to introducing artifacts and inconsistencies, especially in real-world scenarios with complex degradations \[[Xi Yang' 2023-12-01](http://arxiv.org/abs/2312.00853v2)\]. To mitigate these issues, researchers have explored incorporating self-supervised learning to improve the robustness of VSR models. For instance, NegVSR introduces a noise augmentation strategy to generalize noise modeling in real-world VSR tasks, enhancing the model's ability to handle out-of-distribution noise \[[Xin Yuan' 2024-01-18](http://arxiv.org/abs/2401.10404v1)\]. Similarly, the work on self-supervised video diffusion models, such as Upscale-A-Video, leverages text-guided latent diffusion to ensure temporal consistency and improve the quality of generated videos \[[Meiqin Liu' 2022-11-03](http://arxiv.org/abs/2211.01639v1)\].

Another line of research focuses on improving the temporal coherence of VSR results by integrating attention mechanisms. The Video Restoration Transformer (VRT) employs a transformer-based architecture with long-range temporal dependency modeling, achieving state-of-the-art performance on multiple VSR tasks \[[Takashi Isobe' 2020-08-13](http://arxiv.org/abs/2008.05765v2)\]. Additionally, the work on spatial-temporal video super-resolution, such as the one proposed in the NTIRE 2021 challenge, highlights the importance of temporal modeling in achieving high-quality results \[[Wenyi Lian' 2022-08-24](http://arxiv.org/abs/2208.11608v1)\]. These methods often rely on recurrent architectures or sliding window strategies to capture temporal information, but they can be computationally intensive \[[Yi Xiao' 2023-04-10](http://arxiv.org/abs/2304.04421v3)\].

Recent efforts have also focused on reducing artifacts in generated details by leveraging self-supervised learning. For example, the work on self-supervised ControlNet introduces a method that uses high-resolution features as guidance and employs contrastive learning to extract degradation-insensitive features from low-resolution videos \[[Eduardo Pérez-Pellitero' 2018-07-20](http://arxiv.org/abs/1807.07930v2)\]. This approach helps in reducing artifacts and improving the perceptual quality of the generated videos. Furthermore, the integration of self-supervised learning with diffusion models, such as in the case of the Self-supervised ControlNet with Spatio-Temporal Continuous Mamba, has shown promising results in achieving stable and high-quality VSR results \[[Jingyun Liang' 2022-01-28](http://arxiv.org/abs/2201.12288v2)\].

In summary, while diffusion-based VSR methods have made significant strides, they still face challenges in handling real-world degradations and ensuring temporal consistency. Recent works have explored integrating self-supervised learning and attention mechanisms to address these issues, leading to improved performance and robustness in VSR tasks.
