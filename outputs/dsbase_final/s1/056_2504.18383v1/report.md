## Related Works

Cross-domain Sequential Recommendation (CDSR) has emerged as a critical research direction in recommender systems, aiming to leverage user interactions across multiple domains to enhance recommendation accuracy, particularly in data-scarce scenarios \[[Jesse Harte' 2023-09-17](http://arxiv.org/abs/2309.09261v1)\]. Early approaches primarily relied on overlapping users to transfer knowledge between domains, which limits their applicability in real-world settings where such users are rare \[[Muyang Ma' 2020-12-01](http://arxiv.org/abs/2012.00485v3)\]. Recent studies have explored various techniques to address the overlap dilemma, including federated learning \[[Tingjia Shen' 2024-06-05](http://arxiv.org/abs/2406.03085v1)\], graph-based models \[[Xiaolin Zheng' 2022-09-21](http://arxiv.org/abs/2209.10163v1)\], and attention mechanisms \[[Yu Liu' 2025-03-17](http://arxiv.org/abs/2503.13254v1)\]. However, these methods often struggle with modeling the complex transition patterns inherent in mixed behavior sequences \[[Ajay Krishna Vajjala' 2024-11-29](http://arxiv.org/abs/2411.19862v1)\].

The transition complexity in CDSR refers to the challenges of learning intricate user behavior patterns from heterogeneous and sequential interactions \[[Changshuo Zhang' 2024-08-15](http://arxiv.org/abs/2408.08209v2)\]. Several works have attempted to address this by incorporating temporal and contextual information \[[Wujiang Xu' 2023-11-08](http://arxiv.org/abs/2311.04760v3)\]. For example, Déjà vu introduces a contextualized temporal attention mechanism to weigh the influence of historical actions based on both time and context \[[Lei Guo' 2021-05-07](http://arxiv.org/abs/2105.03300v1)\]. Similarly, Mixed Attention Network (MAN) employs local and global attention modules to capture both domain-specific and cross-domain information \[[Lei Guo' 2023-04-09](http://arxiv.org/abs/2304.04218v1)\]. These approaches, while effective, often require substantial computational resources and may not generalize well to non-overlapping domains \[[Wangyu Wu' 2024-12-31](http://arxiv.org/abs/2502.15694v2)\].

To tackle the overlap dilemma, some studies have focused on non-overlapping cross-domain scenarios, such as Automated Prompting for Non-overlapping Cross-domain Sequential Recommendation (PLCR) \[[Pan Li' 2021-04-17](http://arxiv.org/abs/2104.08490v2)\], which utilizes prompt learning to align domain-invariant and domain-specific representations \[[Jibang Wu' 2020-01-29](http://arxiv.org/abs/2002.00741v1)\]. Similarly, FedDCSR proposes a federated learning framework that disentangles domain-shared and domain-exclusive features to enable cross-domain knowledge transfer without requiring shared user data \[[Li Li' 2024-09-23](http://arxiv.org/abs/2409.14810v1)\]. These methods represent significant progress but still face challenges in efficiently capturing complex user transitions \[[Wujiang Xu' 2023-11-08](http://arxiv.org/abs/2311.04590v6)\].

Recent advancements in large language models (LLMs) have opened new possibilities for CDSR. LLMs demonstrate strong semantic reasoning capabilities, making them promising candidates for modeling user preferences from a semantic perspective \[[Millennium Bismay' 2024-10-30](http://arxiv.org/abs/2410.23180v1)\]. For instance, LLM4CDR leverages LLMs to construct context-aware prompts based on users' purchase history and shared features between domains \[[Wujiang Xu' 2024-05-28](http://arxiv.org/abs/2405.17890v4)\]. This approach effectively bridges the gap between domains and improves recommendation accuracy, particularly when the source and target domains exhibit smaller domain gaps \[[Dongping Chen' 2024-07-01](http://arxiv.org/abs/2407.01505v1)\]. Additionally, GenRec introduces a generative sequential recommendation model that utilizes LLMs for masked item prediction, achieving state-of-the-art results on public datasets \[[Hongzhan Lin' 2023-12-09](http://arxiv.org/abs/2312.05434v1)\].

Despite these advances, challenges remain in efficiently integrating LLMs into CDSR frameworks. For example, SLMRec explores the feasibility of distilling large LLMs into smaller models for sequential recommendation, achieving significant speedups while maintaining performance \[[Zhaohao Lin' 2024-01-27](http://arxiv.org/abs/2401.15369v1)\]. These findings highlight the potential of LLMs in CDSR, but further research is needed to address issues such as computational efficiency and generalization across domains \[[Yiming Ai' 2024-02-22](http://arxiv.org/abs/2402.14679v2)\].

Cross-domain Sequential Recommendation (CDSR) focuses on predicting user interactions across multiple domains by leveraging sequential behavior and cross-domain information \[[Jesse Harte' 2023-09-17](http://arxiv.org/abs/2309.09261v1)\]. A key challenge lies in effectively capturing cross-domain user preferences while considering both intra-sequence and inter-sequence item interactions \[[Wangyu Wu' 2024-12-31](http://arxiv.org/abs/2502.15694v2)\]. Existing methods often rely on overlapping users to propagate cross-domain information, but they assume fully overlapping users and unchanged data distribution, which limits their performance in real-world scenarios \[[Wujiang Xu' 2023-11-08](http://arxiv.org/abs/2311.04590v6)\]. To address these limitations, recent approaches have introduced frameworks like the Adaptive Multi-Interest Debiasing (AMID) framework, which operates under open-world assumptions and improves model adaptability by considering both overlapping and non-overlapping users \[[Wujiang Xu' 2023-11-08](http://arxiv.org/abs/2311.04590v6)\]. Additionally, the integration of visual data through methods like Image Fusion for Cross-Domain Sequential Recommendation (IFCDSR) has enhanced the modeling of cross-domain interests by incorporating item images and multiple attention layers \[[Wangyu Wu' 2024-12-31](http://arxiv.org/abs/2502.15694v2)\]. Privacy concerns have also emerged as a critical issue, prompting the development of privacy-preserving systems such as PriCDSR, which introduces differential privacy mechanisms to protect user data while maintaining recommendation quality \[[Zhaohao Lin' 2024-01-27](http://arxiv.org/abs/2401.15369v1)\]. These advancements highlight the evolving focus on robustness, adaptability, and privacy in CDSR, addressing both technical and ethical challenges in the field.
