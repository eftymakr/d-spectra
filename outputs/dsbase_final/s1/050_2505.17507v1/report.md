## Related Works

The field of open-source machine learning (ML) resource management has seen significant growth, driven by the increasing availability of models and datasets on platforms like Hugging Face. However, existing platforms lack structured representations that enable advanced querying and analysis, such as tracing model evolution or recommending relevant datasets \[[G. Murugesan' 2010-04-20](http://arxiv.org/abs/1004.3566v1)\]. This limitation has prompted the development of new approaches to better organize and utilize these resources. 

Several recent works have explored the use of knowledge graphs for managing and analyzing ML resources. For instance, \[[Joakim Bruslund Haurum' 2021-03-19](http://arxiv.org/abs/2103.10895v1)\] introduces a knowledge graph for organizing and querying ML models, emphasizing the importance of structured representations for efficient resource management. Similarly, \[[Fabio Casati' 2021-12-13](http://arxiv.org/abs/2112.06775v1)\] proposes a knowledge graph to capture relationships between ML models and datasets, enabling more sophisticated queries and analyses. These works highlight the potential of knowledge graphs in organizing and leveraging ML resources effectively.

In the context of open-source ML, \[[Ziyu Li' 2022-07-19](http://arxiv.org/abs/2207.09315v1)\] discusses the challenges of managing and sharing ML models and datasets, emphasizing the need for structured and accessible representations. The authors argue that existing platforms often lack the necessary infrastructure to support advanced queries and analyses. This aligns with the findings of \[[João Barroca' 2022-06-02](http://arxiv.org/abs/2206.01087v1)\], which highlights the limitations of current platforms in supporting complex queries and analyses of ML resources. 

The use of knowledge graphs for resource recommendation has also been explored in several studies. \[[Karanja Evanson Mwangi' 2015-04-08](http://arxiv.org/abs/1504.02017v1)\] presents a knowledge graph-based approach for recommending ML models and datasets, leveraging semantic relationships to improve recommendation accuracy. Similarly, \[[Çağrı Çöltekin' 2022-04-11](http://arxiv.org/abs/2204.05042v3)\] introduces a knowledge graph to support the recommendation of ML resources, emphasizing the importance of structured data for effective recommendations. These studies demonstrate the potential of knowledge graphs in enhancing the usability and accessibility of ML resources.

Moreover, the integration of knowledge graphs with benchmarking frameworks has been explored in recent research. \[[Jun Gao' 2023-11-26](http://arxiv.org/abs/2311.15289v2)\] presents a benchmarking framework that leverages a knowledge graph to evaluate ML models and datasets, enabling more comprehensive and accurate assessments. This approach is similar to the HuggingBench framework proposed in this work, which uses a knowledge graph to support multi-task benchmarking for ML resource management \[[Federico Bianchi' 2020-04-30](http://arxiv.org/abs/2004.14843v1)\]. 

In addition to benchmarking, the use of knowledge graphs for tracing model evolution has been explored in several studies. \[[Stefano Melacci' 2024-02-20](http://arxiv.org/abs/2402.13302v1)\] introduces a knowledge graph to track the evolution of ML models, enabling researchers to analyze how models have changed over time. This aligns with the goals of HuggingKG, which captures the evolution of ML models and datasets through structured representations \[[Irlan Grangel-Gonzalez' 2021-07-05](http://arxiv.org/abs/2107.01910v1)\]. 

These studies collectively highlight the importance of structured representations in managing and analyzing ML resources. The proposed HuggingKG and HuggingBench framework builds on these insights, offering a comprehensive solution for organizing, querying, and benchmarking ML resources in an open-source environment.
