## Related Works

Protein dynamics play a crucial role in determining biological functions and properties, and traditional methods for studying these dynamics rely heavily on time-consuming molecular dynamics (MD) simulations . Recent advances in generative modeling, particularly denoising diffusion models, have enabled efficient and accurate protein structure prediction and conformation sampling by learning distributions over crystallographic structures . However, integrating physical supervision into these data-driven approaches remains challenging, as standard energy-based objectives often lead to intractable optimization . 

Several studies have explored the use of diffusion models for protein structure prediction and conformation sampling. Similarly,  introduced Structure Language Modeling (SLM) as a novel framework for efficient protein conformation generation, leveraging discrete variational auto-encoders and conditional language modeling to capture sequence-specific conformation distributions . 

The integration of physical models with generative models has also been explored in other domains. For instance,  investigated the use of momentum in diffusion sampling to mitigate divergence artifacts, demonstrating that incorporating Heavy Ball (HB) momentum into existing diffusion numerical methods can expand their stability regions and improve image quality . Similarly,  leveraged denoising diffusion probabilistic models (DDPMs) to train an uncertainty-aware surrogate model for turbulence simulations, showing that DDPMs can successfully capture the whole distribution of solutions and accurately estimate the uncertainty of the simulations . 

In the context of protein conformation sampling,  introduced Str2Str, a novel structure-to-structure translation framework capable of zero-shot conformation sampling with roto-translation equivariant property. This method leverages an amortized denoising score matching objective trained on general crystal structures and has no reliance on simulation data during both training and inference . 

The work by  focused on fast sampling of protein conformational dynamics, proposing an approach that modifies molecular vibrations analysis to efficiently isolate low-frequency vibrations in proteins and apply it to a set of five proteins of varying complexity. This approach results in collective variables that consistently enhance sampling of protein conformational transitions and associated free energy surfaces on timescales compatible with high throughput applications . 

In addition, \[[Bowen Jing' 2023-04-05](http://arxiv.org/abs/2304.02198v1)\] explored the use of energy-based models for atomic-resolution protein conformations, proposing an energy-based model (EBM) of protein conformations that operates at atomic scale. The model is trained solely on crystallized protein data and achieves performance close to that of the Rosetta energy function, a state-of-the-art method widely used in protein structure prediction and design \[[Bowen Jing' 2023-04-05](http://arxiv.org/abs/2304.02198v1)\]. 

The work by \[[Yikai Liu' 2023-10-03](http://arxiv.org/abs/2310.01768v2)\] proposed BackDiff, a new generative model designed to achieve generalization and reliability in the protein backmapping problem. BackDiff leverages the conditional score-based diffusion model with geometric representations, enabling end-to-end training and efficient sampling across different proteins and diverse CG models without the need for retraining \[[Yikai Liu' 2023-10-03](http://arxiv.org/abs/2310.01768v2)\]. 

In summary, while diffusion models have shown great promise in protein structure prediction and conformation sampling, integrating physical supervision remains a significant challenge. The proposed Energy-based Alignment (EBA) method aims to address this challenge by aligning generative models with feedback from physical models, efficiently calibrating them to appropriately balance conformational states based on their energy differences \[[Jiarui Lu' 2024-02-16](http://arxiv.org/abs/2402.10433v2)\].

### <a id='category-diffusion-models-in-protein-conformation-sampling'></a>Diffusion Models in Protein Conformation Sampling: 3 papers found.
Recent advances in generative modeling, particularly diffusion models, have significantly impacted protein conformation sampling and dynamics prediction. These models leverage large-scale molecular dynamics (MD) data to explore the conformational space of proteins efficiently \[[Jiarui Lu' 2024-02-16](http://arxiv.org/abs/2402.10433v2)\]. However, existing approaches often struggle with capturing temporal dependencies between conformations or generating time-independent samples \[[Yuning Shen' 2025-05-23](http://arxiv.org/abs/2505.17478v2)\]. To address these limitations, ConfRover was introduced as an autoregressive model that simultaneously learns protein conformation and dynamics from MD trajectories, supporting both time-dependent and time-independent sampling \[[Yuning Shen' 2025-05-23](http://arxiv.org/abs/2505.17478v2)\]. At its core, ConfRover uses a modular architecture that includes an encoding layer, a temporal module, and an SE(3) diffusion model as the structure decoder \[[Yuning Shen' 2025-05-23](http://arxiv.org/abs/2505.17478v2)\]. This framework enables the generation of high-quality protein conformations while maintaining the temporal coherence of the generated trajectories. Similarly, ConfDiff, another diffusion-based model, incorporates a force-guided network with a mixture of data-based score models to generate protein conformations with rich diversity and high fidelity \[[Yan Wang' 2024-03-21](http://arxiv.org/abs/2403.14088v2)\]. By integrating physical prior knowledge, ConfDiff ensures that the sampled conformations remain close to the equilibrium distribution, thereby improving the accuracy of conformational predictions. Additionally, BackDiff addresses the challenge of backmapping from coarse-grained (CG) models to all-atom configurations by leveraging conditional score-based diffusion models with geometric representations \[[Yikai Liu' 2023-10-03](http://arxiv.org/abs/2310.01768v2)\]. This approach allows for efficient and accurate generation of all-atom protein structures from CG models, facilitating applications in protein design and drug discovery. These models collectively highlight the growing importance of diffusion models in capturing the complex dynamics of protein conformations while integrating physical constraints to enhance the accuracy and reliability of generated structures.

Prior work has focused on reformulating diffusion sampling as an ordinary differential equation (ODE) or stochastic differential equation (SDE) and applying higher-order numerical methods to speed up the process \[[Suttisak Wizadwongsa' 2023-07-20](http://arxiv.org/abs/2307.11118v1)\]. However, these methods often suffer from instability, leading to divergence artifacts, especially when using a limited number of sampling steps \[[Suttisak Wizadwongsa' 2023-07-20](http://arxiv.org/abs/2307.11118v1)\]. To mitigate this, researchers have explored the integration of optimization techniques such as Heavy Ball (HB) momentum, which expands the stability region of existing diffusion solvers and ensures first-order convergence \[[Suttisak Wizadwongsa' 2023-07-20](http://arxiv.org/abs/2307.11118v1)\]. Additionally, the Generalized Heavy Ball (GHVB) method was introduced to balance accuracy and artifact suppression by offering a flexible trade-off between these factors \[[Suttisak Wizadwongsa' 2023-07-20](http://arxiv.org/abs/2307.11118v1)\]. Experimental results demonstrate that these momentum-based approaches significantly improve image quality and reduce artifacts, outperforming state-of-the-art diffusion solvers in both pixel-based and latent-based models under low-step sampling conditions \[[Suttisak Wizadwongsa' 2023-07-20](http://arxiv.org/abs/2307.11118v1)\]. These advancements highlight the importance of stability and convergence in diffusion sampling and provide a foundation for future research in this area.
