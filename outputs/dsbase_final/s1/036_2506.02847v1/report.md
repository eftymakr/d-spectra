## Related Works

The deployment of large language models (LLMs) on edge devices has garnered significant attention due to the increasing demand for real-time, privacy-preserving AI applications. However, the resource constraints of edge devices, including limited storage, power, and computational capabilities, pose significant challenges for deploying LLMs effectively \[[Nobel Dhar' 2024-12-13](http://arxiv.org/abs/2412.12178v2)\]. Prior work has explored various strategies to address these challenges, such as model compression, hardware acceleration, and efficient inference techniques. These strategies aim to reduce the computational and memory footprint of LLMs while maintaining acceptable levels of accuracy and performance.

Model compression techniques, including pruning, quantization, and knowledge distillation, have been widely studied to reduce the size and computational demands of LLMs \[[Mozhgan Navardi' 2025-02-19](http://arxiv.org/abs/2502.15816v1)\]. These methods aim to maintain model performance while reducing the number of parameters and operations required for inference. However, many of these approaches are either model-specific or require extensive retraining, which may not be feasible for edge deployment \[[Xudong Lu' 2024-02-22](http://arxiv.org/abs/2402.14800v2)\]. In contrast, our work focuses on algorithm-hardware co-design, which enables efficient inference without requiring extensive model retraining \[[Abhimanyu Bambhaniya' 2024-06-03](http://arxiv.org/abs/2406.01698v3)\].

Hardware acceleration has also been a key area of research for improving the performance of LLMs on edge devices. Several studies have proposed custom hardware accelerators tailored for LLM inference, such as the HADES accelerator, which leverages hardware-level speculative decoding to enhance performance and energy efficiency \[[Jude Haris' 2021-10-01](http://arxiv.org/abs/2110.00478v1)\]. Similarly, the C2HLSC framework demonstrates how LLMs can be used to refactor C code into hardware-synthesizable formats, enabling more efficient execution on hardware platforms \[[Moshe Kimhi' 2022-05-30](http://arxiv.org/abs/2205.15437v2)\]. These approaches highlight the potential of combining software and hardware optimizations to improve inference efficiency.

Another important direction is the development of lightweight models that can be deployed on edge devices. For example, the PruneVid method introduces a visual token pruning technique to reduce redundancy in video data, improving the efficiency of multi-modal LLMs \[[Jinhao Li' 2024-10-06](http://arxiv.org/abs/2410.04466v4)\]. Additionally, the activation sparsity method has been proposed to compress LLMs by selectively pruning activations, achieving significant memory and computational savings while maintaining high accuracy \[[Zonghang Li' 2024-10-01](http://arxiv.org/abs/2410.00531v1)\]. These techniques demonstrate the effectiveness of sparsity-based approaches in reducing the resource footprint of LLMs.

In addition to model and hardware optimizations, energy efficiency is a critical consideration for edge deployment. The SECDA framework proposes a hardware/software co-design methodology to reduce the design time of optimized DNN inference accelerators on edge devices, achieving up to 3.5× speedup and 2.9× energy reduction compared to CPU-only inference \[[Yanbiao Liang' 2025-04-07](http://arxiv.org/abs/2505.03745v1)\]. Similarly, the AMED framework introduces automatic mixed-precision quantization to optimize the trade-off between accuracy and hardware efficiency, demonstrating superior performance over existing schemes \[[Zhang Runhua' 2023-02-01](http://arxiv.org/abs/2302.00282v1)\].

These prior works highlight the importance of combining algorithmic and hardware-level optimizations to enable efficient LLM deployment on edge devices. Our work, CLONE, builds upon these advancements by introducing an in-depth algorithm-hardware co-design approach that integrates real-time energy optimization while maintaining robust generality.
