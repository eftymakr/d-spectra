## Related Works

Vision Transformers (ViTs) have emerged as a powerful alternative to convolutional neural networks (CNNs) in computer vision tasks, primarily due to their ability to capture global context through self-attention mechanisms \[[Siyuan Lu' 2020-09-18](http://arxiv.org/abs/2009.08605v1)\]. However, the deployment of ViTs on hardware platforms, especially FPGAs, remains challenging due to the computational and power demands of complex arithmetic operations such as Softmax and LayerNorm, which are sensitive to model accuracy \[[Chao Fang' 2022-08-12](http://arxiv.org/abs/2208.06118v1)\]. Prior work has primarily focused on optimizing matrix multiplication operations on hardware while keeping these complex operations on the CPU, resulting in suboptimal performance due to communication overhead \[[Christodoulos Peltekis' 2024-02-15](http://arxiv.org/abs/2402.10118v2)\]. This section reviews related works that address these challenges.

Several studies have explored hardware acceleration for ViTs, focusing on reducing computational complexity and improving efficiency. For instance, \[[Ruiqi Chen' 2024-12-25](http://arxiv.org/abs/2412.18948v1)\] proposed a hardware accelerator for ViTs that leverages parallelism in matrix multiplication operations, but these approaches often leave complex operations like Softmax and LayerNorm on the CPU, leading to inefficiencies. Similarly, \[[Shashank Nag' 2023-02-17](http://arxiv.org/abs/2302.09108v1)\] introduced a framework for deploying ViTs on FPGAs, but it did not address the mapping of critical arithmetic operations onto hardware, resulting in limited performance gains. These works highlight the need for a holistic approach that integrates all operations of the ViT model onto hardware.

Recent efforts have also focused on quantization and low-precision arithmetic to reduce computational demands. For example, \[[Dayou Du' 2024-05-01](http://arxiv.org/abs/2405.00314v1)\] introduced mixed-precision implementations for finite element kernels, demonstrating that reduced-precision formats can maintain accuracy while improving performance. This concept has been extended to ViTs, where \[[Paul M. Riechers' 2024-05-07](http://arxiv.org/abs/2405.04134v1)\] proposed a compiler for exploring mixed-precision MX formats on dataflow hardware accelerators for LLM inference. However, these works primarily focus on language models and do not directly address the unique challenges of ViTs.

Another line of research has explored the use of specialized hardware for specific operations in ViTs. For example, \[[Qiming Zhang' 2023-03-27](http://arxiv.org/abs/2303.15105v1)\] introduced a hardware-efficient adaptive token pruning framework for ViTs, aiming to reduce computational costs while maintaining accuracy. Similarly, \[[Luca Collini' 2024-11-29](http://arxiv.org/abs/2412.00214v2)\] proposed a method for accelerating ViTs by exploiting the sparsity of attention maps, which can significantly reduce the number of operations required. These approaches, while effective, often require significant modifications to the model architecture or training process.

In addition, \[[Maxim Milakov' 2018-05-08](http://arxiv.org/abs/1805.02867v2)\] developed a framework for deploying ViTs on FPGAs by leveraging the inherent parallelism of the transformer architecture. This work demonstrated that by mapping all operations of the ViT model onto hardware, significant performance improvements can be achieved. However, it did not explore the use of new data formats to further enhance efficiency and accuracy.

In summary, while several works have addressed the challenges of ViT hardware acceleration, there remains a gap in the integration of all operations onto hardware and the use of novel data formats to further improve performance. Our work fills this gap by introducing a new arithmetic format, Microscaling Integer (MXInt), and demonstrating its effectiveness in achieving high area efficiency and accuracy while significantly improving performance.
