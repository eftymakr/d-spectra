## Related Works

In the field of in-context learning (ICL), the quality of demonstrations plays a crucial role in the performance of large language models (LLMs). Existing approaches often rely on perplexity as a metric to detect noisy annotations, assuming that noisy samples yield higher perplexities than clean ones \[[Sumyeong Ahn' 2022-12-01](http://arxiv.org/abs/2212.01189v1)\]. However, this assumption is challenged when the noise ratio is high, leading to unreliable detection of noisy samples \[[Haoyu Wang' 2025-03-11](http://arxiv.org/abs/2503.08684v1)\]. 

Several studies have explored the use of perplexity in text generation under noisy conditions. For instance, \[[Lianli Gao' 2023-08-10](http://arxiv.org/abs/2308.05286v2)\] investigates the use of perplexity to evaluate the quality of generated text, highlighting its limitations in noisy environments. Similarly, \[[Yangdi Lu' 2024-06-22](http://arxiv.org/abs/2406.16966v1)\] proposes a method to improve the robustness of perplexity-based evaluation by incorporating domain-specific knowledge, which is essential for accurate assessment in real-world scenarios. 

The issue of noisy annotations has also been addressed in the context of training data. \[[Wei Shen' 2023-10-08](http://arxiv.org/abs/2310.05199v5)\] discusses the challenges of training models on datasets with noisy labels, emphasizing the need for robust training strategies. This aligns with the findings in \[[Valerio Astuti' 2022-12-19](http://arxiv.org/abs/2212.09813v1)\], which introduces a method to mitigate the impact of noisy labels by using synthetic samples with soft labels. 

In addition to perplexity-based approaches, other methods have been proposed to improve the robustness of ICL. \[[Onil Boussim' 2023-09-19](http://arxiv.org/abs/2309.10642v7)\] presents a framework that uses synthesized neighbors to correct perplexity estimates, which is similar to the dual debiasing framework introduced in this work. \[[Jessica López Espejel' 2023-03-22](http://arxiv.org/abs/2303.12869v1)\] further explores the use of synthetic data to enhance the performance of models trained on noisy datasets. 

The problem of noise in training data has also been studied in the context of image generation. \[[Chenhe Dong' 2023-05-18](http://arxiv.org/abs/2305.10736v1)\] introduces a method to generate high-quality images from corrupted data, which is relevant to the broader challenge of handling noisy inputs. \[[Kazi Toufique Elahi' 2024-01-25](http://arxiv.org/abs/2401.14360v2)\] extends this idea by proposing a framework that leverages the structure of the data to improve the robustness of generative models. 

In the context of text-to-image generation, \[[Mohammadmehdi Naghiaei' 2022-02-27](http://arxiv.org/abs/2202.13446v1)\] addresses the issue of bias in generated images by using debiasing techniques. This is related to the dual debiasing framework proposed here, as both approaches aim to improve the robustness of models in the presence of noise. 

The use of synthetic data to improve model performance has also been explored in the context of reinforcement learning. \[[Jacopo Di Iorio' 2019-07-24](http://arxiv.org/abs/1907.11142v1)\] proposes a method to generate synthetic data that can be used to train models in environments with limited data. In summary, the related works highlight the challenges of handling noisy data in the context of ICL and the various approaches that have been proposed to address these challenges. ### <a id='category-noise-robust-training-strategies'></a>Noise-Robust Training Strategies: 5 papers found.
In the context of noise-robust training strategies, several studies have addressed the challenges posed by noisy labels in deep learning. For instance, improper dataset construction can lead to poor generalization, as models trained on biased or noisy data may fail to perform well on unseen examples \[[Sumyeong Ahn' 2022-12-01](http://arxiv.org/abs/2212.01189v1)\]. To mitigate this, researchers have proposed techniques that aim to debias datasets by underestimating easy-to-learn samples and highlighting difficult ones, but these methods can be undermined by noisy labels, which are often misclassified as difficult samples \[[Sumyeong Ahn' 2022-12-01](http://arxiv.org/abs/2212.01189v1)\]. Additionally, prior work has shown that denoising algorithms run before debiasing can be ineffective, as they may reduce the impact of valuable bias-conflicting samples \[[Sumyeong Ahn' 2022-12-01](http://arxiv.org/abs/2212.01189v1)\]. Another approach focuses on mitigating the impact of noisy labels by leveraging synthetic samples and mixing strategies to improve representation learning, with methods such as estimating soft targets and using mixture models to correct noisy labels \[[Haoyu Wang' 2025-03-11](http://arxiv.org/abs/2503.08684v1)\]. Furthermore, in the realm of sentiment analysis, noise reduction methods have been explored to improve performance on noisy Bangla texts, although current approaches have shown limited success \[[Yangdi Lu' 2024-06-22](http://arxiv.org/abs/2406.16966v1)\]. In the context of scene graph generation, noisy ground-truth annotations have been identified as a critical issue, leading to the development of strategies like NICEST, which aims to correct noisy labels and improve training through multi-teacher knowledge distillation \[[Wei Shen' 2023-10-08](http://arxiv.org/abs/2310.05199v5)\]. These studies collectively highlight the importance of addressing noise in training data and the need for robust strategies to ensure generalization and performance in real-world scenarios.

### <a id='category-debiasing-techniques-in-generative-models'></a>Debiasing Techniques in Generative Models: 8 papers found.
In the context of debiasing techniques in generative models, several studies have addressed the issue of bias arising from various sources, such as data imbalances, model training, and inherent biases in large language models (LLMs). For instance, source bias in PLM-based retrieval models has been identified, where models prefer LLM-generated content over human-written ones, leading to skewed relevance scores \[[Sumyeong Ahn' 2022-12-01](http://arxiv.org/abs/2212.01189v1)\]. Similarly, in scene graph generation, models tend to favor common predicates over informative ones due to semantic and training sample imbalances, necessitating debiasing frameworks like DB-SGG \[[Haoyu Wang' 2025-03-11](http://arxiv.org/abs/2503.08684v1)\]. Reinforcement learning from human feedback also faces challenges, as reward models may develop shortcuts, such as length bias, favoring longer outputs without necessarily improving helpfulness \[[Lianli Gao' 2023-08-10](http://arxiv.org/abs/2308.05286v2)\]. Additionally, non-traditional data sources, like social media, often introduce biases due to self-selection processes, requiring integration with more representative survey data to correct for such issues \[[Yangdi Lu' 2024-06-22](http://arxiv.org/abs/2406.16966v1)\]. In cross-country comparisons, sample selection bias, such as survivor bias, can distort outcomes, and quantile-based correction methods have been proposed to address this \[[Wei Shen' 2023-10-08](http://arxiv.org/abs/2310.05199v5)\]. In abstractive summarization, factual inconsistency is attributed to language and irrelevancy biases, with frameworks like CoFactSum using counterfactual estimation to mitigate these effects \[[Valerio Astuti' 2022-12-19](http://arxiv.org/abs/2212.09813v1)\]. Recommendation systems also suffer from popularity bias, where popular items are overrepresented, and studies have examined user preferences and algorithmic fairness in this context \[[Onil Boussim' 2023-09-19](http://arxiv.org/abs/2309.10642v7)\]. Finally, biclustering algorithms face a bias in evaluation metrics like the H-score, which favors small clusters, and corrections have been proposed to ensure fair comparisons \[[Jessica López Espejel' 2023-03-22](http://arxiv.org/abs/2303.12869v1)\]. These works collectively highlight the multifaceted nature of bias in generative models and the need for diverse debiasing strategies tailored to specific challenges.
