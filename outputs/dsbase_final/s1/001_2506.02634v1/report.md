## Related Works

The efficient serving of large language models (LLMs) has become a critical area of research, particularly with the increasing demand for real-time and scalable inference. Caching intermediate results, such as the key-value (KV) cache, has emerged as a vital technique to reduce redundant computations and improve throughput and latency \[[Junyoung Park' 2025-04-21](http://arxiv.org/abs/2504.15364v4)\]. However, the design of caching strategies remains highly workload-dependent, and prior studies have primarily focused on synthetic workloads, which may not fully capture the complexities of real-world scenarios \[[Yixuan Wang' 2025-05-24](http://arxiv.org/abs/2505.20334v2)\]. This section reviews related works that address KV cache management, eviction policies, and their impact on LLM serving performance.

Several approaches have been proposed to optimize KV cache usage. For instance, **KeyDiff** introduces a training-free KV cache eviction method based on key similarity, allowing efficient processing of arbitrarily long prompts under strict resource constraints \[[Yuan Feng' 2024-07-16](http://arxiv.org/abs/2407.11550v5)\]. It does not rely on attention scores, enabling the use of optimized attention mechanisms like FlashAttention \[[Yilong Chen' 2024-08-07](http://arxiv.org/abs/2408.03675v2)\]. Similarly, **Lookahead Q-Cache (LAQ)** generates pseudo lookahead queries to better approximate the true decoding-stage queries, leading to more consistent and accurate cache eviction \[[Chaoyi Jiang' 2024-11-26](http://arxiv.org/abs/2411.17089v2)\]. Experimental results show that LAQ outperforms existing methods across various budget levels, achieving a 1–4 point improvement on LongBench under limited cache budgets \[[Wonbeom Lee' 2024-06-28](http://arxiv.org/abs/2406.19707v1)\].

The management of KV caches in multi-GPU environments has also received attention. **Ada-KV** proposes an adaptive budget allocation strategy that allocates compression budgets based on the unique attention patterns of each attention head, offering plug-and-play benefits for integration with prior cache eviction methods \[[Guangtao Wang' 2025-03-11](http://arxiv.org/abs/2503.08879v1)\]. Extensive evaluations on 13 datasets from Ruler and 16 datasets from LongBench demonstrate substantial quality improvements over existing methods \[[Zefan Cai' 2025-05-30](http://arxiv.org/abs/2505.24133v4)\].

In the context of private LLM inference, **NACL** proposes a general framework for long-context KV cache eviction that achieves up to 50% reduction in cache size with over 95% performance maintenance \[[Zhaoyuan Su' 2025-05-24](http://arxiv.org/abs/2506.02006v2)\]. This aligns with the findings of the current study, which highlights the need for workload-aware eviction policies that adapt to varying request patterns .

Recent work has also focused on improving the accuracy of KV cache eviction by leveraging attention patterns. **SAGE-KV** proposes a self-attention guided eviction method that performs a one-time top-k selection at both token and head levels to compress the KV cache, achieving 4x higher memory efficiency with improved accuracy . Similarly, **R-KV** introduces a redundancy-aware KV cache compression method specifically targeting redundant tokens in reasoning models, preserving nearly 100% of the full KV cache performance using only 10% of the KV cache .

The importance of workload-aware policies is further emphasized in **MorphServe**, which introduces two asynchronous, token-level runtime mechanisms: quantized layer swapping and pressure-aware KV cache resizing, enabling state-preserving transitions with minimum runtime overhead .

These related works collectively underscore the growing interest in optimizing KV cache management for LLM serving. They highlight the importance of workload-aware strategies, efficient eviction policies, and context-aware caching mechanisms to enhance performance and reduce resource consumption.

### <a id='category-kv-cache-management-strategies'></a>KV Cache Management Strategies: 6 papers found.
Key-value (KV) cache management strategies are critical for optimizing the performance and efficiency of large language models (LLMs) during inference, particularly in scenarios involving long-context generation and resource-constrained environments \[[Junyoung Park' 2025-04-21](http://arxiv.org/abs/2504.15364v4)\]. Existing approaches often rely on attention scores or uniform budget allocation, which may not align with real-world inference patterns \[[Yixuan Wang' 2025-05-24](http://arxiv.org/abs/2505.20334v2)\]. For instance, methods like KeyDiff leverage key similarity to identify and retain important tokens without relying on attention scores, enabling compatibility with optimized attention mechanisms like FlashAttention \[[Junyoung Park' 2025-04-21](http://arxiv.org/abs/2504.15364v4)\]. Similarly, Lookahead Q-Cache (LAQ) introduces pseudo lookahead queries to better approximate real decoding-stage queries, improving the consistency and accuracy of cache eviction \[[Yixuan Wang' 2025-05-24](http://arxiv.org/abs/2505.20334v2)\]. Meanwhile, Ada-KV proposes an adaptive budget allocation strategy that considers the unique attention patterns of each attention head, offering a more nuanced approach to cache management \[[Yuan Feng' 2024-07-16](http://arxiv.org/abs/2407.11550v5)\]. Other works, such as NACL, aim to reduce KV cache size while maintaining performance by combining accurate attention score statistics with diversified random eviction strategies, achieving up to 50% reduction in cache size with minimal performance loss \[[Yilong Chen' 2024-08-07](http://arxiv.org/abs/2408.03675v2)\]. Additionally, KVPR addresses the challenges of offloading KV cache to CPU memory by overlapping GPU recomputation with I/O operations, minimizing idle GPU time and improving inference throughput \[[Chaoyi Jiang' 2024-11-26](http://arxiv.org/abs/2411.17089v2)\]. These strategies collectively highlight the growing need for workload-aware and adaptive approaches to KV cache management, especially as LLMs continue to scale in both model size and inference complexity \[[Wonbeom Lee' 2024-06-28](http://arxiv.org/abs/2406.19707v1)\].
