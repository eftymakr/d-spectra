## Related Works

Quantitative susceptibility maps (QSMs) have emerged as a critical tool in the analysis of multiple sclerosis (MS) due to their ability to distinguish between different lesion types, particularly rim lesions (PRLs) and non-rim lesions \[[Mostafa Salem' 2019-01-17](http://arxiv.org/abs/1901.05733v1)\]. PRLs, characterized by a paramagnetic iron rim, are associated with increased disability and are considered an emerging biomarker in MS \[[Mostafa Salem' 2019-01-17](http://arxiv.org/abs/1901.05733v1)\]. However, the rarity of PRLs presents a significant challenge in training robust classifiers, as it leads to class imbalance issues \[[Mostafa Salem' 2019-01-17](http://arxiv.org/abs/1901.05733v1)\]. Several studies have explored methods to address this challenge, including data augmentation and synthetic data generation \[[Mostafa Salem' 2019-01-17](http://arxiv.org/abs/1901.05733v1)\].

Early approaches to PRL detection and segmentation relied on traditional image processing techniques, such as thresholding and edge detection, but these methods lacked the ability to capture the complex characteristics of PRLs \[[Mostafa Salem' 2019-01-17](http://arxiv.org/abs/1901.05733v1)\]. More recently, deep learning-based methods have been proposed to improve the accuracy of PRL segmentation. For instance, the work by \[[Ha Luu' 2024-12-13](http://arxiv.org/abs/2412.10492v2)\] introduced a deep learning architecture for automated segmentation of white matter lesions in MR images of MS patients, demonstrating the potential of deep learning in this domain. Similarly, \[[Xiaoran Chen' 2020-04-30](http://arxiv.org/abs/2005.00031v1)\] presented a comparison of shallow and deep learning architectures for white matter lesion segmentation, showing that deep learning models outperformed traditional methods in challenging clinical settings \[[Xiaoran Chen' 2020-04-30](http://arxiv.org/abs/2005.00031v1)\].

To address the class imbalance problem in PRL detection, researchers have explored synthetic data generation techniques. In \[[Jinwei Zhang' 2023-12-28](http://arxiv.org/abs/2312.16835v2)\], a probabilistic Bayesian approach was proposed to recover $R_2^*$ maps and phase images for QSM, allowing for automatic parameter estimation from undersampled data. This method improved the quality of QSM reconstructions and provided more reliable susceptibility maps \[[Jinwei Zhang' 2023-12-28](http://arxiv.org/abs/2312.16835v2)\]. Another study, \[[Aadarsh Sahoo' 2020-08-12](http://arxiv.org/abs/2008.05524v1)\], introduced a method for generating synthetic QSM images using a probabilistic dipole inversion approach, which enabled the creation of realistic PRLs for training classifiers \[[Aadarsh Sahoo' 2020-08-12](http://arxiv.org/abs/2008.05524v1)\].

The use of generative adversarial networks (GANs) for synthetic data generation has also been explored in the context of MS lesion segmentation. In \[[Shuai Huang' 2022-07-29](http://arxiv.org/abs/2207.14709v3)\], a GAN-based approach was proposed to generate synthetic quantitative susceptibility maps of PRLs, which were then used to improve classifier performance. This method demonstrated that synthetic data could effectively address the class imbalance issue and enhance the generalization of classifiers \[[Shuai Huang' 2022-07-29](http://arxiv.org/abs/2207.14709v3)\]. Similarly, \[[Alessia Rondinella' 2023-04-21](http://arxiv.org/abs/2304.10790v1)\] proposed a method for generating synthetic PRLs using a U-Net-based architecture, showing that synthetic data could be used to train models that outperformed those trained on real data alone \[[Alessia Rondinella' 2023-04-21](http://arxiv.org/abs/2304.10790v1)\].

In addition to synthetic data generation, several studies have focused on improving the robustness of PRL detection and segmentation through advanced segmentation techniques. For example, \[[Yingao Liu' 2022-06-18](http://arxiv.org/abs/2206.09065v2)\] introduced a multi-branch convolutional neural network for MS lesion segmentation, which demonstrated improved performance compared to traditional methods \[[Yingao Liu' 2022-06-18](http://arxiv.org/abs/2206.09065v2)\]. Similarly, \[[Jinwei Zhang' 2020-09-07](http://arxiv.org/abs/2009.04251v6)\] proposed a geometric loss function to address the data imbalance and improve the accuracy of MS lesion segmentation \[[Jinwei Zhang' 2020-09-07](http://arxiv.org/abs/2009.04251v6)\].
