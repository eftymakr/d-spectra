## Related Works

Dense retrieval has gained significant traction in recent years due to its ability to efficiently retrieve relevant documents by encoding text into dense vector representations. These representations are typically learned through contrastive learning, where the goal is to align positive (relevant) document-query pairs while pushing away negative (irrelevant) pairs in the embedding space \[[Gautier Izacard' 2021-12-16](http://arxiv.org/abs/2112.09118v4)\]. Pre-trained language models (PLMs), such as BERT and LLaMA, serve as the backbone for these dense retrievers, with their fine-tuning on downstream tasks often leading to performance improvements \[[Alireza Salemi' 2023-06-28](http://arxiv.org/abs/2306.16478v1)\]. However, recent studies have questioned the extent to which fine-tuning contributes to retrieval performance, suggesting that much of the knowledge is already embedded during pre-training \[[Xinyu Ma' 2022-04-22](http://arxiv.org/abs/2204.10641v1)\].

The role of fine-tuning in dense retrieval has been explored in several works. For instance, the Dense Passage Retrieval (DPR) framework, which uses BERT as its encoder, demonstrates that fine-tuning primarily adjusts neuron activations rather than reorganizing the underlying knowledge \[[Craig Macdonald' 2021-08-13](http://arxiv.org/abs/2108.06279v2)\]. This implies that the retrieval performance is largely dependent on the pre-trained knowledge, and fine-tuning may not significantly alter the semantic representations \[[Xingliang Lei' 2024-08-27](http://arxiv.org/abs/2408.15011v2)\]. This finding is consistent with other studies that argue that the majority of retrieval knowledge is acquired during pre-training, and fine-tuning serves more as an optimization step \[[Lee Xiong' 2020-07-01](http://arxiv.org/abs/2007.00808v2)\].

However, this pattern is not universal. Some models, such as Contriever, which uses mean pooling over token embeddings, and LLaMA-based models, which rely on decoder-only architectures, show different behavior. In these cases, fine-tuning plays a more active role in shaping the retrieval performance, suggesting that the effectiveness of fine-tuning may depend on the specific architecture and representation strategy used \[[Yibin Lei' 2023-06-05](http://arxiv.org/abs/2306.03166v1)\]. This variability highlights the need for further investigation into how different pre-training and fine-tuning strategies impact retrieval performance.

Additionally, the choice of representation strategy, such as using CLS tokens versus mean pooling, has been shown to influence the effectiveness of fine-tuning. While CLS tokens capture global representations, mean pooling can provide more balanced representations across the entire sequence \[[Atsushi Keyaki' 2024-03-25](http://arxiv.org/abs/2403.16915v3)\]. These differences in representation strategies may explain why some models benefit more from fine-tuning than others \[[Vithursan Thangarasa' 2023-03-18](http://arxiv.org/abs/2303.10464v2)\].

In summary, the role of fine-tuning in dense retrieval is nuanced and depends on factors such as the pre-training strategy, model architecture, and representation method. While pre-trained knowledge forms the foundation of retrieval performance, fine-tuning can still play a crucial role in optimizing and adapting the model to specific tasks \[[Yang Bai' 2024-03-21](http://arxiv.org/abs/2403.14074v1)\].

Contrastive learning has emerged as a powerful paradigm for training dense retrievers, enabling the development of unsupervised models that can achieve strong performance without requiring large amounts of labeled data \[[Gautier Izacard' 2021-12-16](http://arxiv.org/abs/2112.09118v4)\]. This approach has been shown to outperform traditional term-frequency methods like BM25 on several benchmark datasets, particularly in scenarios where training data is limited \[[Gautier Izacard' 2021-12-16](http://arxiv.org/abs/2112.09118v4)\]. Recent studies have further refined contrastive learning by introducing novel tasks such as contrastive span prediction, which allows for more discriminative text representation learning while avoiding the bypass effect of decoders \[[Alireza Salemi' 2023-06-28](http://arxiv.org/abs/2306.16478v1)\]. Additionally, methods like Approximate Nearest Neighbor Negative Contrastive Estimation (ANCE) have been proposed to address the mismatch between training and testing data distributions by constructing more representative negative examples \[[Xinyu Ma' 2022-04-22](http://arxiv.org/abs/2204.10641v1)\]. Relevance-aware contrastive learning has also been introduced to adaptively weigh contrastive losses based on the estimated relevance of positive pairs, leading to improved performance on retrieval benchmarks \[[Craig Macdonald' 2021-08-13](http://arxiv.org/abs/2108.06279v2)\]. Furthermore, multi-task learning approaches such as M3 have been developed to combine multiple learning objectives, enhancing retrieval performance on large-scale datasets \[[Xingliang Lei' 2024-08-27](http://arxiv.org/abs/2408.15011v2)\]. These advancements highlight the ongoing evolution of contrastive learning in dense retrieval, addressing key challenges such as data scarcity, model robustness, and cross-lingual transfer \[[Lee Xiong' 2020-07-01](http://arxiv.org/abs/2007.00808v2)\].
