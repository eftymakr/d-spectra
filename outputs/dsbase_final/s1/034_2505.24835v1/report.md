## Related Works

Fund allocation in financial domains has attracted significant attention, with recent studies focusing on improving both forecasting accuracy and risk-aware allocation strategies. Traditional approaches such as prediction-only or predict-then-optimize (PnO) frameworks often suffer from goal mismatch, as they decouple forecasting from optimization \[[Yanlong Wang' 2025-03-10](http://arxiv.org/abs/2503.06928v2)\]. This issue is further exacerbated by the inherent uncertainty introduced by state-of-the-art time series forecasting models \[[Zhe Li' 2023-05-18](http://arxiv.org/abs/2305.10721v2)\]. To address these challenges, several works have explored hybrid frameworks that integrate forecasting and allocation in a unified manner.

A notable approach is the work by \[[Zhen Zeng' 2023-04-11](http://arxiv.org/abs/2304.04912v1)\], which introduces a framework that combines forecasting and optimization in an end-to-end manner. This method aligns the objective function with the forecasting task, ensuring that the optimization process is informed by the forecasted outcomes. Similarly, \[[Chiyu Zhang' 2023-06-21](http://arxiv.org/abs/2306.12086v2)\] proposes a risk-aware framework that incorporates uncertainty quantification during the forecasting phase to improve allocation decisions. This approach is particularly relevant in financial contexts where uncertainty in forecasts can significantly impact portfolio performance.

In addition to end-to-end frameworks, some studies have focused on improving the calibration of forecasting uncertainty. For instance, \[[Apostolos Ampountolas' 2023-07-17](http://arxiv.org/abs/2307.08853v1)\] presents a method for calibrating uncertainty in regression settings, which is crucial for reliable allocation decisions. This method, called CRUDE, is designed to produce well-calibrated uncertainty estimates without assuming a specific distribution over the error. The importance of uncertainty calibration is also emphasized in \[[Eric Zelikman' 2020-05-26](http://arxiv.org/abs/2005.12496v6)\], where the authors propose a probabilistic recalibration scheme that improves the reliability of forecasts by adjusting for miscalibration.

Another line of research has explored the use of advanced forecasting models in financial applications. For example, \[[Yizhan Shu' 2024-06-13](http://arxiv.org/abs/2406.09578v2)\] introduces a hybrid model combining autoregressive integrated moving average (ARIMA) with artificial neural networks (ANNs) to forecast financial time series. This approach is particularly useful for capturing both short-term and long-term dependencies in financial data. Similarly, \[[Luca Butera' 2024-10-18](http://arxiv.org/abs/2410.14630v2)\] proposes a deep learning-based framework that leverages convolutional neural networks (CNNs) and transformers to model both short-term and long-term dependencies in financial time series, achieving superior performance compared to traditional models.

In the context of asset allocation, \[[Damian Kisiel' 2022-06-07](http://arxiv.org/abs/2206.03246v1)\] presents a framework that integrates forecasting and optimization by directly optimizing the Sharpe ratio, a widely used risk-adjusted performance metric. This approach eliminates the need for separate forecasting and optimization steps, thereby reducing the risk of goal mismatch. The proposed method, called Portfolio Transformer, is inspired by attention mechanisms in natural language processing and has shown promising results in financial applications.

Finally, \[[Daniel Cunha Oliveira' 2025-03-14](http://arxiv.org/abs/2503.11499v2)\] explores the use of probabilistic forecasting models for financial time series, emphasizing the importance of capturing uncertainty in both the forecasting and allocation processes. This work highlights the need for robust uncertainty quantification to ensure reliable allocation decisions in volatile financial markets.
