## Related Works

Brain tumor segmentation remains a critical task in medical imaging, with significant implications for diagnosis, treatment planning, and prognosis. Recent advances in deep learning have enabled substantial improvements in segmentation accuracy, particularly through the use of convolutional neural networks (CNNs) and encoder-decoder architectures. However, challenges such as limited annotated data, high computational demands, and the need for robust generalization across diverse datasets continue to hinder progress \[[Mehdi Cherti' 2021-05-31](http://arxiv.org/abs/2106.00116v4)\]. 

Several studies have explored the use of pre-trained models to address these challenges. For instance, \[[Yiwen Tang' 2023-10-04](http://arxiv.org/abs/2310.03059v8)\] introduced the use of EfficientNet within an asymmetric U-Net architecture to improve 3D semantic segmentation of brain tumors by leveraging 2D pre-trained weights. This approach demonstrated promising performance on the BraTS 2020 challenge, highlighting the potential of transferring knowledge from 2D to 3D domains \[[Yiwen Tang' 2023-10-04](http://arxiv.org/abs/2310.03059v8)\]. Similarly, \[[Bijay Adhikari' 2024-12-18](http://arxiv.org/abs/2412.14100v1)\] proposed a lightweight model, MBDRes-U-Net, which integrates multi-branch residual blocks and fused attention mechanisms to reduce computational overhead while maintaining high segmentation accuracy on BraTS datasets \[[Bijay Adhikari' 2024-12-18](http://arxiv.org/abs/2412.14100v1)\].

The nnU-Net framework has emerged as a state-of-the-art solution for brain tumor segmentation, offering a flexible and robust architecture for handling multi-modal MRI data \[[Shubhaankar Gupta' 2021-11-30](http://arxiv.org/abs/2112.00113v1)\]. However, it requires extensive training and may not fully utilize pre-trained weights, which can be a limitation in low-resource settings \[[Shubhaankar Gupta' 2021-11-30](http://arxiv.org/abs/2112.00113v1)\]. To address this, \[[Haozhe Jia' 2022-02-10](http://arxiv.org/abs/2202.05268v1)\] introduced a method that combines transfer learning with focal loss and oversampling techniques to handle class imbalance, achieving an impressive accuracy of 96% on brain tumor classification tasks \[[Haozhe Jia' 2022-02-10](http://arxiv.org/abs/2202.05268v1)\]. This work underscores the importance of addressing data imbalance in medical imaging tasks \[[Haozhe Jia' 2022-02-10](http://arxiv.org/abs/2202.05268v1)\].

Another notable contribution is the development of the DR-Unet104 model, which incorporates a bottleneck residual block and dropout regularization to enhance performance in brain tumor segmentation \[[Yang Shu' 2022-06-08](http://arxiv.org/abs/2206.03726v2)\]. This model demonstrated competitive results on the BraTS 2020 challenge, achieving a mean Dice score of 0.8673 on testing data \[[Yang Shu' 2022-06-08](http://arxiv.org/abs/2206.03726v2)\]. The use of 2D convolutions in DR-Unet104 also allows for deployment on lower power devices, making it a practical choice for resource-constrained environments \[[Yang Shu' 2022-06-08](http://arxiv.org/abs/2206.03726v2)\].

In addition to CNN-based approaches, \[[Hicham Messaoudi' 2020-11-22](http://arxiv.org/abs/2011.11052v1)\] proposed a 3D encoder-decoder architecture with uncertainty estimation, leveraging test-time dropout and data augmentation to improve robustness in medical image segmentation \[[Hicham Messaoudi' 2020-11-22](http://arxiv.org/abs/2011.11052v1)\]. This method was evaluated on the BraTS 2019 challenge, demonstrating its effectiveness in handling uncertainty in segmentation tasks \[[Hicham Messaoudi' 2020-11-22](http://arxiv.org/abs/2011.11052v1)\].

Furthermore, \[[Hao Dong' 2017-05-10](http://arxiv.org/abs/1705.03820v3)\] introduced a 3D-CNN architecture with uncertainty estimation, trained with patching techniques to reduce memory consumption and decrease the effect of unbalanced data. The model also introduces voxel-wise uncertainty, both epistemic and aleatoric, using test-time dropout and data-augmentation respectively. This work highlights the importance of incorporating uncertainty estimation in medical image segmentation \[[Hao Dong' 2017-05-10](http://arxiv.org/abs/1705.03820v3)\].

The integration of pre-trained models and domain-specific adaptations continues to be a focal point in advancing the field. Recent works have also explored the use of transfer learning strategies to improve performance in brain tumor segmentation \[[Daniel E. Cahall' 2021-08-15](http://arxiv.org/abs/2108.06772v1)\].
