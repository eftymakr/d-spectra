## Related Works

Neural retrieval methods using transformer-based pre-trained language models have advanced multilingual and cross-lingual retrieval capabilities \[[Craig Macdonald' 2021-08-13](http://arxiv.org/abs/2108.06279v2)\]. However, their effectiveness for low-resource, morphologically rich languages such as Amharic remains underexplored due to data scarcity and suboptimal tokenization \[[Aleksandar Petrov' 2023-05-17](http://arxiv.org/abs/2305.15425v2)\]. Prior work has focused on improving retrieval performance for high-resource languages, with limited attention given to low-resource settings \[[Luke Melas-Kyriazi' 2020-01-25](http://arxiv.org/abs/2002.00733v1)\]. 

Several studies have explored the use of pre-trained models for low-resource languages, such as the development of Amharic-specific models for speech recognition and named entity recognition \[[Zhong Zhou' 2018-04-21](http://arxiv.org/abs/1804.07878v2)\]. For instance, the Whisper model has been fine-tuned for Amharic, showing improvements when trained on a mix of existing and new datasets \[[Wayne Xin Zhao' 2022-11-27](http://arxiv.org/abs/2211.14876v1)\]. Similarly, the development of an Amharic named entity recognition system based on bidirectional long short-term memory with a conditional random fields layer has achieved an F1 score of 93% \[[Maharaj Brahma' 2025-04-14](http://arxiv.org/abs/2504.10335v2)\]. These efforts highlight the challenges of working with low-resource languages and the importance of language-specific adaptations.

In the realm of information retrieval, cross-lingual and multilingual retrieval models have been developed to address the scarcity of training data for low-resource languages \[[Anastasia Zhukova' 2024-12-13](http://arxiv.org/abs/2412.10008v1)\]. For example, the work on cross-lingual dense passage retrieval for extremely low-resource languages has shown promising results, although performance remains modest \[[Michael A. Hedderich' 2018-07-02](http://arxiv.org/abs/1807.00745v2)\]. The use of multilingual pre-trained language models has also been explored to improve retrieval effectiveness, with studies demonstrating the potential of cross-lingual transfer learning \[[Thong Nguyen' 2023-06-20](http://arxiv.org/abs/2306.11397v1)\]. 

The challenge of adapting models to low-resource languages has also been addressed through techniques such as data augmentation and transfer learning \[[Yanis Labrak' 2024-02-22](http://arxiv.org/abs/2402.15010v2)\]. For example, back-translation has been used to generate synthetic data for low-resource language pairs, significantly improving translation performance \[[Constantine Lignos' 2022-02-24](http://arxiv.org/abs/2202.12288v1)\]. Similarly, the use of multilingual BERT and other transformer-based models has been explored to enhance retrieval performance for low-resource languages \[[Amin Abolghasemi' 2022-02-01](http://arxiv.org/abs/2202.00373v2)\]. 

The importance of language-specific adaptation has been emphasized in several studies, highlighting the need for models tailored to the linguistic characteristics of low-resource languages \[[Hansi Hettiarachchi' 2024-12-20](http://arxiv.org/abs/2412.16365v1)\]. For instance, the development of an Amharic-specific dense retrieval model based on pre-trained Amharic BERT and RoBERTa backbones has shown significant improvements in retrieval effectiveness \[[Sachin Kumar' 2021-06-12](http://arxiv.org/abs/2106.06797v2)\]. These models outperform existing multilingual baselines, demonstrating the potential of language-specific adaptations in low-resource settings \[[Mohamed Trabelsi' 2021-02-23](http://arxiv.org/abs/2102.11903v2)\].

Additionally, the role of tokenization in low-resource language processing has been highlighted in several studies \[[Hamees Sayed' 2024-11-01](http://arxiv.org/abs/2411.00727v2)\]. For example, the use of morphology-aware segmentation and constrained BPE has been shown to improve the performance of machine translation and language modeling for morphologically rich languages \[[Vijay Chandrasekhar' 2015-08-11](http://arxiv.org/abs/1508.02496v3)\]. These findings underscore the importance of optimizing tokenization strategies for low-resource languages to enhance model performance.

In summary, while there has been progress in developing neural retrieval models for low-resource languages, there remains a need for further research to address the unique challenges of these settings. The work presented in this paper contributes to this body of research by introducing Amharic-specific dense retrieval models that achieve significant improvements in retrieval effectiveness \[[Gustavo Penha' 2023-01-13](http://arxiv.org/abs/2301.05508v1)\].
