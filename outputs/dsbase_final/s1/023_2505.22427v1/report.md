## Related Works

The field of radar-camera geometric calibration has seen significant advancements, particularly in addressing the challenges of data sparsity and measurement uncertainty. Early approaches relied on target-based calibration methods, which required complex setups and well-designed experimental conditions \[[Kun Shi' 2024-10-24](http://arxiv.org/abs/2410.19872v1)\]. These methods, while effective in controlled environments, were not suitable for real-time or automatic calibration during system operation. For instance, the work by \[[Kun Shi' 2024-10-24](http://arxiv.org/abs/2410.19872v1)\] proposed a method that uses a single corner reflector and iteratively collects radar and camera data to solve the perspective-n-point problem, but it still requires a dedicated calibration target.

Recent efforts have shifted towards data-driven and targetless calibration methods. A notable approach is the online targetless radar-camera extrinsic calibration method proposed by \[[Yunfei Long' 2021-08-24](http://arxiv.org/abs/2108.10637v1)\], which uses a coarse and fine convolutional neural network to achieve precise sensor registration without dedicated calibration targets. This method is particularly useful in real-world scenarios where dedicated targets are not available. However, it still relies on a boosting-inspired training algorithm and real-world data collection, which can be time-consuming and resource-intensive.

Another significant contribution is the work by , which presents a continuous-time 3D radar-to-camera extrinsic calibration algorithm that utilizes radar velocity measurements and does not require specialized radar retroreflectors. This approach is more efficient and suitable for real-time applications. However, it focuses primarily on the extrinsic calibration and does not address the issue of height uncertainty in radar data, which is a critical factor in automatic calibration.

The integration of deep learning techniques has also been explored for radar-camera calibration. The paper by \[[Jisong Kim' 2023-07-17](http://arxiv.org/abs/2307.10249v5)\] introduces a novel approach that leverages deep learning to extract common features from raw radar data and camera images, enabling an online targetless calibration method. This method implicitly utilizes common features to match identical objects from both data sources, improving the robustness of the calibration process. However, it does not explicitly address the issue of height uncertainty, which is crucial for accurate geometric calibration.
