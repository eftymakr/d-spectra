## Related Works

Single-pixel imaging (SPI) has attracted significant attention due to its ability to reconstruct images using a single-pixel detector, which is particularly useful in non-visible wavelengths and low-light conditions . Early SPI methods relied on iterative algorithms such as the Gerchberg-Saxton algorithm and the difference of convex (DC) method, which were computationally intensive and had limited performance . To address these limitations, deep learning-based approaches have been introduced, offering faster and more accurate reconstructions .

Deep-unrolling and plug-and-play (PnP) approaches have emerged as de-facto standards for solving SPI inverse problems . PnP methods are iterative algorithms where regularization is implicitly performed by an off-the-shelf deep denoiser . These methods are flexible for varying compression ratios (CRs) but are limited in reconstruction accuracy and speed . In contrast, unrolling approaches, which transform truncated iterative optimization processes into end-to-end trainable networks, typically achieve better accuracy with faster inference but require fine-tuning or retraining when CR changes .

Several studies have explored unrolling techniques for SPI. For instance, the work by  introduced a generative adversarial network (GAN) based reconstruction algorithm for single-pixel imaging, demonstrating efficient reconstruction in 10ms and higher quality. This method was verified with both synthetic and real-world experiments, showing good quality reconstruction of a real-world plaster using a 0.05 sampling rate . Similarly,  compared Hadamard and Fourier single-pixel imaging techniques, finding that Fourier single-pixel imaging (FSI) is more efficient than Hadamard single-pixel imaging (HSI), while HSI is more noise-robust than FSI .

Recent advances in deep unrolling have focused on improving the flexibility and performance of SPI reconstruction. For example,  proposed a method for single-pixel tracking and imaging under weak illumination, leveraging temporal correlation of bucket signals to steer the illumination beam and achieve both trajectory and clear image of the object. This approach was validated experimentally with an average number of detected photons down to 1.88 photons/speckle .

To integrate the strengths of both PnP and unrolling approaches,  designed an efficient deep image restorer (DIR) for the unrolling of HQS (half quadratic splitting) and ADMM (alternating direction method of multipliers). A general proximal trajectory (PT) loss function was proposed to train HQS/ADMM-unrolling networks such that the learned DIR approximates the proximal operator of an ideal explicit restoration regularizer. This approach enables the model to flexibly handle varying CRs with a single model, similar to PnP algorithms, while outperforming previous CR-specific unrolling networks in both reconstruction accuracy and speed .

Additionally, \[[Ernest K. Ryu' 2019-05-14](http://arxiv.org/abs/1905.05406v1)\] demonstrated that Plug-and-Play (PnP) methods can provably converge with properly trained denoisers, under a certain Lipschitz condition on the denoisers. This work provides theoretical guarantees for PnP-FBS and PnP-ADMM, which is crucial for the robustness of the proposed approach \[[Ernest K. Ryu' 2019-05-14](http://arxiv.org/abs/1905.05406v1)\]. Furthermore, \[[Chirayu D. Athalye' 2023-09-28](http://arxiv.org/abs/2309.16899v3)\] showed that PnP-ISTA and PnP-ADMM exhibit linear convergence for symmetric denoisers under mild conditions, which supports the effectiveness of the proposed method in achieving fast and accurate reconstructions \[[Chirayu D. Athalye' 2023-09-28](http://arxiv.org/abs/2309.16899v3)\].

The use of unrolling techniques in SPI has also been explored in other contexts. For example, \[[Junqi Tang' 2022-03-21](http://arxiv.org/abs/2203.11156v3)\] proposed operator sketching for deep unrolling networks, which accelerates the computation of high-dimensional forward and adjoint operators by approximating their products using sketching techniques. This method is particularly useful for large-scale imaging tasks such as 3D cone-beam X-ray CT and 4D MRI imaging \[[Junqi Tang' 2022-03-21](http://arxiv.org/abs/2203.11156v3)\]. Similarly, \[[Antoine Monod' 2022-09-06](http://arxiv.org/abs/2209.02854v2)\] introduced a deep plug-and-play prior for video restoration, demonstrating that a network trained specifically for video denoising can achieve better temporal stability and restoration performance compared to single-image networks \[[Antoine Monod' 2022-09-06](http://arxiv.org/abs/2209.02854v2)\].

In summary, the related works highlight the growing interest in deep learning and unrolling techniques for SPI, as well as the challenges associated with balancing flexibility, accuracy, and computational efficiency. The proposed approach builds on these advancements by integrating the strengths of both PnP and unrolling methods to achieve a robust and efficient solution for SPI reconstruction.

### <a id='category-pnp-methods-and-their-limitations'></a>PnP Methods and Their Limitations: 11 papers found.
Plug-and-Play (PnP) methods have gained significant traction in image restoration tasks by integrating pre-trained deep denoisers into optimization algorithms, offering flexibility and performance improvements \[[Charles A. Bouman' 2023-06-12](http://arxiv.org/abs/2306.07233v1)\]. These methods typically replace proximal steps in algorithms like ADMM or FISTA with denoisers such as BM3D or CNNs, enabling regularization through learned priors \[[Samuel Hurault' 2023-11-02](http://arxiv.org/abs/2311.01216v1)\]. However, PnP approaches face challenges in terms of theoretical guarantees, convergence, and stability, particularly when using non-symmetric denoisers \[[Afonso M. Teodoro' 2018-01-02](http://arxiv.org/abs/1801.00605v1)\]. Recent work has addressed these limitations by proposing modifications to PnP updates to ensure convergence to minima of the objective function \[[Samuel Hurault' 2023-11-02](http://arxiv.org/abs/2311.01216v1)\]. Additionally, efforts have been made to extend PnP to sample from posterior distributions, leading to methods like Generative PnP (GPnP), which uses proximal generators to produce diverse samples \[[Charles A. Bouman' 2023-06-12](http://arxiv.org/abs/2306.07233v1)\]. To improve scalability and efficiency, incremental variants of PnP-ADMM have been introduced, enabling large-scale applications \[[Yu Sun' 2020-06-05](http://arxiv.org/abs/2006.03224v2)\]. Furthermore, the integration of score-based diffusion models (SBMs) into PnP frameworks has been explored, allowing the reuse of powerful SBMs without retraining \[[Chicago Y. Park' 2024-12-15](http://arxiv.org/abs/2412.11108v1)\]. The use of non-Euclidean settings, such as Bregman distances, has also been proposed to broaden the applicability of PnP algorithms \[[Yanan Zhao' 2024-02-20](http://arxiv.org/abs/2402.12872v2)\]. Despite these advancements, challenges remain in ensuring robustness and avoiding hallucinated features, especially when using learned denoisers \[[Afonso M. Teodoro' 2018-01-02](http://arxiv.org/abs/1801.00605v1)\]. Overall, PnP methods continue to evolve, addressing both practical and theoretical limitations while maintaining their effectiveness in image restoration tasks.

### <a id='category-convergence-analysis-of-pnp-algorithms'></a>Convergence Analysis of PnP Algorithms: 8 papers found.
The convergence analysis of Plug-and-Play (PnP) algorithms has been a focal point in recent research, aiming to establish theoretical guarantees for their performance in image restoration tasks. PnP methods replace traditional proximal operators with powerful denoisers, enabling flexible and effective regularization for inverse problems \[[Charles A. Bouman' 2023-06-12](http://arxiv.org/abs/2306.07233v1)\]. However, the convergence of these algorithms has been challenging to analyze due to the lack of explicit objective functions in modern denoisers \[[Yu Sun' 2020-06-05](http://arxiv.org/abs/2006.03224v2)\]. Early works focused on kernel denoisers, such as nonlocal means, and established convergence under strong assumptions on the forward model \[[Samuel Hurault' 2023-11-02](http://arxiv.org/abs/2311.01216v1)\]. Subsequent studies relaxed these assumptions, showing that symmetric kernel denoisers can lead to linear convergence for PnP-ISTA and PnP-ADMM under mild conditions \[[Chirayu D. Athalye' 2023-09-28](http://arxiv.org/abs/2309.16899v3)\]. Additionally, research has extended convergence analysis to nonsymmetric denoisers, proposing a unified framework that ensures global linear convergence for a broader range of denoisers \[[Arghya Sinha' 2025-05-21](http://arxiv.org/abs/2505.15318v1)\]. These advancements have been supported by empirical validation, demonstrating improved accuracy and robustness in tasks like image deblurring and super-resolution \[[Afonso M. Teodoro' 2018-01-02](http://arxiv.org/abs/1801.00605v1)\]. Furthermore, recent work has addressed the convergence of PnP algorithms with deep learning-based denoisers, introducing techniques such as real spectral normalization to ensure Lipschitz conditions necessary for theoretical guarantees \[[Ernest K. Ryu' 2019-05-14](http://arxiv.org/abs/1905.05406v1)\]. Overall, these studies highlight the growing understanding of PnP algorithms' convergence properties, bridging the gap between empirical success and rigorous theoretical analysis \[[Yanan Zhao' 2024-02-20](http://arxiv.org/abs/2402.12872v2)\].
