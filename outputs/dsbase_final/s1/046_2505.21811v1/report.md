## Related Works

Cross-domain sequential recommendation (CDSR) has gained significant attention in recent years due to its ability to leverage user behavior across multiple domains to improve recommendation performance. Several approaches have been proposed to address the challenges of modeling cross-domain sequential interactions, often relying on transformer-based architectures with domain-specific modifications \[[Guy Hadad' 2025-04-29](http://arxiv.org/abs/2504.20859v1)\]. For instance, \[[Jiayi Chen' 2022-11-02](http://arxiv.org/abs/2211.01297v4)\] introduces a model that integrates domain-aware modules to enhance the self-attention mechanism, while \[[Haipeng Li' 2024-10-17](http://arxiv.org/abs/2410.13588v1)\] proposes a reinforcement learning module to simulate the evolution of user preferences across domains. These methods typically focus on explicitly incorporating domain-specific components, often at the expense of leveraging the core self-attention mechanism within the transformer \[[Huiyuan Chen' 2022-12-08](http://arxiv.org/abs/2212.04120v1)\].

A key challenge in CDSR is the effective transfer of knowledge between domains, which is often hindered by domain shift and the lack of overlapping users \[[Yingyi Chen' 2023-05-31](http://arxiv.org/abs/2305.19798v2)\]. To address this, \[[Wangyu Wu' 2024-12-31](http://arxiv.org/abs/2502.15694v2)\] proposes a mixed information flow network that considers both behavioral and knowledge transfer between domains, while \[[Muyang Ma' 2020-12-01](http://arxiv.org/abs/2012.00485v3)\] introduces a privacy-preserving framework that ensures user data is anonymized during cross-domain learning. Additionally, \[[Hansol Jung' 2024-02-15](http://arxiv.org/abs/2402.09784v2)\] explores the use of external attention mechanisms to enhance graph-based models for cross-domain sequential recommendation, demonstrating improved performance over traditional self-attention approaches.

Another line of research focuses on improving the efficiency and scalability of CDSR models. For example, \[[Guanyu Lin' 2023-11-14](http://arxiv.org/abs/2311.08272v1)\] introduces a lightweight self-attentive network (LSAN) that reduces memory usage by leveraging compositional embeddings, while \[[Xinhua Wang' 2023-10-07](http://arxiv.org/abs/2310.04633v3)\] proposes a dual-attention network to mitigate the redundancy of multi-head self-attention. These approaches aim to balance model performance with computational efficiency, making them suitable for deployment in resource-constrained environments.

Recent work has also explored the integration of reinforcement learning and graph neural networks to better model cross-domain interactions. \[[Xiaolin Zheng' 2022-09-21](http://arxiv.org/abs/2209.10163v1)\] introduces a reinforcement learning-enhanced framework for shared-account cross-domain recommendation, while \[[Yuli Liu' 2024-10-28](http://arxiv.org/abs/2410.21048v1)\] proposes a mixed attention network that captures both local and global item relationships. These models demonstrate the potential of combining different learning paradigms to improve cross-domain recommendation accuracy.

In addition to these methods, \[[Yehjin Shin' 2023-12-16](http://arxiv.org/abs/2312.10325v2)\] investigates the use of self-attention mechanisms in music recommendation, highlighting the importance of incorporating negative feedback to refine item embeddings. \[[Wujiang Xu' 2023-11-08](http://arxiv.org/abs/2311.04590v6)\] further extends this idea by proposing a contrastive learning task that structures item embeddings based on user feedback, leading to improved recommendation accuracy. These approaches underscore the value of incorporating user feedback and contextual information in cross-domain settings.

The proposed AutoCDSR framework builds upon these prior works by focusing on enhancing the core self-attention mechanism within the transformer, rather than adding domain-specific components. This approach aligns with the idea that the self-attention module is a powerful tool for learning correlations among behaviors, as noted in \[[Qidong Liu' 2025-04-25](http://arxiv.org/abs/2504.18383v1)\]. By formulating cross-domain learning as a multi-objective optimization problem, AutoCDSR dynamically minimizes cross-domain attention scores while optimizing the recommendation task, leading to improved performance on benchmark datasets \[[Lei Guo' 2023-04-09](http://arxiv.org/abs/2304.04218v1)\].
