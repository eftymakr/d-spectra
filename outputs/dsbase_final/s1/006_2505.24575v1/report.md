## Related Works

The task of summarizing long-form narratives, such as books, movies, and TV scripts, has been extensively explored in recent years. Traditional approaches often rely on single-agent models, which struggle with capturing the intricate plotlines, character interactions, and thematic coherence inherent in such texts \[[Tanya Goyal' 2022-05-19](http://arxiv.org/abs/2205.09641v2)\]. To address these challenges, researchers have proposed various methods, including extractive summarization techniques that rely on sentence selection and abstractive models that generate concise summaries by understanding the overall narrative structure \[[Yijia Xiao' 2024-12-28](http://arxiv.org/abs/2412.20138v7)\]. However, these methods often fail to maintain the coherence and fidelity of the original content, especially when dealing with complex narratives.

Several studies have investigated the use of multi-agent frameworks for improving summarization tasks. For instance, the work by \[[Effi Levi' 2020-07-09](http://arxiv.org/abs/2007.04874v2)\] introduces a multi-agent system that leverages the strengths of different models to enhance the quality of generated summaries. Similarly, \[[Zikang Zhou' 2023-06-18](http://arxiv.org/abs/2306.10508v1)\] proposes a collaborative approach where multiple agents work together to refine and validate the summary, leading to more accurate and coherent outputs. These approaches, however, often require extensive fine-tuning and are not easily adaptable to new domains.

In the context of narrative summarization, \[[Pinelopi Papalampidi' 2022-02-03](http://arxiv.org/abs/2202.01709v1)\] explores the use of a structured pipeline that integrates preprocessing and summarization stages to improve the quality of generated summaries. This approach focuses on standardizing input data and optimizing the summarization process through hierarchical processing. The work by \[[Zehan Li' 2025-04-10](http://arxiv.org/abs/2504.07459v2)\] further extends this idea by introducing a multi-LLM framework that allows for dynamic adjustment of the summarization process based on the complexity of the input text. This framework enables the system to handle long-form narratives more effectively by breaking down the task into manageable subtasks.

Another notable contribution is the work by \[[Timothy R. Tangherlini' 2020-08-23](http://arxiv.org/abs/2008.09961v1)\], which introduces a method for generating summaries of long-form texts by leveraging the strengths of different models. This approach uses a combination of extractive and abstractive techniques to produce summaries that are both accurate and coherent. The results demonstrate that this hybrid approach outperforms single-agent models in terms of both quality and efficiency.

In addition to these approaches, \[[Jinming Zhang' 2024-12-03](http://arxiv.org/abs/2412.02897v1)\] proposes a framework for evaluating the quality of summaries generated by different models. This framework uses a combination of automated metrics and human evaluation to assess the accuracy and coherence of the generated summaries. The results show that this evaluation framework provides a more comprehensive understanding of the strengths and weaknesses of different summarization methods.

The paper also builds on recent advances in multi-agent systems for narrative understanding. For example, \[[Peipei Wei' 2025-02-11](http://arxiv.org/abs/2502.07165v1)\] presents a framework for generating causal micro-narratives from text, which could be useful for understanding the underlying structure of narratives. \[[Ying Wen' 2021-06-12](http://arxiv.org/abs/2106.06828v1)\] introduces a method for learning reward machines in cooperative multi-agent tasks, which could be applied to improve the coordination of agents in summarization tasks. \[[Jingjing Xu' 2018-08-21](http://arxiv.org/abs/1808.06945v2)\] explores the use of multi-agent collaboration in enterprise applications, demonstrating the potential of multi-agent systems for complex tasks such as summarization.

Furthermore, \[[Cinjon Resnick' 2018-09-19](http://arxiv.org/abs/1809.07124v2)\] presents a method for improving multi-agent cooperation in the card game Hanabi by introducing conventions that allow agents to communicate implicitly. This approach could be adapted to improve the coordination of agents in summarization tasks. \[[Paul Van Eecke' 2020-04-09](http://arxiv.org/abs/2004.04722v1)\] discusses the use of multi-agent systems in the context of the Multi-Agent Programming Contest, highlighting the importance of communication and coordination in complex environments. \[[Baicen Xiao' 2022-01-12](http://arxiv.org/abs/2201.04612v1)\] introduces a dataset for character attribution in narrative understanding, which could be used to evaluate the accuracy of summaries in terms of character consistency.
