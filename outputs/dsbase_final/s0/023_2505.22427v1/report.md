## Related Works

The field of radar-camera calibration has seen significant advancements, driven by the need for robust and accurate sensor fusion in autonomous driving systems. Early approaches relied on target-based calibration methods, which required complex setups and were not suitable for real-time applications \[[Yunfei Long' 2025-04-12](http://arxiv.org/abs/2504.09086v1)\]. These methods often involved placing calibration targets in the environment and using them to estimate the extrinsic parameters between the sensors. However, such approaches are not practical for dynamic environments where calibration targets may not be available \[[Ryan Porter' 2012-12-12](http://arxiv.org/abs/1301.0595v1)\]. 

Recent work has shifted towards targetless calibration methods, leveraging deep learning to extract common features from radar and camera data \[[Yunfei Long' 2025-04-12](http://arxiv.org/abs/2504.09086v1)\]. For instance, the work by \[[Yunfei Long' 2025-04-12](http://arxiv.org/abs/2504.09086v1)\] introduces a novel approach that uses deep learning to extract common features from raw radar data and camera images, enabling online targetless calibration. This method implicitly utilizes these common features to match identical objects from both data sources, improving the accuracy and robustness of the calibration process. 

Another notable contribution is the work by \[[Ryan Porter' 2012-12-12](http://arxiv.org/abs/1301.0595v1)\], which proposes a flexible and accurate method for extrinsic calibration of 3D radar and camera. This method does not require a specially designed calibration environment and instead uses a single corner reflector placed on the ground to iteratively collect radar and camera data. The method then uses these data to solve the perspective-n-point (PnP) problem and obtain the extrinsic calibration matrix. The use of RANSAC and Levenberg-Marquardt (LM) nonlinear optimization algorithms enhances the accuracy and robustness of the calibration \[[Ryan Porter' 2012-12-12](http://arxiv.org/abs/1301.0595v1)\]. 

In addition to targetless methods, some studies have focused on improving the robustness of calibration under varying environmental conditions. For example, \[[Jamie Twycross' 2010-03-08](http://arxiv.org/abs/1003.1598v1)\] presents a method for 3D radar and camera co-calibration that is flexible and accurate, suitable for real-world applications. This method leverages the ability of the radar unit to measure its own ego-velocity relative to a fixed, external reference frame, enabling spatiotemporal calibration without the need for specialized infrastructure \[[Jamie Twycross' 2010-03-08](http://arxiv.org/abs/1003.1598v1)\]. 

Furthermore, the work by \[[F. Penunuri' 2011-12-13](http://arxiv.org/abs/1112.2954v2)\] explores the use of cross-domain spatial matching for camera and radar sensor data fusion in autonomous vehicle perception systems. This approach extracts 2D features from camera images and applies a novel Cross-Domain Spatial Matching (CDSM) transformation method to convert these features into 3D space. The method then fuses them with radar data using a complementary fusion strategy to produce a final 3D object representation. This approach demonstrates superior performance over single-sensor solutions and competes with other top-level fusion methods \[[F. Penunuri' 2011-12-13](http://arxiv.org/abs/1112.2954v2)\].
