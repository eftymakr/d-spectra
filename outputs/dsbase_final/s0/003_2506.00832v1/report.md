## Related Works

Recent advancements in Text-to-Speech (TTS) have significantly improved speech naturalness, increasing the demand for precise prosody control and mispronunciation correction \[[Perry Lam' 2024-08-13](http://arxiv.org/abs/2408.06827v1)\]. Existing approaches for prosody manipulation often depend on specialized modules or additional training, limiting their capacity for post-hoc adjustments \[[Yi Ren' 2022-02-16](http://arxiv.org/abs/2202.07816v1)\]. Similarly, traditional mispronunciation correction relies on grapheme-to-phoneme dictionaries, making it less practical in low-resource settings \[[Max Morrison' 2021-02-16](http://arxiv.org/abs/2102.08328v1)\]. This section reviews related works that address these challenges, focusing on methods for prosody control, mispronunciation correction, and post-hoc TTS modifications.

Several studies have explored prosody control in TTS. For example, \[[Tuomo Raitio' 2021-10-06](http://arxiv.org/abs/2110.02952v2)\] introduces a TTS architecture that adds utterance-level prosody control to an existing neural HMM-based TTS system, enabling stable, monotonic alignments for spontaneous speech. This work demonstrates that prosody control does not degrade synthesis quality, as evaluated through perceptual tests \[[Tuomo Raitio' 2021-10-06](http://arxiv.org/abs/2110.02952v2)\]. Similarly, \[[Max Morrison' 2020-08-07](http://arxiv.org/abs/2008.03388v2)\] proposes a non-autoregressive parallel neural TTS front-end model hierarchically conditioned on acoustic speech features to learn a latent prosody space with intuitive and meaningful dimensions. This model effectively controls each prosody dimension and generates a wide variety of speaking styles while maintaining equal or better quality than the baseline model \[[Max Morrison' 2020-08-07](http://arxiv.org/abs/2008.03388v2)\]. \[[Florian Lux' 2022-06-24](http://arxiv.org/abs/2206.12229v2)\] further advances this by introducing a hierarchical prosody modeling approach using Graph Neural Networks (GNNs) to improve the expressiveness of long-form synthetic speech, enabling more natural and coherent prosody generation \[[Florian Lux' 2022-06-24](http://arxiv.org/abs/2206.12229v2)\].

Mispronunciation correction in TTS has been addressed using grapheme-to-phoneme (G2P) dictionaries, but these approaches are limited in low-resource settings \[[Max Morrison' 2021-02-16](http://arxiv.org/abs/2102.08328v1)\]. A more recent approach, \[[Younggun Lee' 2018-11-06](http://arxiv.org/abs/1811.02122v2)\], presents a speech BERT model to extract embedded prosody information in speech segments for improving the prosody of synthesized speech in neural TTS. This method allows the model to extract fine-grained, segment-level prosody, which is complementary to utterance-level prosody to improve the final prosody of the TTS speech \[[Younggun Lee' 2018-11-06](http://arxiv.org/abs/1811.02122v2)\]. Additionally, \[[Giridhar Pamisetty' 2021-10-06](http://arxiv.org/abs/2110.02854v1)\] introduces a model that uses a prosody encoder to learn emotionally-separable prosody embedding as a proxy for emotion, enabling the simulation of primary and secondary emotions, intensity levels, and emotion polarity \[[Giridhar Pamisetty' 2021-10-06](http://arxiv.org/abs/2110.02854v1)\].

Post-hoc modifications to TTS outputs have also been explored. For instance, \[[Zhen Zeng' 2020-08-13](http://arxiv.org/abs/2008.05656v1)\] proposes a TTS system that allows for the transfer of prosody from a parallel text recording of an unseen speaker to various trained TTS voices without quality degradation. This system preserves the target TTS speaker's identity and demonstrates the potential for fine-grained prosody transfer \[[Zhen Zeng' 2020-08-13](http://arxiv.org/abs/2008.05656v1)\]. \[[Marie Kunešová' 2022-09-29](http://arxiv.org/abs/2209.15032v1)\] further extends this by introducing a method that uses a lightweight aligner for extracting fine-grained prosodic features, which can be fine-tuned on individual samples within seconds \[[Marie Kunešová' 2022-09-29](http://arxiv.org/abs/2209.15032v1)\]. This enables more flexible and efficient prosody transfer across speakers and languages.

### <a id='category-neural-prosody-modeling'></a>Neural Prosody Modeling: 6 papers found.
Neural prosody modeling has emerged as a critical area in text-to-speech (TTS) research, aiming to enhance the naturalness and expressiveness of synthesized speech by accurately capturing and modeling prosodic features such as pitch, duration, and energy \[[Perry Lam' 2024-08-13](http://arxiv.org/abs/2408.06827v1)\]. Traditional approaches often rely on autoregressive methods to predict quantized prosody vectors, but these methods face challenges such as long-term dependency and slow inference \[[Max Morrison' 2020-08-07](http://arxiv.org/abs/2008.03388v2)\]. To address these limitations, recent studies have proposed alternative frameworks, including the use of diffusion probabilistic models and normalizing flows, which offer more flexible and efficient ways to model prosody and mel-spectrogram prediction \[[Max Morrison' 2021-02-16](http://arxiv.org/abs/2102.08328v1)\]. Additionally, there is growing interest in leveraging pre-trained language models to incorporate semantic features into prosody prediction, improving the alignment between linguistic content and prosodic patterns \[[Yi Ren' 2022-02-16](http://arxiv.org/abs/2202.07816v1)\]. Furthermore, ensemble learning has been explored as a means to enhance the diversity and robustness of prosody prediction, with simple ensembles of prosody predictors showing significant improvements over individual models \[[Tuomo Raitio' 2021-10-06](http://arxiv.org/abs/2110.02952v2)\]. These advancements highlight the ongoing efforts to better model the complex and variable nature of prosody in TTS systems.
