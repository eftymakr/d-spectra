## Related Works

Recent advancements in Text-to-Speech (TTS) systems have significantly enhanced the naturalness of synthesized speech, with a growing emphasis on precise prosody control and mispronunciation correction \[[Tuomo Raitio' 2021-10-06](http://arxiv.org/abs/2110.02952v2)\]. However, existing methods for prosody manipulation often rely on specialized modules or require additional training, limiting their flexibility for post-hoc adjustments \[[Liping Chen' 2021-06-08](http://arxiv.org/abs/2106.04312v3)\]. Similarly, traditional approaches to mispronunciation correction depend on grapheme-to-phoneme (G2P) dictionaries, which are less effective in low-resource settings \[[Peter Makarov' 2022-06-29](http://arxiv.org/abs/2206.14643v1)\]. This section reviews related works that address these challenges, focusing on methods that enable post-hoc control of prosody and pronunciation without retraining.

Several works have explored the use of neural networks for prosody modeling and control. For instance, \[[Giridhar Pamisetty' 2021-10-06](http://arxiv.org/abs/2110.02854v1)\] proposes a non-autoregressive parallel neural TTS system that hierarchically conditions on both coarse and fine-grained acoustic speech features to learn a latent prosody space with intuitive dimensions. This approach enables effective control of prosodic dimensions, generating a wide variety of speaking styles while maintaining high synthesis quality. Similarly, \[[Dafydd Gibbon' 2018-04-23](http://arxiv.org/abs/1804.09543v4)\] introduces a speech BERT model to extract embedded prosody information from speech segments, which is then used to improve the prosody of synthesized speech in neural TTS. This method allows for fine-grained prosody control and improves the naturalness of the output.

Another line of research focuses on end-to-end TTS systems with explicit prosody control. \[[Sri Karlapati' 2022-06-27](http://arxiv.org/abs/2206.13443v1)\] presents Prosody-TTS, an end-to-end speech synthesis model that explicitly models the duration of phonemes and the fundamental frequency (f0) to enable control during synthesis. This model is trained in an end-to-end fashion to directly generate the speech waveform from the input text, which depends on auxiliary subtasks of predicting phoneme duration, f0, and mel spectrogram. The system achieves state-of-the-art performance with a mean opinion score of 4.08 and low inference time. \[[Zixing Wang' 2024-03-11](http://arxiv.org/abs/2403.06640v2)\] further extends this idea by proposing a system that can transfer prosody from a parallel text recording of an unseen speaker to various trained TTS voices, preserving the target speaker's identity while maintaining synthesis quality.

In addition to prosody control, some works address mispronunciation correction. \[[Harm Lameris' 2022-11-24](http://arxiv.org/abs/2211.13533v2)\] introduces a method for context-aware prosody correction in text-based speech editing, which uses neural networks to generate salient prosody features dependent on the surrounding speech and applies them to standard pitch-shift and time-stretch methods. This approach reduces artifacts and improves the naturalness of edited speech. \[[Danielle C. Tarraf' 2011-05-19](http://arxiv.org/abs/1105.3788v3)\] also explores the use of a hierarchical prosody modeling approach, where a virtual global node in the graph strengthens the interconnection of word nodes, and a contextual attention mechanism broadens the prosody modeling scope from intra-sentence to inter-sentence.

These methods highlight the importance of flexible and controllable TTS systems that can adapt to different prosodic and pronunciation requirements without requiring retraining. Our proposed method, Counterfactual Activation Editing, builds upon these ideas by enabling post-hoc manipulation of internal representations in a pre-trained TTS model, achieving both prosody control and mispronunciation correction while preserving synthesis quality.
