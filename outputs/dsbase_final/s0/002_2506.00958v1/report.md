## Related Works

Nonverbal communication is a fundamental aspect of human interaction, encompassing gestures, facial expressions, and body language that convey intent and emotion \[[Uroš Petković' 2024-07-24](http://arxiv.org/abs/2407.17209v1)\]. However, existing large language models (LLMs) have limited capabilities in incorporating nonverbal elements, which restricts their ability to generate fully immersive conversational experiences \[[Shukang Yin' 2023-06-23](http://arxiv.org/abs/2306.13549v4)\]. This section reviews related works that address nonverbal communication in AI systems, focusing on multimodal models, datasets, and methods for integrating nonverbal cues into language models.

Several studies have explored the integration of nonverbal cues into AI systems. For instance, \[[Cigdem Beyan' 2022-07-20](http://arxiv.org/abs/2207.10574v2)\] discusses the use of nonverbal cues in human-robot interaction, emphasizing the importance of integrating nonverbal codes to create more natural and engaging interactions. Similarly, \[[Hengzhi Li' 2025-02-23](http://arxiv.org/abs/2502.16671v3)\] investigates the impact of nonverbal cues in negotiation, highlighting how technologies such as deepfakes can alter these cues, affecting perception and outcomes. These studies underscore the significance of nonverbal cues in shaping human-AI interactions.

In the realm of multimodal models, \[[Lea Müller' 2018-10-29](http://arxiv.org/abs/1810.12171v1)\] presents a survey on multimodal large language models (MLLMs), highlighting their emergent capabilities such as writing stories based on images and performing OCR-free math reasoning. This work sets the stage for understanding how MLLMs can be extended to incorporate nonverbal elements. Additionally, \[[Sitao Zhang' 2023-05-22](http://arxiv.org/abs/2305.13500v1)\] introduces a framework for multimodal retrieval in visual word sense disambiguation, leveraging large language models (LLMs) as knowledge bases to enhance understanding of ambiguous words. This approach demonstrates the potential of LLMs in multimodal tasks.

The development of datasets for nonverbal communication is also a critical area. \[[Dongyoung Go' 2024-11-19](http://arxiv.org/abs/2411.12287v3)\] introduces a computational model for analyzing nonverbal behavior in educational settings, using RGB classroom videos to assess nonverbal immediacy (NVI) of teachers. This work highlights the importance of annotated datasets in training models to recognize and interpret nonverbal cues. Similarly, \[[Laura Hanu' 2023-09-19](http://arxiv.org/abs/2309.10783v1)\] presents a dataset of 400 30-second video segments from German classrooms, used to train and validate models for analyzing nonverbal immediacy. These datasets are essential for developing and evaluating models that can understand and generate nonverbal cues.

Recent work on multimodal models has also focused on improving the integration of nonverbal cues. \[[Hai Pham' 2018-07-11](http://arxiv.org/abs/1807.03915v2)\] explores the use of implicit behavior cloning (IBC) to replicate nonverbal cues like gaze behaviors, demonstrating the effectiveness of IBC in social interaction tasks. This study shows that IBC can outperform traditional mean squared error (MSE) models in capturing subtle nonverbal behaviors. Additionally, \[[Xingyu Fu' 2024-04-18](http://arxiv.org/abs/2404.12390v4)\] discusses the use of multimodal reasoning to detect harmful memes, leveraging the reasoning capabilities of LLMs to analyze the interplay of text and image in memes. This work highlights the potential of LLMs in understanding complex multimodal content.

In summary, the integration of nonverbal cues into AI systems is an active area of research, with significant contributions from various studies. These works provide a foundation for developing models like MARS, which aim to bridge the gap in conversational AI by incorporating nonverbal elements.
