## Related Works

Dense retrievers have become a cornerstone in information retrieval, leveraging pre-trained language models (PLMs) such as BERT and LLaMA, which are fine-tuned using contrastive learning to encode text into dense representations for efficient comparison \[[Kaiser Sun' 2024-08-13](http://arxiv.org/abs/2408.06663v5)\]. Recent studies have questioned the role of fine-tuning versus pre-training in dense retrievers, suggesting that retrieval knowledge is primarily acquired during pre-training, and that fine-tuning does not significantly alter the underlying knowledge \[[Juhwan Lee' 2024-05-13](http://arxiv.org/abs/2405.13008v1)\]. This claim was initially tested using a BERT-based encoder with the Dense Passage Retrieval (DPR) framework as a representative example \[[Negar Arabzadeh' 2021-09-22](http://arxiv.org/abs/2109.10739v1)\]. However, the analysis was limited to a specific model architecture and dataset, raising questions about its generalizability.

To address these limitations, we extend the previous analysis by examining different representation approaches, such as the use of CLS tokens versus mean pooling, and different backbone architectures, including encoder-only models like BERT and decoder-only models like LLaMA \[[Skatje Myers' 2024-09-23](http://arxiv.org/abs/2409.15163v1)\]. Additionally, we test the findings on a broader range of datasets, including MSMARCO and Natural Questions \[[Xiao Wang' 2021-06-21](http://arxiv.org/abs/2106.11251v2)\]. Our results confirm that in DPR tuning, pre-trained knowledge underpins retrieval performance, with fine-tuning primarily adjusting neuron activation rather than reorganizing knowledge \[[Shoya Wada' 2020-05-14](http://arxiv.org/abs/2005.07202v3)\]. However, this pattern does not hold universally; for example, in mean-pooled models like Contriever and decoder-based models like LLaMA, fine-tuning plays a more significant role in shaping retrieval performance \[[Xinyu Zhang' 2021-08-19](http://arxiv.org/abs/2108.08787v2)\].

Several prior works have explored the effectiveness of different fine-tuning strategies in dense retrievers. For instance, \[[Kun Zhou' 2022-12-15](http://arxiv.org/abs/2212.07841v2)\] investigates the use of very high dropout rates during fine-tuning to obtain rich representations, showing that fine-tuning a large pre-trained model under such conditions can achieve out-of-distribution performance that exceeds both ensembles and weight averaging methods. Similarly, \[[Ori Ram' 2022-12-20](http://arxiv.org/abs/2212.10380v2)\] proposes a framework for fine-tuning with very large dropout rates, demonstrating that this approach can improve robustness to distribution shifts in downstream tasks. These findings align with our observation that fine-tuning primarily adjusts activation patterns rather than reorganizing knowledge in encoder-only models.

In contrast, \[[Heydar Soudani' 2024-03-03](http://arxiv.org/abs/2403.01432v5)\] argues that fine-tuning can significantly impact the performance of dense retrievers when combined with data augmentation techniques, particularly in scenarios where the training data is limited. This suggests that the role of fine-tuning may vary depending on the model architecture and the nature of the training data. Furthermore, \[[Shuaishuai Guo' 2023-02-12](http://arxiv.org/abs/2302.07142v2)\] highlights the importance of pre-training strategies in dense retrievers, emphasizing that the quality of pre-training directly influences the effectiveness of fine-tuning. This aligns with our findings that pre-trained knowledge underpins retrieval performance in many cases.

Additionally, \[[Lin Bo' 2021-08-12](http://arxiv.org/abs/2108.05652v1)\] explores the use of multiple representations in dense retrieval, comparing single representation methods like ANCE with multiple representation methods like ColBERT. The study finds that while ANCE is more efficient, multiple representations are statistically more effective for retrieval metrics such as MAP and MRR@10. This supports the idea that representation strategies can significantly influence retrieval performance. Similarly, \[[Zhenhao Jiang' 2025-01-24](http://arxiv.org/abs/2501.14268v1)\] introduces a method for improving dense retrievers by using a multi-task pre-trained model called MASTER, which integrates various pre-training tasks to enhance the quality of dense vectors.

Overall, our study builds on these prior works by extending the analysis to a broader set of models, representation strategies, and datasets, providing a more comprehensive understanding of the interplay between pre-training and fine-tuning in dense retrievers.
