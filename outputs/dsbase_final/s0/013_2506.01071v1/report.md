## Related Works

Long-tailed recognition has been a focal point in computer vision, particularly due to the skewed class distribution in real-world datasets. Early approaches primarily relied on data reweighting or resampling techniques to mitigate the bias \[[Jianggang Zhu' 2022-07-19](http://arxiv.org/abs/2207.09052v3)\]. However, these methods often fail to address the underlying representation issues that arise from class imbalance \[[Tianhong Li' 2021-11-27](http://arxiv.org/abs/2111.13998v2)\]. More recently, supervised contrastive learning (SCL) has emerged as a promising approach to improve representation learning for long-tailed recognition \[[Chongsheng Zhang' 2024-08-01](http://arxiv.org/abs/2408.00483v1)\]. SCL leverages positive and negative samples to pull similar instances together and push dissimilar ones apart, thereby learning discriminative features \[[Shiyu Xuan' 2024-03-10](http://arxiv.org/abs/2403.06151v1)\]. 

Despite its success, SCL faces challenges in long-tailed scenarios. For instance, the method may struggle with gradient conflicts, where the attraction and repulsion gradients between positive and negative pairs become imbalanced \[[Sanglee Park' 2024-06-04](http://arxiv.org/abs/2406.02223v1)\]. This issue has been observed in various studies, where the performance of SCL degrades as the number of views increases \[[Julien Denize' 2022-12-21](http://arxiv.org/abs/2212.11187v1)\]. To address this, several works have proposed modifications to SCL, such as class-balanced contrastive learning (BCL) and targeted supervised contrastive learning (TSC), which aim to improve the uniformity of feature distributions \[[Minki Jeong' 2022-11-22](http://arxiv.org/abs/2211.11938v1)\]. 

In addition to contrastive learning, other methods have explored data augmentation and re-balancing strategies to enhance long-tailed recognition. For example, the work by \[[Qiong Chen' 2023-09-28](http://arxiv.org/abs/2309.16135v1)\] introduces a novel loss function that balances the gradient contributions of negative classes, while \[[Ravid Shwartz-Ziv' 2023-12-05](http://arxiv.org/abs/2312.02517v1)\] proposes a method that ensures all classes appear in every mini-batch. These approaches aim to create a more equitable learning environment for tail classes. 

Furthermore, the integration of contrastive learning with other techniques has shown promise. For instance, \[[Byoungjip Kim' 2021-01-16](http://arxiv.org/abs/2101.06480v1)\] combines contrastive learning with self-distillation to transfer knowledge from head to tail classes, thereby improving the under-representation of tail classes. Similarly, \[[Weide Liu' 2023-09-13](http://arxiv.org/abs/2309.07186v1)\] proposes a method that uses saliency masking to mitigate the bias introduced by background features common to all classes. 

Theoretical analysis of SCL has also contributed to understanding the challenges in long-tailed recognition. For example, \[[Chaoqun Du' 2024-03-11](http://arxiv.org/abs/2403.06726v2)\] provides a theoretical framework that identifies the harmful causal effect of SGD momentum in long-tailed classification, which can mislead the model towards the head classes. This insight has led to the development of new methods that aim to disentangle these effects \[[Kaihua Tang' 2020-09-28](http://arxiv.org/abs/2009.12991v5)\]. 

In summary, while SCL has shown significant potential for long-tailed recognition, it is not without its limitations. The recent works discussed here highlight the ongoing efforts to address these challenges through various modifications and extensions of SCL. These contributions form the foundation for the proposed Aligned Contrastive Learning (ACL) algorithm, which aims to eliminate the identified issues and achieve superior performance across multiple benchmarks.
