## Related Works

Recent advancements in sequential recommendation have focused on modeling user behavior through attention mechanisms and deep learning architectures. However, existing models often struggle with capturing diverse interaction patterns and efficiently handling variable-length sequences \[[Jibang Wu' 2020-01-29](http://arxiv.org/abs/2002.00741v1)\]. For instance, \[[Xiaofei Zhu' 2024-04-22](http://arxiv.org/abs/2404.13878v2)\] highlights that traditional attention-based models may not adequately represent the evolving and mixed preferences of users, leading to suboptimal recommendations. To address these limitations, several studies have introduced novel frameworks that combine attention with other modeling techniques. 

One such approach is the use of reinforcement learning to simulate the evolution of user preferences, as proposed in \[[Jibang Wu' 2020-01-29](http://arxiv.org/abs/2002.00741v1)\]. This method introduces a reward mechanism to generate sub-sequences that better fit the training data, which is crucial for capturing the dynamic nature of user behavior. Similarly, \[[Huy Nguyen' 2025-03-05](http://arxiv.org/abs/2503.03213v1)\] proposes a Contextualized Temporal Attention Mechanism that dynamically weighs the influence of historical actions based on both time and context, offering a more nuanced understanding of user preferences over time. This aligns with the idea of preference-aware attention in STAR-Rec, which captures both similar and diverse item relationships \[[Zhenrui Yue' 2023-10-03](http://arxiv.org/abs/2310.02367v2)\].

In addition to attention mechanisms, state-space models have been explored for their ability to handle variable-length sequences efficiently. \[[Jianling Wang' 2021-09-22](http://arxiv.org/abs/2109.11059v2)\] introduces a state-space model that compresses temporal dynamics, enabling efficient processing of long sequences. This is particularly relevant to STAR-Rec, which utilizes state-space modeling to process variable-length sequences with linear complexity \[[Zhenrui Yue' 2023-10-03](http://arxiv.org/abs/2310.02367v2)\]. Moreover, \[[Xin He' 2024-10-23](http://arxiv.org/abs/2410.17954v2)\] proposes a framework that integrates temporal information with item embeddings to capture dynamic user interests, further supporting the use of state-space models in recommendation systems.

To address the challenge of modeling diverse user behaviors, several works have introduced mixture-of-experts (MoE) architectures. \[[Isobel Claire Gormley' 2018-06-21](http://arxiv.org/abs/1806.08200v1)\] presents a MoE model that adapts to different behavioral patterns by routing requests to specialized experts, a concept that is central to STAR-Rec. This approach enables the system to handle both focused browsing and diverse exploration scenarios effectively \[[Zhenrui Yue' 2023-10-03](http://arxiv.org/abs/2310.02367v2)\]. Additionally, \[[Mehrnaz Amjadi' 2020-12-06](http://arxiv.org/abs/2012.03323v3)\] introduces a hierarchical MoE framework for multi-modal sequential recommendation, demonstrating the potential of MoE in capturing complex user preferences across different modalities. These studies underscore the importance of adaptive routing in recommendation systems, a key component of STAR-Rec.

Furthermore, \[[Francesca Mangili' 2020-02-12](http://arxiv.org/abs/2002.05063v1)\] explores the use of MoE in the context of multi-domain and multi-task recommendation, emphasizing the need for adaptive and efficient expert routing. This aligns with the design of STAR-Rec, which incorporates a sequence-level MoE component to adaptively route different behavioral patterns to specialized experts. \[[Hal Ashton' 2022-09-14](http://arxiv.org/abs/2209.11801v1)\] also discusses the challenges of handling long-tailed distributions in recommendation systems, highlighting the importance of modeling diverse user preferences, which is a core aspect of STAR-Rec.

In summary, recent works have made significant strides in sequential recommendation by integrating attention mechanisms, state-space modeling, and MoE architectures. These approaches address critical challenges such as handling sequence length variations, capturing diverse interaction patterns, and adapting to user preferences. STAR-Rec builds upon these advancements by combining preference-aware attention with state-space modeling within a sequence-level MoE framework, offering a novel and effective solution to the challenges in sequential recommendation.

Preference-aware modeling in recommendations has gained significant attention, particularly in addressing how user preferences can be dynamically captured and respected within recommender systems. Researchers have emphasized the importance of incorporating meta-preferences to ensure that systems do not manipulate user behavior in a manipulative manner \[[Jibang Wu' 2020-01-29](http://arxiv.org/abs/2002.00741v1)\]. Additionally, there is growing interest in leveraging textual data, such as item descriptions and user reviews, to enhance recommendation accuracy, especially for top-N recommendations. While review-based models have shown promise in rating prediction, they often struggle with top-N tasks, prompting the development of more sophisticated models that integrate both item textual content and high-order user-item interactions \[[Xiaofei Zhu' 2024-04-22](http://arxiv.org/abs/2404.13878v2)\]. Furthermore, the industry's focus on business metrics like profit and revenue has led to explorations of profit-aware recommendation systems, where models are adjusted to align with both customer preferences and business goals \[[Huy Nguyen' 2025-03-05](http://arxiv.org/abs/2503.03213v1)\]. These efforts highlight the evolving landscape of preference-aware modeling, aiming to balance user autonomy, recommendation quality, and business objectives.
