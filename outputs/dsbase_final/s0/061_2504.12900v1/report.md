## Related Works

Personalized outfit generation has become a focal point in fashion technology, aiming to construct sets of compatible and personalized fashion items tailored to individual user preferences. Recent advancements in generative AI models have enabled the creation of fashion items to complete incomplete outfits or generate full outfits, yet these models often face limitations in diversity and reliance on supervised learning paradigms \[[Kedan Li' 2019-06-17](http://arxiv.org/abs/1906.07273v2)\]. This has spurred the development of alternative approaches that leverage direct preference optimization to enhance personalization without the need for task-specific reward functions \[[Yiyan Xu' 2024-02-27](http://arxiv.org/abs/2402.17279v3)\].

Direct preference optimization (DPO) has been explored in the context of language models, where it aligns model outputs with human preferences through pairwise comparisons \[[Dongliang Zhou' 2025-02-03](http://arxiv.org/abs/2502.00992v1)\]. This approach has been extended to fashion generation, where the goal is to refine pre-trained models using automatically generated feedback, ensuring alignment with user preferences while adhering to fashion compatibility principles \[[Debopriyo Banerjee' 2022-03-30](http://arxiv.org/abs/2203.16161v1)\]. The use of DPO in fashion generation allows for a more flexible and efficient fine-tuning process compared to traditional supervised methods \[[Dongliang Zhou' 2025-02-12](http://arxiv.org/abs/2502.08674v1)\].

To ensure comprehensive and objective feedback, several studies have proposed multi-expert feedback generation modules that evaluate generated outfits from multiple perspectives, such as quality, compatibility, and personalization \[[Marjan Celikik' 2022-11-29](http://arxiv.org/abs/2211.16353v1)\]. These modules help in providing diverse and accurate feedback, which is essential for improving the model's ability to align with user preferences \[[Dongliang Zhou' 2025-02-05](http://arxiv.org/abs/2502.06827v1)\]. For instance, the work by \[[Takuma Nakamura' 2018-06-29](http://arxiv.org/abs/1807.03133v3)\] introduces a framework that incorporates multiple evaluation criteria to enhance the quality of generated outfits.

In addition to DPO, other approaches have focused on improving the compatibility and diversity of generated outfits. For example, \[[Jui-Chieh Wu' 2019-08-22](http://arxiv.org/abs/1908.08327v1)\] proposes a framework that uses a combination of image-to-image translation and compatibility modeling to generate diverse and compatible outfits. This approach leverages semantic alignment and collocation classification to ensure that the generated outfits are both visually appealing and functionally compatible \[[Yuncheng Li' 2016-08-10](http://arxiv.org/abs/1608.03016v2)\].

The integration of multi-modal data, such as text and images, has also been explored to enhance the personalization of outfit generation. \[[Myong Chol Jung' 2024-06-29](http://arxiv.org/abs/2407.00289v2)\] presents a framework that combines visual and textual information to generate personalized outfit recommendations, demonstrating the importance of multimodal data in improving the accuracy and relevance of generated outfits \[[Haoxiang Wang' 2024-02-28](http://arxiv.org/abs/2402.18571v3)\].

Furthermore, the use of reinforcement learning from human feedback (RLHF) has been investigated to improve the alignment of generated outfits with user preferences. \[[Takuma Nakamura' 2023-11-28](http://arxiv.org/abs/2311.16630v1)\] introduces a method that uses RLHF to train models to generate outfits that are both compatible and personalized, leveraging human feedback to refine the model's output \[[Debopriyo Banerjee' 2024-02-26](http://arxiv.org/abs/2402.16660v1)\].

In summary, the field of personalized outfit generation has seen significant advancements through the integration of direct preference optimization, multi-expert feedback generation, and multimodal data processing. These approaches aim to overcome the limitations of traditional supervised learning paradigms and provide more diverse and personalized outfit recommendations \[[Xingchen Li' 2020-05-26](http://arxiv.org/abs/2005.12566v1)\].

### <a id='category-generative-ai-in-fashion-generation'></a>Generative AI in Fashion Generation: 6 papers found.

Recently, generative AI models have received widespread attention, as they can generate fashion items for users to complete an incomplete outfit or create a complete outfit. However, they have limitations in terms of lacking diversity and relying on the supervised learning paradigm. Recognizing this gap, we propose a novel framework FashionDPO, which fine-tunes the fashion outfit generation model using direct preference optimization. This framework aims to provide a general fine-tuning approach to fashion generative models, refining a pre-trained fashion outfit generation model using automatically generated feedback, without the need to design a task-specific reward function.
