## Related Works

Federated Learning (FL) has emerged as a promising paradigm for training machine learning models while preserving user privacy by keeping data decentralized \[[Youming Tao' 2024-01-19](http://arxiv.org/abs/2401.11018v1)\]. Recent advancements have focused on extending FL to address the "right to be forgotten," a legal requirement that mandates the ability to remove user data from training sets \[[Ruinan Jin' 2023-06-03](http://arxiv.org/abs/2306.02216v3)\]. This has led to the development of Federated Unlearning (FU), a subfield of FL that enables the removal of specific data points or clients from the global model without retraining the entire system \[[Hyejun Jeong' 2024-03-04](http://arxiv.org/abs/2403.02437v3)\].

Several approaches have been proposed to implement FU. For instance, \[[Nicolò Romandini' 2024-01-10](http://arxiv.org/abs/2401.05146v3)\] introduces a method for label unlearning in Vertical Federated Learning (VFL), where synthetic embeddings are generated to facilitate gradient-based forgetting. Similarly, \[[Hanlin Gu' 2024-05-24](http://arxiv.org/abs/2405.15474v2)\] presents a framework for federated feature unlearning that minimizes feature sensitivity using Lipschitz continuity, enabling the removal of sensitive or biased features from the model.

### <a id='category-privacy-preserving-techniques-in-fl'></a>Privacy-Preserving Techniques in FL: 2 papers found.
The category "Privacy-Preserving Techniques in FL" focuses on addressing privacy and security challenges in federated learning (FL), particularly training data privacy intrusion and poisoning attacks. These threats hinder the widespread adoption of FL in real-world applications \[[Wenqi Wei' 2024-09-19](http://arxiv.org/abs/2409.13004v1)\]. To mitigate data leakage, researchers suggest adding controlled randomized noise to gradient updates before sharing them during federated training, emphasizing the importance of determining the appropriate noise level and placement \[[Wenqi Wei' 2024-09-19](http://arxiv.org/abs/2409.13004v1)\]. Training data poisoning, on the other hand, involves malicious users altering the global model to introduce backdoor attacks, which can lead to significant performance degradation \[[Wenqi Wei' 2024-09-19](http://arxiv.org/abs/2409.13004v1)\]. For instance, backdoor attacks in federated meta-learning can persist even after additional training, making them particularly dangerous \[[Chien-Lun Chen' 2020-06-12](http://arxiv.org/abs/2006.07026v2)\]. To counter these threats, defense strategies such as dynamic model perturbation have been proposed, which aim to protect privacy while maintaining model performance and resilience against poisoning \[[Wenqi Wei' 2024-09-19](http://arxiv.org/abs/2409.13004v1)\]. Additionally, the negative impact of data skewness, algorithmic biases, and misinformation in training data further complicates the security landscape of FL \[[Wenqi Wei' 2024-09-19](http://arxiv.org/abs/2409.13004v1)\]. Researchers have also explored methods like matching networks to reduce the success of backdoor attacks by removing decision logic from shared models \[[Chien-Lun Chen' 2020-06-12](http://arxiv.org/abs/2006.07026v2)\]. These efforts highlight the ongoing need for robust privacy-preserving techniques that balance security, performance, and usability in FL systems.
