## Related Works

Multi-token prediction (MTP) has emerged as a promising pre-training objective for language models, offering potential improvements in downstream performance, inference speed, and training efficiency \[[Nicholas Walker' 2024-10-23](http://arxiv.org/abs/2410.18160v1)\]. Unlike traditional next-token prediction (NTP), which predicts only the immediate next token, MTP extends the prediction horizon by generating the next $k$ tokens at each step using multiple prediction heads \[[Nicholas Walker' 2024-10-23](http://arxiv.org/abs/2410.18160v1)\]. This approach has been shown to enhance the model's ability to capture long-range dependencies and improve generation quality, especially for large models \[[Nicholas Walker' 2024-10-23](http://arxiv.org/abs/2410.18160v1)\]. However, smaller language models (SLMs) face challenges in effectively leveraging the MTP objective due to limitations in computational resources and training dynamics \[[Nicholas Walker' 2024-10-23](http://arxiv.org/abs/2410.18160v1)\].

Prior research has explored various strategies to address these challenges. For instance, curriculum learning has been applied to MTP training, aiming to gradually introduce complexity to the model \[[Nicholas Walker' 2024-10-23](http://arxiv.org/abs/2410.18160v1)\]. A forward curriculum gradually increases the difficulty of the pre-training objective from NTP to MTP, allowing SLMs to better adapt to the MTP objective during pre-training \[[Nicholas Walker' 2024-10-23](http://arxiv.org/abs/2410.18160v1)\]. This approach has been shown to improve downstream NTP performance and generative output quality while retaining the benefits of self-speculative decoding \[[Nicholas Walker' 2024-10-23](http://arxiv.org/abs/2410.18160v1)\]. In contrast, a reverse curriculum starts with MTP and gradually reduces the complexity to NTP, which can lead to stronger NTP performance and output quality but fails to provide the same self-speculative decoding benefits \[[Nicholas Walker' 2024-10-23](http://arxiv.org/abs/2410.18160v1)\].

Curriculum learning has also been extensively studied in other domains, such as reinforcement learning and computer vision, where it has been used to shape exploration by presenting increasingly complex tasks . In the context of language models, curriculum learning has been applied to improve training efficiency and model performance \[[Rasheed el-Bouri' 2020-07-01](http://arxiv.org/abs/2007.01135v1)\]. However, some studies have found that curriculum learning does not necessarily improve language model training, highlighting the need for further investigation \[[Rasheed el-Bouri' 2020-07-01](http://arxiv.org/abs/2007.01135v1)\].

Another related approach is the use of multi-token prediction in the context of large language models, where it has been shown to improve inference speed and generate more accurate outputs \[[Mingyuan Meng' 2022-11-10](http://arxiv.org/abs/2211.05409v1)\]. The method involves training the model to predict multiple future tokens at once, which can lead to higher sample efficiency and better downstream performance \[[Mingyuan Meng' 2022-11-10](http://arxiv.org/abs/2211.05409v1)\]. This approach has been particularly beneficial for generative tasks, where the model consistently outperforms strong baselines \[[Mingyuan Meng' 2022-11-10](http://arxiv.org/abs/2211.05409v1)\].

Additionally, research has explored the use of multi-token prediction in non-autoregressive generation, where models dynamically predict multiple tokens based on their confidence in the predicted joint probability distribution \[[Shikhar Tuli' 2024-05-01](http://arxiv.org/abs/2405.00888v1)\]. This approach has led to significant improvements in inference speed and text generation quality \[[Shikhar Tuli' 2024-05-01](http://arxiv.org/abs/2405.00888v1)\]. Furthermore, multi-token prediction has been shown to be beneficial for small models, as it allows them to better leverage the prediction horizon and improve their performance on downstream tasks \[[Fabian Gloeckle' 2024-04-30](http://arxiv.org/abs/2404.19737v1)\].

In the context of speech and vision models, multi-token prediction has also been explored, with promising results in tasks such as speech classification and image recognition \[[Kai-Wei Chang' 2024-08-23](http://arxiv.org/abs/2408.13040v1)\]. These studies demonstrate the versatility of multi-token prediction across different modalities and tasks.
