## Related Works

Diffusion models have emerged as a powerful paradigm for generating high-quality, coherent video content, with diffusion transformers (DiTs) leading the way in performance \[[Tianchen Zhao' 2024-06-04](http://arxiv.org/abs/2406.02540v3)\]. However, the computational and memory demands of DiTs pose significant challenges for deployment on edge devices, necessitating efficient quantization techniques \[[Zhiteng Li' 2025-05-24](http://arxiv.org/abs/2505.18663v4)\]. Prior work has explored quantization for image generation, but extending these methods to video generation introduces new challenges, such as maintaining temporal consistency and preserving spatiotemporal correlations \[[Sanghyun Yi' 2025-02-20](http://arxiv.org/abs/2502.15077v3)\]. This section reviews related works that address these challenges, focusing on quantization techniques and their application to video generation.

Quantization is a widely studied technique for model compression, particularly in image generation \[[Xiuyu Li' 2023-02-08](http://arxiv.org/abs/2302.04304v3)\]. However, extending these methods to video generation introduces new challenges, such as maintaining temporal consistency and preserving spatiotemporal correlations \[[Xinyan Liu' 2024-11-21](http://arxiv.org/abs/2411.14172v2)\]. Existing quantization methods for image generation typically focus on reducing the bit-width of weights and activations without considering the unique requirements of video generation \[[Debidatta Dwibedi' 2019-04-16](http://arxiv.org/abs/1904.07846v1)\]. For instance, ViDiT-Q addresses the challenges of quantizing diffusion transformers for text-to-image and video tasks by designing an improved quantization scheme \[[Siwei Ma' 2019-04-07](http://arxiv.org/abs/1904.03567v2)\]. This work highlights the importance of addressing quantization errors in both token and feature dimensions, which is a key aspect of our Token-aware Quantization Estimator (TQE) \[[Dengyang Jiang' 2025-05-05](http://arxiv.org/abs/2505.02831v5)\].

The temporal consistency of video generation is another critical challenge. Traditional diffusion models often struggle with maintaining coherence across frames, especially when generating long sequences \[[Farrukh Rahman' 2022-09-15](http://arxiv.org/abs/2209.07474v3)\]. To address this, the Lumiere model introduces a space-time U-Net architecture that generates the entire temporal duration of the video in a single pass \[[Xinyu Zhang' 2025-01-13](http://arxiv.org/abs/2501.07563v1)\]. This approach ensures temporal consistency by leveraging pre-trained text-to-image diffusion models and processing the video in multiple space-time scales \[[Aniruddha Mahapatra' 2025-01-09](http://arxiv.org/abs/2501.05442v2)\]. Similarly, the W.A.L.T model uses a window attention architecture tailored for joint spatial and spatiotemporal generative modeling, enabling efficient video generation \[[Shangchen Zhou' 2023-12-11](http://arxiv.org/abs/2312.06640v1)\].

Recent advancements in quantization for video diffusion transformers have also focused on improving the efficiency and quality of quantized models. The Q-DiT framework introduces a novel approach that integrates automatic quantization granularity allocation and sample-wise dynamic activation quantization to handle the significant variance of weights and activations across input channels \[[Tao Huang' 2023-12-21](http://arxiv.org/abs/2312.14134v3)\]. This method achieves a notable reduction in FID when quantizing DiT-XL/2 to W6A8 on ImageNet \[[Andrew Melnik' 2024-05-06](http://arxiv.org/abs/2405.03150v2)\]. Additionally, the HQ-DiT model proposes a hybrid floating-point quantization method that utilizes 4-bit floating-point precision on both weights and activations, resulting in minimal quantization error \[[Lichuan Xiang' 2021-08-18](http://arxiv.org/abs/2108.08305v1)\].

In summary, prior work has laid the groundwork for quantization techniques in video generation, highlighting the need for methods that preserve temporal consistency and address the unique challenges of video-specific tasks. Our Q-VDiT framework builds on these insights by introducing a novel quantization estimator and optimization strategy tailored for video DiT models \[[Yiran Xu' 2022-06-21](http://arxiv.org/abs/2206.10590v1)\].
