## Related Works

Vision Transformers (ViTs) have emerged as a powerful alternative to convolutional neural networks (CNNs) in computer vision tasks, primarily due to their ability to capture global context through self-attention mechanisms \[[Paul M. Riechers' 2024-05-07](http://arxiv.org/abs/2405.04134v1)\]. However, the deployment of ViTs on hardware platforms, especially FPGAs, has been hindered by the complexity of arithmetic operations such as Softmax and LayerNorm, which are sensitive to precision and difficult to map onto efficient hardware \[[Jyotikrishna Dass' 2022-11-09](http://arxiv.org/abs/2211.05109v1)\]. Prior work has focused on optimizing matrix multiplication operations on hardware while keeping these complex operations on the CPU, resulting in suboptimal performance due to communication overhead between the CPU and accelerator \[[Mariam Rakka' 2024-11-26](http://arxiv.org/abs/2411.17847v1)\]. This section reviews relevant works that address these challenges.

Several studies have explored hardware acceleration for vision transformers, focusing on reducing computational costs and improving efficiency. For instance, \[[Yehui Tang' 2024-02-05](http://arxiv.org/abs/2402.05964v2)\] presents a hardware-efficient image-adaptive token pruning framework called HeatViT, which dynamically identifies and consolidates non-informative tokens from input images. This method improves both model accuracy and inference latency on embedded FPGAs. Additionally, \[[Siyuan Lu' 2020-09-18](http://arxiv.org/abs/2009.08605v1)\] introduces a configurable hardware accelerator for inference of vision transformer models, targeting resource-constrained edge computing devices. This accelerator avoids repeated off-chip memory accesses and employs a head-level pipeline and inter-layer MLP optimizations, supporting multiple vision transformer models with minimal changes to control logic.

The challenge of mapping complex arithmetic operations such as Softmax and LayerNorm onto efficient hardware has also been explored. For example, \[[Kosmas Alexandridis' 2025-05-20](http://arxiv.org/abs/2505.14201v1)\] proposes a hardware-friendly alternative to Softmax, called ConSmax, which utilizes differentiable normalization parameters to eliminate the need for maximum searching and denominator summation. This approach enables extensive parallelization while maintaining the essential functions of Softmax. Additionally, \[[Jacob R. Stevens' 2021-03-16](http://arxiv.org/abs/2103.09301v1)\] introduces Softermax, a hardware-friendly softmax design that consists of base replacement, low-precision softmax computations, and an online normalization calculation, achieving significant energy efficiency improvements.

Another relevant work is \[[Dayou Du' 2024-05-01](http://arxiv.org/abs/2405.00314v1)\], which presents a novel training paradigm for ViTs that trains only one model at a time, enabling improved performance with various computational costs. This approach, known as SuperViT, reduces computational costs while maintaining or improving accuracy. However, it does not directly address the hardware acceleration challenges of ViTs.

Hardware acceleration of ViTs has also been explored in the context of FPGAs. For example, \[[Qihang Yu' 2021-06-04](http://arxiv.org/abs/2106.02277v1)\] proposes a new arithmetic format named Microscaling Integer (MXInt) for datapath designs and evaluates how different design choices can be made to trade off accuracy, hardware performance, and hardware utilization. Our work builds upon this by quantizing ViTs using the MXInt format, achieving both high area efficiency and accuracy. Furthermore, we propose MXInt-specific hardware optimizations that map these complex arithmetic operations into custom hardware, achieving at least 93× speedup compared to Float16 and at least 1.9× speedup compared to related work \[[Shashank Nag' 2023-02-17](http://arxiv.org/abs/2302.09108v1)\].
