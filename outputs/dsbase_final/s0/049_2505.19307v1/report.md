## Related Works

Neural retrieval models have become a cornerstone in modern information retrieval systems, particularly for Web search tasks, due to their ability to learn complex patterns from large-scale data \[[Aman Sinha' 2024-04-15](http://arxiv.org/abs/2404.09473v1)\]. However, these models often require extensive labeled query-document pairs for training, which are expensive and time-consuming to obtain \[[Aman Sinha' 2024-04-15](http://arxiv.org/abs/2404.09473v1)\]. Recent advances have explored the use of synthetic queries generated by large language models (LLMs) as a scalable alternative to labeled data \[[Aman Sinha' 2024-04-15](http://arxiv.org/abs/2404.09473v1)\]. These synthetic queries, while useful, often exhibit variability in quality, leading to suboptimal performance in downstream retrieval tasks \[[Aman Sinha' 2024-04-15](http://arxiv.org/abs/2404.09473v1)\].

To address this, existing approaches typically filter out noisy query-document pairs using signals from an external re-ranker \[[Aman Sinha' 2024-04-15](http://arxiv.org/abs/2404.09473v1)\]. This method, while effective in some cases, can be computationally intensive and may not fully capture the nuances of query relevance \[[Aman Sinha' 2024-04-15](http://arxiv.org/abs/2404.09473v1)\]. In contrast, our framework leverages Direct Preference Optimization (DPO) to integrate ranking signals directly into the query generation process \[[Aman Sinha' 2024-04-15](http://arxiv.org/abs/2404.09473v1)\]. This approach aims to optimize the model to generate high-quality queries that maximize downstream retrieval effectiveness by aligning with human preferences \[[Aman Sinha' 2024-04-15](http://arxiv.org/abs/2404.09473v1)\].

DPO has been previously explored for aligning LLMs with human preferences, particularly in tasks such as counter-speech generation \[[Ziting Wang' 2024-11-01](http://arxiv.org/abs/2411.00744v2)\]. This method uses direct optimization via pairwise ranking to align token-level docid generation with document-level relevance estimation, eliminating the need for explicit reward modeling and reinforcement learning \[[Ziting Wang' 2024-11-01](http://arxiv.org/abs/2411.00744v2)\]. The success of DPO in preference alignment suggests its potential for improving query generation in retrieval tasks by directly optimizing for relevance \[[Ziting Wang' 2024-11-01](http://arxiv.org/abs/2411.00744v2)\].

Furthermore, the use of synthetic data for training retrieval models has been studied in the context of improving robustness against typos . This work proposed a multi-positive contrastive learning approach that leverages multiple positive samples (typoed queries) during training, leading to improved robustness compared to methods that use a single positive sample . Similarly, the work on improving the robustness of dense retrievers against typos via multi-positive contrastive learning highlights the importance of incorporating diverse and high-quality training data .

In addition, the integration of synthetic data with privacy-preserving techniques has been explored in the context of deep retrieval systems . This work proposed using differentially private language models to generate private synthetic queries, which can be used in downstream retrieval system training without compromising privacy . This approach demonstrates the potential of using synthetic data to enhance retrieval performance while maintaining privacy guarantees .

The use of synthetic queries for improving retrieval performance has also been studied in the context of query expansion and reranking \[[Son Pham Tien' 2024-12-01](http://arxiv.org/abs/2412.00657v1)\]. This work proposed a semantic approach to concept-based query expansion, using PMING distance to measure the distance between candidate concepts \[[Son Pham Tien' 2024-12-01](http://arxiv.org/abs/2412.00657v1)\]. The results showed that this approach can provide users with more satisfying expansion results and improve the quality of web document retrieval \[[Son Pham Tien' 2024-12-01](http://arxiv.org/abs/2412.00657v1)\].

In summary, while existing methods rely on external re-rankers to filter noisy query-document pairs, our approach integrates ranking signals directly into the query generation process using DPO, aiming to improve the quality of synthetic queries and downstream retrieval performance \[[Aman Sinha' 2024-04-15](http://arxiv.org/abs/2404.09473v1)\].

For instance, Retrieval-Augmented Generation (RAG) has been widely adopted to integrate private knowledge bases with large language models (LLMs), but its accuracy is heavily dependent on the retrievers' ability to select the most relevant documents \[[Aman Sinha' 2024-04-15](http://arxiv.org/abs/2404.09473v1)\]. To address this, the "Blended RAG" method was introduced, which combines dense vector indexes with sparse encoder indexes and employs hybrid query strategies to achieve better retrieval results \[[Aman Sinha' 2024-04-15](http://arxiv.org/abs/2404.09473v1)\]. Additionally, the theoretical frameworks such as the Probability Ranking Principle and its Interactive Information Retrieval variant have been extended to dynamic information retrieval scenarios, where user signals and multi-stage retrieval processes are critical \[[Mingming Li' 2024-07-29](http://arxiv.org/abs/2407.19829v2)\]. These frameworks derive dynamic utility functions that optimize retrieval tasks by considering the probability of observing user feedback and the utility of each retrieval stage. Furthermore, the integration of bibliometric techniques into information retrieval has been explored to enhance retrieval services for specific communities and large cross-domain collections, highlighting the potential of statistical modeling of scholarship to improve retrieval effectiveness \[[Gustavo Penha' 2021-11-25](http://arxiv.org/abs/2111.13057v3)\]. In the medical domain, a novel framework combining Terminology-Enhanced Information Retrieval (TEIR) and Emotional In-Context Learning (EICL) has been proposed to address the limitations of existing models by leveraging inductive knowledge and emotional context \[[Son Pham Tien' 2024-12-01](http://arxiv.org/abs/2412.00657v1)\]. Additionally, recent advancements in cost-constrained retrieval optimization for RAG have been introduced, utilizing Monte Carlo Tree Search (MCTS) to find optimal chunk combinations and addressing non-monotonic chunk utility through utility computation strategies \[[Mohamed Trabelsi' 2021-02-23](http://arxiv.org/abs/2102.11903v2)\]. These contributions collectively underscore the ongoing efforts to refine semantic query expansion and reranking methods to enhance the performance of information retrieval systems.

### <a id='category-dpo-applications-in-preference-alignment-and-llm-training'></a>DPO Applications in Preference Alignment and LLM Training: 2 papers found.
The category "DPO Applications in Preference Alignment and LLM Training" explores how Direct Preference Optimization (DPO) can be used to align large language models (LLMs) with human preferences, enhancing the quality and effectiveness of generated outputs. This is particularly relevant in tasks such as counter-speech generation, where models must produce contextually appropriate and linguistically adaptable responses \[[Aman Sinha' 2024-04-15](http://arxiv.org/abs/2404.09473v1)\]. DPO allows models to be fine-tuned based on preference data, which typically involves comparing responses to a given prompt and selecting the preferred one \[[Mingming Li' 2024-07-29](http://arxiv.org/abs/2407.19829v2)\]. This approach is especially useful when standard preference datasets lack explicit rationales for human choices, which can hinder efficient learning and robust alignment \[[Mingming Li' 2024-07-29](http://arxiv.org/abs/2407.19829v2)\]. To address this, researchers have proposed augmenting preference data with machine-generated rationales that explain the reasoning behind human preferences, thereby improving learning efficiency and model performance \[[Mingming Li' 2024-07-29](http://arxiv.org/abs/2407.19829v2)\]. Additionally, DPO has been applied to improve the generation of high-quality synthetic queries for neural retrieval models, where it integrates ranking signals directly into the query generation process to maximize downstream retrieval effectiveness \[[Gustavo Penha' 2021-11-25](http://arxiv.org/abs/2111.13057v3)\]. These efforts highlight the versatility and potential of DPO in aligning models with human preferences across diverse applications, from counter-speech generation to retrieval tasks \[[Aman Sinha' 2024-04-15](http://arxiv.org/abs/2404.09473v1)\]\[[Mingming Li' 2024-07-29](http://arxiv.org/abs/2407.19829v2)\]\[[Gustavo Penha' 2021-11-25](http://arxiv.org/abs/2111.13057v3)\].
