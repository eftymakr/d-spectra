## Related Works

Open-vocabulary object detection has evolved significantly, with recent advances focusing on improving the alignment between visual and textual representations, enhancing robustness to background samples, and enabling zero-shot detection of unseen categories. Early methods relied on pre-trained vision-language models like CLIP to provide semantic embeddings for both images and text \[[Peter Anderson' 2016-12-02](http://arxiv.org/abs/1612.00576v2)\]. These models enable zero-shot detection by aligning object features with textual descriptions. However, they often struggle with background samples and misclassified partial regions, leading to suboptimal performance \[[Lewei Yao' 2024-04-14](http://arxiv.org/abs/2404.09216v1)\]. To address these limitations, researchers have proposed methods such as BIRDet, which introduces background information modeling to enhance the classification of background regions \[[Lewei Yao' 2024-04-14](http://arxiv.org/abs/2404.09216v1)\].

Recent work has focused on improving the generalization of open-vocabulary detectors to new and unseen categories. The work in \[[Sara Sarto' 2025-03-18](http://arxiv.org/abs/2503.14604v2)\] introduces a method called MEDet, which uses proposal mining and prediction equalization to improve the detection of novel categories. This approach addresses the issue of confidence bias toward base categories and enhances the overall performance of open-vocabulary detectors. Similarly, \[[Jiancheng Pan' 2024-08-17](http://arxiv.org/abs/2408.09110v3)\] proposes a framework that integrates vision-language models with 3D point cloud data to enable open-vocabulary segmentation of camouflaged objects, demonstrating the potential of combining multiple modalities for improved performance.

The proposed method, VocAda, builds on these advancements by introducing a plug-and-play approach to refine the user-defined vocabulary. Unlike previous methods that require extensive training or complex alignment strategies, VocAda operates at inference time, using an image captioner to describe visible objects and then parsing nouns from those captions to select relevant classes from the user-defined vocabulary \[[Peter Anderson' 2016-12-02](http://arxiv.org/abs/1612.00576v2)\]. This approach does not require any training, making it highly versatile and applicable to a wide range of detectors.

In addition to refining the vocabulary, several works have explored the use of pseudo-labels and dynamic label queues to maintain high-quality pseudo-labels during training, improving the robustness of open-vocabulary detectors \[[Lewei Yao' 2024-04-14](http://arxiv.org/abs/2404.09216v1)\]. For example, \[[Shizhe Chen' 2018-06-22](http://arxiv.org/abs/1806.08854v1)\] proposes a framework that leverages the zero-shot capabilities of vision-language models to detect objects in unseen categories, demonstrating the potential of vision-language pre-training for open-vocabulary detection.

Another line of research has focused on improving the alignment between visual and textual representations. The work in \[[Jianghang Lin' 2023-12-19](http://arxiv.org/abs/2312.12437v1)\] introduces a CLIP-activated student-teacher framework, where a pre-trained CLIP model acts as a teacher to guide the student model in generating high-quality proposals and pseudo-labels. This approach significantly improves the detection performance on aerial images, achieving state-of-the-art results.

Furthermore, \[[Mingxuan Liu' 2024-05-16](http://arxiv.org/abs/2405.10053v1)\] explores the use of pseudo-labels and dynamic label queues to maintain high-quality pseudo-labels during training, improving the robustness of open-vocabulary detectors. This work highlights the importance of maintaining consistency in pseudo-labels for effective training.

In summary, the field of open-vocabulary object detection has seen significant progress, with researchers exploring various strategies to improve the alignment between visual and textual representations, enhance the robustness of detectors, and improve the generalization to new and unseen categories. The proposed method, VocAda, contributes to this body of work by introducing a novel and efficient approach to refine the user-defined vocabulary, further enhancing the performance of open-vocabulary object detection.
